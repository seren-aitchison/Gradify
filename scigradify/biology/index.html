Biology: a free course on SciGradify

SciGradify's free Biology course runs from Foundations of Biology to Experimental Biology and Research Methods: 391 lessons in 14 schools, each with notes, flashcards and check questions at three levels (Foundation, Application, Biological reasoning). It's written for science students.

Schools and lessons

School 1: Foundations of Biology

20 lessons

Foundations of Biology: key terms, sample flashcards and a quick quiz

  1. What is biology?: Biology is the scientific study of living things: what they are made of, how they work, how they develop, how they interact and how they came to be.
  2. Characteristics of living organisms: Living things share a set of properties that, taken together, distinguish them from non-living matter.
  3. Levels of biological organisation: Living things are organised in a hierarchy, from atoms and molecules through cells, tissues, organs and organisms to populations, communities, ecosystems and the biosphere.
  4. The characteristics of life: How do we know whether a newly discovered organism, a dormant seed or a frozen bacterium is alive?
  5. Classification and taxonomy: With an estimated several million species alive today, biologists need a system to name and organise them.
  6. The three domains of life: All living organisms fall into three domains: Bacteria, Archaea and Eukarya.
  7. The kingdoms of life: Within the domain Eukarya, organisms are traditionally divided into kingdoms: animals, plants, fungi and protists.
  8. Scientific method: Science is a way of finding out about the natural world by testing ideas against evidence.
  9. Hypotheses and predictions: A good investigation starts with a clear hypothesis and a precise prediction.
  10. Variables and experimental design: A fair experiment changes one factor, measures its effect on another and keeps everything else the same.
  11. Controls and reproducibility: Controls show that a result is caused by the factor being tested and not by something else.
  12. Accuracy and precision: Accuracy is how close a measurement is to the true value; precision is how close repeated measurements are to each other.
  13. Reliability and validity: A result is reliable if it can be obtained consistently, and valid if it actually measures what it claims to and answers the question asked.
  14. Biological measurements: Biologists measure things across an enormous range of scales, from nanometre-sized molecules to whole ecosystems.
  15. Units and conversions: Science uses the International System of Units (SI) so that measurements can be compared worldwide.
  16. Scientific notation: Biology deals with numbers from the 10⁻¹⁰ m of an atom to the 10¹³ cells of a human body.
  17. Graphs and data presentation: Choosing the right table or graph makes patterns in data clear and honest.
  18. Statistical foundations: Living things vary, so biologists need statistics to summarise data and decide whether differences are real or due to chance.
  19. Sampling methods: Biologists can rarely measure every individual, so they study samples.
  20. Ethical considerations in biological research: Biological research can involve animals, human volunteers, human tissue and data, and organisms that could affect the environment.

School 2: Cell Biology

30 lessons

Cell Biology: key terms, sample flashcards and a quick quiz

  1. Cell theory: Cell theory says that every living thing is made of cells, that the cell is the basic unit of life, and that new cells come only from existing cells.
  2. Prokaryotic cells: Prokaryotes, the bacteria and archaea, are cells without a nucleus or membrane-bound organelles.
  3. Eukaryotic cells: Eukaryotic cells, found in animals, plants, fungi and protists, keep their DNA in a nucleus and divide their interior into membrane-bound compartments.
  4. Animal cells: Animal cells are eukaryotic cells without a cell wall or chloroplasts.
  5. Plant cells: Plant cells share the eukaryotic plan with animal cells but add three features: a cellulose wall, chloroplasts and a large permanent vacuole.
  6. Fungal cells: Fungi, including yeasts, moulds and mushrooms, are eukaryotes with cell walls made of chitin rather than cellulose.
  7. Bacterial cells: Bacteria are the most familiar prokaryotes.
  8. Cell organelles: Organelles are the specialised compartments and structures inside eukaryotic cells.
  9. Nucleus and nucleolus: The nucleus holds almost all of a eukaryotic cell's DNA and controls which genes are used.
  10. Mitochondria: Mitochondria are the organelles where aerobic respiration releases most of the energy in food, storing it as ATP.
  11. Chloroplasts: Chloroplasts are the organelles of photosynthesis in plants and algae.
  12. Ribosomes: Ribosomes are the cell's protein factories: they read the code in messenger RNA and join amino acids in the order it specifies.
  13. Endoplasmic reticulum: The endoplasmic reticulum is a network of membranes spreading through the cytoplasm from the nuclear envelope.
  14. Golgi apparatus: The Golgi apparatus is the cell's processing and dispatch centre.
  15. Lysosomes and peroxisomes: Lysosomes and peroxisomes are small membrane-bound organelles that break things down.
  16. Cytoskeleton: The cytoskeleton is a network of protein filaments that gives the cell its shape, holds organelles in place, moves materials around and lets cells crawl, contract and divide.
  17. Cell membranes: Every cell is bounded by a plasma membrane, and eukaryotic cells are full of internal membranes too.
  18. Membrane structure: Membranes are built from a double layer of phospholipids with proteins floating in it, a structure described by the fluid mosaic model.
  19. Membrane transport: Substances cross membranes in several ways, which differ in whether they need energy and whether they need a protein.
  20. Diffusion: Diffusion is the net movement of particles from where they are more concentrated to where they are less concentrated, driven by their random motion.
  21. Osmosis: Osmosis is the diffusion of water across a partially permeable membrane.
  22. Facilitated diffusion: Facilitated diffusion is passive movement down a concentration gradient through specific membrane proteins.
  23. Active transport: Active transport moves substances against their concentration gradient using energy, usually from ATP.
  24. Endocytosis and exocytosis: Some cargo is far too large for channels or carriers.
  25. Cell specialisation: In a multicellular organism, cells take on different shapes and contents to do particular jobs.
  26. Stem cells: Stem cells are unspecialised cells that can divide to renew themselves and can also give rise to specialised cells.
  27. Cell differentiation: Differentiation is the process by which a cell becomes specialised.
  28. Cell signalling: Cells constantly send and receive chemical signals that coordinate growth, metabolism and defence.
  29. Cell junctions: In tissues, cells are physically joined to their neighbours.
  30. Cellular organisation: Multicellular organisms are organised in levels: specialised cells form tissues, tissues form organs, and organs work together in organ systems.

School 3: Biochemistry and Biomolecules

30 lessons

Biochemistry and Biomolecules: key terms, sample flashcards and a quick quiz

  1. Chemistry of life: Living things are built from a small set of elements arranged into large, complex molecules.
  2. Water and biological systems: Water makes up most of the mass of cells, and its unusual properties make life possible.
  3. Hydrogen bonding: Hydrogen bonds are weak attractions, yet they shape water, proteins and DNA.
  4. pH and biological buffers: The pH of body fluids and cells must stay within narrow limits.
  5. Carbohydrates: Carbohydrates provide energy, store energy and build structures.
  6. Monosaccharides: Monosaccharides are the building blocks of all carbohydrates.
  7. Disaccharides: Disaccharides form when two monosaccharides join by a glycosidic bond.
  8. Polysaccharides: Polysaccharides are long chains of sugar units used for storage and structure.
  9. Lipids: Lipids are a diverse group of molecules that are insoluble in water.
  10. Fatty acids: Fatty acids are the main components of triglycerides and phospholipids.
  11. Triglycerides: Triglycerides are the main form of stored fat in plants and animals.
  12. Phospholipids: Phospholipids form the basic structure of all cell membranes.
  13. Steroids: Steroids are lipids with a ring structure rather than fatty acid chains.
  14. Proteins: Proteins carry out most of the work in cells, from catalysis to transport and structure.
  15. Amino acids: Amino acids are the monomers of proteins.
  16. Peptide bonds: Amino acids join through peptide bonds to form polypeptides.
  17. Protein primary structure: The primary structure is the sequence of amino acids in a polypeptide.
  18. Protein secondary structure: Secondary structure is the regular folding of the polypeptide backbone into helices and sheets.
  19. Protein tertiary structure: Tertiary structure is the overall 3D shape of a single polypeptide.
  20. Protein quaternary structure: Some proteins are made of more than one polypeptide chain.
  21. Protein folding: Newly made polypeptides must fold into precise shapes to function.
  22. Protein denaturation: Denaturation is the loss of a protein's 3D shape and function.
  23. Enzymes: Enzymes are biological catalysts that speed up reactions in living cells.
  24. Enzyme-substrate interactions: Enzymes bind their substrates with great specificity.
  25. Enzyme kinetics: Enzyme kinetics describes how reaction rate depends on substrate concentration.
  26. Enzyme inhibition: Inhibitors reduce enzyme activity and are important in metabolic control and medicine.
  27. Factors affecting enzyme activity: Enzyme activity depends on temperature, pH, substrate concentration and enzyme concentration.
  28. Nucleic acids: Nucleic acids store and transmit genetic information.
  29. DNA and RNA: DNA and RNA are both nucleic acids but differ in structure and role.
  30. ATP and biological energy: ATP is the main energy currency of cells.

School 4: Genetics and Molecular Biology

35 lessons

Genetics and Molecular Biology: key terms, sample flashcards and a quick quiz

  1. Introduction to genetics: Genetics is the study of genes, heredity and variation.
  2. DNA structure: The structure of DNA explains how it stores and copies genetic information.
  3. DNA replication: Before a cell divides, its DNA must be copied accurately.
  4. DNA repair: DNA is constantly damaged and must be repaired to keep genetic information intact.
  5. Chromosomes: Chromosomes package DNA so that it fits in cells and can be divided accurately.
  6. Genes and alleles: Genes come in different versions called alleles.
  7. Genomes: A genome is all the genetic material of an organism.
  8. RNA structure: RNA molecules fold into shapes that suit their roles in gene expression.
  9. Transcription: Transcription copies the information in a gene into RNA.
  10. RNA processing: In eukaryotes, pre-mRNA must be processed before it can be translated.
  11. Translation: Translation converts the code in mRNA into a sequence of amino acids.
  12. Genetic code: The genetic code is the set of rules linking codons to amino acids.
  13. Protein synthesis: Protein synthesis brings together transcription, processing and translation.
  14. Gene regulation: Cells control which genes are expressed, when and how much.
  15. Operons: In bacteria, genes for related functions are often grouped in operons and controlled together.
  16. Epigenetics: Epigenetic changes alter gene expression without changing the DNA sequence.
  17. Mutations: Mutations are changes in DNA sequence.
  18. Types of mutation: Gene mutations take several forms, with very different consequences.
  19. DNA damage: DNA is damaged thousands of times a day in every cell.
  20. Mendelian genetics: Gregor Mendel's experiments with pea plants revealed the basic rules of inheritance.
  21. Dominant and recessive inheritance: Some alleles mask others in heterozygotes.
  22. Genotype and phenotype: Genotype describes the alleles an organism has, while phenotype describes its characteristics.
  23. Monohybrid crosses: A monohybrid cross follows the inheritance of one gene.
  24. Dihybrid crosses: A dihybrid cross follows two genes at once.
  25. Incomplete dominance: In incomplete dominance, heterozygotes show an intermediate phenotype.
  26. Codominance: In codominance, both alleles in a heterozygote are fully expressed.
  27. Sex-linked inheritance: Genes on the sex chromosomes are inherited differently in males and females.
  28. Pedigree analysis: Pedigrees show how a trait is passed through a family.
  29. Population genetics: Population genetics studies how allele frequencies vary and change in populations.
  30. Genetic linkage: Genes close together on the same chromosome tend to be inherited together.
  31. Recombination: Recombination creates new combinations of alleles.
  32. Chromosomal abnormalities: Changes in chromosome number or structure can have major effects on development.
  33. Genomics: Genomics studies whole genomes, using sequencing and computing.
  34. Human genetic variation: Humans differ from one another at millions of positions in the genome.
  35. Genome-wide association studies: Genome-wide association studies scan the genome for variants linked to traits and diseases.

School 5: Cell Division and Development

23 lessons

Cell Division and Development: key terms, sample flashcards and a quick quiz

  1. Cell cycle: The cell cycle is the sequence of events by which a cell grows and divides.
  2. Interphase: Interphase is the longest part of the cell cycle and prepares the cell for division.
  3. Mitosis: Mitosis divides the nucleus to produce two genetically identical nuclei.
  4. Meiosis: Meiosis is the division that produces gametes with half the chromosome number.
  5. Comparison of mitosis and meiosis: Mitosis and meiosis are both forms of nuclear division but have different outcomes and roles.
  6. Chromosome segregation: Accurate chromosome segregation ensures each daughter cell receives the correct chromosomes.
  7. Cell-cycle regulation: The cell cycle is controlled by proteins that drive cells from one phase to the next.
  8. Checkpoints: Checkpoints are control points where the cell checks whether conditions are right to continue the cycle.
  9. Apoptosis: Apoptosis is programmed cell death, a controlled process that removes unwanted cells.
  10. Necrosis: Necrosis is uncontrolled cell death caused by injury or damage.
  11. Cellular senescence: Senescent cells stop dividing permanently but remain alive.
  12. Cancer biology: Cancer is uncontrolled cell division caused by changes in genes that regulate the cell cycle.
  13. Tumour development: Tumours develop in stages from a single altered cell to an invasive cancer.
  14. Stem-cell biology: Stem cells can divide to renew themselves and differentiate into specialised cells.
  15. Embryonic development: Embryonic development turns a single cell into a complex organism.
  16. Fertilisation: Fertilisation is the fusion of a sperm and egg to form a zygote.
  17. Cleavage: Cleavage is the series of rapid cell divisions that follows fertilisation.
  18. Gastrulation: Gastrulation reorganises the embryo into germ layers, setting up the body plan.
  19. Organogenesis: Organogenesis is the formation of organs from the germ layers.
  20. Developmental signalling: Cells in an embryo communicate using signalling molecules that control their fates.
  21. Cell fate determination: During development, cells become committed to particular fates.
  22. Tissue formation: Tissues form as cells of similar types organise and adhere together.
  23. Regeneration: Some animals can regrow lost body parts, while others, including humans, have limited regeneration.

School 6: Evolution and Natural Selection

24 lessons

Evolution and Natural Selection: key terms, sample flashcards and a quick quiz

  1. Origins of evolutionary thought: Ideas about how species change developed over centuries before and after Darwin.
  2. Evidence for evolution: Evolution is supported by many independent lines of evidence.
  3. Natural selection: Natural selection is the main mechanism of adaptive evolution.
  4. Adaptation: Adaptations are inherited features that improve survival and reproduction in a particular environment.
  5. Genetic variation: Genetic variation is the raw material for evolution.
  6. Mutation and evolution: Mutations provide the new genetic variation on which evolution depends.
  7. Genetic drift: Genetic drift is change in allele frequencies due to chance.
  8. Gene flow: Gene flow is the movement of alleles between populations.
  9. Sexual selection: Sexual selection favours traits that improve mating success.
  10. Selection pressures: Selection pressures are environmental factors that affect survival and reproduction.
  11. Fitness: In evolutionary biology, fitness means reproductive success, not physical strength.
  12. Speciation: Speciation is the formation of new species.
  13. Reproductive isolation: Reproductive isolation keeps species separate.
  14. Allopatric speciation: Allopatric speciation occurs when populations are geographically separated.
  15. Sympatric speciation: Sympatric speciation occurs without geographic separation.
  16. Phylogenetics: Phylogenetics reconstructs the evolutionary relationships between organisms.
  17. Evolutionary trees: Evolutionary trees show relationships between groups of organisms.
  18. Common ancestry: All living organisms share a common ancestor.
  19. Fossil evidence: Fossils provide direct evidence of past life and evolutionary change.
  20. Molecular evolution: Molecular evolution studies how DNA and protein sequences change over time.
  21. Human evolution: Human evolution traces the hominin lineage from our common ancestor with chimpanzees to modern humans.
  22. Evolutionary medicine: Evolutionary medicine applies evolutionary thinking to health and disease.
  23. Evolution of resistance: Resistance to antibiotics, pesticides and other treatments evolves by natural selection.
  24. Population genetics: Population genetics provides the mathematical framework for evolution.

School 7: Human Anatomy and Physiology

34 lessons

Human Anatomy and Physiology: key terms, sample flashcards and a quick quiz

  1. Organisation of the human body: The human body is organised from cells into tissues, organs and organ systems.
  2. Homeostasis: Homeostasis keeps the internal environment stable.
  3. Nervous system: The nervous system provides rapid communication throughout the body.
  4. Neurones: Neurones are cells specialised to transmit electrical impulses.
  5. Action potentials: An action potential is a brief electrical impulse along a neurone.
  6. Synaptic transmission: Synapses pass signals between neurones or to effectors.
  7. Central nervous system: The central nervous system integrates information and controls the body.
  8. Peripheral nervous system: The peripheral nervous system connects the CNS to the rest of the body.
  9. Endocrine system: The endocrine system uses hormones carried in the blood to coordinate the body.
  10. Hormones and signalling: Hormones signal through receptors on or inside target cells.
  11. Cardiovascular system: The cardiovascular system transports substances around the body.
  12. Heart anatomy: The heart is a muscular double pump.
  13. Cardiac cycle: The cardiac cycle is the sequence of events in one heartbeat.
  14. Blood vessels: Arteries, capillaries and veins have structures suited to their functions.
  15. Blood composition: Blood is a tissue made of cells suspended in plasma.
  16. Circulation: Humans have a double circulation with pulmonary and systemic circuits.
  17. Respiratory system: The respiratory system takes in oxygen and removes carbon dioxide.
  18. Ventilation: Ventilation moves air into and out of the lungs.
  19. Gas exchange: Gas exchange in the alveoli supplies oxygen and removes carbon dioxide.
  20. Oxygen transport: Haemoglobin carries oxygen from the lungs to the tissues.
  21. Digestive system: The digestive system breaks down food and absorbs nutrients.
  22. Digestion and absorption: Food molecules are digested into small units and absorbed in the small intestine.
  23. Liver and pancreas: The liver and pancreas have essential digestive and metabolic roles.
  24. Renal system: The renal system removes wastes and controls water and salt balance.
  25. Kidney function: The nephron filters blood and adjusts the composition of urine.
  26. Urine formation: Urine composition reflects how the kidneys regulate the body.
  27. Musculoskeletal system: The musculoskeletal system supports the body and allows movement.
  28. Bone physiology: Bone is a living tissue that is constantly remodelled.
  29. Muscle contraction: Skeletal muscle contracts by the sliding of protein filaments.
  30. Immune system: The immune system defends the body against pathogens.
  31. Reproductive system: The reproductive systems produce gametes and allow fertilisation.
  32. Human development: Human development continues from fertilisation through birth, childhood and ageing.
  33. Skin and thermoregulation: The skin protects the body and helps control temperature.
  34. Fluid and electrolyte balance: The body controls the volume and composition of its fluids.

School 8: Plant Biology

34 lessons

Plant Biology: key terms, sample flashcards and a quick quiz

  1. Plant cell structure: Plant cells share features with animal cells but have distinctive structures.
  2. Plant tissues: Plants are built from a few main tissue types.
  3. Roots and root systems: Roots anchor plants and absorb water and minerals.
  4. Stems: Stems support plants and transport materials between roots and leaves.
  5. Leaves: Leaves are the main organs of photosynthesis.
  6. Plant vascular tissue: Vascular tissue transports water, minerals and sugars through plants.
  7. Xylem: Xylem is specialised for transporting water and providing support.
  8. Phloem: Phloem transports sugars and other organic substances.
  9. Water transport: Water moves from soil to leaves through roots and xylem.
  10. Transpiration: Transpiration is the loss of water vapour from leaves.
  11. Mineral nutrition: Plants need mineral ions from the soil for growth.
  12. Photosynthesis: Photosynthesis converts light energy into chemical energy in sugars.
  13. Light-dependent reactions: The light-dependent reactions capture light energy to make ATP and NADPH.
  14. Calvin cycle: The Calvin cycle uses ATP and NADPH to fix carbon dioxide into sugars.
  15. C3 photosynthesis: Most plants use C3 photosynthesis, named after its first product.
  16. C4 photosynthesis: C4 plants concentrate CO₂ around RuBisCO to avoid photorespiration.
  17. CAM photosynthesis: CAM plants separate CO₂ uptake and the Calvin cycle in time.
  18. Cellular respiration in plants: Plants respire as well as photosynthesise.
  19. Plant hormones: Plant hormones coordinate growth and responses to the environment.
  20. Auxins: Auxins control cell elongation and many growth responses.
  21. Gibberellins: Gibberellins promote stem elongation and seed germination.
  22. Cytokinins: Cytokinins promote cell division and delay ageing.
  23. Abscisic acid: Abscisic acid helps plants respond to stress and controls dormancy.
  24. Ethylene: Ethylene is a gaseous hormone that controls ripening and ageing.
  25. Tropisms: Tropisms are growth responses to directional stimuli.
  26. Photoperiodism: Photoperiodism is the response of plants to day length.
  27. Flowering: Flowers are the reproductive structures of flowering plants.
  28. Pollination: Pollination is the transfer of pollen to a stigma.
  29. Fertilisation: In flowering plants, fertilisation follows pollination.
  30. Seed formation: Seeds contain an embryo and food store protected by a coat.
  31. Germination: Germination is the start of growth of a seed into a seedling.
  32. Plant reproduction: Plants reproduce sexually and asexually.
  33. Plant responses to stress: Plants cannot move away from danger, so they respond to stress in other ways.
  34. Plant biotechnology: Plant biotechnology uses scientific techniques to improve and propagate plants.

School 9: Microbiology

30 lessons

Microbiology: key terms, sample flashcards and a quick quiz

  1. Introduction to microbiology: Microbiology is the study of microorganisms.
  2. Bacterial structure: Bacteria have a simple but highly effective cell structure.
  3. Bacterial classification: Bacteria are classified using their appearance, biochemistry and genes.
  4. Bacterial growth: Bacteria multiply by binary fission and can grow very rapidly.
  5. Bacterial metabolism: Bacteria show enormous metabolic diversity.
  6. Bacterial genetics: Bacterial genetics explains how bacteria store, express and change their genes.
  7. Horizontal gene transfer: Bacteria can share genes without reproducing.
  8. Viral structure: Viruses are tiny infectious particles with a simple structure.
  9. Viral replication: Viruses hijack host cells to make copies of themselves.
  10. Viral evolution: Viruses evolve rapidly, producing new strains and variants.
  11. Fungi: Fungi are eukaryotic organisms including yeasts, moulds and mushrooms.
  12. Protozoa: Protozoa are single-celled eukaryotes, some of which cause major diseases.
  13. Archaea: Archaea are prokaryotes that form a separate domain of life.
  14. Microbial ecology: Microbial ecology studies microbes in their environments.
  15. Microbiomes: A microbiome is the community of microbes living in a particular environment, including the human body.
  16. Host-microbe interactions: Microbes and hosts interact along a spectrum from mutualism to parasitism.
  17. Pathogenic microorganisms: Pathogens are microbes that cause disease.
  18. Virulence: Virulence is the degree of harm a pathogen causes.
  19. Transmission: Pathogens spread between hosts by several routes.
  20. Infection: Infection progresses through stages from exposure to recovery or death.
  21. Antibiotics: Antibiotics kill or inhibit bacteria by targeting structures they need.
  22. Antimicrobial resistance: Antimicrobial resistance is a growing global health threat.
  23. Sterilisation: Sterilisation destroys all microorganisms, including spores.
  24. Disinfection: Disinfection reduces the number of microbes on surfaces and skin.
  25. Microbial culture: Microbes are grown in the laboratory to study and identify them.
  26. Microscopy: Microscopes allow us to see microbes and their structures.
  27. Molecular identification: Molecular methods identify microbes quickly and accurately from their genes or proteins.
  28. Environmental microbiology: Environmental microbiology studies microbes in soil, water and air.
  29. Industrial microbiology: Industrial microbiology uses microbes to make products on a large scale.
  30. Applied microbiology: Applied microbiology uses microbes to solve problems in health, food, agriculture and the environment.

School 10: Immunology

27 lessons

Immunology: key terms, sample flashcards and a quick quiz

  1. Introduction to immunology: Immunology is the study of how the body defends itself.
  2. Innate immunity: Innate immunity provides the first, rapid line of defence.
  3. Adaptive immunity: Adaptive immunity is specific and improves with exposure.
  4. Physical barriers: Physical and chemical barriers stop most pathogens entering the body.
  5. Immune cells: The immune system uses many types of white blood cell.
  6. Neutrophils: Neutrophils are the most abundant white cells and the first to arrive at infections.
  7. Macrophages: Macrophages are long-lived phagocytes in tissues.
  8. Dendritic cells: Dendritic cells are the main link between innate and adaptive immunity.
  9. Natural killer cells: Natural killer cells destroy infected and cancerous cells without prior sensitisation.
  10. B lymphocytes: B lymphocytes produce antibodies.
  11. T lymphocytes: T lymphocytes coordinate immune responses and kill infected cells.
  12. Antibodies: Antibodies are proteins that bind specific antigens.
  13. Antigen recognition: Lymphocytes recognise a vast range of antigens.
  14. Antigen presentation: Antigen presentation displays fragments of proteins to T cells.
  15. MHC molecules: MHC molecules display peptides to T cells and vary greatly between people.
  16. Cytokines: Cytokines are signalling proteins that coordinate immune responses.
  17. Complement system: The complement system is a group of blood proteins that help destroy pathogens.
  18. Inflammation: Inflammation is the body's response to infection and injury.
  19. Immune memory: Immune memory allows faster and stronger responses to pathogens met before.
  20. Vaccination: Vaccination trains the immune system safely to prevent disease.
  21. Hypersensitivity: Hypersensitivity reactions are harmful immune responses.
  22. Allergy: Allergies are type I hypersensitivity reactions to harmless substances.
  23. Autoimmunity: Autoimmune diseases occur when the immune system attacks the body's own tissues.
  24. Immunodeficiency: Immunodeficiency is a weakened immune system that increases infection risk.
  25. Transplant immunology: Transplanted tissues can be rejected by the immune system.
  26. Cancer immunology: The immune system can recognise and destroy cancer cells, but tumours can evade it.
  27. Immunotherapy: Immunotherapy uses the immune system to treat disease, especially cancer.

School 11: Ecology and Environmental Biology

30 lessons

Ecology and Environmental Biology: key terms, sample flashcards and a quick quiz

  1. Introduction to ecology: Ecology is the study of interactions between organisms and their environment.
  2. Organisms and populations: Populations have characteristics such as size, density and distribution.
  3. Habitats and niches: Every species has a habitat and a niche.
  4. Ecosystems: Ecosystems are communities interacting with their physical environment.
  5. Biomes: Biomes are large regions with similar climate and vegetation.
  6. Food chains: Food chains show how energy passes from one organism to another.
  7. Food webs: Food webs show the many interlinked feeding relationships in an ecosystem.
  8. Energy flow: Energy flows through ecosystems in one direction and is lost at each step.
  9. Trophic levels: Trophic levels describe an organism's position in a food chain.
  10. Primary productivity: Primary productivity measures how fast producers make organic matter.
  11. Nutrient cycles: Nutrients cycle between living organisms and the environment.
  12. Carbon cycle: Carbon cycles between the atmosphere, living organisms, oceans and rocks.
  13. Nitrogen cycle: Nitrogen cycles between the atmosphere, soil and organisms through processes mainly carried out by bacteria.
  14. Water cycle: Water moves continuously between the oceans, atmosphere and land.
  15. Population dynamics: Populations change in size over time.
  16. Competition: Competition occurs when organisms need the same limited resources.
  17. Predation: Predation shapes populations and drives adaptations in both predators and prey.
  18. Mutualism: Mutualism is a relationship in which both species benefit.
  19. Parasitism: Parasites live on or in a host and harm it.
  20. Succession: Succession is the change in a community over time.
  21. Biodiversity: Biodiversity is the variety of life at all levels.
  22. Species richness: Species richness and diversity indices measure biodiversity.
  23. Conservation biology: Conservation biology aims to protect biodiversity.
  24. Habitat fragmentation: Habitat fragmentation breaks habitats into small, isolated patches.
  25. Invasive species: Invasive species are non-native species that spread and cause harm.
  26. Climate change and ecosystems: Climate change is altering ecosystems worldwide.
  27. Pollution: Pollution harms organisms and ecosystems.
  28. Environmental monitoring: Environmental monitoring tracks the state of ecosystems over time.
  29. Conservation strategies: Conservation uses in-situ and ex-situ strategies to protect species.
  30. Sustainable ecosystems: Sustainability means meeting present needs without harming future generations.

School 12: Biotechnology and Genetic Engineering

25 lessons

Biotechnology and Genetic Engineering: key terms, sample flashcards and a quick quiz

  1. Foundations of biotechnology: Biotechnology uses living organisms and their components to make useful products.
  2. Recombinant DNA: Recombinant DNA combines DNA from different sources.
  3. Restriction enzymes: Restriction enzymes cut DNA at specific sequences.
  4. DNA ligase: DNA ligase seals breaks in DNA by joining fragments.
  5. Plasmids: Plasmids are small circular DNA molecules widely used as vectors.
  6. Cloning vectors: Different vectors carry different sizes of DNA into different hosts.
  7. Polymerase chain reaction: PCR amplifies a specific DNA sequence into millions of copies.
  8. Gel electrophoresis: Gel electrophoresis separates DNA fragments by size.
  9. DNA sequencing: DNA sequencing determines the order of bases in DNA.
  10. Sanger sequencing: Sanger sequencing uses chain-terminating nucleotides to read DNA.
  11. Next-generation sequencing: Next-generation sequencing reads millions of DNA fragments at once.
  12. Whole-genome sequencing: Whole-genome sequencing reads an organism's entire DNA.
  13. CRISPR-Cas systems: CRISPR-Cas systems are bacterial immune defences that have been adapted as gene-editing tools.
  14. Gene editing: Gene editing makes targeted changes to DNA.
  15. Gene expression systems: Expression systems are host cells used to make proteins from introduced genes.
  16. Recombinant proteins: Recombinant proteins are made from cloned genes in host cells.
  17. Molecular diagnostics: Molecular diagnostics detect disease using DNA, RNA or proteins.
  18. Genetic testing: Genetic testing identifies changes in genes, chromosomes or proteins.
  19. Gene therapy: Gene therapy treats disease by adding, replacing or editing genes.
  20. Cell therapy: Cell therapy uses living cells as treatments.
  21. Biopharmaceuticals: Biopharmaceuticals are medicines made using living systems.
