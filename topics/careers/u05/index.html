Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What is the main purpose of a CV?

To persuade an employer to invite you to interview. From “What a CV is for” →

In plain English: A CV is a short marketing document that persuades an employer to invite you to interview. Key idea: A CV is a concise, targeted marketing document, usually no more than two A4 sides in the UK, designed to win an interview by showing quickly that you meet a role's criteria; recruiters skim first, and UK CVs should omit photos, dates of birth and other personal details.

What does CAR stand for?

Context, Action, Result. From “Writing achievement statements” →

In plain English: Strong CVs describe achievements, not just duties. Key idea: Achievement statements begin with action verbs, describe what you did and show results, ideally with numbers such as people, money, time or percentages, often following a Context, Action, Result pattern, which makes a CV far more persuasive than lists of duties.

What is a hybrid CV?

A CV opening with key skills followed by a chronological history. From “CV formats: chronological and skills-based” →

In plain English: Most CVs use a chronological format, but a skills-based format can work better in some situations. Key idea: Chronological CVs show history and progression and suit most candidates; skills-based CVs group evidence by skill and can help career changers or those with gaps, but may raise suspicion; hybrid CVs combine a skills summary with a chronological history.

What does "résumé" usually mean in the United States?

A short CV, typically one page. From “Academic and international CVs” →

In plain English: Academic posts and jobs abroad often need a different kind of CV. Key idea: Academic CVs are longer and focus on research, publications, conferences, grants and teaching, while international CVs follow local conventions on length, format and personal details, from the one-page US résumé to the Europass format, so applicants should research expectations and explain their qualifications.

What should the "why you" paragraph contain?

Two or three pieces of evidence matching the key requirements, with results. From “Structuring a cover letter” →

In plain English: A clear structure makes a cover letter easy to read and persuasive. Key idea: A UK cover letter has contact details, date, a suitable salutation and sign-off, and a clear four-part structure: an opening that names the role with a hook, why this employer, why you with evidence, and a positive close, all within one page.

Key takeaways

  • A CV is a concise, targeted marketing document, usually no more than two A4 sides in the UK, designed to win an interview by showing quickly that you meet a role's criteria; recruiters skim first, and UK CVs should omit photos, dates of birth and other personal details.
  • A UK CV usually has contact details, an optional profile, education, experience and skills, with optional extra sections; the most relevant section should come first, entries should be in reverse chronological order, and a clean, consistent layout with bullet points helps both people and applicant tracking systems.
  • Achievement statements begin with action verbs, describe what you did and show results, ideally with numbers such as people, money, time or percentages, often following a Context, Action, Result pattern, which makes a CV far more persuasive than lists of duties.
  • Tailoring means analysing the job description and person specification, matching specific evidence to each essential criterion, using accurate keywords, reordering and trimming content, and keeping a master CV to create targeted versions efficiently.
  • Chronological CVs show history and progression and suit most candidates; skills-based CVs group evidence by skill and can help career changers or those with gaps, but may raise suspicion; hybrid CVs combine a skills summary with a chronological history.
  • Applicant tracking systems store and help filter applications, so CVs should use simple layouts and standard headings that software can read, and include accurate keywords from the job description in context, while still persuading the human recruiter.

Common mistakes in CVs and Cover Letters

  • Treating a CV as a complete life history. It should be selective and focused on the role.
  • Using elaborate templates with columns and graphics. They can confuse applicant tracking systems and distract readers.
  • Starting bullets with "Responsible for". It describes a duty, not what you achieved.
  • Sending the same generic CV to every vacancy. Tailored CVs show employers clearly how you fit their role.
  • Using a skills-based CV without saying where each skill was developed. Recruiters need context to judge your claims.
  • Hiding keywords in white text to fool an ATS. Recruiters see it when the CV is read, and it damages trust.

CVs and Cover Letters: questions students ask

What is reverse chronological order?

Listing the most recent items first.

What is a person specification?

A list of the skills, qualifications and qualities an employer is looking for.

Who might benefit from a skills-based CV?

Career changers or people with gaps or varied short-term roles.

What does an applicant tracking system do?

It stores applications, manages candidates through stages and helps recruiters search CVs.
