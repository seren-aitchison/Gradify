Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check.

What is employability?

The achievements, skills, understandings and attributes that make a person more likely to gain work and succeed in it; it is not the same as being employed. From “What career development is” →

Which Gatsby Benchmark covers one-to-one guidance?

Benchmark 8, personal guidance from a careers adviser trained to an appropriate level. From “The Gatsby Benchmarks and careers support” →

Name Tomlinson's five graduate capitals.

Human, social, cultural, identity and psychological capital. From “Transferable skills and graduate attributes” →

Which Big Five trait most consistently predicts job performance?

Conscientiousness. From “Interests and personality tools: uses and limits” →

In Herzberg's theory, what is the difference between hygiene factors and motivators?

Hygiene factors (pay, conditions) prevent dissatisfaction; motivators (achievement, recognition, the work itself) create satisfaction. From “Motivations and work-life priorities” →

Lessons in this school

  1. What career development is
    Career development is the lifelong process of managing learning, work and life.
  2. Career theories: matching, development and chance
    Career theories explain how people come to do the work they do.
  3. The Gatsby Benchmarks and careers support
    The Gatsby Benchmarks define good careers guidance in English schools and colleges, and universities and the National Careers Service extend that support into adulthood.
  4. Identifying your skills
    Employers recruit for skills as much as for qualifications.
  5. Transferable skills and graduate attributes
    Universities describe the qualities they aim to develop in every student as graduate attributes, and employers publish the skills they look for.
  6. Values and what matters to you at work
    Work values are the things you most want work to give you, such as security, creativity, helping others or high pay.
  7. Strengths and how to use them
    A strength is something you are good at and energised by, not just something you can do.
  8. Interests and personality tools: uses and limits
    Career quizzes, interest inventories and personality questionnaires are everywhere.
  9. Reflective practice and the skills audit
    Reflection turns experience into learning you can talk about.
  10. Motivations and work-life priorities
    Why you want to work shapes which jobs will satisfy you.
  11. Your career story and personal brand
    Recruiters remember a clear, credible story better than a list of facts.
  12. Setting career goals
    Goals turn self-knowledge into action.

Key terms

Career development
The lifelong process of managing learning, work and life roles and the transitions between them
RIASEC
Holland's six interest types: Realistic, Investigative, Artistic, Social, Enterprising and Conventional
Gatsby Benchmarks
Eight standards of good career guidance in schools and colleges, set out by the Gatsby Foundation in 2014
Transferable skills
Skills that can be used across many different jobs and settings, such as communication, teamwork and problem solving
Graduate attributes
The qualities a university aims to develop in all its graduates, beyond subject knowledge
Work values
The things a person wants work to provide, such as security, autonomy, income or helping others
Strength
Something a person performs well, uses often and is energised by
Interest inventory
A questionnaire comparing a person's likes and dislikes with those of people in different occupations
Reflective practice
Deliberately thinking about experience to learn from it and change future action
Intrinsic motivation
Doing something because it is interesting or satisfying in itself
Elevator pitch
A short spoken introduction (30 to 60 seconds) summarising who you are, what you've done and what you're looking for
SMART goal
A goal that is Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Time-bound
