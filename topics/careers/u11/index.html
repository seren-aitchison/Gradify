Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What do the letters DOTS stand for?

Decision learning, Opportunity awareness, Transition learning and Self awareness. From “Career planning models” →

In plain English: Career planning models give a structure for thinking about where you are and where you want to go. Key idea: The DOTS model breaks career learning into self awareness, opportunity awareness, decision learning and transition learning, while career adaptability (concern, control, curiosity and confidence) helps you keep planning as a repeating cycle.

What does GROW stand for?

Goal, Reality, Options and Will (or Way forward). From “Action planning” →

In plain English: An action plan turns career goals into steps you actually take. Key idea: An action plan turns goals into dated steps, and the GROW model (Goal, Reality, Options, Will) structures it, while implementation intentions ("When X, I will Y") and regular reviews increase follow-through.

What are Bridges's three phases of transition?

An ending, a neutral zone and a new beginning. From “Changing career” →

In plain English: Many people change career at least once. Key idea: Successful career changers usually test new directions through experiments such as side projects, volunteering and networking, use transferable skills and plan finances, while understanding that transitions include an unsettling neutral zone.

What are the three dimensions of explanatory style?

Permanent, pervasive and personal. From “Dealing with rejection” →

In plain English: Rejection hurts, and how you interpret it affects your motivation to keep going. Key idea: Explaining setbacks as permanent, pervasive and personal undermines persistence, so reframe rejections as temporary and specific, treat yourself with self-compassion, set process goals and seek support if low mood persists.

How do planned happenstance and chaos theory view indecision?

As a reasonable response to an uncertain world rather than a problem. From “Uncertainty and planned happenstance” →

In plain English: Careers are shaped by chance as well as planning. Key idea: Chance plays a large part in careers, so planned happenstance and chaos theory encourage curiosity, persistence, flexibility, optimism and risk taking, balanced with enough planning to be ready when unexpected opportunities arise.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Career Planning and Resilience

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Who developed the DOTS model of career learning? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Bill Law and Tony Watts. Law and Watts set out the DOTS model in 1977 as a framework for careers education.

  2. Which is an example of a career management skill? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Researching labour market information on a sector. Researching opportunities is a career management skill; the others are job-specific skills.

  3. In the GROW model, what does the R stand for? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Reality. Reality means assessing where you are now before considering options.

  4. Researching what jobs a geography degree can lead to belongs to which part of DOTS? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Opportunity awareness. Finding out what options exist is opportunity awareness.

  5. A graduate is an excellent nurse but struggles to plan her next career move. What does she need to develop? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Career management skills such as exploration and decision making. Strong job skills do not automatically include the skills of managing a career.

  6. Which is an implementation intention? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    When I finish lectures on Monday, I will spend an hour on applications. It links a specific situation to a specific action, which research shows improves follow-through.

  7. A student knows her values well and has researched options but keeps putting off choosing. Which area needs most work? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Decision learning. She has self and opportunity awareness, so the gap is in making and acting on decisions.

  8. A student wants to improve his career management skills over a year. What is the most effective approach? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Audit them, set actions, practise and review progress. Deliberate auditing, practice and review build skills more reliably than passive approaches.

  9. A student's plan stalls because a key event is cancelled. What is the best response? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Adjust the actions while keeping the goal. Reviewing and adapting actions keeps progress going when circumstances change.

Key takeaways

  • The DOTS model breaks career learning into self awareness, opportunity awareness, decision learning and transition learning, while career adaptability (concern, control, curiosity and confidence) helps you keep planning as a repeating cycle.
  • Career management skills, such as self-management, exploration, decision making, opportunity creation and transition management, help you manage your career throughout life and can be developed through auditing, practice, reflection and feedback.
  • An action plan turns goals into dated steps, and the GROW model (Goal, Reality, Options, Will) structures it, while implementation intentions ("When X, I will Y") and regular reviews increase follow-through.
  • CPD is planned lifelong learning that many professions require, built from formal courses and experience, and Kolb's cycle helps you learn from experience, while UK routes include employer training, Skills Bootcamps, Free Courses for Jobs and part-time study.
  • Successful career changers usually test new directions through experiments such as side projects, volunteering and networking, use transferable skills and plan finances, while understanding that transitions include an unsettling neutral zone.
  • Automation and AI change tasks within jobs more often than they remove whole jobs, estimates of jobs at risk vary widely with method, and skills such as analytical thinking, digital literacy, interpersonal skills and adaptability are likely to grow in value.

Common mistakes in Career Planning and Resilience

  • Treating a career plan as fixed once written. Plans should be reviewed and adapted as circumstances change.
  • Assuming good job skills are enough to manage a career. You also need skills to explore, decide, network and adapt.
  • Writing a goal with no first step. Identify a small action you can take this week to start momentum.
  • Thinking CPD only means formal courses. Reading, mentoring, projects and reflection all count if they develop your practice.
  • Waiting to be completely certain before trying anything. Small experiments create the clarity that thinking alone often cannot.
  • Treating a single headline percentage as a prediction. Estimates depend heavily on method and remain uncertain.

Career Planning and Resilience: questions students ask

How are career management skills different from job skills?

They help you manage your career, such as exploring, deciding and networking, rather than do a particular job.

Why should you record CPD?

It provides evidence for appraisals, promotions and professional registration.

What did Ibarra find about how successful career changers work out new directions?

They experimented through side projects, volunteering and new networks.

Why do task-based estimates of automation risk tend to be lower than occupation-based ones?

Many jobs contain tasks that are hard to automate, such as caring or negotiating.
