Lessons in this school

  1. How application forms work
    Most large graduate employers, the public sector and the NHS use online application forms rather than CVs.
  2. Competency-based questions
    Competency-based questions ask for a specific example of when you showed a skill or behaviour.
  3. The STAR method
    STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the standard way to structure answers to competency questions.
  4. Strengths-based questions
    Strengths-based questions explore what you enjoy and do naturally, rather than what you've done.
  5. Motivational questions
    "Why do you want this job?" and "Why us?" appear on almost every application.
  6. Supporting and personal statements
    Many public sector, NHS, education and charity applications ask for a supporting statement that shows how you meet the person specification.
  7. Civil Service Success Profiles
    The UK Civil Service assesses candidates using Success Profiles, a framework of five elements including nine behaviours.
  8. What psychometric tests measure
    Psychometric tests are standardised assessments of ability, aptitude or personality used in selection.
  9. Numerical reasoning tests
    Numerical reasoning tests check how well you interpret data in tables and charts and calculate under time pressure.
  10. Verbal reasoning tests
    Verbal reasoning tests check how accurately you understand and draw conclusions from written passages.
  11. Logical and inductive reasoning tests
    Logical, inductive and diagrammatic reasoning tests assess how you spot patterns and rules in abstract shapes or sequences.
  12. Situational judgement tests
    Situational judgement tests (SJTs) present realistic work scenarios and ask how you would or should respond.
  13. Personality questionnaires
    Many employers use personality questionnaires to understand candidates' preferred ways of working.
  14. Game-based assessments
    Some employers now replace traditional tests with short online games that measure cognitive abilities and behavioural traits.
  15. Reasonable adjustments in assessment
    Disabled candidates have a legal right to reasonable adjustments in recruitment, including tests and interviews.

Key terms

Application form
A standardised set of questions every candidate answers, allowing consistent comparison and scoring
Competency
An observable behaviour or skill linked to effective performance in a role
STAR
A structure for describing an example: Situation, Task, Action, Result
Strengths-based question
A question exploring what a candidate enjoys, does naturally and is energised by
Motivational question
A question assessing why a candidate wants the role, sector and organisation
Supporting statement
A section of an application explaining, with evidence, how the candidate meets each criterion of the person specification
Success Profiles
The UK Civil Service recruitment framework assessing behaviours, strengths, ability, experience and technical skills
Norm group
The comparison group against which an individual's test score is interpreted
Percentage change
The change between two values divided by the original value, times 100
Cannot Say
The answer when a passage doesn't give enough information to decide whether a statement is true or false
Inductive reasoning
Inferring general rules from specific examples, such as a sequence of shapes
Situational judgement test
An assessment presenting realistic work scenarios and asking candidates to judge the effectiveness of responses
Forced-choice format
A questionnaire format asking candidates to choose between statements of similar desirability, reducing faking
Game-based assessment
A selection assessment using short games to measure cognitive abilities and behavioural traits
