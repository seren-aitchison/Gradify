Key terms and formulas at a glance

Work shadowing
Observing someone doing their job, with little or no hands-on work
Internship
A fixed period of real work in an organisation, usually paid, for students or recent graduates
Sandwich degree
A degree with a period of work, usually a year, between the taught years
Spring week
A short programme, often around a week in the Easter vacation, introducing first-year students to an employer
Volunteer
Someone who gives time freely, without a contract or payment beyond genuine expenses
Commercial awareness
Understanding how a business makes money and what affects its performance
Speculative application
An unsolicited, tailored approach to an employer asking about possible opportunities
Worker
A person who personally does work under a contract or arrangement for reward, entitled to the minimum wage and other basic rights
Learning objective
A specific skill or achievement an intern aims to gain during a placement, agreed with a supervisor
Virtual work experience
Online, often self-paced tasks designed by employers to simulate real work in their organisation
Experience log
A regular record of situations, actions, results, skills, feedback and contacts from experiences
Selection criteria
The competencies, strengths, values and requirements against which recruiters assess candidates

Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What is the difference between work experience and work shadowing?

Work experience involves doing supervised tasks; shadowing involves observing someone at work with little or no hands-on work. From “Work experience and work shadowing” →

In plain English: Short work experience and work shadowing let you see a job from the inside before committing to it. Key idea: Work experience means doing supervised tasks and shadowing means observing; both are short tasters that test interest and give material for applications when you reflect on what you learned.

When does a placement year usually take place in a sandwich degree?

Between the second and final years, making a three-year degree four years long. From “Placement years and sandwich degrees” →

In plain English: A placement year, or sandwich year, is a year of paid work taken as part of a degree. Key idea: A placement year is usually a paid year of work between the second and final years, supported by the university; it gives substantial experience, often a return offer and clarity about career fit, at the cost of an extra year.

Name two ways volunteering helps a career.

Any two: builds skills, gives sector experience, shows motivation, provides contacts, gives achievements to use as evidence. From “Volunteering” →

In plain English: Volunteering means giving time unpaid to help a cause or organisation. Key idea: Volunteering is unpaid work freely given for a cause; chosen well it builds skills, sector experience and evidence of motivation, but a "volunteer" with set hours and pay-like benefits may legally be a worker.

Which body enforces the National Minimum Wage?

HMRC (His Majesty's Revenue and Customs). From “Unpaid internships and the National Minimum Wage” →

In plain English: Whether an intern must be paid depends on their employment status, not the label on the role. Key idea: An intern who is a worker must be paid at least the National Minimum Wage; exemptions include required course placements of up to a year, school work experience, shadowing and genuine voluntary work for charities. HMRC enforces the law.

How can a remote intern make up for fewer informal learning opportunities?

Book short calls with colleagues, ask for regular check-ins, keep cameras on in meetings and take part actively. From “Virtual and remote experience” →

In plain English: Since 2020, virtual work experience, remote internships and online job simulations have become a common part of early careers programmes. Key idea: Virtual programmes, live online events, remote internships and micro-internships widen access to experience; they are most valuable when you reflect on what you learned and use them alongside other experience.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Work Experience, Internships and Placements

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which Gatsby Benchmark covers experiences of workplaces? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Benchmark 6. Benchmark 6 is experiences of workplaces; 2 is labour market information, 4 links curriculum to careers and 8 is personal guidance.

  2. Who are classic summer internships at large employers mainly aimed at? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Penultimate-year undergraduates. Summer internships usually target students in the year before their final year, so offers can be made before final year starts.

  3. How long is a typical placement year? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    About 9 to 12 months. A sandwich placement normally lasts most of a year, which is what distinguishes it from a summer internship.

  4. Why is work shadowing common in medicine and law courts? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Untrained people can't safely or legally do the work, but can observe it. Where tasks need qualifications or carry risk, observing is the safe way to learn about the job.

  5. An employer calls a role an "unpaid internship" but the intern must work set hours on real tasks. What is the likely legal position? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    The intern is probably a worker entitled to the minimum wage. Employment status depends on the reality of the arrangement, not the label; set work and hours usually make someone a worker.

  6. Leila can't find a placement by the summer of her second year. What is the most sensible course of action? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Check the university's deadline and plan to return to final year if needed. Many students don't secure a placement; knowing the switch-back deadline protects her degree while she keeps applying.

  7. Which description of work experience would most impress an admissions tutor or employer? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    What the student observed, a task they did and what it taught them. Reflection on specific observations and learning shows insight and motivation; lists and hours don't show what was gained.

  8. Which is the strongest fairness concern about internships as a route into graduate jobs? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Unpaid or London-based roles favour those who can afford them. When internships are unpaid or costly to take, students from poorer backgrounds are excluded from a key route into graduate work.

  9. Which is the strongest evaluation of the claim that "placement years improve graduate outcomes"? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Evidence is positive, but part may reflect who chooses placements. Studies find consistent positive associations that persist when controlling for prior attainment, but selection means the true causal effect is likely smaller than raw comparisons suggest.

Key takeaways

  • Work experience means doing supervised tasks and shadowing means observing; both are short tasters that test interest and give material for applications when you reflect on what you learned.
  • Internships are fixed periods of real work, often summer schemes for penultimate-year students that act as extended assessment for graduate roles; interns who are workers must be paid at least the minimum wage.
  • A placement year is usually a paid year of work between the second and final years, supported by the university; it gives substantial experience, often a return offer and clarity about career fit, at the cost of an extra year.
  • Insight days and spring weeks are short programmes for first-year students that introduce a firm and sector, build contacts and may fast-track students to summer internships; access programmes target under-represented groups.
  • Volunteering is unpaid work freely given for a cause; chosen well it builds skills, sector experience and evidence of motivation, but a "volunteer" with set hours and pay-like benefits may legally be a worker.
  • Part-time jobs build customer service, reliability, teamwork and commercial awareness that graduate recruiters value; describe them with specific achievements, and keep hours in balance with study.

Common mistakes in Work Experience, Internships and Placements

  • Treating a list of placements as the evidence. Admissions tutors and employers want to know what you observed, did and learned, not just where you went.
  • Waiting until summer to look for summer internships. Large employers often open applications the previous autumn and fill places on a rolling basis.
  • Assuming the higher final grades of placement students prove placements cause better grades. More motivated students may be more likely to take placements, so some of the difference is selection.
  • Treating an insight day as a passive tour. Many firms assess participants informally or formally, and a fast-track offer can depend on how you engage.
  • Treating volunteering as less valuable than paid work in applications. Recruiters assess the skills and achievements shown, not whether you were paid.
  • Leaving part-time work off a graduate CV or describing only duties ("served customers"). Show achievements and what you learned about the business.

  1. Work experience and work shadowing
    Short work experience and work shadowing let you see a job from the inside before committing to it.
  2. Internships
    An internship is a fixed period of work experience, usually during the summer, designed to give students or graduates real work and employers a chance to assess them.
  3. Placement years and sandwich degrees
    A placement year, or sandwich year, is a year of paid work taken as part of a degree.
  4. Insight days and spring weeks
    Insight days and spring weeks are short programmes for students early in their degrees, run mainly by large employers.
  5. Volunteering
    Volunteering means giving time unpaid to help a cause or organisation.
  6. Part-time work and its value
    Most students now have part-time jobs, often in retail, hospitality or care.
  7. Finding opportunities: advertised and speculative
    Some opportunities are advertised on job boards; many others are never advertised and are filled through contacts and direct approaches.
  8. Unpaid internships and the National Minimum Wage
    Whether an intern must be paid depends on their employment status, not the label on the role.
  9. Making the most of a placement
    Getting a placement or internship is only the start: what you learn and achieve there determines its value.
  10. Virtual and remote experience
    Since 2020, virtual work experience, remote internships and online job simulations have become a common part of early careers programmes.
  11. Reflecting on and recording experience
    Experience only becomes useful evidence when you can remember and explain it.
  12. Turning experience into evidence
    Recruiters assess evidence, not experience itself.

Work Experience, Internships and Placements: questions students ask

When do large employers typically recruit summer interns?

From the autumn of the penultimate year, often on a rolling basis, so early application matters.

Who are spring weeks usually aimed at?

First-year undergraduates (second-years on four-year courses), and sometimes school leavers.

What can a volunteer legally receive?

Genuine out-of-pocket expenses only, such as travel; payments or benefits that look like pay may make them a worker.

How many hours a week can a Student visa holder at degree level usually work in term time?

Up to 20 hours a week.
