Lessons in this school

  1. The future tense
    The simple future tense says what will happen.
  2. Irregular futures
    A small group of common verbs have irregular stems in the future.
  3. The future of probability
    Spanish can use the future tense to guess or wonder about the present.
  4. The conditional
    The conditional says what would happen.
  5. Polite requests and advice
    The conditional and other structures help you make polite requests and give advice.
  6. Si clauses: possible conditions
    Si clauses say what will happen if something occurs.
  7. Jobs and workplaces
    Talking about jobs is a key GCSE topic.
  8. Future plans and careers
    GCSE students often talk about their plans after school.
  9. Applying for a job: formal letters and emails
    Formal writing in Spanish follows set conventions.
  10. Part-time jobs and money
    Many young people have part-time jobs and earn pocket money.
  11. Relative pronouns: que, quien and donde
    Relative pronouns join ideas and make sentences longer and more complex.
  12. Lo que and lo with adjectives
    The neuter word lo helps express abstract ideas.
  13. Adverbs
    Adverbs describe how, when, where and how often something happens.

Key terms

Simple future
A tense formed with the infinitive plus endings, meaning "will"
Stem
The part of a verb to which endings are added
Future of probability
Using the future tense to guess about the present
Conditional
A tense expressing what would happen
Quisiera
A very polite form meaning "I would like"
Si clause
A clause introduced by "if" that sets a condition
El/la periodista
Journalist, same form for both genders
El bachillerato
Post-16 academic study, similar to A levels
Estimado/a
Dear, in formal letters
Un trabajo a tiempo parcial
A part-time job
Relative pronoun
A word that introduces a clause describing a noun
Lo que
What, which (referring to an idea)
Adverb
A word describing how, when, where or how often
