Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What is the yo preterite of trabajar?

trabajé. From “The regular preterite” →

In plain English: The preterite is the past tense for completed actions: what happened, once or a set number of times, at a definite point. Key idea: Regular preterite: -é, -aste, -ó, -amos, -asteis, -aron (-ar) and -í, -iste, -ió, -imos, -isteis, -ieron (-er, -ir); the accents distinguish habló from hablo.

What does fuimos mean?

We went, or we were (ir or ser). From “Irregular preterites: ser, ir, hacer and tener” →

In plain English: The commonest verbs are irregular in the preterite, and four of them, ser, ir, hacer and tener, appear in almost every account of the past. Key idea: Ser and ir share fui, fuiste, fue, fuimos, fuisteis, fueron; hacer is hice ... hizo, tener is tuve ... tuvo; irregular preterites have no written accents.

How do you say "There were lots of people" in the past?

Había mucha gente. From “The imperfect tense” →

In plain English: The imperfect describes the past as ongoing: what used to happen, what was going on, and what things were like. Key idea: The imperfect (-aba; -ía) describes habits, descriptions, background, age and time in the past; only ser (era), ir (iba) and ver (veía) are irregular.

What does al salir mean?

On leaving, when leaving. From “Narrating a story in the past” →

In plain English: Telling a story combines the preterite and imperfect with connectives that move the action forward. Key idea: Set the scene with the imperfect, narrate events with the preterite, and link them with connectives (de repente, luego, al final) and al + infinitive.

Which tense goes with ayer in all varieties?

The preterite. From “Preterite or perfect: Spain and Latin America” →

In plain English: English speakers often find the choice between comí and he comido confusing, and native speakers themselves differ by region. Key idea: The perfect covers periods including now (hoy, este año, ya); the preterite covers finished periods (ayer, el año pasado); Spain uses the perfect for today's events, most of Latin America the preterite.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Talking about the Past

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. What is the él preterite of escribir? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    escribió. -ir verbs take -ió in the third-person singular preterite.

  2. What is the yo preterite of tocar? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    toqué. The c becomes qu before é to keep the /k/ sound: toqué.

  3. Complete: Ayer ___ (yo) al cine. (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    fui. The yo preterite of ir is fui, with no accent.

  4. Which form means "she spoke"? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    habló. Habló (stress on the last syllable) is the third-person preterite; hablo is "I speak".

  5. What is the él preterite of creer? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    creyó. The unstressed i between vowels becomes y: creyó.

  6. In La fiesta fue genial, which verb is fue? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    ser: the party was great. There's no destination with a; fue + adjective evaluates the event, so it is ser.

  7. Which sentence about yesterday's tennis is correct? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Ayer mis amigos y yo jugamos al tenis y luego comimos pizza. Mis amigos y yo is nosotros, and ayer requires the preterite for both actions.

  8. Why is the yo preterite of empezar written empecé? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Spanish writes ce rather than ze, so z changes to c before e. Spanish spelling avoids ze; the sound is unchanged, so the verb is regular in speech.

  9. Which sentence about dinner and television uses the preterite correctly? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Mis padres hicieron la cena y yo vi la tele. Hicieron (c before e) and vi without an accent are the correct forms.

Key takeaways

  • Regular preterite: -é, -aste, -ó, -amos, -asteis, -aron (-ar) and -í, -iste, -ió, -imos, -isteis, -ieron (-er, -ir); the accents distinguish habló from hablo.
  • In the preterite, -car, -gar and -zar verbs change in the yo form only (busqué, llegué, empecé); vowel-stem -er and -ir verbs change i to y in the third persons (leyó, leyeron).
  • Ser and ir share fui, fuiste, fue, fuimos, fuisteis, fueron; hacer is hice ... hizo, tener is tuve ... tuvo; irregular preterites have no written accents.
  • Strong preterites (estuve, pude, puse, supe, quise, vine, dije, traje) take unaccented -e, -iste, -o, -imos, -isteis, -ieron, with -eron after j; in the preterite quise means "tried", pude "managed" and supe "found out".
  • -ir stem-changers change e → i and o → u in the preterite only in él and ellos forms (pidió, durmieron); -ar and -er stem-changers are regular (pensó, volvió).
  • The imperfect (-aba; -ía) describes habits, descriptions, background, age and time in the past; only ser (era), ir (iba) and ver (veía) are irregular.

Common mistakes in Talking about the Past

  • Dropping the accent: Hablo con mi madre ayer means nothing correct; the preterite is Hablé. Without its accent, habló becomes hablo, "I speak".
  • Applying the yo change to other forms: buscó and llegaste are regular. Only the yo form changes.
  • Writing hació or tenió: the forms are hizo and tuvo. And fué, dió and vió no longer carry accents.
  • Writing dijieron or trajieron: after j the ending is -eron: dijeron, trajeron.
  • Carrying the present diphthong into the preterite: forms such as piensó and vuelví are wrong; it is pensó and volví.
  • Forgetting the accent on -ía: comia and vivian are wrong. All -er and -ir imperfect forms carry the accent: comía, vivían. For -ar verbs only -ábamos has one.

Lessons in this school

  1. The regular preterite
    The preterite is the past tense for completed actions: what happened, once or a set number of times, at a definite point.
  2. Spelling changes in the preterite
    Some regular verbs change their spelling in the preterite, not because they are irregular but to keep the same sound.
  3. Irregular preterites: ser, ir, hacer and tener
    The commonest verbs are irregular in the preterite, and four of them, ser, ir, hacer and tener, appear in almost every account of the past.
  4. More irregular preterites: estar, poder, poner, querer, venir and decir
    A second group of common verbs has an irregular stem in the preterite with the same unstressed endings.
  5. Stem-changing -ir verbs in the preterite
    Verbs ending in -ir that change their stem in the present also change in the preterite, but only in the third persons and in a different way.
  6. The imperfect tense
    The imperfect describes the past as ongoing: what used to happen, what was going on, and what things were like.
  7. Describing childhood and the past
    Describing what life was like when you were younger, and contrasting it with now, is a high-scoring exam task.
  8. Preterite or imperfect?
    Choosing between the preterite and the imperfect is the central skill of talking about the past in Spanish.
  9. Narrating a story in the past
    Telling a story combines the preterite and imperfect with connectives that move the action forward.
  10. The past continuous
    The past continuous, estaba + gerund, stresses that an action was in progress at a moment in the past.
  11. The perfect tense
    The perfect tense, he hablado, links the past to the present: what you have done, in a period that includes now.
  12. Preterite or perfect: Spain and Latin America
    English speakers often find the choice between comí and he comido confusing, and native speakers themselves differ by region.
  13. The pluperfect
    The pluperfect, había hablado, describes what had happened before another past event.
  14. Acabar de and hace with time
    Two short structures handle time very precisely: acabar de + infinitive for something that has just happened, and hace for "ago" and for how long something has been going on.

What does no quise mean?

What is the él preterite of pensar?

pensó (no stem change).

What is the difference between conocí and conocía?

Conocí: I met (first time); conocía: I knew.

What does estuvimos bailando toda la noche mean?

We spent the whole night dancing (we danced all night).
