Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What are the present subjunctive endings for -ar verbs?

-e, -es, -e, -emos, -éis, -en. From “Forming the present subjunctive” →

In plain English: The present subjunctive is a set of verb forms used after certain triggers such as wishes, emotions and doubt. Key idea: Form the present subjunctive by taking the yo present, removing -o and adding -e endings to -ar verbs or -a endings to -er and -ir verbs, keeping irregular yo stems, stem changes and spelling changes such as busque, llegue and empiece.

How do you say "it worries me that"?

Me preocupa que (+ subjunctive). From “The subjunctive after emotion” →

In plain English: Expressions of feeling about someone else's actions or a situation take que plus the subjunctive. Key idea: Expressions of emotion about someone else or a situation, such as me alegro de que, me molesta que and me preocupa que, take que + subjunctive, while the same subject uses the infinitive.

How do you say "when I am older"?

Cuando sea mayor. From “The subjunctive after cuando and time conjunctions” →

In plain English: Cuando and other time words take the subjunctive when they refer to the future. Key idea: Cuando and other time conjunctions take the present subjunctive when they refer to the future, but the indicative for habits and past events, while antes de que always takes the subjunctive.

How do you form a negative tú command?

No + present subjunctive tú form, e.g. no hables. From “Negative commands” →

In plain English: Negative commands in Spanish are formed with no plus the present subjunctive for every person. Key idea: Every negative command is no + present subjunctive, irregular verbs keep their subjunctive stems, and object and reflexive pronouns move in front of the verb.

How do you say "If I were you, I would study more"?

Si yo fuera tú, estudiaría más. From “Si clauses: hypothetical conditions” →

In plain English: To talk about unlikely or imaginary situations, Spanish uses si plus the imperfect subjunctive with the conditional. Key idea: Hypothetical conditions use si + imperfect subjunctive and the conditional in the other clause, in either order, while likely conditions use si + present, and the conditional never directly follows si.

Quick quiz: test yourself on The Subjunctive

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which ending does an -ar verb take in the present subjunctive for "they"? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    -en. The -ar verbs swap to the opposite vowel e, so ellos form ends in -en, as in hablen.

  2. Which verb has the present subjunctive "sepa"? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Saber. Sepa is the irregular subjunctive of saber, one of the six DISHES verbs.

  3. Which sentence means "I want to go out"? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Quiero salir. The subject does not change, so the infinitive salir is used, not que + subjunctive.

  4. Which is the present subjunctive of "salir" for "nosotros"? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Salgamos. Salir has the yo form salgo, so the stem salg- plus -amos gives salgamos.

  5. Why does "dé" (from dar) carry a written accent? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    To distinguish it from de. The accent separates the verb form dé from the preposition de (of or from).

  6. Complete: "Mis profesores quieren que nosotros ___ los deberes." (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    hagamos. A change of subject after quieren que needs the subjunctive, here hagamos.

  7. Which is the correct present subjunctive of "dormir" for "nosotros"? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Durmamos. -ir stem-changers change o to u in nosotros, so the correct form is durmamos.

  8. Which sentence uses an irregular subjunctive correctly? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Espero que estés contento. Estar has the subjunctive estés with an accent, needed after espero que.

  9. How would you say "The doctor advises me to drink more water"? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    El médico me aconseja que beba más agua. Aconsejar que with a new subject needs the subjunctive beba.

Key takeaways

  • Form the present subjunctive by taking the yo present, removing -o and adding -e endings to -ar verbs or -a endings to -er and -ir verbs, keeping irregular yo stems, stem changes and spelling changes such as busque, llegue and empiece.
  • Dar, ir, saber, haber, estar and ser (DISHES) have irregular present subjunctives, dé, vaya, sepa, haya, esté and sea, which then take regular endings.
  • Verbs of wishing and influence take que + subjunctive when the subject changes, and the infinitive when it stays the same; decir que takes the subjunctive only when it is an order.
  • Expressions of emotion about someone else or a situation, such as me alegro de que, me molesta que and me preocupa que, take que + subjunctive, while the same subject uses the infinitive.
  • Belief and certainty (creo que, estoy seguro de que, no dudo que) take the indicative, while doubt and denial (no creo que, dudo que, niego que, no estoy seguro de que) take the subjunctive.
  • Impersonal expressions of necessity, possibility, judgement and emotion take que + subjunctive, expressions of certainty take the indicative unless negated, and the infinitive is used when no particular person is meant.

Common mistakes in The Subjunctive

  • Starting from the infinitive instead of the yo form, as in "que tena". Always start from yo: tengo gives tenga.
  • Writing "que voy" or "que soy" after a subjunctive trigger. Use the subjunctive forms: que vaya, que sea.
  • Translating "I want you to come" as "quiero tú venir". Spanish needs que + subjunctive: quiero que vengas.
  • Writing "me molesta que la gente tira basura". After an emotion trigger the verb must be subjunctive: tire.
  • Using the subjunctive after creo que, as in "creo que sea". Positive belief takes the indicative: creo que es.
  • Writing "es posible que va a llover". Possibility triggers the subjunctive: es posible que llueva.

Lessons in this school

  1. Forming the present subjunctive
    The present subjunctive is a set of verb forms used after certain triggers such as wishes, emotions and doubt.
  2. Irregular present subjunctives
    A handful of very common verbs do not follow the yo-form rule in the present subjunctive.
  3. The subjunctive after wishes and influence
    When one person wants, asks or advises someone else to do something, Spanish uses que plus the subjunctive.
  4. The subjunctive after emotion
    Expressions of feeling about someone else's actions or a situation take que plus the subjunctive.
  5. The subjunctive after doubt and denial
    Spanish uses the subjunctive when the speaker doubts or denies that something is true.
  6. Impersonal expressions and the subjunctive
    Phrases such as es importante que and es posible que take the subjunctive, but phrases of certainty such as es verdad que do not.
  7. The subjunctive after cuando and time conjunctions
    Cuando and other time words take the subjunctive when they refer to the future.
  8. The subjunctive after para que and other conjunctions
    Some conjunctions always take the subjunctive because they introduce purposes, conditions or events that have not happened.
  9. The subjunctive in relative clauses
    A relative clause describing something unknown, hypothetical or non-existent takes the subjunctive.
  10. Negative commands
    Negative commands in Spanish are formed with no plus the present subjunctive for every person.
  11. Ojalá, quizás and tal vez
    Ojalá expresses hopes and wishes and always takes the subjunctive, while quizás and tal vez can take either mood.
  12. The imperfect subjunctive
    The imperfect subjunctive is the past form of the subjunctive, used in si clauses, after past triggers and in polite requests.
  13. Si clauses: hypothetical conditions
    To talk about unlikely or imaginary situations, Spanish uses si plus the imperfect subjunctive with the conditional.
  14. Sequence of tenses
    The tense of the main verb decides whether a following subjunctive is present or imperfect.
  15. Como si and the pluperfect subjunctive
    Como si always takes a past subjunctive, and the pluperfect subjunctive expresses unreal past situations.

The Subjunctive: questions students ask

What does the memory aid DISHES stand for?

Dar, ir, saber, haber, estar, ser.

When does "querer" take the subjunctive?

When the person wanting is different from the person acting, with que.

How do you say "it is important to recycle" in general?

Es importante reciclar.

What does "con tal de que" mean?

Provided that (+ subjunctive).
