Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What is the nosotros ending for -ar verbs?

-amos: hablamos. From “Regular -ar verbs in the present” →

In plain English: Most Spanish verbs end in -ar, and nearly all of them follow one pattern in the present tense. Key idea: Regular -ar verbs take -o, -as, -a, -amos, -áis, -an on the stem; the ending alone shows the subject.

How do you say "I'm thirsty"?

Tengo sed. From “Tener and expressions with tener” →

In plain English: Tener (to have) is irregular and appears in dozens of everyday expressions where English uses "to be". Key idea: Tener (tengo, tienes, tiene, tenemos, tenéis, tienen) expresses age, hunger, thirst, cold, heat, sleepiness, fear, being right and being in a hurry; "very" in these phrases is mucho or mucha.

What is the tú form of pensar?

piensas. From “Stem-changing verbs: e to ie” →

In plain English: In many common verbs the stressed e of the stem becomes ie in four of the six present-tense forms. Key idea: In e to ie verbs, the stem e becomes ie when stressed: in every present form except nosotros and vosotros.

What is the difference between es aburrido and está aburrido?

Es aburrido: he is boring; está aburrido: he is bored. From “Ser or estar?” →

In plain English: Choosing between ser and estar is one of the first real challenges of Spanish, and some adjectives change meaning depending on which verb you use. Key idea: Ser describes what something is, including where events take place; estar describes where people and things are and the state they are in; listo, aburrido, malo and rico change meaning with the verb.

How do you say "She has blue eyes"?

Tiene los ojos azules. From “Describing people: appearance and personality” →

In plain English: Describing what people look like and what they are like combines ser, tener and llevar with agreeing adjectives. Key idea: Use ser for build and personality, tener for hair and eyes (with the article: el pelo, los ojos), and llevar for glasses and clothes; adjectives agree with the person.

Quick quiz: test yourself on The Present Tense, Family and Friends

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. What is the tú form of trabajar? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    trabajas. Stem trabaj- plus the tú ending -as gives trabajas.

  2. Complete: Yo ___ (leer) el periódico. (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    leo. The yo ending for every regular verb is -o: leo.

  3. Which English translation of Trabajo en una tienda is NOT possible? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    I worked in a shop. The present tense can mean "I work" or "I'm working" but never the past "I worked".

  4. Who is the subject of Compran fruta en el mercado? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    They or you (ustedes). The ending -an marks the third-person plural: they, or ustedes.

  5. What is the nosotros form of abrir? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    abrimos. -ir verbs take -imos in the nosotros form: abrimos.

  6. Translate "We have been living in Glasgow for ten years". (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Vivimos en Glasgow desde hace diez años. An action continuing into the present uses the present tense with desde hace.

  7. Which sentence about parents' jobs is correct? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Mis padres trabajan en un hospital. Mis padres is third-person plural, so the verb takes -an.

  8. Which sentence about grandparents is correct? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Mis abuelos viven en el campo y beben mucho té. Third-person plural -er and -ir verbs end in -en; -an would be the subjunctive.

  9. A friend texts Mañana como con mis abuelos. What does it mean? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Tomorrow I'm having lunch with my grandparents. With a future time expression, the present describes a fixed plan.

Key takeaways

  • Regular -ar verbs take -o, -as, -a, -amos, -áis, -an on the stem; the ending alone shows the subject.
  • -er and -ir verbs share -o, -es, -e, -en; they differ only in nosotros (-emos, -imos) and vosotros (-éis, -ís).
  • The Spanish present means "I do", "I am doing", "shall I...?", fixed plans with a time expression, and, with desde hace, "I have been doing".
  • Tener (tengo, tienes, tiene, tenemos, tenéis, tienen) expresses age, hunger, thirst, cold, heat, sleepiness, fear, being right and being in a hurry; "very" in these phrases is mucho or mucha.
  • Ir is voy, vas, va, vamos, vais, van; ir a + infinitive forms the near future, and the second verb never changes.
  • Hacer, poner, salir, traer, tener, venir, decir and oír have -go yo forms; -cer and -cir verbs take -zco; saber is "know a fact or how to", conocer "know a person or place".

Common mistakes in The Present Tense, Family and Friends

  • Using jugar for instruments: you tocas la guitarra (play an instrument) but juegas al tenis (play a sport or game).
  • Giving -er and -ir verbs -ar endings: Yo como is right, but él coma or ellos viván are wrong in the present. -er and -ir verbs use e in the endings.
  • Translating "I have been living here for a year" with a past tense: Spanish says Vivo aquí desde hace un año, in the present, because the action continues.
  • Saying Estoy caliente for "I'm hot": a person who feels hot tiene calor. Estar caliente refers to objects (La sopa está caliente) and, of people, has a sexual meaning.
  • Forgetting the a: Voy estudiar is wrong. The near future always needs a: Voy a estudiar.
  • Saying sabo or conoco: the yo forms are sé and conozco. And Sé a María is wrong: knowing a person is Conozco a María.

Lessons in this school

  1. Regular -ar verbs in the present
    Most Spanish verbs end in -ar, and nearly all of them follow one pattern in the present tense.
  2. Regular -er and -ir verbs in the present
    The second and third groups of Spanish verbs, ending in -er and -ir, share almost all their present-tense endings.
  3. Using the present tense
    The Spanish present tense covers more ground than the English simple present, translating "I do", "I am doing", "I have been doing" and sometimes "I will do".
  4. Tener and expressions with tener
    Tener (to have) is irregular and appears in dozens of everyday expressions where English uses "to be".
  5. Ir, ir a and the near future
    Ir (to go) is completely irregular and doubles as the easiest way to talk about the future.
  6. Irregular yo forms: hacer, poner, salir and others
    A group of very common verbs is regular in the present except for the yo form, which often ends in -go or -zco.
  7. Stem-changing verbs: e to ie
    In many common verbs the stressed e of the stem becomes ie in four of the six present-tense forms.
  8. Stem-changing verbs: o to ue and e to i
    Two more groups of stem-changing verbs follow the same boot pattern as e to ie: stressed o becomes ue, and in some -ir verbs stressed e becomes i.
  9. Estar: location and states
    Estar is the second verb for "to be", used for where things are and how people and things are at a particular moment.
  10. Ser or estar?
    Choosing between ser and estar is one of the first real challenges of Spanish, and some adjectives change meaning depending on which verb you use.
  11. Hay and estar compared
    "There is" and "there are" are both hay in Spanish, and choosing between hay and estar depends on whether you are introducing something new or locating something already known.
  12. Family and relationships
    Family is a core GCSE theme and the subject of countless conversations.
  13. Describing people: appearance and personality
    Describing what people look like and what they are like combines ser, tener and llevar with agreeing adjectives.
  14. Possessive adjectives
    Possessive adjectives say whose something is: my, your, his, our, their.
  15. Gustar and expressing likes
    Gustar doesn't work like English "to like": the thing liked is the subject and the person who likes it is the indirect object, as in "it pleases me".

The Present Tense, Family and Friends: questions students ask

What does escucho la radio mean?

I listen to the radio.

In which two forms do -er and -ir verbs differ in the present?

Nosotros (-emos against -imos) and vosotros (-éis against -ís).

How do you say "I have been learning Spanish for three years"?

Aprendo español desde hace tres años (or Hace tres años que aprendo español).

Which stem change does tener make in its stressed forms?

e changes to ie: tienes, tiene, tienen.
