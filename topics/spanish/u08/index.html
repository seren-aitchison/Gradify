Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

Which word expresses a deadline, por or para?

Para, as in los deberes son para el lunes. From “Por and para” →

In plain English: Por and para can both translate "for", but they are not interchangeable. Key idea: Para expresses purpose, recipient, destination, deadline, opinion and employer, while por expresses cause, route, exchange, means, duration, rate and the passive agent, alongside fixed phrases such as por favor and gracias por.

What does "volver a llamar" mean?

To call again. From “Verbs with prepositions” →

In plain English: Many Spanish verbs need a particular preposition before a noun or infinitive, and some English verbs with prepositions have none in Spanish. Key idea: Learn each verb with its preposition, such as pensar en, soñar con, dejar de and empezar a, and remember that buscar, esperar, escuchar, mirar, pagar and pedir take no preposition.

How do you say "I dropped the plate"?

Se me cayó el plato. From “Se for unplanned events” →

In plain English: Spanish has a special structure for accidents and things that happen unintentionally, such as se me olvidó. Key idea: Unplanned events use se + indirect object pronoun + third person verb agreeing with the thing, as in se me olvidó el libro and se nos perdieron las llaves.

How do you say "she became a doctor" (by study)?

Se hizo médica. From “Verbs of becoming” →

In plain English: Spanish has no single verb for "to become". Key idea: Use ponerse for sudden temporary changes, volverse for lasting changes of character, hacerse for changes by effort, llegar a ser for gradual achievement, convertirse en for transformation into a noun and quedarse for results.

What does "por lo tanto" mean?

Therefore. From “Connectives and discourse markers” →

In plain English: Connectives link ideas and help a text flow logically. Key idea: Use connectives for adding, contrasting, cause, result and examples, together with discourse markers for opening, balancing, opinion and concluding, to produce linked and developed answers.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Advanced Grammar and Accuracy

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which completes "Este pastel es ___ ti"? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    para. The cake is intended for you, a recipient, so para is required.

  2. Which sentence needs the personal a? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Veo ___ mi primo. Mi primo is a specific person receiving the action, so the personal a is required.

  3. Which preposition follows "soñar" (to dream of)? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    con. Spanish says soñar con, as in sueño con ser actor (I dream of being an actor).

  4. Complete: "Te cambio mi bocadillo ___ tu manzana." (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    por. An exchange of one thing for another is expressed with por.

  5. Complete: "Mañana voy a visitar ___ profesor." (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    al. A + el contracts to al before a specific person: visitar al profesor.

  6. Complete: "Mi padre dejó ___ fumar el año pasado." (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    de. Dejar de + infinitive means to stop doing something.

  7. Which sentence uses por and para correctly? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Salí para Londres y pasé por Oxford. Para marks the destination London, and por marks the route through Oxford.

  8. Which sentence correctly uses or omits the personal a? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Busco un médico que hable inglés. The doctor is non-specific (any doctor who speaks English), so no personal a is used.

  9. How do you say "My sister married a Mexican"? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Mi hermana se casó con un mexicano. Casarse takes con, so the correct form is se casó con un mexicano.

Key takeaways

  • Para expresses purpose, recipient, destination, deadline, opinion and employer, while por expresses cause, route, exchange, means, duration, rate and the passive agent, alongside fixed phrases such as por favor and gracias por.
  • Use a before a specific person or pet as direct object, contracting a + el to al, but not after tener, after hay, with things and places, or with non-specific people.
  • Learn each verb with its preposition, such as pensar en, soñar con, dejar de and empezar a, and remember that buscar, esperar, escuchar, mirar, pagar and pedir take no preposition.
  • The passive uses ser in any tense with a past participle agreeing with the subject and por for the agent, describing an action, whereas estar + participle describes a resulting state.
  • The passive se takes a third person verb agreeing with the noun, as in se venden pisos, while the impersonal se uses a singular verb for general statements, as in se vive bien and se puede.
  • Unplanned events use se + indirect object pronoun + third person verb agreeing with the thing, as in se me olvidó el libro and se nos perdieron las llaves.

Common mistakes in Advanced Grammar and Accuracy

  • Writing "gracias para el regalo". Thanks are given in exchange for something, so use por: gracias por el regalo.
  • Writing "conozco tu hermana". A specific person as direct object needs the personal a: conozco a tu hermana.
  • Writing "busco para mis llaves" or "espero para el autobús". Buscar and esperar already include "for": busco mis llaves, espero el autobús.
  • Forgetting agreement, as in "la casa fue construido". The participle must agree with the subject: la casa fue construida.
  • Writing "se vende pisos" on a sign. In the passive se the verb agrees with the plural noun: se venden pisos.
  • Making the verb agree with the person, as in "se me olvidé". The verb agrees with the thing: se me olvidó.

Lessons in this school

  1. Por and para
    Por and para can both translate "for", but they are not interchangeable.
  2. Personal a
    Spanish puts a small word, a, before a direct object that is a specific person or a pet.
  3. Verbs with prepositions
    Many Spanish verbs need a particular preposition before a noun or infinitive, and some English verbs with prepositions have none in Spanish.
  4. The passive with ser
    The true passive in Spanish uses ser plus a past participle that agrees with the subject.
  5. Passive and impersonal se
    Spanish often uses se instead of the ser passive or a vague subject such as "people" or "you".
  6. Se for unplanned events
    Spanish has a special structure for accidents and things that happen unintentionally, such as se me olvidó.
  7. Gerunds and infinitives
    English uses the -ing form in many places where Spanish uses the infinitive.
  8. Verbs of becoming
    Spanish has no single verb for "to become".
  9. The future perfect and conditional perfect
    The future perfect says what will have happened and the conditional perfect says what would have happened.
  10. Diminutives and augmentatives
    Spanish endings such as -ito and -ón change the size or tone of a word.
  11. Connectives and discourse markers
    Connectives link ideas and help a text flow logically.
  12. Register: formal and informal Spanish
    Register is the level of formality chosen to suit the reader and situation.
  13. Common errors for English speakers
    Many mistakes made by English-speaking learners come from translating word for word.
