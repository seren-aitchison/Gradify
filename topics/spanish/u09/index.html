How many autonomous communities does Spain have?

17, plus the two autonomous cities of Ceuta and Melilla. From “Spain's regions and languages” →

In plain English: Spain is divided into autonomous communities, several of which have their own co-official language alongside Spanish. Key idea: Spain has 17 autonomous communities and two autonomous cities; Castilian is official throughout, while Catalan (and Valencian), Galician, Basque and Aranese are co-official in their own regions.

What was the capital of the Aztecs?

Tenochtitlan, where Mexico City now stands. From “Mexico: history and culture” →

In plain English: Mexico has the largest Spanish-speaking population in the world and a rich mix of Indigenous and Spanish heritage. Key idea: Mexico grew from Maya and Aztec civilisations, Spanish conquest (1519 to 1521) and three centuries of New Spain to independence in 1821 and revolution in 1910, and its traditions include the Día de Muertos, mariachi and a cuisine recognised by UNESCO.

Which Spanish-speaking territory in the Caribbean belongs to the United States?

Puerto Rico. From “Central America and the Caribbean” →

In plain English: Six Central American countries and three Caribbean territories have Spanish as a main language. Key idea: Spanish is spoken in six Central American countries and in Cuba, the Dominican Republic and Puerto Rico, a region shaped by Maya civilisation, Columbus's arrival in 1492, the Panama Canal and the Cuban Revolution, and famous for music such as salsa, merengue and bachata.

How do you say "fake news"?

Noticias falsas. From “Technology and cyberspace” →

In plain English: Technology and social media are central GCSE topics. Key idea: Discussing technology needs key vocabulary such as redes sociales, descargar and contraseña, balanced opinions on benefits and risks such as ciberacoso and noticias falsas, and advice using negative commands and the subjunctive.

How do you say "the film is about" in natural Spanish?

La película trata de... From “Literature and film in Spanish” →

In plain English: Spanish-language writers and directors are celebrated around the world. Key idea: Key writers include Cervantes, Lorca, García Márquez, Allende and Esquivel, key directors include Almodóvar, del Toro and Cuarón, and reviews use phrases such as trata de, lo que más me gustó and la recomendaría.

Quick quiz: test yourself on The Spanish-Speaking World

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. In which region is gallego spoken? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Galicia. Gallego is the co-official language of Galicia in north-west Spain.

  2. Which festival involves a giant tomato fight? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    La Tomatina. La Tomatina takes place in Buñol on the last Wednesday of August.

  3. Who led the Nationalist side in the Spanish Civil War? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Francisco Franco. General Francisco Franco led the Nationalists and then ruled as dictator until 1975.

  4. When do the Reyes Magos traditionally bring presents in Spain? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    6 January. The Three Kings bring presents on 6 January, after the parade on 5 January.

  5. What is an "ofrenda" in the Día de Muertos? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    An altar with offerings. The ofrenda is an altar decorated with photos, flowers and food for the dead.

  6. Which conquistador captured the Inca emperor Atahualpa in 1532? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Francisco Pizarro. Pizarro captured Atahualpa at Cajamarca, leading to the fall of the Inca Empire.

  7. Which sentence about languages in Spain is accurate? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Euskera has no known link to Latin. Basque is a language isolate, unlike Catalan, Galician and Spanish, which come from Latin.

  8. Which sentence correctly describes a past visit to San Fermín? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Fui a Pamplona y vi el encierro por la mañana. The preterite fui and vi match a completed past visit, with consistent time phrases.

  9. Which sentence about the period is accurate? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    The 1981 coup attempt failed and democracy survived. The 23-F coup attempt of 1981 failed, and the democratic system established by the 1978 Constitution survived.

Key takeaways

  • Spain has 17 autonomous communities and two autonomous cities; Castilian is official throughout, while Catalan (and Valencian), Galician, Basque and Aranese are co-official in their own regions.
  • Spain celebrates religious festivals such as Semana Santa and the Reyes Magos as well as local fiestas such as Las Fallas, San Fermín and La Tomatina, which can be described using the passive se, past tenses and opinions.
  • The Civil War (1936 to 1939) ended with Franco's victory and a dictatorship lasting until 1975, after which the transition brought free elections in 1977 and the Constitution of 1978, while debate continues over how to remember victims.
  • Mexico grew from Maya and Aztec civilisations, Spanish conquest (1519 to 1521) and three centuries of New Spain to independence in 1821 and revolution in 1910, and its traditions include the Día de Muertos, mariachi and a cuisine recognised by UNESCO.
  • The Inca Empire, centred on Cuzco, fell to Pizarro after 1532, but Indigenous cultures remain strong in the Andes, where Quechua, Aymara and Guaraní are spoken by millions and have official status.
  • The Southern Cone of Argentina, Chile and Uruguay is known for landscapes such as Patagonia and the Atacama, for tango, gauchos, asado and mate, and for the military dictatorships of the 1970s and 1980s that are still remembered today.

Common mistakes in The Spanish-Speaking World

  • Thinking that everyone in Spain speaks only Spanish. Many people are bilingual in Spanish and a co-official language such as Catalan or Basque.
  • Saying children in Spain only get presents on 25 December. Many receive them from the Reyes Magos on 6 January.
  • Thinking Spain became a democracy immediately in 1939. The war ended in 1939, but democracy came only after Franco's death in 1975.
  • Confusing the Día de Muertos with Halloween. It is a separate tradition on 1 and 2 November that honours and remembers the dead.
  • Thinking Indigenous languages disappeared after the conquest. Quechua, Aymara and Guaraní are still spoken by millions and have official status.
  • Thinking the tango comes from Spain. It developed in Buenos Aires and Montevideo in the late nineteenth century.
