Lessons in this school

  1. Reflexive verbs
    Reflexive verbs describe actions people do to themselves, and Spanish uses many more of them than English.
  2. Daily routine
    Describing a typical day is a staple of GCSE speaking and writing.
  3. Frequency and time expressions
    Saying how often you do something adds detail to every answer and is rewarded in GCSE marking.
  4. School subjects and opinions
    School is a GCSE theme in its own right, and giving and justifying opinions about subjects is the easiest way to show a range of language.
  5. Obligation: tener que, hay que and deber
    Rules, duties and advice are expressed with three structures, each followed by an infinitive.
  6. Comparatives
    Comparing people, places and things is essential for giving opinions.
  7. Superlatives
    Superlatives single out the most or least of a group, and the -ísimo ending intensifies an adjective.
  8. Food and meals
    Food is a favourite exam topic because it combines vocabulary, opinions and culture.
  9. At the restaurant
    Ordering food and drink is a classic GCSE role play.
  10. Shopping for food: quantities and large numbers
    Buying food needs quantities, containers and prices, and prices need numbers above 100.
  11. Demonstratives
    Demonstratives point things out: this, that, these, those.
  12. My home and my room
    Describing where you live combines vocabulary for types of home, rooms and furniture with hay, tener and estar.
  13. Prepositions of place
    Prepositions of place say where things are in relation to each other, and most are two- or three-word phrases ending in de.
  14. Household chores and helping at home
    Talking about chores combines obligation, frequency and opinion, which is why it is a favourite exam topic.

Key terms

Verbo reflexivo
A verb whose action falls on the subject, used with me, te, se, nos, os, se
Jornada continua
A school or working day without a long lunch break, finishing in the early afternoon
Soler
To be in the habit of (+ infinitive): suelo cenar a las nueve
Asignatura
A school subject
Obligación
Obligation: tener que, hay que, deber
Comparativo
A comparative: más ... que, menos ... que, tan ... como
Superlativo relativo
The most or least of a group: el más alto de la clase
Merienda
An afternoon snack, often around 5 to 6 pm in Spain
Quisiera
I would like (polite, from querer)
Docena
A dozen: una docena de huevos
Demostrativo
A word pointing something out by distance: este, ese, aquel
Piso
A flat (Spain); also a floor or storey of a building
Encima de
On top of
Tareas domésticas
Household chores
