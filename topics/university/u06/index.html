Lessons in this school

  1. Why we reference
    Referencing is more than a rule to avoid penalties: it is how scholars give credit, show their evidence and join an academic conversation.
  2. What needs a reference
    Knowing when to reference is as important as knowing how.
  3. Referencing styles: an overview
    Universities use several referencing styles, and different departments may require different ones.
  4. Harvard referencing
    Harvard is the most widely used referencing style in UK universities.
  5. APA style
    APA style, from the American Psychological Association, is used in psychology, education, nursing and many social sciences.
  6. Numeric styles: Vancouver and IEEE
    Numeric styles are standard in medicine, health sciences, engineering and computing.
  7. Footnote styles: OSCOLA and Chicago
    Law, history and some humanities use footnote styles, which put source details at the bottom of the page.
  8. Secondary referencing
    Sometimes you read about one author's work in another author's book or article.
  9. Reference management software
    Reference managers store your sources and format citations automatically.
  10. Academic integrity and its values
    Academic integrity is the commitment to honest and responsible scholarship.
  11. Plagiarism and how to avoid it
    Plagiarism is the most common form of academic misconduct, and much of it is unintentional.
  12. Collusion and group work boundaries
    Working with others is encouraged at university, but collaboration has limits in individual assessments.
  13. Contract cheating and essay mills
    Contract cheating means getting someone else to complete assessed work for you.
  14. Text-matching software
    Most UK universities run submitted work through text-matching software such as Turnitin.
  15. Generative AI and academic integrity
    Generative AI tools such as chatbots raise new questions for academic integrity.
  16. Using generative AI responsibly in study
    Used well and within the rules, generative AI can support learning; used badly, it can replace the thinking that learning requires.
  17. Academic misconduct procedures
    If a university suspects academic misconduct, it follows a formal procedure.

Key terms

Citation
A short marker in the text indicating a source has been used
Common knowledge
Information widely known and found in many sources without dispute
Author-date style
A style that cites author and year in the text, such as Harvard or APA
Harvard
An author-date referencing style widely used in UK universities
APA
The referencing style of the American Psychological Association
Vancouver
A numeric referencing style used in medicine and health sciences
Footnote
A note at the bottom of a page giving source details
Secondary referencing
Citing a work you have read about in another source rather than read yourself
Reference manager
Software that stores references and formats citations
Academic integrity
The commitment to honest and responsible behaviour in academic work
Plagiarism
Presenting others' work or ideas as your own without full acknowledgement
Collusion
Unauthorised cooperation on work meant to be done individually
Contract cheating
Arranging for a third party to complete assessed work submitted as your own
Similarity report
A report highlighting passages in a submission that match other sources
