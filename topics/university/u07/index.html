Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check.

What is information literacy?

The ability to think critically and make balanced judgements about information we find and use. From “Information literacy” →

What is the difference between a catalogue and a discovery tool?

A catalogue lists items the library holds; a discovery tool searches the catalogue and many electronic resources at once. From “Library catalogues and discovery tools” →

Which organisation produces the UK census?

The Office for National Statistics, in England and Wales. From “Grey literature and official statistics” →

What is the difference between misinformation and disinformation?

Misinformation is shared without intent to deceive; disinformation is created deliberately to deceive. From “Recognising misinformation” →

When should a student collect data for a project involving people?

Only after receiving ethical approval. From “Research ethics for students” →

Lessons in this school

  1. Information literacy
    Information literacy is the ability to find, evaluate and use information well.
  2. Defining a research question
    A good research question gives focus to an assignment or project.
  3. Search strategies: keywords and Boolean operators
    Effective searching saves time and finds better sources.
  4. Library catalogues and discovery tools
    University libraries offer catalogues and discovery tools to find books, articles and other resources.
  5. Subject databases
    Subject databases index research in specific disciplines and are essential for finding scholarly sources.
  6. Google Scholar and the open web
    Google Scholar and the open web are useful starting points, but they have limits.
  7. Grey literature and official statistics
    Not all useful research is published in journals.
  8. Evaluating sources
    Judging the quality of sources is at the heart of academic work.
  9. Peer review and the publication process
    Peer review is how academic research is checked before publication.
  10. Primary and secondary sources
    Different disciplines use the terms primary and secondary sources in slightly different ways.
  11. Recognising misinformation
    Misinformation spreads quickly online and can shape beliefs and behaviour.
  12. Open access
    Open access makes research free to read online.
  13. Organising your research
    Good organisation saves time and prevents accidental plagiarism.
  14. Research ethics for students
    Any research involving people must be ethical.
  15. Designing a small project
    Many students complete a small research project or dissertation.
  16. Collecting data: surveys and interviews
    Surveys and interviews are the most common ways students collect data from people.
  17. Writing up a project
    Writing up a project communicates what you did and found.

Key terms

Information literacy
The ability to find, evaluate and use information critically and ethically
Research question
A specific question that a piece of research aims to answer
Boolean operators
The words AND, OR and NOT used to combine search terms
Discovery tool
A library search interface that searches many resources at once
Subject database
A searchable index of research in a particular discipline
Google Scholar
A search engine for scholarly literature
Grey literature
Research produced outside commercial academic publishing, such as reports and theses
CRAAP test
A checklist for evaluating sources: currency, relevance, authority, accuracy, purpose
Peer review
Evaluation of research by experts before publication
Primary source
Original first-hand evidence or material
Disinformation
False information deliberately created to deceive
Open access
Research that is free to read online
Reference manager
Software that stores references and generates citations and bibliographies
Informed consent
Agreement to take part based on understanding the research and the right to withdraw
