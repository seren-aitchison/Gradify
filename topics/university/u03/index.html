Lessons in this school

  1. How students use their time
    University gives students far more control over their time than school.
  2. Semester and weekly planning
    Good planning turns a busy semester into manageable steps.
  3. Prioritising tasks
    There is rarely time to do everything at once.
  4. Procrastination
    Almost every student procrastinates sometimes.
  5. Focus and distraction
    Deep concentration is increasingly rare and valuable.
  6. Balancing study, work and life
    Many students balance study with paid work, caring, commuting and wellbeing.
  7. Preparing for lectures
    A little preparation makes lectures much easier to follow.
  8. Active listening in lectures
    Lectures are most useful when you listen actively rather than just copying.
  9. Note-making methods: linear, Cornell and maps
    Notes are tools for understanding and revision, not transcripts.
  10. Handwriting or typing notes
    Should students take notes by hand or on a laptop?
  11. Using lecture recordings and slides
    Many universities record lectures and post slides online.
  12. Reviewing and consolidating notes
    Notes are only useful if you return to them.
  13. Seminars and discussion
    Seminars and tutorials are where students test and develop ideas through discussion.
  14. Practical classes and lab notebooks
    Practical classes build hands-on skills, and lab notebooks record what was actually done.

Key terms

Notional learning hours
The expected total time for learning associated with a module or credit
Semester plan
An overview of deadlines and commitments for a term
Urgent–important matrix
A tool sorting tasks by urgency and importance
Procrastination
Voluntary delay of an intended action despite expecting to be worse off
Task switching
Moving attention between tasks, with a cost in time and accuracy
Mitigating circumstances
University processes for considering events that affected a student's performance
Module handbook
A guide to a module's content, assessment and readings
Active listening
Thinking about and selecting information while listening
Note-making
Organising and transforming information into your own notes
Verbatim
Word for word
Lecture capture
Recording lectures for students to review
Forgetting curve
The rapid decline of memory over time without review
Seminar
A small-group teaching session based on discussion
Protocol
Written instructions for carrying out a procedure
