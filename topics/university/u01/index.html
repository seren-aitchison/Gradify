Lessons in this school

  1. What is different about university study
    University study differs from school or college less in difficulty than in who is responsible for learning.
  2. Independent learning
    Independent learning doesn't mean learning alone or without help.
  3. How degrees are structured: credits, levels and modules
    A UK degree is built from modules worth credits at defined levels.
  4. Learning outcomes and the QAA level descriptors
    Every module states learning outcomes: what you should be able to do by the end.
  5. Assessment at university: types, criteria and marks
    University assessment comes in many forms and is marked against published criteria.
  6. Academic regulations and the student handbook
    Every university has regulations that decide how you progress, what happens if you fail or fall ill, and how to appeal.
  7. Teaching formats: lectures, seminars, labs and tutorials
    Universities teach through several formats, each with a different purpose and a different job for you.
  8. Personal tutors and academic staff
    Most UK universities give each student a personal or academic tutor.
  9. Writing to academic staff
    Email is how most students contact lecturers, tutors and offices, and a clear, polite message gets a faster, more useful answer.
  10. The virtual learning environment and digital study
    The virtual learning environment (VLE) is where most modules live: materials, recordings, announcements and submission.
  11. The university library
    A university library is far more than a building with books: it is a gateway to databases, journals, e-books and specialist staff.
  12. Support services and reasonable adjustments
    Universities offer a wide range of support, from academic skills advice to disability, wellbeing, money and careers services.
  13. Self-assessment of study skills
    Knowing your strengths and gaps lets you spend effort where it matters.
  14. Setting goals for your degree
    Clear goals direct effort and help you persist.
  15. Motivation and self-efficacy
    Motivation isn't a fixed amount you either have or lack; it depends on what you believe about yourself and the task.
  16. Self-regulated learning
    Self-regulated learners plan, monitor and reflect on their own learning, adjusting as they go.
  17. Building study habits
    Much of what successful students do is habit, not willpower.
  18. Peer learning and study groups
    Learning with and from other students is one of the most effective and least used resources at university.
  19. Belonging and the transition to university
    Starting university is a major life transition, and doubts about fitting in are common and usually temporary.

Key terms

Contact hours
Timetabled teaching time with staff: lectures, seminars, labs and tutorials
Self-directed learning
Learning in which the learner takes the initiative in diagnosing needs, setting goals, choosing strategies and evaluating outcomes (Knowles)
FHEQ
The Framework for Higher Education Qualifications of degree-awarding bodies in England, Wales and Northern Ireland
Learning outcome
A statement of what a learner will be able to do at the end of a module or programme
Formative assessment
Assessment for learning that gives feedback and usually carries no marks
Extenuating circumstances
Serious, unforeseen circumstances outside a student's control that affected assessment, considered through a formal process
Seminar
A small-group class discussing prepared reading or problems
Personal tutor
A member of staff responsible for supporting a student's overall academic progress and development
Subject line
The email's title; in academic email it should give the module code and topic
Virtual learning environment (VLE)
The online platform that hosts a university's modules, materials and submissions
Discovery service
A library search tool covering books, e-books, journals and articles in one interface
Reasonable adjustment
A change to a provision, criterion or practice to remove a substantial disadvantage for a disabled person
Skills audit
A structured self-rating of skills against their importance, used to set development priorities
Implementation intention
A plan linking a specific situation to a goal-directed action: "When X, I will Y"
