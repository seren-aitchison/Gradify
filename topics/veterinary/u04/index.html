What are Fraser's three views of animal welfare?

Biological functioning, affective states and natural living. From “What animal welfare means” →

What are the four principles of the Welfare Quality protocols?

Good feeding, good housing, good health and appropriate behaviour. From “Assessing welfare in practice” →

Why should horses have companions?

What proportion of a rabbit's diet should be hay or grass?

Why are horses prone to gastric ulcers when forage is restricted?

Lessons in this school

  1. What animal welfare means
    Animal welfare is the state of an animal as it experiences it.
  2. The Five Freedoms and the Five Domains
    Two frameworks dominate practical welfare assessment.
  3. The Animal Welfare Act 2006
    The Animal Welfare Act 2006 is the main welfare law in England and Wales.
  4. Assessing welfare in practice
    Welfare must be assessed with evidence, not assumptions.
  5. Behaviour and its role in welfare
    Behaviour is both a window on an animal's welfare and part of its welfare.
  6. Pain recognition and scoring
    Animals cannot tell us they are in pain, so vets must recognise it from behaviour and physiology.
  7. Husbandry of dogs and cats
    Dogs and cats are the most common pets in the UK, and their welfare depends on owners understanding their needs.
  8. Husbandry of horses
    Horses evolved as grazing, herd-living animals on open plains.
  9. Dairy and beef cattle systems
    Cattle farming in the UK ranges from intensive dairy units to extensive upland suckler herds.
  10. Sheep and pig systems
    Sheep and pig farming present very different welfare challenges.
  11. Poultry systems
    Billions of chickens are farmed worldwide, so poultry welfare has enormous scale.
  12. Rabbits, small mammals and exotic pets
    Rabbits, guinea pigs and exotic pets are often kept in ways that do not meet their needs.
  13. Biosecurity on farm
    Biosecurity means keeping disease out of a farm and stopping it spreading within and beyond it.
  14. Principles of animal nutrition
    Every animal needs energy, protein, fats, carbohydrates, vitamins, minerals and water in the right balance.
  15. Feeding dogs and cats
    Dogs and cats have different nutritional needs.
  16. Feeding horses
    The horse's digestive system is built for a near-continuous supply of fibre.
  17. Ruminant nutrition and rationing
    Ruminants rely on rumen microbes to digest fibre.
  18. Body condition scoring
    Body condition scoring assesses an animal's fat reserves by sight and touch.
  19. Welfare at transport and slaughter
    Transport and slaughter are critical points for farm animal welfare.

Key terms

Animal welfare
The state of an individual animal as it experiences its life
Five Freedoms
A framework of five freedoms describing good animal welfare
Duty of care
A legal duty to meet an animal's welfare needs
Resource-based measure
A measure of what an animal is provided with
Stereotypy
A repetitive, unvarying behaviour with no obvious function
Pain scale
A structured tool for scoring pain
Socialisation
Exposing young animals to people, animals and experiences to reduce later fear
Trickle feeder
An animal that naturally eats small amounts almost continuously
Suckler herd
Beef cows that rear their own calves
Stratified system
A sheep industry in which hill, upland and lowland flocks are linked
Broiler
A chicken bred and raised for meat
Gut stasis
A dangerous slowing of gut movement in rabbits
Biosecurity
Measures to prevent disease entering and spreading on a farm
Metabolisable energy
Feed energy available to the animal after losses in faeces, urine and gases
