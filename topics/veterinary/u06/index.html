Lessons in this school

  1. Bacteria of veterinary importance
    Bacteria cause many of the most important diseases of domestic animals.
  2. Viruses of veterinary importance
    Viruses are obligate intracellular parasites that cause many notifiable and economically important animal diseases.
  3. Fungi and prions
    Fungi and prions are less common causes of disease than bacteria and viruses but include important zoonoses and fatal neurological diseases.
  4. Antimicrobial resistance
    Antimicrobial resistance threatens both animal and human health.
  5. Disinfection and sterilisation
    Killing or removing microorganisms from surfaces, instruments and skin prevents infection in practices and on farms.
  6. Nematodes of domestic animals
    Nematodes, or roundworms, are the most important parasites of grazing livestock and common in pets.
  7. Cestodes and trematodes
    Tapeworms and flukes are flatworms with complex life cycles that involve intermediate hosts.
  8. Ectoparasites: fleas, lice, mites and ticks
    Ectoparasites live on or in the skin and cause itching, skin disease and the spread of infections.
  9. Protozoa
    Protozoa are single-celled eukaryotic parasites that cause diarrhoea, abortion and blood disease in animals.
  10. Parasite control and anthelmintic resistance
    Parasite control keeps animals healthy and productive, but overuse of wormers has bred resistant worms.
  11. Innate immunity
    Innate immunity is the body's first, fast and non-specific defence against infection.
  12. Adaptive immunity
    Adaptive immunity is specific, slower to start and creates memory.
  13. Antibodies and the humoral response
    Antibodies are the effector molecules of humoral immunity.
  14. Hypersensitivity and autoimmunity
    The immune system can cause disease when it overreacts or attacks the body's own tissues.
  15. Maternally derived immunity and colostrum
    Newborn animals depend on antibodies from their mother for early protection.
  16. Principles of vaccination
    Vaccination is one of the most effective tools in veterinary medicine.
  17. Vaccination of companion animals
    Vaccination protects dogs, cats and rabbits against serious infectious diseases.
  18. Vaccination of farm animals and horses
    Vaccination is central to herd health planning and to equine competition rules.

Key terms

Endotoxin
Lipopolysaccharide in the outer membrane of Gram-negative bacteria that triggers fever and shock
Capsid
The protein coat that surrounds a viral genome
Dermatophyte
A fungus that infects keratin in skin, hair or claws, causing ringworm
Horizontal gene transfer
Movement of genes between bacteria by conjugation, transformation or transduction
Sterilisation
The destruction of all microorganisms, including bacterial spores
Hypobiosis
Arrested development of larvae inside the host
Definitive host
The host in which a parasite reaches sexual maturity
Pinnal-pedal reflex
A scratch reflex when the ear margin is rubbed, suggesting sarcoptic mange
Oocyst
A thick-walled stage of apicomplexan protozoa passed in faeces
Anthelmintic resistance
The heritable ability of worms to survive a normally effective treatment
PAMP
A pathogen-associated molecular pattern recognised by innate receptors
MHC
Major histocompatibility complex molecules that present peptides to T cells
Epitope
The part of an antigen bound by an antibody
Anaphylaxis
A severe, life-threatening type I reaction affecting the whole body
