Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check.

Roughly what proportion of an adult animal's body weight is water?

About 60%, two-thirds of it intracellular and one-third extracellular. From “Homeostasis and body fluids” →

Why may atropine be ineffective in some rabbits?

Many rabbits produce atropinesterase, which rapidly breaks atropine down. From “The autonomic nervous system” →

What is the main chemical stimulus to breathing in a healthy animal?

Carbon dioxide, acting through pH on the central chemoreceptors in the medulla. From “Respiration and gas exchange” →

What are caecotrophs?

Soft faecal pellets from caecal fermentation, rich in microbial protein and B vitamins, which rabbits eat directly from the anus. From “Monogastric and hindgut digestion” →

Which hormones are released from the posterior pituitary?

Antidiuretic hormone (vasopressin) and oxytocin, made in hypothalamic neurones. From “The hypothalamus and pituitary” →

Lessons in this school

  1. Homeostasis and body fluids
    Physiology is the study of how an animal keeps its internal environment stable while the world around it changes.
  2. Membrane potentials and nerve conduction
    Nerves and muscles signal with electrical changes across their membranes.
  3. The neuromuscular junction and muscle contraction
    Every voluntary movement depends on acetylcholine crossing the neuromuscular junction and calcium switching on actin and myosin.
  4. The autonomic nervous system
    The autonomic nervous system controls the heart, blood vessels, glands and gut without conscious effort.
  5. The cardiac cycle
    The heart is two pumps in series whose valves open and close as pressures change.
  6. Blood pressure and its control
    Blood pressure drives blood through the tissues and must be held within limits from moment to moment.
  7. Blood: cells, clotting and blood groups in animals
    Blood carries oxygen, fights infection and seals its own leaks, and its cells and blood groups differ between species.
  8. Respiration and gas exchange
    Breathing brings oxygen to the blood and removes carbon dioxide, and the haemoglobin–oxygen curve explains how oxygen is loaded in the lung and unloaded in the tissues.
  9. Avian respiration
    Birds breathe with rigid lungs ventilated by air sacs, a system more efficient than the mammalian lung.
  10. Rumen fermentation and volatile fatty acids
    A ruminant feeds its microbes, and its microbes feed it.
  11. Monogastric and hindgut digestion
    Dogs, cats and pigs digest mainly with their own enzymes, horses and rabbits rely on microbes in the hindgut, and the cat has metabolic needs that make it a true carnivore.
  12. Thermoregulation
    Mammals and birds hold their core temperature within narrow limits by balancing heat production and heat loss.
  13. Renal function and urine concentration
    The kidneys filter the plasma, reclaim what the body needs and excrete the rest, adjusting water and electrolyte balance minute by minute.
  14. Acid–base balance
    Enzymes work only within a narrow pH range, so the body buffers acid, breathes off carbon dioxide and excretes acid in urine.
  15. The hypothalamus and pituitary
    The hypothalamus links the nervous system to the endocrine system through the pituitary gland.
  16. Thyroid, adrenal and endocrine pancreas
    The thyroid sets metabolic rate, the adrenal glands manage stress, salt and water, and the endocrine pancreas controls blood glucose.
  17. Reproductive cycles in domestic species
    Each domestic species has its own breeding pattern, from the cow's year-round 21-day cycle to the bitch's single long cycle and the cat's induced ovulation.
  18. Pregnancy, parturition and lactation
    Pregnancy, birth and milk production are controlled by the placenta, the fetus and the mother's hormones, and they differ between species in ways that matter for every vet.

Key terms

Homeostasis
The maintenance of a stable internal environment by physiological control systems
Threshold
The membrane potential at which enough voltage-gated sodium channels open to trigger an action potential
Acetylcholinesterase
The enzyme in the synaptic cleft that hydrolyses acetylcholine, ending neuromuscular transmission
Muscarinic receptor
An acetylcholine receptor on parasympathetic target organs, blocked by atropine
Stroke volume
The volume of blood ejected by a ventricle in one beat
Mean arterial pressure
The average arterial pressure over a cardiac cycle, approximately diastolic plus one-third of the pulse pressure
Packed cell volume
The percentage of blood volume occupied by red cells, measured by centrifugation
Dead space
The part of each breath that does not take part in gas exchange (airways and unperfused alveoli)
Parabronchus
The tubular gas-exchange unit of the avian lung, surrounded by air and blood capillaries
Volatile fatty acids
Short-chain fatty acids (acetate, propionate, butyrate) produced by microbial fermentation and absorbed as the ruminant's main energy source
Obligate carnivore
An animal that must eat animal tissue to meet nutrient needs it cannot synthesise, such as the cat's need for taurine and preformed vitamin A
Thermoneutral zone
The range of environmental temperature over which an animal maintains core temperature without extra energy for heat production or loss
Glomerular filtration rate
The volume of plasma filtered by all the glomeruli per unit time
Compensation
The secondary change in the other system (respiratory or renal) that moves pH back towards normal
