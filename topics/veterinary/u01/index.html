Five sample flashcards

What do palmar and plantar describe?

The back surface of the forelimb below the carpus (palmar) and of the hindlimb below the tarsus (plantar). From “Anatomical terms of position and direction” →

Which spinal nerves form the brachial plexus?

C6 to T2. From “The thoracic limb of the dog and cat” →

What is a Hansen type I disc extrusion?

Rupture of a degenerate, calcified nucleus pulposus through the annulus into the vertebral canal, typical of chondrodystrophic breeds. From “The vertebral column and spinal cord” →

What is a tracheal bronchus?

A bronchus to the right cranial lung lobe that arises directly from the trachea, found in cattle and pigs. From “The thorax, heart and lungs” →

Where do uroliths commonly lodge in a male dog?

Just caudal to the os penis, where the urethra runs in a bony groove. From “The urinary system” →

Lessons in this school

  1. Anatomical terms of position and direction
    Veterinary anatomy uses a standard language, set out in the Nomina Anatomica Veterinaria, so that a radiograph report, a surgical note or a referral letter means the same thing to every reader.
  2. The skeleton and joints compared
    The skeletons of domestic mammals share one plan, modified for how each species moves and feeds.
  3. Muscles, tendons and fascia
    Skeletal muscles move the skeleton through tendons, while ligaments, fascia, bursae and tendon sheaths shape and protect that movement.
  4. The thoracic limb of the dog and cat
    The carnivore forelimb carries about 60% of the body's weight and is attached to the trunk by muscle.
  5. The equine distal limb and hoof
    The horse stands on a single digit, and most equine lameness arises below the carpus and tarsus.
  6. The pelvic limb and the equine stay apparatus
    The pelvic limb is the engine of propulsion, joined to the spine at the sacroiliac joint.
  7. The bovine foot and claw
    Cattle walk on two claws per foot, and lameness from claw lesions is one of the biggest welfare and economic problems in dairy herds.
  8. The vertebral column and spinal cord
    The vertebral column supports the body and protects the spinal cord, and it is the site of some of the commonest neurological problems in dogs.
  9. The skull, teeth and dental formulae
    The skull houses the brain and sense organs and carries the teeth, whose number, shape and growth reflect each species' diet.
  10. The brain and cranial nerves
    The brain's regions and the twelve pairs of cranial nerves are tested every time a vet examines a head.
  11. The thorax, heart and lungs
    The thorax holds the heart and lungs within the ribs and diaphragm.
  12. The carnivore digestive tract
    Dogs and cats have a simple stomach and a short intestine suited to a diet rich in protein and fat.
  13. The equine digestive tract
    The horse is a hindgut fermenter: a small stomach and a long small intestine feed a huge caecum and colon where microbes ferment fibre.
  14. The ruminant stomach
    Ruminants digest grass with the help of microbes in a four-chambered stomach.
  15. The urinary system
    The kidneys, ureters, bladder and urethra are built on one plan, but kidney shape and the male urethra differ markedly between species.
  16. Male and female reproductive anatomy
    Reproductive anatomy varies more between domestic species than almost any other system, and the differences matter for breeding, neutering and obstetrics.
  17. Avian anatomy
    Birds are built for flight and egg-laying: a light, fused skeleton, a gut without teeth, a cloaca and, in the hen, a single functional ovary.
  18. Skin, hoof, horn and the mammary gland
    The skin and its derivatives, hair, glands, claws, horn and the mammary gland, protect the body, regulate temperature and in mammals feed the young.

Key terms

Palmar
The caudal surface of the forelimb distal to the carpus (plantar is the equivalent surface of the hindlimb distal to the tarsus)
Synsarcosis
The attachment of the forelimb to the trunk by muscles alone, without a bony joint, in species lacking a functional clavicle
Aponeurosis
A flat, sheet-like tendon spreading a muscle's force over a wide attachment
Brachial plexus
The network of spinal nerves C6–T2 that supplies the thoracic limb
Suspensory apparatus
The suspensory ligament, proximal sesamoid bones and distal sesamoidean ligaments, which support the fetlock
Reciprocal apparatus
The tendinous linkage of the equine stifle and hock (fibularis tertius and superficial digital flexor) that makes them move together
Digital cushion
Fat and connective tissue pads in the heel beneath the pedal bone that absorb concussion
Nucleus pulposus
The gelatinous centre of the intervertebral disc
Hypsodont
A tooth with a long reserve crown that erupts continuously as it wears (equine cheek teeth)
Menace response
A learned blink to a threatening gesture, testing CN II, the forebrain, the cerebellum and CN VII
Mediastinum
The partition between the two pleural sacs, containing the heart, oesophagus, trachea, great vessels and thymus
Greater omentum
The double fold of peritoneum hanging from the stomach's greater curvature, enclosing the omental bursa
Margo plicatus
The line in the equine stomach separating the non-glandular squamous mucosa from the glandular mucosa
Reticular groove
A fold in the reticulum wall that closes into a channel, carrying milk from the oesophagus to the omasum in suckling ruminants
