Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What is subclinical mastitis?

Udder infection with no visible signs but a raised somatic cell count and reduced yield. From “Mastitis” →

In plain English: Mastitis, inflammation of the udder, is one of the most costly diseases of dairy cattle. Key idea: Mastitis can be clinical or subclinical and contagious or environmental; SCC above about 200,000 cells/mL suggests infection, and bulk SCC above 400,000 cells/mL fails sale standards; control combines milking hygiene, housing, records and selective dry cow therapy.

Why are calves born with very few antibodies?

Because the bovine placenta does not let antibodies pass from cow to calf. From “Calf health and the colostrum” →

In plain English: Calves are born without protective antibodies and rely on colostrum. Key idea: Calves depend on colostrum for passive immunity, absorbed best in the first hours; good management follows quality, quantity, quickly and clean; scour is caused by viruses, Cryptosporidium and E. coli and treated with oral rehydration, with colostrum and hygiene key to prevention.

What is a persistently infected BVD calf?

A calf infected before birth that sheds BVD virus for life without developing antibodies. From “Bovine viral diarrhoea and Johne's disease” →

In plain English: Bovine viral diarrhoea and Johne's disease are endemic diseases that cause major losses in cattle. Key idea: BVD is spread mainly by persistently infected animals, found by ear notch testing and removed, with vaccination and biosecurity enabling eradication; Johne's disease, caused by MAP, infects calves early and shows signs in adults, controlled through testing and hygiene.

How much colostrum does a newborn lamb need in the first 24 hours?

About 200 mL per kg of body weight. From “Lambing and neonatal lamb losses” →

In plain English: Lambing is the most important time of the sheep year, and lamb losses are a major welfare and economic issue. Key idea: Lamb losses are mainly from starvation, hypothermia, dystocia and infection; lambs need 50 mL/kg colostrum soon after birth and 200 mL/kg in 24 hours; twin lamb disease is prevented by nutrition matched to scanning results.

Why must catering waste never be fed to pigs?

It is illegal and can spread diseases such as African swine fever and foot-and-mouth disease. From “Pig health and production” →

In plain English: Pig health depends on biosecurity, housing and careful medicine use. Key idea: Pigs suffer endemic diseases such as PRRS and swine dysentery and face exotic threats such as African swine fever; strict biosecurity, including never feeding catering waste, is vital; enrichment reduces tail biting, and antibiotic use has fallen through stewardship.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Farm Animal Practice and Herd Health

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which pathogen is a classic contagious cause of mastitis? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Staphylococcus aureus. Contagious pathogens spread between cows during milking.

  2. Where does most lameness in dairy cattle arise? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    In the foot, usually the outer hind claw. Most bovine lameness is caused by foot lesions.

  3. Which mineral is low in milk fever? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Calcium. Milk fever is periparturient hypocalcaemia.

  4. What does an individual cow SCC of 450,000 cells/mL suggest? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Likely intramammary infection. Counts above about 200,000 cells/mL suggest infection.

  5. Why is a block glued to the healthy claw when treating a sole ulcer? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    It takes weight off the claw with the lesion to allow healing. Offloading the injured claw reduces pain and aids healing.

  6. Why are metabolic diseases common around calving? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Milk production rises rapidly while appetite is reduced. Demand outstrips supply in early lactation.

  7. Records show most new infections occur in early lactation with environmental pathogens. What should be the main focus? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Improving hygiene of calving and early-lactation housing. Environmental infections around calving point to housing hygiene.

  8. Which housing change most reduces claw horn lesions? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Comfortable cubicles that encourage cows to lie down longer. Long standing times on hard floors increase claw lesions.

  9. A cow on lush spring grass has tremors and convulsions. What is the priority? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Treat urgently for hypomagnesaemia with magnesium and calcium. Grass staggers is an emergency and can cause sudden death without treatment.

Key takeaways

  • Mastitis can be clinical or subclinical and contagious or environmental; SCC above about 200,000 cells/mL suggests infection, and bulk SCC above 400,000 cells/mL fails sale standards; control combines milking hygiene, housing, records and selective dry cow therapy.
  • Most cattle lameness arises in the foot, from sole ulcers, white line disease, digital dermatitis and foul; mobility scoring from 0 to 3 detects cases early; treatment uses trimming, blocks and NSAIDs, and prevention addresses comfort, trimming, tracks and footbaths.
  • Around calving, high demands for calcium and energy cause milk fever, ketosis and displaced abomasum, while low magnesium causes grass staggers; prevention focuses on body condition, transition diets such as low DCAB, intake and magnesium supplementation.
  • Calves depend on colostrum for passive immunity, absorbed best in the first hours; good management follows quality, quantity, quickly and clean; scour is caused by viruses, Cryptosporidium and E. coli and treated with oral rehydration, with colostrum and hygiene key to prevention.
  • Bovine respiratory disease is multifactorial, involving viruses such as BRSV and bacteria such as Mannheimia, with ventilation, mixing and immunity as key risks; treatment uses antibiotics and NSAIDs, and prevention relies on colostrum, housing and vaccination.
  • Bovine TB, caused by Mycobacterium bovis, is notifiable and zoonotic; the SICCT skin test, read at 72 hours, identifies reactors for removal; control includes testing, movement restrictions and biosecurity, with badger culling and vaccination debated, aiming for TB freedom in England by 2038.

Common mistakes in Farm Animal Practice and Herd Health

  • Treating every cow with antibiotics at drying off. Selective dry cow therapy with teat sealants reduces antibiotic use without harming udder health when well managed.
  • Waiting until a cow is severely lame before treating. Early detection and prompt treatment improve recovery.
  • Giving intravenous calcium quickly without monitoring the heart. Rapid infusion can cause dangerous arrhythmias.
  • Stopping milk feeding in scouring calves. Calves need continued milk for energy alongside electrolytes.
  • Treating pneumonia only with antibiotics and ignoring housing. Without fixing ventilation and management, outbreaks recur.
  • Believing badgers are the only source of TB in cattle. Cattle-to-cattle spread is also important, which is why testing and movement controls matter.

Farm Animal Practice and Herd Health: questions students ask

What does an AHDB mobility score of 3 mean?

Severely impaired mobility.

What is the treatment for milk fever?

Slow intravenous calcium borogluconate, with monitoring of the heart.

Why is ventilation important in calf housing?

It removes moisture and pathogens from the air, reducing respiratory disease.

How is pregnancy rate calculated?

Submission rate multiplied by conception rate.
