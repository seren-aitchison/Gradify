Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check.

What is subclinical mastitis?

Udder infection with no visible signs but a raised somatic cell count and reduced yield. From “Mastitis” →

Why are calves born with very few antibodies?

Because the bovine placenta does not let antibodies pass from cow to calf. From “Calf health and the colostrum” →

What is a persistently infected BVD calf?

A calf infected before birth that sheds BVD virus for life without developing antibodies. From “Bovine viral diarrhoea and Johne's disease” →

How much colostrum does a newborn lamb need in the first 24 hours?

About 200 mL per kg of body weight. From “Lambing and neonatal lamb losses” →

Why must catering waste never be fed to pigs?

It is illegal and can spread diseases such as African swine fever and foot-and-mouth disease. From “Pig health and production” →

Lessons in this school

  1. Mastitis
    Mastitis, inflammation of the udder, is one of the most costly diseases of dairy cattle.
  2. Lameness in cattle
    Lameness is a major welfare and productivity problem in dairy cattle.
  3. Metabolic disease of dairy cows
    The transition period around calving puts great metabolic stress on dairy cows.
  4. Calf health and the colostrum
    Calves are born without protective antibodies and rely on colostrum.
  5. Bovine respiratory disease
    Bovine respiratory disease, or calf pneumonia, is a leading cause of illness and death in young cattle.
  6. Bovine tuberculosis
    Bovine tuberculosis is one of the most serious and costly animal diseases in Great Britain.
  7. Bovine viral diarrhoea and Johne's disease
    Bovine viral diarrhoea and Johne's disease are endemic diseases that cause major losses in cattle.
  8. Fertility and herd health planning
    Good fertility and proactive herd health planning keep dairy and beef herds productive and healthy.
  9. Sheep health planning
    Flock health planning helps sheep farmers prevent disease and improve productivity.
  10. Lambing and neonatal lamb losses
    Lambing is the most important time of the sheep year, and lamb losses are a major welfare and economic issue.
  11. Sheep scab, fly strike and lameness
    Sheep scab, blowfly strike and lameness are major welfare problems in UK sheep.
  12. Goat medicine
    Goats are not simply small sheep: they have distinct diseases, behaviour and drug responses.
  13. Pig health and production
    Pig health depends on biosecurity, housing and careful medicine use.
  14. Poultry health and avian influenza
    Poultry health is shaped by housing, biosecurity and the threat of avian influenza.
  15. Notifiable diseases of livestock
    Some animal diseases are so serious that suspicion must be reported to the government.

Key terms

Somatic cell count
The number of cells, mainly white blood cells, per millilitre of milk
Mobility score
A 0 to 3 score of how well a cow walks
Transition period
The weeks around calving when cows face major metabolic change
Passive transfer
Transfer of antibodies from colostrum to the newborn
Bovine respiratory disease
A multifactorial respiratory disease complex of cattle
SICCT test
The single intradermal comparative cervical tuberculin skin test for bovine TB
Persistently infected
An animal infected before birth that sheds BVD virus for life without antibodies
Submission rate
The percentage of eligible cows served in a 21-day period
Body condition score
A 1 to 5 assessment of fat cover in sheep
Dystocia
Difficult birth
Sheep scab
A contagious skin disease caused by the mite Psoroptes ovis
Caprine arthritis encephalitis
A viral disease of goats spread mainly through colostrum and milk
African swine fever
A highly lethal, notifiable viral disease of pigs with no widely used vaccine
Avian influenza
A viral disease of birds, with highly pathogenic strains that are notifiable
