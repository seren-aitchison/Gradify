Lessons in this school

  1. Vomiting and diarrhoea in dogs and cats
    Vomiting and diarrhoea are among the commonest reasons pets are brought to a vet.
  2. Cardiac disease in dogs and cats
    Heart disease is common in small animals and differs between dogs and cats.
  3. Respiratory disease in dogs and cats
    Respiratory disease ranges from mild infections to life-threatening airway obstruction.
  4. Chronic kidney disease
    Chronic kidney disease is common in older cats and dogs.
  5. Diabetes mellitus
    Diabetes mellitus is a common endocrine disease of dogs and cats.
  6. Hyperthyroidism and hypothyroidism
    Thyroid disease is common in small animals: hyperthyroidism mostly affects older cats and hypothyroidism mostly affects dogs.
  7. Hyperadrenocorticism and hypoadrenocorticism
    The adrenal glands can produce too much or too little cortisol.
  8. Skin and ear disease
    Skin and ear problems are among the commonest reasons dogs see a vet.
  9. Infectious diseases of dogs and cats
    Infectious diseases remain important in small animal practice despite vaccination.
  10. Seizures and neurological disease
    Seizures are a common neurological emergency in dogs and cats.
  11. Common poisonings
    Pets often eat things that are toxic to them.
  12. Oncology in practice
    Cancer is a leading cause of death in older dogs and cats.
  13. Neutering
    Neutering is one of the commonest operations in small animal practice.
  14. Gastric dilatation and volvulus
    Gastric dilatation and volvulus is a life-threatening emergency in large, deep-chested dogs.
  15. Intestinal foreign bodies
    Dogs and cats often swallow objects that can block the gut.
  16. Fracture management principles
    Fractures are common in dogs and cats after road traffic accidents and falls.
  17. Cruciate ligament disease
    Cranial cruciate ligament disease is the commonest cause of hind limb lameness in dogs.
  18. Hip and elbow dysplasia
    Hip and elbow dysplasia are inherited developmental diseases that cause lameness and arthritis in many dog breeds.
  19. Dental disease
    Dental disease is the most common health problem in adult dogs and cats.
  20. Rabbit medicine
    Rabbits are the third most popular mammalian pet in the UK and have very different needs from dogs and cats.
  21. Reptiles and birds in practice
    Reptiles and birds are increasingly common pets, and most of their illnesses come from poor husbandry.

Key terms

Regurgitation
Passive return of food from the oesophagus without abdominal effort
HCM
Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, thickening of the ventricular walls
Stertor
Low-pitched snoring noise from the nose or pharynx
SDMA
Symmetric dimethylarginine, a blood marker of kidney function
Fructosamine
A blood protein marker that reflects average glucose over the previous one to two weeks
Total T4
The main thyroid hormone measured in blood
PDH
Pituitary-dependent hyperadrenocorticism, caused by a pituitary tumour secreting ACTH
Pruritus
Itch, which causes scratching, licking or chewing
Barrier nursing
Nursing infectious patients with protective clothing and separate equipment
Status epilepticus
A seizure lasting over five minutes or repeated seizures without recovery
Theobromine
The toxic compound in chocolate, most concentrated in dark chocolate
Fine needle aspirate
Collecting cells from a lump with a needle for cytology
Ovariohysterectomy
Removal of the ovaries and uterus
Volvulus
Twisting of an organ on its axis
