Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What is a kenning?

A compressed metaphorical compound, such as "whale-road" for the sea. From “Old English literature” →

In plain English: Old English literature, written between about 650 and 1100, is the earliest literature in English. Key idea: Old English poetry uses alliterative, unrhymed lines with a caesura, kennings and variation; it survives in four main manuscripts, and its themes join heroic values with Christian faith and a strong sense of loss.

Who performed the mystery plays?

Town craft guilds. From “Medieval romance and drama” →

In plain English: Romance and drama were among the most popular medieval forms. Key idea: Romance celebrates and tests chivalric ideals; Sir Gawain uses alliterative verse to show a near-perfect knight's small failing; mystery plays staged Bible history through guilds, and morality plays allegorised the soul's journey.

What is a metaphysical conceit?

An extended, surprising comparison between very different things, developed through argument. From “Metaphysical poetry” →

In plain English: The metaphysical poets used wit, argument and startling comparisons to write about love and faith. Key idea: Metaphysical poetry uses daring conceits, argumentative structure and dramatic voices to explore love and faith; Johnson criticised its strained wit, while twentieth-century critics revived it.

In what year was the King James Bible published?

1611. From “The English Bible and Renaissance prose” →

In plain English: English prose was transformed in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries by Bible translation, the Prayer Book and new forms such as the essay. Key idea: Tyndale's translations and the King James Bible shaped English prose and idiom; Bacon introduced the essay; and writers such as Browne, Hobbes and Bunyan developed contrasting prose styles.

How did Fielding describe Joseph Andrews?

As a "comic epic poem in prose". From “The eighteenth-century novel” →

In plain English: The eighteenth century saw rapid experiment with the novel. Key idea: Richardson's epistolary novels offered inwardness and moral intensity, Fielding answered with comic epic and narratorial irony, and the picaresque, Sterne's experiment and Burney's letters show the form's variety.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Medieval to Eighteenth-Century Literature

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. What links the two halves of an Old English verse line? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Alliteration on stressed syllables. Old English verse uses alliteration rather than rhyme.

  2. Which verse form does Chaucer use for most of The Canterbury Tales? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Rhyming couplets of iambic pentameter. Chaucer's pentameter couplets helped establish the form in English.

  3. Which work did William Caxton print in 1485? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Le Morte d'Arthur. Caxton printed Malory's Arthurian prose cycle.

  4. What does the kenning "whale-road" refer to? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    The sea. The compound describes the sea as the road of whales.

  5. Why does Chaucer's narrator praise pilgrims who seem corrupt? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    To create irony, letting readers see faults the narrator misses. The naïve persona invites readers to judge for themselves.

  6. In Everyman, which character finally accompanies Everyman to his grave? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Good Deeds. Only Good Deeds stays with him, teaching that virtuous acts matter at death.

  7. How does The Dream of the Rood combine heroic and Christian values? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    It presents Christ as a young warrior who eagerly mounts the cross. The poem uses the language of heroic loyalty to describe Christ and the cross.

  8. A critic argues the Wife of Bath both challenges and confirms anti-feminist ideas. What supports this? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    She attacks clerical authorities but also embodies stereotypes they describe. Her lively challenge is voiced through traits the anti-feminist tradition mocked.

  9. Why does Gawain's acceptance of the green girdle matter? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    It shows a small failure of honesty rooted in fear of death. He keeps the girdle to save his life, breaking his word to his host.

Key takeaways

  • Old English poetry uses alliterative, unrhymed lines with a caesura, kennings and variation; it survives in four main manuscripts, and its themes join heroic values with Christian faith and a strong sense of loss.
  • Chaucer wrote in London Middle English, using iambic pentameter couplets; The Canterbury Tales frames varied genres within a pilgrimage, and its naïve narrator and vivid pilgrims create layers of irony.
  • Romance celebrates and tests chivalric ideals; Sir Gawain uses alliterative verse to show a near-perfect knight's small failing; mystery plays staged Bible history through guilds, and morality plays allegorised the soul's journey.
  • The English Renaissance combined humanist learning, printing and the Reformation, producing a flowering of poetry, drama and prose shaped by classical and Italian models and by Protestant culture.
  • Wyatt personalised Petrarch, Sidney combined a sonnet sequence with a defence of poetry's moral power, and Spenser created an allegorical epic in his distinctive stanza.
  • Metaphysical poetry uses daring conceits, argumentative structure and dramatic voices to explore love and faith; Johnson criticised its strained wit, while twentieth-century critics revived it.

Common mistakes in Medieval to Eighteenth-Century Literature

  • Treating Beowulf as purely pagan. It was composed and copied in a Christian culture, and its narrator interprets events in Christian terms.
  • Taking the narrator's praise of pilgrims at face value. Chaucer's narrator often admires characters whom the details expose.
  • Assuming romance means a love story. Medieval romance is a broader genre of chivalric adventure, in which love is one element.
  • Treating the Renaissance as a sudden break with the Middle Ages. Medieval forms and beliefs continued alongside the new learning.
  • Reading Renaissance sonnets as straightforward autobiography. Poets worked with conventions and personas, even when drawing on their own lives.
  • Calling any striking image a conceit. A metaphysical conceit is an extended, often intellectual comparison between strikingly unlike things, developed through argument.

Lessons in this school

  1. Old English literature
    Old English literature, written between about 650 and 1100, is the earliest literature in English.
  2. Chaucer and Middle English
    Geoffrey Chaucer (c. 1343–1400) helped establish English as a literary language.
  3. Medieval romance and drama
    Romance and drama were among the most popular medieval forms.
  4. The English Renaissance
    The English Renaissance, roughly 1500 to 1660, brought new learning, printing and religious change that transformed literature.
  5. Renaissance poetry: Wyatt, Sidney and Spenser
    Sixteenth-century poets adapted European forms to create a new English poetry.
  6. Metaphysical poetry
    The metaphysical poets used wit, argument and startling comparisons to write about love and faith.
  7. Milton and Paradise Lost
    John Milton's Paradise Lost (1667) is the great English epic.
  8. The English Bible and Renaissance prose
    English prose was transformed in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries by Bible translation, the Prayer Book and new forms such as the essay.
  9. Restoration literature
    The restoration of Charles II in 1660 reopened the theatres and changed literary culture.
  10. Augustan satire: Dryden, Pope and Swift
    Writers of the late seventeenth and early eighteenth centuries used satire to attack folly and vice.
  11. The eighteenth-century novel
    The eighteenth century saw rapid experiment with the novel.
  12. Sensibility and the Gothic beginnings
    In the mid-eighteenth century, writers turned from reason towards feeling, melancholy and the sublime.
  13. Women writers of the long eighteenth century
    Women wrote across every genre between the Restoration and the 1790s, though many were later forgotten.
