Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

Name three key ideas of Romanticism.

Three of: imagination, nature, emotion and the individual, the sublime, the child and common person, rebellion. From “Romanticism: an overview” →

In plain English: Romanticism was a movement in literature, art and thought from the late eighteenth to the mid-nineteenth century. Key idea: Romanticism valued imagination, nature, emotion, the individual and the sublime, and reacted against revolution, repression and industrialisation; Blake's "London" fuses social protest with visionary imagination.

What is a Byronic hero?

A brooding, proud, rebellious hero haunted by guilt. From “The second-generation Romantics” →

In plain English: Byron, Shelley and Keats, the younger Romantics, wrote in the decades after Waterloo. Key idea: Byron created the rebellious Byronic hero and wrote witty satire, Shelley wrote radical political poetry such as "Ozymandias", and Keats's odes explore beauty, mortality and negative capability.

Who wrote "Goblin Market"?

Christina Rossetti. From “Victorian poetry” →

In plain English: Victorian poets experimented with forms and voices while confronting faith, love and society. Key idea: Victorian poets include Tennyson's elegiac In Memoriam, Browning's dramatic monologues that expose speakers, Barrett Browning's love sonnets and Aurora Leigh, Rossetti's "Goblin Market" and Hopkins's sprung rhythm.

What is Imagism?

A modernist movement favouring precise images, economy and free verse. From “Modernism” →

In plain English: Modernism transformed literature in the early twentieth century through experiment with form and language. Key idea: Modernism broke with tradition through fragmentation, allusion, free verse and new perspectives; Imagism sought precise images, Eliot's The Waste Land fragmented voices, and Yeats's "The Second Coming" voiced fears of collapse.

What is doublethink?

Holding two contradictory beliefs at the same time, from Nineteen Eighty-Four. From “Post-war literature” →

In plain English: After 1945, British literature responded to the Second World War, the Cold War, the welfare state, the end of empire and social change. Key idea: Post-war literature includes Orwell's dystopia of language and power, Beckett's absurdist drama, the "angry young men", the plain-spoken Movement poets, and the new voices of the Windrush generation.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Romantic to Contemporary Literature

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which idea is central to Romanticism? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    The power of imagination. Applying the standard result: Romantics prized creative imagination.

  2. Which poem did Coleridge contribute to Lyrical Ballads? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    "The Rime of the Ancient Mariner". Coleridge's long ballad opened the 1798 edition.

  3. Who wrote "Ozymandias"? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Percy Bysshe Shelley. Applying the standard result: Shelley published it in 1818.

  4. What does Blake suggest with "mind-forg'd manacles"? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    People are imprisoned by ways of thinking imposed by institutions. The image turns physical chains into mental oppression.

  5. What kind of language did Wordsworth say he aimed to use? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    A selection of language really used by men. Applying the standard result: He rejected artificial "poetic diction".

  6. What is the effect of the inscription in "Ozymandias"? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    It becomes ironic, as the tyrant's works have vanished. Applying the standard result: The boast is undercut by the ruins.

  7. Why did many Romantics respond to the Industrial Revolution with images of nature? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    They saw nature as a source of renewal threatened by industry and cities. Nature offered a counterpoint to industrial change.

  8. What is the central concern of "Tintern Abbey"? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    How memories of nature sustain the mind and change across a life. The poem explores mind, nature and memory.

  9. Which line best shows Keats's concern with art outlasting human life? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    "Beauty is truth, truth beauty" in "Ode on a Grecian Urn". The urn's art endures beyond its viewers.

Key takeaways

  • Romanticism valued imagination, nature, emotion, the individual and the sublime, and reacted against revolution, repression and industrialisation; Blake's "London" fuses social protest with visionary imagination.
  • Lyrical Ballads joined Wordsworth's poems of ordinary life in plain language with Coleridge's supernatural poems; Wordsworth's Preface defined poetry as powerful feeling recollected in tranquillity, and Coleridge sought a willing suspension of disbelief.
  • Byron created the rebellious Byronic hero and wrote witty satire, Shelley wrote radical political poetry such as "Ozymandias", and Keats's odes explore beauty, mortality and negative capability.
  • Romantic-period women such as Wollstonecraft, Mary Shelley, Barbauld, Smith, Hemans and Dorothy Wordsworth wrote on education, politics, war and nature; widely read in their time, many were later neglected and are now being recovered.
  • The Victorian age combined industrialisation, political reform, empire and scientific discovery; a mass reading public grew through serialisation and libraries, and writers addressed the "Condition of England" and religious doubt.
  • Victorian poets include Tennyson's elegiac In Memoriam, Browning's dramatic monologues that expose speakers, Barrett Browning's love sonnets and Aurora Leigh, Rossetti's "Goblin Market" and Hopkins's sprung rhythm.

Common mistakes in Romantic to Contemporary Literature

  • Equating "Romantic" with love poetry. The movement is about imagination, nature and the self, and is often political.
  • Assuming Wordsworth simply describes landscapes. His nature poetry is about the mind's relationship with nature and the work of memory.
  • Treating the second-generation Romantics as identical. Byron's satire, Shelley's radicalism and Keats's sensuous lyricism differ greatly.
  • Assuming Romanticism was only the "big six" male poets. Women were among the best-selling and most influential writers of the period.
  • Seeing Victorian literature as uniformly moralistic and confident. Much of it expresses doubt, anxiety and social criticism.
  • Treating the speaker of a dramatic monologue as the poet. Browning creates characters who reveal themselves, often ironically.

Lessons in this school

  1. Romanticism: an overview
    Romanticism was a movement in literature, art and thought from the late eighteenth to the mid-nineteenth century.
  2. Wordsworth, Coleridge and Lyrical Ballads
    Lyrical Ballads (1798), by William Wordsworth and Samuel Taylor Coleridge, is often seen as the start of English Romantic poetry.
  3. The second-generation Romantics
    Byron, Shelley and Keats, the younger Romantics, wrote in the decades after Waterloo.
  4. Women writers of the Romantic period
    Women were central to Romantic-era literature, as poets, novelists and thinkers, though many were later neglected.
  5. The Victorian age
    The Victorian age, from 1837 to 1901, was a time of rapid industrial, scientific and social change.
  6. Victorian poetry
    Victorian poets experimented with forms and voices while confronting faith, love and society.
  7. The Victorian novel
    The novel became the dominant literary form in Victorian Britain.
  8. Late Victorian literature and the fin de siècle
    The last decades of the nineteenth century brought pessimism, aestheticism and new anxieties about gender, science and empire.
  9. Modernism
    Modernism transformed literature in the early twentieth century through experiment with form and language.
  10. Literature of the First World War
    The First World War produced some of the most powerful writing in English.
  11. Literature between the wars
    The years between 1918 and 1939 saw experiment, social criticism and political engagement.
  12. Post-war literature
    After 1945, British literature responded to the Second World War, the Cold War, the welfare state, the end of empire and social change.
  13. Postcolonial literature
    Postcolonial literature responds to colonialism and its aftermath.
  14. Contemporary literature
    Contemporary literature, from the late twentieth century to today, is diverse in voices, forms and themes.

Romantic to Contemporary Literature: questions students ask

Which political event strongly influenced early Romantic writers?

The French Revolution of 1789.

How did Wordsworth define poetry in the Preface to Lyrical Ballads?

As "the spontaneous overflow of powerful feelings", taking its origin from "emotion recollected in tranquillity".

What is negative capability?

Keats's idea of remaining in uncertainty and doubt without irritably reaching for fact and reason.

What did Mary Wollstonecraft argue in A Vindication of the Rights of Woman?

That women deserved rational education and should not be confined to ornamental roles.
