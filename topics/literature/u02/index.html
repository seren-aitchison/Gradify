Lessons in this school

  1. What makes a poem?
    Poetry is language organised by the line as well as the sentence, using sound, rhythm and compression to make meaning.
  2. The speaker and the poetic voice
    Every poem has a speaker, the voice that says the words, who isn't necessarily the poet.
  3. Imagery in poetry
    Imagery is language that appeals to the senses.
  4. Rhyme and sound patterning
    Poets organise sound as carefully as meaning.
  5. Stress and the metrical foot
    English metre is built from patterns of stressed and unstressed syllables.
  6. Iambic pentameter
    Iambic pentameter, five iambs per line, has been the dominant metre of English poetry since Chaucer.
  7. Other metres and metrical variation
    English poetry uses many metres besides iambic pentameter.
  8. Free verse
    Free verse has no regular metre or rhyme scheme, but it isn't formless: it builds rhythm through lines, repetition and syntax.
  9. The sonnet
    The sonnet is a fourteen-line poem with a strict rhyme scheme and a turn in thought.
  10. The ballad
    The ballad is a narrative poem or song, often in quatrains, rooted in oral tradition.
  11. The ode
    The ode is a lyric poem of praise or meditation in an elevated style, usually addressed to someone or something.
  12. The elegy
    An elegy is a poem of mourning.
  13. The dramatic monologue
    A dramatic monologue is a poem spoken by a character to a silent listener at a dramatic moment, revealing more than the speaker intends.
  14. The lyric
    The lyric is a short poem expressing a single speaker's thoughts or feelings.
  15. Blank verse
    Blank verse is unrhymed iambic pentameter, the metre of Shakespeare's plays, Milton's Paradise Lost and Wordsworth's Prelude.
  16. Narrative and epic poetry
    Narrative poems tell stories; epics tell them on a heroic scale.
  17. Stanza forms
    A stanza is a group of lines forming a unit in a poem.
  18. Enjambment, caesura and the line
    The relationship between the line and the sentence is one of poetry's main resources.
  19. Analysing a poem: a method
    Close reading a poem means looking carefully at how its language, form and structure create meaning.
  20. Comparing poems
    Comparing poems sharpens understanding of each by showing what they share and how they differ.

Key terms

Verse
Language arranged in lines, often with a regular rhythm
Speaker
The voice that speaks in a poem, which may differ from the poet
Imagery
Language that appeals to the senses
Half rhyme
Rhyme in which sounds are similar but not identical, also called slant rhyme
Scansion
Marking the stressed and unstressed syllables of a line and dividing it into feet
Iambic pentameter
A line of five iambic feet, usually ten syllables
Common measure
Alternating lines of iambic tetrameter and trimeter, the usual hymn metre
Free verse
Poetry without a regular metre or rhyme scheme
Volta
The turn in thought or argument in a sonnet
Ballad
A narrative poem or song, usually in quatrains, from or imitating oral tradition
Ode
An elevated lyric poem addressed to a person, object or idea
Elegy
A poem of mourning or reflection on death and loss
Dramatic monologue
A poem spoken by a character, not the poet, to a silent listener at a dramatic moment
Lyric
A short poem expressing the thoughts or feelings of a single speaker
