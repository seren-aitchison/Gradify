Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What does the term fabula mean?

The chronological sequence of events underlying a narrative. From “Story, plot and narrative” →

In plain English: Every novel tells a sequence of events, but it can arrange and present them in many ways. Key idea: Story is what happens in chronological order; plot is how events are arranged and connected; narrative discourse is how they are told; the gap between them is a key source of meaning and suspense.

What is indirect characterisation?

Revealing character through speech, actions, thoughts and others' reactions. From “Characterisation” →

In plain English: Novelists create characters through description, action, speech and thought. Key idea: Characters are built directly by narratorial statement and indirectly through speech, action and thought; they may be flat or round, static or dynamic, and each method shapes how readers judge them.

Give one function of dialogue in a novel.

Characterisation, revealing relationships, advancing plot or slowing pace. From “Dialogue in fiction” →

In plain English: Dialogue brings characters to life, advances plot and reveals relationships. Key idea: Dialogue characterises, reveals relationships, advances plot and controls pace; idiolect and dialect individualise voices, and subtext lets meaning lie beneath the words.

What does Bildungsroman mean?

A novel of formation or education. From “The Bildungsroman” →

In plain English: The Bildungsroman, or novel of formation, follows a young person's growth to maturity. Key idea: The Bildungsroman follows a protagonist's growth to maturity through education and conflict with society; Goethe set the model, and Dickens, Brontë, Eliot and Joyce adapted it, often exposing limits of class and gender.

What is an epiphany in Joyce's stories?

A sudden moment of insight or revelation. From “The short story” →

In plain English: The short story is a compact form that achieves its effects through compression, suggestion and a single focus. Key idea: Short stories achieve unity through compression and suggestion; Poe stressed a single effect, Joyce the epiphany, and Chekhov and Mansfield the moment of mood and awareness.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Prose Fiction and the Novel

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which term describes the order in which a text presents its events? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Syuzhet. Syuzhet is the formalist term for the presented arrangement of events.

  2. Which type of narrator can describe the thoughts of every character? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    A third-person omniscient narrator. An omniscient narrator has access to all characters' minds.

  3. Which example is free indirect discourse? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    She would never forgive him. How could he?. Third person and past tense with the character's own emotional idiom, without a tag, is free indirect.

  4. In Forster's distinction, what turns a story into a plot? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Causal connections between events. Plot adds "why" to the sequence of "and then".

  5. In Great Expectations, the adult Pip narrates his childhood. What is the effect? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Gentle irony as the older narrator looks back on his younger self. The gap between the narrating and experiencing self allows ironic reflection.

  6. Why does "seemed" matter in the opening sentence of Emma? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    It hints that Emma's happy self-image may not be the whole truth. The word introduces ironic distance from Emma's view of herself.

  7. A novel opens with a murder and then moves back to show the events leading to it. What effect does this arrangement most likely create? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Suspense about how and why, rather than what, happened. Revealing the outcome first shifts interest to causes and motives.

  8. A narrator praises his employer while revealing details that suggest the employer's actions were shameful. How should a reader respond? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Read against the narrator, recognising he may be unreliable. Gaps between a narrator's claims and the evidence signal unreliability.

  9. A reader quotes a snobbish judgement from a free indirect passage as proof of Austen's own snobbery. What is the weakness? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    The judgement may be the character's, presented ironically. Free indirect discourse can voice a character's views while inviting the reader to judge them.

Key takeaways

  • Story is what happens in chronological order; plot is how events are arranged and connected; narrative discourse is how they are told; the gap between them is a key source of meaning and suspense.
  • Narrative perspective, first-person, omniscient or limited, controls what the reader knows and feels; unreliable narrators invite the reader to read between the lines.
  • Free indirect discourse merges narrator and character, keeping third-person past tense while adopting a character's idiom; it creates both intimacy and irony.
  • Characters are built directly by narratorial statement and indirectly through speech, action and thought; they may be flat or round, static or dynamic, and each method shapes how readers judge them.
  • Setting establishes time and place, creates atmosphere, carries symbolic meaning and can act as a force shaping characters' lives.
  • Novels reorder time through analepsis and prolepsis, vary pace through scene, summary and ellipsis, and use frame narratives to layer and filter stories.

Common mistakes in Prose Fiction and the Novel

  • Retelling the story in an essay instead of analysing the plot. Marks come from explaining how the arrangement of events creates effects, not from summary.
  • Treating a first-person narrator's judgements as the author's. The narrator is a character, and authors often create distance between their views and their narrators'.
  • Attributing every judgement in free indirect passages to the narrator or author. The thoughts may be the character's, and the irony lies in the gap.
  • Writing about characters as if they were real people. Analyse how the author constructs them and why.
  • Describing the setting without explaining its effect. Analysis should link details of place and atmosphere to theme, mood or character.
  • Calling every reference to the past a flashback. An analepsis is a shift in the narrative to present an earlier event, not a character's brief memory.

Lessons in this school

  1. Story, plot and narrative
    Every novel tells a sequence of events, but it can arrange and present them in many ways.
  2. Narrators and point of view
    Who tells a story, and from where, shapes everything the reader learns.
  3. Free indirect discourse
    Free indirect discourse blends a narrator's voice with a character's thoughts.
  4. Characterisation
    Novelists create characters through description, action, speech and thought.
  5. Setting and atmosphere
    Setting is where and when a story takes place, but in good fiction it also shapes mood, theme and character.
  6. Time and structure in narrative
    Novels manipulate time by reordering events, speeding up and slowing down, and framing stories within stories.
  7. Dialogue in fiction
    Dialogue brings characters to life, advances plot and reveals relationships.
  8. The rise of the novel
    The novel became a dominant literary form in eighteenth-century Britain.
  9. The realist novel
    The realist novel aimed to represent ordinary life faithfully, especially in nineteenth-century Britain and France.
  10. The Gothic novel
    The Gothic novel explores fear, the supernatural and transgression in haunted settings.
  11. The Bildungsroman
    The Bildungsroman, or novel of formation, follows a young person's growth to maturity.
  12. The modernist novel
    Modernist novelists of the early twentieth century broke with realist conventions to capture consciousness, time and fragmentation.
  13. The postmodern novel
    Postmodern novels question the conventions of fiction and the grand narratives of history and truth.
  14. The short story
    The short story is a compact form that achieves its effects through compression, suggestion and a single focus.
  15. The novella
    The novella sits between the short story and the novel.
  16. Genre fiction
    Genre fiction, such as detective, science fiction, romance and horror, follows recognisable conventions that readers expect.
  17. Reading a novel: a method
    Studying a novel well means reading actively, noting key features and building an argument.

What does the fog symbolise in Bleak House?

The obscurity and delay of the legal system.

What is prolepsis?

A narrative anticipation of a later event, a flash-forward.
