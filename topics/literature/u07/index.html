How does literary theory differ from literary criticism?

Criticism analyses particular texts; theory examines the assumptions and methods behind such analysis. From “What is literary theory?” →

What is defamiliarisation?

What is the uncanny?

The eerie feeling when something familiar becomes strange, often linked to repressed material returning. From “Psychoanalytic criticism” →

What is gynocriticism?

Lessons in this school

  1. What is literary theory?
    Literary theory asks what literature is, how meaning is made and what reading is for.
  2. Liberal humanism and practical criticism
    Before "theory", most English criticism rested on liberal humanist assumptions.
  3. New Criticism
    New Criticism dominated American criticism from the 1940s to the 1960s.
  4. Formalism and structuralism
    Russian Formalists and later structuralists asked what makes literature literary and what structures underlie texts.
  5. Poststructuralism and deconstruction
    Poststructuralism questioned whether meaning can ever be fixed.
  6. Reader-response theory
    Reader-response theories argue that meaning arises in the act of reading.
  7. Psychoanalytic criticism
    Psychoanalytic criticism reads literature in the light of the unconscious.
  8. Narratology
    Narratology is the systematic study of how stories are told.
  9. Marxist criticism
    Marxist criticism reads literature in relation to class, economics and ideology.
  10. Feminist criticism
    Feminist criticism examines how literature represents women, how gender shapes writing and reading, and how women writers have been treated.
  11. Gender and queer theory
    Gender and queer theory question fixed ideas of gender and sexuality.
  12. Postcolonial criticism
    Postcolonial criticism examines literature in relation to empire, colonialism and their aftermath.
  13. New historicism and cultural materialism
    New historicism and cultural materialism read literary texts alongside other texts of their time to explore power and ideology.
  14. Ecocriticism
    Ecocriticism studies the relationship between literature and the physical environment.
  15. Critical views and debate
    Advanced literary study requires using critics' views, comparing interpretations and building an argument.
  16. Literary value and evaluation
    What makes a work of literature good, and who decides?

Key terms

Literary theory
Reflection on the assumptions and methods of reading literature
Liberal humanism
The view that great literature expresses timeless, universal human values
Intentional fallacy
The error of judging a work by its author's intentions
Defamiliarisation
Making the familiar strange so it is perceived afresh
Poststructuralism
Theories holding that meaning is unstable and never fully fixed
Horizon of expectations
The assumptions readers bring to a text from their culture and period
The unconscious
Mental processes outside awareness that influence thought and behaviour
Focalisation
The perspective through which events are presented
Ideology
Ideas and values that make social arrangements seem natural
Gynocriticism
The study of women's writing as a tradition
Performativity
The idea that gender is produced through repeated acts
Orientalism
Western representations constructing the East as exotic and inferior
New historicism
Reading literary texts alongside other texts of their time to explore power
Ecocriticism
The study of literature and the physical environment
