Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

How does literary theory differ from literary criticism?

Criticism analyses particular texts; theory examines the assumptions and methods behind such analysis. From “What is literary theory?” →

In plain English: Literary theory asks what literature is, how meaning is made and what reading is for. Key idea: Literary theory examines the assumptions behind criticism about authors, texts, readers and contexts; since every reading rests on such assumptions, theory helps readers recognise, compare and test them.

What is defamiliarisation?

Making familiar things strange so they are perceived freshly. From “Formalism and structuralism” →

In plain English: Russian Formalists and later structuralists asked what makes literature literary and what structures underlie texts. Key idea: Formalists studied literariness and defamiliarisation and distinguished story from its presentation; structuralists, building on Saussure, analysed texts as systems of signs and binary oppositions, as in Propp's functions of the folktale.

What is the uncanny?

The eerie feeling when something familiar becomes strange, often linked to repressed material returning. From “Psychoanalytic criticism” →

In plain English: Psychoanalytic criticism reads literature in the light of the unconscious. Key idea: Psychoanalytic criticism uses ideas of the unconscious, repression, dream-work, the Oedipus complex and the uncanny to read hidden desires and fears in characters, authors and texts, but risks reductiveness and rests on contested theories.

What is gynocriticism?

The study of women's writing as a literary tradition in its own right. From “Feminist criticism” →

In plain English: Feminist criticism examines how literature represents women, how gender shapes writing and reading, and how women writers have been treated. Key idea: Feminist criticism examines representations of women, recovers women's writing and analyses how gender, together with race and class, shapes literature, from Wollstonecraft and Woolf to Gilbert and Gubar and intersectional critics.

What does cultural materialism stress more than new historicism?

Dissidence and resistance, and how texts are used in later culture. From “New historicism and cultural materialism” →

In plain English: New historicism and cultural materialism read literary texts alongside other texts of their time to explore power and ideology. Key idea: New historicism reads literary and non-literary texts together to explore how power circulates and often contains subversion, while cultural materialism, more explicitly political, stresses dissidence and how texts are used in later culture.

Key takeaways

  • Literary theory examines the assumptions behind criticism about authors, texts, readers and contexts; since every reading rests on such assumptions, theory helps readers recognise, compare and test them.
  • Liberal humanism treats great literature as timeless and morally improving, and practical criticism teaches close reading of the words on the page; the method remains valuable, but critics question its universal claims and narrow canon.
  • New Criticism treats the text as an autonomous, unified object, rejects judging it by author's intention or reader's emotion, and reads closely for irony, paradox, ambiguity and tension resolved in organic unity.
  • Formalists studied literariness and defamiliarisation and distinguished story from its presentation; structuralists, building on Saussure, analysed texts as systems of signs and binary oppositions, as in Propp's functions of the folktale.
  • Poststructuralism argues that meaning is unstable and always deferred; Barthes shifted authority from author to reader, and Derrida's deconstruction shows how a text's hierarchies depend on what they exclude.
  • Reader-response theory locates meaning in the interaction between reader and text; Iser stresses gaps the reader fills, Jauss the shifting horizons of expectation, and Fish the interpretive communities that shape shared readings.

Common mistakes in Literary Theory and Criticism

  • Treating theory as a set of fixed answers. Theories are lenses that raise questions, and many readings combine several.
  • Thinking close reading belongs to one school. It began with practical criticism but is used by almost every theoretical approach.
  • Thinking New Critics ignored meaning. They pursued meaning intensely, but located it in the text's form and language.
  • Confusing the signifier with the thing itself. In Saussure's model, the signified is a concept, not an object in the world.
  • Believing deconstruction means a text can mean anything. Deconstruction is a rigorous reading of tensions actually present in the text.
  • Assuming reader-response means any reading is equally valid. Most reader-response critics see the text as guiding and limiting interpretation.

Lessons in this school

  1. What is literary theory?
    Literary theory asks what literature is, how meaning is made and what reading is for.
  2. Liberal humanism and practical criticism
    Before "theory", most English criticism rested on liberal humanist assumptions.
  3. New Criticism
    New Criticism dominated American criticism from the 1940s to the 1960s.
  4. Formalism and structuralism
    Russian Formalists and later structuralists asked what makes literature literary and what structures underlie texts.
  5. Poststructuralism and deconstruction
    Poststructuralism questioned whether meaning can ever be fixed.
  6. Reader-response theory
    Reader-response theories argue that meaning arises in the act of reading.
  7. Psychoanalytic criticism
    Psychoanalytic criticism reads literature in the light of the unconscious.
  8. Narratology
    Narratology is the systematic study of how stories are told.
  9. Marxist criticism
    Marxist criticism reads literature in relation to class, economics and ideology.
  10. Feminist criticism
    Feminist criticism examines how literature represents women, how gender shapes writing and reading, and how women writers have been treated.
  11. Gender and queer theory
    Gender and queer theory question fixed ideas of gender and sexuality.
  12. Postcolonial criticism
    Postcolonial criticism examines literature in relation to empire, colonialism and their aftermath.
  13. New historicism and cultural materialism
    New historicism and cultural materialism read literary texts alongside other texts of their time to explore power and ideology.
  14. Ecocriticism
    Ecocriticism studies the relationship between literature and the physical environment.
  15. Critical views and debate
    Advanced literary study requires using critics' views, comparing interpretations and building an argument.
  16. Literary value and evaluation
    What makes a work of literature good, and who decides?
