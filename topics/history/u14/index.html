Lessons in this school

  1. The Ottoman Empire
    The Ottoman Empire lasted from around 1300 until 1922 and at its height ruled south-eastern Europe, the Middle East and North Africa.
  2. The Mughal Empire
    The Mughal Empire ruled most of the Indian subcontinent from the sixteenth to the early eighteenth century and was one of the richest states in the world.
  3. The Qing dynasty
    The Qing dynasty ruled China from 1644 to 1912, presiding over a golden age in the eighteenth century and a century of crisis afterwards.
  4. The rise of modern Japan
    In the half-century after 1853 Japan transformed itself from an isolated feudal state into an industrial and imperial power.
  5. European colonialism
    From the late fifteenth century European states built empires across the Americas, Africa and Asia.
  6. The Atlantic slave trade
    Between the sixteenth and nineteenth centuries about 12.5 million Africans were forced onto ships bound for the Americas.
  7. Abolition and emancipation
    Between the 1780s and 1888 the Atlantic slave trade and then slavery itself were abolished across the Americas.
  8. The Haitian Revolution
    Between 1791 and 1804 enslaved people in the French colony of Saint-Domingue overthrew slavery and founded Haiti, the first state created by formerly enslaved people.
  9. Latin American independence
    Between 1808 and 1826 most of Spain's and Portugal's American colonies became independent.
  10. The Scramble for Africa: African experiences
    The partition of Africa looks different when seen from African perspectives rather than European capitals.
  11. Anti-colonial resistance
    In the twentieth century colonised peoples built mass movements that brought down European empires.
  12. Indian independence
    India won independence from Britain on 15 August 1947 after decades of nationalist campaigning.
  13. Partition
    The independence of India and Pakistan in August 1947 was accompanied by partition, mass violence and one of the largest migrations in history.
  14. The Chinese Revolution
    Between 1911 and 1949 China moved from the fall of its last dynasty through warlordism, civil war and Japanese invasion to communist victory.
  15. Mao and the People's Republic of China
    Mao Zedong ruled China from 1949 until his death in 1976, launching campaigns that transformed the country and caused immense suffering.
  16. Post-colonial states
    Dozens of new states emerged from the European empires between 1945 and 1975.
  17. Globalisation and historical change
    Globalisation, the growing connection of the world's economies and societies, has transformed life since 1945.

Key terms

Millet system
The Ottoman system allowing religious communities to run many of their own affairs
Jizya
A tax on non-Muslims under Islamic rule
Unequal treaties
Treaties forced on China giving foreign powers special rights
Shogun
The military ruler of Japan under the Tokugawa
Encomienda
The Spanish system granting colonists the labour of Indigenous people
Middle Passage
The forced voyage of enslaved Africans across the Atlantic
Abolition
Ending the slave trade or slavery by law
Saint-Domingue
The French Caribbean colony that became Haiti
Creole
A person of European descent born in the Americas
Hut tax
A colonial tax on dwellings, payable in cash
Satyagraha
Gandhi's method of non-violent resistance
Non-cooperation
Gandhi's campaign of refusing to work with British institutions
Partition
The division of British India into India and Pakistan in 1947
Guomindang
The Chinese Nationalist Party, led by Chiang Kai-shek
