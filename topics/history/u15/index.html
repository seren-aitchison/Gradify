Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What did Rainsborough argue at Putney?

That "the poorest he" had a life to live as the greatest, so every man should consent to his government. From “The English Revolution” →

In plain English: Historians have long argued about whether the upheavals in England between 1640 and 1660 amounted to a revolution. Key idea: Between 1640 and 1660 England executed its king, abolished monarchy and Lords and heard radical ideas such as those of the Putney Debates (1647), but the Restoration of 1660 reversed much; historians debate whether to call it a revolution.

When was the Bastille stormed?

On 14 July 1789. From “The French Revolution” →

In plain English: The French Revolution, beginning in 1789, overthrew the monarchy, executed a king and spread ideas of liberty and equality across Europe. Key idea: Bankruptcy and resistance to tax reform led to the Estates-General (1789), the Bastille and the Rights of Man; the king's flight (1791), war (1792) and popular pressure radicalised the revolution into the republic, the king's execution (1793) and the Terror.

What did the Second Republic in France introduce in 1848?

Universal male suffrage. From “The European revolutions of 1848” →

In plain English: In 1848 revolutions broke out across Europe, from Paris to Vienna, Berlin, Budapest and the Italian states. Key idea: Hunger, economic crisis, liberalism and nationalism produced revolutions across Europe in 1848; divisions among revolutionaries, loyal armies and Russian intervention defeated them by 1849, though some changes, such as the end of serfdom in Austria, lasted.

Which three revolutions did Skocpol compare?

The French, Russian and Chinese Revolutions. From “The Chinese Revolution in comparison” →

In plain English: China's communist revolution, completed in 1949, differed sharply from Russia's in 1917, though both were led by Marxist parties. Key idea: Russia's Bolsheviks seized the cities in 1917, while China's Communists fought a rural war from 1927 to 1949, with Mao adapting Marxism to the peasantry; Skocpol explains both through state crisis, elite division and peasant revolt.

What did Locke argue in Two Treatises of Government?

That government rests on consent and that people may resist tyranny. From “Revolutionary ideologies” →

In plain English: Revolutionaries have been inspired by very different sets of ideas, from liberal rights to Marxist class struggle and Islamic government. Key idea: Revolutionary ideologies range from Locke, Rousseau and Paine's ideas of rights and popular sovereignty to Marxism, Leninism, Maoism, nationalism and Khomeini's Islamic government; ideas shape revolutions' aims, while crises usually create the opportunity.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Revolutions and Political Change

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which radical group proposed the Agreement of the People? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    The Levellers. It called for a written constitution and wider voting rights.

  2. Who mainly drafted the Declaration of Independence? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Thomas Jefferson. It was adopted by the Continental Congress on 4 July 1776.

  3. Who led the Committee of Public Safety during the Terror? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Maximilien Robespierre. Robespierre was himself executed in July 1794.

  4. Why do revisionists question calling the period a revolution? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Few sought revolution in 1642, and much was reversed in 1660. Intentions and outcomes cut against the label.

  5. Why did Britain try to tax the colonies after 1763? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    To pay debts from the Seven Years' War and the cost of defending the colonies. War debts drove Britain to seek colonial revenue.

  6. Why did the flight to Varennes radicalise the revolution? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    It showed the king could not be trusted to accept a constitutional monarchy. The king's betrayal undermined the case for monarchy.

  7. Which judgement about the English Revolution is best supported? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    A revolution in events and ideas, though not in intentions or immediate outcome. Events and ideas were revolutionary; aims and results were mixed.

  8. Which judgement about the radicalism of the American Revolution is best supported? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Radical in ideas and political forms, limited in who shared its rights. Its ideals reached further than its practice.

  9. Which explanation of the revolution's radicalisation is best supported? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    War and the king's betrayal, under pressure from Paris, drove it from reform to terror. War, betrayal and popular pressure combined to radicalise it.

Key takeaways

  • Between 1640 and 1660 England executed its king, abolished monarchy and Lords and heard radical ideas such as those of the Putney Debates (1647), but the Restoration of 1660 reversed much; historians debate whether to call it a revolution.
  • Taxation after 1763, the Stamp Act (1765), the Boston Massacre (1770) and Tea Party (1773) led to war from 1775, the Declaration of Independence (4 July 1776) and recognition in 1783; the revolution created a republic but kept slavery.
  • Bankruptcy and resistance to tax reform led to the Estates-General (1789), the Bastille and the Rights of Man; the king's flight (1791), war (1792) and popular pressure radicalised the revolution into the republic, the king's execution (1793) and the Terror.
  • Compared with the American and French Revolutions, which proclaimed universal rights but kept or restored slavery, the Haitian Revolution abolished slavery permanently and created a state of formerly enslaved people; Trouillot argued it was long silenced in Western histories.
  • Latin American independence was led by creoles, triggered by Napoleon's invasion of Spain (1808) and shaped by fear of popular revolt, as in Hidalgo's rising (1810); it created republics but little social change, and caudillos often ruled after independence.
  • Hunger, economic crisis, liberalism and nationalism produced revolutions across Europe in 1848; divisions among revolutionaries, loyal armies and Russian intervention defeated them by 1849, though some changes, such as the end of serfdom in Austria, lasted.

Common mistakes in Revolutions and Political Change

  • Assuming that people in 1642 set out to make a revolution. Few did; radical changes came as the war and its aftermath unfolded.
  • Thinking "all men are created equal" applied to everyone in 1776. Slavery continued, and women and Indigenous peoples were excluded.
  • Thinking the revolution aimed at a republic from 1789. Most early revolutionaries wanted a constitutional monarchy.
  • Treating the Atlantic revolutions as a story only of Europe and North America. Haiti tested their ideals most radically.
  • Assuming independence brought democracy. Many new states were ruled by caudillos and elites.
  • Calling 1848 a complete failure. Serfdom was abolished in Austria, and liberal and nationalist ideas spread.

Lessons in this school

  1. The English Revolution
    Historians have long argued about whether the upheavals in England between 1640 and 1660 amounted to a revolution.
  2. The American Revolution
    Between 1763 and 1783 thirteen British colonies in North America rebelled and won independence as the United States.
  3. The French Revolution
    The French Revolution, beginning in 1789, overthrew the monarchy, executed a king and spread ideas of liberty and equality across Europe.
  4. The Haitian Revolution in comparison
    The Haitian Revolution was the most radical of the Atlantic revolutions, the only one in which enslaved people overthrew slavery and created their own state.
  5. The Latin American revolutions
    The wars of independence in Spanish America ended three centuries of colonial rule, but they changed society far less than politics.
  6. The European revolutions of 1848
    In 1848 revolutions broke out across Europe, from Paris to Vienna, Berlin, Budapest and the Italian states.
  7. The Russian Revolution in comparison
    The Russian Revolution of 1917 is often compared with the French Revolution of 1789.
  8. The Chinese Revolution in comparison
    China's communist revolution, completed in 1949, differed sharply from Russia's in 1917, though both were led by Marxist parties.
  9. Decolonisation movements
    The end of European empires after 1945 transferred power to new states across Asia and Africa.
  10. The Iranian Revolution
    The Iranian Revolution of 1979 overthrew the Shah and created an Islamic Republic, surprising observers who expected revolutions to be secular.
  11. Revolutionary ideologies
    Revolutionaries have been inspired by very different sets of ideas, from liberal rights to Marxist class struggle and Islamic government.
  12. The causes and consequences of revolution
    What makes revolutions happen, and what do they leave behind?
  13. Comparative revolutionary history
    Comparing revolutions can reveal patterns that a single case hides, but it also risks forcing different events into one model.

Revolutions and Political Change: questions students ask

What was the colonists' slogan against the Stamp Act?

"No taxation without representation".

What was the Tennis Court Oath?

The Third Estate's vow on 20 June 1789 not to separate until France had a constitution.

What did Trouillot argue about the Haitian Revolution?

That it was "unthinkable" to many Europeans at the time and later silenced in Western histories.

What was a caudillo?

A military strongman who seized or held power in nineteenth-century Latin America.
