Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What was the Nazi–Soviet Pact?

The non-aggression pact of 23 August 1939 between Germany and the Soviet Union, which secretly divided eastern Europe. From “The causes of the Second World War” →

In plain English: Europe went to war again in September 1939, only twenty years after Versailles. Key idea: Versailles, the Depression and the League's failures created the conditions; Hitler's step-by-step challenges from 1935, enabled by appeasement and the Nazi–Soviet Pact, led to war over Poland in September 1939.

Who led Vichy France?

Marshal Pétain. From “The fall of France” →

In plain English: In six weeks in May and June 1940 Germany defeated France, the Low Countries and the British army in France. Key idea: Germany's attack through the Ardennes from 10 May 1940 split the Allied armies; Dunkirk saved about 338,000 troops, but France signed an armistice on 22 June, leaving Vichy France and occupation.

What was Operation Bagration?

The Soviet offensive of June 1944 that destroyed German Army Group Centre. From “The Eastern Front, 1941–1945” →

In plain English: The war in the east was the largest and deadliest part of the Second World War. Key idea: Stalingrad (surrender on 2 February 1943) and Kursk (July 1943) turned the war in the east; the Red Army then advanced to Berlin, at the cost of around 27 million Soviet lives.

What were Beveridge's five giants?

Want, disease, ignorance, squalor and idleness. From “The Home Front, 1939–1945” →

In plain English: The Second World War mobilised British society even more than the First. Key idea: Conscription of men and, from December 1941, women, the Home Guard, rationing from January 1940 and the Beveridge Report of 1942 mobilised Britain and built support for the post-war welfare state.

When was the first atomic test?

The Trinity test in New Mexico on 16 July 1945. From “The atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki” →

In plain English: In August 1945 the United States dropped atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki, killing many tens of thousands of people at once and more in the months that followed. Key idea: After the Trinity test in July 1945, the United States bombed Hiroshima (6 August) and Nagasaki (9 August), killing over 200,000 people by the end of 1945; historians still debate whether the bombings were justified.

Quick quiz: test yourself on The Second World War

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. When did Britain and France declare war on Germany? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    3 September 1939. They declared war two days after Germany invaded Poland.

  2. Which British prime minister signed the Munich Agreement? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Neville Chamberlain. Chamberlain returned promising "peace for our time".

  3. On what date did Germany invade Poland? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    1 September 1939. The invasion began the war in Europe; Britain and France declared war two days later.

  4. Why was the Nazi–Soviet Pact important for Hitler? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    It removed the threat of a war on two fronts if he invaded Poland. Soviet neutrality made the invasion of Poland less risky.

  5. Why was Czechoslovakia's absence from Munich significant? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Its fate was decided by the Great Powers without its consent. The country affected had no say, which made the agreement look like a betrayal.

  6. Why did the Soviet Union invade Poland in September 1939? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Under the secret terms of the Nazi–Soviet Pact, which divided Poland. The secret protocol divided eastern Europe.

  7. Which judgement about responsibility for the war is best supported? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Hitler's aims drove events, but others' failures shaped when and how war came. Most historians combine Hitler's aims with others' choices.

  8. Which judgement about appeasement is best supported? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Understandable in the circumstances, but based on a misjudgement of Hitler. Historians weigh circumstances against misjudgement.

  9. Which is the best explanation of Poland's rapid defeat? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    German armour and air power, Poland's position and the Soviet invasion together. Several factors combined: German methods, Poland's position and the Soviet attack.

Key takeaways

  • Versailles, the Depression and the League's failures created the conditions; Hitler's step-by-step challenges from 1935, enabled by appeasement and the Nazi–Soviet Pact, led to war over Poland in September 1939.
  • Chamberlain appeased Hitler because of public opinion, unpreparedness, sympathy with German grievances and fear of bombing; the Munich Agreement of September 1938 gave Germany the Sudetenland, and appeasement collapsed in March 1939.
  • Germany invaded Poland on 1 September 1939 and the Soviet Union on 17 September; Poland was defeated within weeks, partitioned and brutally occupied, while the west fought a Phoney War.
  • Germany's attack through the Ardennes from 10 May 1940 split the Allied armies; Dunkirk saved about 338,000 troops, but France signed an armistice on 22 June, leaving Vichy France and occupation.
  • The RAF defeated the Luftwaffe's attempt to win air control between July and October 1940 through radar, the Dowding system, good aircraft and German mistakes, forcing Hitler to postpone the invasion of Britain.
  • The Blitz (September 1940 to May 1941) killed more than 40,000 civilians; shelters, evacuation and civil defence helped people cope, and historians have shown the "Blitz spirit" to be real but simplified.

Common mistakes in The Second World War

  • Thinking war in 1939 began over the Sudetenland. It began when Germany invaded Poland on 1 September 1939.
  • Assuming appeasement had no supporters. In 1938 it was popular, and Chamberlain was cheered on his return from Munich.
  • Thinking Poland was defeated by Germany alone. The Soviet invasion from the east sealed its defeat.
  • Assuming Germany won because it had many more tanks. The Allies had as many tanks; German planning and speed made the difference.
  • Thinking the RAF pilots were all British. Many came from Poland, Czechoslovakia, the Commonwealth and other countries.
  • Thinking the Blitz affected only London. Coventry, Liverpool, Glasgow, Belfast, Plymouth and many other cities were heavily bombed.

The Second World War mind map

Lessons in this school

  1. The causes of the Second World War
    Europe went to war again in September 1939, only twenty years after Versailles.
  2. Appeasement
    In the 1930s Britain and France tried to avoid war by meeting some of Hitler's demands.
  3. The invasion of Poland
    Germany invaded Poland on 1 September 1939 and the Soviet Union invaded from the east on 17 September.
  4. The fall of France
    In six weeks in May and June 1940 Germany defeated France, the Low Countries and the British army in France.
  5. The Battle of Britain
    In the summer and autumn of 1940 the Luftwaffe tried to win control of the air over southern England as a step towards invasion.
  6. The Blitz
    From September 1940 to May 1941 German bombers attacked British cities, killing over 40,000 civilians.
  7. Operation Barbarossa
    On 22 June 1941 Germany invaded the Soviet Union in the largest military operation in history.
  8. The Eastern Front, 1941–1945
    The war in the east was the largest and deadliest part of the Second World War.
  9. The Pacific War
    From 1941 to 1945 Japan fought the United States, Britain and their allies across Asia and the Pacific.
  10. Pearl Harbor
    On 7 December 1941 Japan attacked the US Pacific Fleet at Pearl Harbor in Hawaii, bringing the United States into the war.
  11. The Holocaust in wartime
    During the war the Nazi regime deported Jews from across occupied Europe to their deaths.
  12. The Home Front, 1939–1945
    The Second World War mobilised British society even more than the First.
  13. Wartime economies
    The Second World War was won as much in factories as on battlefields.
  14. D-Day
    On 6 June 1944 Allied forces landed in Normandy in the largest seaborne invasion in history.
  15. The defeat of Nazi Germany
    In the last year of the war Germany was squeezed between the Soviet advance from the east and the Western Allies from the west, while bombers destroyed its cities.
  16. The atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki
    In August 1945 the United States dropped atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki, killing many tens of thousands of people at once and more in the months that followed.
  17. The surrender of Japan
    Japan announced its surrender on 15 August 1945 and signed it on 2 September.
  18. The post-war settlement
    The Allied leaders who met at Yalta and Potsdam in 1945 reshaped Europe.
  19. The creation of the United Nations
    The United Nations was founded in 1945 to keep the peace where the League of Nations had failed.
