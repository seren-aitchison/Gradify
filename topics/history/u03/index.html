Lessons in this school

  1. Henry VII and dynastic security
    Henry Tudor won the crown at Bosworth in 1485 with a weak claim and spent his reign making it secure.
  2. Henry VIII's government
    Henry VIII ruled from 1509 to 1547 through powerful ministers, above all Thomas Wolsey and Thomas Cromwell.
  3. The English Reformation
    In the 1530s Henry VIII broke with the Roman Catholic Church and made himself head of the Church in England.
  4. The dissolution of the monasteries
    Between 1536 and 1540 Henry VIII closed every monastery, nunnery and friary in England and Wales and took their wealth.
  5. Edward VI and Protestant reform
    Edward VI became king at nine in 1547, and in his six-year reign England's church became clearly Protestant.
  6. Mary I and Catholic restoration
    Mary I, queen from 1553 to 1558, restored Catholicism and the Pope's authority, and burned around 280 Protestants.
  7. Elizabeth I and the religious settlement
    Elizabeth I's religious settlement of 1559 created a Protestant Church of England that tried to include as many people as possible.
  8. Elizabethan government
    Elizabeth I ruled from 1558 to 1603 through a small Privy Council, Parliament and unpaid local officials.
  9. The Spanish Armada
    In 1588 Philip II of Spain sent a great fleet, the Armada, to escort an army to invade England.
  10. Elizabethan exploration
    Elizabethan sailors crossed the Atlantic, raided Spanish America and sailed around the world.
  11. Poverty and social policy
    Poverty grew in the sixteenth century as population and prices rose.
  12. Tudor culture and society
    Tudor society was a strict hierarchy, but the sixteenth century also saw a growing gentry, rising literacy and a flowering of drama.
  13. Tudor foreign policy
    Tudor monarchs balanced the two great powers of France and Spain, fought Scotland and struggled to control Ireland.
  14. Succession and the end of the Tudor dynasty
    The Tudors were repeatedly troubled by the question of who would succeed.

Key terms

Pretender
Someone who falsely claims a right to the throne
Lord Chancellor
The king's chief minister and head of the legal system
Annulment
A declaration that a marriage was never valid
Valor Ecclesiasticus
The 1535 survey of the wealth of the Church in England and Wales
Book of Common Prayer
The English service book, first issued in 1549
Heresy
Beliefs contrary to the official teaching of the Church
Supreme Governor
Elizabeth's title as head of the Church of England from 1559
Justice of the Peace
An unpaid local gentleman responsible for law and order in a county
Armada
The Spanish fleet sent against England in 1588
Circumnavigation
A voyage all the way around the world
Vagrant
A person without a settled home or work, wandering from place to place
Gentry
Landowners below the nobility, often JPs and MPs
Rough Wooing
Henry VIII's war against Scotland in the 1540s to force a royal marriage
Succession
The order in which people inherit the throne
