Lessons in this school

  1. Source analysis questions
    Source questions test whether you can use evidence from the past to answer a historical question.
  2. Interpretation analysis questions
    Interpretation questions ask you to explain and evaluate how historians and others have presented the past.
  3. Causation questions
    Causation questions ask why something happened.
  4. Consequence questions
    Consequence questions ask what happened as a result of an event, and how important those results were.
  5. Change and continuity questions
    Change and continuity questions ask how far things changed over a period, and how quickly.
  6. Significance questions
    Significance questions ask why an event, person or development mattered.
  7. Comparison questions
    Comparison questions ask you to identify and explain similarities and differences between two events, people or periods.
  8. Evidence-based essays
    Examiners reward essays that support every point with precise, relevant evidence.
  9. Extended historical arguments
    A level essays reward a sustained argument that weighs factors and reaches a substantiated judgement.
  10. Planning examination answers
    A few minutes of planning in an exam produces a clearer, better-argued answer.
  11. Time management
    Good history answers are wasted if the exam time runs out.
  12. Mark-scheme interpretation
    Understanding how examiners mark helps students write answers that meet the criteria.
  13. GCSE practice examinations
    Practising full questions under exam conditions is the best preparation for GCSE History.
  14. A level practice examinations
    A level examinations test sustained argument, source evaluation and the analysis of interpretations.

Key terms

Usefulness
How far a source helps answer a specific historical question
Interpretation
A deliberate view of the past constructed by a historian or other writer
Trigger
The event that sparks a larger development
Unintended consequence
A result that decision-makers did not expect or want
Extent of change
How much something changed
Significance
Why an event, person or development mattered, judged by criteria
Point-by-point comparison
Comparing two cases on each theme in turn
Precise evidence
Specific dates, figures, names and quotations that support a point
Line of argument
The consistent answer an essay develops from start to finish
Command word
The instruction in a question, such as "explain" or "assess"
Practice testing
Testing yourself from memory to strengthen learning
Levels-based marking
Placing an answer in the level whose description best fits its overall quality
Describe question
A question asking for accurate, developed features
Extract
A passage from a historian's work set for analysis in an exam
