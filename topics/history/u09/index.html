Lessons in this school

  1. Post-war Italy
    Italy was on the winning side in 1918, yet many Italians felt cheated by the peace and frightened by unrest at home.
  2. Mussolini
    Benito Mussolini moved from revolutionary socialism to nationalism during the First World War, founded the Fascist movement in 1919 and became prime minister in 1922.
  3. The development of Italian Fascism
    Mussolini took office as head of a coalition in 1922 and built a dictatorship by 1926.
  4. The Weimar Republic
    Germany became a democratic republic in November 1918, as the war ended in defeat.
  5. The Treaty of Versailles and Germany
    For many Germans, the new republic was born with the humiliation of Versailles.
  6. Political instability in Weimar Germany
    Between 1919 and 1923 the Weimar Republic faced uprisings from left and right, political murders and an attempted putsch by Hitler.
  7. Hyperinflation
    In 1923 the German mark collapsed: by November one US dollar was worth about 4.2 trillion marks.
  8. The Great Depression in Germany
    The Wall Street Crash of October 1929 plunged Germany into an economic and political crisis.
  9. Hitler's rise to power
    Adolf Hitler went from a failed putsch in 1923 to chancellor of Germany on 30 January 1933.
  10. Nazi ideology
    Nazism was built on a set of racial and nationalist ideas that shaped everything the regime did.
  11. Nazi consolidation of power
    In January 1933 Hitler led a coalition cabinet; by August 1934 he was Führer of a one-party state.
  12. The police state
    The Nazi regime controlled Germans through the SS, the Gestapo, concentration camps and courts.
  13. Propaganda and censorship in Nazi Germany
    Joseph Goebbels ran a Ministry of Propaganda that tried to control everything Germans read, heard and saw.
  14. Nazi economic policy
    Unemployment in Germany fell from about six million in 1933 to near full employment by 1938.
  15. Youth and education in Nazi Germany
    The Nazis saw young people as the future of the national community and set out to shape them through schools and youth organisations.
  16. Women and family policy in Nazi Germany
    Nazi ideology assigned women the role of wives and mothers serving the national community.
  17. Persecution of minorities
    Nazi racial ideology targeted many groups besides Jews: Roma and Sinti, disabled people, gay men and others judged a threat to the national community.
  18. Antisemitism
    Antisemitism was at the centre of Nazi ideology.
  19. The Holocaust
    Between 1941 and 1945 the Nazi regime and its collaborators murdered about six million Jews.
  20. Resistance to Nazi rule
    Opposition to Nazi rule ranged from grumbling and nonconformity to attempts to kill Hitler.

Key terms

Mutilated victory
The nationalist claim that Italy had been cheated of its rewards in the peace settlement
Squadristi
Fascist action squads that used violence against opponents
Acerbo Law
The 1923 law giving two-thirds of seats to the largest party with at least a quarter of the vote
Proportional representation
An electoral system giving parties seats in proportion to their share of the vote
November criminals
The insulting name nationalists gave to the politicians who agreed the Armistice and Versailles
Freikorps
Volunteer paramilitary units of ex-soldiers in post-war Germany
Hyperinflation
Extremely rapid inflation in which money loses its value very quickly
Austerity
Cutting government spending to balance the budget
NSDAP
The National Socialist German Workers' Party, the Nazi Party
Volksgemeinschaft
The Nazi ideal of a racially pure national community above class divisions
Enabling Act
The 1933 law allowing Hitler's cabinet to make laws without the Reichstag
SS
The Schutzstaffel, the Nazi elite force led by Himmler
Propaganda ministry
Goebbels's Ministry of Public Enlightenment and Propaganda, set up in March 1933
Autarky
Economic self-sufficiency
