Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

Which industries did Labour nationalise between 1946 and 1951?

The Bank of England, coal, railways, electricity, gas, and iron and steel. From “Post-war reconstruction” →

In plain English: In July 1945 Labour won a landslide and set out to rebuild a bankrupt, bomb-damaged Britain. Key idea: Attlee's Labour government, elected in July 1945, nationalised major industries and built the welfare state while managing near-bankruptcy, rationing and austerity.

What was the eleven-plus?

An exam at eleven that selected children for grammar, technical or secondary modern schools. From “Housing and education reform” →

In plain English: Post-war governments set out to rehouse millions and to give every child a secondary education. Key idea: The 1944 Education Act gave free secondary education through a tripartite system, later challenged by comprehensives from 1965; prefabs, council houses, new towns and Macmillan's 300,000 homes a year rebuilt housing.

What was UDI?

Rhodesia's Unilateral Declaration of Independence by its white minority government in 1965. From “Decolonisation” →

In plain English: Decolonisation was often violent and its legacy contested. Key idea: Kenya's Mau Mau emergency (from 1952) brought detention camps and abuses later acknowledged by the British government in 2013, while Rhodesia's UDI in 1965 delayed majority rule until 1980; historians have challenged the image of orderly decolonisation.

What happened on Bloody Sunday?

On 30 January 1972 British soldiers shot dead 13 civil rights marchers in Derry; another died later. From “The Troubles in Northern Ireland” →

In plain English: From the late 1960s to 1998 Northern Ireland suffered a violent conflict known as the Troubles, in which around 3,500 people were killed. Key idea: Discrimination and the clash between unionists and nationalists led to the Troubles from 1968; Bloody Sunday, internment and paramilitary violence followed, until the Good Friday Agreement of 1998 created power-sharing.

When did the Scottish Parliament and Welsh Assembly open?

In 1999. From “New Labour” →

In plain English: Under Tony Blair, Labour won three general elections from 1997, combining market economics with investment in public services. Key idea: New Labour dropped Clause IV in 1995, won in 1997 and introduced Bank of England independence, the minimum wage, devolution and the Good Friday Agreement, but the Iraq War (2003) and the 2008 crash shaped its legacy.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Britain since 1945

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Who led the Labour government elected in 1945? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Clement Attlee. Attlee was prime minister from 1945 to 1951.

  2. Which act of 1946 created contributory benefits for unemployment and old age? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    The National Insurance Act. National Insurance covered the main risks of life.

  3. Which minister created the NHS? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Aneurin Bevan. Bevan steered the 1946 Act through Parliament as Minister of Health.

  4. Why did Britain need an American loan in 1946? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    It was close to bankrupt after selling investments and borrowing to fight the war. The war left Britain's finances exhausted.

  5. Why did some poverty remain after the welfare state was created? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Benefits were set low, and some groups fell through gaps. Poverty was "rediscovered" by researchers in the 1960s.

  6. How did Bevan win over hospital consultants? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    He let them keep private patients and paid them well. His phrase was "stuffed their mouths with gold".

  7. Which judgement about the Attlee government is best supported? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    It transformed the state and society under severe economic constraints. Its reforms outlasted it, largely kept by the Conservatives after 1951.

  8. Which judgement about the welfare state is best supported? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    A major advance, though not a complete victory over any giant. The evidence shows real progress, with clear limits for each giant.

  9. Which judgement about the early NHS is best supported? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    A lasting success that never fully matched the founders' cost estimates. Popularity and cost pressures went together.

Key takeaways

  • Attlee's Labour government, elected in July 1945, nationalised major industries and built the welfare state while managing near-bankruptcy, rationing and austerity.
  • Family allowances (1945), National Insurance (1946), National Assistance (1948) and the NHS created a welfare state aimed at Beveridge's five giants, a major but incomplete advance.
  • Bevan's NHS began on 5 July 1948, universal and free at the point of use; he won over consultants, but demand and costs soon led to charges and his resignation in 1951.
  • The 1944 Education Act gave free secondary education through a tripartite system, later challenged by comprehensives from 1965; prefabs, council houses, new towns and Macmillan's 300,000 homes a year rebuilt housing.
  • From the Windrush in 1948, migrants from the Commonwealth helped rebuild Britain and made it multicultural, facing racism that led to Race Relations Acts from 1965 and controls on immigration from 1962.
  • India's independence in 1947 began rapid decolonisation; by the late 1960s most colonies were independent, driven by nationalist pressure, Britain's weakness, international opinion and changing British priorities.

Common mistakes in Britain since 1945

  • Thinking rationing ended with the war. Some rationing tightened after 1945, and it did not finally end until 1954.
  • Thinking the welfare state was created entirely by Labour from nothing. It built on earlier reforms, the wartime coalition's plans and the 1944 Education Act passed under the coalition.
  • Thinking the NHS has always been completely free. Charges for dentures and spectacles began in 1951 and prescription charges in 1952.
  • Thinking comprehensives replaced grammar schools everywhere. Some areas kept selection, and grammar schools still exist in parts of England.
  • Thinking post-war Caribbean migrants were foreigners entering Britain. They were British subjects under the 1948 Act.
  • Thinking decolonisation was always peaceful. Partition, Malaya and Kenya show how violent it could be.

Lessons in this school

  1. Post-war reconstruction
    In July 1945 Labour won a landslide and set out to rebuild a bankrupt, bomb-damaged Britain.
  2. The welfare state
    Between 1945 and 1948 Britain built a welfare state designed to protect people "from the cradle to the grave".
  3. The creation of the NHS
    On 5 July 1948 the National Health Service began, offering medical care free at the point of use to everyone in Britain.
  4. Housing and education reform
    Post-war governments set out to rehouse millions and to give every child a secondary education.
  5. Immigration and multicultural Britain
    After 1945 migrants from the Caribbean, South Asia and elsewhere helped rebuild Britain and changed its society.
  6. The end of the British Empire
    In 1945 Britain ruled the largest empire in the world; by the late 1960s most of it had gone.
  7. Decolonisation
    Decolonisation was often violent and its legacy contested.
  8. The Suez Crisis
    In 1956 Britain and France secretly planned with Israel to seize the Suez Canal from Egypt, and were forced to withdraw under American pressure.
  9. The Swinging Sixties
    The 1960s are remembered as a decade of pop music, fashion and new freedoms.
  10. Social and cultural change
    Between the 1950s and the 1990s British society changed in women's lives, education, religion and popular culture.
  11. The Troubles in Northern Ireland
    From the late 1960s to 1998 Northern Ireland suffered a violent conflict known as the Troubles, in which around 3,500 people were killed.
  12. Thatcherism
    Margaret Thatcher, prime minister from 1979 to 1990, set out to reverse Britain's economic decline by reducing the role of the state.
  13. Industrial decline
    The industries that had made Britain the first industrial nation shrank dramatically after 1945.
  14. New Labour
    Under Tony Blair, Labour won three general elections from 1997, combining market economics with investment in public services.
  15. Britain and Europe
    Britain stayed out of European integration in the 1950s, joined the European Economic Community in 1973 and argued over its place in Europe for the next four decades.
  16. Brexit
    In June 2016 the United Kingdom voted to leave the European Union, and it left in January 2020.
  17. Contemporary British society
    Britain in the 2020s is older, more diverse, less religious and more devolved than in 1945.

When did the Empire Windrush arrive?
