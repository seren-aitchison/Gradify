Lessons in this school

  1. Post-war reconstruction
    In July 1945 Labour won a landslide and set out to rebuild a bankrupt, bomb-damaged Britain.
  2. The welfare state
    Between 1945 and 1948 Britain built a welfare state designed to protect people "from the cradle to the grave".
  3. The creation of the NHS
    On 5 July 1948 the National Health Service began, offering medical care free at the point of use to everyone in Britain.
  4. Housing and education reform
    Post-war governments set out to rehouse millions and to give every child a secondary education.
  5. Immigration and multicultural Britain
    After 1945 migrants from the Caribbean, South Asia and elsewhere helped rebuild Britain and changed its society.
  6. The end of the British Empire
    In 1945 Britain ruled the largest empire in the world; by the late 1960s most of it had gone.
  7. Decolonisation
    Decolonisation was often violent and its legacy contested.
  8. The Suez Crisis
    In 1956 Britain and France secretly planned with Israel to seize the Suez Canal from Egypt, and were forced to withdraw under American pressure.
  9. The Swinging Sixties
    The 1960s are remembered as a decade of pop music, fashion and new freedoms.
  10. Social and cultural change
    Between the 1950s and the 1990s British society changed in women's lives, education, religion and popular culture.
  11. The Troubles in Northern Ireland
    From the late 1960s to 1998 Northern Ireland suffered a violent conflict known as the Troubles, in which around 3,500 people were killed.
  12. Thatcherism
    Margaret Thatcher, prime minister from 1979 to 1990, set out to reverse Britain's economic decline by reducing the role of the state.
  13. Industrial decline
    The industries that had made Britain the first industrial nation shrank dramatically after 1945.
  14. New Labour
    Under Tony Blair, Labour won three general elections from 1997, combining market economics with investment in public services.
  15. Britain and Europe
    Britain stayed out of European integration in the 1950s, joined the European Economic Community in 1973 and argued over its place in Europe for the next four decades.
  16. Brexit
    In June 2016 the United Kingdom voted to leave the European Union, and it left in January 2020.
  17. Contemporary British society
    Britain in the 2020s is older, more diverse, less religious and more devolved than in 1945.

Key terms

Nationalisation
Taking an industry into public ownership
Welfare state
A system in which the state provides social security, health care and other services
Free at the point of use
Treatment without payment when received, funded through taxation
Eleven-plus
The exam used to select children for grammar schools at age eleven
Windrush generation
Caribbean migrants who came to Britain between 1948 and the early 1970s
Decolonisation
The process by which colonies became independent
Mau Mau
The rebellion against British rule in Kenya from 1952
Suez Canal
The canal linking the Mediterranean and the Red Sea, vital for oil and trade
Permissive society
The term for the more liberal laws and attitudes of the 1960s
Secularisation
The decline of religious belief and practice in society
Unionist
Someone who wants Northern Ireland to remain in the United Kingdom
Monetarism
Controlling the money supply to reduce inflation
Deindustrialisation
The shrinking of manufacturing industry in an economy
Clause IV
The part of Labour's constitution committing it to common ownership, rewritten in 1995
