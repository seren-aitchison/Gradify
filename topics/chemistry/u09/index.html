Lessons in this school

  1. Introduction to acids and bases
    Acids and bases are among the most important classes of substance in chemistry.
  2. Arrhenius theory
    The Arrhenius theory defined acids and bases by the ions they produce in water.
  3. Brønsted-Lowry theory
    The Brønsted-Lowry theory defines acids and bases by proton transfer.
  4. Lewis acid-base theory
    Lewis theory defines acids and bases by electron pairs, covering even more reactions.
  5. Strong acids and bases
    Strong acids and bases ionise completely in water.
  6. Weak acids and bases
    Weak acids and bases only partially ionise in water.
  7. pH
    pH measures the acidity of a solution on a logarithmic scale.
  8. pOH
    pOH and the ionic product of water connect acidity and basicity.
  9. Acid dissociation constants
    The acid dissociation constant Ka measures the strength of a weak acid.
  10. Base dissociation constants
    The base dissociation constant Kb measures the strength of a weak base.
  11. Ka and Kb
    Ka and Kb of a conjugate pair are linked through Kw.
  12. Buffers
    Buffers resist changes in pH when small amounts of acid or base are added.
  13. Henderson-Hasselbalch equation
    The Henderson-Hasselbalch equation calculates buffer pH.
  14. Acid-base titrations
    Titrations measure the concentration of an acid or base accurately.
  15. Indicators
    Indicators change colour over a pH range.
  16. Equivalence points
    The equivalence point is reached when reactants have reacted in exact stoichiometric amounts.
  17. Solubility equilibria
    Sparingly soluble salts set up equilibria between solid and dissolved ions.
  18. Ksp
    The solubility product Ksp quantifies the solubility of sparingly soluble salts.
  19. Common-ion effect
    Adding an ion already present in an equilibrium reduces solubility or ionisation.
  20. Complex ion equilibria
    Metal ions form complexes with ligands in equilibrium.
  21. Amphoteric substances
    Amphoteric substances can act as acids or bases.
  22. Polyprotic acids
    Polyprotic acids can donate more than one proton.

Key terms

Acid
A substance releasing H⁺ ions in water
Arrhenius acid
A substance producing H⁺ in water
Proton donor
A Brønsted-Lowry acid
Lewis acid
An electron pair acceptor
Strong acid
An acid that ionises completely in water
Weak acid
An acid that partially ionises in water
pH
−log₁₀ of the hydrogen ion concentration
Kw
Ionic product of water, [H⁺][OH⁻]
Ka
Acid dissociation constant
Kb
Base dissociation constant
Conjugate pair
An acid and base differing by one proton
Buffer
A solution resisting changes in pH
Henderson-Hasselbalch equation
pH = pKa + log([A⁻]/[HA])
Titre
The volume of solution added from a burette
