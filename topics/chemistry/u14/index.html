Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What type of alcohol is propan-2-ol?

Secondary. From “Alcohols” →

In plain English: Alcohols contain the –OH group on a saturated carbon. Key idea: Alcohols are classified as primary, secondary or tertiary, hydrogen bond to give high boiling points and water solubility and undergo combustion, oxidation, dehydration, substitution to haloalkanes and reaction with sodium.

What suffix names a ketone?

-one. From “Ketones” →

In plain English: Ketones have a carbonyl group within the carbon chain. Key idea: Ketones have C=O between two carbons, are named with -one, are made by oxidising secondary alcohols, resist mild oxidation, are reduced to secondary alcohols, add nucleophiles more slowly than aldehydes and methyl ketones give the iodoform test.

What is biodiesel made of?

Methyl esters of fatty acids. From “Esters” →

In plain English: Esters contain the –COO– linkage. Key idea: Esters are named alkyl alkanoate, are volatile and fruity-smelling, are made from acids and alcohols reversibly or from acyl chlorides and anhydrides irreversibly and are used as solvents, flavourings and plasticisers, with fats as natural triesters and biodiesel made by transesterification.

What type of halogenoalkane is 2-bromo-2-methylpropane?

Tertiary. From “Halogenoalkanes” →

In plain English: Halogenoalkanes contain a halogen on an sp³ carbon. Key idea: Halogenoalkanes are classified as primary, secondary or tertiary, have polar C–X bonds, react in the order iodo > bromo > chloro > fluoro because of bond enthalpy and follow SN1, SN2 or elimination depending on structure and conditions.

What intermediate forms when a nucleophile attacks a carbonyl?

A tetrahedral intermediate. From “Carbonyl chemistry” →

In plain English: The carbonyl group links aldehydes, ketones, acids and their derivatives. Key idea: Carbonyl compounds react with nucleophiles at the δ+ carbon; aldehydes and ketones undergo addition, while acid derivatives undergo addition-elimination through a tetrahedral intermediate, with reactivity acyl chloride > anhydride > ester > amide > carboxylate.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Functional Group Chemistry

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which alcohol is tertiary? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    2-methylpropan-2-ol. The carbinol carbon carries three alkyl groups.

  2. Which reagent reacts with phenol but not with ethanol? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Sodium hydroxide solution. Phenol is acidic enough to be deprotonated by hydroxide.

  3. Which reagents make an ether in the Williamson synthesis? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    An alkoxide and a primary haloalkane. The alkoxide ion displaces halide from the haloalkane by an SN2 reaction.

  4. Why do alcohols boil higher than alkanes of similar mass? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Their –OH groups form hydrogen bonds between molecules. Hydrogen bonds are stronger than London forces alone.

  5. Why does phenol react with bromine water without a catalyst? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    The –OH lone pair activates the ring towards electrophiles. Electron donation makes the ring much more reactive than benzene.

  6. Why do ethers boil lower than isomeric alcohols? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Ether molecules cannot hydrogen bond to each other. Without O–H bonds, only weaker forces act between ether molecules.

  7. Why is methanol poisonous although ethanol is less so? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    The body oxidises methanol to toxic methanal and methanoic acid. Toxic metabolites damage the optic nerve and other tissues.

  8. Why does phenol not release CO₂ from sodium carbonate? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Phenol is a weaker acid than carbonic acid. Only acids stronger than H₂CO₃ release CO₂.

  9. Why are epoxides much more reactive than other ethers? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Ring strain in the three-membered ring is released on opening. Bond angles near 60° make the ring easy to open.

Key takeaways

  • Alcohols are classified as primary, secondary or tertiary, hydrogen bond to give high boiling points and water solubility and undergo combustion, oxidation, dehydration, substitution to haloalkanes and reaction with sodium.
  • Phenols are more acidic than alcohols because phenoxide is stabilised by delocalisation, react with NaOH but not carbonate, and have activated rings that react with bromine water to give 2,4,6-tribromophenol without a catalyst.
  • Ethers, R–O–R′, cannot hydrogen bond to each other, so they are volatile and fairly unreactive solvents; they are made by Williamson synthesis from alkoxides and primary haloalkanes, form peroxides on standing and strained epoxides are reactive.
  • Aldehydes have a terminal –CHO group named with -al, are made by oxidising primary alcohols and are oxidised to acids, reduced to primary alcohols and attacked by nucleophiles; 2,4-DNPH detects carbonyls and Tollens' or Fehling's distinguishes aldehydes.
  • Ketones have C=O between two carbons, are named with -one, are made by oxidising secondary alcohols, resist mild oxidation, are reduced to secondary alcohols, add nucleophiles more slowly than aldehydes and methyl ketones give the iodoform test.
  • Carboxylic acids are weak acids whose carboxylate is stabilised by delocalisation; they react with metals, carbonates and alcohols, are reduced by LiAlH₄ and form acyl chlorides, and electron-withdrawing groups increase their acidity.

Common mistakes in Functional Group Chemistry

  • Thinking alcohols are alkaline because they contain OH. The –OH is covalently bonded and not released as hydroxide.
  • Thinking phenol is an alcohol. The –OH on a ring changes its acidity and reactions.
  • Using a tertiary haloalkane in a Williamson synthesis. Elimination wins instead of substitution.
  • Thinking 2,4-DNPH distinguishes aldehydes from ketones. It reacts with both; Tollens' tells them apart.
  • Thinking all ketones give the iodoform test. Only those with a CH₃CO– group do.
  • Thinking carboxylic acids are strong acids. Most ionise only slightly in water.

Functional Group Chemistry: questions students ask

What forms when sodium reacts with ethanol?

Sodium ethoxide and hydrogen.

Why is phenol more acidic than ethanol?

Phenoxide is stabilised by delocalisation into the ring.

What is an epoxide?

A three-membered cyclic ether.

What does 2,4-DNPH show?

The presence of a carbonyl group.
