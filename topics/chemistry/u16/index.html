Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What is the point group of water?

C₂ᵥ. From “Molecular structure” →

In plain English: Molecular structure covers bond lengths, angles and symmetry. Key idea: Molecular structure is defined by bond lengths, angles and torsion angles and classified by symmetry into point groups, which govern polarity, chirality and spectroscopic activity; diffraction, spectroscopy and computation determine structures.

What is the energy formula for a particle in a box?

Eₙ = n²h² / (8mL²). From “Particle in a box” →

In plain English: The particle in a box is the simplest quantum model. Key idea: In the particle-in-a-box model, Eₙ = n²h²/8mL², with nonzero zero-point energy; energy gaps grow as the box shrinks, explaining colour in conjugated dyes and size-dependent emission from quantum dots.

Which transition gives a weak band in carbonyl compounds?

n → π*. From “Electronic transitions” →

In plain English: Electronic transitions move electrons between molecular orbitals. Key idea: Electronic transitions include σ → σ, π → π, n → π* and charge transfer; the Franck-Condon principle explains vibrational structure in bands and spin and Laporte selection rules make some transitions weak.

What does the steric factor account for?

The need for correct orientation in collisions. From “Chemical kinetics” →

In plain English: This lesson explores advanced kinetics, including collision and transition state theories and complex mechanisms. Key idea: Collision theory considers collision frequency, energy and orientation, while transition state theory uses an activated complex and the Eyring equation; the steady-state approximation handles intermediates, and chain and Michaelis-Menten kinetics describe complex mechanisms.

What contact angle shows a hydrophobic surface?

More than 90°. From “Interfaces” →

In plain English: Interfaces are boundaries between phases. Key idea: Surface tension arises from unbalanced forces at interfaces; surfactants lower it and form micelles above the CMC, removing grease, while contact angles describe wetting, from hydrophilic to superhydrophobic surfaces.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Physical Chemistry and Molecular Science

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which symmetry element rules out a permanent dipole moment? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    An inversion centre. Opposite equivalent atoms cancel any dipole.

  2. Why is O₂ paramagnetic? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    It has two unpaired electrons in π* orbitals. Hund's rule places one electron in each π* orbital.

  3. Which method uses electron density rather than the full wavefunction? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Density functional theory. DFT builds energy from the electron density.

  4. Why are measured bond lengths averages? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Molecules constantly vibrate about their equilibrium positions. Vibrational motion blurs the exact positions.

  5. What happens to the HOMO-LUMO gap as conjugation increases? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    It decreases, shifting absorption to longer wavelengths. More combined p orbitals bring levels closer.

  6. Why can the Born-Oppenheimer approximation be used? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Nuclei are much heavier and slower than electrons. Electrons adjust almost instantly to nuclear positions.

  7. Why is symmetry useful in spectroscopy? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    It determines which vibrations and transitions are allowed. Selection rules follow from symmetry, so it decides which bands appear.

  8. Why does s-p mixing change the MO order in N₂ but not in O₂? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    The 2s and 2p energies are closer in lighter atoms, allowing mixing. Larger s-p gaps in O and F suppress mixing.

  9. Why are approximations essential in quantum chemistry? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Exact solutions exist only for one-electron systems. Electron-electron repulsion makes exact solution impossible.

Key takeaways

  • Molecular structure is defined by bond lengths, angles and torsion angles and classified by symmetry into point groups, which govern polarity, chirality and spectroscopic activity; diffraction, spectroscopy and computation determine structures.
  • Atomic orbitals combine into bonding and antibonding MOs; bond order is half the difference in electrons, O₂'s two unpaired π* electrons make it paramagnetic and Hückel theory describes conjugated π systems, whose HOMO-LUMO gap narrows with conjugation.
  • Quantum chemistry describes electrons as wavefunctions with quantised energy; the Born-Oppenheimer approximation separates electronic and nuclear motion and methods such as Hartree-Fock and DFT approximate many-electron systems.
  • The time-independent Schrödinger equation Ĥψ = Eψ gives allowed wavefunctions and energies; acceptable wavefunctions must be single-valued, continuous, finite and normalisable, and boundary conditions lead to quantisation, as in the hydrogen atom.
  • In the particle-in-a-box model, Eₙ = n²h²/8mL², with nonzero zero-point energy; energy gaps grow as the box shrinks, explaining colour in conjugated dyes and size-dependent emission from quantum dots.
  • The harmonic oscillator gives equally spaced vibrational levels Eᵥ = (v + ½)hν with frequency depending on force constant and reduced mass; real bonds are anharmonic, with converging levels, overtones and dissociation described by the Morse potential.

Common mistakes in Physical Chemistry and Molecular Science

  • Thinking molecules are rigid with fixed bond lengths. Vibration means measured values are averages.
  • Using Lewis structures to predict magnetism. They fail for O₂, which MO theory explains.
  • Thinking quantum chemistry solves molecules exactly. Only one-electron systems have exact solutions.
  • Thinking ψ itself is a probability. The probability density is |ψ|².
  • Thinking the particle can have zero energy. Confinement guarantees a minimum zero-point energy.
  • Thinking vibrational energy can be zero. The lowest level has zero-point energy ½hν.
