Lessons in this school

  1. Reversible reactions
    Many reactions can go in both directions.
  2. Dynamic equilibrium
    At dynamic equilibrium, forward and reverse reactions continue at equal rates.
  3. Equilibrium constants
    The equilibrium constant expresses the position of equilibrium as a number.
  4. Kc
    Kc is the equilibrium constant in terms of concentrations.
  5. Kp
    Kp is the equilibrium constant for gases in terms of partial pressures.
  6. Reaction quotients
    The reaction quotient Q describes a mixture that may not be at equilibrium.
  7. Le Chatelier's principle
    Le Chatelier's principle predicts how an equilibrium responds to change.
  8. Temperature and equilibrium
    Temperature is the only factor that changes the equilibrium constant.
  9. Pressure and equilibrium
    Pressure changes affect equilibria involving gases.
  10. Concentration and equilibrium
    Changing concentrations shifts the position of equilibrium.
  11. Heterogeneous equilibria
    Heterogeneous equilibria involve more than one phase.
  12. Equilibrium calculations
    Equilibrium calculations find unknown concentrations or constants.
  13. Industrial equilibrium
    Industry chooses conditions to make equilibrium reactions economical.
  14. Coupled equilibria
    Coupled equilibria share species, so a change in one affects the other.
  15. Thermodynamic interpretation
    Thermodynamics explains why equilibrium positions are where they are.

Key terms

Reversible reaction
A reaction that can go in both directions
Dynamic equilibrium
Forward and reverse rates equal with constant concentrations
Equilibrium constant
A ratio of equilibrium concentrations raised to their coefficients
Kc
Equilibrium constant in terms of concentration
Mole fraction
Moles of one gas divided by total moles
Reaction quotient
Q, the K expression using current concentrations
Le Chatelier's principle
A disturbed equilibrium shifts to oppose the change
Compromise temperature
A temperature balancing yield and rate
Gaseous equilibrium
An equilibrium involving gases
Excess reactant
A reactant added in more than the stoichiometric amount
Heterogeneous equilibrium
An equilibrium involving more than one phase
Percentage conversion
Fraction of reactant converted, as a percentage
Single-pass conversion
Proportion converted in one pass through a reactor
Coupled equilibria
Equilibria linked by shared species
