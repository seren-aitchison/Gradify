Five sample flashcards

What does inorganic chemistry study?

Elements and compounds other than most organic compounds. From “Introduction to inorganic chemistry” →

In plain English: Inorganic chemistry studies the elements and compounds other than most carbon compounds. Key idea: Inorganic chemistry covers all elements and their compounds other than most organic compounds, including main-group, transition metal, f-block, solid-state, organometallic and bioinorganic chemistry, and underpins catalysis, materials, medicines and biology.

What colour precipitate does Fe³⁺ give with NaOH?

Orange-brown. From “Transition metal chemistry” →

In plain English: This lesson explores characteristic reactions of transition metal ions, including precipitation, ligand exchange and redox. Key idea: Transition metal ions form coloured hydroxide precipitates, undergo ligand exchange with ammonia and chloride, changing colour and coordination, and take part in redox reactions such as Fe²⁺ to Fe³⁺, which are used in qualitative analysis.

Which isomer of [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] is an anticancer drug?

The cis isomer, cisplatin. From “Isomerism in complexes” →

In plain English: Complexes can show geometric and optical isomerism. Key idea: Complexes show cis-trans isomerism in square planar and octahedral geometries and optical isomerism when chiral, as with three bidentate ligands; isomerism matters in medicine, as cisplatin is active while transplatin is not.

What is the highest oxidation state of manganese?

+7. From “Oxidation states” →

In plain English: Transition metals show many oxidation states. Key idea: First-row transition metals show variable oxidation states rising to +7 at manganese; high states are stabilised by oxide and fluoride ligands, low states by π-acceptors, and stability also depends on pH and d-electron configuration.

What are the products of the chlor-alkali process?

Chlorine, hydrogen and sodium hydroxide. From “Industrial inorganic chemistry” →

In plain English: Inorganic chemicals are made on a vast scale. Key idea: Major industrial inorganic processes include the Haber, Contact and Ostwald processes and chlor-alkali electrolysis; compromise conditions, catalysts and recycling balance rate, yield, cost and safety.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Inorganic Chemistry

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which is an area of inorganic chemistry? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Coordination chemistry of transition metals. Coordination chemistry studies metal complexes.

  2. Which chloride is an ionic solid? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Sodium chloride. Sodium and chlorine form an ionic lattice.

  3. Which compound does sodium mainly form when burned in oxygen? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Sodium peroxide, Na₂O₂. Na⁺ is large enough to stabilise the peroxide ion.

  4. Why is inorganic chemistry important in biology? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Metal ions are essential in proteins such as haemoglobin. Iron, zinc and copper act in enzymes and carriers.

  5. Why does SiCl₄ hydrolyse while CCl₄ does not? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Silicon is larger and can accept a lone pair from water. Small carbon is shielded and cannot be attacked easily.

  6. Why does MgCO₃ decompose at a lower temperature than BaCO₃? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Mg²⁺ is smaller and polarises carbonate more strongly. Greater polarisation weakens the C–O bonds in carbonate.

  7. Why does iron appear in many areas of inorganic chemistry? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Its variable oxidation states and metallic properties suit many roles. Iron's chemistry spans alloys, catalysis, biology and corrosion.

  8. Why can't period 2 elements form hypervalent compounds? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    They are too small and lack accessible orbitals to hold more electrons. Small atoms cannot accommodate more than four electron pairs.

  9. Why does lithium carbonate decompose like a group 2 carbonate? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    The small Li⁺ ion has a high charge density. High charge density polarises carbonate, as Mg²⁺ does.

Key takeaways

  • Inorganic chemistry covers all elements and their compounds other than most organic compounds, including main-group, transition metal, f-block, solid-state, organometallic and bioinorganic chemistry, and underpins catalysis, materials, medicines and biology.
  • Main-group hydrides, oxides and chlorides change from ionic on the left to covalent on the right of a period, covalent chlorides hydrolyse in water and heavier elements form hypervalent compounds that exceed the octet.
  • s-block metals form oxides, peroxides and superoxides as cation size increases; carbonate and nitrate stability increases down group 2 because smaller cations polarise the anions more, and lithium and beryllium are anomalous due to high charge density.
  • p-block compounds include silicates built from SiO₄ tetrahedra, electron-deficient boranes with three-centre bonds, zeolites, oxoacids whose strength rises with more oxygens and interhalogens that act as fluorinating agents.
  • Transition metal ions form coloured hydroxide precipitates, undergo ligand exchange with ammonia and chloride, changing colour and coordination, and take part in redox reactions such as Fe²⁺ to Fe³⁺, which are used in qualitative analysis.
  • Coordination compounds have a central metal ion bonded to ligands within a coordination sphere balanced by counter-ions; IUPAC names list ligands alphabetically with prefixes, anionic complexes end in -ate and the metal's oxidation state is given in Roman numerals.

Common mistakes in Inorganic Chemistry

  • Thinking inorganic chemistry only covers metals. It includes non-metals and their compounds too.
  • Thinking all chlorides simply dissolve in water. Covalent chlorides such as SiCl₄ react with it.
  • Thinking all group 1 metals form the same oxide. Lithium, sodium and potassium form different oxygen compounds.
  • Thinking all bonds are two-centre two-electron bonds. Boranes contain three-centre two-electron bonds.
  • Thinking all metal hydroxide precipitates are permanent. Some dissolve in excess ammonia or hydroxide.
  • Forgetting that anionic complexes take the -ate ending. For example, [Fe(CN)₆]³⁻ is a ferrate.

Inorganic Chemistry: questions students ask

What happens when SiCl₄ is added to water?

It hydrolyses to SiO₂ and HCl.

What does potassium form when burned in excess oxygen?

Potassium superoxide, KO₂.

What is the basic unit of silicates?

The SiO₄ tetrahedron.

What forms when excess ammonia is added to Cu(OH)₂?

Deep blue [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺.
