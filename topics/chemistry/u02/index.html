Lessons in this school

  1. Structure of the atom
    An atom is a tiny, dense, positive nucleus surrounded by a much larger cloud of electrons.
  2. Protons, neutrons and electrons
    Atoms are built from three particles with very different masses and charges.
  3. Isotopes
    Isotopes are atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons.
  4. Atomic number and mass number
    Two numbers identify any nucleus: the atomic number, which fixes the element, and the mass number, which identifies the isotope.
  5. Electron shells
    Electrons in atoms occupy shells at increasing distances and energies from the nucleus.
  6. Atomic orbitals
    Within each shell, electrons occupy orbitals: regions where an electron is likely to be found.
  7. Quantum numbers
    Four quantum numbers describe each electron in an atom: which shell, which type of orbital, which orientation and which spin.
  8. Electron configurations
    An electron configuration lists the subshells an atom's electrons occupy and how many are in each, such as 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s¹ for sodium.
  9. Aufbau principle
    The aufbau ("building up") principle says electrons fill the lowest-energy orbitals available first.
  10. Pauli exclusion principle
    The Pauli exclusion principle says no two electrons in an atom can have the same four quantum numbers.
  11. Hund's rule
    Hund's rule says that when electrons fill a set of orbitals of equal energy, they occupy them singly first, with parallel spins, before pairing up.
  12. Ionisation energy
    Ionisation energy is the energy needed to remove electrons from atoms.
  13. Electron affinity
    Electron affinity describes the energy change when atoms gain electrons.
  14. Atomic spectra
    When atoms are given energy, they emit light at only certain wavelengths, producing a line spectrum unique to each element.
  15. Emission and absorption
    Atoms emit light at particular wavelengths when excited electrons fall back, and absorb the same wavelengths when electrons are lifted up.
  16. Wave-particle duality
    Light and electrons both behave sometimes like waves and sometimes like particles.
  17. Quantum mechanical model
    The quantum mechanical model treats the electron in an atom as a wave described by the Schrödinger equation.
  18. Atomic orbitals and shapes
    Each type of orbital has a characteristic shape: s orbitals are spherical, p orbitals have two lobes, and d orbitals mostly have four.
  19. Periodic trends
    Atomic radius, ionisation energy and electronegativity change in regular ways across periods and down groups.
  20. Electronic structure and chemical properties
    An element's chemistry follows from its electron structure.

Key terms

Nucleus
The tiny, dense, positively charged centre of an atom, containing protons and neutrons
Atomic number
The number of protons in the nucleus of an atom (Z)
Isotope
An atom of an element with a different number of neutrons from other atoms of that element
Nuclide
An atom with a specific number of protons and neutrons, written with its mass and atomic numbers
Electron shell
A group of electron energy levels with the same principal quantum number n
Atomic orbital
A region around the nucleus where there is a high probability of finding an electron of a particular energy
Principal quantum number
n, the quantum number for an orbital's shell, size and main energy
Electron configuration
The arrangement of an atom's electrons in subshells, written as 1s² 2s² and so on
Aufbau principle
Electrons fill the lowest-energy orbitals available first
Pauli exclusion principle
No two electrons in an atom can have the same four quantum numbers
Hund's rule
Electrons occupy degenerate orbitals singly with parallel spins before pairing
First ionisation energy
The energy to remove one mole of electrons from one mole of gaseous atoms to form one mole of gaseous 1+ ions
First electron affinity
The enthalpy change when each atom in one mole of gaseous atoms gains an electron to form 1− ions
Photon
A packet of light energy, E = hf
