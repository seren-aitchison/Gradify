Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What is the difference between a hazard and a risk?

A hazard can cause harm; risk is how likely and severe that harm is. From “Laboratory safety” →

In plain English: Safe laboratory work protects people and results. Key idea: Laboratory safety relies on recognising hazards from pictograms and safety data sheets, carrying out risk assessments with a hierarchy of controls and using protective equipment, fume cupboards and emergency procedures.

What sets the temperature of a reflux?

The boiling point of the solvent. From “Reflux” →

In plain English: Heating under reflux lets reactions run hot for long periods without losing solvent. Key idea: Reflux heats a reaction mixture in a flask with a vertical condenser so vapour returns, preventing solvent loss, keeping volatile substances in the flask and holding the temperature near the solvent's boiling point.

What pushes solvent through a flash column?

Applied gas pressure. From “Chromatographic purification” →

In plain English: Chromatography can purify compounds as well as analyse them. Key idea: Chromatographic purification separates compounds on a stationary phase at preparative scale using flash columns, preparative TLC, preparative HPLC, ion exchange or size exclusion, chosen by scale, separation difficulty and compound properties.

What does elemental analysis compare?

Measured %C, %H and %N with calculated values. From “Purity assessment” →

In plain English: Chemists must check that products are pure. Key idea: Purity is shown by sharp melting points matching literature, constant boiling points, single spots or peaks in TLC, GC or HPLC, clean NMR spectra and elemental analysis or HRMS agreeing with expected values, combining several methods.

What is telescoping?

Carrying crude product into the next step without isolating it. From “Multi-step synthesis” →

In plain English: This lesson looks at the practical management of multi-step syntheses in the laboratory. Key idea: Multi-step synthesis requires characterising intermediates, making enough material early, telescoping steps where possible, keeping records and troubleshooting failures by checking reagents, monitoring and testing conditions on a small scale.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Synthetic Chemistry and Laboratory Methods

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which document gives detailed handling information for a chemical? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    A safety data sheet from the supplier. Safety data sheets list hazards, handling, storage and first aid.

  2. In a rate experiment varying temperature, what is the independent variable? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    The temperature of the reaction mixture. The independent variable is the one deliberately changed.

  3. Which apparatus measures 25.00 cm³ of liquid most precisely? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    A volumetric pipette. Volumetric pipettes deliver fixed volumes very precisely.

  4. Why is a water bath used instead of a Bunsen burner to heat ethanol? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    It avoids an open flame near flammable vapour. Removing ignition sources controls the fire risk.

  5. Why is a control experiment included? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    It shows whether the effect is really caused by the variable studied. Comparing with a control rules out other causes.

  6. Why should unused reagent never go back into the stock bottle? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    It may have been contaminated and would spoil the stock. Contamination of the stock affects all later users.

  7. Why is substitution placed above protective equipment in the hierarchy of controls? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    It reduces the hazard itself rather than relying on people using equipment. Removing hazards at source is more reliable than barriers.

  8. Why might a chemist run a small pilot experiment first? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    To find practical problems before committing time and materials. Early small tests reveal practical issues cheaply before a full run.

  9. Why are oxidisers stored away from flammable solvents? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Mixing could cause fire or explosion if containers leak. Separating incompatibles prevents dangerous reactions.

Key takeaways

  • Laboratory safety relies on recognising hazards from pictograms and safety data sheets, carrying out risk assessments with a hierarchy of controls and using protective equipment, fume cupboards and emergency procedures.
  • Experimental planning identifies independent, dependent and control variables, includes controls and repeats, uses suitable apparatus and scale and sets out a clear method with a risk assessment.
  • Reagents are measured with apparatus matched to the needed precision, weighed by difference, kept uncontaminated and labelled; sensitive reagents are handled under dry or inert conditions and incompatible chemicals are stored apart.
  • Reactions are set up in suitable glassware such as round-bottomed flasks with condensers and dropping funnels, with heating, cooling, stirring and slow addition used to control conditions, and apparatus is clamped and never sealed when heated.
  • Reflux heats a reaction mixture in a flask with a vertical condenser so vapour returns, preventing solvent loss, keeping volatile substances in the flask and holding the temperature near the solvent's boiling point.
  • Simple distillation separates liquids from solids or widely different boiling points, fractional distillation separates close-boiling liquids using a column and vacuum and steam distillation protect heat-sensitive or isolate volatile water-insoluble compounds.

Common mistakes in Synthetic Chemistry and Laboratory Methods

  • Relying only on gloves and goggles. Removing or substituting hazards comes first in the hierarchy of controls.
  • Changing more than one variable at once. It becomes impossible to tell which caused the effect.
  • Pouring unused reagent back into the bottle. This can contaminate the whole stock.
  • Heating a sealed system. Pressure can build up and break the glassware.
  • Stoppering the top of the reflux condenser. The system must stay open to the air.
  • Placing the thermometer bulb in the liquid. It must read the vapour at the side-arm.
