Lessons in this school

  1. Curly arrow notation
    Curly arrows are the language of organic mechanisms.
  2. Electrophiles and nucleophiles
    Nucleophiles and electrophiles drive most polar reactions.
  3. Homolytic and heterolytic cleavage
    Bonds break in two different ways.
  4. Carbocations
    Carbocations are positively charged carbon intermediates.
  5. Carbanions
    Carbanions are negatively charged carbon species.
  6. Radical intermediates
    Radicals are species with unpaired electrons.
  7. Reaction energy profiles
    Energy profiles show how energy changes along a reaction.
  8. Substitution reactions
    Substitution reactions replace one group with another.
  9. SN1 mechanisms
    SN1 reactions proceed through a carbocation.
  10. SN2 mechanisms
    SN2 reactions occur in one concerted step.
  11. Elimination reactions
    Elimination reactions remove atoms to form multiple bonds.
  12. E1 mechanisms
    E1 eliminations proceed through a carbocation.
  13. E2 mechanisms
    E2 eliminations occur in one concerted step.
  14. Addition reactions
    Addition reactions join atoms across multiple bonds.
  15. Electrophilic addition
    This lesson details the mechanisms of electrophilic addition to alkenes, including bromination and hydration.
  16. Nucleophilic addition
    Aldehydes and ketones undergo nucleophilic addition at the carbonyl carbon.
  17. Electrophilic aromatic substitution
    This lesson details the mechanism of electrophilic aromatic substitution and how electrophiles are generated.
  18. Nucleophilic aromatic substitution
    Aromatic rings can undergo nucleophilic substitution when activated.
  19. Oxidation reactions
    Oxidation in organic chemistry increases bonds to oxygen or decreases bonds to hydrogen.
  20. Reduction reactions
    Reduction in organic chemistry adds hydrogen or removes oxygen.
  21. Condensation reactions
    Condensation reactions join molecules with loss of a small molecule.
  22. Hydrolysis
    Hydrolysis splits molecules using water.
  23. Rearrangements
    Rearrangements change a molecule's carbon skeleton or connectivity.
  24. Reaction selectivity
    Selectivity describes which of several possible products forms.
  25. Chemoselectivity
    Chemoselective reactions change one functional group while leaving others.
  26. Regioselectivity
    Regioselective reactions favour one position or orientation.
  27. Stereoselectivity
    Stereoselective reactions favour one stereoisomer.

Key terms

Curly arrow
A symbol showing electron movement in a mechanism
Nucleophilicity
How quickly a nucleophile reacts
Homolytic cleavage
Bond breaking giving one electron to each atom
Carbocation
A carbon intermediate with a positive charge
Carbanion
A carbon species with a negative charge and lone pair
Radical
A species with an unpaired electron
Transition state
The highest-energy point of a step
Leaving group
The group that departs with the bonding electrons
SN1
Unimolecular nucleophilic substitution
SN2
Bimolecular nucleophilic substitution
Elimination
Removal of atoms to form a multiple bond
E1
Unimolecular elimination
E2
Bimolecular elimination
Addition reaction
Two molecules combine to form one product
