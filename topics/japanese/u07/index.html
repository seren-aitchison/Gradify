Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What is the potential form of はなす (hanasu, speak)?

はなせる (hanaseru). From “The potential form” →

In plain English: The potential form says what someone can do. Key idea: Ru-verbs add られる, u-verbs change u to e and add る, する becomes できる and くる becomes こられる; the object usually takes が, and casual speech often drops ら from ru-verb potentials.

How do you say "I can ski"?

スキーが できます (sukī ga dekimasu). From “Koto ga dekiru” →

In plain English: ～ことが できる is another way to say "can". Key idea: Dictionary form + ことが できます and noun + が できます express ability or possibility, meaning the same as the potential form but sounding slightly more formal; できる also means "be completed".

How do you say "If you don't hurry, you'll be late"?

いそがないと、おくれます (isoganai to, okuremasu). From “The to conditional” →

In plain English: The と conditional expresses natural or automatic consequences: whenever A happens, B follows. Key idea: Plain non-past + と expresses natural or automatic consequences, habits, machines and directions; it is not followed by requests, suggestions or intentions, which need たら, and with a past result it can show discovery.

Which conditional is usually safest when unsure?

たら (tara). From “Choosing a conditional” →

In plain English: Japanese has four main conditionals: と, ば, たら and なら. Key idea: と is for automatic results, ば for conditions and advice, たら is the most flexible and works before requests, and なら responds to the listener's topic or plans; たら is usually safest when unsure.

Which particle marks the doer in a passive sentence?

に (ni). From “The passive form” →

In plain English: The passive form shows that something is done to the subject. Key idea: Ru-verbs add られる, u-verbs change u to a and add れる, する becomes される and くる こられる; the affected person is the subject, the doer takes に, impersonal passives are common in news, and によって marks creators.

Quick quiz: test yourself on N4 Grammar: Ability, Intention and Conditions

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. What is the potential form of のむ (nomu, drink)? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    のめる. U-verbs change the final u sound to e and add る, so のむ becomes のめる.

  2. What is the volitional form of たべる (taberu, eat)? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    たべよう. Ru-verbs drop る and add よう, so たべる becomes たべよう.

  3. How do you say "I intend to study medicine" (igaku o benkyō suru tsumori desu)? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    いがくを べんきょうする つもりです. つもり follows the dictionary form of the verb.

  4. Which particle normally marks the object of a potential verb, as in にほんご__ はなせます (nihongo __ hanasemasu)? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    が. Potential verbs usually take が for their object in standard Japanese.

  5. How do you say "I'm thinking of buying a new phone" (kaō to omotte imasu)? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    あたらしい スマホを かおうと おもって います. The volitional of かう is かおう, followed by と おもって います.

  6. Which expression suits a scheduled meeting (kaigi wa sanji kara no yotei desu)? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    よてい. よてい describes arranged schedules, including meetings.

  7. What is たべれる (tabereru)? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    A colloquial ra-less potential of たべる. Dropping ら from たべられる is common in casual speech but non-standard.

  8. How does ～ようと おもって います (yō to omotte imasu) differ from つもりです (tsumori desu)? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    It sounds softer and less fixed, like a plan still being considered. The volitional with と おもって います suggests a plan you are still thinking about.

  9. Which sentence expresses an intention not to do something (sumanai tsumori desu)? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    もう たばこを すわない つもりです. The nai-form before つもり expresses an intention not to do something.

Key takeaways

  • Ru-verbs add られる, u-verbs change u to e and add る, する becomes できる and くる becomes こられる; the object usually takes が, and casual speech often drops ら from ru-verb potentials.
  • The volitional is made with よう for ru-verbs, o + う for u-verbs, しよう and こよう; it is the casual "let's", and volitional + と おもって います means "I'm thinking of doing".
  • Dictionary form + つもりです means firm intention, ない つもり and つもりは ありません give negative intentions, and つもりでした means an intention that did not happen; よてい is for scheduled plans and ～ようと おもって います for plans being considered.
  • Dictionary form + ことが できます and noun + が できます express ability or possibility, meaning the same as the potential form but sounding slightly more formal; できる also means "be completed".
  • Dictionary or potential form + ように なりました describes gradual change, なく なりました describes stopping, and dictionary or ない-form + ように して います describes deliberate effort; ～ように して ください asks people to make sure to do something.
  • Plain form + はずです means "should be, is expected to", with な for な-adjectives and の for nouns; はずが ありません means "cannot possibly be", ない はずです means "is expected not to" and はずでした means "was supposed to".

Common mistakes in N4 Grammar: Ability, Intention and Conditions

  • Using を routinely with potential verbs in exams, as in にほんごを はなせます. Textbook standard is が: にほんごが はなせます.
  • Saying いくと おもって います for "I'm thinking of going". Use the volitional: いこうと おもって います.
  • Using the masu form before つもり, as in いきます つもりです. Use the dictionary form: いく つもりです.
  • Using the masu form before こと, as in のりますことが できます. Use the dictionary form: のることが できます.
  • Using ように なる for a sudden one-off event. It describes gradual change, not a single action.
  • Using はず for your own intentions, as in わたしは いく はずです for "I intend to go". Use つもりです for intentions.

Lessons in this school

  1. The potential form
    The potential form says what someone can do.
  2. The volitional form and to omotte imasu
    The volitional form is the casual way to say "let's" and, with と おもって います, expresses what you are thinking of doing.
  3. Tsumori desu: plans
    つもりです expresses a firm intention.
  4. Koto ga dekiru
    ～ことが できる is another way to say "can".
  5. You ni naru and you ni suru
    ～ように なる describes gradual change in ability or habit, while ～ように する describes making an effort.
  6. Hazu desu: expectation
    はずです expresses what the speaker expects to be true based on information or logic.
  7. The to conditional
    The と conditional expresses natural or automatic consequences: whenever A happens, B follows.
  8. The ba conditional
    The ば conditional expresses hypothetical conditions, often focusing on what is needed for a result.
  9. The tara conditional
    The たら conditional is the most flexible Japanese conditional, meaning "if" or "when".
  10. Nara: if that is the case
    なら means "if that is the case" or "if it's about…".
  11. Choosing a conditional
    Japanese has four main conditionals: と, ば, たら and なら.
  12. Ageru, kureru and morau
    Japanese has three verbs for giving and receiving, chosen by who gives to whom.
  13. Te ageru, te kureru and te morau
    The giving verbs combine with the て-form to show who does a favour for whom.
  14. The passive form
    The passive form shows that something is done to the subject.
  15. The suffering passive
    Japanese has an indirect passive that shows the subject was negatively affected by something.
  16. The causative form
    The causative form means "make" or "let" someone do something.
  17. The causative-passive
    The causative-passive means "be made to do" something, usually unwillingly.
