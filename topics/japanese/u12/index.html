Which is the easiest JLPT level?

N5. From “The JLPT: levels and format” →

In plain English: The Japanese-Language Proficiency Test is the most widely taken Japanese exam in the world. Key idea: The JLPT has five levels from N5 to N1, multiple-choice sections for language knowledge, reading and listening, a 180-point scale with section minimums, and no speaking or writing.

What is the difference between びょういん and びよういん?

びょういん is a hospital; びよういん is a beauty salon. From “Listening strategies” →

In plain English: Listening exams test understanding at natural speed. Key idea: Read questions first, predict answers, listen for key words, numbers and time expressions, take brief notes and watch for distractors, changes of mind signalled by でも or やっぱり and similar sounds such as びょういん and びよういん.

What should you do if you do not understand the teacher in a role play?

Ask もう いちど いって ください. From “Speaking: role plays” →

In plain English: GCSE speaking role plays test your ability to handle everyday situations. Key idea: GCSE role plays use prompts in set situations, with an unexpected question and a required question at Higher; cover every prompt, use polite masu forms, prepare question patterns and prioritise clear communication.

Which script is used for loanwords such as tennis?

Katakana. From “Writing: short texts in kana and kanji” →

In plain English: Short writing tasks test accurate use of kana, kanji and basic grammar. Key idea: Short writing tasks require covering every bullet point with simple, accurate sentences, correct kana including long vowels and small kana, katakana for loanwords, correct particles and accurate kanji, using hiragana when unsure.

Where does the verb go in a Japanese translation?

At the end of the sentence. From “Translation into Japanese” →

In plain English: Translating from English into Japanese tests accurate grammar, word order and script. Key idea: Translate into Japanese by identifying time, people, place, object and verb, arranging them in Japanese order with the verb last, choosing correct tense, politeness and particles and checking verb and adjective forms, katakana and kanji.

Key takeaways

  • The JLPT has five levels from N5 to N1, multiple-choice sections for language knowledge, reading and listening, a 180-point scale with section minimums, and no speaking or writing.
  • GCSE Japanese has four equally weighted papers in listening, speaking, reading and writing, at Foundation or Higher tier, covering set themes; learners need kana and specified kanji and should revise all four skills regularly.
  • Read questions first, predict answers, listen for key words, numbers and time expressions, take brief notes and watch for distractors, changes of mind signalled by でも or やっぱり and similar sounds such as びょういん and びよういん.
  • Skim for main ideas and scan for details, read questions first, find evidence in the text, watch for paraphrase, negatives and connectors such as しかし and つまり, remember that Japanese conclusions often come at the end and manage time.
  • JLPT vocabulary questions include kanji reading, kanji writing, context, synonyms and usage; grammar questions include form choice, ★ sentence ordering and passage grammar; build ordering answers from natural chunks and watch for small sound differences.
  • GCSE role plays use prompts in set situations, with an unexpected question and a required question at Higher; cover every prompt, use polite masu forms, prepare question patterns and prioritise clear communication.

Common mistakes in Exam Skills: GCSE and JLPT

  • Assuming passing the JLPT shows speaking ability. It tests only reading, listening and language knowledge.
  • Revising only reading and writing. Each of the four skills is worth 25%.
  • Choosing the first option mentioned. Speakers often change their minds, so listen to the end.
  • Answering from general knowledge rather than the text. Answers must be based on what the text says.
  • Choosing a kanji reading option without checking long vowels and small っ. Distractors often differ only in these details.
  • Skipping a prompt you find difficult. Give a simple answer to every prompt instead.
