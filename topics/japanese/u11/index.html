Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

When did the Meiji Restoration begin?

In 1868. From “A short history of Japan” →

In plain English: Knowing the main periods of Japanese history helps you understand culture, place names and everyday references. Key idea: Japanese history runs from the Jōmon and Yayoi periods through Nara and Heian, the samurai governments and the Edo period to the Meiji Restoration of 1868, defeat in 1945, post-war growth and the Heisei and Reiwa eras.

What were man'yōgana?

Kanji used for their sounds to write Japanese. From “The history of the writing system” →

In plain English: The Japanese writing system developed over many centuries by adapting Chinese characters. Key idea: Kanji arrived from China and were used for sounds in man'yōgana; hiragana developed from cursive kanji and katakana from parts of kanji; post-war reforms set the 当用漢字 list, replaced by the 常用漢字 list of 1981 and 2010.

What is wabi-sabi?

An aesthetic valuing simplicity, imperfection and transience. From “The tea ceremony and traditional arts” →

In plain English: Traditional arts such as the tea ceremony, flower arranging, calligraphy and theatre express key Japanese aesthetic values. Key idea: The tea ceremony, developed by Sen no Rikyū, values harmony, respect, purity and tranquillity; other arts include ikebana, calligraphy, nō, kabuki and bunraku, and aesthetic ideas include wabi-sabi and ichigo ichie.

What is 就活 (shūkatsu)?

Job hunting by university students. From “Work culture and society” →

In plain English: Japanese work culture has distinctive traditions and is changing quickly. Key idea: Traditional practices include lifetime employment, seniority pay and group graduate hiring each April; long hours and karōshi led to the 2018 Work Style Reform laws, and work culture is changing with job-changing, more women working and non-regular employment.

What is 標準語 (hyōjungo)?

Standard Japanese, based on Tokyo speech. From “Dialects: Kansai-ben and standard Japanese” →

In plain English: Japanese has many regional dialects, and Kansai-ben is the most widely heard after standard Japanese. Key idea: Standard Japanese, based on Tokyo speech, is used in education and media; Kansai-ben uses や for だ, ～へん for negatives and words such as ほんま, あかん and おおきに, and dialects are now generally valued as local identity.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Japanese Culture and Society

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which period lasted from 1603 to 1868 under the Tokugawa shoguns? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    The Edo period. The Edo period was a long era of peace under the Tokugawa shogunate.

  2. What are kami (kami) in Shinto? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Spirits of nature, places and ancestors. Shinto centres on kami, spirits found in nature and places.

  3. How did katakana develop? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    From parts of kanji, used to annotate texts. Monks used parts of kanji as quick sound symbols, which became katakana.

  4. What was a major effect of the Meiji Restoration? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Rapid modernisation and adoption of Western institutions. From 1868, Japan modernised quickly and moved its capital to Tokyo.

  5. Which ceremony is most commonly Buddhist in Japan? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Funerals. Most Japanese funerals follow Buddhist rites.

  6. Which major work was written in hiragana by a Heian court woman? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    『源氏物語』 (The Tale of Genji). Murasaki Shikibu wrote The Tale of Genji around 1000.

  7. Why is knowing era names such as 令和 (Reiwa) useful today? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    They are still used for dates on official documents. Japanese dates often combine era names with year numbers.

  8. What is the usual way to pray at a Shinto shrine? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Bow twice, clap twice and bow once. 二礼二拍手一礼 is the common shrine prayer etiquette.

  9. Why is the mixed script system efficient for readers despite its complexity? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Different scripts signal word types and boundaries. Kanji, hiragana and katakana visually separate content, grammar and loanwords.

Key takeaways

  • Japanese history runs from the Jōmon and Yayoi periods through Nara and Heian, the samurai governments and the Edo period to the Meiji Restoration of 1868, defeat in 1945, post-war growth and the Heisei and Reiwa eras.
  • Shinto centres on kami at shrines marked by torii, and Buddhism on temples; many Japanese people take part in both as customs, from shichi-go-san and New Year visits to Buddhist funerals, following etiquette such as two bows, two claps and one bow at shrines.
  • Kanji arrived from China and were used for sounds in man'yōgana; hiragana developed from cursive kanji and katakana from parts of kanji; post-war reforms set the 当用漢字 list, replaced by the 常用漢字 list of 1981 and 2010.
  • Japan uses the Gregorian calendar alongside era names, with 令和 beginning in 2019; holiday periods include Golden Week and Obon, Coming of Age Day follows the 2022 change of adulthood to 18, and seasonal customs are strong.
  • On 大晦日 people clean, eat toshikoshi soba and hear temple bells rung 108 times; at お正月 they make hatsumōde visits, eat osechi and ozōni, send nengajō and give otoshidama, greeting with あけまして おめでとうございます.
  • The tea ceremony, developed by Sen no Rikyū, values harmony, respect, purity and tranquillity; other arts include ikebana, calligraphy, nō, kabuki and bunraku, and aesthetic ideas include wabi-sabi and ichigo ichie.

Common mistakes in Japanese Culture and Society

  • Thinking Tokyo has always been the capital. Kyoto was the imperial capital from 794 until the Meiji era.
  • Clapping at a Buddhist temple. Clapping is for shrines; at temples, put your hands together quietly.
  • Thinking hiragana and katakana were invented separately from kanji. Both developed from kanji.
  • Writing 令和1年 for 2019. The first year of an era is written 元年: 令和元年.
  • Saying あけまして おめでとうございます before New Year's Day. Before 1 January, say よい おとしを.
  • Gulping tea quickly at a tea ceremony. Each movement is deliberate, and guests drink calmly after turning the bowl.

Lessons in this school

  1. A short history of Japan
    Knowing the main periods of Japanese history helps you understand culture, place names and everyday references.
  2. Shinto and Buddhism in everyday life
    Shinto and Buddhism both shape Japanese customs, often side by side.
  3. The history of the writing system
    The Japanese writing system developed over many centuries by adapting Chinese characters.
  4. Festivals and the Japanese calendar
    The Japanese calendar combines the Western calendar, era names and seasonal customs.
  5. New Year in Japan
    New Year (お正月) is the most important holiday in Japan, a time for family, food and fresh beginnings.
  6. The tea ceremony and traditional arts
    Traditional arts such as the tea ceremony, flower arranging, calligraphy and theatre express key Japanese aesthetic values.
  7. Gift-giving, bowing and etiquette
    Etiquette shapes everyday interactions in Japan, from giving gifts to exchanging business cards.
  8. Anime, manga and popular culture
    Anime, manga, games and music have made Japanese popular culture famous worldwide.
  9. Work culture and society
    Japanese work culture has distinctive traditions and is changing quickly.
  10. An ageing society
    Japan has one of the oldest populations in the world and a falling birth rate.
  11. Japanese cities and regions
    Japan's regions differ in climate, food and character.
  12. Dialects: Kansai-ben and standard Japanese
    Japanese has many regional dialects, and Kansai-ben is the most widely heard after standard Japanese.
  13. Washoku: Japanese food culture
    Washoku, traditional Japanese food culture, is recognised by UNESCO for its respect for nature and seasons.
  14. Japan and the world
    Japan plays a major role in the world through its economy, culture, diplomacy and tourism.

What is shichi-go-san?

A shrine visit on 15 November for children aged three, five and seven.

When is Golden Week?

From late April to early May.

What is eaten on New Year's Eve?

年越しそば (toshikoshi soba).
