Lessons in this school

  1. Te shimau: completion and regret
    ～て しまう adds the meaning of completing something fully or doing something regrettable.
  2. Te oku: doing in advance
    ～て おく means doing something in advance or leaving something in a certain state.
  3. Te aru: deliberate results
    ～て ある describes a state that someone deliberately created.
  4. Te iku and te kuru
    ～て いく and ～て くる add direction in space or time.
  5. Te miru: trying
    ～て みる means trying something to see what it is like.
  6. Sou desu: hearsay
    Plain form + そうです reports information heard from others.
  7. Sou desu: it looks like
    Stem + そうです describes how something looks or what seems about to happen, based on what you can see.
  8. Rashii
    らしい has two meanings: "it seems" based on indirect evidence, and "typical of" a type.
  9. You da and mitai da
    ようだ and みたいだ express that something seems so, based on the speaker's judgement, and make comparisons.
  10. Kamoshirenai and darou
    かもしれない and だろう express possibility and probability.
  11. Noni: despite and in order to
    のに has two main uses: "despite" with a feeling of surprise or frustration, and "in order to" for purpose.
  12. Tame ni and you ni: purpose
    ために and ように both express purpose, but they are used with different kinds of verbs.
  13. Bakari and tokoro
    ばかり and ところ add precise timing and emphasis.
  14. Wake and hazu: reasoning
    わけ and はず help express reasoning, conclusions and explanations.
  15. Nagara and aida ni
    ながら and あいだ express actions happening at the same time.
  16. Shika and dake
    しか and だけ both mean "only", but しか needs a negative verb and adds a feeling of "not enough".
  17. Sentence-ending particles ne, yo and yo ne
    Sentence-ending particles such as ね, よ and よね shape how a statement relates to the listener.
