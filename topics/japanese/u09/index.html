Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What are the two meanings of ～て しまう (te shimau)?

Completing an action fully, and doing something regrettable. From “Te shimau: completion and regret” →

In plain English: ～て しまう adds the meaning of completing something fully or doing something regrettable. Key idea: ～て しまう means completing something fully or doing something regrettable or unintended, especially with わすれる and なくす; casual speech shortens it to ～ちゃう and ～じゃう.

What does あめが ふって きました (ame ga futte kimashita) mean?

It has started to rain. From “Te iku and te kuru” →

In plain English: ～て いく and ～て くる add direction in space or time. Key idea: ～て いく and ～て くる show movement away from or towards the speaker and change from now on or up to now; ～て くる also shows something starting, such as あめが ふって きました.

What is the "looks" form of いい (ii, good)?

よさそう (yosasō). From “Sou desu: it looks like” →

In plain English: Stem + そうです describes how something looks or what seems about to happen, based on what you can see. Key idea: い-adjective stem + そう and な-adjective + そう mean "looks", with いい and ない becoming よさそう and なさそう; masu stem + そう means something seems about to happen; this そう is based on observation, unlike hearsay.

How do you say "Even though it's Sunday, I have work"?

にちようびなのに、しごとが あります (nichiyōbi na noni, shigoto ga arimasu). From “Noni: despite and in order to” →

In plain English: のに has two main uses: "despite" with a feeling of surprise or frustration, and "in order to" for purpose. Key idea: Plain form + のに means "despite", often with frustration or surprise, with な after nouns and な-adjectives; dictionary form + のに means "in order to" with words such as べんり, かかる and ひつよう.

How does わけ differ from はず?

わけ explains known facts; はず is an expectation before the result. From “Wake and hazu: reasoning” →

In plain English: わけ and はず help express reasoning, conclusions and explanations. Key idea: わけです draws a conclusion from known facts, わけではありません means "it's not that", わけがありません means "there's no way"; はず is an expectation before the result, while わけ explains known facts.

Quick quiz: test yourself on N3 Grammar: Nuance and Viewpoint

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. What does さいふを なくして しまいました (saifu o nakushite shimaimashita) express? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Regret at losing a wallet. ～て しまう with なくす expresses an unfortunate result the speaker regrets.

  2. What does きっぷを かって おきます (kippu o katte okimasu) mean? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    I'll buy the ticket in advance. ～て おく shows buying the ticket as preparation for later.

  3. What does まどが あけて あります (mado ga akete arimasu) mean? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    The window has been opened on purpose and is open. ～て ある with the transitive あける shows a deliberate result.

  4. What is the casual form of たべて しまった (tabete shimatta)? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    たべちゃった. ～て しまった shortens to ～ちゃった in casual speech.

  5. How do you ask someone to leave the light on (denki o tsukete oite kudasai)? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    でんきを つけて おいて ください. ～て おいて ください asks someone to leave something in a state.

  6. Which sentence describes an open window without implying anyone opened it (mado ga aite imasu)? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    まどが あいて います. ～て いる with the intransitive あく describes the state only.

  7. How does でんしゃに わすれものを して しまいました (wasuremono o shite shimaimashita) differ from ～を しました? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    It shows the speaker did not want the result. ～て しまう adds a feeling of regret about the result.

  8. Which sentence best expresses preparing for guests (kaimono o shite okimashita)? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    おきゃくさんの ために、かいものを して おきました. ～て おく expresses doing something beforehand for a later purpose.

  9. What is the relationship between ～て おく (te oku) and ～て ある (te aru)? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    ～て おく is the preparing action; ～て ある is the resulting state. Preparing in advance with ～て おく leaves a state described with ～て ある.

Key takeaways

  • ～て しまう means completing something fully or doing something regrettable or unintended, especially with わすれる and なくす; casual speech shortens it to ～ちゃう and ～じゃう.
  • ～て おく means doing something in advance, often with まえに or ために, or leaving something in a state; ～て おいて ください asks for preparation, and casual speech uses ～とく and ～どく.
  • Transitive te-form + ある describes a state deliberately created, with the object marked by が; ～て いる with intransitive verbs describes a state without implying an agent, and ～て ある is the result of ～て おく.
  • ～て いく and ～て くる show movement away from or towards the speaker and change from now on or up to now; ～て くる also shows something starting, such as あめが ふって きました.
  • て-form + みる means trying something to see what it is like, ～て みたいです means wanting to try and ～て みて ください invites someone to try; ～ようと する means attempting with effort, often unsuccessfully.
  • Plain form + そうです reports hearsay, with だ after nouns and な-adjectives; ～に よると gives the source, casual speech uses ～って, and the "looks like" そうです attaches to stems instead.

Common mistakes in N3 Grammar: Nuance and Viewpoint

  • Using ～て しまう for neutral events with no nuance. It adds completion or regret, so use the plain past for neutral facts.
  • Confusing ～て おく with ～て いる. ～て おく focuses on preparing for a later purpose.
  • Using ～て ある with intransitive verbs, as in まどが あいて あります. Use the transitive verb: あけて あります.
  • Using ～て いく for change up to the present. Change up to now uses ～て くる.
  • Using ～て みる for effortful attempts that fail, as in ドアを あけて みましたが、あきませんでした. This is acceptable, but ～ようと しましたが stresses the effort better.
  • Saying しずかそうです to report hearsay. Hearsay needs だ: しずかだそうです. しずかそうです means "it looks quiet".

Lessons in this school

  1. Te shimau: completion and regret
    ～て しまう adds the meaning of completing something fully or doing something regrettable.
  2. Te oku: doing in advance
    ～て おく means doing something in advance or leaving something in a certain state.
  3. Te aru: deliberate results
    ～て ある describes a state that someone deliberately created.
  4. Te iku and te kuru
    ～て いく and ～て くる add direction in space or time.
  5. Te miru: trying
    ～て みる means trying something to see what it is like.
  6. Sou desu: hearsay
    Plain form + そうです reports information heard from others.
  7. Sou desu: it looks like
    Stem + そうです describes how something looks or what seems about to happen, based on what you can see.
  8. Rashii
    らしい has two meanings: "it seems" based on indirect evidence, and "typical of" a type.
  9. You da and mitai da
    ようだ and みたいだ express that something seems so, based on the speaker's judgement, and make comparisons.
  10. Kamoshirenai and darou
    かもしれない and だろう express possibility and probability.
  11. Noni: despite and in order to
    のに has two main uses: "despite" with a feeling of surprise or frustration, and "in order to" for purpose.
  12. Tame ni and you ni: purpose
    ために and ように both express purpose, but they are used with different kinds of verbs.
  13. Bakari and tokoro
    ばかり and ところ add precise timing and emphasis.
  14. Wake and hazu: reasoning
    わけ and はず help express reasoning, conclusions and explanations.
  15. Nagara and aida ni
    ながら and あいだ express actions happening at the same time.
  16. Shika and dake
    しか and だけ both mean "only", but しか needs a negative verb and adds a feeling of "not enough".
  17. Sentence-ending particles ne, yo and yo ne
    Sentence-ending particles such as ね, よ and よね shape how a statement relates to the listener.
