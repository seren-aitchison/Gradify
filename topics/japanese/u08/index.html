Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What is そんけいご (sonkeigo)?

Respectful language that raises the actions of others. From “Plain, polite and honorific speech” →

In plain English: Japanese speakers adjust their language to the situation and the listener. Key idea: Japanese has plain speech, polite masu speech and keigo; keigo includes respectful そんけいご for others' actions, humble けんじょうご for your own and polite ていねいご such as です, ます and ございます.

Which prefix usually goes with なまえ (namae, name)?

お, as in おなまえ. From “Beautifying o and go” →

In plain English: The prefixes お and ご make words sound more polite or refined. Key idea: お and ご beautify words and show respect for others' things and actions; お usually goes with native Japanese words and ご with Sino-Japanese words, with exceptions such as おでんわ, and they are not used for your own things in the respectful sense.

How do you say "I'm glad to meet you" humbly?

おめに かかれて うれしいです (ome ni kakarete ureshii desu). From “Kenjougo: humble verbs” →

In plain English: Humble language lowers your own actions to show respect for the listener. Key idea: Kenjōgo uses special verbs such as まいる, おる, いただく, もうす, はいけんする, いたす, さしあげる, うかがう and おめに かかる for your own actions; ～て おります replaces ～て います in formal speech, and humble verbs are never used for the listener.

What does かしこまりました (kashikomarimashita) mean?

Certainly, understood. From “Keigo in shops and restaurants” →

In plain English: Shop and restaurant staff in Japan use a set of fixed keigo phrases. Key idea: Staff use fixed keigo such as いらっしゃいませ, かしこまりました, しょうしょう おまち ください and おまたせ いたしました; customers reply in polite masu speech, and some manual keigo phrases such as ～に なります are non-standard.

What are クッションことば (kusshon kotoba)?

Cushion words that soften requests or refusals. From “Requests and refusals: indirectness” →

In plain English: Japanese communication often prefers indirectness, especially in requests and refusals. Key idea: Requests are softened with cushion words such as おそれいりますが and もうしわけ ありませんが and with reasons; refusals use ちょっと…, あいにく, apologies, reasons and phrases such as けんとう します or いたしかねます rather than a direct no.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Keigo and Social Register

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which speech level would you use with a close friend of the same age? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Plain speech with dictionary forms. Friends normally use plain speech with each other.

  2. Which group is そと (soto)? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    People outside your own group. そと means the out-group, such as clients and strangers.

  3. Which prefix normally goes with りょうしん (ryōshin, parents)? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    ご. りょうしん is a Sino-Japanese word, so it takes ご: ごりょうしん.

  4. Which type of keigo is used for a teacher's actions, as in せんせいが いらっしゃいます (sensei ga irasshaimasu)? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Respectful language, そんけいご. そんけいご raises the actions of the person you respect.

  5. How should a receptionist tell a customer that her boss Tanaka is out (Tanaka wa dekakete orimasu)? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    たなかは でかけて おります. To outsiders, in-group members are referred to humbly without さん.

  6. Which word is an exception that takes お despite being Sino-Japanese (o-denwa)? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    でんわ. でんわ is Sino-Japanese but usually becomes おでんわ.

  7. Why does a young shop assistant use keigo with an older customer and a younger customer alike? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Keigo follows the role of customer, not only age. Service roles call for keigo with all customers.

  8. Why is the same boss described with respectful language inside the company but humble language to clients? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    The in-group boundary shifts depending on who you are talking to. Uchi-soto depends on the listener's position relative to your group.

  9. Why is わたしの おなまえは (watashi no onamae wa) unnatural? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Respectful お is for others' things, not your own. Respectful prefixes show respect to others, not to yourself.

Key takeaways

  • Japanese has plain speech, polite masu speech and keigo; keigo includes respectful そんけいご for others' actions, humble けんじょうご for your own and polite ていねいご such as です, ます and ございます.
  • うち is the in-group and そと the out-group; to outsiders, speak humbly about in-group members, even superiors, dropping さん and using humble verbs, while using respectful language for outsiders.
  • お and ご beautify words and show respect for others' things and actions; お usually goes with native Japanese words and ご with Sino-Japanese words, with exceptions such as おでんわ, and they are not used for your own things in the respectful sense.
  • Sonkeigo uses special verbs such as いらっしゃる, めしあがる, おっしゃる, ごらんになる, なさる, くださる and ごぞんじだ; いらっしゃる, おっしゃる, なさる and くださる form masu forms with い, and these verbs are only used for others.
  • お + masu stem + に なる and ご + noun + に なる form respectful verbs, the passive form is a lighter respectful form, and お + masu stem + ください makes respectful requests; avoid double honorifics such as おめしあがりに なる.
  • Kenjōgo uses special verbs such as まいる, おる, いただく, もうす, はいけんする, いたす, さしあげる, うかがう and おめに かかる for your own actions; ～て おります replaces ～て います in formal speech, and humble verbs are never used for the listener.

Common mistakes in Keigo and Social Register

  • Using respectful verbs for your own actions, as in わたしが いらっしゃいます. Respectful language is only for others.
  • Saying たなかさんは いらっしゃいません to a client about your own boss. To outsiders, say たなかは おりません.
  • Saying わたしの おなまえは. Do not use respectful お for your own name.
  • Saying いらっしゃります. The masu form is いらっしゃいます.
  • Combining special verbs with お～に なる, as in おめしあがりに なる. Use めしあがる alone.
  • Using humble verbs for a superior's action, as in せんせいが まいります. Use the respectful いらっしゃいます.

Lessons in this school

  1. Plain, polite and honorific speech
    Japanese speakers adjust their language to the situation and the listener.
  2. Uchi and soto: in-group and out-group
    Keigo depends on whether people are inside or outside your group.
  3. Beautifying o and go
    The prefixes お and ご make words sound more polite or refined.
  4. Sonkeigo: respectful verbs
    Respectful language uses special verbs to describe the actions of superiors, customers and guests.
  5. O ni naru and the honorific passive
    Verbs without special respectful forms can be made respectful with お～に なる or with the passive form.
  6. Kenjougo: humble verbs
    Humble language lowers your own actions to show respect for the listener.
  7. O shimasu: humble patterns
    Verbs without special humble forms can be made humble with お～します or ご～します.
  8. Choosing sonkeigo or kenjougo
    Choosing between respectful and humble language depends on whose action you describe.
  9. Keigo in shops and restaurants
    Shop and restaurant staff in Japan use a set of fixed keigo phrases.
  10. Telephone Japanese
    Telephone calls use set phrases, especially in business.
  11. Formal emails and letters
    Formal written Japanese follows set structures.
  12. Requests and refusals: indirectness
    Japanese communication often prefers indirectness, especially in requests and refusals.
  13. Apologies and gratitude in formal settings
    Japanese has many expressions for apologising and thanking, chosen by formality and situation.
  14. Aizuchi and conversational etiquette
    Japanese conversation relies on frequent listener responses called aizuchi.
