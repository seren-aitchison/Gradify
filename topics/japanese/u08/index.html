Lessons in this school

  1. Plain, polite and honorific speech
    Japanese speakers adjust their language to the situation and the listener.
  2. Uchi and soto: in-group and out-group
    Keigo depends on whether people are inside or outside your group.
  3. Beautifying o and go
    The prefixes お and ご make words sound more polite or refined.
  4. Sonkeigo: respectful verbs
    Respectful language uses special verbs to describe the actions of superiors, customers and guests.
  5. O ni naru and the honorific passive
    Verbs without special respectful forms can be made respectful with お～に なる or with the passive form.
  6. Kenjougo: humble verbs
    Humble language lowers your own actions to show respect for the listener.
  7. O shimasu: humble patterns
    Verbs without special humble forms can be made humble with お～します or ご～します.
  8. Choosing sonkeigo or kenjougo
    Choosing between respectful and humble language depends on whose action you describe.
  9. Keigo in shops and restaurants
    Shop and restaurant staff in Japan use a set of fixed keigo phrases.
  10. Telephone Japanese
    Telephone calls use set phrases, especially in business.
  11. Formal emails and letters
    Formal written Japanese follows set structures.
  12. Requests and refusals: indirectness
    Japanese communication often prefers indirectness, especially in requests and refusals.
  13. Apologies and gratitude in formal settings
    Japanese has many expressions for apologising and thanking, chosen by formality and situation.
  14. Aizuchi and conversational etiquette
    Japanese conversation relies on frequent listener responses called aizuchi.
