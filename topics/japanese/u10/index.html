Lessons in this school

  1. The Joyo kanji list and the JLPT levels
    Japan has an official list of kanji for everyday use, and learners plan their study around it and the JLPT levels.
  2. Radicals and semantic-phonetic kanji
    Most kanji are built from smaller parts called radicals and components.
  3. Kanji compounds: on and kun readings together
    Most Japanese words written in kanji are compounds of two or more characters.
  4. Rendaku and sound changes in compounds
    When words combine in Japanese, sounds often change.
  5. Homophones and choosing the right kanji
    Japanese has many words that sound the same but are written with different kanji.
  6. Native, Sino-Japanese and loan vocabulary
    Japanese vocabulary comes from three main sources, and each tends to have a different feel.
  7. Four-character compounds and set phrases
    Four-character compounds, or yojijukugo, express ideas concisely, often with origins in Chinese classics.
  8. Onomatopoeia: giongo and gitaigo
    Japanese has a rich system of sound-symbolic words that describe sounds, states and feelings.
  9. Using a Japanese dictionary
    Finding an unknown kanji or word is an important skill.
  10. Reading signs and notices
    Signs and notices in Japan use short, formal kanji expressions.
  11. Reading emails and messages
    Emails and messages range from formal business mail to casual chats.
  12. Reading news and articles
    News articles use a formal written style with dense kango vocabulary.
  13. Reading stories
    Stories in Japanese use narrative techniques such as plain past narration, dialogue and explanatory のだ.
  14. Vertical writing and Japanese punctuation
    Japanese can be written vertically or horizontally, and it uses its own punctuation marks.
  15. Strategies for unknown kanji
    Readers at every level meet unknown kanji.
