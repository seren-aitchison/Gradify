Lessons in this school

  1. I-adjectives: present and negative
    Japanese adjectives come in two types, and each changes in its own way.
  2. Na-adjectives: present and negative
    な-adjectives behave more like nouns than い-adjectives do.
  3. Past tense of adjectives
    Adjectives in Japanese have past forms, so "it was cold" changes the adjective itself or the following です.
  4. Adjectives before nouns and joining adjectives
    Adjectives can describe nouns directly, and two adjectives can be joined in one sentence.
  5. Suki, kirai, jouzu and heta
    Likes, dislikes and skills are expressed with special な-adjectives that take が.
  6. Native numbers and the tsu counter
    Japanese has two sets of numbers.
  7. Counters: nin, mai, hon, hiki and others
    Japanese uses different counters depending on the type of thing being counted.
  8. Sound changes in counters
    Many counters change sound after certain numbers.
  9. Large numbers, prices and shopping
    Shopping in Japanese needs numbers into the thousands and tens of thousands, plus key shopping phrases.
  10. Ages, years and duration
    Talking about age, years and how long something lasts uses special counters.
  11. Comparisons: yori and no hou ga
    Comparing two things is common in conversation.
  12. Superlatives with ichiban
    To say something is the most or best in a group, Japanese uses いちばん.
  13. Position words: ue, shita, naka and more
    Position words describe exactly where things are.
  14. Describing a room and a town
    Describing your room, home and town brings together adjectives, existence verbs and position words.

Key terms

い-adjective
An adjective ending in い that conjugates by changing its ending
な-adjective
An adjective that conjugates like a noun and takes な before nouns
