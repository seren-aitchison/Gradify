Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What is the negative of おおきい (ōkii, big)?

おおきくないです (ōkikunai desu). From “I-adjectives: present and negative” →

In plain English: Japanese adjectives come in two types, and each changes in its own way. Key idea: い-adjectives end in い and form the present negative by replacing い with くない; いい is irregular, giving よくない, and きれい and ゆうめい are not い-adjectives.

How do you ask "How was it?"

どうでしたか (dō deshita ka). From “Past tense of adjectives” →

In plain English: Adjectives in Japanese have past forms, so "it was cold" changes the adjective itself or the following です. Key idea: い-adjectives form the past with かったです and くなかったです, with いい becoming よかった; な-adjectives use でした and じゃなかったです; ask about past events with どうでしたか.

How do you ask "how many" for general objects?

いくつ (ikutsu). From “Native numbers and the tsu counter” →

In plain English: Japanese has two sets of numbers. Key idea: Native numbers ひとつ to とお count general objects with つ, いくつ asks how many, and counter phrases usually follow the noun and particle before the verb.

How do you ask "How much is it?"

いくらですか (ikura desu ka). From “Large numbers, prices and shopping” →

In plain English: Shopping in Japanese needs numbers into the thousands and tens of thousands, plus key shopping phrases. Key idea: Large numbers use ひゃく, せん and まん, with sound changes such as さんびゃく and はっせん; 10,000 is いちまん; ask prices with いくらですか and buy with これを ください.

What does いちばん mean?

Most, number one. From “Superlatives with ichiban” →

In plain English: To say something is the most or best in a group, Japanese uses いちばん. Key idea: Superlatives use group の なかで X が いちばん adjective です; questions use なに, だれ, どこ or いつ with が, and いちばん すきな names favourites.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Adjectives, Counters and Description

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. What is the present negative of やすい (yasui, cheap)? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    やすくないです. Replace the final い with くない and add です: やすくないです.

  2. What is the negative of べんりです (benri desu, convenient)? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    べんりじゃないです. べんり is a な-adjective, so です becomes じゃないです.

  3. What is the past of たかい (takai, expensive)? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    たかかったです. い-adjectives replace い with かった, then add です.

  4. Which word is not an い-adjective despite ending in い? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    きれい (kirei). きれい is a な-adjective, so it does not take くない.

  5. How do you say "This room is not clean" with きれい (kirei)? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    この へやは きれいじゃないです. きれい is a な-adjective, so it takes じゃないです.

  6. What is the past negative of ひま (hima, free)? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    ひまじゃなかったです. ひま is a な-adjective, so its past negative is じゃなかったです.

  7. Which sentence says "This film isn't interesting"? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    この えいがは おもしろくないです. おもしろい is an い-adjective, so い becomes くない.

  8. Why do な-adjectives form the negative like nouns? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Only the following です changes, not the adjective itself. Like nouns, な-adjectives use じゃないです after an unchanging stem.

  9. Which reply best answers りょこうは どうでしたか (ryokō wa dō deshita ka)? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    とても たのしかったです. The past of たのしい is たのしかったです, which correctly answers a question about a past trip.

Key takeaways

  • い-adjectives end in い and form the present negative by replacing い with くない; いい is irregular, giving よくない, and きれい and ゆうめい are not い-adjectives.
  • な-adjectives such as しずか, にぎやか, げんき and きれい take です in the affirmative and じゃないです or ではありません in the negative, like nouns, without changing their own ending.
  • い-adjectives form the past with かったです and くなかったです, with いい becoming よかった; な-adjectives use でした and じゃなかったです; ask about past events with どうでしたか.
  • い-adjectives go straight before nouns, な-adjectives take な, and nouns take の; join い-adjectives with くて and な-adjectives with で, and use が or でも for contrast.
  • すき, きらい, じょうず and へた are な-adjectives taking が for the thing liked or skill; use とくい and にがて for your own strengths, and answer praise modestly.
  • Native numbers ひとつ to とお count general objects with つ, いくつ asks how many, and counter phrases usually follow the noun and particle before the verb.

Common mistakes in Adjectives, Counters and Description

  • Saying いくないです for "not good". The negative of いい is よくないです.
  • Treating きれい as an い-adjective, as in きれくないです. It is a な-adjective: きれいじゃないです.
  • Saying おいしいでした. い-adjectives change their own ending: おいしかったです.
  • Dropping な before a noun, as in しずか まち. な-adjectives need な: しずかな まち.
  • Using を with すき, as in ねこを すきです. The pattern is ねこが すきです.
  • Putting the counter before the noun in requests, as in みっつ りんごを ください. Say りんごを みっつ ください.

Lessons in this school

  1. I-adjectives: present and negative
    Japanese adjectives come in two types, and each changes in its own way.
  2. Na-adjectives: present and negative
    な-adjectives behave more like nouns than い-adjectives do.
  3. Past tense of adjectives
    Adjectives in Japanese have past forms, so "it was cold" changes the adjective itself or the following です.
  4. Adjectives before nouns and joining adjectives
    Adjectives can describe nouns directly, and two adjectives can be joined in one sentence.
  5. Suki, kirai, jouzu and heta
    Likes, dislikes and skills are expressed with special な-adjectives that take が.
  6. Native numbers and the tsu counter
    Japanese has two sets of numbers.
  7. Counters: nin, mai, hon, hiki and others
    Japanese uses different counters depending on the type of thing being counted.
  8. Sound changes in counters
    Many counters change sound after certain numbers.
  9. Large numbers, prices and shopping
    Shopping in Japanese needs numbers into the thousands and tens of thousands, plus key shopping phrases.
  10. Ages, years and duration
    Talking about age, years and how long something lasts uses special counters.
  11. Comparisons: yori and no hou ga
    Comparing two things is common in conversation.
  12. Superlatives with ichiban
    To say something is the most or best in a group, Japanese uses いちばん.
  13. Position words: ue, shita, naka and more
    Position words describe exactly where things are.
  14. Describing a room and a town
    Describing your room, home and town brings together adjectives, existence verbs and position words.
