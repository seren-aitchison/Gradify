Lessons in this school

  1. Planning backwards from outcomes
    Good planning starts from what pupils should know and be able to do at the end, not from an activity.
  2. Planning a sequence of lessons
    Learning rarely fits into a single lesson, so teachers plan sequences that build knowledge over time.
  3. Learning intentions and success criteria
    Learning intentions say what pupils will learn, and success criteria describe what successful learning looks like.
  4. Activating prior knowledge
    New learning is built on what pupils already know, so lessons often begin by bringing relevant knowledge to mind.
  5. The structure of a lesson
    Most lessons follow a recognisable shape, from review to new content, practice and checking.
  6. Choosing tasks and activities
    The tasks pupils do determine what they think about, and so what they learn.
  7. Planning explanations and models
    Clear explanations rarely happen by chance, so expert teachers plan the words, examples and models they will use.
  8. Planning questions in advance
    Good questions are often prepared before the lesson rather than invented on the spot.
  9. Homework and independent study
    Homework can extend learning beyond the lesson, but its value depends on its purpose and design.
  10. Planning for adaptive teaching
    Adaptive teaching starts at the planning stage, by anticipating where pupils may struggle and planning support and challenge.
  11. Planning practical work and trips
    Practical work and educational visits can make learning memorable, but only if they are planned with a clear purpose and managed safely.
  12. Evaluating a lesson
    Teachers improve by reflecting on what happened in a lesson and why, then changing what they do next.
  13. Lesson observation and feedback
    Trainees and early-career teachers are regularly observed, and the feedback can speed up their development.
  14. Planning and workload
    Planning is essential, but it can also become one of the biggest drivers of teacher workload.
  15. Digital technology in the classroom
    Digital technology can support explanations, practice and feedback, but it does not improve learning by itself.
  16. Artificial intelligence and teaching
    Generative artificial intelligence tools can help teachers plan and prepare, but they also bring risks to accuracy, data protection and assessment.

Key terms

Backward design
Planning that starts from intended outcomes and evidence before choosing activities
Medium-term plan
A plan for a sequence of lessons across a topic, usually a half-term
Learning intention
A statement of what pupils are meant to learn in a lesson or sequence
Advance organiser
An introduction that shows how new material relates to what learners already know
Gradual release of responsibility
Moving from teacher modelling to guided then independent work as pupils become secure
Busy work
Activity that keeps pupils occupied without advancing learning
Non-example
A case that resembles the concept but is not an instance of it
Key question
A planned question that checks an essential idea of the lesson
Independent study
Learning pupils do on their own, outside direct teaching
Barrier
Something in a lesson that may stop a pupil accessing the learning
Risk assessment
A record of hazards, who could be harmed and the control measures in place
Reflection-on-action
Thinking back on a lesson after it has finished
Action step
A specific, practical change a teacher agrees to make after feedback
Unnecessary workload
Tasks that take time without improving pupils' learning
