For safeguarding purposes, who counts as a child?

Anyone under the age of 18. From “What safeguarding means” →

What is a section 47 enquiry?

An enquiry made when there is reasonable cause to suspect a child is suffering or likely to suffer significant harm. From “Working Together to Safeguard Children” →

Does UK GDPR prevent sharing information to keep a child safe?

No; data protection law does not prevent sharing for safeguarding. From “Recording and reporting concerns” →

Which law created the Prevent duty for schools?

Who should an allegation about the headteacher be reported to?

Lessons in this school

  1. What safeguarding means
    Safeguarding is the action taken to promote the welfare of children and protect them from harm, and it is everyone's responsibility.
  2. Keeping Children Safe in Education
    Keeping Children Safe in Education is the statutory guidance that sets out what schools and colleges in England must do to safeguard children.
  3. The designated safeguarding lead
    Every school must have a designated safeguarding lead who takes lead responsibility for safeguarding and child protection.
  4. Working Together to Safeguard Children
    Working Together to Safeguard Children is the statutory guidance on how organisations and agencies should work together to protect children.
  5. The four categories of abuse
    Statutory guidance describes four categories of abuse: physical, emotional, sexual abuse and neglect.
  6. Signs of abuse and neglect
    Children rarely tell an adult directly that they are being abused, so staff need to notice signs and changes.
  7. Responding to a disclosure
    When a child tells an adult about abuse, how the adult responds matters greatly.
  8. Recording and reporting concerns
    Clear, accurate records help the DSL and other agencies protect children, and they may later be used in court.
  9. Child-on-child abuse
    Children can abuse other children, and schools must take this seriously rather than dismiss it as banter or growing up.
  10. Online safety
    Online life brings benefits and risks for children, and online safety is part of safeguarding.
  11. Child criminal and sexual exploitation
    Children can be exploited by individuals or groups who take advantage of an imbalance of power to coerce them into criminal or sexual activity.
  12. Radicalisation and the Prevent duty
    Schools have a legal duty to have due regard to the need to prevent people from being drawn into terrorism.
  13. Female genital mutilation: the mandatory reporting duty
    Female genital mutilation is illegal in the UK and a form of child abuse with serious lifelong consequences.
  14. Children missing education
    A child who is not on a school roll or not receiving suitable education may be at risk of harm and of falling behind.
  15. Pupil mental health
    Many pupils experience mental health difficulties, and teachers are often the first to notice.
  16. Allegations against staff and low-level concerns
    Schools must deal promptly and fairly with concerns about adults who work with children.
  17. Safer working practice
    Safer working practice protects children and staff by setting clear professional boundaries.
  18. Safeguarding children with SEND
    Children with special educational needs and disabilities are at greater risk of abuse, and face extra barriers to being recognised and protected.
  19. Attendance as a safeguarding issue
    Regular attendance is vital for learning and is also one of the ways schools keep children safe.

Key terms

Safeguarding
Protecting children from maltreatment and promoting their welfare and best outcomes
Statutory guidance
Guidance issued under law that organisations must have regard to
Designated safeguarding lead
The senior member of staff who takes lead responsibility for safeguarding and child protection
Safeguarding partners
The local authority, integrated care board and chief officer of police
Neglect
Persistent failure to meet a child's basic physical or psychological needs
Indicator
A sign that may suggest a child is at risk of harm
Disclosure
When a child tells someone they have been or are being harmed
Factual record
A written account of what was seen and heard, without interpretation
Child-on-child abuse
Abuse of a child by another child, in or out of school and online
Four Cs
Content, contact, conduct and commerce, the four areas of online risk
Child criminal exploitation
Coercing a child into criminal activity through an imbalance of power
Prevent duty
The legal duty to have due regard to preventing people being drawn into terrorism
Female genital mutilation
Procedures that remove or injure female genitalia for non-medical reasons
Children missing education
Children of compulsory school age not registered at a school and not receiving suitable education otherwise
