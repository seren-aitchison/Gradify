What does Teachers' Standard 5 require?

That teachers adapt teaching to respond to the strengths and needs of all pupils. From “What adaptive teaching is” →

Who attracts Pupil Premium funding?

Pupils eligible for free school meals in the last six years, looked-after and previously looked-after children, and (separately) service children. From “Disadvantaged pupils and the Pupil Premium” →

Name the four broad areas of need.

Communication and interaction; cognition and learning; social, emotional and mental health; sensory and/or physical needs. From “The four broad areas of need” →

What did the DISS project find?

Pupils who received the most TA support made less progress than similar pupils with less support, because of how TAs were deployed. From “Working with teaching assistants” →

What are the two main areas of difference in the diagnostic criteria for autism?

Social communication and interaction; and restricted, repetitive patterns of behaviour, interests or activities (including sensory differences). From “Autism in the classroom” →

Lessons in this school

  1. What adaptive teaching is
    Adaptive teaching means keeping high expectations for every pupil while adjusting how you teach, in response to what pupils know and need, so that all of them can succeed.
  2. Adaptive teaching versus differentiation by task
    For many years "differentiation" in English schools often meant planning different tasks for different groups.
  3. Responding to pupils within the lesson
    Much of adaptive teaching happens in the moment: noticing that pupils are stuck and changing what you do.
  4. Attainment grouping and its evidence
    Schools often group pupils by prior attainment, between classes (setting and streaming) or within a class.
  5. Disadvantaged pupils and the Pupil Premium
    Pupils from disadvantaged backgrounds in England do less well at school, on average, than their peers.
  6. Pupils with English as an additional language
    About one in five pupils in England speaks English as an additional language (EAL).
  7. Challenging high-attaining pupils
    Pupils who already do well also need to be challenged, or they may coast and fall behind their potential.
  8. The SEND Code of Practice 2015
    The Special Educational Needs and Disability Code of Practice: 0 to 25 years (2015) is the statutory guidance that sets out how schools must identify and support pupils with SEND in England.
  9. The four broad areas of need
    The SEND Code of Practice groups special educational needs into four broad areas.
  10. The graduated approach: assess, plan, do, review
    When a pupil needs SEN support, the Code of Practice expects schools to follow a graduated approach: a cycle of assess, plan, do and review.
  11. The role of the SENCO
    Every mainstream school in England must have a special educational needs co-ordinator (SENCO).
  12. Education, health and care plans
    An education, health and care (EHC) plan is a legal document for a child or young person aged 0 to 25 whose needs cannot be met through SEN support alone.
  13. Working with teaching assistants
    Teaching assistants (TAs) make up a large part of the school workforce, but research has shown that how they are deployed matters greatly.
  14. The Equality Act 2010 and reasonable adjustments
    The Equality Act 2010 protects pupils from discrimination, including disabled pupils, and requires schools to make reasonable adjustments.
  15. Communication and interaction needs
    Speech, language and communication needs are among the most common special educational needs in primary schools, and they are often missed.
  16. Dyslexia and specific learning difficulties
    Specific learning difficulties affect particular aspects of learning, such as reading, spelling, number or coordination, rather than learning as a whole.
  17. Autism in the classroom
    Autism is a lifelong neurodevelopmental difference affecting social communication and interaction, often with focused interests, a preference for routine and sensory differences.
  18. ADHD in the classroom
    Attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) affects attention, activity and impulse control, and can have a large effect on learning and relationships at school.
  19. Social, emotional and mental health needs
    Social, emotional and mental health (SEMH) needs range from anxiety and low mood to attachment difficulties and behaviour that challenges.
  20. Sensory and physical needs
    Some pupils have a vision impairment, hearing impairment, multi-sensory impairment, physical disability or medical condition that affects access to learning.

Key terms

Adaptive teaching
Adjusting support and teaching in response to pupils' needs while maintaining high expectations and the same goals for all
Differentiation by task
Giving different groups different tasks, usually varying in difficulty
Responsive teaching
Checking pupils' understanding during teaching and adjusting instruction in response
Setting
Grouping pupils by attainment for a particular subject
Pupil Premium
Additional school funding in England for pupils recorded as disadvantaged, including those eligible for free school meals in the last six years
English as an additional language
A pupil whose first language is known or believed to be other than English
High-attaining pupil
A pupil whose current attainment is well above that of most peers, which may change over time
Special educational needs
A learning difficulty or disability that calls for special educational provision to be made
Communication and interaction
An area of need covering speech, language and communication needs, often including autism
SEN support
Special educational provision made by a school for a pupil with SEN without an EHC plan
SENCO
The special educational needs co-ordinator: a qualified teacher responsible for co-ordinating SEN provision in a school
EHC plan
A legal document for a 0 to 25-year-old setting out education, health and care needs and the provision to meet them
Teaching assistant
A member of support staff who works with teachers to support pupils' learning
Disability (Equality Act)
A physical or mental impairment with a substantial and long-term adverse effect on the ability to carry out normal day-to-day activities
