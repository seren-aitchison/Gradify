Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

Name three functions of sleep.

Any three of: tissue repair, memory consolidation, brain waste clearance, immune support, emotional regulation, energy conservation. From “Why we sleep” →

In plain English: Sleep is essential for physical health, learning and emotional wellbeing. Key idea: Sleep supports body repair, memory, brain waste clearance, immunity and mood; chronic sleep loss impairs performance and is linked to many diseases, often without people realising how impaired they are.

What is process S in the two-process model?

Sleep pressure, which builds during wakefulness and falls during sleep. From “Circadian rhythms” →

In plain English: Circadian rhythms are roughly 24-hour cycles in body processes, controlled by an internal clock and set by light. Key idea: The SCN keeps a roughly 24-hour clock reset mainly by light, with melatonin marking night; sleep depends on sleep pressure and the circadian signal together, explaining jet lag and teenagers' later sleep.

What is stimulus control?

A CBT-I technique: using the bed only for sleep and getting up if unable to sleep, to strengthen the bed–sleep link. From “Insomnia” →

In plain English: Insomnia is persistent difficulty falling or staying asleep that affects daytime functioning. Key idea: Insomnia is persistent sleep difficulty with daytime effects; it is kept going by behaviours and worry, and CBT-I is the first-line treatment, with medicines only for limited situations.

What is cataplexy?

Sudden muscle weakness triggered by strong emotion, a feature of narcolepsy type 1. From “Sleep disorders beyond insomnia” →

In plain English: Several sleep disorders other than insomnia are common and treatable. Key idea: Sleep apnoea, narcolepsy, restless legs, parasomnias and circadian disorders are common and treatable; daytime sleepiness, breathing pauses and dream enactment need medical assessment.

What is allostatic load?

The cumulative wear and tear on the body from repeated or prolonged stress responses. From “Stress and health” →

In plain English: Chronic stress affects both physical and mental health. Key idea: Chronic stress causes allostatic load, which is linked to heart disease, weaker immunity and mental illness, both directly and through behaviour; low control at work is a key source of harmful stress.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Sleep, Stress and Rest

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which hormone is released mainly during deep sleep and supports growth and repair? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Growth hormone. Growth hormone secretion peaks during slow-wave (deep) sleep.

  2. In which stage of sleep do sleep spindles and K-complexes occur? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    N2. Spindles and K-complexes are the markers of stage N2.

  3. Where is the body's master clock located? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    The suprachiasmatic nucleus of the hypothalamus. The SCN coordinates circadian rhythms throughout the body.

  4. A driver has slept 5 hours a night all week and feels "fine". What does research suggest? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    His reaction time and attention are likely impaired, increasing crash risk. Chronic restriction impairs performance despite little feeling of sleepiness.

  5. Why is deep sleep more common early in the night? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Sleep pressure is highest at sleep onset, so slow-wave sleep is prioritised in early cycles. The drive for slow-wave sleep builds during wakefulness and is discharged first.

  6. How does caffeine reduce sleepiness? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    It blocks adenosine receptors, masking sleep pressure. Caffeine blocks the signal of accumulated adenosine, part of process S.

  7. Which is the best evaluation of the glymphatic theory of sleep? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    It is supported by animal studies, but evidence in humans is still developing. Brain clearance during sleep has been shown in mice; human evidence is promising but limited.

  8. A person woken at 3 am from deep sleep feels groggy and confused for several minutes. What best explains this? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Sleep inertia, which is strongest on waking from slow-wave sleep. Waking from N3 produces marked grogginess, known as sleep inertia.

  9. A school wants to help sleepy teenagers. Which change best fits circadian evidence? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Later start times, since adolescent clocks are shifted later. Adolescent body clocks are biologically delayed; later starts align school with their rhythms.

Key takeaways

  • Sleep supports body repair, memory, brain waste clearance, immunity and mood; chronic sleep loss impairs performance and is linked to many diseases, often without people realising how impaired they are.
  • Sleep cycles through N1, N2, N3 (deep) and REM about every 90 minutes; deep sleep dominates early in the night and REM later.
  • The SCN keeps a roughly 24-hour clock reset mainly by light, with melatonin marking night; sleep depends on sleep pressure and the circadian signal together, explaining jet lag and teenagers' later sleep.
  • Adults need 7–9 hours, teenagers 8–10 and children more; the best guide is daytime alertness, and both short and long sleep are associated with poorer health, though causes differ.
  • Sleep moves from polyphasic infant sleep to later adolescent timing and then to lighter, earlier, more fragmented sleep in older age; advice should match each stage.
  • Insomnia is persistent sleep difficulty with daytime effects; it is kept going by behaviours and worry, and CBT-I is the first-line treatment, with medicines only for limited situations.

Common mistakes in Sleep, Stress and Rest

  • Believing people can adapt fully to short sleep. Studies show performance keeps declining with chronic restriction even when people report feeling only a little tired.
  • Thinking dreaming only happens in REM sleep. Dreams can occur in NREM sleep too, though REM dreams are usually more vivid and story-like.
  • Assuming teenagers stay up late only by choice. The adolescent body clock shifts later biologically, though screens and social life can add to the delay.
  • Concluding that long sleep causes ill health from observational data. Long sleep often reflects underlying illness, depression or poor-quality sleep.
  • Assuming poor sleep is an inevitable part of ageing that can't be helped. Many causes, such as pain, nocturia, medicines, low activity and sleep disorders, can be treated.
  • Spending longer in bed to make up for poor sleep. This weakens sleep drive and links the bed with wakefulness, making insomnia worse.

Lessons in this school

  1. Why we sleep
    Sleep is essential for physical health, learning and emotional wellbeing.
  2. Sleep stages and cycles
    Sleep isn't a single state but a cycle of distinct stages.
  3. Circadian rhythms
    Circadian rhythms are roughly 24-hour cycles in body processes, controlled by an internal clock and set by light.
  4. How much sleep we need
    Sleep needs vary with age and between individuals.
  5. Sleep across the lifespan
    Sleep changes dramatically from birth to old age.
  6. Insomnia
    Insomnia is persistent difficulty falling or staying asleep that affects daytime functioning.
  7. Improving sleep
    Many everyday habits affect sleep.
  8. Shift work and sleep
    Shift work, especially at night, conflicts with the body clock.
  9. Sleep disorders beyond insomnia
    Several sleep disorders other than insomnia are common and treatable.
  10. What stress is
    Stress is the response to demands that challenge or exceed our ability to cope.
  11. The stress response
    The body responds to stress through two linked systems: a fast nervous and hormonal response, and a slower hormonal response.
  12. Stress and health
    Chronic stress affects both physical and mental health.
  13. Coping and stress management
    Coping is what people do to manage stress.
  14. Work-related stress and burnout
    Work is a major source of stress for many people.

Sleep, Stress and Rest: questions students ask

Why is chronic sleep restriction dangerous?

Performance keeps declining while people feel only slightly sleepy, so they underestimate their impairment.

What is muscle atonia?

Near-paralysis of most skeletal muscles during REM sleep.

What is sleep debt?

The accumulated shortfall between the sleep a person needs and the sleep they get.

How does sleep timing change in older age?

It shifts earlier: older adults tend to go to bed and wake earlier.
