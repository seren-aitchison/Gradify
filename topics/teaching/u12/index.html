Lessons in this school

  1. Functionalist perspectives on education
    Functionalists see education as serving the needs of society: building shared values, teaching skills and sorting people into roles.
  2. Marxist perspectives on education
    Marxists see education as reproducing class inequality and legitimating it.
  3. Feminist perspectives on education
    Feminist sociologists examine how education reproduces or challenges gender inequality.
  4. Education, socialisation and social control
    Schools socialise young people and regulate their behaviour.
  5. The hidden curriculum
    The hidden curriculum is what pupils learn from the way schools are organised, not from formal lessons.
  6. Educational achievement and social class
    Social class is one of the strongest predictors of educational achievement in England.
  7. Educational achievement and gender
    Girls have outperformed boys on average in English schools for decades.
  8. Educational achievement and ethnicity
    Educational outcomes vary widely by ethnic group.
  9. Cultural capital and education
    Cultural capital is the knowledge, tastes and confidence that bring educational advantage.
  10. Linguistic codes
    Bernstein argued that differences in how families use language affect school success.
  11. Labelling in education
    Labelling theory examines how teachers' judgements about pupils can shape pupils' identities and outcomes.
  12. Teacher expectations
    Teacher expectations are beliefs teachers hold about pupils' future achievement.
  13. The self-fulfilling prophecy
    A self-fulfilling prophecy is a prediction that comes true because it was made.
  14. Selection
    Selection refers to how schools and systems sort pupils by ability or other criteria.
  15. Educational inequality
    Educational inequality is unequal access to, experience of and outcomes from education.
  16. Private education
    Private (independent) schools educate a small share of pupils but a large share of elites.
  17. Higher education
    Higher education has expanded greatly, but access and outcomes remain unequal.
  18. Education policy
    Education policy shapes how schools are organised, funded and held accountable.
  19. Global education
    Access to and quality of education vary greatly around the world.
  20. Digital learning
    Digital technology is changing how people learn, from online courses to AI tools.
  21. Educational reform
    Educational reform aims to improve standards and reduce inequality.

Key terms

Social solidarity
The sense of belonging and shared values that binds a society
Ideological state apparatus
An institution spreading ideas that maintain ruling-class power
Liberal feminism
Feminism focusing on equal opportunities and reform
Secondary socialisation
Socialisation by institutions beyond the family
Hidden curriculum
Norms and values learned from school organisation rather than lessons
Disadvantage gap index
A DfE measure summarising the attainment gap between disadvantaged pupils and others
Laddish culture
Peer culture valuing appearing effortless and rejecting visible academic effort
Ethnocentric curriculum
A curriculum centred on one culture's perspective
Cultural capital
Knowledge, tastes and confidence that bring educational advantage
Restricted code
Context-bound speech relying on shared understanding
Ideal pupil
A teacher's image of the perfect pupil, often reflecting middle-class norms
Teacher expectations
Beliefs teachers hold about pupils' future achievement
Self-fulfilling prophecy
A prediction that comes true because it was made
Streaming
Placing pupils in the same ability group for all subjects
