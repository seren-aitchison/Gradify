Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What is the CGT annual exempt amount for 2026–27?

£3,000. From “How investments are taxed in the UK” →

In plain English: Investment returns in the UK can be taxed as income or as capital gains, depending on their form and the account they are held in. Key idea: UK investment returns are taxed as income (interest or dividends) or as capital gains, each with its own allowance, and ISAs and pensions shelter returns from much of this tax.

What is a general investment account?

An ordinary investment account with no special tax status or limits. From “General investment accounts” →

In plain English: A general investment account (GIA) is an ordinary, unsheltered account for holding investments. Key idea: A GIA is a flexible, unsheltered account for investments beyond ISA and pension limits; income and gains in it are taxed after the usual allowances, so it is usually filled last.

What is the maximum yearly Lifetime ISA bonus?

£1,000, which is 25% of £4,000. From “Lifetime ISAs and Junior ISAs” →

In plain English: Lifetime ISAs help adults under 40 save for a first home or later life with a government bonus, and Junior ISAs let families save tax-free for children. Key idea: The LISA adds a 25% bonus on up to £4,000 a year for a first home or later life, but a 25% charge on other withdrawals can cost more than the bonus; a JISA lets up to £9,000 a year grow tax-free for a child, who gets control at 18.

Who must be auto-enrolled?

Workers aged 22 to State Pension age, working in the UK, earning over £10,000 a year. From “Workplace pensions and auto-enrolment” →

In plain English: Auto-enrolment requires employers to put eligible workers into a workplace pension and contribute. Key idea: Employers must auto-enrol eligible workers aged 22 to State Pension age earning over £10,000, paying at least 8% of qualifying earnings in total, with at least 3% from the employer; inertia keeps most people saving.

What is the triple lock?

Annual rises by the highest of earnings growth, CPI inflation or 2.5%. From “The State Pension” →

In plain English: The State Pension is a regular payment from the government, based on your National Insurance record. Key idea: The full new State Pension is £241.30 a week in 2026–27 for 35 qualifying years, with a minimum of 10; it rises under the triple lock, State Pension age is rising to 67, and gaps can often be filled with voluntary contributions.

Quick quiz: test yourself on UK Wrappers, Pensions and Tax

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which tax normally applies to dividends received in a general investment account? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Income tax. Dividends are income, taxed at dividend rates after the £500 allowance.

  2. How much interest can a basic-rate taxpayer receive tax-free each year under the personal savings allowance? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    £1,000. Basic-rate taxpayers get a £1,000 personal savings allowance; higher-rate taxpayers get £500.

  3. Which of these is exempt from Capital Gains Tax? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Gains on gilts. Gilts and qualifying corporate bonds are exempt; shares held outside wrappers are not.

  4. What is the main tax difference between an ISA and a pension? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    An ISA gives no relief on payments in; a pension does, but most withdrawals are taxed. ISAs are funded from taxed income with tax-free withdrawals; pensions get relief going in and are mostly taxed coming out.

  5. A higher-rate taxpayer receives £1,500 of interest in 2026–27. How much tax is due on it? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    £400. The PSA covers £500, leaving £1,000 taxed at 40%, which is £400.

  6. Why can transferring shares to a spouse before sale reduce CGT? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Transfers between spouses are at no gain or loss, so both annual exempt amounts and bands can be used. Inter-spouse transfers are made at no gain, no loss, letting the couple use two exemptions and possibly a lower rate.

  7. An investor has £40,000 in a GIA producing interest, dividends and gains well above her allowances, and has not used this year's ISA allowance. What is the most sensible step? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Consider moving investments into an ISA over time to shelter future returns. Sheltering returns in an ISA removes future income tax and CGT on them.

  8. How will the planned April 2027 changes affect an investor holding bonds in a GIA? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Interest above the PSA will be taxed at higher rates, raising the case for an ISA. Budget 2025 raised savings rates by two points from April 2027, increasing the benefit of sheltering interest.

  9. An investor sells shares at a £4,000 loss in December to offset a gain, then buys the same shares back two weeks later. What is the effect? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    The repurchase is matched under the 30-day rule, so the loss is not crystallised as intended. Selling and rebuying within 30 days matches the sale with the new purchase, undermining the plan.

Key takeaways

  • UK investment returns are taxed as income (interest or dividends) or as capital gains, each with its own allowance, and ISAs and pensions shelter returns from much of this tax.
  • Interest is taxed after the personal savings allowance and dividends after the £500 dividend allowance, at rates for 2026–27 of 20/40/45% on interest and 10.75/35.75/39.35% on dividends.
  • A chargeable gain is the disposal value less cost and allowable expenses; after losses and the £3,000 annual exempt amount, gains on shares are taxed at 18% or 24% in 2026–27.
  • A GIA is a flexible, unsheltered account for investments beyond ISA and pension limits; income and gains in it are taxed after the usual allowances, so it is usually filled last.
  • ISAs shelter interest, dividends and gains from tax, with a £20,000 overall allowance in 2026–27 split across ISA types; transfers must go provider to provider, and cash ISA limits are set to fall to £12,000 for under-65s from April 2027.
  • A stocks and shares ISA can hold most mainstream investments free of UK tax on income and gains with flexible access; pensions offer tax relief but lock money away, so many investors use both.

Common mistakes in UK Wrappers, Pensions and Tax

  • Assuming all investment returns are taxed the same way. Interest, dividends and gains have different allowances and rates, and the account they are held in changes the tax completely.
  • Thinking the dividend allowance means the first £500 of dividends does not count towards your tax band. It is taxed at 0%, but it still uses up part of your band.
  • Thinking you avoid CGT by selling and buying straight back. Under the 30-day rule, a repurchase within 30 days is matched with the sale, so no gain or loss is crystallised; Bed and ISA works because the new holding is inside an ISA.
  • Leaving large sums in a GIA for years while ISA allowances go unused. Each year's ISA allowance is lost if not used, and sheltering investments early saves tax for decades.
  • Withdrawing money from one ISA to pay it into another provider's ISA. That counts as a new subscription and loses the tax shelter on previous years' money; use a formal transfer instead.
  • Thinking an ISA is always better than a pension for retirement. Pension tax relief and employer contributions often outweigh the ISA's flexibility, especially for higher-rate taxpayers.

Lessons in this school

  1. How investments are taxed in the UK
    Investment returns in the UK can be taxed as income or as capital gains, depending on their form and the account they are held in.
  2. Income tax on savings and dividends
    Interest and dividends are taxed under special rules with their own allowances and rates.
  3. Capital gains tax
    Capital Gains Tax is charged on the profit from selling investments held outside tax wrappers.
  4. General investment accounts
    A general investment account (GIA) is an ordinary, unsheltered account for holding investments.
  5. Individual Savings Accounts
    ISAs are tax-free accounts for saving and investing.
  6. Stocks and shares ISAs
    A stocks and shares ISA lets you invest in funds, shares and bonds with no UK tax on income or gains.
  7. Lifetime ISAs and Junior ISAs
    Lifetime ISAs help adults under 40 save for a first home or later life with a government bonus, and Junior ISAs let families save tax-free for children.
  8. Why save in a pension?
    Pensions are long-term savings accounts for retirement with generous tax treatment.
  9. Pension tax relief
    Pension tax relief returns income tax on contributions, within limits.
  10. Workplace pensions and auto-enrolment
    Auto-enrolment requires employers to put eligible workers into a workplace pension and contribute.
  11. Defined benefit and defined contribution pensions
    Pensions are either defined benefit, promising an income based on salary and service, or defined contribution, building a pot from contributions and returns.
  12. Self-invested personal pensions (SIPPs)
    A self-invested personal pension (SIPP) is a personal pension in which you choose the investments.
  13. The State Pension
    The State Pension is a regular payment from the government, based on your National Insurance record.
  14. Accessing your pension
    From the normal minimum pension age, defined contribution pensions can be accessed in several ways.
  15. Inheritance tax basics
    Inheritance Tax is charged on the value of an estate on death above certain thresholds.
  16. Wills, nominations and estate planning
    Estate planning makes sure your money goes where you want after death, and that someone can act for you if you lose capacity.

UK Wrappers, Pensions and Tax: questions students ask

When does the UK tax year run?

From 6 April to the following 5 April.

What is the personal savings allowance for a higher-rate taxpayer?

£500 of interest a year.

What are the CGT rates on shares from 30 October 2024?

18% within the basic-rate band and 24% above it.

How should you move an ISA to a new provider?

Through a formal ISA transfer arranged by the providers, not by withdrawing the money.
