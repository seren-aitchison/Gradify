Lessons in this school

  1. How investments are taxed in the UK
    Investment returns in the UK can be taxed as income or as capital gains, depending on their form and the account they are held in.
  2. Income tax on savings and dividends
    Interest and dividends are taxed under special rules with their own allowances and rates.
  3. Capital gains tax
    Capital Gains Tax is charged on the profit from selling investments held outside tax wrappers.
  4. General investment accounts
    A general investment account (GIA) is an ordinary, unsheltered account for holding investments.
  5. Individual Savings Accounts
    ISAs are tax-free accounts for saving and investing.
  6. Stocks and shares ISAs
    A stocks and shares ISA lets you invest in funds, shares and bonds with no UK tax on income or gains.
  7. Lifetime ISAs and Junior ISAs
    Lifetime ISAs help adults under 40 save for a first home or later life with a government bonus, and Junior ISAs let families save tax-free for children.
  8. Why save in a pension?
    Pensions are long-term savings accounts for retirement with generous tax treatment.
  9. Pension tax relief
    Pension tax relief returns income tax on contributions, within limits.
  10. Workplace pensions and auto-enrolment
    Auto-enrolment requires employers to put eligible workers into a workplace pension and contribute.
  11. Defined benefit and defined contribution pensions
    Pensions are either defined benefit, promising an income based on salary and service, or defined contribution, building a pot from contributions and returns.
  12. Self-invested personal pensions (SIPPs)
    A self-invested personal pension (SIPP) is a personal pension in which you choose the investments.
  13. The State Pension
    The State Pension is a regular payment from the government, based on your National Insurance record.
  14. Accessing your pension
    From the normal minimum pension age, defined contribution pensions can be accessed in several ways.
  15. Inheritance tax basics
    Inheritance Tax is charged on the value of an estate on death above certain thresholds.
  16. Wills, nominations and estate planning
    Estate planning makes sure your money goes where you want after death, and that someone can act for you if you lose capacity.

Key terms

Capital gain
The profit made when an asset is sold or disposed of for more than its cost
Personal savings allowance
The amount of interest a basic or higher-rate taxpayer can receive tax-free each year
Chargeable gain
The taxable profit on disposal of an asset after allowable costs
General investment account
An investment account with no special tax treatment
ISA allowance
The maximum that can be paid into ISAs in a tax year
Stocks and shares ISA
An ISA that holds investments such as funds, shares and bonds free of UK tax
Lifetime ISA
An ISA for those aged 18 to 39 that adds a 25% government bonus for a first home or retirement
Tax relief
Income tax returned or not charged on money paid into a pension
Relief at source
A system where contributions are paid net and the provider claims basic-rate relief from HMRC
Auto-enrolment
The legal duty on employers to place eligible workers into a workplace pension
Defined benefit pension
A pension promising an income based on salary and years of service
SIPP
A personal pension in which the holder chooses the investments
New State Pension
The State Pension for people reaching State Pension age on or after 6 April 2016
Flexi-access drawdown
Keeping a pension invested while taking income or lump sums as needed
