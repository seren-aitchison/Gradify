What is information asymmetry in financial services?

When firms know much more about a product's risks and costs than the customers buying it. From “Why financial services are regulated” →

What is the main difference between advice and guidance?

Advice gives a personal recommendation; guidance gives general information without telling you what to do. From “Advice, guidance and execution-only” →

Lessons in this school

  1. Why financial services are regulated
    Financial products are complex, and mistakes or misconduct can cost people their savings.
  2. The FCA and the PRA
    Two main regulators oversee UK financial services.
  3. The Consumer Duty
    The Consumer Duty raised the standard of care that firms must give retail customers.
  4. Advice, guidance and execution-only
    Investors can get regulated advice, free guidance or make their own decisions.
  5. Financial promotions and finfluencers
    Adverts and social media posts about investments must follow strict rules.
  6. The Financial Services Compensation Scheme
    The Financial Services Compensation Scheme protects customers if an authorised firm fails.
  7. The Financial Ombudsman Service
    The Financial Ombudsman Service settles disputes between consumers and financial firms.
  8. Client money and asset protection
    When you invest through a platform or broker, your money and investments should be kept separate from the firm's own.
  9. Investment scams
    Investment scams cost UK victims hundreds of millions of pounds a year.
  10. Red flags and the FCA Warning List
    Recognising warning signs can stop a scam before money is lost.
  11. Pension scams
    Pensions are a major target for scammers because they can be large and are often hard to monitor.
  12. Ethics in investment
    Investment raises ethical questions for firms, professionals and individual investors.
  13. The CFA Institute Code of Ethics
    Professional bodies set standards for investment professionals.
  14. ESG and responsible investing
    Many investors now consider environmental, social and governance factors.
  15. Stewardship and engagement
    Investors who own shares can influence how companies behave.
  16. Greenwashing and sustainability labels
    Greenwashing means making misleading claims about environmental benefits.

Key terms

Information asymmetry
When one party to a transaction knows much more than the other
Conduct regulation
Regulation of how firms behave towards customers and markets
Consumer Duty
An FCA rule requiring firms to deliver good outcomes for retail customers
Suitability
The requirement that a personal recommendation meets the client's needs and circumstances
Financial promotion
An invitation or inducement to engage in investment activity
Financial Services Compensation Scheme
The UK scheme that compensates customers when authorised financial firms fail
Final response
A firm's formal reply to a complaint
Client money
Money a firm holds on behalf of its clients, kept separate from the firm's own money
Ponzi scheme
A fraud that pays earlier investors with money from new investors
FCA Warning List
A list of firms and individuals the FCA warns are operating without authorisation or running scams
Pension liberation
Unlawfully releasing pension money before the minimum pension age
Fiduciary duty
A duty to act in another person's best interests
Code of Ethics
Principles that members of a professional body must follow
ESG
Environmental, social and governance factors used in investment analysis
