Lessons in this school

  1. Price and value
    The price of a share is what you pay; its value is what the business is worth to an owner.
  2. Discounted cash flow valuation
    A discounted cash flow (DCF) valuation estimates what a business is worth by forecasting its future free cash flows and discounting them to today.
  3. The dividend discount model
    The dividend discount model values a share as the present value of the dividends it is expected to pay.
  4. The Gordon growth model
    The Gordon growth model values a share whose dividends are expected to grow at a constant rate for ever.
  5. The required return and the cost of equity
    Every valuation needs a discount rate, the return investors require for bearing the risk.
  6. Earnings per share
    Earnings per share (EPS) is the profit attributable to each ordinary share.
  7. The price-to-earnings ratio
    The price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio compares a share's price with its earnings per share.
  8. Dividend yield and dividend cover
    Dividend yield shows the income a share pays relative to its price, and dividend cover shows how comfortably profits support that dividend.
  9. Price-to-book
    The price-to-book ratio compares a company's market value with the net assets shown in its accounts.
  10. Enterprise value and EV/EBITDA
    Enterprise value measures the value of the whole business, to shareholders and lenders together.
  11. Valuing a bond
    A bond's price is the present value of its coupons and its final repayment, discounted at the market yield.
  12. Valuing the whole market: CAPE
    The cyclically adjusted price-to-earnings ratio, or CAPE, compares a market's price with its average real earnings over ten years.
  13. Growth and value investing
    Growth and value are two broad styles of choosing shares: one pays up for fast-growing companies, the other buys shares that look cheap on measures such as price-to-book and P/E.
  14. The limits of valuation
    Every valuation method rests on uncertain forecasts and assumptions, and markets can stay away from estimated value for long periods.

Key terms

Intrinsic value
An estimate of what an investment is worth, based on the cash it is expected to generate
Free cash flow
Cash generated by operations after the investment needed to sustain and grow the business
Dividend discount model
A model valuing a share as the present value of its expected future dividends
Gordon growth model
A formula valuing a share as D1 ÷ (r − g) when dividends grow at a constant rate for ever
Cost of equity
The return shareholders require for investing in a company's shares
Earnings per share
Profit attributable to ordinary shareholders divided by the weighted average number of ordinary shares
Price-to-earnings ratio
Share price divided by earnings per share
Dividend yield
Annual dividend per share as a percentage of the share price
Book value
Shareholders' equity in the balance sheet: total assets minus total liabilities
Enterprise value
Market capitalisation plus net debt and other claims on the business
Yield to maturity
The discount rate that equates a bond's price with the present value of its cash flows
CAPE
Real price divided by the average of the last ten years of real earnings
Value investing
Buying shares that are cheap relative to fundamentals such as book value or earnings
Sensitivity analysis
Testing how a valuation changes when its key inputs are varied
