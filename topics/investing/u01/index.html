What is the main difference between saving and investing?

Saving keeps the amount you put in safe; investing accepts that the value can fall in return for a higher expected long-run return. From “Saving and investing: what is the difference?” →

Why is repaying a 20% APR credit card like earning 20%?

Each pound repaid saves 20p a year in interest, with certainty. From “Debt before investing” →

What is the compound interest formula for a lump sum?

FV = P(1 + r)^t, with r the rate per period and t the number of periods. From “Simple and compound interest” →

What is the future value of a lump sum formula?

FV = PV × (1 + r)^n. From “The time value of money” →

Why did some UK property funds suspend dealing in 2016 and 2020?

They held buildings that take months to sell, so they could not meet redemption requests quickly. From “Time horizon and liquidity” →

Lessons in this school

  1. Saving and investing: what is the difference?
    Saving keeps money safe and available; investing puts money to work in assets whose value can rise or fall, in return for the chance of a higher long-run return.
  2. Budgeting and cash flow
    Before anyone can invest, they need money left over each month.
  3. The emergency fund
    An emergency fund is easily accessible cash set aside for unexpected costs or loss of income.
  4. Debt before investing
    Paying off expensive debt is a guaranteed, risk-free return equal to its interest rate.
  5. Protecting your finances: insurance and resilience
    Insurance transfers the cost of rare but serious events to an insurer in return for a premium.
  6. Interest rates: AER, APR and the base rate
    Interest is the price of money: what borrowers pay and savers earn.
  7. Simple and compound interest
    Simple interest is paid only on the original sum; compound interest is paid on the interest already earned as well.
  8. Doubling time and the rule of 72
    The rule of 72 is a mental shortcut: dividing 72 by an annual percentage rate gives the approximate number of years for money to double.
  9. Inflation and real returns
    Inflation is the general rise in prices over time.
  10. The time value of money
    A pound today is worth more than a pound in the future, because today's pound can be invested, is certain, and is not eroded by inflation.
  11. Present value and discounting
    Discounting is compounding in reverse: it converts a future sum into its value today.
  12. Setting financial goals
    A plan starts with goals.
  13. Time horizon and liquidity
    Time horizon is how long until money is needed; liquidity is how quickly an asset can become cash without loss.
  14. Attitude to risk and capacity for loss
    Attitude to risk is how much uncertainty you are willing to accept; capacity for loss is how much you can afford to lose without harming your life.
  15. Cash savings accounts
    Cash accounts differ in access, rate and protection.
  16. Investing, speculation and gambling
    Investing, speculation and gambling all involve uncertainty, but they differ in what drives the expected return.

Key terms

Saving
Setting money aside in a form that keeps its nominal value, such as a bank account, with low risk and quick access
Net income
Take-home pay after tax, National Insurance and other deductions
Emergency fund
Easily accessible cash kept to cover unexpected costs or a loss of income
APR
Annual percentage rate: the yearly cost of borrowing including interest and compulsory fees, used to compare credit
Premium
The regular payment made to an insurer in exchange for cover
Bank Rate
The Bank of England's main policy interest rate, set by the Monetary Policy Committee
Principal
The original sum invested or borrowed
Doubling time
The time for a quantity growing at a constant compound rate to double
Inflation
The rate at which the general level of prices rises over time
Time value of money
The principle that money available now is worth more than the same amount in the future
Present value
The value today of a future cash flow, discounted at a required rate of return
Financial goal
A specific amount needed by a specific date for a stated purpose
Liquidity
How quickly and cheaply an asset can be turned into cash at close to its fair value
Attitude to risk
An investor's willingness to accept uncertainty and falls in value
