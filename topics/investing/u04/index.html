Lessons in this school

  1. Holding period return
    The holding period return measures what an investment earned over the whole time it was held, combining price change and income.
  2. Arithmetic and geometric average returns
    The arithmetic average of yearly returns is not the rate at which money actually grew.
  3. Total return and reinvested income
    Total return combines price changes with income.
  4. Nominal and real returns in practice
    Nominal returns are measured in pounds; real returns measure the change in purchasing power.
  5. Volatility and standard deviation
    Volatility measures how widely returns vary around their average, and standard deviation is the usual statistic for it.
  6. Drawdowns and recovery
    A drawdown is the fall from a peak to a subsequent low.
  7. Sequence of returns risk
    When money is being added or withdrawn, the order of returns matters, not just their average.
  8. Types of investment risk
    Investment risk is not one thing.
  9. The risk–return trade-off
    Investors require higher expected returns to hold riskier assets.
  10. The equity risk premium
    The equity risk premium is the extra return shares are expected to earn over a risk-free asset.
  11. The long-run evidence: shares, bonds and cash
    More than a century of data on UK and global markets shows shares beating bonds and cash on average, with much larger swings.
  12. Risk-adjusted return: the Sharpe ratio
    Comparing returns without considering risk can mislead.
  13. The range of outcomes and probability
    Investment results are uncertain, so it is better to think in ranges and probabilities than in single forecasts.
  14. Survivorship bias in performance data
    Survivorship bias arises when failed or closed funds and companies drop out of the data, leaving only the survivors.

Key terms

Holding period return
The total return over the period an investment is held, including income, as a percentage of the starting value
Arithmetic mean return
The simple average of periodic returns
Total return
The return from price changes plus income, assuming income is reinvested
Nominal return
A return measured in money terms, without adjusting for inflation
Volatility
The degree of variation in an investment's returns over time
Drawdown
The percentage fall in value from a peak to a subsequent trough
Sequence of returns risk
The risk that the order of returns, combined with withdrawals or contributions, harms the final outcome
Systematic risk
Risk affecting the whole market, which diversification cannot remove
Risk aversion
A preference for certain outcomes over uncertain ones with the same expected value
Equity risk premium
The extra return expected from equities over a risk-free asset
Real return
Return after adjusting for inflation
Sharpe ratio
Excess return over the risk-free rate divided by the standard deviation of returns
Percentile
The value below which a given percentage of outcomes fall
Survivorship bias
The distortion caused by analysing only those funds, companies or people that survived
