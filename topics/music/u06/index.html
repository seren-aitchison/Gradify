Lessons in this school

  1. Hearing pulse and metre
    Every aural skill rests on feeling the beat.
  2. Rhythmic dictation
    Rhythmic dictation means hearing a rhythm and writing it down.
  3. Sight-singing and pitch matching
    Sight-singing turns notation into sound without an instrument.
  4. Solfège and movable do
    Solfège gives each scale degree a syllable, so singers hear notes by their function in the key.
  5. Recognising intervals by ear
    Interval recognition is the core of hearing melody and harmony.
  6. Melodic dictation
    Melodic dictation combines rhythm, pitch and memory.
  7. Hearing chord quality
    Chords have characteristic colours.
  8. Hearing cadences
    Cadences mark the ends of phrases, and graded aural tests often ask you to name them.
  9. Hearing bass lines and inversions
    The bass line carries the harmony.
  10. Harmonic dictation
    Harmonic dictation means writing down the chords of a progression.
  11. Hearing instruments and texture
    Listening questions often ask what instruments are playing and how the parts fit together.
  12. Recognising style and period by ear
    Aural tests and listening papers often ask when a piece was written.
  13. Hearing modulation
    Modulation is a change of key.
  14. Sight-reading strategies
    Sight-reading on an instrument is a skill that improves with method and regular practice.
  15. Aural tests in graded exams
    Graded music exams from boards such as ABRSM and Trinity include aural tests that draw on all the skills in this school.

Key terms

Pulse
The steady regular beat underlying music
Rhythmic dictation
Writing down a rhythm heard aloud
Pitch matching
Singing a note at exactly the pitch heard
Movable do
Solfège in which do is the tonic of the current key
Harmonic interval
Two notes sounded together
Melodic dictation
Notating a melody from hearing
Triad
A three-note chord built from stacked thirds
Perfect cadence
V–I, a complete close
Inversion
A chord with a note other than the root in the bass
Harmonic dictation
Notating the chords of a progression heard aloud
Timbre
The tone colour that distinguishes instruments
Continuo
A bass line with improvised chords, typical of Baroque music
Modulation
A change of key established within a piece
Reading ahead
Looking beyond the notes currently being played
