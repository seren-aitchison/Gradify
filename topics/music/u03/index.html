Lessons in this school

  1. Triads: root, third and fifth
    A triad is a three-note chord built from a root, a third above it and a fifth above it.
  2. Major, minor, diminished and augmented triads
    Triads come in four qualities, depending on the size of their thirds and fifth.
  3. Inversions and figured bass symbols
    A triad can be arranged with its root, third or fifth in the bass.
  4. Diatonic triads and Roman numerals
    Each degree of a scale can carry a triad built from the notes of the key.
  5. Chord symbols in popular music
    Popular and jazz music uses chord symbols written above the melody instead of Roman numerals.
  6. The dominant seventh
    The dominant seventh chord, V7, adds a minor seventh to the dominant triad and is the strongest chord leading to the tonic.
  7. Types of seventh chord
    Adding a seventh to a triad creates a seventh chord.
  8. Diatonic seventh chords
    Adding a seventh to each diatonic triad gives the diatonic seventh chords of a key.
  9. Tonic, predominant and dominant function
    Chords in a key fall into functional groups: tonic chords give rest, predominant chords prepare the dominant, and dominant chords drive back to the tonic.
  10. Progressions by fifth and the circle of fifths sequence
    Root movement by falling fifth is the strongest and most common progression in tonal music.
  11. Harmonic rhythm
    Harmonic rhythm is the rate at which chords change.
  12. Harmonising a melody
    Harmonising a melody means choosing chords that fit its notes and support its phrases.
  13. Secondary dominants
    A secondary dominant is the dominant of a chord other than the tonic, borrowed from another key.
  14. Modulation to related keys
    Modulation is a change of key confirmed by a cadence.
  15. Modal mixture
    Modal mixture (borrowed chords) means using chords from the parallel minor in a major key, or vice versa.
  16. The Neapolitan sixth
    The Neapolitan sixth is a major chord built on the flattened second degree, usually in first inversion, used as a dramatic predominant.
  17. Augmented sixth chords
    Augmented sixth chords are chromatic predominants containing an augmented sixth that expands outwards to an octave on the dominant.
  18. The diminished seventh chord
    The diminished seventh is a four-note chord of stacked minor thirds, built on the leading note in minor keys.
  19. Extended chords: ninths, elevenths and thirteenths
    Extended chords add notes beyond the seventh by continuing to stack thirds.

Key terms

Triad
A three-note chord built from a root, a third and a fifth
Major triad
A triad with a major third and a perfect fifth above the root
First inversion
A chord with its third in the bass
Diatonic
Using only the notes of the key
Chord symbol
A label above the music naming a chord's root and quality, such as Am7
Dominant seventh
A major triad on the dominant with a minor seventh added: V7
Major seventh chord
A major triad with a major seventh above the root
Diatonic seventh chord
A seventh chord using only the notes of the key
Tonic function
Harmonic stability and arrival, centred on I
Root motion
The interval by which the roots of successive chords move
Harmonic rhythm
The rate at which chords change in a passage of music
Harmonisation
Adding chords to a melody
Secondary dominant
A dominant or dominant seventh chord of a diatonic chord other than the tonic
Modulation
A change of key, confirmed by a cadence in the new key
