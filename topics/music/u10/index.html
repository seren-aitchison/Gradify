Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What is an idée fixe?

A recurring melody that represents an idea or person, as in Berlioz's Symphonie fantastique. From “Romanticism in music” →

In plain English: Romanticism dominated music for most of the nineteenth century. Key idea: Romanticism valued emotion, individuality, nature and the supernatural, expressed through expanded orchestras, chromatic harmony, wide dynamics and lyrical melody, shaped by middle-class concert life and the idea of the composer as artist.

What is a concert overture?

A one-movement orchestral piece, often programmatic, written for concert performance rather than to open an opera. From “Programme music and the symphonic poem” →

In plain English: Programme music tells a story or depicts a scene without words. Key idea: Programme music links instrumental music to a story or image, unlike absolute music; Romantic genres include the programme symphony, concert overture and Liszt's symphonic poem; composers depict stories through orchestration, motifs and thematic transformation.

How did Bartók collect folk songs?

He travelled to villages and recorded singers on a phonograph, then transcribed the songs. From “Nationalism in music” →

In plain English: In the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, many composers sought to express national identity through music. Key idea: Musical nationalism grew from political movements and Romantic ideas, using folk song, dance, legend and landscape; examples include the Russian Five, Smetana, Dvořák, Grieg, Sibelius, Vaughan Williams and Bartók; it enriched music but could idealise the nation.

What are the four basic forms of a tone row?

Prime, retrograde, inversion and retrograde inversion. From “Serialism” →

In plain English: Serialism gave composers a systematic way to organise atonal music. Key idea: Schoenberg's twelve-tone method orders all twelve pitches in a row, used in prime, retrograde, inversion and retrograde inversion forms at any transposition (48 forms); total serialism extended this to other parameters after 1945.

What is musique concrète?

Music made from recorded sounds manipulated by techniques such as cutting, looping, reversing and speed change. From “Electronic and experimental music” →

In plain English: Technology and new ideas about sound transformed music in the twentieth century. Key idea: Musique concrète manipulated recorded sounds while electronic studios generated new ones; the BBC Radiophonic Workshop, Daphne Oram and Delia Derbyshire pioneered electronic sound in Britain; Cage used prepared piano, chance and silence to question what music is.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Music History: Romantic to the Present

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which feature is typical of Romantic music? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Chromatic harmony and a wide dynamic range. Romantic composers used richer harmony and greater contrasts.

  2. How many characters does the singer portray in Erlkönig? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Four. The narrator, father, son and Erl-King are all sung by one voice.

  3. Which work depicts a river from its source to Prague? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Smetana's Vltava. Vltava traces the river's journey through Bohemia.

  4. Why did public concerts grow in the nineteenth century? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    The growing middle class could pay to attend. Middle-class audiences supported public concert life.

  5. What does the piano depict in Gretchen am Spinnrade? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    The turning of a spinning wheel. Continuous figuration imitates the spinning wheel.

  6. What is thematic transformation? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Changing a theme's character to reflect events in a story. Liszt transformed themes to follow programmes.

  7. Which critic defended absolute music against programme music? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Eduard Hanslick. Hanslick argued music's beauty lies in its forms rather than stories.

  8. Why is Erlkönig through-composed rather than strophic? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    The music follows the rising drama of the story. A strophic setting could not follow the escalating tension.

  9. Why did Hanslick criticise programme music? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    He believed music's value lies in its forms, not in stories. Hanslick defended absolute music, valued for its musical forms alone.

Key takeaways

  • Romanticism valued emotion, individuality, nature and the supernatural, expressed through expanded orchestras, chromatic harmony, wide dynamics and lyrical melody, shaped by middle-class concert life and the idea of the composer as artist.
  • The Lied sets German poetry for voice and piano, with the piano as an equal partner; Schubert's Erlkönig is through-composed with distinct characters and a galloping accompaniment; song cycles such as Winterreise and Dichterliebe link songs into a story.
  • Programme music links instrumental music to a story or image, unlike absolute music; Romantic genres include the programme symphony, concert overture and Liszt's symphonic poem; composers depict stories through orchestration, motifs and thematic transformation.
  • Iron frames, wider ranges and the sustaining pedal transformed the piano; Romantic composers wrote character pieces such as nocturnes, études and mazurkas; Chopin favoured lyrical, refined expression while Liszt pursued orchestral virtuosity.
  • Verdi's operas focus on vocal melody and drama and became linked with Italian nationalism; Wagner's music dramas aimed at a total work of art, using continuous music, leitmotifs and chromatic harmony, with a legacy complicated by his antisemitism.
  • Musical nationalism grew from political movements and Romantic ideas, using folk song, dance, legend and landscape; examples include the Russian Five, Smetana, Dvořák, Grieg, Sibelius, Vaughan Williams and Bartók; it enriched music but could idealise the nation.

Common mistakes in Music History: Romantic to the Present

  • Thinking Romantic music is about romantic love only. "Romantic" refers to a broad movement valuing emotion, imagination and the individual.
  • Treating the piano in a Lied as a mere accompaniment. It depicts images and emotions as an equal partner to the voice.
  • Assuming listeners can hear the story without the programme. Much programme music depends on titles and written descriptions.
  • Thinking études are only technical exercises. Chopin's and Liszt's études are concert works of great musical depth.
  • Thinking leitmotifs are simply repeated tunes. They are transformed and combined to express developing meanings in the drama.
  • Assuming nationalist composers simply quoted folk songs. Many created original melodies in folk style or absorbed folk features into their own language.

    Romanticism dominated music for most of the nineteenth century.

    The Lied, a German art song for voice and piano, was one of the great Romantic genres.
  3. Programme music and the symphonic poem
    Programme music tells a story or depicts a scene without words.

    The piano was the central instrument of Romantic music-making.
  5. Romantic opera: Verdi and Wagner

  6. Nationalism in music
    In the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, many composers sought to express national identity through music.
  7. Impressionism
    Impressionism in music, led by Claude Debussy, moved away from German Romantic rhetoric towards colour, atmosphere and suggestion.
  8. Expressionism and atonality
    In the early twentieth century, some composers abandoned tonality altogether.
  9. Serialism
    Serialism gave composers a systematic way to organise atonal music.
  10. Neoclassicism
    Between the world wars, many composers turned back to the forms and clarity of earlier music.
  11. Minimalism
    Minimalism emerged in the United States in the 1960s as a radical simplification of musical language.
  12. Electronic and experimental music
    Technology and new ideas about sound transformed music in the twentieth century.
  13. Film music
    Film music shapes how audiences feel and understand what they see.
  14. Music since 1975
    Since the 1970s, classical composition has become highly diverse, with no single dominant style.

Music History: Romantic to the Present: questions students ask

What is a Lied?

A German art song for solo voice and piano setting a poem of literary quality.

What technical changes made the Romantic piano more powerful?

Iron frames, thicker strings, wider range and improved actions.

What is a Gesamtkunstwerk?

Wagner's ideal of a total work of art uniting music, poetry, drama and staging.

What is planing?

Moving a chord shape in parallel motion, rather than following functional harmonic progressions.
