Lessons in this school

  1. Melodic contour and range
    A melody's shape and span give it character before harmony or rhythm are considered.
  2. Phrases and periods
    Melodies are organised into phrases, which combine into larger units such as periods and sentences.
  3. Motif and sequence
    Short musical ideas, or motifs, give music unity, and sequences repeat them at different pitches.
  4. Melodic development
    Composers transform musical ideas to sustain interest while maintaining unity.
  5. Writing a melody
    Writing a good melody combines craft and imagination.
  6. Binary form
    Binary form divides a piece into two sections, each usually repeated.
  7. Ternary form
    Ternary form, ABA, presents a section, a contrast and a return.
  8. Strophic and through-composed forms
    Songs can repeat the same music for each verse or set the text with continuously new music.
  9. Rondo form
    Rondo form alternates a recurring main theme with contrasting episodes.
  10. Theme and variations
    In theme and variations, a theme is stated and then transformed in a series of variations.
  11. Minuet and trio, scherzo and trio
    The minuet and trio, and later the scherzo and trio, provided the dance-like third movement of many Classical works.
  12. Ground bass, ostinato and passacaglia
    Some music is built over a repeating bass or pattern.
  13. Sonata form
    Sonata form is the most important instrumental form of the Classical and Romantic periods.
  14. The concerto
    The concerto sets a soloist or group of soloists against an orchestra.
  15. The symphony
    The symphony became the most prestigious orchestral genre.
  16. Cyclic form and leitmotif
    Nineteenth-century composers sought unity across large works by bringing back themes.

Key terms

Contour
The overall shape of a melody's rise and fall
Phrase
A musical unit ending with a cadence
Motif
A short, distinctive musical idea that recurs and is developed
Inversion
Reversing the direction of each interval in a melody
Gap-fill
A leap followed by stepwise motion filling in the gap
Binary form
A form in two sections, A and B, usually each repeated
Ternary form
A three-part form, A B A
Strophic
A song form using the same music for every verse
Rondo
A form alternating a recurring refrain with contrasting episodes
Variation
A transformed version of a theme, keeping some of its features
Minuet
A dance in triple time, the usual third movement in Classical works
Ostinato
A short pattern repeated persistently
Exposition
The first section of sonata form, presenting the themes in tonic and second key
Ritornello
A recurring orchestral passage in a Baroque concerto
