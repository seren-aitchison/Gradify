Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

Name the five parts of the Mass Ordinary.

Kyrie, Gloria, Credo, Sanctus and Agnus Dei. From “Plainchant” →

In plain English: Plainchant is the unaccompanied, single-line sung music of the medieval Latin church, and the foundation of Western music. Key idea: Plainchant is monophonic, unaccompanied Latin liturgical song in free rhythm, organised by the church modes and sung in the Mass and Office; staff notation developed from neumes, helped by Guido of Arezzo.

What is a cantus firmus Mass?

A Mass built on a pre-existing melody sung in long notes in one voice. From “Renaissance sacred polyphony” →

In plain English: Renaissance church music developed a smooth, balanced polyphony built on imitation between voices. Key idea: Renaissance sacred polyphony uses smooth lines, careful dissonance and points of imitation; Masses were built on cantus firmus, paraphrase or parody techniques; the Reformation and the Council of Trent encouraged clarity of text.

Which instruments typically play the continuo?

A bass instrument such as a cello or bassoon and a chordal instrument such as a harpsichord, organ or lute. From “Baroque style and the basso continuo” →

In plain English: Baroque music, from about 1600 to 1750, is built on a bass line with chords filled in above it. Key idea: The basso continuo combines a bass instrument and an improvising chordal instrument reading figured bass; Baroque style features tonality, terraced dynamics, motor rhythms, ornamentation and the expression of a single affection.

What post did Bach hold in Leipzig from 1723?

Thomaskantor, director of music at St Thomas Church. From “Bach and Handel” →

In plain English: Johann Sebastian Bach and George Frideric Handel, both born in Germany in 1685, are the towering figures of the late Baroque. Key idea: Bach, Leipzig's Thomaskantor, wrote cantatas, passions and keyboard fugues of supreme contrapuntal skill; Handel, a London-based opera and oratorio composer, wrote Messiah and Zadok the Priest; both shaped the late Baroque.

Which instruments make up a string quartet?

Two violins, a viola and a cello. From “The string quartet” →

In plain English: The string quartet became the most prestigious chamber genre of the Classical period. Key idea: The Classical string quartet for two violins, viola and cello was developed by Haydn, especially in Op. 33, and by Mozart in his quartets dedicated to Haydn; its equal, conversational textures made it a prestigious genre continued by Beethoven.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Music History: Medieval to Classical

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. What is the texture of plainchant? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Monophonic. Chant is a single melodic line sung without harmony.

  2. In parallel organum, at what interval does the added voice usually move? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    A fourth or a fifth. Early treatises describe parallel motion at the fourth or fifth.

  3. Who composed the Messe de Nostre Dame? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Guillaume de Machaut. Machaut set the complete Mass Ordinary polyphonically.

  4. Which part of the Mass changes according to the day or feast? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    The Proper. Proper texts vary; Ordinary texts stay the same.

  5. Which composer wrote four-voice organa such as "Viderunt omnes"? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Pérotin. Pérotin expanded organum to three and four voices.

  6. What is isorhythm? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    A repeating rhythmic pattern combined with a repeating melody. Talea and color combine to structure a piece.

  7. What was Guido of Arezzo's main contribution? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    A staff that fixed pitch and solmisation syllables for teaching. His methods made it possible to learn new chants from notation.

  8. How did the motet originate? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    By adding new words to the upper voices of clausulae. Texted clausulae became the earliest motets.

  9. Which development most distinguished the ars nova from the ars antiqua? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    The use of duple as well as triple rhythmic divisions. New note divisions allowed more varied rhythm.

Key takeaways

  • Plainchant is monophonic, unaccompanied Latin liturgical song in free rhythm, organised by the church modes and sung in the Mass and Office; staff notation developed from neumes, helped by Guido of Arezzo.
  • Polyphony grew from parallel organum to free and florid organum; the Notre Dame composers Léonin and Pérotin used rhythmic modes and wrote three- and four-voice organa; adding texts to clausulae created the motet.
  • The ars nova introduced duple divisions, new rhythms and isorhythm; Machaut wrote the first known complete polyphonic Mass Ordinary by one composer and mastered the formes fixes; troubadours, trouvères and Italian composers shaped medieval song.
  • Renaissance sacred polyphony uses smooth lines, careful dissonance and points of imitation; Masses were built on cantus firmus, paraphrase or parody techniques; the Reformation and the Council of Trent encouraged clarity of text.
  • The madrigal set secular poetry for several solo voices, using word painting and contrasting textures; the English madrigal followed Musica transalpina, and Monteverdi's seconda pratica let expression of words justify freer dissonance.
  • Renaissance instrumental music used viols, lutes, recorders, shawms, sackbuts and keyboards in whole and broken consorts; genres included paired dances, variations and fantasias; Gabrieli's Venice introduced polychoral effects and specified dynamics and instruments.

Common mistakes in Music History: Medieval to Classical

  • Assuming Pope Gregory composed Gregorian chant. The repertory developed over centuries and was named after him later.
  • Thinking the tenor was the highest voice. In medieval polyphony the tenor held the chant and was usually the lowest part.
  • Assuming all medieval music was sacred. Secular songs of love and politics flourished alongside church music.
  • Believing Palestrina single-handedly saved church polyphony at Trent. This is a later legend, though his style became a model.
  • Assuming madrigals are always cheerful. Many Italian madrigals set serious, melancholy poetry with intense chromaticism.
  • Assuming Renaissance music always specified instruments. Most parts could be played by whatever instruments were available.

Lessons in this school

  1. Plainchant
    Plainchant is the unaccompanied, single-line sung music of the medieval Latin church, and the foundation of Western music.
  2. Organum and early polyphony
    Polyphony, music in several independent parts, grew from adding voices to chant.
  3. Ars nova and medieval song
    In fourteenth-century France a "new art" brought new rhythms and forms to music.
  4. Renaissance sacred polyphony
    Renaissance church music developed a smooth, balanced polyphony built on imitation between voices.
  5. The madrigal
    The madrigal was the leading secular vocal form of the sixteenth century, setting poetry with vivid expression.
  6. Renaissance instrumental music
    Instrumental music grew in importance during the Renaissance, from dance music to keyboard variations and the brass music of Venice.
  7. Baroque style and the basso continuo
    Baroque music, from about 1600 to 1750, is built on a bass line with chords filled in above it.
  8. Baroque opera and oratorio
    Opera was born around 1600 and quickly spread across Europe, while oratorio offered sacred drama without staging.
  9. The concerto grosso and the suite
    The concerto and the suite were the leading instrumental forms of the Baroque.
  10. Bach and Handel
    Johann Sebastian Bach and George Frideric Handel, both born in Germany in 1685, are the towering figures of the late Baroque.
  11. The Classical style
    The Classical period, from about 1750 to 1820, valued clarity, balance and contrast.
  12. Haydn, Mozart and the Classical symphony
    The symphony became the central orchestral genre of the Classical period.
  13. The string quartet
    The string quartet became the most prestigious chamber genre of the Classical period.
  14. Classical opera
    Classical opera reformed the conventions of Baroque opera and developed comic opera into a vehicle for drama and character.
  15. Beethoven
    Ludwig van Beethoven transformed the Classical style and opened the way to Romanticism.

Music History: Medieval to Classical: questions students ask

What is syllabic text setting?

One note for each syllable of text.

What was the Magnus liber organi?

The "great book of organum", two-voice organa associated with Léonin at Notre Dame.

What are the three formes fixes?

The ballade, rondeau and virelai.

What is word painting?

Illustrating the meaning of words in the music, such as rising notes for "ascend".
