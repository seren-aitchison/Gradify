Lessons in this school

  1. Sound waves: frequency, amplitude and pitch
    Musical sound is a pressure wave travelling through the air.
  2. The harmonic series and timbre
    A single musical note contains many frequencies sounding together.
  3. Standing waves in strings and pipes
    Strings and air columns produce musical notes by forming standing waves.
  4. Beats, tuning and temperament
    When two nearly matching notes sound together they produce beats, which musicians use to tune.
  5. Loudness, decibels and hearing
    Our ears respond to an enormous range of sound intensities, so loudness is measured on a logarithmic scale.
  6. Room acoustics
    The room is part of the instrument: its size, shape and surfaces shape how music sounds.
  7. The string family
    Bowed strings form the core of the orchestra.
  8. The woodwind family
    Woodwind instruments make sound by setting an air column vibrating with an edge or a reed.
  9. The brass family
    Brass players make sound by buzzing their lips into a cup-shaped mouthpiece.
  10. Percussion
    Percussion instruments are struck, shaken or scraped and range from tuned timpani to unpitched cymbals.
  11. Keyboard instruments
    Keyboard instruments let one player control many notes at once.
  12. The voice
    The voice is the oldest instrument and the only one carried inside the body.
  13. Electronic instruments and music technology
    Electronic instruments generate and shape sound with circuits and software.
  14. Transposing instruments
    For some instruments, the written note is not the note that sounds.
  15. Ranges and registers
    Every instrument has a range of possible notes and distinct registers with different colours and strengths.
  16. The orchestra and its layout
    The symphony orchestra grew from a small Baroque ensemble into the large Romantic orchestra.
  17. Doubling and balance
    Doubling a line on more than one instrument changes its weight, colour and clarity.
  18. Writing for strings
    The string section is the most flexible part of the orchestra.
  19. Orchestral colour
    Orchestral colour is the art of combining instruments to create distinctive timbres.
  20. Reading a full score
    A full score shows every instrument's part, arranged in a standard order.
  21. Chamber ensembles
    Chamber music is played by small groups with one player to a part and usually no conductor.

Key terms

Frequency
The number of complete vibrations per second, measured in hertz
Fundamental
The lowest frequency of a vibrating system, which determines the perceived pitch
Standing wave
A wave pattern with fixed nodes and antinodes formed by two waves travelling in opposite directions
Beats
Periodic variations in loudness when two tones of slightly different frequency sound together
Decibel
A logarithmic unit comparing a sound level with a reference level
Reverberation time
The time for sound in a room to decay by 60 decibels after the source stops
Pizzicato
Plucking the strings instead of bowing
Double reed
Two blades of cane that vibrate against each other, as in the oboe and bassoon
Valve
A mechanism that diverts air through extra tubing to lower the pitch
Idiophone
An instrument whose own body vibrates to produce sound
Plectrum
A small pick that plucks a string, as in a harpsichord jack
Vocal folds
Folds of tissue in the larynx that vibrate to produce voiced sound
Oscillator
A circuit or algorithm that generates a repeating waveform
Transposing instrument
An instrument whose music is written at a different pitch from the sound produced
