Which chord is used in bar 9 of a standard twelve-bar blues?

The dominant, V (often V7). From “The blues” →

In plain English: The blues grew out of African American communities in the southern United States around the end of the nineteenth century and became the foundation of jazz, rhythm and blues and rock. Key idea: The blues combines a twelve-bar I–IV–V framework, AAB lyrics, blue notes and call and response, and developed from rural solo performance to amplified urban bands.

Which 1959 Miles Davis album is the best-known example of modal jazz?

Kind of Blue. From “Bebop and modern jazz” →

In plain English: In the 1940s bebop turned jazz from dance music into a demanding art music for listening, and later styles from cool jazz to free jazz grew from it. Key idea: Bebop brought small groups, fast tempos, extended harmony, chromatic lines and contrafacts, and was followed by cool, hard bop, modal and free jazz.

How many bars are there in each section of a standard AABA song?

Eight bars, giving 32 in total. From “Popular song structure” →

In plain English: Popular songs are built from a small set of sections arranged in recognisable forms. Key idea: Popular songs use strophic, AABA 32-bar and verse–chorus forms, built from intro, verse, pre-chorus, chorus, bridge and outro, with hooks to make them memorable.

What is an eleven o'clock number?

A showstopping song late in the second act. From “Musical theatre” →

In plain English: Musical theatre combines songs, spoken dialogue and dance to tell a story on stage. Key idea: The musical grew from operetta and vaudeville to the integrated musical of Oklahoma!, then to concept musicals, rock musicals and megamusicals, using song types such as the I want song and reprise to drive drama.

What instrument is the main sound of tango, besides violin and piano?

The bandoneon. From “Latin American music” →

In plain English: Latin American music combines Indigenous, European and especially African traditions in a wide range of styles. Key idea: Afro-Cuban music is built around clave, with son and salsa using montuno and tumbao, while tango, samba and bossa nova developed in Argentina and Brazil, and Latin rhythms shaped jazz from the Spanish tinge to Afro-Cuban jazz and bossa nova.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Popular Music, Jazz and World Music

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. In a standard twelve-bar blues, how many bars of the tonic chord open the progression? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Four. The first line of the blues sits on four bars of chord I before the move to IV in bar 5.

  2. Which piano style, associated with Scott Joplin, was a major source of early jazz? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Ragtime. Joplin's ragtime, with its syncopation and multi-strain forms, fed directly into early jazz.

  3. Which instruments form the rhythm section of a swing big band? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Piano, guitar, double bass and drums. The swing rhythm section was piano, guitar, double bass and drums.

  4. Which notes are usually treated as blue notes? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    The flattened third, fifth and seventh. Blue notes are the flattened or bent 3rd, 5th and 7th, often pitched between major and minor.

  5. In New Orleans collective improvisation, what does the clarinet usually do? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Weave fast decorative lines above the lead. The clarinet adds an obbligato above the cornet lead in New Orleans style.

  6. In a big band arrangement, what is a soli? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    A whole section playing a melody in close harmony. A soli is a harmonised melody played by a complete section, not a single soloist.

  7. An early 1950s Chicago recording features amplified guitar, harmonica, bass and drums. Which style is this most likely to be? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Urban electric blues. Amplified small bands in Chicago after the Great Migration define urban electric blues.

  8. Why are the Hot Five recordings seen as a turning point in jazz history? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    They put an individual soloist at the centre of the music. Armstrong's virtuoso solos shifted jazz away from collective polyphony towards the soloist.

  9. A band builds a blues on riffs worked out by ear, with a light, driving rhythm section and spare piano. Which bandleader's approach does this most resemble? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Count Basie. Riff-based head arrangements and a light rhythm section were hallmarks of Basie's Kansas City band.

Key takeaways

  • The blues combines a twelve-bar I–IV–V framework, AAB lyrics, blue notes and call and response, and developed from rural solo performance to amplified urban bands.
  • Jazz grew in New Orleans from ragtime, blues, brass bands and Creole traditions, first as collective improvisation by cornet, clarinet and trombone, then, through Louis Armstrong, as a soloist's art.
  • The swing big band used saxophone, trumpet, trombone and rhythm sections, a four-beat walking rhythm and arranged riffs and call and response, with Ellington, Basie, Goodman and Miller as leading figures.
  • Bebop brought small groups, fast tempos, extended harmony, chromatic lines and contrafacts, and was followed by cool, hard bop, modal and free jazz.
  • The ii–V–I uses a minor seventh, dominant seventh and major seventh with roots falling by fifths; guide tones move smoothly, and extensions and tritone substitution add colour.
  • Jazz improvisers prepare through transcription, practice and learning tunes, then build solos through motivic development, paraphrase, licks and interaction within a shared form; many other traditions also balance rules and spontaneity.

Common mistakes in Popular Music, Jazz and World Music

  • Thinking every blues is twelve bars long. Eight-bar and sixteen-bar blues are common, and early rural players often stretched or shortened bars to fit the words.
  • Assuming early jazz was mainly solo improvisation. In New Orleans style the front line improvised together; the extended solo became central only in the later 1920s.
  • Thinking big band music was fully improvised. Most of it was arranged and written down; improvisation was confined to solos set within the arrangement.
  • Believing bebop had no link to swing. Bebop players came from swing bands, used the same popular-song and blues forms, and Parker's style grew from blues and Kansas City swing.
  • Treating the 7 in a chord symbol as always a major seventh. In jazz notation C7 is a dominant seventh with B♭; a major seventh is written Cmaj7 or CΔ7.
  • Thinking that improvisation means playing anything at all. Improvisers work within shared forms, harmonies and style rules, and draw on a large learned vocabulary.

Lessons in this school

  1. The blues
    The blues grew out of African American communities in the southern United States around the end of the nineteenth century and became the foundation of jazz, rhythm and blues and rock.
  2. The origins of jazz
    Jazz developed in New Orleans in the first two decades of the twentieth century from a mix of blues, ragtime, brass band music and Caribbean and Creole traditions.
  3. Swing and the big band
    In the 1930s and early 1940s jazz became America's popular dance music through the big bands of the swing era.
  4. Bebop and modern jazz
    In the 1940s bebop turned jazz from dance music into a demanding art music for listening, and later styles from cool jazz to free jazz grew from it.
  5. Jazz harmony and the ii–V–I
    The ii–V–I progression is the most important chord pattern in jazz.
  6. Improvisation
    Improvisation is the creation of music in the moment, and it is central to jazz but also found in many other traditions.
  7. Rock and roll and the birth of pop
    Rock and roll emerged in the United States in the mid-1950s from rhythm and blues, country music and gospel, and created a new teenage popular music.
  8. Popular song structure
    Popular songs are built from a small set of sections arranged in recognisable forms.
  9. Rock, soul and funk
    In the 1960s and 1970s rock, soul and funk developed from rock and roll and rhythm and blues in different directions.
  10. Hip hop and electronic dance music
    Hip hop and electronic dance music both grew from DJ culture in the 1970s and 1980s and changed how popular music is made.
  11. Musical theatre
    Musical theatre combines songs, spoken dialogue and dance to tell a story on stage.
  12. Indian classical music: raga and tala
    Indian classical music is built on raga, a framework for melody, and tala, a cycle of beats.
  13. West African drumming
    West African drumming traditions build music from layered, interlocking rhythmic parts guided by a timeline and led by a master drummer.
  14. Indonesian gamelan
    Gamelan is the ensemble music of Java and Bali, played mainly on bronze gongs and metallophones.
  15. Latin American music
    Latin American music combines Indigenous, European and especially African traditions in a wide range of styles.
  16. Arabic music and maqam
    Arabic classical music is based on the maqam, a modal system that includes intervals not found in Western equal temperament.
  17. Folk music of the British Isles
    The folk music of England, Scotland, Wales and Ireland was passed on orally and has been collected, revived and reinvented.
  18. Ethnomusicology
    Ethnomusicology is the study of music in its cultural context, across all the world's traditions, including Western ones.

Popular Music, Jazz and World Music: questions students ask

Which group made the first jazz recordings, in 1917?

The Original Dixieland Jass Band.

Which instruments normally make up a swing saxophone section?

Two altos, two tenors and a baritone.

Where were many early bebop jam sessions held?

Minton's Playhouse in Harlem, New York.

Which chord quality is usually used on ii in a minor-key ii–V–I?

Half-diminished seventh (m7♭5).
