Lessons in this school

  1. The founding of the NHS
    The National Health Service was launched on 5 July 1948 to provide health care for everyone, free at the point of use.
  2. How the NHS in England is organised
    The NHS in England is a complex system of national bodies, local planning organisations and providers.
  3. Health care across the four UK nations
    Health is a devolved responsibility, so the NHS in England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland has developed differently.
  4. Primary care
    Primary care is the first point of contact with health services for most people.
  5. Secondary and tertiary care
    Secondary care is specialist care usually provided in hospitals after referral, and tertiary care is highly specialised care.
  6. Funding health care
    Health care can be paid for through taxation, social insurance, private insurance or direct payments.
  7. Social care
    Social care supports people who need help with daily living because of age, illness or disability.
  8. Integrated care
    Integrated care joins up health and social care services around the needs of people rather than organisations.
  9. Urgent and emergency care
    Urgent and emergency care covers services for people who need help quickly, from NHS 111 to ambulances and emergency departments.
  10. Choosing the right NHS service
    Using the most appropriate service helps people get the right care quickly and eases pressure on the NHS.
  11. Mental health services
    Mental health services range from self-help and talking therapies to crisis teams and inpatient care.
  12. Quality and safety in health care
    High-quality health care is safe, effective and centred on patients.
  13. Regulation of health and social care
    Regulators protect the public by setting standards for services and professionals and acting when they are not met.
  14. Consent and mental capacity
    People have the right to decide what happens to their bodies.
  15. Confidentiality and health data
    People share personal information with health and care services on the understanding that it will be kept confidential.
  16. Ethical principles in health care
    Health and care professionals regularly face ethical choices.
  17. NICE and priority setting
    No health system can afford every treatment, so choices must be made.
  18. Comparing health systems
    Countries organise and fund health care in different ways, with different results.

Key terms

Beveridge Report
The 1942 report proposing a welfare state to tackle want, disease, ignorance, squalor and idleness
Integrated care system
A partnership of NHS and local organisations planning health and care for an area
Devolution
The transfer of powers from the UK Parliament to the governments of Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland
Primary care
First-contact health care in the community, including GPs, pharmacists, dentists and optometrists
Secondary care
Specialist care usually provided after referral, often in hospital
General taxation
Revenue raised from taxes such as income tax and VAT, used for public spending
Social care
Support with personal care and daily living for people with care needs
Integrated care
Care that is coordinated across services around the needs of the person
Urgent treatment centre
A walk-in or booked service for urgent but not life-threatening conditions
Self-care
Looking after one's own minor health problems with rest and over-the-counter remedies
Stepped care
Providing the least intensive effective treatment first and stepping up as needed
Clinical governance
The framework through which organisations are accountable for improving the quality of care
Care Quality Commission
The independent regulator of health and adult social care in England
Mental capacity
The ability to make a particular decision at the time it needs to be made
