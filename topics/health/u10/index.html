Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

When was the NHS launched?

On 5 July 1948. From “The founding of the NHS” →

In plain English: The National Health Service was launched on 5 July 1948 to provide health care for everyone, free at the point of use. Key idea: The NHS was founded in 1948 after the Beveridge Report, led by Aneurin Bevan, to be comprehensive, free at the point of use and based on clinical need; demand and cost pressures led to some charges from the early 1950s.

What is gatekeeping in the NHS?

GPs controlling access to specialist hospital care through referral. From “Primary care” →

In plain English: Primary care is the first point of contact with health services for most people. Key idea: Primary care includes general practice, pharmacy, dentistry and optometry; GPs provide first contact, continuity and gatekeeping, supported by primary care networks and expanded pharmacy services.

What is the Better Care Fund?

A pooled budget linking NHS and local authority spending to support integrated care. From “Integrated care” →

In plain English: Integrated care joins up health and social care services around the needs of people rather than organisations. Key idea: Integrated care joins up health and social care around people's needs through structures, budgets, teams and shared records; it improves experience, but evidence on reducing admissions and costs is mixed.

What does parity of esteem mean?

Giving mental health the same value and priority as physical health. From “Mental health services” →

In plain English: Mental health services range from self-help and talking therapies to crisis teams and inpatient care. Key idea: Mental health care uses stepped care from primary care and talking therapies to community, crisis and inpatient services; the Mental Health Act allows detention with safeguards; parity of esteem aims to value mental and physical health equally.

What is a Caldicott Guardian?

A senior person in an organisation responsible for protecting the confidentiality of people's information. From “Confidentiality and health data” →

In plain English: People share personal information with health and care services on the understanding that it will be kept confidential. Key idea: Confidentiality underpins trust; the Caldicott principles and data protection law govern health data; information may be shared with consent, when required by law or in the public interest to prevent serious harm.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Health and Social Care Systems

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which minister led the creation of the NHS? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Aneurin Bevan. Aneurin Bevan was Minister of Health and steered the 1946 Act through Parliament.

  2. Which body sets health policy for England? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    The Department of Health and Social Care. The DHSC, led by the Secretary of State, sets policy and funding.

  3. In which year did health become a devolved responsibility in Scotland and Wales? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    1999. Devolved legislatures took on health responsibilities in 1999.

  4. Which principle means patients do not pay when they receive treatment? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Free at the point of use. NHS care is funded mainly by taxation, not charges at the time of care.

  5. What is the main role of an integrated care board? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Planning and paying for most local NHS services. ICBs commission services for their population.

  6. How is NHS care planned and delivered in Wales? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    By seven local health boards. Welsh local health boards plan and deliver care for their areas.

  7. Why did Aneurin Bevan resign in 1951? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Charges were introduced for dentures and glasses. Bevan resigned in protest at charges that breached the free-at-the-point-of-use principle.

  8. What was the main aim of the Health and Care Act 2022? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    To encourage collaboration rather than competition. ICSs bring organisations together to plan and integrate care.

  9. Which policy most clearly distinguishes Scotland's social care from England's? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Free personal care for adults assessed as needing it. Scotland funds personal care without means-testing; England means-tests social care.

Key takeaways

  • The NHS was founded in 1948 after the Beveridge Report, led by Aneurin Bevan, to be comprehensive, free at the point of use and based on clinical need; demand and cost pressures led to some charges from the early 1950s.
  • The DHSC sets policy and funding; national bodies oversee, regulate and advise; 42 integrated care systems plan and pay for local services; trusts, GP practices and others provide care.
  • Health is devolved: England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland organise services differently, with differences in structure, prescription charges, personal care and public health policy, while sharing NHS principles.
  • Primary care includes general practice, pharmacy, dentistry and optometry; GPs provide first contact, continuity and gatekeeping, supported by primary care networks and expanded pharmacy services.
  • Secondary care provides specialist hospital care after referral, tertiary care highly specialised care in regional centres; waiting time standards include 18 weeks for planned treatment and four hours in emergency departments, both under pressure.
  • The NHS is funded mainly through general taxation; other systems use social insurance, private insurance or out-of-pocket payment; spending rises with ageing, technology, expectations and the labour-intensive nature of care.

Common mistakes in Health and Social Care Systems

  • Thinking the NHS has always been completely free for everything. Charges for prescriptions (in England), dental care and some other services have existed since the early 1950s.
  • Thinking the NHS is one single organisation. It is a system of many separate bodies with different roles.
  • Assuming NHS policy in England applies across the UK. Health policy is decided separately in each nation.
  • Assuming the GP is the only route into primary care. Pharmacists, dentists, optometrists and other professionals are also first points of contact.
  • Using "secondary care" to mean only hospitals. Secondary care also includes community and mental health services provided by specialists.
  • Assuming tax-funded systems are always cheaper. The UK spends a similar share of GDP to many social insurance systems; outcomes depend on how money is used.

Lessons in this school

  1. The founding of the NHS
    The National Health Service was launched on 5 July 1948 to provide health care for everyone, free at the point of use.
  2. How the NHS in England is organised
    The NHS in England is a complex system of national bodies, local planning organisations and providers.
  3. Health care across the four UK nations
    Health is a devolved responsibility, so the NHS in England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland has developed differently.
  4. Primary care
    Primary care is the first point of contact with health services for most people.
  5. Secondary and tertiary care
    Secondary care is specialist care usually provided in hospitals after referral, and tertiary care is highly specialised care.
  6. Funding health care
    Health care can be paid for through taxation, social insurance, private insurance or direct payments.
  7. Social care
    Social care supports people who need help with daily living because of age, illness or disability.
  8. Integrated care
    Integrated care joins up health and social care services around the needs of people rather than organisations.
  9. Urgent and emergency care
    Urgent and emergency care covers services for people who need help quickly, from NHS 111 to ambulances and emergency departments.
  10. Choosing the right NHS service
    Using the most appropriate service helps people get the right care quickly and eases pressure on the NHS.
  11. Mental health services
    Mental health services range from self-help and talking therapies to crisis teams and inpatient care.
  12. Quality and safety in health care
    High-quality health care is safe, effective and centred on patients.
  13. Regulation of health and social care
    Regulators protect the public by setting standards for services and professionals and acting when they are not met.
  14. Consent and mental capacity
    People have the right to decide what happens to their bodies.
  15. Confidentiality and health data
    People share personal information with health and care services on the understanding that it will be kept confidential.
  16. Ethical principles in health care
    Health and care professionals regularly face ethical choices.
  17. NICE and priority setting
    No health system can afford every treatment, so choices must be made.
  18. Comparing health systems
    Countries organise and fund health care in different ways, with different results.

Health and Social Care Systems: questions students ask

What were Beveridge's five giants?

Want, disease, ignorance, squalor and idleness.

What replaced clinical commissioning groups in 2022?

Integrated care boards, within integrated care systems.

Where are prescriptions free in the UK?

In Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.

What is a primary care network?

A group of GP practices working together to serve about 30,000 to 50,000 people, sharing staff and services.
