Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

Why do antibiotics not treat flu?

Flu is caused by a virus, and antibiotics act only on bacteria. From “Pathogens and how infections spread” →

In plain English: Infectious diseases are caused by pathogens that pass from one host to another. Key idea: Bacteria, viruses, fungi and parasites cause infection; they spread by direct and indirect contact, droplets and aerosols, the faecal–oral route, blood and vectors, and age, illness, treatment and living conditions affect who becomes ill.

What is antimicrobial stewardship?

Coordinated actions to ensure antimicrobials are used only when needed and in the right way. From “Antimicrobial resistance” →

In plain English: Antimicrobial resistance threatens to make common infections untreatable. Key idea: Antimicrobial resistance develops through natural selection when antibiotics are used; it already causes over a million deaths a year, and tackling it requires stewardship, infection prevention, surveillance, reduced farm use, new drugs and public cooperation.

Why is a TIA important even though symptoms resolve?

It is a warning sign that a full stroke may follow and needs urgent assessment. From “Stroke” →

In plain English: A stroke happens when the blood supply to part of the brain is cut off. Key idea: Strokes are ischaemic or haemorrhagic, and a TIA is an urgent warning; FAST signs mean calling 999 immediately, because rapid treatment saves brain tissue, and controlling blood pressure is key to prevention.

What is an asthma action plan?

A written plan explaining daily treatment and what to do if symptoms worsen. From “Chronic respiratory disease: asthma and COPD” →

In plain English: Asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease are the most common long-term lung conditions. Key idea: Asthma is variable, often reversible airway inflammation with triggers, managed with inhalers and an action plan; COPD is largely fixed airflow obstruction usually caused by smoking, slowed by stopping smoking; severe breathlessness needs urgent help.

Give two examples of long-term conditions.

Diabetes and asthma (also COPD, arthritis, epilepsy and dementia). From “What a long-term condition is” →

In plain English: Long-term conditions cannot currently be cured but can be managed. Key idea: Long-term conditions cannot be cured but can be managed; they affect millions of people in England, use most health service resources, and need ongoing, personalised and community-based care rather than one-off acute treatment.

  1. Which type of pathogen causes measles? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    A virus. Applying the standard result: Measles is a viral infection.

  2. Which link is the place where a pathogen lives and multiplies? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    The reservoir. Applying the standard result: Reservoirs harbour pathogens.

  3. What does a vaccine train the immune system to do? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Recognise a pathogen and respond quickly. Applying the standard result: Vaccines create immune memory.

  4. Malaria is spread by mosquitoes. What route of transmission is this? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Vector-borne. Applying the standard result: Mosquitoes act as vectors.

  5. Covering coughs and sneezes mainly breaks which link? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    The portal of exit. Applying the standard result: It stops pathogens leaving the body.

  6. Who benefits most from herd immunity? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    People who cannot be vaccinated, such as young babies. Applying the standard result: Herd immunity protects the unprotected.

  7. Which person is likely to be most vulnerable to a serious infection? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Someone receiving chemotherapy. Applying the standard result: Chemotherapy weakens immunity.

  8. Why is handwashing with soap preferred to alcohol gel during a norovirus outbreak? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Alcohol gel is less effective against norovirus. Applying the standard result: Some pathogens resist alcohol.

  9. MMR coverage in an area falls from 95% to 85%. What is the likely consequence? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Measles outbreaks become more likely. Applying the standard result: Coverage is below the threshold.

Key takeaways

  • Bacteria, viruses, fungi and parasites cause infection; they spread by direct and indirect contact, droplets and aerosols, the faecal–oral route, blood and vectors, and age, illness, treatment and living conditions affect who becomes ill.
  • Infection spreads through six links, from agent to susceptible host; breaking any link, through cleaning, respiratory hygiene, hand hygiene, protecting entry points, vaccination or isolation, stops transmission.
  • Vaccines train the immune system to recognise pathogens; when enough people are immune, herd immunity protects others, with the threshold about 1 − 1 ÷ R0, and maintaining high uptake requires confidence, convenience and good information.
  • Antimicrobial resistance develops through natural selection when antibiotics are used; it already causes over a million deaths a year, and tackling it requires stewardship, infection prevention, surveillance, reduced farm use, new drugs and public cooperation.
  • Cardiovascular disease mainly results from atherosclerosis narrowing and blocking arteries; most risk comes from modifiable factors such as smoking, blood pressure, cholesterol and diabetes, which prevention targets, and suspected heart attack needs a 999 call.
  • Hypertension usually causes no symptoms but damages many organs; NICE guidance confirms it with ambulatory or home monitoring, and it is managed with lifestyle change and, where appropriate, medicines with regular review.

Common mistakes in Disease and Long-term Conditions

  • Expecting antibiotics to cure viral infections such as colds and flu. Antibiotics act only on bacteria.
  • Relying on alcohol hand gel for every infection. Soap and water are needed for norovirus and Clostridioides difficile.
  • Believing herd immunity means unvaccinated people are always safe. When coverage falls below the threshold, outbreaks occur, as with measles.
  • Thinking people become resistant to antibiotics. It is the bacteria that become resistant.
  • Waiting to see whether chest pain passes. Suspected heart attack is an emergency: call 999.
  • Assuming feeling well means blood pressure is normal. Hypertension rarely causes symptoms, so regular checks matter.

Lessons in this school

  1. Pathogens and how infections spread
    Infectious diseases are caused by pathogens that pass from one host to another.
  2. Breaking the chain of infection
    Infection spreads through a chain of linked steps.
  3. Vaccination and herd immunity
    Vaccines are among the most effective public health interventions ever developed.
  4. Antimicrobial resistance
    Antimicrobial resistance threatens to make common infections untreatable.
  5. Cardiovascular disease
    Cardiovascular disease, affecting the heart and blood vessels, is a leading cause of death in the UK and worldwide.
  6. Hypertension
    High blood pressure rarely causes symptoms but greatly increases the risk of stroke and heart disease.
  7. Stroke
    A stroke happens when the blood supply to part of the brain is cut off.
  8. Type 2 diabetes
    Type 2 diabetes is one of the fastest-growing long-term conditions in the UK.
  9. Type 1 diabetes
    Type 1 diabetes is an autoimmune condition in which the body cannot make insulin.
  10. Cancer: causes and prevention
    About one in two people in the UK will develop cancer during their lifetime, yet many cancers are preventable.
  11. Chronic respiratory disease: asthma and COPD
    Asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease are the most common long-term lung conditions.
  12. Obesity
    More than a quarter of adults in England are living with obesity.
  13. Dementia
    Nearly a million people in the UK live with dementia.
  14. What a long-term condition is
    Long-term conditions cannot currently be cured but can be managed.
  15. Multimorbidity
    Many people live with several long-term conditions at once.
  16. Self-management and supported self-care
    People with long-term conditions manage their own health almost all of the time.
  17. Disability and health
    About a quarter of people in the UK report a disability.

Disease and Long-term Conditions: questions students ask

Which link does vaccination target?

The susceptible host.

What are memory cells?

Immune cells that remember a pathogen and enable a faster response on later exposure.

What is the difference between angina and a heart attack?

Angina is chest pain from reduced blood flow; a heart attack is when an artery is blocked and heart muscle dies.

Why is hypertension called a "silent killer"?

It usually causes no symptoms while damaging the heart, brain, kidneys and arteries.
