Lessons in this school

  1. What public health is
    Public health is about the health of whole populations rather than individual patients.
  2. A short history of public health
    Modern public health grew out of the overcrowded, disease-ridden cities of the industrial revolution.
  3. The three domains of public health
    Public health work is often divided into three domains: health protection, health improvement and improving health services.
  4. Levels of prevention
    Prevention can act before disease starts, catch it early, or reduce its harm once it has developed.
  5. Health promotion and the Ottawa Charter
    Health promotion enables people to increase control over their health and its determinants.
  6. The prevention paradox
    Geoffrey Rose observed that most cases of disease come from the many people at moderate risk, not the few at high risk.
  7. Incidence and prevalence
    Incidence and prevalence are the two basic measures of how common a disease is.
  8. Rates and standardisation
    Comparing death or disease rates between places is misleading if their populations differ in age.
  9. Relative risk and odds ratios
    Epidemiologists measure how strongly an exposure is linked with a disease by comparing groups.
  10. Epidemiological study designs
    Different questions need different study designs.
  11. Bias, confounding and chance
    Before deciding that an association is real, epidemiologists must rule out bias, confounding and chance.
  12. Causation and the Bradford Hill viewpoints
    An association between an exposure and a disease does not prove that one causes the other.
  13. Outbreak investigation
    An outbreak is more cases of a disease than expected in a particular place and time.
  14. Health surveillance
    Public health surveillance is the continuous collection, analysis and use of health data to guide action.
  15. The global burden of disease
    Measuring the burden of disease shows which conditions cause the most death and disability worldwide.
  16. Global health goals and organisations
    Global health is shaped by international goals and by organisations that fund, coordinate and deliver health programmes.
  17. Climate change and health
    The WHO describes climate change as the biggest health threat facing humanity.
  18. Pandemic preparedness
    Pandemics are infrequent but cause enormous harm to health, society and the economy.

Key terms

Public health
The science and art of preventing disease, prolonging life and promoting health through the organised efforts of society
Sanitary movement
The nineteenth-century campaign for clean water, sewers and better housing to reduce disease
Health protection
Protecting the population from infectious diseases and environmental hazards
Screening
Testing apparently healthy people to identify those at higher risk of a condition
Health promotion
The process of enabling people to increase control over, and to improve, their health
Prevention paradox
A measure that brings large benefits to the population may offer little to each individual
Incidence
The number of new cases in a population at risk over a stated period
Crude rate
The number of events divided by the total population over a period
Relative risk
The risk of disease in an exposed group divided by the risk in an unexposed group
Cohort study
A study following groups with and without an exposure over time to compare outcomes
Bias
A systematic error that leads to a wrong estimate of an association
Causation
A relationship in which a change in one factor produces a change in another
Outbreak
More cases of a disease than expected in a particular place, group or time
Surveillance
The continuous, systematic collection, analysis and interpretation of health data for public health action
