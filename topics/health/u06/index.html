Lessons in this school

  1. Mental health and mental illness
    Mental health is more than the absence of mental illness.
  2. Mental health in the UK
    Mental health problems are common in the UK, affect some groups more than others, and carry large social and economic costs.
  3. Risk and protective factors for mental health
    Mental health is shaped by the interaction of biological, psychological and social factors across life.
  4. Stigma and discrimination
    Stigma about mental illness discourages people from seeking help and harms their lives.
  5. Anxiety
    Anxiety is a normal response to threat, but anxiety disorders are persistent and disabling.
  6. Depression
    Depression is one of the most common causes of disability worldwide.
  7. Self-harm and suicide prevention
    Suicide is a leading cause of death among young adults in the UK, and much is preventable.
  8. Severe mental illness
    Severe mental illnesses such as schizophrenia and bipolar disorder can profoundly affect people's lives.
  9. Mental health in children and young people
    Many mental health problems begin in childhood or adolescence.
  10. Perinatal mental health
    Pregnancy and the first year after birth bring a higher risk of mental health problems for parents.
  11. The Five Ways to Wellbeing
    The Five Ways to Wellbeing are evidence-based actions that people can take to improve their mental wellbeing.
  12. Resilience
    Resilience is the capacity to adapt well in the face of adversity.
  13. Loneliness and social connection
    Social connection is vital for mental and physical health, and loneliness is a significant public health issue.
  14. Digital life and wellbeing
    Digital technology shapes how people connect, learn and relax, with both benefits and risks for wellbeing.
  15. Getting help: NHS talking therapies and other support
    Knowing how and where to get help is an essential part of mental health literacy.

Key terms

Mental health
A state of wellbeing that enables people to cope, realise their abilities and contribute
Prevalence
The proportion of a population with a condition at a given time
Biopsychosocial model
A model explaining health through biological, psychological and social factors
Stigma
Negative attitudes that discredit a person because of an attribute
Fight or flight
The body's automatic response to perceived threat
Anhedonia
Loss of interest or pleasure in activities
Self-harm
Intentional self-poisoning or self-injury, regardless of motive
Psychosis
A state involving loss of contact with reality, such as hallucinations or delusions
CYPMHS
Children and Young People's Mental Health Services
Baby blues
Common, brief low mood and tearfulness in the days after birth
Five Ways to Wellbeing
Five evidence-based actions to improve mental wellbeing
Resilience
Positive adaptation in the face of adversity
Loneliness
A subjective, unwelcome feeling of lacking desired social connection
Cyberbullying
Bullying through digital technologies
