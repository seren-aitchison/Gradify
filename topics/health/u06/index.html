Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What does the dual continua model propose?

That mental health and mental illness are separate but related dimensions. From “Mental health and mental illness” →

In plain English: Mental health is more than the absence of mental illness. Key idea: Mental health is a positive state of wellbeing, not just the absence of illness; the dual continua model separates wellbeing from illness, and disorders are diagnosed using ICD-11 or DSM-5-TR criteria.

What is self-stigma?

Internalising negative stereotypes about one's own mental health condition. From “Stigma and discrimination” →

In plain English: Stigma about mental illness discourages people from seeking help and harms their lives. Key idea: Public, self- and structural stigma discourage help-seeking and harm life chances; the Equality Act 2010 can protect people with long-term mental health conditions, and contact-based campaigns such as Time to Change have improved attitudes.

What number can you call to reach Samaritans in the UK?

116 123, free, 24 hours a day. From “Self-harm and suicide prevention” →

In plain English: Suicide is a leading cause of death among young adults in the UK, and much is preventable. Key idea: Suicide claims thousands of lives each year in England and Wales, mostly men; self-harm is a major risk factor; asking directly is safe and helpful, and urgent help is available through 999, NHS 111, Samaritans and Shout.

How common is postpartum psychosis?

About 1 to 2 in every 1,000 births. From “Perinatal mental health” →

In plain English: Pregnancy and the first year after birth bring a higher risk of mental health problems for parents. Key idea: The baby blues are common and brief; perinatal depression and anxiety are longer-lasting and need support; postpartum psychosis is a rare emergency requiring urgent help; specialist perinatal services and mother and baby units support recovery.

What is the difference between loneliness and social isolation?

Loneliness is a subjective feeling of lacking connection; isolation is an objective lack of contacts. From “Loneliness and social connection” →

In plain English: Social connection is vital for mental and physical health, and loneliness is a significant public health issue. Key idea: Loneliness is a subjective feeling and isolation an objective state; poor social connection is linked with worse mental and physical health and higher mortality; responses include national strategy and social prescribing.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Mental Health and Wellbeing

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. In the dual continua model, what is "languishing"? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Low wellbeing without a diagnosable illness. Languishing describes low wellbeing in the absence of illness.

  2. What does the Adult Psychiatric Morbidity Survey measure? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    The prevalence of mental health problems among adults in England. It surveys adults' mental health using standard methods.

  3. Who proposed the biopsychosocial model in 1977? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    George Engel. Engel challenged the purely biomedical model.

  4. A person with well-managed depression reports enjoying life and work. Where are they on the dual continua model? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Coping: illness present with good wellbeing. The model allows good wellbeing alongside a diagnosis.

  5. In a survey of 500 adults, 80 report a common mental health problem. What is the prevalence? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    16%. Applying the standard result: 80 ÷ 500 = 16%.

  6. What did the ACE Study find about the number of ACEs? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    The more ACEs, the higher the risk of poor adult health. The relationship showed a graded, dose–response pattern.

  7. Why might critics argue against relying only on diagnostic categories? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Distress varies along a spectrum, and categories may medicalise ordinary difficulties. Dimensional views see distress as continuous rather than all-or-nothing.

  8. Rates of common mental health problems are higher among people in poverty. What does this suggest for prevention? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Tackling social and economic factors such as income, housing and work is part of improving mental health. Social determinants shape mental health, so prevention goes beyond healthcare.

  9. A school wants to use ACE scores to label pupils as "high risk". What is the main concern? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    ACE scores predict poorly for individuals and may stigmatise children. Population associations do not determine individual futures.

Key takeaways

  • Mental health is a positive state of wellbeing, not just the absence of illness; the dual continua model separates wellbeing from illness, and disorders are diagnosed using ICD-11 or DSM-5-TR criteria.
  • Common mental health problems affect around 1 in 6 adults in England, and about 1 in 5 children and young people; rates are unequal across groups, and the social and economic costs are large.
  • Mental health reflects interacting biological, psychological and social factors; adverse childhood experiences raise later risk in a graded way, while protective factors at individual, family, community and structural levels build resilience.
  • Public, self- and structural stigma discourage help-seeking and harm life chances; the Equality Act 2010 can protect people with long-term mental health conditions, and contact-based campaigns such as Time to Change have improved attitudes.
  • Anxiety is a normal threat response that becomes a disorder when excessive and persistent; disorders include GAD, panic, social anxiety and phobias, screened with GAD-7 and treated by stepped care from self-help to CBT and specialist care.
  • Depression involves persistent low mood or loss of interest for at least two weeks with other symptoms that impair life; the PHQ-9 helps measure severity, and NICE recommends psychological therapies, medication or both according to severity and choice.

Common mistakes in Mental Health and Wellbeing

  • Treating mental health as a yes-or-no state. People move along a spectrum, and wellbeing can be supported whether or not someone has a diagnosis.
  • Assuming rising figures only reflect more willingness to talk. Changes in surveys using consistent methods suggest real increases in some groups, especially young women.
  • Treating ACEs as destiny. ACEs increase risk on average, but many people with ACEs do well, especially with supportive relationships.
  • Using mental illness terms casually, such as "I'm so OCD". Casual misuse trivialises real conditions and reinforces stigma.
  • Avoiding everything that triggers anxiety. Avoidance relieves anxiety short term but maintains it long term; gradual exposure, with support, is more effective.
  • Telling someone with depression to "cheer up" or "snap out of it". Depression is a health condition, and blame worsens self-criticism.

Lessons in this school

  1. Mental health and mental illness
    Mental health is more than the absence of mental illness.
  2. Mental health in the UK
    Mental health problems are common in the UK, affect some groups more than others, and carry large social and economic costs.
  3. Risk and protective factors for mental health
    Mental health is shaped by the interaction of biological, psychological and social factors across life.
  4. Stigma and discrimination
    Stigma about mental illness discourages people from seeking help and harms their lives.
  5. Anxiety
    Anxiety is a normal response to threat, but anxiety disorders are persistent and disabling.
  6. Depression
    Depression is one of the most common causes of disability worldwide.
  7. Self-harm and suicide prevention
    Suicide is a leading cause of death among young adults in the UK, and much is preventable.
  8. Severe mental illness
    Severe mental illnesses such as schizophrenia and bipolar disorder can profoundly affect people's lives.
  9. Mental health in children and young people
    Many mental health problems begin in childhood or adolescence.
  10. Perinatal mental health
    Pregnancy and the first year after birth bring a higher risk of mental health problems for parents.
  11. The Five Ways to Wellbeing
    The Five Ways to Wellbeing are evidence-based actions that people can take to improve their mental wellbeing.
  12. Resilience
    Resilience is the capacity to adapt well in the face of adversity.
  13. Loneliness and social connection
    Social connection is vital for mental and physical health, and loneliness is a significant public health issue.
  14. Digital life and wellbeing
    Digital technology shapes how people connect, learn and relax, with both benefits and risks for wellbeing.
  15. Getting help: NHS talking therapies and other support
    Knowing how and where to get help is an essential part of mental health literacy.
