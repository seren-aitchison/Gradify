Lessons in this school

  1. What professionalism means
    Professionalism is about how you do your job, not just what job you do.
  2. Professional codes of conduct
    Codes of conduct turn values into expected behaviour.
  3. Ethical decision making
    Ethical dilemmas at work rarely come labelled.
  4. Integrity and conflicts of interest
    A conflict of interest can undermine trust even when nothing goes wrong.
  5. Whistleblowing
    Whistleblowers expose wrongdoing that might otherwise stay hidden.
  6. The Equality Act 2010
    The Equality Act 2010 is the main law protecting people from discrimination in Great Britain.
  7. Discrimination, harassment and victimisation
    The Equality Act defines several forms of prohibited conduct.
  8. Reasonable adjustments
    Employers must remove barriers that disabled people face at work.
  9. Inclusion and unconscious bias
    Legal compliance is a minimum; inclusion is about everyone being able to contribute fully.
  10. Employment status
    Your rights at work depend on your legal employment status.
  11. The contract of employment
    The contract of employment sets out the rights and duties of employer and employee.
  12. Working time, pay and holiday
    Core rights on hours, pay and holiday apply to almost all workers.
  13. Family-friendly rights
    UK law gives working parents and carers rights to leave and pay.
  14. Discipline and grievance: the ACAS Code
    Problems at work should be handled fairly and consistently.
  15. Dismissal and redundancy basics
    Ending employment fairly protects both employees and employers.
  16. Getting help: ACAS and advice services
    Knowing where to get help early can resolve problems before they escalate.

Key terms

Profession
An occupation based on specialised knowledge, qualification and service to others
Code of conduct
A set of expected standards of behaviour for members of an organisation or profession
Utilitarianism
Judging actions by whether they produce the greatest good for the greatest number
Conflict of interest
When personal interests could influence, or appear to influence, your work
Qualifying disclosure
Information a worker reasonably believes shows certain types of wrongdoing, in the public interest
Protected characteristic
One of the nine grounds on which discrimination is unlawful under the Equality Act
Indirect discrimination
An unjustified provision, criterion or practice that disadvantages a protected group
Disability
An impairment with a substantial and long-term adverse effect on day-to-day activities
Inclusion
The extent to which people feel valued and able to contribute and progress
Employee
Someone working under a contract of employment, with the fullest employment rights
Express term
A contract term explicitly agreed, in writing or orally
Opt-out
A worker's written agreement to work more than an average 48 hours a week
Statutory Maternity Pay
Pay for up to 39 weeks to eligible employees on maternity leave
Grievance
A concern, problem or complaint raised by an employee with their employer
