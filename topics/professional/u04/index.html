Lessons in this school

  1. What a project is
    Projects are temporary efforts to create something new, unlike the routine work of running an organisation.
  2. Project life cycles
    Projects move through phases from idea to closure.
  3. Project roles and governance
    Projects need clear roles and decision-making structures.
  4. The business case
    The business case explains why a project is worth doing.
  5. Scope and requirements
    Scope defines what a project will and will not deliver.
  6. The work breakdown structure
    A work breakdown structure breaks a project into manageable pieces.
  7. Scheduling and the critical path
    A schedule shows when work will happen.
  8. Estimating and budgeting
    Projects need realistic estimates of time and cost.
  9. Risk management
    Risks are uncertain events that could affect a project.
  10. Stakeholders and communication in projects
    Projects succeed or fail through people.
  11. PRINCE2 principles
    PRINCE2 is a widely used project management method, especially in the UK.
  12. Agile and the Agile Manifesto
    Agile approaches deliver value in small increments and respond to change.
  13. Scrum
    Scrum is the most widely used agile framework.
  14. Kanban
    Kanban is a method for visualising and improving the flow of work.
  15. Choosing a delivery approach
    No single approach suits every project.
  16. Monitoring and controlling progress
    Projects rarely go exactly to plan.
  17. Closing a project and learning lessons
    Closing a project properly is as important as starting it well.

Key terms

Project
A unique, temporary endeavour to achieve planned objectives
Life cycle
The series of phases a project passes through
Sponsor
The senior person accountable for a project's success
Business case
The justification for a project's investment
Scope
The outputs, outcomes and work a project includes
Work breakdown structure
A hierarchical breakdown of all project work
Critical path
The longest path through a project network, setting its minimum duration
Bottom-up estimating
Estimating each work package and adding them together
Risk
An uncertain event that would affect objectives if it occurred
Project stakeholder
Anyone with an interest in or affected by a project
PRINCE2
A process-based project management method
Agile
An approach that delivers value incrementally and adapts to change
Sprint
A fixed time-box of one month or less in Scrum
Kanban board
A visual board showing work items moving through stages
