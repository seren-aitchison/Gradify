Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What two features make work a project?

It is temporary and produces a unique output. From “What a project is” →

In plain English: Projects are temporary efforts to create something new, unlike the routine work of running an organisation. Key idea: A project is a unique, temporary endeavour to bring about change, unlike repeated business-as-usual work; projects balance time, cost and scope around quality, and project management applies methods, skills and knowledge to deliver objectives, with related projects grouped into programmes and portfolios.

What are the five cases in the HM Treasury model?

Strategic, economic, commercial, financial and management. From “The business case” →

In plain English: The business case explains why a project is worth doing. Key idea: A business case justifies a project by comparing options, including doing nothing, and setting out benefits, costs, risks and timescales, using measures such as payback period, ROI and NPV; it is reviewed at each stage gate, and benefits realisation checks whether benefits are achieved.

What is a milestone?

A significant point or event in a schedule, with no duration. From “Scheduling and the critical path” →

In plain English: A schedule shows when work will happen. Key idea: Activities have finish-to-start and other dependencies; the critical path is the longest path through the network and sets the minimum project duration, critical activities have zero float, and Gantt charts show activities, dependencies and milestones against time.

What does PRINCE2 stand for?

PRojects IN Controlled Environments. From “PRINCE2 principles” →

In plain English: PRINCE2 is a widely used project management method, especially in the UK. Key idea: PRINCE2 is a process-based method with seven principles: continued business justification, learning from experience, defined roles, managing by stages, managing by exception, focusing on products and tailoring; it uses seven practices and seven processes and can be tailored or combined with agile approaches.

What is a pull system?

A system where new work starts only when there is capacity. From “Kanban” →

In plain English: Kanban is a method for visualising and improving the flow of work. Key idea: Kanban, from the Toyota Production System, visualises work on boards, limits work in progress, uses a pull system and manages flow, measured by lead time, cycle time and throughput, which Little's Law links to work in progress.

Key takeaways

  • A project is a unique, temporary endeavour to bring about change, unlike repeated business-as-usual work; projects balance time, cost and scope around quality, and project management applies methods, skills and knowledge to deliver objectives, with related projects grouped into programmes and portfolios.
  • Linear life cycles move through concept, definition, deployment and transition in sequence and suit stable requirements; iterative life cycles deliver in repeated cycles for uncertain requirements, hybrid life cycles combine both, and stage gates allow sponsors to continue, change or stop projects.
  • The sponsor is accountable for the project and its business case, the project manager manages the work, the board makes key decisions and the team delivers; governance defines authority, reporting, tolerances and escalation, and a RACI matrix clarifies who is responsible, accountable, consulted and informed.
  • A business case justifies a project by comparing options, including doing nothing, and setting out benefits, costs, risks and timescales, using measures such as payback period, ROI and NPV; it is reviewed at each stage gate, and benefits realisation checks whether benefits are achieved.
  • Scope sets out what the project will and will not deliver, based on clear, testable functional and non-functional requirements gathered from stakeholders; MoSCoW prioritises requirements, and change control prevents scope creep by assessing changes before approval.
  • A work breakdown structure decomposes all project work hierarchically into work packages that can be estimated, scheduled and assigned, following the 100% rule; a product breakdown structure focuses on deliverables, with product descriptions setting quality criteria.

Common mistakes in Project Management

  • Calling all work a project. Ongoing routine operations are business as usual, not projects.
  • Assuming stopping a project at a stage gate is a failure. Stopping a project that no longer makes sense saves money.
  • Giving a task several "accountable" people. Accountability should rest with one person.
  • Writing the business case once and forgetting it. It should be reviewed throughout the project.
  • Leaving out the "out of scope" list. Stating what is excluded prevents later misunderstandings.
  • Missing work from the WBS. The 100% rule means all work, including project management itself, must be included.

What does the W in MoSCoW mean?

What is a work package?

The lowest level of a WBS, which can be estimated, assigned and monitored.

What is a finish-to-start dependency?

One activity must finish before the next can start.
