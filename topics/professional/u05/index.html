Lessons in this school

  1. Setting goals: SMART objectives and OKRs
    Clear goals direct effort and make progress visible.
  2. Prioritisation: urgency and importance
    Not all tasks deserve equal attention.
  3. Planning your week
    A short weekly plan connects big goals to daily action.
  4. Focus, interruptions and deep work
    Knowledge work depends on sustained concentration.
  5. Procrastination
    Procrastination is common and costly but is not simply laziness.
  6. Managing email and information overload
    Email and messaging can swallow the working day.
  7. Task management systems
    A trusted system for tracking tasks frees the mind to focus.
  8. Saying no and managing expectations
    Taking on everything leads to overload and missed commitments.
  9. Working remotely and flexibly
    Remote, hybrid and flexible working are now common in the UK.
  10. How adults learn
    Continuing to learn is part of professional life.
  11. Reflective practice
    Reflecting on experience turns everyday work into learning.
  12. Learning from mistakes
    Mistakes are inevitable, but organisations differ in whether they learn from them.

Key terms

Goal-setting theory
Locke and Latham's finding that specific, challenging goals raise performance
Urgent
A task that demands attention now, often because of a close deadline
Weekly review
A regular session to clear inputs, review progress and set the coming week's priorities
Deep work
Focused, distraction-free work that stretches your abilities
Procrastination
Voluntary delay of an intended action despite expecting to be worse off
Information overload
When the volume of information exceeds the capacity to process it
Trusted system
A single, regularly reviewed place where all commitments are recorded
Assertiveness
Expressing your needs and views clearly and respectfully
Flexible working
Changes to when, where or how much someone works
Andragogy
Knowles's theory of how adults learn
Reflection-in-action
Thinking about and adjusting what you are doing while you are doing it
Psychological safety
A shared belief that a team is safe for interpersonal risk-taking
