Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check.

What one-sentence test shows you are ready to write a message?

Completing "After reading this, I want [who] to [know, decide or do what] by [when]." From “Communication at work: purpose and audience” →

Why avoid multiple questions?

Bundling several questions together confuses the listener, who usually answers only the last or easiest one. From “Questioning skills” →

What does MECE mean?

Mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive: points don't overlap and together cover everything important. From “Structuring reports and briefings” →

When should you not hold a meeting?

When the purpose is only to share information that could be read, or when the person who must decide can't attend. From “Running effective meetings” →

What is the illusion of transparency?

Overestimating how visible our nervousness or other feelings are to other people. From “Delivering a presentation” →

Lessons in this school

  1. Communication at work: purpose and audience
    Every message at work exists to make something happen: a decision, an action, a change in understanding.
  2. Active listening
    Listening is the communication skill people use most and are taught least.
  3. Questioning skills
    Good questions uncover facts, test assumptions and help people think.
  4. Non-verbal communication
    Tone of voice, facial expression, posture, gesture and timing all carry meaning alongside words.
  5. Plain English and clear writing
    Clear writing gets read, understood and acted on.
  6. Structuring reports and briefings
    A report or briefing exists to support a decision.
  7. Writing effective emails
    Email remains the main written channel at work, and most professionals receive more than they can read carefully.
  8. Tone, etiquette and choosing the channel
    The same message can land well or badly depending on its tone and the channel it travels through.
  9. Running effective meetings
    Meetings take a large share of professional time and are often judged a poor use of it.
  10. Minutes, decisions and action points
    The record of a meeting is what turns talk into action and accountability.
  11. Planning a presentation
    Most presentations succeed or fail before they begin, in how clearly the message is defined and structured.
  12. Delivering a presentation
    Delivery turns a well-planned talk into one people remember.
  13. Communicating in virtual and hybrid teams
    Many teams now work partly or fully remotely.
  14. Giving and receiving feedback
    Feedback helps people learn, but badly given feedback can reduce performance.

Key terms

Noise
Anything that distorts or blocks a message between sender and receiver, whether physical, semantic, psychological or cultural
Active listening
Listening for the speaker's full meaning, including feelings, and showing understanding through attention, paraphrase, reflection and summary
Funnel technique
Sequencing questions from broad open questions through probing questions to closed questions that confirm detail
Paralanguage
The vocal features that accompany speech, such as tone, pitch, pace, volume and pauses
Active voice
A sentence in which the subject does the action: "The manager signed the contract"
Pyramid principle
Minto's method of structuring writing with the main answer first, supported by grouped arguments and then evidence
Call to action
The specific action you want the reader to take, with a deadline
Register
The level of formality in language, chosen to suit the audience and situation
Agenda
A list of the items a meeting will cover, ideally phrased as outcomes with owners and timings
Action log
A running list of actions from meetings, each with an owner, deadline and status
Key message
The single main point the audience should remember and act on
Illusion of transparency
The belief that our internal states, such as nervousness, are more visible to others than they are
Asynchronous communication
Communication where people contribute at different times, such as email, shared documents and recorded updates
SBI model
A feedback structure: describe the Situation, the specific Behaviour, and its Impact
