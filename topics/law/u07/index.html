Case card: Jones v Kernott [2011] UKSC 53. Facts, decision and principle?

Facts: An unmarried couple bought a house in joint names; after they separated he stopped contributing for about 14 years. Held: Their intentions changed after separation; shares of 90% and 10% were the fair result. Principle: Where intention about shares can't be inferred, the court may impute the intention it considers fair in light of the whole course of dealing. From “Tenancy in common” →

Case card: Cuckmere Brick Co Ltd v Mutual Finance Ltd [1971] Ch 949. Facts, decision and principle?

Facts: A mortgagee sold land advertising planning permission for houses but not for flats, which would have raised the price. Held: The mortgagee was liable for the shortfall. Principle: A selling mortgagee owes a duty to take reasonable care to obtain the true market value at the time of sale. From “Mortgages” →

Case card: Link Lending Ltd v Bustard [2010] EWCA Civ 424. Facts, decision and principle?

Facts: The registered owner, detained in a psychiatric unit, had been tricked into transferring her home, which was then mortgaged. Held: She was still in actual occupation, so her right to set aside the transfer bound the lender. Principle: Actual occupation can continue during involuntary absence where there is continued intention to return and a physical link to the property. From “Priorities and third-party rights” →

Lessons in this school

  1. Introduction to land law
    Land law governs the rights people can hold in land: who owns it, who can use it, and whose rights win when they conflict.
  2. Nature of land
    "Land" in law means more than the soil.
  3. Estates in land
    English law doesn't recognise outright ownership of land by anyone but the Crown.
  4. Freehold estates
    The fee simple absolute in possession is the freehold estate: the largest estate in land, lasting indefinitely and freely transferable.
  5. Leasehold estates
    The term of years absolute is the leasehold estate.
  6. Legal and equitable interests
    Interests in land can be legal or equitable.
  7. Registered land
    Most land in England and Wales is now registered under the Land Registration Act 2002.
  8. Unregistered land
    A shrinking minority of land is still unregistered.
  9. Land registration
    Land registration involves first registration of unregistered land and the registration of later dealings.
  10. Title investigation
    Title investigation is the buyer's solicitor checking that the seller owns the land and can sell it free of unexpected burdens.
  11. Co-ownership
    Co-ownership arises whenever two or more people own land at the same time.
  12. Joint tenancy
    A joint tenancy treats co-owners as a single owner: each owns the whole, and no one has a separate share.
  13. Tenancy in common
    Tenants in common each have a distinct share in the land, which they can sell, mortgage or leave by will.
  14. Severance
    Severance converts a beneficial joint tenancy into a tenancy in common, so that the severing co-owner's share no longer passes by survivorship.
  15. Trusts of land
    The Trusts of Land and Appointment of Trustees Act 1996 governs trusts of land, including all co-owned land.
  16. Overreaching
    Overreaching allows a buyer of trust land to take free of the beneficiaries' interests, which shift from the land into the purchase money.
  17. Easements
    An easement is a right enjoyed by one piece of land (the dominant tenement) over another (the servient tenement), such as a right of way or a right to light.
  18. Covenants
    A covenant is a promise in a deed.
  19. Mortgages
    A mortgage is security for a debt: the borrower (mortgagor) grants the lender (mortgagee) rights over land that can be enforced if the loan isn't repaid.
  20. Proprietary estoppel
    Proprietary estoppel prevents a landowner from going back on an assurance that someone would have rights in land, where that person relied on it to their detriment and it would be unconscionable to deny the right.
  21. Adverse possession
    Adverse possession allows a person in long, factual possession of another's land, with the intention to possess, to acquire title.
  22. Leases
    A lease gives exclusive possession of land for a term certain.
  23. Licences
    A licence is a permission to be on land that would otherwise be a trespass.
  24. Landlord and tenant relationships
    The landlord and tenant relationship is shaped by the lease, implied covenants and a large body of statute.
  25. Priorities and third-party rights
    Priority rules decide whether a buyer or lender takes land subject to an existing third-party right.
  26. Restrictive covenants
    A restrictive covenant is a promise not to do something on land, such as not to build or not to run a business.
  27. Freehold covenants
    Positive freehold covenants, such as obligations to repair or contribute to costs, don't bind later owners of the burdened land.
  28. Land disputes
    Land disputes include boundary disputes, rights of way, trespass, nuisance and disagreements between co-owners.
  29. Property transactions
    A property transaction moves through three main stages: pre-contract, exchange of contracts and completion, followed by post-completion steps.
  30. Conveyancing fundamentals
    Conveyancing is the legal work of transferring land.

Key terms

Estate
A right to possess land for a period of time
Fixture
An object attached to land so as to become part of it
Doctrine of tenure
All land is held, directly or indirectly, from the Crown
Fee simple
An estate that can be inherited and lasts as long as there are successors
Term of years absolute
A lease for a fixed or periodic term, including terms of less than a year
Legal interest
An interest listed in s 1(2) LPA 1925 and created with the required formality
Mirror principle
The register should reflect all the rights affecting the land
Root of title
A document at least 15 years old that starts the chain of title
Compulsory first registration
The duty to register title after a triggering event (LRA 2002, s 4)
Official copies
Up-to-date copies of the register and title plan issued by HM Land Registry
Trust of land
Any trust of property that consists of or includes land (TOLATA 1996, s 1)
Four unities
Possession, interest, title and time: all required for a joint tenancy
Tenancy in common
Co-ownership in distinct, undivided shares without survivorship
Severance
Converting a beneficial joint tenancy into a tenancy in common
