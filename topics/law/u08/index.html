Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

Which document sets out lenders' instructions to conveyancers?

The UK Finance Mortgage Lenders' Handbook. From “Residential conveyancing” →

In plain English: Residential conveyancing is the legal process of buying and selling homes. Key idea: Residential conveyancing follows the Protocol and Standard Conditions, with lenders' requirements and chain management central.

Statute card: what does Proceeds of Crime Act 2002, ss 327 to 329 provide?

The principal money laundering offences: concealing, converting or transferring criminal property (s 327), entering into an arrangement facilitating it (s 328), and acquiring, using or possessing it (s 329), with a defence for an authorised disclosure (a suspicious activity report). From “Anti-money laundering” →

In plain English: Property is a common target for money laundering. Key idea: Report suspicions to the MLRO, don't proceed without consent where needed, and never tip off the client.

What is the legal effect of the seller's replies to enquiries?

They are representations that can found a misrepresentation claim if untrue. From “Raising enquiries” →

In plain English: After reviewing the contract package and search results, the buyer's solicitor raises additional enquiries targeted at issues specific to the property. Key idea: Raise precise enquiries arising from the title, searches, forms and survey; replies are representations the buyer can rely on.

Statute card: what does Finance Act 2003, Part 4 provide?

Stamp Duty Land Tax: charged on land transactions in England and Northern Ireland, with a return and payment due within 14 days of the effective date (normally completion). Rates and reliefs change: check the current figures. From “Stamp Duty Land Tax” →

In plain English: Stamp Duty Land Tax is payable by buyers of land in England and Northern Ireland. Key idea: Calculate SDLT using the current bands, applying first-time buyer relief or the higher rates as the buyer's circumstances require.

Is a transfer of a home under a divorce agreement chargeable to SDLT?

No; it is exempt under Sch 3, para 3 FA 2003. From “Transfers of equity” →

In plain English: A transfer of equity adds or removes a person from the ownership of a property without a sale to an outsider: for example, adding a partner or transferring a home on separation. Key idea: Check conflicts and lender consent, document beneficial shares, and analyse SDLT including assumed debt.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Property Law and Practice

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which scheme requires member firms to follow the Conveyancing Protocol? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    The Conveyancing Quality Scheme. Firms accredited under the Law Society's Conveyancing Quality Scheme must follow the Protocol.

  2. Which standard conditions are used for commercial sales? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Standard Commercial Property Conditions. The SCPCs are designed for commercial transactions.

  3. Which SRA Code paragraph governs conflicts between clients? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Paragraph 6.2. Paragraph 6.2 restricts acting for clients whose interests conflict, subject to narrow exceptions.

  4. A sale is in a chain of four. The seller's onward purchase is delayed. What is the practical effect? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Exchange may need to wait for the whole chain. Chains need synchronised exchange, so delay affects everyone.

  5. A buyer purchases a let office building from a seller who has opted to tax. The buyer will continue the letting business. What might avoid VAT? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Treatment as a transfer of a going concern. TOGC treatment can take the sale outside the scope of VAT if conditions are met.

  6. A married couple buying a home in joint names ask about how to hold it. What should the solicitor advise on? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Joint tenancy or tenancy in common, and a declaration of trust. Joint buyers should decide how they hold the beneficial interest.

  7. A seller of an empty, mortgage-free flat insists on completing within a week and will only communicate by email. What should the seller's solicitor do? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Treat it as high risk and verify identity rigorously. These are classic warning signs of identity fraud.

  8. A solicitor suspects the purchase money is criminal property. The client asks why completion has been delayed. What should the solicitor say? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Nothing that would tip off the client. Disclosing the report risks the s 333A tipping off offence.

  9. A buyer's father will live in the property and has contributed money, but isn't a borrower. What will the lender usually require? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    A signed occupier's consent. Lenders want occupiers' rights postponed to the mortgage.

Key takeaways

  • Residential conveyancing follows the Protocol and Standard Conditions, with lenders' requirements and chain management central.
  • Commercial conveyancing uses the SCPCs and CPSE, with wider due diligence and careful VAT analysis.
  • Before acting, check conflicts, identity, risk and authority, and set clear terms.
  • Verify identity from reliable independent sources, apply enhanced checks where risk is high, and watch for impersonation.
  • Trace the money for the transaction, and where risk is higher, how the client acquired their wealth; gifted deposits need donor checks and lender disclosure.
  • Report suspicions to the MLRO, don't proceed without consent where needed, and never tip off the client.

Common mistakes in Property Law and Practice

  • Assuming the Standard Conditions apply automatically: they must be incorporated into the contract.
  • Ignoring VAT on a commercial purchase: if the seller has opted to tax, VAT may add 20% to the price.
  • Starting work before completing customer due diligence.
  • Relying on photocopied ID without verifying it, especially for a mortgage-free, unoccupied property.
  • Accepting a large transfer from an unknown third party without checking its origin.
  • Telling the client the transaction is delayed "because we've reported it to the NCA".

Lessons in this school

  1. Residential conveyancing
    Residential conveyancing is the legal process of buying and selling homes.
  2. Commercial conveyancing
    Commercial conveyancing covers buying, selling and leasing business premises.
  3. Client onboarding
    Onboarding is the set of checks and steps a firm completes before acting: conflict checks, identifying the client and their authority, risk assessment and anti-money laundering checks, and setting out terms of engagement.
  4. Identity verification
    Verifying identity is a legal requirement under the Money Laundering Regulations 2017 and a key defence against property fraud.
  5. Source of funds and source of wealth
    Conveyancers must understand where the money for a purchase comes from.
  6. Anti-money laundering
    Property is a common target for money laundering.
  7. Instructions and client care
    Taking full instructions at the start of a matter avoids problems later.
  8. Searches and enquiries
    Searches and enquiries reveal information that a buyer can't get from inspecting the property or the title.
  9. Official copies and title documents
    Title investigation starts with the official copies of the register and title plan for registered land, and the deeds for unregistered land.
  10. Contract packages
    The seller's solicitor prepares a contract package for the buyer's solicitor at the start of the transaction.
  11. Raising enquiries
    After reviewing the contract package and search results, the buyer's solicitor raises additional enquiries targeted at issues specific to the property.
  12. Mortgage finance
    Most buyers fund their purchase with a mortgage.
  13. Exchange of contracts
    Exchange of contracts makes the sale binding.
  14. Completion
    Completion is when the purchase money is paid and the seller hands over the transfer deed and keys.
  15. Post-completion formalities
    After completion, both solicitors have important tasks.
  16. Stamp Duty Land Tax
    Stamp Duty Land Tax is payable by buyers of land in England and Northern Ireland.
  17. Land Registry applications
    Registration completes the buyer's legal title and the lender's security.
  18. Leasehold transactions
    Buying or selling a leasehold property, usually a flat, adds layers to conveyancing: checking the lease, the landlord's consent, service charges, ground rent, management information and the length of the lease.
  19. New-build property
    Buying a newly built home involves a developer's standard documentation, a property that may not be finished at exchange, and extra checks on roads, drainage, warranties and estate charges.
  20. Remortgaging
    A remortgage replaces an existing mortgage with a new one, without a sale.
  21. Transfers of equity
    A transfer of equity adds or removes a person from the ownership of a property without a sale to an outsider: for example, adding a partner or transferring a home on separation.
  22. Commercial leases
    Commercial leases are negotiated contracts with terms on rent, repair, use, alienation and ending the lease.
  23. Property taxation
    Property transactions raise several taxes beyond SDLT: VAT on commercial property, capital gains tax on disposals, income tax on rents, inheritance tax, and the annual tax on enveloped dwellings.
  24. Professional conduct
    Property work raises frequent conduct issues: conflicts, undertakings, contract races, referral arrangements, confidentiality and fraud prevention.
  25. Solicitors' accounts in property transactions
    Property transactions involve large sums of client money: deposits, mortgage advances, purchase money and sale proceeds.

Property Law and Practice: questions students ask

What are the CPSE?

The Commercial Property Standard Enquiries.

Can a firm normally act for both buyer and seller?

Generally not, because of the conflict (SRA Code, para 6.2).

Case card: Dreamvar (UK) Ltd v Mishcon de Reya [2018] EWCA Civ 1082. Facts, decision and principle?

Facts: A fraudster posed as the owner of a property and 'sold' it; the buyer's solicitors paid the money to the fraudster's solicitors. Held: The seller's solicitors were liable for breach of trust (holding money for completion that never truly occurred); the buyer's solicitors weren't relieved under s 61. Principle: In identity fraud the seller's solicitor, holding money on trust for genuine completion, can bear the loss, though acting honestly.

What is a gift letter?

A letter from the donor confirming the money is a gift, not a loan, with no interest in the property.
