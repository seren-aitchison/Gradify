Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

Does completing a law degree make you a solicitor?

No. You must also meet the SRA's requirements, including passing the SQE, completing qualifying work experience and passing character and suitability checks. From “Academic study and professional qualification” →

In plain English: Studying law and qualifying as a lawyer are different things. Key idea: Use LawGradify to learn the law and prepare for assessments. Use the SRA and BSB websites for the current requirements to qualify.

How is ethics assessed in the SQE?

Pervasively, throughout both SQE1 and SQE2, not in a separate paper. From “The Solicitors Qualifying Examination” →

In plain English: The SQE is a centralised assessment set by the SRA and delivered by its assessment provider. Key idea: The SQE tests application, not recall. Expect client scenarios that require you to identify the issue and choose the best course of action.

How long is qualifying work experience?

Two years full-time or equivalent. From “Qualifying work experience” →

In plain English: Qualifying work experience (QWE) is two years' full-time (or equivalent) experience of providing legal services that gives the opportunity to develop the competences in the SRA's Statement of Solicitor Competence. Key idea: QWE is flexible about where and when, but strict about confirmation by a solicitor.

Name the four Inns of Court.

Lincoln's Inn, Inner Temple, Middle Temple and Gray's Inn. From “Becoming a barrister in England and Wales” →

In plain English: Barristers qualify through three components set by the Bar Standards Board: the academic component, the vocational component and pupillage (work-based learning). Key idea: Call to the Bar is not the end: without pupillage and a practising certificate, a barrister cannot practise as one.

What is the role of a pupil supervisor?

To supervise and train the pupil, and sign off that they have met the required competences. From “Pupillage” →

In plain English: Pupillage is the final, work-based stage of Bar training. Key idea: Consider your chances of pupillage before committing to the cost of the vocational component, and keep other legal careers in view.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Routes to Qualification

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which statement is correct? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    A law degree builds legal knowledge but does not by itself authorise anyone to practise. Qualification depends on the regulators' routes, not academic study alone.

  2. Which of these is NOT a requirement of the SQE route? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Completing the Legal Practice Course. The LPC belonged to the old route; it is not required under the SQE route.

  3. Which is assessed in SQE1? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Functioning legal knowledge. SQE1 tests functioning legal knowledge through single-best-answer questions; the practical skills such as advocacy and drafting are assessed in SQE2.

  4. A friend who has finished an LLB offers to represent you in court for a fee, calling herself a "legal adviser". What is the main concern? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Rights of audience are a reserved legal activity she isn't authorised to carry on. Exercising rights of audience requires authorisation under the Legal Services Act 2007.

  5. A history graduate wants to become a solicitor. Must they first complete a law conversion course? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    No: a degree in any subject is accepted. The SQE assesses knowledge directly; a law degree or conversion course is optional preparation.

  6. A candidate plans to sit SQE2 before SQE1 to get the skills part done first. What is the position? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    SQE1 must normally be passed before SQE2. The SRA requires SQE1 to be passed first (subject to exemptions).

  7. In an SQE2 interview, a candidate gives perfect legal advice but doesn't notice that the client is acting under pressure from a relative. What is the main weakness? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Missing a professional conduct issue. Ethics is assessed pervasively; identifying whose instructions you take is essential.

  8. A candidate has worked 18 months as a paralegal in one firm and 6 months in a university law clinic supervised by a solicitor. Can both count? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Yes, if each is confirmed and suitable. QWE can be in up to four organisations, including clinics, if confirmed.

  9. A person has completed the vocational component and been called to the Bar but has not obtained pupillage. Can they represent clients in court as a barrister? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    No: pupillage and a practising certificate are needed. Practising as a barrister requires pupillage and a practising certificate.

Key takeaways

  • Use LawGradify to learn the law and prepare for assessments. Use the SRA and BSB websites for the current requirements to qualify.
  • The SQE is the assessment; QWE is the experience; character and suitability is the gateway. All four requirements must be met.
  • The SQE tests application, not recall. Expect client scenarios that require you to identify the issue and choose the best course of action.
  • Many SQE1 questions turn on one precise rule applied to one detail of the facts. Read the final sentence of the question first, then the facts.
  • In SQE2, a technically correct answer that ignores the client's objectives or the professional conduct issue will not score well.
  • QWE is flexible about where and when, but strict about confirmation by a solicitor.

Lessons in this school

  1. Academic study and professional qualification
    Studying law and qualifying as a lawyer are different things.
  2. Becoming a solicitor in England and Wales
    Since September 2021, the main route to qualifying as a solicitor is the Solicitors Qualifying Examination (SQE) route.
  3. The Solicitors Qualifying Examination
    The SQE is a centralised assessment set by the SRA and delivered by its assessment provider.
  4. SQE1: structure and approach
    SQE1 consists of two assessments, FLK1 and FLK2, each of 180 single-best-answer questions.
  5. SQE2: structure and approach
    SQE2 assesses practical legal skills through 16 stations: written assessments and oral assessments, set in five practice areas.
  6. Qualifying work experience
    Qualifying work experience (QWE) is two years' full-time (or equivalent) experience of providing legal services that gives the opportunity to develop the competences in the SRA's Statement of Solicitor Competence.
  7. Character and suitability
    Before admission, the SRA must be satisfied that a candidate is of suitable character to be a solicitor.
  8. Becoming a barrister in England and Wales
    Barristers qualify through three components set by the Bar Standards Board: the academic component, the vocational component and pupillage (work-based learning).
  9. The academic component for the Bar
    The academic component gives the foundation of legal knowledge that every barrister needs.
  10. The vocational component for the Bar
    The vocational component prepares students for practice at the Bar.
  11. Pupillage
    Pupillage is the final, work-based stage of Bar training.
  12. Other legal careers
    Law offers many careers beyond solicitor and barrister, each with its own route and, often, its own regulator.
  13. Answering single-best-answer questions
    Single-best-answer (SBA) questions present a scenario and five options, all of which may look plausible.

Routes to Qualification: questions students ask

What are the four requirements to qualify as a solicitor under the SQE route?

A degree or equivalent; pass SQE1 and SQE2; two years' qualifying work experience; meet character and suitability requirements.

What does the SQE assess?

Whether candidates have the knowledge and skills of a competent newly qualified solicitor (day-one competence).

How many questions are in each of FLK1 and FLK2?

180 single-best-answer questions each, sat in two sessions of 90.

What two things are SQE2 candidates marked on?

Their legal skills and their application of law.
