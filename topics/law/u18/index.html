Does completing a law degree make you a solicitor?

No. You must also meet the SRA's requirements, including passing the SQE, completing qualifying work experience and passing character and suitability checks. From “Academic study and professional qualification” →

How is ethics assessed in the SQE?

Pervasively, throughout both SQE1 and SQE2, not in a separate paper. From “The Solicitors Qualifying Examination” →

How long is qualifying work experience?

Two years full-time or equivalent. From “Qualifying work experience” →

Name the four Inns of Court.

Lincoln's Inn, Inner Temple, Middle Temple and Gray's Inn. From “Becoming a barrister in England and Wales” →

What is the role of a pupil supervisor?

To supervise and train the pupil, and sign off that they have met the required competences. From “Pupillage” →

Lessons in this school

  1. Academic study and professional qualification
    Studying law and qualifying as a lawyer are different things.
  2. Becoming a solicitor in England and Wales
    Since September 2021, the main route to qualifying as a solicitor is the Solicitors Qualifying Examination (SQE) route.
  3. The Solicitors Qualifying Examination
    The SQE is a centralised assessment set by the SRA and delivered by its assessment provider.
  4. SQE1: structure and approach
    SQE1 consists of two assessments, FLK1 and FLK2, each of 180 single-best-answer questions.
  5. SQE2: structure and approach
    SQE2 assesses practical legal skills through 16 stations: written assessments and oral assessments, set in five practice areas.
  6. Qualifying work experience
    Qualifying work experience (QWE) is two years' full-time (or equivalent) experience of providing legal services that gives the opportunity to develop the competences in the SRA's Statement of Solicitor Competence.
  7. Character and suitability
    Before admission, the SRA must be satisfied that a candidate is of suitable character to be a solicitor.
  8. Becoming a barrister in England and Wales
    Barristers qualify through three components set by the Bar Standards Board: the academic component, the vocational component and pupillage (work-based learning).
  9. The academic component for the Bar
    The academic component gives the foundation of legal knowledge that every barrister needs.
  10. The vocational component for the Bar
    The vocational component prepares students for practice at the Bar.
  11. Pupillage
    Pupillage is the final, work-based stage of Bar training.
  12. Other legal careers
    Law offers many careers beyond solicitor and barrister, each with its own route and, often, its own regulator.
  13. Answering single-best-answer questions
    Single-best-answer (SBA) questions present a scenario and five options, all of which may look plausible.

Key terms

Reserved legal activity
An activity only authorised persons may carry out, such as conducting litigation
SQE route
The main route to qualifying as a solicitor since September 2021
Day one solicitor
The standard of competence the SQE assesses
FLK
Functioning legal knowledge, assessed in FLK1 and FLK2
Station
A single SQE2 assessment task
QWE
Two years' full-time equivalent qualifying work experience
Character and suitability
The SRA's assessment of whether a candidate can be trusted as a solicitor
Call to the Bar
The ceremony by which an Inn admits a person as a barrister
Qualifying law degree
A degree covering the foundations of legal knowledge
Vocational component
The Bar course preparing students for practice
Pupillage
The work-based stage of Bar training under a pupil supervisor
Chartered legal executive
A lawyer qualified through CILEx
Distractor
A plausible wrong option in a multiple-choice question
