How many legal systems are there in the UK?

Three: England and Wales, Scotland, and Northern Ireland. From “Three legal systems in one UK” →

In plain English: The United Kingdom has three legal systems: England and Wales, Scotland, and Northern Ireland. Key idea: Always state the jurisdiction. A rule of English law may not be the law in Scotland or Northern Ireland, even if the statute has the same name.

Case card: AXA General Insurance Ltd v HM Advocate [2011] UKSC 46. Facts, decision and principle?

Facts: Insurers challenged an Act of the Scottish Parliament making pleural plaques actionable. Held: The Act was valid; Acts of the Scottish Parliament aren't reviewable on common law grounds of irrationality. Principle: Acts of the Scottish Parliament can be challenged only for exceeding competence or, exceptionally, offending the rule of law. From “The Scottish Parliament and devolved law” →

In plain English: The Scotland Act 1998 created the Scottish Parliament, which can make primary legislation (Acts of the Scottish Parliament) on all matters not reserved to Westminster. Key idea: To find the law in Scotland, check both Westminster Acts that extend to Scotland and Acts of the Scottish Parliament.

What is the age of criminal responsibility in Scotland?

12. From “Scots criminal law and procedure” →

In plain English: Scots criminal law is largely common law, defined by the courts and the institutional writer Hume, with growing statutory reform. Key idea: Never assume an English offence, defence or procedural rule applies in Scotland. The structures, the language and often the substance differ.

What is the Northern Ireland equivalent of PACE 1984?

The Police and Criminal Evidence (Northern Ireland) Order 1989. From “Northern Ireland's legal system” →

In plain English: Northern Ireland has a common law legal system closely related to England's, but with its own courts, legal professions, legislature and a substantial body of separate legislation. Key idea: Northern Ireland law is often similar to English law but contained in different instruments. Find the NI statute, not the English one.

What is the age of criminal responsibility in Northern Ireland?

10. From “Northern Ireland criminal law and procedure” →

In plain English: Northern Ireland's criminal law and procedure are close to England's but contained in separate legislation, with some important differences in practice and structure. Key idea: When advising on an NI criminal matter, find the NI statute and code of practice; don't cite the English Act even where the wording is the same.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Scotland and Northern Ireland

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which instrument preserved Scotland's separate legal system? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    The Treaty and Acts of Union 1707. The Union settlement preserved Scots law and courts.

  2. What is the Scots law equivalent of the English law of tort? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Delict. Civil wrongs in Scotland are called delict.

  3. In Scottish civil proceedings, what is the person bringing the action called? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    The pursuer. The Scottish term is pursuer; the other side is the defender.

  4. A client in Glasgow asks whether an English Court of Appeal decision on tenancy law binds their case. What is the best answer? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Not binding in Scotland, though possibly persuasive. English decisions don't bind Scottish courts; housing law is devolved and different.

  5. A Scottish court faces a novel issue not covered by statute or case law. Which source might it consult that would not be authoritative in England? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    The institutional writers, such as Stair. Institutional writings are a formal source of Scots law.

  6. A business in Edinburgh is owed £3,000 by a customer. Which procedure is most likely? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Simple procedure in the sheriff court. Simple procedure covers sheriff court claims up to £5,000.

  7. What did the UK Supreme Court decide in the 2022 independence referendum reference? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    It related to reserved matters, so was outside competence. Reference by the Lord Advocate [2022] UKSC 31: a referendum on independence would relate to the Union and the UK Parliament, which are reserved, so the bill was outside competence.

  8. A student applies the English doctrine of overreaching to a Scottish property problem. What is the main issue? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Scots law has no legal/equitable split of ownership. Scots property law uses different concepts.

  9. Why did Westminster legislate on abortion in Northern Ireland in 2019? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    The Assembly wasn't sitting, so Parliament legislated. Westminster legislated while devolved institutions were suspended.

Key takeaways

  • Always state the jurisdiction. A rule of English law may not be the law in Scotland or Northern Ireland, even if the statute has the same name.
  • Many Scottish legal concepts look familiar but work differently. Learn the Scots terms and structures, not just the English equivalents.
  • Scottish civil procedure uses its own vocabulary: pursuer (claimant), defender (defendant), interdict (injunction), decree (judgment).
  • To find the law in Scotland, check both Westminster Acts that extend to Scotland and Acts of the Scottish Parliament.
  • In Scotland, a promise can bind without anything given in return. That changes the analysis of many English "consideration" problems.
  • Many English tort cases are persuasive in Scotland, but always check the Scots statute and terminology.

Lessons in this school

  1. Three legal systems in one UK
    The United Kingdom has three legal systems: England and Wales, Scotland, and Northern Ireland.
  2. Scots law: a mixed legal system
    Scots law is a "mixed" or "hybrid" legal system, drawing on both the civilian (Roman law) tradition of continental Europe and the common law tradition shared with England.
  3. The Scottish courts
    Scotland has its own hierarchy of civil and criminal courts, with distinct names, procedures and personnel.
  4. The Scottish Parliament and devolved law
    The Scotland Act 1998 created the Scottish Parliament, which can make primary legislation (Acts of the Scottish Parliament) on all matters not reserved to Westminster.
  5. Scots contract law and promise
    Scots contract law shares many principles with English law, but differs in important ways: it has no doctrine of consideration, it enforces unilateral promises, and its rules on formation have been reformed by statute.
  6. Delict: the Scots law of wrongs
    Delict is the Scots law of civil wrongs, equivalent in function to the English law of tort.
  7. Scots criminal law and procedure
    Scots criminal law is largely common law, defined by the courts and the institutional writer Hume, with growing statutory reform.
  8. Scots property law and land registration
    Scots property law has its own concepts and terminology.
  9. Succession in Scotland
    Scots succession law limits testamentary freedom more than English law: a surviving spouse, civil partner and children have "legal rights" to part of the moveable estate whatever the will says.
  10. Northern Ireland's legal system
    Northern Ireland has a common law legal system closely related to England's, but with its own courts, legal professions, legislature and a substantial body of separate legislation.
  11. The Northern Ireland courts
    Northern Ireland has its own court system, structured much like England and Wales, with the UK Supreme Court as the final appeal court.
  12. The Northern Ireland Assembly and devolved law
    The Northern Ireland Assembly makes laws for Northern Ireland on transferred matters, under a power-sharing system established by the Northern Ireland Act 1998.
  13. Northern Ireland criminal law and procedure
    Northern Ireland's criminal law and procedure are close to England's but contained in separate legislation, with some important differences in practice and structure.
  14. Northern Ireland land law and conveyancing
    Northern Ireland land law shares English common law roots but retains older features and has its own registration system and conveyancing practice.
  15. Cross-border issues within the UK
    Because the UK has three legal systems, lawyers must decide which courts have jurisdiction, which law applies and how judgments are enforced when a matter crosses internal borders.
  16. Qualifying and practising in Scotland and Northern Ireland
    Each UK jurisdiction has its own legal professions and qualification routes.

Scotland and Northern Ireland: questions students ask

Why is Scots law called a mixed legal system?

It combines civilian (Roman law) principles and common law influences.

What is the supreme civil court in Scotland?

The Court of Session (Outer House at first instance, Inner House on appeal), subject to appeal to the UK Supreme Court.

Does Scots law require consideration for a contract?

No. Gratuitous obligations can be binding.

What is solatium?

Damages for pain, suffering and loss of amenity (non-patrimonial loss) in Scots law.
