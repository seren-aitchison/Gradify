What is the minimum allotted share capital of a public company?

£50,000 (CA 2006, s 763). From “Business organisations” →

Case card: Prest v Petrodel Resources Ltd [2013] UKSC 34. Facts, decision and principle?

Facts: In divorce proceedings a wife sought properties held by companies the husband controlled. Held: The veil wasn't pierced, but the companies held the properties on resulting trust for him. Principle: The veil can be pierced only where a person deliberately evades or frustrates an existing legal obligation through a company they control. From “Corporate personality” →

Within how many days must a special resolution be filed at Companies House?

15 days (s 30 CA 2006). From “Company decision-making” →

Case card: Aluminium Industrie Vaassen BV v Romalpa Aluminium Ltd [1976] 1 WLR 676. Facts, decision and principle?

Facts: A Dutch supplier sold aluminium foil on terms that ownership stayed with it until payment; the buyer became insolvent. Held: The supplier could recover unsold foil and the proceeds of sales. Principle: Retention of title (Romalpa) clauses can protect sellers on the buyer's insolvency. From “Commercial transactions” →

Case card: BNY Corporate Trustee Services Ltd v Eurosail-UK 2007-3BL plc [2013] UKSC 28. Facts, decision and principle?

Facts: Noteholders argued that the issuer was balance-sheet insolvent, triggering default. Held: Not shown: the test is whether the company can reasonably be expected to meet future and contingent liabilities. Principle: The balance sheet test under s 123(2) of the Insolvency Act 1986 is a question of fact, not a strict accounting exercise. From “Insolvency” →

Lessons in this school

  1. Business organisations
    Businesses can be run through several legal structures: sole traders, partnerships, limited partnerships, limited liability partnerships and companies.
  2. Sole traders
    A sole trader runs a business personally, without a separate legal entity.
  3. Partnerships
    A partnership is the relation between persons carrying on a business in common with a view of profit (Partnership Act 1890, s 1).
  4. Limited liability partnerships
    A limited liability partnership is a body corporate with separate legal personality, created under the Limited Liability Partnerships Act 2000.
  5. Companies
    A registered company is a legal person separate from its members, created by registration under the Companies Act 2006.
  6. Company formation
    A company is formed by sending an application for registration to Companies House.
  7. Corporate personality
    A registered company is a legal person separate from its shareholders and directors.
  8. Directors
    Directors manage the company's business.
  9. Directors' duties
    Directors owe general duties to the company, codified in ss 171–177 of the Companies Act 2006.
  10. Shareholders
    Shareholders own the company but don't manage it.
  11. Share capital
    Share capital is the money members invest in exchange for shares.
  12. Corporate governance
    Corporate governance is the system by which companies are directed and controlled.
  13. Company decision-making
    Company decisions are made either by the board of directors or by the shareholders.
  14. Company meetings
    Shareholders make decisions at general meetings unless they use written resolutions.
  15. Dividends
    Dividends are distributions of profit to shareholders.
  16. Company accounts
    Companies must keep accounting records, prepare annual accounts and reports, and file them at Companies House.
  17. Corporate finance
    Companies raise money through equity (shares) or debt (loans, overdrafts and bonds).
  18. Business contracts
    Business contracts are governed by general contract law, with commercial terms that allocate risk: payment, delivery, liability limits, boilerplate clauses and dispute resolution.
  19. Commercial transactions
    Businesses use a range of commercial arrangements to sell goods and services: agency, distribution, franchising, licensing and joint ventures.
  20. Agency
    Agency arises when one person (the agent) is authorised to act on behalf of another (the principal) and to affect the principal's legal relations with third parties.
  21. Sale of goods
    Business-to-business sales of goods are governed by the Sale of Goods Act 1979.
  22. Supply of goods and services
    Contracts for services and for work and materials between businesses are governed by the Supply of Goods and Services Act 1982.
  23. Consumer protection
    Businesses dealing with consumers face stricter rules than in B2B contracts.
  24. Business taxation
    Business structure affects tax.
  25. Insolvency
    A company is insolvent when it can't pay its debts as they fall due or its liabilities exceed its assets.
  26. Liquidation
    Liquidation (winding up) ends a company's life: a liquidator collects and sells its assets, distributes the proceeds to creditors in a statutory order, and the company is dissolved.
  27. Administration
    Administration is a rescue procedure under Schedule B1 to the Insolvency Act 1986.
  28. Restructuring
    Restructuring tools let financially distressed companies compromise their debts and continue trading.
  29. Corporate transactions
    Corporate transactions include buying and selling companies or businesses, investments, mergers and joint ventures.

Key terms

Unlimited liability
Personal liability for all the business's debts
Sole trader
An individual who carries on business on their own account
Partnership
The relation between persons carrying on a business in common with a view of profit
Member
A person who belongs to an LLP, broadly equivalent to a partner
Articles of association
The company's internal rules
Memorandum of association
A statement by the subscribers that they wish to form a company (s 8)
Veil of incorporation
The separation between a company and its members
De facto director
A person who acts as a director without being validly appointed
Duty to promote success
The duty to act in good faith to promote the company's success for the members' benefit (s 172)
Ordinary resolution
Passed by a simple majority (s 282)
Allotment
Creating and issuing new shares to a person
Comply or explain
Companies either follow the Code's provisions or explain why not
Board resolution
A decision of the directors at a board meeting or in writing
Clear days
Notice periods excluding the day of service and the day of the meeting
