Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What three things are assessed across SQE2 stations?

Legal skills, application of law, and professional conduct (ethics), at the level of a newly qualified solicitor. From “How the SQE2 stations work” →

In plain English: Each SQE2 station gives you a short case file, a task and a strict time limit, and expects the work a competent newly qualified solicitor would produce. Key idea: Read the instructions twice: who is the reader, what format, what decision must they be able to make after reading your work?

Which section of UCTA 1977 governs exclusion of the implied terms in a B2B sale?

Section 6: liability for description, quality and fitness can be excluded only if the term is reasonable. From “Client interview: a contract dispute” →

In plain English: In this simulation you interview a business owner whose supplier has delivered faulty goods. Key idea: In a commercial dispute interview, cover facts, documents, losses, the other side's likely response, the client's goals, costs and timescales.

What is the duty to mitigate?

A claimant cannot recover for loss it could reasonably have avoided; it must give credit for replacement profits it actually earns. From “Case and matter analysis: a business dispute” →

In plain English: A case and matter analysis station gives you a file and asks for a written analysis for a supervising partner: the legal issues, the evidence, the options and a recommendation, with any conduct issues identified. Key idea: A good analysis moves from issue to law to facts to conclusion for each question, then recommends what the client should do next.

What does "as of right" mean for prescription?

Use without force, without secrecy and without permission (nec vi, nec clam, nec precario). From “Legal research: a property question” →

In plain English: This research simulation asks you to find and apply the law on a neighbour's claimed right of way over a client's land, using a supplied set of sources. Key idea: For property research, always ask how the right was created and whether it binds a buyer of registered land.

Why carve out death and personal injury caused by negligence?

Because UCTA 1977, s 2(1) makes exclusions of that liability void, and carving it out helps the remaining clause satisfy reasonableness. From “Legal drafting: a clause in a commercial agreement” →

In plain English: Drafting stations ask you to draft or amend a legal document, such as a clause in a commercial agreement, so that it achieves the client's aims precisely, using clear and consistent language. Key idea: Good drafting is precise, consistent and complete: say what you mean once, and use the same word for the same thing throughout.

Quick quiz: test yourself on SQE2 Skills Practice

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. What is the best first step in any SQE2 written station? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Read the instructions for reader and purpose. The task and reader determine what a good answer looks like.

  2. Which question best explores the funding? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    "How is your uncle providing the £15,000?". An open, neutral question uncovers whether it's a gift, a loan or an interest.

  3. Which is best practice in an attendance note? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Record advice and instructions clearly. An accurate, structured record protects client and firm.

  4. A station asks for a letter to a lay client. Your draft is technically correct but full of case citations and Latin. What is the main weakness? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    It isn't written appropriately for a lay reader. Client focus and appropriate language are marked skills.

  5. The client says, "My uncle wants his name kept out of it, but he'll get his money back when I sell." What should the solicitor do? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Explain it must be disclosed to the lender. Concealing a repayable loan or interest would mislead the lender; AML duties also apply.

  6. The client asked you not to tell the lender about the loan. What should the attendance note record? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Your advice that the lender must be told. The note should record your advice and the conduct issue.

  7. The client tells you privately that he did miss a second hearing that isn't on his record. The court asks if he has ever failed to surrender before. What should you do? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Don't mislead the court; seek instructions or withdraw. You must never mislead the court, but you also owe confidentiality; you cannot make a positive false statement.

  8. A partner asks whether Greystone should disclose an internal email admitting that some deliveries were "late more often than records show". What is the correct position? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    It must be preserved and disclosed if relevant. The duty of disclosure covers adverse documents; destroying them is serious misconduct.

  9. A son brings his elderly mother to make a new will leaving everything to him and does most of the talking. What should the solicitor do? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    See the mother alone. Instructions must come from the client, free from undue influence.

Key takeaways

  • Read the instructions twice: who is the reader, what format, what decision must they be able to make after reading your work?
  • In conveyancing interviews, always explore the source of every pound of the purchase price.
  • An attendance note is a professional record: if it isn't recorded, it can't be proved that advice was given.
  • In a commercial dispute interview, cover facts, documents, losses, the other side's likely response, the client's goals, costs and timescales.
  • In advocacy, say what you want, why you should get it and what the evidence is, in that order.
  • A bail application succeeds by answering the specific risk the prosecution alleges with specific facts and conditions.

Lessons in this school

  1. How the SQE2 stations work
    Each SQE2 station gives you a short case file, a task and a strict time limit, and expects the work a competent newly qualified solicitor would produce.
  2. Client interview: a residential purchase
    In this simulation you interview a client who is buying her first home.
  3. Attendance note: recording the interview
    After an interview, the SQE2 asks you to write an attendance note or legal analysis recording the meeting and your advice.
  4. Client interview: a contract dispute
    In this simulation you interview a business owner whose supplier has delivered faulty goods.
  5. Advocacy: an interim application
    In this simulation you make a short oral application to a district judge for relief from sanctions, after your client missed a deadline.
  6. Advocacy: a bail application
    In this simulation you apply for bail in the magistrates' court for a client charged with an either-way offence.
  7. Case and matter analysis: a business dispute
    A case and matter analysis station gives you a file and asks for a written analysis for a supervising partner: the legal issues, the evidence, the options and a recommendation, with any conduct issues identified.
  8. Case and matter analysis: an estate
    In this simulation you analyse a probate file and advise the partner on who inherits, the executor's next steps and a potential claim against the estate.
  9. Legal research: a limitation question
    Legal research stations give you a question from a partner and a set of sources (some irrelevant), and ask for a research note with a clear answer, the authorities and how they apply.
  10. Legal research: a property question
    This research simulation asks you to find and apply the law on a neighbour's claimed right of way over a client's land, using a supplied set of sources.
  11. Legal writing: a letter of advice
    A legal writing station asks for a letter to a client or another party.
  12. Legal writing: a letter to the other side
    Letters to the other side must be accurate, firm and professional.
  13. Legal drafting: a clause in a commercial agreement
    Drafting stations ask you to draft or amend a legal document, such as a clause in a commercial agreement, so that it achieves the client's aims precisely, using clear and consistent language.
  14. Legal drafting: a witness statement
    In this simulation you draft a witness statement for civil proceedings from a client's interview notes.
  15. Ethics in the skills assessments
    Professional conduct issues are built into SQE2 stations without warning.

SQE2 Skills Practice: questions students ask

Why does it matter whether the £15,000 is a gift or a loan?

It affects the lender's security and must be disclosed to the lender; it also affects anti-money laundering checks.

What sections should an attendance note include?

Heading, purpose, facts obtained, advice given, analysis, conduct issues and action points.

What is the Denton test for relief from sanctions?

(1) The seriousness and significance of the breach; (2) why the default occurred; (3) all the circumstances, including the CPR 3.9(1) factors.

What happens to a gift of residue to a beneficiary who predeceases?

It lapses; unless the will or s 33 Wills Act 1837 saves it, it passes on intestacy.
