Lessons in this school

  1. Human rights theory
    Human rights theory asks what rights are, where they come from and why they matter.
  2. Universal human rights
    International human rights law began with the Universal Declaration of Human Rights in 1948.
  3. European Convention on Human Rights
    The ECHR protects civil and political rights across 46 Council of Europe states.
  4. Human Rights Act 1998
    The Human Rights Act 1998 brings Convention rights into UK law.
  5. Convention rights
    The Convention protects a set of core civil and political rights.
  6. Article 2: the right to life
    Article 2 protects the right to life.
  7. Article 3: prohibition of torture
    Article 3 prohibits torture and inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment.
  8. Article 4: slavery and forced labour
    Article 4 prohibits slavery and servitude absolutely, and forbids forced or compulsory labour, subject to listed exceptions.
  9. Article 5: liberty and security
    Article 5 protects against arbitrary detention.
  10. Article 6: fair trial
    Article 6 guarantees a fair and public hearing within a reasonable time by an independent and impartial tribunal, in the determination of civil rights and obligations or criminal charges.
  11. Article 8: private and family life
    Article 8 protects respect for private and family life, home and correspondence.
  12. Article 9: thought, conscience and religion
    Article 9 protects freedom of thought, conscience and religion.
  13. Article 10: freedom of expression
    Article 10 protects freedom of expression, including the press and the right to receive information.
  14. Article 11: assembly and association
    Article 11 protects peaceful assembly and freedom of association, including forming and joining trade unions.
  15. Article 14: discrimination
    Article 14 prohibits discrimination in the enjoyment of Convention rights.
  16. Protocol 1 Article 1: property
    Article 1 of Protocol 1 protects the peaceful enjoyment of possessions.
  17. Positive obligations
    Convention rights don't just stop the state interfering; they can require it to act.
  18. Proportionality
    Proportionality is central to qualified rights.
  19. Derogations
    Article 15 allows states to derogate from some Convention obligations in time of war or other public emergency threatening the life of the nation, to the extent strictly required.
  20. Remedies
    Remedies for breaches of Convention rights include declarations of incompatibility, damages under the HRA, quashing unlawful decisions, and, at Strasbourg, findings of violation and just satisfaction.
  21. Public authorities
    Section 6 of the HRA makes it unlawful for a public authority to act incompatibly with Convention rights.
  22. Human rights litigation
    Human rights claims can be brought as standalone HRA claims, through judicial review, or raised in other proceedings.
  23. International human rights frameworks
    Beyond the ECHR, human rights are protected by the UN system, regional systems in the Americas and Africa, international criminal law, and customary international law.

Key terms

Natural rights
Rights held by virtue of being human, independent of law
Universal Declaration of Human Rights
The 1948 UN General Assembly declaration of fundamental rights
Autonomous concept
A Convention term with its own meaning, independent of national law
Schedule 1
The schedule setting out the Convention rights given effect by the HRA
Engagement
Whether the facts fall within the scope of a right
Operational duty
The duty to take reasonable steps to protect a person from a real and immediate risk to life the state knew or ought to have known about
Minimum level of severity
The threshold ill-treatment must reach to fall within Article 3
Servitude
A particularly serious form of denial of freedom, including an obligation to live on another's property
Deprivation of liberty
Confinement that goes beyond a mere restriction on movement
Equality of arms
Each party must have a reasonable opportunity to present their case without substantial disadvantage
Private life
A broad concept covering physical and psychological integrity, identity, personal data and relationships
Forum internum
The inner realm of belief, absolutely protected
Political expression
Speech on matters of public interest, given the highest protection
Peaceful assembly
A gathering that isn't intended to be violent, even if disruptive
