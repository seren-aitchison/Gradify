Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

Give an example of an absolute Convention right.

Article 3: prohibition of torture and inhuman or degrading treatment. From “Human rights theory” →

In plain English: Human rights theory asks what rights are, where they come from and why they matter. Key idea: Rights theories differ on their source and scope; in law, rights are absolute, limited or qualified, with both negative and positive duties.

How do courts resolve conflicts between Article 8 and Article 10?

By an intense focus on the comparative importance of the rights in the specific case. From “Convention rights” →

In plain English: The Convention protects a set of core civil and political rights. Key idea: Identify the right, decide whether it is engaged and interfered with, then apply the test that matches its category.

Case card: Saunders v United Kingdom (1997) 23 EHRR 313. Facts, decision and principle?

Facts: Statements compelled by DTI inspectors under threat of penalty were used in Saunders's criminal trial. Held: A breach of Article 6. Principle: The privilege against self-incrimination is part of the right to a fair trial. From “Article 6: fair trial” →

In plain English: Article 6 guarantees a fair and public hearing within a reasonable time by an independent and impartial tribunal, in the determination of civil rights and obligations or criminal charges. Key idea: Article 6 requires an independent, impartial, public and timely hearing, with implied rights and extra criminal safeguards.

Which case is authority for this? For obstruction of the highway, the 'lawful excuse' defence requires a fact-specific proportionality assessment of the Article 10 and 11 rights.

DPP v Ziegler [2021] UKSC 23 From “Article 11: assembly and association” →

In plain English: Article 11 protects peaceful assembly and freedom of association, including forming and joining trade unions. Key idea: Article 11 protects peaceful, even disruptive, protest and association, but recent law narrows protection for serious disruption and damage.

Statute card: what does Human Rights Act 1998, s 14 provide?

Provides for designated derogations: the UK's derogations from Convention rights under Article 15 in time of war or public emergency threatening the life of the nation. From “Derogations” →

In plain English: Article 15 allows states to derogate from some Convention obligations in time of war or other public emergency threatening the life of the nation, to the extent strictly required. Key idea: Derogation needs a genuine emergency, strictly required measures and notification; some rights can't be derogated from.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Human Rights Law

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which category of right may be restricted if prescribed by law, for a legitimate aim and necessary in a democratic society? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Qualified rights. Articles 8–11 are qualified rights subject to this test.

  2. Which body monitors the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    The UN Human Rights Committee. The Human Rights Committee reviews ICCPR compliance.

  3. Which Protocol abolishes the death penalty in all circumstances? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Protocol 13. Protocol 13 abolishes it in all circumstances.

  4. Which theory holds that rights exist only when created by law? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Legal positivism. Positivists such as Bentham deny rights without legal recognition.

  5. A claimant argues that a UK benefit rule breaches the CRC, which the UK hasn't incorporated. What is the likely approach? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    The treaty isn't directly enforceable. SC confirmed the limited role of unincorporated treaties.

  6. UK soldiers detain a civilian at a base abroad under their control. Is the detainee within UK jurisdiction under Article 1? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Yes, through authority and control. Al-Skeini recognised jurisdiction based on authority and control.

  7. A hospital lets a voluntary mental health patient at high, known suicide risk go home on leave, and she dies. Is Article 2 engaged? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Yes: the operational duty may apply. Rabone extended the operational duty to voluntary patients.

  8. The government wants to deport a suspected terrorist to a state where he faces a real risk of torture, citing public safety. What is the position? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Deportation is prohibited by Article 3. Chahal and Saadi: Article 3 risk can't be weighed against security.

  9. A woman with severe learning disabilities lives in a pleasant care home, is supervised at all times and isn't allowed to leave alone. Is she deprived of liberty? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Yes, under the acid test. Cheshire West: comfort doesn't prevent a deprivation of liberty.

Key takeaways

  • Rights theories differ on their source and scope; in law, rights are absolute, limited or qualified, with both negative and positive duties.
  • UN instruments set global standards, but in the UK unincorporated treaties have only limited indirect effect.
  • Read Convention rights as practical and effective, using autonomous meanings and evolving standards.
  • Use the HRA within one year, expect modest damages, and remember s 12's protection of expression.
  • Identify the right, decide whether it is engaged and interfered with, then apply the test that matches its category.
  • The state must not take life unlawfully, must protect against known real and immediate risks, and must investigate deaths properly.

Common mistakes in Human Rights Law

  • Treating all Convention rights as qualified and subject to balancing.
  • Arguing that the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child is directly enforceable in English courts.
  • Assuming national labels decide whether proceedings involve a "criminal charge".
  • Missing the one-year time limit for a standalone HRA claim.
  • Applying a proportionality justification to an absolute right such as Article 3.
  • Applying the Osman duty where the risk wasn't real and immediate or known to the authorities.

Lessons in this school

  1. Human rights theory
    Human rights theory asks what rights are, where they come from and why they matter.
  2. Universal human rights
    International human rights law began with the Universal Declaration of Human Rights in 1948.
  3. European Convention on Human Rights
    The ECHR protects civil and political rights across 46 Council of Europe states.
  4. Human Rights Act 1998
    The Human Rights Act 1998 brings Convention rights into UK law.
  5. Convention rights
    The Convention protects a set of core civil and political rights.
  6. Article 2: the right to life
    Article 2 protects the right to life.
  7. Article 3: prohibition of torture
    Article 3 prohibits torture and inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment.
  8. Article 4: slavery and forced labour
    Article 4 prohibits slavery and servitude absolutely, and forbids forced or compulsory labour, subject to listed exceptions.
  9. Article 5: liberty and security
    Article 5 protects against arbitrary detention.
  10. Article 6: fair trial
    Article 6 guarantees a fair and public hearing within a reasonable time by an independent and impartial tribunal, in the determination of civil rights and obligations or criminal charges.
  11. Article 8: private and family life
    Article 8 protects respect for private and family life, home and correspondence.
  12. Article 9: thought, conscience and religion
    Article 9 protects freedom of thought, conscience and religion.
  13. Article 10: freedom of expression
    Article 10 protects freedom of expression, including the press and the right to receive information.
  14. Article 11: assembly and association
    Article 11 protects peaceful assembly and freedom of association, including forming and joining trade unions.
  15. Article 14: discrimination
    Article 14 prohibits discrimination in the enjoyment of Convention rights.
  16. Protocol 1 Article 1: property
    Article 1 of Protocol 1 protects the peaceful enjoyment of possessions.
  17. Positive obligations
    Convention rights don't just stop the state interfering; they can require it to act.
  18. Proportionality
    Proportionality is central to qualified rights.
  19. Derogations
    Article 15 allows states to derogate from some Convention obligations in time of war or other public emergency threatening the life of the nation, to the extent strictly required.
  20. Remedies
    Remedies for breaches of Convention rights include declarations of incompatibility, damages under the HRA, quashing unlawful decisions, and, at Strasbourg, findings of violation and just satisfaction.
  21. Public authorities
    Section 6 of the HRA makes it unlawful for a public authority to act incompatibly with Convention rights.
  22. Human rights litigation
    Human rights claims can be brought as standalone HRA claims, through judicial review, or raised in other proceedings.
  23. International human rights frameworks
    Beyond the ECHR, human rights are protected by the UN system, regional systems in the Americas and Africa, international criminal law, and customary international law.

Human Rights Law: questions students ask

What did Bentham call natural rights?

"Nonsense upon stilts".

What three instruments form the International Bill of Rights?

The UDHR, the ICCPR and the ICESCR.

What does Article 1 ECHR require?

States must secure Convention rights to everyone within their jurisdiction.

Case card: Campbell v MGN Ltd [2004] UKHL 22. Facts, decision and principle?

Facts: The Daily Mirror published that the model Naomi Campbell was attending Narcotics Anonymous, with photographs of her leaving a meeting. Held: The details of treatment and the photographs were a misuse of private information. Principle: Misuse of private information: a reasonable expectation of privacy, then a balance between Articles 8 and 10.
