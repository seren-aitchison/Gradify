Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check.

Name three sources of solicitors' professional obligations.

The SRA Principles and Codes, statute (such as the Solicitors Act 1974 and POCA 2002), and the general law (contract, tort, fiduciary duties). From “Professional ethics” →

Are there any exceptions to the bar on own-interest conflicts?

No. A solicitor must not act where there is an own-interest conflict or significant risk of one. From “Conflicts of interest” →

Case card: Bolton v Law Society [1994] 1 WLR 512. Facts, decision and principle?

Facts: A solicitor who had not been dishonest was suspended for misusing client money; he argued the sanction was too harsh. Held: The suspension stood. Principle: The most fundamental purpose of sanctions is to maintain the reputation of the profession: 'a profession whose members may be trusted to the ends of the earth'. From “Honesty and integrity” →

What is the corporate offence under s 7 of the Bribery Act 2010?

Failure by a commercial organisation to prevent bribery by persons associated with it, with a defence of adequate procedures. From “Financial crime” →

Case card: Bristol and West Building Society v Mothew [1998] Ch 1. Facts, decision and principle?

Facts: A solicitor acting for a buyer and a lender failed to tell the lender about a second charge, through carelessness, not deliberately. Held: No breach of fiduciary duty; the claim was in negligence. Principle: Not every breach by a fiduciary is a breach of fiduciary duty; the distinctive obligation is loyalty. From “Duties to clients” →

Lessons in this school

  1. Professional ethics
    Professional ethics are the standards of behaviour expected of lawyers because of their special role: they serve clients, but also the courts, the rule of law and the public.
  2. SRA Principles
    The seven SRA Principles are the fundamental ethical requirements for everyone the SRA regulates.
  3. SRA Codes of Conduct
    The SRA has two Codes of Conduct: one for individuals (solicitors, registered European and foreign lawyers) and one for firms.
  4. Client confidentiality
    Solicitors must keep the affairs of current and former clients confidential unless disclosure is required or permitted by law or the client consents (Code, para 6.3).
  5. Legal professional privilege
    Legal professional privilege (LPP) protects certain communications between lawyers and clients from disclosure, even to a court.
  6. Conflicts of interest
    A solicitor must not act where there is a conflict of interests, or a significant risk of one, unless a narrow exception applies.
  7. Client money
    Client money is money held or received by a firm for or on behalf of a client or third party (and certain other categories).
  8. Solicitors' accounts
    The SRA Accounts Rules require firms to keep accurate accounting records, reconcile client accounts regularly and, in most cases, obtain an accountant's report.
  9. Client care
    Client care is about giving clients the information and service they need: who will do the work, what it will cost, what will happen and how to complain.
  10. Competence
    Solicitors must provide a proper standard of service, be competent to carry out their role and keep their professional knowledge and skills up to date (Code paras 3.2–3.6).
  11. Honesty and integrity
    Honesty and integrity are among the most important requirements of being a solicitor.
  12. Independence
    Solicitors must act with independence (Principle 3): their professional judgement must not be compromised by the interests of third parties, introducers, funders, employers or their own interests.
  13. Professional duties to the court
    Solicitors and barristers owe duties to the court that override their duties to clients.
  14. Undertakings
    An undertaking is a statement, given orally or in writing, by which a solicitor (or someone in a firm) agrees to do or not do something.
  15. Anti-money laundering
    Solicitors are a target for money launderers because they handle large sums and give credibility to transactions.
  16. Financial crime
    Beyond money laundering, solicitors must understand other financial crimes that can affect their practice or their clients: fraud, bribery, sanctions breaches, tax evasion facilitation and terrorist financing.
  17. Equality and diversity
    Solicitors must act in a way that encourages equality, diversity and inclusion (Principle 6), and must not unfairly discriminate (Code para 1.1).
  18. Complaints
    Clients who are unhappy with a firm's service have a right to complain, first to the firm and then to the Legal Ombudsman.
  19. Professional negligence
    A solicitor who fails to exercise the reasonable care and skill of a competent solicitor and causes loss may be liable to the client in contract and tort, and sometimes to third parties.
  20. Regulatory compliance
    Firms and individual solicitors must have systems to comply with regulatory requirements, cooperate with the SRA and report serious breaches.
  21. Duties to clients
    Solicitors owe clients duties under the SRA Principles and Code, in contract, in tort, and as fiduciaries.
  22. Duties to third parties
    Solicitors owe duties not only to clients but also to third parties, including the other side, witnesses and others affected by their work.
  23. Duties to the public
    Solicitors serve the public interest: upholding the rule of law and the administration of justice, maintaining public trust in the profession, and protecting the public from harm.
  24. Professional boundaries
    Professional boundaries keep the relationship between solicitor and client, and between colleagues, appropriate.
  25. Ethical decision-making
    Ethical decision-making is a structured way of working out the right course of action when duties, interests and pressures conflict.

Key terms

Officer of the court
A solicitor's status carrying duties to the administration of justice
SRA Principles
The seven fundamental ethical requirements for SRA-regulated people and firms
Code of Conduct for Solicitors
The SRA's standards for individual solicitors, RELs, RFLs and RSLs
Confidentiality
The duty to keep clients' affairs confidential
Legal advice privilege
Protection for confidential lawyer-client communications for legal advice
Own-interest conflict
A conflict between the solicitor's own interests and the client's
Client money
Money held or received for or on behalf of a client or third party
Reconciliation
Comparing client ledger, cash book and bank statements
Client care letter
A letter setting out terms, costs and complaints information
Continuing competence
The SRA's requirement to keep skills and knowledge up to date
Dishonesty
Conduct dishonest by the standards of ordinary decent people
Independence
Professional judgement free from compromise by others' interests
Duty not to mislead
The duty not to mislead or attempt to mislead the court
Undertaking
A statement by which a solicitor agrees to do or not do something
