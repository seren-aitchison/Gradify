Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What is the overriding objective?

Enabling the court to deal with cases justly and at proportionate cost (CPR 1.1). From “Civil justice system” →

In plain English: Civil disputes in England and Wales are resolved under the Civil Procedure Rules 1998 (CPR), which aim to deal with cases justly and at proportionate cost. Key idea: The CPR serve the overriding objective; the track a case is allocated to sets its procedure and costs regime.

Within what period must a claim form be served after issue?

Four months (CPR 7.5). From “Commencing proceedings” →

In plain English: Proceedings begin when the court issues a claim form. Key idea: Issue, serve within four months, and track the defendant's 14 or 28 days to respond.

What does standard disclosure require?

Disclosure of documents relied on, and documents that adversely affect or support any party's case. From “Disclosure” →

In plain English: Disclosure is the process by which parties reveal documents relevant to the issues. Key idea: Disclose documents that help or harm either side, after a proportionate search; privilege protects some documents from inspection.

Which case is authority for this? A 'realistic' prospect of success is more than merely arguable, and the court shouldn't conduct a mini-trial.

Easyair Ltd v Opal Telecom Ltd [2009] EWHC 339 (Ch) From “Summary judgment” →

In plain English: Summary judgment lets the court decide a claim or issue without a trial where one side has no real prospect of success and there's no other compelling reason for a trial. Key idea: Summary judgment requires no real prospect of success and no compelling reason for trial; it isn't a mini-trial.

Are civil trials normally held in public?

Yes (CPR 39.2), subject to exceptions. From “Trial procedure” →

In plain English: At trial, each party presents its case through evidence and submissions, and the judge decides the facts and the law. Key idea: Trial follows a set order; each side calls evidence, cross-examines and makes submissions, then the judge gives judgment.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Dispute Resolution and Civil Litigation

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which track normally covers claims between £25,000 and £100,000 with a short trial? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Intermediate track. The intermediate track was introduced in October 2023.

  2. Which applies where no specific pre-action protocol covers the claim? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    The Practice Direction on Pre-Action Conduct. The Practice Direction sets general expectations for all claims.

  3. What must a letter before claim include under the Practice Direction? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Details of the claim and key documents. Paragraph 6 requires concise details and key documents.

  4. A party ignores court directions, causing delay and expense. Which part of the overriding objective is most relevant? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Enforcing compliance with rules and orders. CPR 1.1(2)(f) includes enforcing compliance with rules, practice directions and orders.

  5. A claimant issues proceedings without any pre-action letter and wins. The defendant would have paid if asked. What might the court do? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Refuse the claimant some or all of its costs. Non-compliance can affect costs even for a winning party.

  6. A business is owed £5,000 by an individual customer. What must its letter before claim include? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    An information sheet and reply form. The Debt Claims Protocol requires these documents for individual debtors.

  7. Particulars are served on 1 March. The defendant files an acknowledgment of service. When is the defence due, ignoring extensions? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    28 days after service, around 29 March. CPR 15.4: 28 days after service of the particulars where an acknowledgment is filed.

  8. A party serves witness statements one day late because of a diary error, with no effect on the trial date. How is relief likely to be treated? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Likely granted: the breach is not serious. Under Denton, a trivial breach with no effect on the timetable usually attracts relief.

  9. A defendant makes a valid Part 36 offer of £50,000. The claimant rejects it and wins £45,000 at trial. What is the usual costs outcome? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    The claimant pays the defendant's costs from expiry of the relevant period. CPR 36.17(3): failing to beat a defendant's offer shifts costs.

Key takeaways

  • The CPR serve the overriding objective; the track a case is allocated to sets its procedure and costs regime.
  • Follow the specific protocol or the Practice Direction before issuing; the court can penalise non-compliance in costs and interest.
  • A letter before claim should explain the claim, enclose key documents, invite ADR and allow a reasonable time to respond.
  • Identify the cause of action, find the period, then consider date of knowledge, longstops and extensions.
  • Choose the right domestic court by value and complexity; for foreign defendants, check service as of right, gateways and Hague Conventions.
  • Issue, serve within four months, and track the defendant's 14 or 28 days to respond.

Common mistakes in Dispute Resolution and Civil Litigation

  • Treating the CPR as a rigid code: courts interpret them to further the overriding objective.
  • Issuing proceedings without any pre-action letter in an ordinary claim.
  • Threatening to report the defendant to the police unless they pay: improper and potentially a conduct breach.
  • Applying the six-year tort period to a personal injury claim.
  • Applying the Brussels Recast Regulation to new cases after Brexit.
  • Calculating the defendant's time from the date of issue rather than service of the particulars.

Lessons in this school

  1. Civil justice system
    Civil disputes in England and Wales are resolved under the Civil Procedure Rules 1998 (CPR), which aim to deal with cases justly and at proportionate cost.
  2. Pre-action protocols
    Before issuing proceedings, parties are expected to exchange information, try to settle and consider ADR.
  3. Letter before claim
    The letter before claim is the claimant's formal statement of the claim before proceedings.
  4. Limitation
    Limitation periods set time limits for bringing claims.
  5. Jurisdiction
    Jurisdiction decides which court can hear a case.
  6. Commencing proceedings
    Proceedings begin when the court issues a claim form.
  7. Particulars of claim
    The particulars of claim set out the claimant's case: the facts relied on, the legal basis and the remedy sought.
  8. Defence and counterclaims
    The defence responds to each allegation in the particulars of claim.
  9. Statements of case
    Statements of case are the formal documents setting out each party's case: the claim form, particulars of claim, defence, counterclaim, reply and further information.
  10. Case management
    Courts actively manage cases to further the overriding objective.
  11. Disclosure
    Disclosure is the process by which parties reveal documents relevant to the issues.
  12. Evidence
    Evidence proves the facts in dispute.
  13. Witness statements
    Witness statements set out the evidence a witness will give at trial, in their own words.
  14. Expert witnesses
    Expert evidence helps the court with matters outside its own knowledge.
  15. Interim applications
    Interim applications ask the court for orders before trial, such as directions, injunctions, interim payments or security for costs.
  16. Summary judgment
    Summary judgment lets the court decide a claim or issue without a trial where one side has no real prospect of success and there's no other compelling reason for a trial.
  17. Strike-out
    The court can strike out all or part of a statement of case that discloses no reasonable grounds, is an abuse of process, or follows failure to comply with rules or orders.
  18. Settlement
    Most civil cases settle.
  19. Mediation
    Mediation is a confidential, voluntary process in which a neutral mediator helps the parties negotiate a settlement.
  20. Trial preparation
    Preparing for trial means complying with final directions, completing the pre-trial checklist, preparing bundles and skeleton arguments, and making sure witnesses and counsel are ready.
  21. Trial procedure
    At trial, each party presents its case through evidence and submissions, and the judge decides the facts and the law.
  22. Costs
    In civil litigation, the general rule is that the losing party pays the winner's costs, but the court has a wide discretion.
  23. Enforcement
    Winning a judgment doesn't guarantee payment.
  24. Appeals
    An appeal challenges a decision of a lower court.
  25. Alternative dispute resolution
    Alternative dispute resolution covers methods other than court trial: negotiation, mediation, early neutral evaluation, expert determination, adjudication, arbitration and ombudsman schemes.
  26. Professional conduct in litigation
    Litigation tests a solicitor's duties most sharply.

Dispute Resolution and Civil Litigation: questions students ask

What can the court do if a party fails to follow pre-action conduct?

Order costs, adjust interest, or stay proceedings.

Case card: Cartledge v E Jopling and Sons Ltd [1963] AC 758. Facts, decision and principle?

Facts: Workers' lungs were damaged by dust before they could know of it; they sued after six years. Held: Time-barred: the cause of action accrued on damage, even though undiscoverable. Principle: The injustice prompted the date of knowledge rules now in the Limitation Act 1980, ss 11 and 14.

Case card: Spiliada Maritime Corp v Cansulex Ltd [1987] AC 460. Facts, decision and principle?

Facts: A shipowner sued a Canadian sulphur shipper in England over damage to a ship's cargo hold. Held: England was the appropriate forum: very similar proceedings involving the same experts were already there. Principle: Forum non conveniens: is there another available forum clearly more appropriate, and does justice require a stay anyway?

What must particulars of claim contain?

A concise statement of the facts relied on, any interest claim, and other specified matters.
