Lessons in this school

  1. Civil justice system
    Civil disputes in England and Wales are resolved under the Civil Procedure Rules 1998 (CPR), which aim to deal with cases justly and at proportionate cost.
  2. Pre-action protocols
    Before issuing proceedings, parties are expected to exchange information, try to settle and consider ADR.
  3. Letter before claim
    The letter before claim is the claimant's formal statement of the claim before proceedings.
  4. Limitation
    Limitation periods set time limits for bringing claims.
  5. Jurisdiction
    Jurisdiction decides which court can hear a case.
  6. Commencing proceedings
    Proceedings begin when the court issues a claim form.
  7. Particulars of claim
    The particulars of claim set out the claimant's case: the facts relied on, the legal basis and the remedy sought.
  8. Defence and counterclaims
    The defence responds to each allegation in the particulars of claim.
  9. Statements of case
    Statements of case are the formal documents setting out each party's case: the claim form, particulars of claim, defence, counterclaim, reply and further information.
  10. Case management
    Courts actively manage cases to further the overriding objective.
  11. Disclosure
    Disclosure is the process by which parties reveal documents relevant to the issues.
  12. Evidence
    Evidence proves the facts in dispute.
  13. Witness statements
    Witness statements set out the evidence a witness will give at trial, in their own words.
  14. Expert witnesses
    Expert evidence helps the court with matters outside its own knowledge.
  15. Interim applications
    Interim applications ask the court for orders before trial, such as directions, injunctions, interim payments or security for costs.
  16. Summary judgment
    Summary judgment lets the court decide a claim or issue without a trial where one side has no real prospect of success and there's no other compelling reason for a trial.
  17. Strike-out
    The court can strike out all or part of a statement of case that discloses no reasonable grounds, is an abuse of process, or follows failure to comply with rules or orders.
  18. Settlement
    Most civil cases settle.
  19. Mediation
    Mediation is a confidential, voluntary process in which a neutral mediator helps the parties negotiate a settlement.
  20. Trial preparation
    Preparing for trial means complying with final directions, completing the pre-trial checklist, preparing bundles and skeleton arguments, and making sure witnesses and counsel are ready.
  21. Trial procedure
    At trial, each party presents its case through evidence and submissions, and the judge decides the facts and the law.
  22. Costs
    In civil litigation, the general rule is that the losing party pays the winner's costs, but the court has a wide discretion.
  23. Enforcement
    Winning a judgment doesn't guarantee payment.
  24. Appeals
    An appeal challenges a decision of a lower court.
  25. Alternative dispute resolution
    Alternative dispute resolution covers methods other than court trial: negotiation, mediation, early neutral evaluation, expert determination, adjudication, arbitration and ombudsman schemes.
  26. Professional conduct in litigation
    Litigation tests a solicitor's duties most sharply.

Key terms

Overriding objective
The aim of dealing with cases justly and at proportionate cost (CPR 1.1)
Pre-action protocol
A set of steps parties are expected to take before issuing a particular type of claim
Letter before claim
The claimant's pre-action letter setting out the claim
Limitation period
The time within which a claim must be brought
Service out of the jurisdiction
Serving proceedings on a defendant abroad
Claim form
The document that starts proceedings (CPR Part 7, form N1)
Particulars of claim
The statement of case setting out the claimant's claim
Admission
Accepting an allegation as true
Statement of case
A claim form, particulars of claim, defence, Part 20 claim or reply, including further information (CPR 2.3)
Directions
Court orders setting the steps and timetable to trial
Standard disclosure
Disclosing documents relied on and those that adversely affect or support a party's case (CPR 31.6)
Balance of probabilities
The civil standard of proof: more likely than not
Evidence in chief
A witness's main evidence, usually given by their statement
Overriding duty
An expert's duty to help the court, which overrides any obligation to the instructing party (CPR 35.3)
