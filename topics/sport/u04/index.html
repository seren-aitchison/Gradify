Lessons in this school

  1. Thermoregulation during exercise
    Exercising muscles produce large amounts of heat, which the body must lose to stay safe.
  2. Exercise in the heat and acclimatisation
    Hot conditions reduce performance and increase the risk of heat illness.
  3. Exercise in the cold
    Cold environments challenge the body to conserve heat.
  4. Altitude and hypoxia
    At altitude, the air contains less oxygen pressure, challenging aerobic performance.
  5. Altitude training
    Many endurance athletes train at altitude hoping to improve performance at sea level.
  6. Neuromuscular adaptations to strength training
    Early strength gains happen before muscles grow much larger.
  7. Muscle hypertrophy
    Over longer periods, resistance training increases muscle size.
  8. Overtraining and underperformance
    Training too hard without enough recovery can reduce performance and harm health.
  9. Detraining
    When training stops or is greatly reduced, fitness gains are gradually lost.
  10. Exercise and ageing
    Ageing brings changes in muscle, bone, the heart and balance, but regular exercise slows many of them.
  11. Children and young athletes
    Children are not small adults: they differ in physiology, growth and maturation.
  12. Female athletes and the menstrual cycle
    Female athletes have been under-represented in sport science research.
  13. Relative energy deficiency in sport (RED-S)
    Athletes who do not eat enough to fuel training can develop serious health and performance problems.
  14. Exercise in pregnancy
    Physical activity during pregnancy benefits both parent and baby.
  15. Exercise for long-term conditions
    Exercise is an important part of managing many long-term conditions.

Key terms

Thermoregulation
Control of body temperature within a narrow range
Heat acclimatisation
Physiological adaptation to repeated heat exposure
Vasoconstriction
Narrowing of blood vessels
Partial pressure
The pressure exerted by one gas in a mixture
Erythropoietin
A hormone that stimulates red blood cell production
Motor unit
A motor neuron and the muscle fibres it controls
Hypertrophy
An increase in muscle size
Functional overreaching
A short-term performance drop followed by improvement
Reversibility
The principle that training adaptations are lost without continued training
Sarcopenia
Age-related loss of muscle mass and function
Biological age
A measure of physical maturity rather than years lived
Follicular phase
The first phase of the cycle, from menstruation to ovulation
Energy availability
Dietary energy minus exercise energy, relative to fat-free mass
Relaxin
A pregnancy hormone that increases joint laxity
