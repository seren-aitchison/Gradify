Lessons in this school

  1. The nature of skill
    Skill is a learned ability to produce a chosen result consistently and efficiently.
  2. Classifying skills
    Skills can be classified on continua according to their features.
  3. Abilities
    Abilities are mostly innate traits that underpin the learning of skills.
  4. Types of practice
    How practice is organised affects how quickly and how well skills are learned.
  5. Transfer of skill
    Learning one skill can help, hinder or not affect the learning of another.
  6. Stages of learning
    Learners progress through recognisable stages as they acquire a skill.
  7. Learning curves and plateaus
    Learning curves show how performance changes with practice.
  8. Theories of learning
    Different theories explain how people learn movement skills.
  9. Guidance
    Guidance is information given to help a learner perform a skill.
  10. Feedback
    Feedback is information about a performance that helps the learner correct errors and stay motivated.
  11. Information-processing models
    Information-processing models describe how performers take in information, make decisions and produce movement.
  12. Reaction time and Hick's law
    Reaction time is the time from the onset of a stimulus to the start of a response.
  13. Memory in skill learning
    Memory allows performers to store and recall information and motor programmes.
  14. Schema theory
    Schema theory explains how performers adapt learned movements to new situations.
  15. Ecological and constraints-led approaches
    Ecological approaches see skill as emerging from the interaction between the performer, the task and the environment.
  16. The role of the coach
    Coaches have many roles beyond teaching technique.
  17. Coaching styles
    Coaches can use different styles depending on the learner, the skill and the situation.
  18. Planning a coaching session
    A well-planned session is safe, progressive and matched to participants' needs.
  19. Long-term athlete development
    Long-term athlete development models aim to match training to children's growth and maturation.
  20. Safeguarding and ethics in coaching
    Coaches have a duty to keep children and adults at risk safe and to behave ethically.

Key terms

Skill
A learned ability to bring about pre-determined results with maximum certainty and minimum outlay of time or energy
Open skill
A skill affected by a changing environment
Ability
An innate, stable trait that underpins skill
Distributed practice
Practice with rest intervals or other activities between attempts
Positive transfer
When one skill helps the learning of another
Cognitive stage
The first stage of learning, when the learner tries to understand the skill
Learning curve
A graph of performance against trials or time
Positive reinforcement
Giving a pleasant stimulus to strengthen a correct response
Visual guidance
Information given through demonstrations, video or images
Knowledge of results
Feedback about the outcome of a performance
Display
The sporting environment from which information is taken
Reaction time
Time from the onset of a stimulus to the start of a response
Short-term sensory store
A very brief store for information from the senses
Schema
A set of generalised rules from experience used to adapt movements
