Lessons in this school

  1. Linear motion: distance, displacement, speed, velocity and acceleration
    Linear motion is movement in a straight or curved line where all parts of the body move the same distance in the same direction at the same time.
  2. Distance–time and velocity–time graphs
    Graphs show how motion changes during a performance.
  3. Angular motion
    Angular motion is movement around an axis, as when a gymnast somersaults or a limb rotates at a joint.
  4. Centre of mass and stability
    The centre of mass is the point at which the mass of a body can be considered to be concentrated.
  5. Newton's laws of motion
    Newton's three laws describe how forces change motion.
  6. Types of force in sport
    Forces are pushes or pulls that can change the motion or shape of a body.
  7. Free-body diagrams
    Free-body diagrams show all the external forces acting on a body at one instant.
  8. Impulse and momentum
    Momentum is the quantity of motion a body has, and impulse is how a force changes it over time.
  9. Friction
    Friction is the force that opposes two surfaces sliding over each other.
  10. Levers
    The skeleton and muscles work together as lever systems to produce movement.
  11. Mechanical advantage of lever systems
    Different lever classes favour either force or speed and range of movement.
  12. Projectile motion
    A projectile is any body released into the air that is acted on only by weight and air resistance.
  13. Factors affecting projectile distance
    The horizontal distance a projectile travels depends on its speed, angle and height of release, together with aerodynamic factors.
  14. Fluid mechanics: drag
    Drag is the force that opposes a body moving through air or water.
  15. The Bernoulli principle and lift
    Lift is a force that acts at right angles to the airflow and can keep projectiles in the air for longer or press vehicles onto the ground.
  16. Spin and the Magnus effect
    Spinning balls curve in flight because of the Magnus effect.
  17. Moment of inertia and angular momentum
    Moment of inertia is a body's resistance to changes in rotation, and angular momentum is the quantity of rotation it has.
  18. Conservation of angular momentum
    Once a performer leaves the ground, their angular momentum stays the same unless an external torque acts.
  19. Biomechanical analysis and technology
    Coaches and scientists use qualitative and quantitative methods to analyse movement and improve technique.

Key terms

Displacement
The straight-line change in position of a body in a given direction
Gradient
The steepness of a line, change in y divided by change in x
Eccentric force
A force applied outside the centre of mass, causing rotation
Centre of mass
The point at which a body's mass is concentrated and balanced
Inertia
The resistance of a body to a change in its state of motion
Weight
The gravitational force on a body, W = mg, acting downwards
Free-body diagram
A sketch showing all external forces acting on a body at one instant
Momentum
The quantity of motion, mass × velocity, in kg m/s
Coefficient of friction
A number describing the grip between two surfaces
Fulcrum
The pivot point of a lever, usually a joint
Effort arm
The perpendicular distance from the fulcrum to the effort
Projectile
A body launched into the air, acted on only by weight and air resistance
Range
The horizontal distance travelled by a projectile
Drag
A force opposing the motion of a body through a fluid
