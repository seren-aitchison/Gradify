Lessons in this school

  1. Why test?
    Fitness testing gives coaches and athletes objective information about current fitness.
  2. Validity, reliability and objectivity
    A fitness test is only useful if it measures the right thing consistently and fairly.
  3. Pre-test procedures and health screening
    Fitness testing places stress on the body, so testers must check that it is safe for each person.
  4. Ethics and informed consent in testing
    Testing people raises ethical responsibilities.
  5. Health-related and skill-related fitness
    Fitness is made up of health-related and skill-related components.
  6. Testing aerobic capacity
    Aerobic capacity is measured most accurately as VO2max in a laboratory, but field tests are widely used to estimate it.
  7. Testing strength and power
    Strength is the maximum force a muscle can produce, and power is how quickly force can be applied.
  8. Testing speed and agility
    Speed and agility tests measure how quickly an athlete can sprint and change direction.
  9. Testing flexibility
    Flexibility tests measure the range of movement at a joint.
  10. Body composition
    Body composition describes the proportions of fat mass and fat-free mass in the body.
  11. The Wingate anaerobic test
    The Wingate anaerobic test is a 30-second all-out cycle test used to measure anaerobic power and capacity.
  12. Normative data and interpreting results
    Test scores mean little without something to compare them with.
  13. Laboratory versus field testing
    Fitness can be tested in a controlled laboratory or in the field where sport is played.
  14. Performance and notational analysis
    Performance analysis records and examines what happens in training and competition.
  15. Wearable technology and GPS
    Wearable devices allow coaches to monitor athletes' movements and physiological responses in training and matches.

Key terms

Baseline
An initial measurement used for comparison later
Validity
The extent to which a test measures what it claims to measure
PAR-Q+
The Physical Activity Readiness Questionnaire for Everyone, a health screening tool
Informed consent
Voluntary agreement to take part after understanding the purpose, risks and right to withdraw
Health-related fitness
Components linked to health and everyday function
VO2max
The maximum rate of oxygen uptake and use, in ml/kg/min
One-repetition maximum
The heaviest load that can be lifted once with correct technique
Timing gates
Electronic sensors that start and stop timing as an athlete passes
Sit-and-reach test
A field test of hamstring and lower back flexibility
Body mass index
Body mass in kg divided by height in metres squared
Peak power
The highest power output in the test, usually in the first few seconds
Normative data
Reference scores from a large group, used for comparison
Laboratory test
A test carried out under controlled conditions with specialist equipment
Notational analysis
Recording events in performance using a coding system
