Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

Why is a baseline test useful?

It gives a starting point against which later improvement can be measured. From “Why test?” →

In plain English: Fitness testing gives coaches and athletes objective information about current fitness. Key idea: Fitness tests identify strengths and weaknesses, provide baselines, monitor progress, set goals and motivate, but they have limitations such as poor specificity, dependence on motivation and the influence of test conditions.

What must participants be told for consent to be informed?

The purpose, procedures, risks, benefits and their right to withdraw. From “Ethics and informed consent in testing” →

In plain English: Testing people raises ethical responsibilities. Key idea: Ethical testing requires voluntary informed consent with the right to withdraw, confidential and lawful handling of data, and extra safeguards such as parental consent and sensitivity when testing young people.

What does a handgrip dynamometer measure?

Static (isometric) grip strength. From “Testing strength and power” →

In plain English: Strength is the maximum force a muscle can produce, and power is how quickly force can be applied. Key idea: Strength is tested with 1RM, multiple-repetition and handgrip tests, and power with vertical, countermovement and broad jumps and throws, with jump height and mass used to estimate peak power; tests must be safe, specific and compared relative to body mass.

What is the formula for BMI?

Body mass (kg) ÷ height (m) squared. From “Body composition” →

In plain English: Body composition describes the proportions of fat mass and fat-free mass in the body. Key idea: Body composition can be assessed by BMI, waist-to-height ratio, skinfolds, BIA and DEXA; each balances cost and accuracy, BMI cannot distinguish muscle from fat, and results must be handled privately and sensitively.

Give one advantage of a field test.

It is cheap and allows a whole group to be tested at once. From “Laboratory versus field testing” →

In plain English: Fitness can be tested in a controlled laboratory or in the field where sport is played. Key idea: Laboratory tests are direct, controlled, reliable and precise but costly and less realistic; field tests are cheap, specific and suit groups but are less controlled, so the choice depends on purpose, resources and the need for ecological validity.

  2. What does it mean if a test is valid? (Foundation recall)

  4. A coach retests a squad after eight weeks and sees no improvement in aerobic scores. What should she do? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Review and adjust the training programme. Lack of change suggests the programme needs changing.

  5. A sprint is timed by three different coaches with stopwatches, giving different results. What does this show? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Poor inter-tester reliability. Different testers disagreeing indicates poor inter-tester reliability.

  6. A participant's resting blood pressure is 150/95 mmHg. What should the tester do? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Delay strenuous testing and advise a GP check. A reading of 140/90 or above should be followed up before strenuous testing.

  7. Why should a coach be cautious about using tests alone for team selection? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Tests do not capture skill, tactics and decision-making. Selection needs a broader picture of performance.

  8. A coach tests aerobic fitness in a hot sports hall in June and a cool one in December. What is the main concern? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Changing conditions reduce reliability. Different environmental conditions can change results.

  9. During a bleep test, a student stops and says he has chest pain that is not easing. What is the correct action? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Stop testing, call 999 and give first aid. Chest pain that does not settle may be a medical emergency.

Key takeaways

  • Fitness tests identify strengths and weaknesses, provide baselines, monitor progress, set goals and motivate, but they have limitations such as poor specificity, dependence on motivation and the influence of test conditions.
  • Validity means a test measures what it claims, reliability means it gives consistent results, and objectivity means it is free from tester bias; standardised protocols, calibrated equipment and the same conditions improve test quality.
  • Health screening with the PAR-Q+, resting heart rate and blood pressure identifies people who need medical clearance, and testers must follow pre-test procedures and stop tests immediately if warning signs appear, calling 999 for chest pain that does not settle or collapse.
  • Ethical testing requires voluntary informed consent with the right to withdraw, confidential and lawful handling of data, and extra safeguards such as parental consent and sensitivity when testing young people.
  • Health-related components are cardiorespiratory endurance, muscular strength, muscular endurance, flexibility and body composition; skill-related components are agility, balance, coordination, power, reaction time and speed, and each sport needs its own profile.
  • VO2max is measured directly by gas analysis in the laboratory or estimated in the field with the multistage fitness test, Cooper 12-minute run or Yo-Yo test, which are cheaper and suit groups but are less valid and depend on motivation.

Common mistakes in Fitness Testing and Performance Analysis

  • Using a single test score to judge a player's overall ability. Tests measure isolated components and should be combined with match performance and other information.
  • Thinking a reliable test must be valid. A test can give consistent results while measuring the wrong thing, such as using a 12-minute run to assess sprint speed.
  • Testing everyone maximally without screening. Screening identifies people at increased risk who need medical clearance first.
  • Treating a signature as enough for consent. Consent is only informed if the person understood the purpose, risks and right to withdraw, and agreed without pressure.
  • Confusing reaction time and speed. Reaction time is the delay before movement begins; speed is how fast the movement is once it has started.
  • Treating a bleep test score as an exact VO2max. Field tests only predict VO2max and are influenced by motivation, running economy and turning skill.

Lessons in this school

  1. Why test?
    Fitness testing gives coaches and athletes objective information about current fitness.
  2. Validity, reliability and objectivity
    A fitness test is only useful if it measures the right thing consistently and fairly.
  3. Pre-test procedures and health screening
    Fitness testing places stress on the body, so testers must check that it is safe for each person.
  4. Ethics and informed consent in testing
    Testing people raises ethical responsibilities.
  5. Health-related and skill-related fitness
    Fitness is made up of health-related and skill-related components.
  6. Testing aerobic capacity
    Aerobic capacity is measured most accurately as VO2max in a laboratory, but field tests are widely used to estimate it.
  7. Testing strength and power
    Strength is the maximum force a muscle can produce, and power is how quickly force can be applied.
  8. Testing speed and agility
    Speed and agility tests measure how quickly an athlete can sprint and change direction.
  9. Testing flexibility
    Flexibility tests measure the range of movement at a joint.
  10. Body composition
    Body composition describes the proportions of fat mass and fat-free mass in the body.
  11. The Wingate anaerobic test
    The Wingate anaerobic test is a 30-second all-out cycle test used to measure anaerobic power and capacity.
  12. Normative data and interpreting results
    Test scores mean little without something to compare them with.
  13. Laboratory versus field testing
    Fitness can be tested in a controlled laboratory or in the field where sport is played.
  14. Performance and notational analysis
    Performance analysis records and examines what happens in training and competition.
  15. Wearable technology and GPS
    Wearable devices allow coaches to monitor athletes' movements and physiological responses in training and matches.

Fitness Testing and Performance Analysis: questions students ask

What is test–retest reliability?

The consistency of results when the same test is repeated under the same conditions.

What is the PAR-Q+?

A health screening questionnaire used before physical activity or testing.

What is power?

The ability to exert force quickly, combining strength and speed.

What are the units of relative VO2max?

Millilitres of oxygen per kilogram of body mass per minute (ml/kg/min).
