Lessons in this school

  1. Acute and chronic injuries
    Sports injuries are usually classified by how they happen and how quickly they develop.
  2. Soft-tissue injuries
    Most sports injuries affect soft tissues: muscles, tendons and ligaments.
  3. Hard-tissue injuries
    Hard-tissue injuries involve bones and joints.
  4. Concussion
    Concussion is a brain injury that can follow any blow to the head or body.
  5. Risk factors for injury
    Injuries rarely have a single cause.
  6. Injury prevention strategies
    Many sports injuries can be prevented.
  7. Screening and movement assessment
    Many teams screen athletes before a season to identify injury risk.
  8. Load management and injury
    How much athletes train, and how quickly that changes, strongly influences injury risk.
  9. First aid in sport
    Knowing what to do in the first minutes after an injury can save a life.
  10. The stages of tissue healing
    Injured tissues heal in a predictable sequence.
  11. Rehabilitation
    Rehabilitation restores an injured athlete's function and readiness to perform.
  12. Return to play
    Deciding when an athlete can safely return to competition is one of the hardest judgements in sport.
  13. Recovery methods
    Recovery between sessions allows athletes to adapt and perform.
  14. Psychological responses to injury
    Injury affects athletes' minds as well as their bodies.

Key terms

Acute injury
An injury caused suddenly by a single event
Strain
Damage to a muscle or tendon
Stress fracture
A small crack in bone caused by repeated loading
Concussion
A traumatic brain injury causing temporary disturbance of brain function
Intrinsic risk factor
A factor within the athlete that affects injury risk
Sequence of prevention
A four-step model for developing and evaluating injury prevention
Pre-participation screening
Assessment before sport to identify medical and injury risks
Session RPE
Rating of perceived exertion multiplied by session duration, a measure of internal load
Primary survey
A rapid check of danger, response, airway, breathing and circulation
Inflammatory phase
The first stage of healing, with bleeding, swelling and clearance of damaged tissue
Criteria-based progression
Moving through rehab stages when measurable goals are met rather than after set times
Return to performance
Competing at or above the pre-injury level
Active recovery
Light exercise after training to aid recovery
Cognitive appraisal
How an athlete interprets their injury and its meaning
