Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What is an overuse injury?

An injury that develops gradually from repeated stress on tissue without enough recovery. From “Acute and chronic injuries” →

In plain English: Sports injuries are usually classified by how they happen and how quickly they develop. Key idea: Acute injuries result from a single event and appear immediately; chronic overuse injuries build gradually from repeated load without recovery; this shapes first aid, management and prevention.

Why are growth plates vulnerable in young athletes?

They are weaker than the surrounding ligaments, so forces that would sprain an adult can fracture them. From “Hard-tissue injuries” →

In plain English: Hard-tissue injuries involve bones and joints. Key idea: Fractures may be closed or open with various patterns; stress fractures result from repeated load and low energy availability; dislocations displace joints and damage soft tissues; young athletes are vulnerable at growth plates and apophyses.

What is FIFA 11+?

A structured neuromuscular warm-up programme designed to reduce injuries in football. From “Injury prevention strategies” →

In plain English: Many sports injuries can be prevented. Key idea: Prevention follows a sequence: measure the problem, identify causes, introduce measures and evaluate; neuromuscular warm-ups, eccentric strength, protective equipment and rule changes reduce injuries, but only with good compliance.

What does DRABC stand for?

Danger, Response, Airway, Breathing and Circulation. From “First aid in sport” →

In plain English: Knowing what to do in the first minutes after an injury can save a life. Key idea: The primary survey is danger, response, airway, breathing and circulation; cardiac arrest needs immediate CPR and defibrillation; bleeding needs pressure, fractures support, suspected spinal injuries immobilisation and heat stroke rapid cooling.

What are the three stages of the return-to-sport continuum?

Return to participation, return to sport and return to performance. From “Return to play” →

In plain English: Deciding when an athlete can safely return to competition is one of the hardest judgements in sport. Key idea: Return to sport is a continuum from participation to performance; decisions should combine healing, functional tests, psychological readiness and risk tolerance through shared decision-making, resisting pressures for premature return.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Injury Prevention and Rehabilitation

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which is an overuse injury? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    A stress fracture of the shin in a runner. Stress fractures develop from repeated loading over time.

  2. Which tissue is damaged in an ankle sprain? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    A ligament. Sprains are injuries to ligaments, which join bone to bone.

  3. Which fracture type is caused by a twisting force? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Spiral. A twisting force creates a spiral break along the bone.

  4. What is the most common cause of overuse injuries? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Increasing training load faster than tissues can adapt. Overuse injuries reflect a mismatch between load and tissue capacity.

  5. Why did PEACE & LOVE update the RICE approach? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    It emphasises education and early progressive loading rather than prolonged rest. Gradual loading helps tissues remodel and recover function.

  6. Which factor most increases the risk of stress fractures? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Rapid increases in training with low energy availability. Bone stress injuries reflect load exceeding the bone's capacity to adapt.

  7. A tennis player's long-standing Achilles pain ends with a sudden rupture. How is this best described? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    An acute-on-chronic injury, where overuse weakened the tendon before it failed. Chronic degeneration can predispose a tissue to sudden failure.

  8. A player feels a pop in the calf and cannot rise onto tiptoe, but has little pain. What should be suspected? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    A complete rupture, such as of the Achilles tendon, needing urgent assessment. Grade 3 injuries can cause marked loss of function with surprisingly little pain.

  9. A teenage footballer has pain and a bony lump just below the kneecap during a growth spurt. What is likely? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Osgood–Schlatter disease, an apophysitis at the tibial tuberosity. Traction at the patellar tendon attachment causes this condition in growing athletes.

Key takeaways

  • Acute injuries result from a single event and appear immediately; chronic overuse injuries build gradually from repeated load without recovery; this shapes first aid, management and prevention.
  • Strains affect muscles and tendons, sprains affect ligaments and contusions are bruises from blows; injuries are graded 1 to 3; early care now emphasises protection, education and progressive loading (PEACE & LOVE).
  • Fractures may be closed or open with various patterns; stress fractures result from repeated load and low energy availability; dislocations displace joints and damage soft tissues; young athletes are vulnerable at growth plates and apophyses.
  • Concussion is a brain injury from forces to the head or body, often without loss of consciousness; suspected cases must be removed from play ("if in doubt, sit them out"), assessed within 24 hours and follow a graduated return, not before 21 days in grassroots sport.
  • Intrinsic factors (age, sex, previous injury, strength, technique) predispose athletes, extrinsic factors (load, equipment, surface, rules) make them susceptible, and an inciting event triggers injury; modifiable factors are the main targets for prevention.
  • Prevention follows a sequence: measure the problem, identify causes, introduce measures and evaluate; neuromuscular warm-ups, eccentric strength, protective equipment and rule changes reduce injuries, but only with good compliance.

Common mistakes in Injury Prevention and Rehabilitation

  • Ignoring early overuse pain. Mild pain after training can be the first sign of a stress fracture or tendinopathy.
  • Confusing strains and sprains. Strains affect muscles or tendons; sprains affect ligaments.
  • Assuming a child who can move a limb cannot have a fracture. Greenstick and growth-plate fractures can still allow some movement.
  • Thinking concussion requires loss of consciousness. Most concussions occur without it.
  • Blaming an injury solely on the inciting event. Multiple predisposing and environmental factors usually contribute.
  • Assuming a proven programme will work if it is simply announced. Benefits depend on consistent use and coach buy-in.

Injury Prevention and Rehabilitation: questions students ask

What is the difference between a strain and a sprain?

A strain damages a muscle or tendon; a sprain damages a ligament.

What is an open fracture?

A fracture in which the bone breaks through the skin, with a risk of infection.

What is the key message of the UK grassroots concussion guidelines?

If in doubt, sit them out: remove anyone with suspected concussion from play immediately.

What is an inciting event?

The specific event, such as a tackle or landing, that directly causes the injury.
