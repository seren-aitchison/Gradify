Lessons in this school

  1. The ABCDE assessment of the acutely ill patient
    The ABCDE approach is a systematic way to assess and treat an acutely unwell patient, dealing with life-threatening problems in order of urgency.
  2. Shock
    Shock is circulatory failure leading to inadequate oxygen delivery to the tissues.
  3. Anaphylaxis
    Anaphylaxis is a severe, life-threatening systemic hypersensitivity reaction.
  4. Cardiac arrest and advanced life support
    Cardiac arrest needs immediate, coordinated resuscitation.
  5. Poisoning and overdose
    Poisoning is a common reason for hospital attendance, often after intentional self-harm.
  6. Major trauma
    Major trauma is a leading cause of death in younger adults.
  7. Burns
    Burns range from minor scalds to life-threatening injuries with airway compromise and major fluid loss.
  8. The acute abdomen
    The acute abdomen is sudden, severe abdominal pain that may need urgent surgery.
  9. Appendicitis
    Appendicitis is the commonest surgical emergency of the abdomen.
  10. Bowel obstruction
    Bowel obstruction is a common surgical emergency in which intestinal contents cannot pass.
  11. Hernias
    A hernia is the protrusion of a structure through a defect in the wall that normally contains it.
  12. Preoperative assessment
    Preoperative assessment identifies and reduces risk before surgery and helps patients decide whether to proceed.
  13. Anaesthesia and airway management
    Anaesthesia makes surgery possible, and airway management is a core skill in anaesthesia and resuscitation.
  14. Postoperative complications
    Complications after surgery can be general or specific to the operation and tend to occur at characteristic times.
  15. Fluid prescribing
    Intravenous fluids are among the most commonly prescribed treatments, and errors cause real harm.
  16. Urological emergencies and urinary retention
    Several urological conditions need urgent recognition and treatment.
  17. Breast disease
    Breast symptoms are common and most are benign, but breast cancer is the commonest cancer in the UK.
  18. Frailty and comprehensive geriatric assessment
    Frailty is a state of reduced reserve that makes older people vulnerable to sudden decline after minor stresses.
  19. Falls
    Falls are common in older people and cause injury, fear and loss of independence.
  20. Palliative care and symptom control
    Palliative care aims to improve the quality of life of people with life-limiting illness and their families.
  21. Care in the last days of life
    Good care in the last days of life focuses on comfort, dignity and the needs of the person and those close to them.

Key terms

ABCDE
A structured approach: airway, breathing, circulation, disability, exposure
Shock
Circulatory failure causing inadequate tissue oxygen delivery
Anaphylaxis
A severe, life-threatening systemic hypersensitivity reaction
Shockable rhythm
VF or pulseless VT, which may respond to defibrillation
Toxidrome
A cluster of signs characteristic of a particular class of poison
Major trauma centre
A hospital providing specialist, round-the-clock care for severely injured patients
Total body surface area
The percentage of body surface affected by a burn
Peritonitis
Inflammation of the peritoneum causing guarding, rebound tenderness and rigidity
McBurney's point
The point two-thirds of the way from the umbilicus to the anterior superior iliac spine
Strangulation
Compromise of the blood supply to obstructed bowel, risking necrosis
Indirect inguinal hernia
A hernia passing through the deep inguinal ring lateral to the inferior epigastric vessels
ASA grade
The American Society of Anesthesiologists physical status classification
Rapid sequence induction
Induction of anaesthesia and intubation designed to reduce the risk of aspiration
Atelectasis
Collapse of small airways and alveoli, common after anaesthesia
