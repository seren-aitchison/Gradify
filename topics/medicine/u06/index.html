Lessons in this school

  1. Renal physiology and glomerular filtration
    The kidneys filter the blood, regulate fluids and electrolytes, and produce hormones.
  2. Acute kidney injury
    Acute kidney injury (AKI) is a sudden fall in kidney function, common in hospital and often preventable.
  3. Chronic kidney disease
    Chronic kidney disease (CKD) is common, often silent, and increases cardiovascular risk.
  4. Glomerulonephritis and nephrotic syndrome
    Glomerular diseases damage the kidney's filters and present with nephrotic or nephritic patterns.
  5. Urinary tract infection
    Urinary tract infections are among the most common bacterial infections.
  6. Hyperkalaemia and hyponatraemia
    Potassium and sodium disorders are common and can be life-threatening.
  7. Type 1 diabetes
    Type 1 diabetes is an autoimmune condition in which the pancreas stops producing insulin.
  8. Type 2 diabetes
    Type 2 diabetes is the most common form of diabetes and is closely linked with obesity and insulin resistance.
  9. Diabetic ketoacidosis and hyperosmolar hyperglycaemic state
    Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) and the hyperosmolar hyperglycaemic state (HHS) are life-threatening emergencies.
  10. Hypoglycaemia
    Hypoglycaemia is the most common acute complication of diabetes treatment.
  11. Complications of diabetes
    Long-term high glucose damages small and large blood vessels.
  12. The hypothalamus and pituitary
    The hypothalamus and pituitary control much of the endocrine system.
  13. Hypothyroidism
    Hypothyroidism is common, especially in women, and easily treated once recognised.
  14. Hyperthyroidism
    Hyperthyroidism speeds up metabolism and can affect the heart, bones and eyes.
  15. Adrenal insufficiency
    Adrenal insufficiency means the adrenal glands do not make enough cortisol.
  16. Cushing's syndrome
    Cushing's syndrome is caused by prolonged exposure to excess cortisol.
  17. Primary hyperaldosteronism and phaeochromocytoma
    Some cases of hypertension have endocrine causes that can be treated or cured.
  18. Calcium disorders and the parathyroid glands
    Calcium is tightly regulated by parathyroid hormone and vitamin D.
  19. Osteoporosis and metabolic bone disease
    Osteoporosis weakens bones and causes fragility fractures, a major cause of disability in older people.
  20. Lipid disorders
    Raised cholesterol is a major modifiable cause of cardiovascular disease.

Key terms

Glomerular filtration rate
The volume of plasma filtered by the glomeruli per minute
Acute kidney injury
A rapid reduction in kidney function over hours to days
Chronic kidney disease
Abnormal kidney structure or function lasting more than 3 months
Nephrotic syndrome
Heavy proteinuria with hypoalbuminaemia and oedema
Pyelonephritis
Infection of the kidney, causing fever and loin pain
Hyperkalaemia
Raised serum potassium, above 5.5 mmol/L
Basal–bolus regimen
Insulin therapy with long-acting background and rapid-acting mealtime insulin
Insulin resistance
Reduced response of tissues to insulin
Diabetic ketoacidosis
An emergency of ketosis, hyperglycaemia and acidosis due to insulin deficiency
Hypoglycaemia
Blood glucose below 4.0 mmol/L in people with diabetes
Retinopathy
Damage to the blood vessels of the retina
Acromegaly
A condition caused by excess growth hormone in adults
Hashimoto's thyroiditis
Autoimmune destruction of the thyroid gland
Graves' disease
Autoimmune hyperthyroidism caused by TSH receptor-stimulating antibodies
