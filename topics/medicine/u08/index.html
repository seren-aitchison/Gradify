Why do Gram-negative bacteria stain pink?

Their thin peptidoglycan wall does not retain the crystal violet, so they take up the pink counterstain. From “Principles of microbiology” →

What is an HIV indicator condition?

A condition associated with undiagnosed HIV, such as tuberculosis or lymphoma, that should prompt an HIV test. From “HIV infection” →

Within what time does NICE advise analgesia for a sickle cell painful crisis?

Lessons in this school

  1. Principles of microbiology
    Choosing the right investigation and treatment for an infection starts with basic microbiology.
  2. Antimicrobial therapy and stewardship
    Antibiotics save lives, but every prescription selects for resistance.
  3. Sepsis
    Sepsis is life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated response to infection.
  4. Fever in the returning traveller
    Fever after travel may be a routine infection or something life-threatening.
  5. HIV infection
    HIV is now a manageable long-term condition, but late diagnosis remains common.
  6. Healthcare-associated infection and infection control
    Infections acquired in hospital harm patients and are often preventable.
  7. Vaccination and immunisation
    Immunisation prevents disease in individuals and populations.
  8. Anaemia
    Anaemia is a reduced haemoglobin concentration, and its cause must always be sought.
  9. Haemoglobinopathies: sickle cell disease and thalassaemia
    Inherited disorders of haemoglobin are common worldwide and in the UK.
  10. Haemostasis and bleeding disorders
    Normal haemostasis balances bleeding and clotting.
  11. Anticoagulation
    Anticoagulants prevent stroke and treat venous thromboembolism but carry a risk of bleeding.
  12. Blood transfusion
    Blood transfusion can save lives but carries risks, and many transfusions are avoidable.
  13. Leukaemia
    Leukaemias are cancers of blood-forming cells.
  14. Lymphoma
    Lymphomas are cancers of lymphocytes, usually presenting with enlarged lymph nodes.
  15. Myeloma
    Myeloma is a cancer of plasma cells in the bone marrow, often diagnosed late because its symptoms are non-specific.
  16. Myeloproliferative neoplasms
    Myeloproliferative neoplasms are chronic blood cancers in which the marrow makes too many blood cells.
  17. Principles of cancer staging and treatment
    Cancer care depends on accurate diagnosis, staging and a plan agreed by a multidisciplinary team.
  18. Systemic anticancer therapy and its toxicities
    Systemic anticancer therapy includes chemotherapy, targeted therapy and immunotherapy, each with characteristic side effects.
  19. Oncological emergencies
    Cancer and its treatment can cause emergencies that need rapid recognition.
  20. Cancer screening programmes
    Screening aims to detect cancer or pre-cancer before symptoms appear.

Key terms

Gram-positive
Bacteria with a thick cell wall that stain purple
Antimicrobial stewardship
Coordinated actions to promote appropriate antimicrobial use
Sepsis
Life-threatening organ dysfunction due to a dysregulated response to infection
Malaria
A parasitic infection transmitted by Anopheles mosquitoes
CD4 count
The number of CD4 T cells, a marker of immune function in HIV
Healthcare-associated infection
An infection acquired as a result of healthcare
Live attenuated vaccine
A vaccine containing a weakened form of the organism
Mean cell volume
The average size of red blood cells
Vaso-occlusion
Blockage of small vessels by sickled cells
Primary haemostasis
Formation of a platelet plug
CHA2DS2-VASc
A score estimating stroke risk in atrial fibrillation
Restrictive threshold
Transfusing only below a lower haemoglobin level
Blast cell
An immature white blood cell, increased in acute leukaemia
Reed–Sternberg cell
A large abnormal cell characteristic of Hodgkin lymphoma
