Which organelle is defective in Tay–Sachs and Gaucher disease?

The lysosome: each is an inherited deficiency of one lysosomal enzyme, so its substrate accumulates. From “Cell structure and function in health” →

How is plasma osmolality calculated?

2 × Na⁺ + glucose + urea (all in mmol/L). From “Body fluids, osmolality and electrolytes” →

Which cytokine is the main stimulus for CRP synthesis by the liver?

Interleukin-6 (IL-6). From “Acute inflammation” →

State Knudson's two-hit hypothesis.

Both alleles of a tumour suppressor gene must be inactivated for cancer to develop; in hereditary cases one is inherited, so only one further somatic hit is needed. From “The hallmarks and genetics of cancer” →

Which antibody is most specific for rheumatoid arthritis?

Anti-cyclic citrullinated peptide (anti-CCP). From “Hypersensitivity and autoimmunity” →

Lessons in this school

  1. Cell structure and function in health
    Every disease is ultimately a disorder of cells.
  2. Membranes, transport and the resting potential
    Cells keep a different ionic composition from the fluid around them, and that difference powers nerve, muscle and heart.
  3. Homeostasis and negative feedback
    The body holds temperature, glucose, blood pressure and hormone levels within narrow limits by negative feedback.
  4. Body fluids, osmolality and electrolytes
    Where water and salt sit in the body explains dehydration, oedema, hyponatraemia and how intravenous fluids behave.
  5. Acid–base physiology
    Blood pH is held between 7.35 and 7.45 by buffers, the lungs and the kidneys.
  6. Cell injury, adaptation and death
    Cells under stress first adapt, then suffer reversible injury, and finally die.
  7. Acute inflammation
    Acute inflammation is the body's rapid, stereotyped response to infection and injury.
  8. Chronic inflammation and repair
    When injury persists, inflammation becomes chronic and repair runs alongside destruction.
  9. Thrombosis, embolism and infarction
    A thrombus is a clot in a living vessel; an embolus is anything that travels in the blood and lodges; infarction is the tissue death that follows.
  10. Neoplasia: benign and malignant tumours
    A neoplasm is an abnormal mass of tissue whose growth is uncoordinated and persists after the stimulus stops.
  11. The hallmarks and genetics of cancer
    Cancer is a genetic disease of somatic cells, built up mutation by mutation.
  12. Innate immunity
    Innate immunity is the fast, inborn defence that responds within minutes to hours and recognises broad classes of danger.
  13. Adaptive immunity
    Adaptive immunity recognises specific antigens with enormous diversity and remembers them.
  14. Hypersensitivity and autoimmunity
    The immune system can harm the body it protects, by over-reacting to harmless antigens or attacking self.
  15. Patterns of inheritance in clinical genetics
    Many diseases run in families in predictable ways.
  16. Pharmacodynamics: receptors and dose–response
    Pharmacodynamics is what a drug does to the body.
  17. Pharmacokinetics: absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion
    Pharmacokinetics is what the body does to a drug.

Key terms

Lysosomal storage disorder
An inherited deficiency of a lysosomal enzyme, so its substrate accumulates inside cells
Equilibrium potential
The membrane voltage at which there is no net flow of a given ion, calculated by the Nernst equation
Negative feedback
A response that opposes the initial change, returning a variable towards its set point
Osmolality
The concentration of osmotically active particles per kilogram of water
Anion gap
Na⁺ minus the sum of Cl⁻ and HCO₃⁻: an estimate of unmeasured anions in plasma
Metaplasia
Reversible replacement of one differentiated cell type by another in response to chronic stress
Exudate
Protein-rich extravascular fluid produced by increased vascular permeability in inflammation
Granuloma
A focal collection of epithelioid macrophages, often with giant cells and lymphocytes, in chronic inflammation
Embolus
A detached mass (thrombus, fat, air, amniotic fluid, tumour) carried in the blood to lodge in a distant vessel
Metastasis
Spread of a malignant tumour to a site discontinuous with the primary
Oncogene
A mutated or over-expressed proto-oncogene whose gain of function drives cell growth
Pattern recognition receptor
A germline-encoded receptor, such as a Toll-like receptor, that detects conserved microbial or damage molecules
MHC class I
Molecules on nearly all nucleated cells that present intracellular peptides to CD8 T cells
Type I hypersensitivity
An immediate IgE- and mast-cell-mediated reaction to an allergen
