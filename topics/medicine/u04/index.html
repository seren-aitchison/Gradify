What is dead space ventilation?

Ventilation of alveoli that aren't perfused, so no gas exchange occurs. From “Ventilation, gas exchange and oxygen transport” →

Name three life-threatening features of acute asthma.

Any three of: PEF below 33%, SpO2 below 92%, silent chest, cyanosis, poor respiratory effort, arrhythmia, exhaustion, altered consciousness, hypotension. From “Asthma” →

What does CURB-65 stand for?

Confusion, urea over 7 mmol/L, respiratory rate 30 or more, low blood pressure, age 65 or over. From “Community-acquired pneumonia” →

What advice is given about scuba diving after a pneumothorax?

Avoid it permanently unless definitive surgical treatment has been performed. From “Pneumothorax” →

Which PE patients are given systemic thrombolysis?

Those with high-risk (massive) PE causing hypotension or shock, unless contraindicated. From “Pulmonary embolism” →

Lessons in this school

  1. Ventilation, gas exchange and oxygen transport
    The lungs move air in and out, exchange oxygen and carbon dioxide with the blood, and haemoglobin carries oxygen to the tissues.
  2. Lung function tests
    Lung function tests measure how much air the lungs hold and how quickly it can be moved.
  3. Respiratory failure: type 1 and type 2
    Respiratory failure occurs when the lungs fail to oxygenate the blood or remove carbon dioxide adequately.
  4. Asthma
    Asthma is a chronic inflammatory airway disease with variable symptoms and airflow obstruction.
  5. Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD)
    COPD is a common, progressive lung disease with airflow obstruction, mainly caused by smoking.
  6. Bronchiectasis and cystic fibrosis
    Bronchiectasis is permanent dilatation of the airways with chronic infection; cystic fibrosis is an inherited cause.
  7. Community-acquired pneumonia
    Community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) is infection of the lung tissue acquired outside hospital.
  8. Tuberculosis
    Tuberculosis (TB) is a bacterial infection, usually of the lungs, that remains a major global cause of death.
  9. Pleural effusion
    A pleural effusion is fluid in the pleural space.
  10. Pneumothorax
    A pneumothorax is air in the pleural space, causing the lung to collapse.
  11. Interstitial lung disease
    Interstitial lung diseases (ILDs) are a group of conditions causing inflammation and scarring of the lung tissue.
  12. Sarcoidosis
    Sarcoidosis is a multisystem disease of unknown cause characterised by non-caseating granulomas.
  13. Pulmonary embolism
    Pulmonary embolism (PE) ranges from small, low-risk clots to massive PE causing shock.
  14. Pulmonary hypertension
    Pulmonary hypertension is raised pressure in the pulmonary arteries, which strains the right heart.
  15. Lung cancer
    Lung cancer is the leading cause of cancer death in the UK.
  16. Obstructive sleep apnoea
    Obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA) is repeated collapse of the upper airway during sleep, causing disturbed sleep and daytime sleepiness.

Key terms

Ventilation–perfusion matching
The balance between air reaching the alveoli and blood flow through the pulmonary capillaries
FEV1
Forced expiratory volume in one second
Type 1 respiratory failure
PaO2 below 8 kPa with normal or low PaCO2
Anti-inflammatory reliever
An as-needed inhaler containing ICS and formoterol, used to relieve symptoms while treating inflammation
COPD
A chronic lung disease with persistent, usually progressive airflow obstruction, mainly caused by smoking
Bronchiectasis
Permanent, abnormal dilatation of the bronchi with impaired mucus clearance
CURB-65
A pneumonia severity score using confusion, urea, respiratory rate, blood pressure and age 65 or over
Latent TB infection
Infection with M. tuberculosis without symptoms or infectiousness
Exudate
Pleural fluid rich in protein and LDH, caused by inflammation or increased capillary permeability
Primary spontaneous pneumothorax
Pneumothorax without underlying lung disease
Interstitial lung disease
A group of disorders causing inflammation and fibrosis of the lung interstitium
Non-caseating granuloma
A cluster of immune cells without central necrosis, characteristic of sarcoidosis
Massive pulmonary embolism
PE causing hypotension or shock, also called high-risk PE
Pulmonary hypertension
Mean pulmonary arterial pressure above 20 mmHg at rest
