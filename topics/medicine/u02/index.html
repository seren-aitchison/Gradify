What does SOCRATES stand for?

Site, Onset, Character, Radiation, Associated symptoms, Time course, Exacerbating and relieving factors, Severity. From “The medical history” →

What does SPIKES stand for?

Setting, Perception, Invitation, Knowledge, Emotions (empathy), Strategy and summary. From “Breaking bad news” →

Which murmur is heard best with the patient sitting forward in expiration?

Aortic regurgitation (early diastolic, lower left sternal edge). From “The cardiovascular examination” →

What is premature closure?

Accepting a diagnosis before it is verified, so other possibilities aren't considered: the most common cognitive cause of diagnostic error. From “Clinical reasoning and diagnostic error” →

What is the approximate expected PaO₂ on 40% inspired oxygen?

About 30 kPa (roughly FiO₂ % minus 10). From “Arterial blood gas interpretation” →

Lessons in this school

  1. The medical history
    Most diagnoses are made, or strongly suggested, by the history.
  2. Consultation models and patient-centred care
    Consultation models turn communication into teachable, observable skills.
  3. Communicating risk and shared decision-making
    Patients can only decide well if risks are explained in ways they understand.
  4. Breaking bad news
    How bad news is given affects patients for years.
  5. Principles of physical examination
    Examination is a skill of observation, technique and interpretation, built on consent and dignity.
  6. Vital signs and the early warning score (NEWS2)
    Deterioration is usually preceded by hours of abnormal observations.
  7. The cardiovascular examination
    The cardiovascular examination looks for the signs of heart failure, valve disease, endocarditis and vascular disease, from the hands to the heart.
  8. The respiratory examination
    The respiratory examination combines inspection, palpation, percussion and auscultation to locate disease in the airways, lung tissue or pleura.
  9. The abdominal examination
    The abdominal examination detects organ enlargement, masses, fluid, tenderness and the signs of chronic liver disease.
  10. The neurological examination
    The neurological examination localises a lesion, which narrows the causes dramatically.
  11. Clinical reasoning and diagnostic error
    Diagnosis is a reasoning process that can be studied and improved.
  12. Interpreting tests: sensitivity, specificity and predictive values
    No test is perfect, and the same result means different things in different patients.
  13. Interpreting a full blood count and electrolytes
    The full blood count and urea and electrolytes are the most requested tests in hospital.
  14. Arterial blood gas interpretation
    An arterial blood gas tells you about oxygenation, ventilation and acid–base status within minutes.
  15. The 12-lead ECG: a systematic approach
    The ECG is cheap, fast and diagnostic for arrhythmias, ischaemia and electrolyte disturbance.
  16. Chest X-ray interpretation
    The chest X-ray is the most common imaging test in medicine.
  17. Presenting a patient and documenting care
    Clinical information only helps patients if it is communicated clearly.

Key terms

ICE
The patient's ideas about the cause, their concerns and their expectations of the consultation
Safety-netting
Advising the patient which symptoms or changes should prompt them to seek help, where and how quickly
Number needed to treat
The number of patients who must be treated for a stated time to prevent one additional adverse outcome
Warning shot
A brief statement that prepares the patient for bad news before it is given
Chaperone
An impartial observer present during an intimate examination, for the patient's support and both parties' protection
NEWS2
The National Early Warning Score 2, an aggregate score of six physiological parameters plus oxygen use
Apex beat
The lowest and most lateral point at which the cardiac impulse can be felt, normally the 5th intercostal space in the mid-clavicular line
Bronchial breathing
Harsh breath sounds with a gap between inspiration and expiration, heard over consolidated lung
Asterixis
A flapping tremor of the outstretched hands, seen in hepatic encephalopathy, CO₂ retention and uraemia
Upper motor neurone
A neurone whose cell body is in the motor cortex and whose axon runs in the corticospinal tract to the anterior horn
Illness script
A clinician's stored mental model of a disease: who gets it, how it presents and why
Sensitivity
The proportion of people with the condition correctly identified by a positive test
Mean cell volume
The average volume of red cells, used to classify anaemia as microcytic, normocytic or macrocytic
Type 2 respiratory failure
PaO₂ below 8 kPa with PaCO₂ above 6.0 kPa, from inadequate ventilation
