Lessons in this school

  1. Good medical practice and professionalism
    The General Medical Council sets the standards every UK doctor must meet, and medical students are held to the same values.
  2. Medical ethics: principles and frameworks
    Ethical reasoning helps doctors make and justify difficult decisions.
  3. Consent and capacity
    Valid consent and an understanding of capacity underpin every examination and treatment.
  4. Confidentiality and information governance
    Patients must be able to trust doctors with personal information.
  5. Raising concerns and the duty of candour
    Doctors and students have a duty to act when patient safety is at risk and to be open when things go wrong.
  6. Writing a safe prescription
    Prescribing errors are common and can cause serious harm.
  7. Adverse drug reactions and interactions
    Adverse drug reactions are a major cause of hospital admission and harm.
  8. Prescribing in renal and hepatic impairment
    The kidneys and liver clear most drugs, so impairment of either can lead to accumulation and toxicity.
  9. Prescribing in pregnancy, children and older people
    Pregnancy, childhood and older age each change how medicines act and the harms they can cause.
  10. Therapeutic drug monitoring
    For a small number of medicines, measuring blood levels helps keep treatment effective and safe.
  11. Patient safety and human factors
    Most harm in healthcare comes from flawed systems rather than careless individuals.
  12. Clinical governance and audit
    Clinical governance is the framework through which NHS organisations are accountable for improving quality.
  13. Teamwork, handover and leadership
    Safe care depends on how well people work together.
  14. Epidemiology: measures of disease frequency and association
    Epidemiology measures how common disease is and how strongly it is linked to exposures.
  15. Study design and critical appraisal
    Different research questions need different study designs, each with its own strengths and biases.
  16. Evidence-based medicine
    Evidence-based medicine combines the best research evidence with clinical expertise and patient values.
  17. Screening: principles and criteria
    Screening aims to find disease or risk early in people without symptoms, but it can harm as well as help.
  18. Health inequalities and the social determinants of health
    Health differs systematically between social groups, and much of that difference is avoidable.
  19. Health promotion and behaviour change
    Many leading causes of ill health are linked to behaviours such as smoking, inactivity and alcohol.
  20. The NHS and the organisation of healthcare
    Doctors work within a complex health system that shapes what care patients receive.

Key terms

Fitness to practise
Whether a doctor or student has the skills, knowledge, health and character to practise safely
Beneficence
The duty to act in the best interests of the patient
Material risk
A risk a reasonable person in the patient's position, or this particular patient, would think significant
Caldicott guardian
A senior person in an organisation responsible for protecting the confidentiality of patient information
Duty of candour
The duty to be open and honest with patients when something goes wrong with their care
Approved name
The generic name of a medicine, preferred over brand names in prescribing
Type A reaction
A predictable, dose-related adverse reaction caused by the drug's known pharmacology
Creatinine clearance
An estimate of glomerular filtration based on serum creatinine, age, weight and sex
Teratogen
A substance that can cause malformations in a developing embryo or fetus
Narrow therapeutic index
A small difference between effective and toxic concentrations of a drug
Latent condition
A weakness built into a system, such as poor design or staffing, that makes errors more likely
Clinical governance
The framework through which NHS organisations are accountable for continuously improving quality
SBAR
A structured communication tool: Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation
Incidence
The number of new cases in a population at risk over a defined period
