What are the X-ray features of osteoarthritis?

Loss of joint space, osteophytes, subchondral sclerosis and subchondral cysts. From “Osteoarthritis” →

Which antibody is specific for lupus and linked to nephritis?

Anti-double-stranded DNA (anti-dsDNA). From “Systemic lupus erythematosus and connective tissue disease” →

What is the earliest reliable sign of compartment syndrome?

Pain out of proportion to the injury, worse on passive stretch of the muscles in the compartment. From “Fractures and orthopaedic trauma” →

How is scabies treated?

Permethrin cream for the patient and all close contacts at the same time, repeated after 7 days, with washing of bedding and clothes. From “Skin infections and infestations” →

What features define proliferative diabetic retinopathy?

Lessons in this school

  1. Osteoarthritis
    Osteoarthritis is the commonest joint disease, a process of cartilage loss and joint remodelling that causes pain and stiffness.
  2. Rheumatoid arthritis
    Rheumatoid arthritis is a chronic autoimmune inflammatory arthritis that destroys joints if not treated early.
  3. Gout and pseudogout
    Gout and pseudogout are crystal arthropathies that cause sudden, very painful joint inflammation.
  4. Seronegative spondyloarthropathies
    The spondyloarthropathies are inflammatory arthritides linked to HLA-B27 that affect the spine, entheses and joints, usually with negative rheumatoid factor.
  5. Systemic lupus erythematosus and connective tissue disease
    Systemic lupus erythematosus is a multisystem autoimmune disease driven by autoantibodies and immune complexes.
  6. Vasculitis and polymyalgia rheumatica
    Vasculitis is inflammation of blood vessel walls, classified by the size of vessel affected.
  7. Septic arthritis and osteomyelitis
    Bacterial infection of a joint or bone can destroy tissue within days and cause sepsis.
  8. Back pain
    Low back pain is very common and usually non-specific and self-limiting, but a few patients have serious causes that must not be missed.
  9. Fractures and orthopaedic trauma
    Fractures range from minor wrist injuries to life-threatening trauma.
  10. Describing skin lesions
    Accurate description of skin lesions is the basis of dermatological diagnosis and referral.
  11. Eczema and psoriasis
    Eczema and psoriasis are the two commonest chronic inflammatory skin diseases.
  12. Skin cancer
    Skin cancer is the commonest cancer in the UK.
  13. Skin infections and infestations
    Bacterial, viral and fungal skin infections and infestations such as scabies are common in every setting.
  14. Urticaria and drug reactions
    Urticaria and cutaneous drug reactions range from a mild itchy rash to life-threatening emergencies.
  15. The red eye
    A red eye is usually benign, but some causes threaten sight within hours.
  16. Sudden loss of vision
    Sudden loss of vision is an emergency until proven otherwise.
  17. Glaucoma, cataract and diabetic eye disease
    Chronic glaucoma, cataract and diabetic retinopathy are among the commonest causes of visual impairment.
  18. Hearing loss and tinnitus
    Hearing loss is common and often under-recognised, and some causes need urgent action.
  19. Vertigo
    Vertigo is the illusion of movement, usually rotational.
  20. Ear, nose and throat infections
    Sore throat, earache and sinusitis are among the commonest reasons for consultation, and most are viral and self-limiting.
  21. Head and neck cancer
    Head and neck cancers arise mainly in the mouth, pharynx and larynx and are usually squamous cell carcinomas.

Key terms

Osteophyte
A bony outgrowth at the joint margin, characteristic of osteoarthritis
Anti-CCP antibody
An autoantibody highly specific for rheumatoid arthritis
Tophus
A deposit of urate crystals in soft tissue, a sign of chronic gout
Enthesitis
Inflammation at the site where a tendon or ligament inserts into bone
Antinuclear antibody
An autoantibody against nuclear components, sensitive but not specific for lupus
Vasculitis
Inflammation of the blood vessel wall
Septic arthritis
Infection of a joint space, usually bacterial
Sciatica
Radicular pain in the distribution of the sciatic nerve, usually from a lumbar nerve root
Compartment syndrome
Raised pressure in a closed fascial compartment that compromises tissue perfusion
Plaque
A raised, flat-topped lesion broader than it is high
Emollient
A moisturising treatment that restores the skin barrier
Basal cell carcinoma
A slow-growing keratinocyte cancer with a pearly rolled edge, rarely metastasising
Cellulitis
Infection of the deep dermis and subcutaneous tissue
Angioedema
Swelling of the deeper dermis and subcutaneous or submucosal tissue
