Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What are the X-ray features of osteoarthritis?

Loss of joint space, osteophytes, subchondral sclerosis and subchondral cysts. From “Osteoarthritis” →

In plain English: Osteoarthritis is the commonest joint disease, a process of cartilage loss and joint remodelling that causes pain and stiffness. Key idea: Osteoarthritis is diagnosed clinically in people over 45 with activity-related pain and short morning stiffness; exercise and weight loss are the core treatment.

Which antibody is specific for lupus and linked to nephritis?

Anti-double-stranded DNA (anti-dsDNA). From “Systemic lupus erythematosus and connective tissue disease” →

In plain English: Systemic lupus erythematosus is a multisystem autoimmune disease driven by autoantibodies and immune complexes. Key idea: Lupus is a multisystem disease with ANA and anti-dsDNA antibodies; always check urine and blood pressure for nephritis, and use hydroxychloroquine as the backbone of treatment.

What is the earliest reliable sign of compartment syndrome?

Pain out of proportion to the injury, worse on passive stretch of the muscles in the compartment. From “Fractures and orthopaedic trauma” →

In plain English: Fractures range from minor wrist injuries to life-threatening trauma. Key idea: Assess the patient, then the limb's neurovascular status; open fractures and compartment syndrome are surgical emergencies, and hip fractures need surgery within a day or so.

How is scabies treated?

Permethrin cream for the patient and all close contacts at the same time, repeated after 7 days, with washing of bedding and clothes. From “Skin infections and infestations” →

In plain English: Bacterial, viral and fungal skin infections and infestations such as scabies are common in every setting. Key idea: Cellulitis is usually unilateral; bilateral red legs are rarely infection; pain out of proportion suggests necrotising fasciitis, a surgical emergency.

What features define proliferative diabetic retinopathy?

New vessels on the optic disc or elsewhere on the retina. From “Glaucoma, cataract and diabetic eye disease” →

In plain English: Chronic glaucoma, cataract and diabetic retinopathy are among the commonest causes of visual impairment. Key idea: Glaucoma steals peripheral vision silently and is treated by lowering pressure; cataract blurs vision and is cured by surgery; diabetic retinopathy is found by screening and treated before sight is lost.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Musculoskeletal, Skin and the Special Senses

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which finding suggests osteoarthritis rather than rheumatoid arthritis? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Heberden's nodes. Heberden's nodes at the DIP joints are typical of OA; prolonged stiffness and MCP synovitis suggest inflammatory arthritis.

  2. Which joints are typically involved early in rheumatoid arthritis? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    MCP and PIP joints of the hands. RA is a symmetrical small-joint polyarthritis affecting MCP, PIP, wrist and MTP joints.

  3. Which joint is most typically affected in an acute attack of gout? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    The first metatarsophalangeal joint. Podagra, gout of the first MTP joint, is the classic presentation.

  4. A 58-year-old has typical activity-related knee pain and 15 minutes of morning stiffness. What does NICE recommend? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    A clinical diagnosis without imaging. NICE advises diagnosing OA clinically in people aged 45 or over with typical features.

  5. A patient has swollen MCP joints for six weeks but a negative rheumatoid factor. What should happen? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Urgent referral to rheumatology. NICE advises urgent referral for suspected persistent synovitis even with normal blood tests.

  6. Aspirated knee fluid in an 80-year-old shows rhomboid, positively birefringent crystals. What is the diagnosis? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Calcium pyrophosphate deposition (pseudogout). Rhomboid positively birefringent crystals are calcium pyrophosphate.

  7. Which treatment does NICE recommend for knee osteoarthritis? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    A tailored strengthening exercise programme. Exercise is core treatment; NICE advises against arthroscopic lavage, glucosamine and paracetamol alone.

  8. A patient with long-standing RA needs a general anaesthetic. What specific risk should be checked? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Atlantoaxial subluxation. Neck manipulation during intubation can cause cord compression if the atlantoaxial joint is unstable.

  9. A man with three gout attacks this year has a serum urate of 480 micromol/L. What should be offered once the flare settles? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Allopurinol titrated to a urate target, with flare prophylaxis. NICE advises offering treat-to-target ULT with prophylaxis to prevent the flare that starting it can cause.

Key takeaways

  • Osteoarthritis is diagnosed clinically in people over 45 with activity-related pain and short morning stiffness; exercise and weight loss are the core treatment.
  • Rheumatoid arthritis is a symmetrical small-joint polyarthritis; refer suspected synovitis urgently and start DMARDs early, escalating to target.
  • Gout is urate crystal arthritis typically of the first MTP joint; treat attacks promptly and use urate-lowering therapy to a target to prevent recurrence.
  • Inflammatory back pain in a young adult, with enthesitis, uveitis, psoriasis or IBD, suggests a spondyloarthropathy; refer to rheumatology.
  • Lupus is a multisystem disease with ANA and anti-dsDNA antibodies; always check urine and blood pressure for nephritis, and use hydroxychloroquine as the backbone of treatment.
  • Suspected giant cell arteritis is an emergency: start glucocorticoids immediately and refer for fast-track ultrasound or biopsy; visual symptoms need same-day ophthalmology.

Common mistakes in Musculoskeletal, Skin and the Special Senses

  • Ordering an X-ray to confirm typical OA. NICE advises a clinical diagnosis; imaging is for atypical features or diagnostic doubt.
  • Waiting for a positive rheumatoid factor before referral. Seronegative RA is common; refer on clinical suspicion of synovitis.
  • Ruling out gout because the serum urate is normal during an attack. Urate often falls during acute attacks; aspiration and later recheck are more reliable.
  • Labelling inflammatory back pain as mechanical. Diagnostic delay in axial spondyloarthritis averages several years.
  • Diagnosing lupus on a positive ANA alone. ANA is common in healthy people; diagnosis needs compatible clinical features and specific antibodies.
  • Waiting for a biopsy result before starting steroids in suspected GCA. Delay risks permanent blindness.

Lessons in this school

  1. Osteoarthritis
    Osteoarthritis is the commonest joint disease, a process of cartilage loss and joint remodelling that causes pain and stiffness.
  2. Rheumatoid arthritis
    Rheumatoid arthritis is a chronic autoimmune inflammatory arthritis that destroys joints if not treated early.
  3. Gout and pseudogout
    Gout and pseudogout are crystal arthropathies that cause sudden, very painful joint inflammation.
  4. Seronegative spondyloarthropathies
    The spondyloarthropathies are inflammatory arthritides linked to HLA-B27 that affect the spine, entheses and joints, usually with negative rheumatoid factor.
  5. Systemic lupus erythematosus and connective tissue disease
    Systemic lupus erythematosus is a multisystem autoimmune disease driven by autoantibodies and immune complexes.
  6. Vasculitis and polymyalgia rheumatica
    Vasculitis is inflammation of blood vessel walls, classified by the size of vessel affected.
  7. Septic arthritis and osteomyelitis
    Bacterial infection of a joint or bone can destroy tissue within days and cause sepsis.
  8. Back pain
    Low back pain is very common and usually non-specific and self-limiting, but a few patients have serious causes that must not be missed.
  9. Fractures and orthopaedic trauma
    Fractures range from minor wrist injuries to life-threatening trauma.
  10. Describing skin lesions
    Accurate description of skin lesions is the basis of dermatological diagnosis and referral.
  11. Eczema and psoriasis
    Eczema and psoriasis are the two commonest chronic inflammatory skin diseases.
  12. Skin cancer
    Skin cancer is the commonest cancer in the UK.
  13. Skin infections and infestations
    Bacterial, viral and fungal skin infections and infestations such as scabies are common in every setting.
  14. Urticaria and drug reactions
    Urticaria and cutaneous drug reactions range from a mild itchy rash to life-threatening emergencies.
  15. The red eye
    A red eye is usually benign, but some causes threaten sight within hours.
  16. Sudden loss of vision
    Sudden loss of vision is an emergency until proven otherwise.
  17. Glaucoma, cataract and diabetic eye disease
    Chronic glaucoma, cataract and diabetic retinopathy are among the commonest causes of visual impairment.
  18. Hearing loss and tinnitus
    Hearing loss is common and often under-recognised, and some causes need urgent action.
  19. Vertigo
    Vertigo is the illusion of movement, usually rotational.
  20. Ear, nose and throat infections
    Sore throat, earache and sinusitis are among the commonest reasons for consultation, and most are viral and self-limiting.
  21. Head and neck cancer
    Head and neck cancers arise mainly in the mouth, pharynx and larynx and are usually squamous cell carcinomas.

Musculoskeletal, Skin and the Special Senses: questions students ask

Which antibody is most specific for rheumatoid arthritis?

Anti-cyclic citrullinated peptide (anti-CCP) antibody.

What crystals are seen in gout?

Needle-shaped, negatively birefringent monosodium urate crystals.

What are the features of inflammatory back pain?

Onset under 40, gradual onset, morning stiffness, improvement with exercise but not rest, and night pain.

What supports a diagnosis of polymyalgia rheumatica?

Girdle pain and stiffness over 50 with raised inflammatory markers and a rapid response to low-dose steroids.
