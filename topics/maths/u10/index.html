Key terms and formulas at a glance

Order
The highest derivative in the equation: y″ + y = 0 is second order
Newton's law of cooling
dT/dt = −k(T − Ts): the rate of cooling is proportional to the difference from the surroundings
Separable equation
dy/dx = g(x)h(y), rearranged to dy/h(y) = g(x) dx
Integrating factor
μ(x) = e∫P(x) dx, which makes μy′ + μPy = (μy)′
Exact equation
M dx + N dy = 0 with ∂M/∂y = ∂N/∂x (on a simply connected region)
Homogeneous equation (first-order)
y′ = F(y/x), equivalently M and N are homogeneous functions of the same degree
Bernoulli equation
y′ + P(x)y = Q(x)yⁿ, with n a real number other than 0 or 1
Characteristic equation
ar2 + br + c = 0, obtained by trying y = erx
Characteristic polynomial
anrⁿ + … + a₁r + a₀ = 0 for any(n) + … + a₀y = 0
Superposition
Adding solutions to get new solutions; true for linear homogeneous equations, false for nonlinear ones
Equilibrium
A value y* with f(y*) = 0, giving a constant solution
Linear system
x′ = Ax, with x a vector and A a constant matrix

What is the order of y‴ + xy′ = 0?

3. From “Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs)” →

In plain English: An ordinary differential equation relates an unknown function of one variable to its derivatives. Key idea: Classify by order and linearity; verify by substitution; n constants for order n, fixed by conditions; existence and uniqueness need f Lipschitz in y.

What is the period of a small-angle pendulum of length L?

2π√(L/g). From “Nonlinear Differential Equations” →

In plain English: Nonlinear ODEs, where y or its derivatives appear non-linearly, describe real pendulums, populations, chemical reactions and much more. Key idea: Nonlinear ODEs lack superposition, can blow up, and are studied by linearisation, phase planes and numerics; the small-angle pendulum is the classic linearisation.

Are Sturm–Liouville eigenvalues real?

Yes. From “Sturm–Liouville Theory” →

In plain English: Sturm–Liouville problems are second-order eigenvalue problems whose eigenfunctions form orthogonal families: sines and cosines, Legendre polynomials, Bessel functions. Key idea: Write (py′)′ + qy + λwy = 0; eigenvalues are real and increase to infinity; eigenfunctions are orthogonal with weight w and complete.

What does the CFL condition limit?

The time step relative to the grid spacing and wave speed. From “Advection Equations” →

In plain English: The advection equation ut + cux = 0 moves a profile along at constant speed c without changing its shape. Key idea: u = f(x − ct) moves profiles unchanged along characteristics; numerically use upwind schemes and respect |c|Δt/Δx ≤ 1.

What do limiters do?

Prevent spurious oscillations near sharp gradients. From “Finite Volume Methods” →

In plain English: Finite volume methods divide a domain into cells and track the average of a conserved quantity in each, updating it by the fluxes through cell faces. Key idea: Update cell averages by face flux differences; this conserves exactly; good flux choices and limiters capture shocks; CFD relies on it.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Differential Equations

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which equation is linear? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    y″ + x2y′ + y = ex. y and its derivatives appear only to the first power, multiplied by functions of x.

  2. Which method suits y′ = x2y3? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Separation of variables. The right side factorises into a function of x times a function of y.

  3. A logistic population has K = 1,000. At what size is it growing fastest? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    500. rP(1 − P/K) is a parabola in P with its maximum at P = K/2.

  4. Why does a direction field help even without solving the ODE? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    It shows the slope at each point, so solution curves can be sketched. Each arrow has slope f(x, y); solutions follow the arrows.

  5. What does a slope field show for dy/dx = f(x, y)? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    The gradient a solution must have at each point. Each short segment has gradient f(x, y), and solutions follow them.

  6. A tank holds 100 L of water with 5 kg of salt. Brine at 0.2 kg/L flows in at 3 L/min, and the mixture flows out at 3 L/min. Which ODE models the salt S(t)? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    S′ = 0.6 − 3S/100. In: 3 × 0.2 = 0.6 kg/min; out: 3 L/min at concentration S/100.

  7. After substituting v = y1−n, what equation does v satisfy? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    v′ + (1 − n)P v = (1 − n)Q. Multiplying the divided equation by (1 − n) gives this linear form.

  8. Why does y″ + y = cos x need a particular integral of the form x(a cos x + b sin x)? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    cos x and sin x already solve the homogeneous equation, so plain multiples can't be a PI. Forcing at the natural frequency is resonance, so the guess is multiplied by x.

  9. Trace 4 and determinant 3. What kind of equilibrium is it? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    An unstable node. Eigenvalues 1 and 3 (from λ2 − 4λ + 3 = 0) are both positive and real.

Key takeaways

  • Classify by order and linearity; verify by substitution; n constants for order n, fixed by conditions; existence and uniqueness need f Lipschitz in y.
  • Identify the type (separable, linear, exact, homogeneous, Bernoulli) and apply its method; model with rates in minus out; use direction fields for insight.
  • Separate, integrate both sides, solve and apply conditions; check for lost constant solutions; the logistic solution is K/(1 + Ae−rt).
  • Standard form, multiply by e∫P, integrate the product; solution = transient + steady state.
  • Exact means My = Nx; build F from ∫M dx + g(y); solutions are F = C; integrating factors can create exactness.
  • Write y′ = F(y/x), substitute y = vx, separate and integrate; don't confuse with homogeneous linear equations.

Common mistakes in Differential Equations

  • Forgetting that the general solution of an nth-order equation needs n constants.
  • Using the actual temperature T instead of the difference T − Ts in Newton's law.
  • Dividing by h(y) and losing the solution where h(y) = 0.
  • Forgetting to put the equation in standard form (coefficient of y′ equal to 1) before finding μ.
  • Forgetting the "+ g(y)" when integrating M with respect to x.
  • Substituting y = vx but forgetting the product rule term in y′ = v + xv′.

Lessons in this school

  1. Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs)
    An ordinary differential equation relates an unknown function of one variable to its derivatives.
  2. First-Order Differential Equations
    First-order ODEs y′ = f(x, y) model rates of change: cooling, decay, growth, mixing tanks and simple circuits.
  3. Separable Differential Equations
    A separable equation can be rearranged so that all the y terms are on one side and all the x terms on the other, then each side is integrated.
  4. Linear Differential Equations
    A first-order linear ODE y′ + P(x)y = Q(x) can always be solved with an integrating factor, which turns the left side into the derivative of a product.
  5. Exact Differential Equations
    An exact equation M dx + N dy = 0 is the total differential of some function F(x, y), so its solutions are the level curves F = C.
  6. Homogeneous Differential Equations
    A first-order equation is homogeneous (in this sense) if it can be written y′ = F(y/x).
  7. Bernoulli Equations
    A Bernoulli equation y′ + P(x)y = Q(x)yⁿ is nonlinear when n ≠ 0, 1, but the substitution v = y1−n turns it into a linear equation.
  8. Second-Order Differential Equations
    Second-order linear ODEs ay″ + by′ + cy = f(x) describe vibrating springs, swinging pendulums (approximately) and electrical oscillators.
  9. Higher-Order Differential Equations
    Linear ODEs of order three and above work like second-order ones: a characteristic polynomial gives the homogeneous solutions, and particular integrals handle the forcing.
  10. Nonlinear Differential Equations
    Nonlinear ODEs, where y or its derivatives appear non-linearly, describe real pendulums, populations, chemical reactions and much more.
  11. Autonomous Differential Equations
    An autonomous equation y′ = f(y) has no explicit dependence on the independent variable.
  12. Systems of Differential Equations
    Systems of ODEs track several interacting quantities: predators and prey, coupled springs, chemical species.
  13. Initial Value Problems
    An initial value problem gives an ODE together with the value of the solution (and enough derivatives) at a starting point.
  14. Boundary Value Problems
    A boundary value problem specifies conditions at two or more points, such as both ends of a beam or a string.
  15. Laplace Transforms
    The Laplace transform turns a function of time into a function of a complex variable s, converting derivatives into multiplication by s.
  16. Inverse Laplace Transforms
    The inverse Laplace transform recovers a time function from its transform.
  17. Fourier Transform Methods
    The Fourier transform turns derivatives in space into multiplication by frequency, so linear PDEs on infinite domains become ODEs that are easy to solve.
  18. Green's Functions
    A Green's function is the response of a linear system to a point source.
  19. Sturm–Liouville Theory
    Sturm–Liouville problems are second-order eigenvalue problems whose eigenfunctions form orthogonal families: sines and cosines, Legendre polynomials, Bessel functions.
  20. Partial Differential Equations (PDEs)
    Partial differential equations involve functions of several variables and their partial derivatives.
  21. Heat Equation
    The heat equation ut = kuxx describes how temperature spreads through a material, and more generally any diffusion process.
  22. Wave Equation
    The wave equation utt = c2uxx describes vibrating strings, sound, light and water waves.
  23. Laplace Equation
    Laplace's equation ∇2u = 0 describes steady states: equilibrium temperature, electrostatic potential in empty space, ideal fluid flow.
  24. Poisson Equation
    Poisson's equation ∇2u = f adds a source term to Laplace's equation.
  25. Diffusion Equations
    Diffusion equations describe how substances spread from high to low concentration: perfume in a room, oxygen in tissue, pollutants in groundwater.
  26. Reaction-Diffusion Equations
    Reaction–diffusion equations combine local reactions (growth, decay, chemistry) with diffusion.
  27. Transport Equations
    Transport equations describe how a quantity is carried and conserved as it moves: traffic along a road, pollutants in a river, particles in a reactor.
  28. Advection Equations
    The advection equation ut + cux = 0 moves a profile along at constant speed c without changing its shape.
  29. Fluid Dynamics Equations
    The equations of fluid dynamics express conservation of mass, momentum and energy for liquids and gases.
  30. Navier–Stokes Equations
    The Navier–Stokes equations describe the motion of viscous fluids: water, air, blood.
  31. Maxwell's Equations
    Maxwell's four equations unify electricity, magnetism and light.
  32. Schrödinger Equation
    The Schrödinger equation governs how quantum states evolve.
  33. Numerical Solutions of ODEs
    Most differential equations can't be solved exactly, so computers step forward in small increments.
  34. Numerical Solutions of PDEs
    Numerical methods for PDEs replace continuous space and time with grids, meshes or basis functions, turning the equation into a large system of algebraic equations.
  35. Finite Difference Methods
    Finite difference methods approximate derivatives by differences of function values at nearby grid points.
  36. Finite Element Methods
    The finite element method splits a domain into small elements and approximates the solution by simple functions on each.
  37. Finite Volume Methods
    Finite volume methods divide a domain into cells and track the average of a conserved quantity in each, updating it by the fluxes through cell faces.
  38. Spectral Methods
    Spectral methods represent the solution with global smooth functions such as Fourier modes or Chebyshev polynomials.
  39. Dynamical Systems
    Dynamical systems theory studies how states evolve under fixed rules, whether continuous flows from ODEs or discrete maps iterated step by step.
  40. Stability Analysis
    Stability analysis asks whether a system returns to equilibrium after a small disturbance.
  41. Bifurcation Theory
    Bifurcation theory studies how the qualitative behaviour of a system changes as a parameter varies: equilibria appearing, disappearing or swapping stability, and oscillations being born.
  42. Chaos Theory
    Chaos is deterministic behaviour that's unpredictable in practice because tiny differences in starting conditions grow exponentially.
  43. Solitons
    A soliton is a solitary wave that keeps its shape while travelling and survives collisions with other solitons.
  44. Delay Differential Equations
    A delay differential equation makes the rate of change depend on past states as well as the present: populations responding to resources a generation ago, control systems with lag, physiological feedback.
  45. Stochastic Differential Equations
    Stochastic differential equations add random noise to differential equations, modelling share prices, particles jostled by molecules and populations with random environments.
  46. Fractional Differential Equations
    Fractional differential equations use derivatives of non-integer order to model systems with memory: anomalous diffusion in crowded cells, viscoelastic materials and long-tailed relaxation.

Differential Equations: questions students ask

Is y′ = sin y linear?

No: sin y isn't linear in y.

What are the solution curves of an exact equation?

Level curves F(x, y) = C.

Is y′ = (x2 + y2)/(xy) homogeneous?

Yes: it's (x/y) + (y/x), a function of y/x.

Which famous growth model is a Bernoulli equation?

The logistic equation.
