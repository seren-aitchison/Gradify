Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What does APR include?

Interest and compulsory fees, as a yearly rate. From “Financial Literacy” →

In plain English: Financial literacy is the knowledge and confidence to manage money well: budgeting, saving, borrowing, comparing products and avoiding scams. Key idea: Compare savings by AER and loans by APR and total repayable; build an emergency fund; clear expensive debt first; check firms are regulated.

What is the overall discount from 20% off then a further 25% off?

40% (0.8 × 0.75 = 0.6). From “Discounts” →

In plain English: Discounts reduce prices by a percentage, a fixed amount or a deal such as "3 for 2". Key idea: Discounts multiply; compare deals as percentage savings; use unit prices to compare sizes.

What is the Bank of England's inflation target?

2% CPI inflation. From “Inflation Calculations” →

In plain English: Inflation is the general rise in prices over time, measured in the UK mainly by the Consumer Prices Index (CPI). Key idea: Inflation = change in index ÷ old index; real = nominal ÷ price ratio; pay rises below inflation are real cuts.

Roughly how many km is 50 miles?

About 80 km. From “Unit Conversion” →

In plain English: Unit conversion changes a quantity from one unit to another without changing its size: metres to centimetres, square metres to square centimetres, miles to kilometres. Key idea: Square the factor for areas and cube it for volumes; 1 m3 = 1,000 L; 1 inch = 2.54 cm; keep units in the working.

How can you check that a rectangular frame is square?

Measure the diagonals: they should be equal. From “Construction Mathematics” →

In plain English: Construction mathematics turns plans into materials and measurements: setting out square corners, calculating concrete and bricks, working out roof pitches and designing stairs within building regulations. Key idea: Use 3-4-5 and equal diagonals for square corners; tan for pitches; volume plus waste for concrete; check stairs against Part K.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Everyday and Practical Mathematics

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which figure should you compare when choosing between two loans? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    The APR and the total amount repayable. APR includes fees; the total repayable shows the full cost.

  2. Which is a fixed expense for most people? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Rent. Rent stays the same each month; the others vary.

  3. A 100 W light bulb is on for 10 hours. How much energy does it use? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    1 kWh. 100 W is 0.1 kW, and energy = power × time = 0.1 kW × 10 h = 1 kWh.

  4. Someone has a credit card at 24% APR and savings earning 4%. What's usually wise? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Use spare savings (beyond an emergency fund) to clear the card. Debt at 24% costs far more than savings at 4% earn.

  5. Monthly take-home pay is £1,800 and spending is £1,950. What does the budget show? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    A £150 monthly deficit that needs cutting back or more income. Spending of £1,950 exceeds income of £1,800, a monthly shortfall of £150 to close.

  6. Gas use doubles in winter. What happens to the standing charge part of the bill? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    It stays the same. The standing charge is fixed per day, regardless of use.

  7. What is the average income tax rate on £35,000 (tax £4,486)? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    About 12.8%. £4,486 ÷ £35,000 ≈ 0.128: well below the 20% marginal rate.

  8. Shop X offers 40% off. Shop Y offers 25% off then a further 20% off. Which is cheaper? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    They're the same. Successive discounts multiply: 0.75 × 0.8 = 0.6, exactly the same as 40% off.

  9. £1 = $1.20 and $1 = ¥150. What is £1 in yen? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    ¥180. Chain the rates: £1 buys $1.20, and each dollar buys ¥150, so £1 buys 1.20 × 150 = ¥180.

Key takeaways

  • Compare savings by AER and loans by APR and total repayable; build an emergency fund; clear expensive debt first; check firms are regulated.
  • Budget from take-home pay; list fixed and variable costs; 50/30/20 as a guide; convert weekly to monthly with × 52 ÷ 12.
  • Energy bill = kWh × unit rate + days × standing charge; split costs equally, by income or by use; budget for annual bills monthly.
  • Tax each slice of income at its band's rate; personal allowance first; NI is separate; check current rates and Scottish bands.
  • Add VAT by multiplying by 1.2; extract by dividing by 1.2 (VAT is one sixth); know the 5% and 0% rates.
  • Discounts multiply; compare deals as percentage savings; use unit prices to compare sizes.

Common mistakes in Everyday and Practical Mathematics

  • Choosing a loan by the lowest monthly payment without looking at the total cost.
  • Converting weekly to monthly by multiplying by 4, which understates yearly costs.
  • Forgetting the standing charge, which is paid even with no use.
  • Taxing the whole salary at the highest rate it reaches.
  • Finding VAT in an inclusive price by taking 20% of it (that overstates the VAT).
  • Adding successive percentage discounts (30% + 10% = 40%).

Lessons in this school

  1. Financial Literacy
    Financial literacy is the knowledge and confidence to manage money well: budgeting, saving, borrowing, comparing products and avoiding scams.
  2. Personal Budgeting
    A personal budget plans how income is spent and saved, so that money lasts the month and goals are reached.
  3. Household Budgeting
    Household budgeting manages shared costs: housing, energy, council tax, food and bills split between members.
  4. Tax Calculations
    Income tax and National Insurance are taken from earnings using bands: different rates apply to different slices of income.
  5. VAT Calculations
    Value Added Tax (VAT) is a tax on most goods and services in the UK.
  6. Discounts
    Discounts reduce prices by a percentage, a fixed amount or a deal such as "3 for 2".
  7. Profit and Loss
    Profit is what's left from sales after costs.
  8. Currency Conversion
    Currency conversion turns an amount in one currency into another using an exchange rate.
  9. Currency and Exchange Rates
    Exchange rates move with trade, interest rates, inflation and confidence.
  10. Percent Change
    Percentage change measures how much a quantity has grown or shrunk relative to where it started.
  11. Compound Growth
    Compound growth happens when something grows by a fixed percentage each period, so the growth itself grows.
  12. Inflation Calculations
    Inflation is the general rise in prices over time, measured in the UK mainly by the Consumer Prices Index (CPI).
  13. Loan Repayments
    Most loans are repaid in equal monthly instalments that cover both interest and part of the balance.
  14. Mortgage Calculations
    A mortgage is a long-term loan to buy property, secured on the home.
  15. Investment Returns
    Investment returns combine income (dividends or interest) and changes in value.
  16. Measurement
    Measurement assigns numbers to quantities like length, mass, time and temperature using standard units.
  17. Unit Conversion
    Unit conversion changes a quantity from one unit to another without changing its size: metres to centimetres, square metres to square centimetres, miles to kilometres.
  18. Time Calculations
    Time calculations involve the 24-hour clock, durations, timetables, time zones and converting between hours, minutes and decimals.
  19. Speed and Distance
    Everyday speed and distance problems include planning journeys, fuel use, running pace and stopping distances.
  20. Ratios in Recipes
    Recipes are ratios: the proportions of ingredients matter more than the amounts.
  21. Scaling and Proportions
    Proportion problems appear everywhere: map scales, model sizes, best-buy comparisons, staffing and dosages.
  22. Area and Volume Calculations
    Practical area and volume calculations decide how much paint, turf, flooring, concrete or water a job needs.
  23. Construction Mathematics
    Construction mathematics turns plans into materials and measurements: setting out square corners, calculating concrete and bricks, working out roof pitches and designing stairs within building regulations.
  24. Surveying Mathematics
    Surveying measures land: distances, angles, heights and levels, for maps, building sites and roads.
  25. Reading Graphs
    Everyday graphs show energy use, prices, tariffs and conversions.
  26. Reading Tables
    Tables organise information for timetables, nutrition labels, distance charts and tax bands.
  27. Risk and Probability
    Everyday decisions involve risk: insurance, health choices, travel, investments and weather.
  28. Estimation and Mental Maths
    In everyday life, quick estimates check bills, budgets, tips and quantities on the spot.

Everyday and Practical Mathematics: questions students ask

How do you convert a weekly amount to monthly accurately?

Multiply by 52, then divide by 12.

What is a standing charge?

A fixed daily charge regardless of how much energy is used.

Does a pay rise into the higher-rate band reduce take-home pay?

No: only the income above the threshold is taxed at 40%.

What is VAT?

Value Added Tax, a tax on most goods and services in the UK.
