Key terms and formulas at a glance

Axiom
A rule assumed to hold in a structure, from which theorems are proved
Monoid
A set with an associative operation and an identity element
Order
The number of elements of a group; the order of an element g is the smallest k > 0 with gk = e
Homomorphism
A map φ: G → H with φ(ab) = φ(a)φ(b) for all a, b in G
Isomorphism
A bijective homomorphism; its inverse is also a homomorphism
Automorphism group
Aut(G), the group of isomorphisms G → G under composition
Normal subgroup
A subgroup N with gNg−1 = N for every g; exactly the subgroups that give quotient groups
Direct product
G × H, the set of pairs (g, h) with (g, h)(g′, h′) = (gg′, hh′)
Free group
F(S), the group of reduced words in the generators S and their inverses, multiplied by concatenation and cancellation
Zero divisor
A non-zero a with ab = 0 for some non-zero b
Degree of an extension
[K : F], the dimension of K as a vector space over F
R-module
An abelian group M with scalar multiplication by a ring R satisfying the vector-space-style axioms

Five sample flashcards

Name three structures studied in abstract algebra.

For example groups, rings and fields. From “Abstract Algebra” →

In plain English: Abstract algebra studies structures defined by rules (axioms) rather than by particular numbers: groups, rings, fields, modules. Key idea: Abstract algebra studies structures defined by axioms; one theorem covers every example; check axioms one by one and use counterexamples.

How many groups of order 6 are there up to isomorphism?

Two: ℤ₆ and S₃. From “Isomorphisms” →

In plain English: An isomorphism is a bijective homomorphism: it shows two structures are "the same" apart from the names of their elements. Key idea: Isomorphic structures differ only in names; prove isomorphism with an explicit map and non-isomorphism with an invariant; small groups can be classified.

What does Nielsen–Schreier say?

Subgroups of free groups are free. From “Free Groups” →

In plain English: A free group on a set of generators has no relations except those forced by the group axioms. Key idea: Free groups consist of reduced words; every group is a quotient of a free group; presentations ⟨S | R⟩ describe groups by generators and relations.

Is (x2 + 1) maximal in ℝ[x]?

Yes: ℝ[x]/(x2 + 1) ≅ ℂ is a field. From “Commutative Algebra” →

In plain English: Commutative algebra studies commutative rings and their ideals. Key idea: P is prime iff R/P is a domain; M is maximal iff R/M is a field; maximal implies prime; Noetherian rings have finitely generated ideals.

What is the trivial representation?

Every group element acts as the identity map. From “Representation Theory” →

In plain English: Representation theory studies abstract algebraic objects by making them act as linear maps on vector spaces. Key idea: Representations make groups act by matrices; irreducibles are the building blocks; Maschke and Schur govern decomposition; applications span science.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Abstract Algebra

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which set and operation form a group? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    The integers under addition. Addition on ℤ has identity 0 and inverses −a; the others lack inverses or associativity.

  2. Which structure is a field? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    The rationals with + and ×. Every non-zero rational has a multiplicative inverse; the others lack some inverses.

  3. Can a group of order 15 have a subgroup of order 4? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    No: 4 doesn't divide 15. Lagrange's theorem forbids subgroup orders that don't divide the group order.

  4. Why is it useful to prove theorems for groups in general? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Each theorem then applies to every example of a group at once. Abstraction means one argument covers integers, symmetries, matrices and more.

  5. Strings of letters under concatenation form which structure? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    A monoid but not a group. Concatenation is associative with the empty string as identity, but there are no inverses.

  6. A homomorphism from a group of order 12 has image of order 4. What is the order of its kernel? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    3. By the first isomorphism theorem, |ker| = 12/4 = 3.

  7. What is the order of 9 in ℤ₁₂? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    4. The order of k in ℤn is n/gcd(k, n) = 12/3 = 4: the multiples run 9, 6, 3, 0.

  8. Is ℤ₂ × ℤ₄ isomorphic to ℤ₈? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    No: ℤ₂ × ℤ₄ has no element of order 8. ℤm × ℤn ≅ ℤmn only when gcd(m, n) = 1; here the largest element order is lcm(2, 4) = 4.

  9. Which group is cyclic? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    ℤ₉ × ℤ₄. gcd(9, 4) = 1, so ℤ₉ × ℤ₄ ≅ ℤ₃₆; each of the others has a shared factor.

Key takeaways

  • Abstract algebra studies structures defined by axioms; one theorem covers every example; check axioms one by one and use counterexamples.
  • Each structure adds axioms: semigroup (associative), monoid (identity), group (inverses), ring (two operations), field (division); check every axiom.
  • Groups have closure, associativity, identity and inverses; element orders divide |G| (Lagrange); ℤn has one subgroup per divisor; |Sn| = n! and |Dn| = 2n.
  • Homomorphisms preserve operations; kernels are normal subgroups (ideals for rings); injective iff the kernel is trivial; G/ker φ ≅ im φ.
  • Isomorphic structures differ only in names; prove isomorphism with an explicit map and non-isomorphism with an invariant; small groups can be classified.
  • Automorphisms are self-isomorphisms; Aut(ℤn) ≅ (ℤn)× with order φ(n); conjugations are inner automorphisms; outer ones are the rest.

Common mistakes in Abstract Algebra

  • Assuming a property of one example (such as commutativity) holds for every structure of that type.
  • Forgetting to check associativity because it's true for familiar operations.
  • Assuming the converse of Lagrange: a divisor of |G| need not be the order of a subgroup.
  • Forgetting to check the operation is preserved for all pairs, not just a few.
  • Concluding two groups are isomorphic because they have the same order.
  • Thinking every bijection of a group to itself is an automorphism: it must preserve the operation.

Lessons in this school

  1. Abstract Algebra
    Abstract algebra studies structures defined by rules (axioms) rather than by particular numbers: groups, rings, fields, modules.
  2. Algebraic Structures
    An algebraic structure is a set with one or more operations satisfying axioms.
  3. Group Theory
    Group theory studies symmetry.
  4. Homomorphisms
    A homomorphism is a map between algebraic structures that respects their operations.
  5. Isomorphisms
    An isomorphism is a bijective homomorphism: it shows two structures are "the same" apart from the names of their elements.
  6. Automorphisms
    An automorphism is an isomorphism from a structure to itself: a symmetry of the structure.
  7. Quotient Structures
    A quotient structure "divides out" a substructure, treating elements as equal when they differ by something in it.
  8. Direct Products
    The direct product combines two structures into one by working component by component.
  9. Free Groups
    A free group on a set of generators has no relations except those forced by the group axioms.
  10. Ring Theory
    A ring has addition and multiplication, linked by the distributive law.
  11. Field Theory
    Field theory studies fields and their extensions: bigger fields containing smaller ones, like ℚ ⊂ ℚ(√2) ⊂ ℝ.
  12. Modules
    A module is like a vector space but with scalars from a ring instead of a field.
  13. Algebras
    An algebra over a field is a vector space with a compatible multiplication.
  14. Commutative Algebra
    Commutative algebra studies commutative rings and their ideals.
  15. Galois Theory
    Galois theory links field extensions to groups: the symmetries of the roots of a polynomial form its Galois group.
  16. Homological Algebra
    Homological algebra studies sequences of algebraic objects and maps, measuring how far they fail to be exact.
  17. Universal Algebra
    Universal algebra studies algebraic structures in general: sets with operations satisfying identities.
  18. Representation Theory
    Representation theory studies abstract algebraic objects by making them act as linear maps on vector spaces.
  19. Group Representations
    The representations of a finite group are captured by characters: the traces of the representing matrices.
  20. Ring Representations
    A representation of a ring is the same thing as a module: the ring acts on an abelian group by linear maps.
  21. Lie Groups
    A Lie group is a group that's also a smooth space, with smooth multiplication and inversion.
  22. Lie Algebras
    A Lie algebra captures the infinitesimal structure of a Lie group: its tangent space at the identity with the bracket [X, Y] = XY − YX.

Abstract Algebra: questions students ask

Is ℤ₆ a field?

No: 2 has no multiplicative inverse (2 × 3 = 0).

Why is every group of prime order cyclic?

Any non-identity element has order dividing p, so its order is p and it generates the group.
