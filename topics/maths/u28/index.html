What does the concrete–pictorial–abstract approach mean?

Moving from objects to pictures to symbols. From “Primary Mathematics” →

In plain English: Primary mathematics builds number sense, calculation, shape, measure and early data handling from age 5 to 11. Key idea: Primary maths covers number, calculation, fractions, measures, geometry and statistics; fluency plus reasoning; check with the inverse operation.

What fraction of A level Mathematics is pure mathematics?

About two thirds. From “A-Level Mathematics” →

In plain English: A level Mathematics is the main advanced maths qualification for students aged 16 to 18 in England, essential for degrees in mathematics, physics, engineering, economics and computing. Key idea: A level maths is two thirds pure, one third statistics and mechanics; themes are proof, problem solving and modelling; know your board's large data set.

What grades does IB mathematics use?

1 to 7. From “International Baccalaureate Mathematics” →

In plain English: The International Baccalaureate (IB) Diploma offers two mathematics courses, Analysis and Approaches and Applications and Interpretation, each at Standard Level or Higher Level. Key idea: IB offers AA (algebra and calculus) and AI (modelling and statistics) at SL and HL; the exploration rewards a focused question, appropriate maths and reflection.

Translate "the sum of a number and 4 is 3 times the number".

x + 4 = 3x. From “Word Problems” →

In plain English: Word problems describe a situation in words and ask you to turn it into mathematics. Key idea: Read, define variables, translate carefully, solve, answer in context and check against the wording.

What are Pólya's four stages?

Understand, plan, carry out, look back. From “Mathematical Problem Solving” →

In plain English: Problem solving is what you do when you don't immediately know a method. Key idea: Understand, plan, carry out, look back; try small cases, work backwards, use symmetry and invariants; verify and generalise.

Quick quiz: test yourself on School and Examination Mathematics

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which bar-model picture helps most with "3/5 of 40"? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    A bar of 40 split into 5 equal parts, with 3 shaded. Each fifth is 8, so three fifths is 24: the model shows the division and the multiplication.

  2. Which levels does Functional Skills Mathematics offer? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Entry 1 to 3, Level 1 and Level 2. Functional Skills uses Entry Levels then Levels 1 and 2.

  3. Make r the subject of A = πr2. (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    r = √(A/π). Divide both sides by π to get r2 = A/π, then take the positive square root.

  4. A pupil writes 52 − 37 = 25. What's the likely error? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Taking 2 from 7 in the ones column instead of exchanging. 7 − 2 = 5 in the ones and 5 − 3 = 2 in the tens gives 25; the correct answer is 15.

  5. Paint covers 12 m2 per litre and the wall is 50 m2, needing two coats. How many 2.5-litre tins? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    4. 100 m2 needs 100/12 ≈ 8.33 litres, so 8.33/2.5 ≈ 3.33, rounded up to 4 tins.

  6. What is the highest grade available on the GCSE Foundation tier? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    5. Foundation tier targets grades 1 to 5; Higher covers 4 to 9.

  7. To prove there's no largest even number by contradiction, what should you assume? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    There is a largest even number N. Assume the negation, then note N + 2 is a larger even number.

  8. Compare A: 3n and B: n + 3 for an integer n. Which answer is correct? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    It cannot be determined. n = 2 gives 6 > 5 but n = 1 gives 3 < 4, so different integers give different answers.

  9. Three pens and two pencils cost £4.10; one pen and two pencils cost £1.90. What does a pen cost? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    £1.10. Subtract: two pens cost £2.20, so one costs £1.10.

Key takeaways

  • Primary maths covers number, calculation, fractions, measures, geometry and statistics; fluency plus reasoning; check with the inverse operation.
  • Functional Skills tests maths in real contexts at Entry 1 to Level 2; plan multi-step problems, show working, use units and check sense.
  • Foundation maths secures number and algebra before advanced work; GCSE Foundation tier reaches grade 5; rearranging formulae is essential.
  • GCSE is graded 9 to 1 with two tiers and three papers; marks reward method and reasoning; practise with mark schemes.
  • Core Maths applies maths to real decisions; state assumptions, estimate sensibly and communicate clearly.
  • A level maths is two thirds pure, one third statistics and mechanics; themes are proof, problem solving and modelling; know your board's large data set.

Common mistakes in School and Examination Mathematics

  • Subtracting the smaller digit from the larger in each column instead of exchanging.
  • Answering the first calculation (the area) and forgetting the rest of the question (waste and cost).
  • Dividing only part of an expression when rearranging, such as writing t = v − u/a.
  • Writing only the final answer, which loses method marks if it's wrong.
  • Giving a falsely precise answer to an estimation problem built on rough assumptions.
  • Practising only routine questions and neglecting proof and modelling.

Lessons in this school

  1. Primary Mathematics
    Primary mathematics builds number sense, calculation, shape, measure and early data handling from age 5 to 11.
  2. Functional Skills Mathematics
    Functional Skills Mathematics is an English qualification in practical maths for work and life, available at Entry Levels 1 to 3, Level 1 and Level 2.
  3. Foundation Mathematics
    "Foundation mathematics" covers the core skills needed before more advanced study: the GCSE Foundation tier, and foundation-year maths that prepares students for university courses in science, engineering and economics.
  4. GCSE Mathematics
    GCSE Mathematics is the main maths qualification in England for students aged 14 to 16, graded 9 (highest) to 1.
  5. Core Mathematics
    Core Maths is a Level 3 qualification in England for students aged 16 to 18 who passed GCSE maths but aren't taking A level maths.
  6. A-Level Mathematics
    A level Mathematics is the main advanced maths qualification for students aged 16 to 18 in England, essential for degrees in mathematics, physics, engineering, economics and computing.
  7. Pure Mathematics
    Pure mathematics at A level is the core of algebra, functions, calculus, trigonometry and proof that every applied topic relies on.
  8. Mechanics
    Mechanics applies mathematics to motion and forces: how objects move, why they move and what holds them still.
  9. Statistics (A level)
    The statistics in A level Mathematics teaches sampling, data presentation, probability, the binomial and normal distributions and hypothesis testing, using a real large data set.
  10. Further Mathematics
    A level Further Mathematics extends A level Mathematics with complex numbers, matrices, further calculus, proof by induction and optional applied or pure modules.
  11. International Baccalaureate Mathematics
    The International Baccalaureate (IB) Diploma offers two mathematics courses, Analysis and Approaches and Applications and Interpretation, each at Standard Level or Higher Level.
  12. University Entrance Mathematics
    Some universities set extra mathematics admissions tests for competitive courses, such as the MAT (Oxford) and STEP (Cambridge), and the TMUA used by several universities.
  13. SAT Mathematics
    The SAT is a US college admissions test with a Reading and Writing section and a Math section, now taken digitally.
  14. ACT Mathematics
    The ACT is a US college admissions test whose Mathematics section covers pre-algebra through elementary trigonometry, testing speed and accuracy.
  15. GRE Quantitative Reasoning
    The GRE General Test is used for many graduate programmes, and its Quantitative Reasoning measure tests arithmetic, algebra, geometry and data analysis at roughly high school level, with an emphasis on reasoning.
  16. Word Problems
    Word problems describe a situation in words and ask you to turn it into mathematics.
  17. Graph Interpretation
    Graph interpretation means reading information from charts and graphs, describing trends and drawing conclusions, while spotting misleading presentations.
  18. Percentages and Interest
    Percentage questions appear in every maths exam: percentage of an amount, increases and decreases, reverse percentages, and simple and compound interest.
  19. Speed, Distance and Time
    Speed, distance and time problems link three quantities with one formula.
  20. Probability Problems
    Exam probability problems test listing outcomes, tree diagrams, Venn diagrams, two-way tables and conditional probability.
  21. Mathematical Problem Solving
    Problem solving is what you do when you don't immediately know a method.
  22. Exam Techniques
    Exam technique turns knowledge into marks: planning time, reading questions precisely, showing working and checking.
  23. Mental Arithmetic
    Mental arithmetic is calculating in your head using number facts and flexible strategies.
  24. Calculator Skills
    A calculator is a powerful tool only if you use it well: entering expressions correctly, using memory and special functions, and recognising when an answer is wrong.
  25. Estimation and Checking
    Estimation gives a quick approximate answer by rounding, and checking confirms an answer is right.
