Lessons in this school

  1. Mathematical Optimisation
    Optimisation is choosing the best option from a set of possibilities: the cheapest route, the strongest design or the most accurate model.
  2. Unconstrained Optimisation
    Unconstrained optimisation finds minima of a function over all of ℝⁿ.
  3. Constrained Optimisation
    Most real problems have constraints: budgets, capacities, physical laws.
  4. Lagrange Multipliers
    Lagrange multipliers solve optimisation problems with equality constraints.
  5. Karush–Kuhn–Tucker Conditions
    The KKT conditions extend Lagrange multipliers to inequality constraints.
  6. Convex Optimisation
    In convex optimisation the objective is convex and the feasible set is convex.
  7. Nonconvex Optimisation
    Nonconvex problems can have many local minima, saddle points and flat regions, so finding the global optimum is generally hard.
  8. Nonlinear Programming
    Nonlinear programming (NLP) covers problems where the objective or constraints are nonlinear functions.
  9. Quadratic Programming
    Quadratic programming minimises a quadratic objective subject to linear constraints.
  10. Multiobjective Optimisation
    Many decisions involve several conflicting goals, such as cost against quality or profit against emissions.
  11. Stochastic Optimisation
    Stochastic optimisation handles problems where data are random or the objective is only observed with noise.
  12. Robust Optimisation
    Robust optimisation protects against the worst case within a set of possible data values, rather than averaging.
  13. Optimal Control
    Optimal control chooses inputs to a dynamical system over time to minimise a cost, such as steering a rocket with least fuel.
  14. Linear Programming
    Linear programming optimises a linear objective subject to linear inequalities.
  15. Simplex Algorithm
    The simplex algorithm, invented by George Dantzig in 1947, solves linear programs by moving from vertex to neighbouring vertex, improving the objective each step.
  16. Interior-Point Methods
    Interior-point methods solve linear and convex programs by travelling through the interior of the feasible region rather than along its edges.
  17. Integer Programming
    Integer programming requires some or all variables to take whole-number values.
  18. Mixed-Integer Programming
    Mixed-integer programs combine continuous and integer variables, modelling problems such as unit commitment in power systems, where plants are on or off but output levels vary continuously.
  19. Branch and Bound
    Branch and bound solves integer and combinatorial problems by splitting them into smaller subproblems and discarding any whose bound shows they can't beat the best solution found so far.
  20. Combinatorial Optimisation
    Combinatorial optimisation seeks the best object from a finite but huge set, such as a tour, schedule, matching or tree.
  21. Dynamic Programming
    Dynamic programming solves problems by breaking them into overlapping subproblems and reusing their solutions.
  22. Gradient Descent
    Gradient descent minimises a function by repeatedly stepping in the direction of steepest decrease, the negative gradient.
  23. Newton's Method
    Newton's method for optimisation uses both gradient and curvature, stepping to the minimum of a local quadratic model.
  24. Quasi-Newton Methods
    Quasi-Newton methods build an approximation to the Hessian from the gradients seen so far, getting fast convergence without computing second derivatives.
  25. Heuristic Optimisation
    Heuristics are practical rules that find good solutions quickly without guaranteeing optimality.
  26. Metaheuristic Algorithms
    Metaheuristics are general strategies that guide heuristics to explore beyond local optima.
  27. Genetic Algorithms
    Genetic algorithms evolve a population of candidate solutions using selection, crossover and mutation, inspired by natural evolution.
  28. Simulated Annealing
    Simulated annealing explores solutions like a cooling metal: it accepts worse moves with a probability that falls as the "temperature" decreases.
  29. Particle Swarm Optimisation
    Particle swarm optimisation moves a swarm of candidate points through the search space, each pulled towards its own best position and the swarm's best.
  30. Bayesian Optimisation
    Bayesian optimisation finds the best settings of expensive black-box functions, such as machine learning hyperparameters or experiments, using few evaluations.
  31. Operations Research
    Operations research (OR) applies mathematical models and analysis to decisions in organisations: how many staff to roster, where to put depots, how to route deliveries.
  32. Decision Analysis
    Decision analysis structures choices under uncertainty using decision trees, probabilities and utilities.
  33. Queueing Theory
    Queueing theory models waiting lines at checkouts, call centres, hospitals and computer servers.
  34. Inventory Theory
    Inventory theory decides how much stock to order and when, balancing ordering costs against holding costs and the risk of running out.
  35. Scheduling Theory
    Scheduling theory decides when and where to process jobs on machines, staff or processors to meet deadlines or minimise completion times.
  36. Supply Chain Optimisation
    Supply chain optimisation coordinates suppliers, factories, warehouses and retailers to deliver products at low cost and high service.
  37. Network Optimisation
    Network optimisation solves problems on graphs: shortest paths, maximum flows and minimum-cost flows.
  38. Facility Location
    Facility location chooses where to open warehouses, hospitals, fire stations or cell towers to serve demand at least cost or best coverage.
  39. Transportation Problems
    The transportation problem ships goods from supply points to demand points at least total cost.
  40. Assignment Problems
    The assignment problem matches n workers to n tasks, one each, at least total cost.
  41. Resource Allocation
    Resource allocation distributes limited budget, staff, equipment or time among competing activities to achieve the most benefit.
  42. Markov Decision Processes
    A Markov decision process models sequential decisions under uncertainty: in each state you choose an action, receive a reward and move randomly to a new state.
  43. Reinforcement Learning Mathematics
    Reinforcement learning learns good policies from experience when the MDP's transitions and rewards are unknown.

Key terms

Objective function
The quantity f(x) to be minimised or maximised
Stationary point
A point where the gradient ∇f(x) = 0
Active constraint
An inequality constraint holding with equality at a point, gᵢ(x) = 0
Lagrangian
L(x, λ) = f(x) + λh(x) for a constraint h(x) = 0
Complementary slackness
μᵢgᵢ(x*) = 0: each inequality multiplier is zero unless its constraint is active
Convex function
f(θx + (1 − θ)y) ≤ θf(x) + (1 − θ)f(y) for all x, y and 0 ≤ θ ≤ 1
Local minimum
A point better than all nearby points but not necessarily the best overall
Sequential quadratic programming
SQP: solving a sequence of quadratic programs that approximate the NLP near the current point
Quadratic program
Minimise ½xᵀQx + cᵀx subject to Ax ≤ b (and possibly Ex = d)
Pareto optimal
A solution where no objective can be improved without worsening at least one other
Two-stage stochastic program
Choose first-stage decisions now, then recourse decisions after uncertainty is revealed, minimising expected total cost
Uncertainty set
The set U of data values the solution must withstand, such as a box or ellipsoid
Pontryagin's maximum principle
Necessary conditions: the optimal control optimises a Hamiltonian H(x, u, p) at each time, with costate p
Linear program
Minimise or maximise cᵀx subject to Ax ≤ b and x ≥ 0 (or other linear constraints)
