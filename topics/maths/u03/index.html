Lessons in this school

  1. Basic Algebra
    Algebra uses letters to stand for numbers, so that we can describe patterns, rules and relationships that hold for many values at once.
  2. Elementary Algebra
    Elementary algebra is the toolkit of school algebra: the rules for manipulating expressions and equations, built on the properties of numbers.
  3. Algebraic Expressions
    An algebraic expression is built from terms joined by + and −.
  4. Simplifying Expressions
    Simplifying an expression means writing it in an equivalent, shorter form by collecting like terms and using index laws.
  5. Expanding Brackets
    Expanding brackets means multiplying out so that an expression is written as a sum of terms.
  6. Factorisation
    Factorising writes an expression as a product of factors.
  7. Algebraic Manipulation
    Algebraic manipulation is rearranging, substituting and transforming expressions and formulae while keeping them equal.
  8. Algebraic Fractions
    Algebraic fractions have expressions on the top or bottom.
  9. Linear Equations
    A linear equation has the unknown only to the first power, like 3x + 5 = 20.
  10. Simultaneous Equations
    Simultaneous equations are two or more equations that must be true at the same time.
  11. Quadratic Equations
    A quadratic equation has the unknown squared, like x2 − 5x + 6 = 0.
  12. Completing the Square
    Completing the square rewrites x2 + bx + c as (x + p)2 + q.
  13. The Quadratic Formula
    The quadratic formula gives the solutions of any quadratic equation directly from its coefficients.
  14. Cubic Equations
    A cubic equation has the unknown cubed, like x3 − 6x2 + 11x − 6 = 0.
  15. Polynomial Equations
    Polynomial equations of any degree arise in modelling and science.
  16. Inequalities
    An inequality compares two quantities using <, >, ≤ or ≥.
  17. Simultaneous Inequalities
    Simultaneous inequalities are several conditions that must all hold at once.
  18. Absolute Value Equations
    The absolute value (modulus) |x| is the distance of x from zero, so it's never negative.
  19. Rational Equations
    A rational equation contains algebraic fractions, like 2/x + 3/(x + 1) = 1.
  20. Surds
    A surd is a root that can't be simplified to a rational number, such as √2 or √12.
  21. Logarithms
    A logarithm answers the question "what power?": log2 8 = 3 because 23 = 8.
  22. Exponential Equations
    In an exponential equation the unknown is in a power, like 3x = 20.
  23. Logarithmic Equations
    Logarithmic equations have the unknown inside a logarithm.
  24. Polynomials
    A polynomial is a sum of terms each made of a number times a whole-number power of the variable, like 3x4 − 2x + 7.
  25. Polynomial Algebra
    Polynomial algebra is adding, subtracting and multiplying polynomials, and comparing their coefficients.
  26. Polynomial Division
    Polynomials can be divided like numbers, giving a quotient and a remainder.
  27. Remainder Theorem
    The remainder theorem gives the remainder of dividing a polynomial by (x − a) without doing the division: it's simply f(a).
  28. Factor Theorem
    The factor theorem is the remainder theorem with remainder zero: (x − a) is a factor of f(x) exactly when f(a) = 0.
  29. Roots of Polynomials
    The roots of a polynomial are the values that make it zero.
  30. Vieta's Formulas
    Vieta's formulas link the roots of a polynomial to its coefficients: for example, the roots of x2 + bx + c add to −b and multiply to c.
  31. Symmetric Polynomials
    A symmetric polynomial is unchanged when its variables are swapped, like x + y or x2 + y2.
  32. Partial Fractions
    Partial fractions split a complicated algebraic fraction into a sum of simpler ones.
  33. Functions
    A function is a rule that takes each input and gives exactly one output.
  34. Domain and Range
    The domain of a function is the set of inputs it accepts; the range is the set of outputs it actually produces.
  35. Composite Functions
    A composite function applies one function and then another: fg(x) means "do g, then f".
  36. Inverse Functions
    An inverse function undoes a function: if f takes 3 to 11, then f−1 takes 11 back to 3.
  37. Transformations of Functions
    Transformations move, stretch and reflect graphs.
  38. Piecewise Functions
    A piecewise function uses different rules on different parts of its domain, like a tax system with bands or a phone tariff.
  39. Parametric Equations
    Parametric equations describe a curve using a third variable, the parameter, for both coordinates: x = t2, y = 2t.
  40. Implicit Equations
    An implicit equation links x and y without making y the subject, like x2 + y2 = 25 or x3 + y3 = 6xy.
  41. Recurrence Relations
    A recurrence relation defines each term of a sequence from earlier terms, like un+1 = 2un + 3.
  42. Intermediate Algebra
    Intermediate algebra bridges GCSE and A level: confident manipulation of quadratics, functions, inequalities, surds and indices together, in multi-step problems.
  43. Advanced Algebra
    Advanced algebra extends school methods with proof by induction, the binomial theorem for any power, series and complex roots.
  44. Complex Numbers
    Complex numbers extend the reals with i, where i2 = −1.
  45. Boolean Algebra
    Boolean algebra is the algebra of true and false (1 and 0), with operations AND, OR and NOT.

Key terms

Variable
A letter standing for a number that can change, such as x in y = 2x + 1
Identity
A statement true for every value of the variable, such as 2(x + 3) ≡ 2x + 6
Term
A part of an expression separated by + or −, such as 5x2, −3x and 7 in 5x2 − 3x + 7
Collecting like terms
Adding or subtracting the coefficients of like terms: 5x + 3x − x = 7x
Expand
Multiply out brackets to remove them
Factorise
Write an expression as a product of its factors
Subject of a formula
The variable on its own on one side, such as v in v = u + at
Algebraic fraction
A fraction whose numerator or denominator is an algebraic expression, such as (x + 2)/(x2 − 4)
Linear equation
An equation in which the unknown appears only to the power 1
Elimination
Adding or subtracting equations to remove one unknown
Quadratic equation
An equation of the form ax2 + bx + c = 0 with a ≠ 0
Completed-square form
A quadratic written as a(x + p)2 + q
Quadratic formula
x = (−b ± √(b2 − 4ac))/(2a) for ax2 + bx + c = 0
Cubic
A polynomial of degree 3: ax3 + bx2 + cx + d with a ≠ 0
