Lessons in this school

  1. Applied Mathematics
    Applied mathematics develops and uses mathematical methods to understand real-world problems in science, engineering, medicine and society.
  2. Mathematical Modelling
    Mathematical modelling turns a real situation into mathematics, solves or simulates it, and checks the results against reality.
  3. Dimensional Analysis
    Dimensional analysis uses the units of physical quantities to constrain how they can be related.
  4. Similarity Analysis
    Similarity analysis finds solutions whose shape stays the same as they spread or shrink, needing only a rescaled variable.
  5. Scaling Laws
    Scaling laws describe how one quantity changes as a power of another, such as metabolic rate rising as body mass to the ¾.
  6. Asymptotic Analysis
    Asymptotic analysis studies how functions and solutions behave when a parameter becomes very small or very large.
  7. Perturbation Theory
    Perturbation theory solves problems with a small parameter ε by expanding the solution as a power series in ε.
  8. Sensitivity and Uncertainty Analysis
    In modelling practice, sensitivity and uncertainty analysis ask which assumptions drive the conclusions and how confident we can be.
  9. Biological Modelling
    Biological modelling builds mathematical descriptions of living systems, from molecules and cells to organs, populations and ecosystems.
  10. Mathematical Biology
    Mathematical biology is the discipline that uses mathematics to discover biological principles.
  11. Biomathematics
    Biomathematics covers the quantitative tools biologists use every day: genetics, growth and decay, allometry, phylogenetics and biostatistics.
  12. Population Dynamics
    Population dynamics models how the number of individuals changes through births, deaths and migration.
  13. Ecological Modelling
    Ecological modelling describes interactions among species and their environment: predation, competition, mutualism and the flow of energy and nutrients.
  14. Epidemiological Modelling
    Epidemiological models forecast how infectious diseases spread and how interventions change their course.
  15. Mathematical Epidemiology
    Mathematical epidemiology studies the theory behind epidemic models: threshold theorems, final epidemic sizes, endemic equilibria and the next-generation matrix.
  16. Compartmental Models
    Compartmental models divide a population into groups, such as susceptible, infectious and recovered, and track flows between them.
  17. Compartmental Modelling
    Beyond epidemics, compartmental modelling describes any system where material moves between well-mixed pools: drugs in the body, tracers in organs, carbon in the climate system.
  18. Pharmacokinetic Modelling
    Pharmacokinetics (PK) models what the body does to a drug: absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion.
  19. Pharmacodynamic Modelling
    Pharmacodynamics (PD) models what a drug does to the body: how effect depends on concentration.
  20. Systems Biology
    Systems biology studies how networks of genes, proteins and metabolites work together to produce cell behaviour.
  21. Chemical Modelling
    Chemical modelling uses mathematics to describe molecules, reactions and reactors.
  22. Reaction Kinetics
    Reaction kinetics measures how fast reactions go and how rates depend on concentrations.
  23. Chemical Kinetics
    Chemical kinetics explains rates through mechanisms, sequences of elementary steps, and through temperature via the Arrhenius equation.
  24. Reaction Network Theory
    Reaction network theory studies what the structure of a network of chemical reactions implies about its dynamics, regardless of rate constant values.
  25. Reaction-Diffusion Systems
    Reaction–diffusion systems combine local chemical reactions with spatial diffusion.
  26. Physical Modelling
    Physical modelling describes the physical world with mathematics, starting from conservation laws and constitutive relations.
  27. Mathematical Physics
    Mathematical physics develops the mathematics needed for physical theories and proves results about them rigorously.
  28. Mathematical Physics Equations
    A handful of partial differential equations, the wave, heat, Laplace and Schrödinger equations, describe vast areas of physics.
  29. Classical Mechanics
    Classical mechanics describes how bodies move under forces, from falling apples to planetary orbits.
  30. Continuum Mechanics
    Continuum mechanics treats materials as continuous, ignoring atoms, and describes how they deform and flow under forces.
  31. Solid Mechanics
    Solid mechanics predicts how structures and components deform, carry loads and fail.
  32. Elasticity Theory
    Elasticity theory describes materials that return to their original shape when loads are removed.
  33. Plasticity Theory
    Plasticity theory describes permanent deformation once a material yields.
  34. Fluid Mechanics
    Fluid mechanics studies liquids and gases at rest and in motion.
  35. Fluid Dynamics
    Fluid dynamics describes moving fluids with the Navier–Stokes equations.
  36. Aerodynamics
    Aerodynamics studies how air flows around moving bodies and the forces it produces: lift and drag.
  37. Wave Mechanics
    Waves carry energy and information without carrying matter: ripples, sound, light and quantum wavefunctions.
  38. Acoustics
    Acoustics is the science of sound: pressure waves in air, water and solids.
  39. Thermodynamics
    Thermodynamics describes energy, heat and work in systems of many particles, using a few universal laws.
  40. Heat Transfer
    Heat transfer studies how thermal energy moves by conduction, convection and radiation.
  41. Mass Transfer
    Mass transfer describes how chemical species move from regions of high to low concentration, by molecular diffusion and by flow.
  42. Transport Phenomena
    Transport phenomena unifies the transport of momentum, heat and mass, which obey closely analogous laws.
  43. Diffusion Processes
    Diffusion arises from random motion: many particles each taking tiny random steps spread out according to the diffusion equation.
  44. Electromagnetism
    Electromagnetism describes electric and magnetic fields and their interactions with charges and currents.
  45. Quantum Mechanics
    Quantum mechanics describes nature at atomic scales, where energy is quantised and particles behave as waves.
  46. Statistical Mechanics
    Statistical mechanics derives the thermodynamic behaviour of matter from the statistics of its many microscopic constituents.
  47. Nonlinear Dynamics
    Nonlinear dynamics studies systems whose behaviour isn't proportional to their inputs.
  48. Chaos and Complex Systems
    Chaotic systems are deterministic yet unpredictable in the long run, because tiny differences in starting conditions grow exponentially.
  49. Agent-Based Modelling
    Agent-based models simulate many individual agents, such as people, animals, cells or firms, each following simple rules and interacting locally.
  50. Cellular Automata Modelling
    Cellular automata are grids of cells that update their states simultaneously by local rules.
  51. Systems Theory
    Systems theory studies how parts interact to form wholes with their own behaviour, using ideas such as feedback, stocks and flows, and hierarchy.

Key terms

Applied mathematics
Mathematics developed or used to model, analyse and solve problems arising outside mathematics
Modelling cycle
Formulate, solve, interpret and validate, then refine the model and repeat
Dimension
The physical nature of a quantity expressed in base dimensions such as mass M, length L and time T
Similarity solution
A solution of the form u(x, t) = tα f(x/tβ), whose profile keeps its shape under rescaling
Power law
A relation y = Cxᵅ, a straight line of slope α on a log–log plot
Asymptotic equivalence
f ~ g as x → x₀ when f/g → 1
Regular perturbation
A problem whose solution expands as a power series in ε with the ε = 0 problem giving the leading term
Structural uncertainty
Uncertainty about the form of the model itself, such as which processes to include
Multiscale model
A model linking processes at different biological scales, such as genes, cells and tissues
Turing instability
A stable uniform state becomes unstable when two chemicals diffuse at different rates, producing spatial patterns
Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium
With allele frequencies p and q = 1 − p, genotype frequencies p2, 2pq and q2 stay constant without selection, mutation, migration or drift
Carrying capacity
K, the population size an environment can sustain in the long run
Competitive exclusion principle
Two species competing for exactly the same limiting resource can't coexist indefinitely
Basic reproduction number
R₀, the average number of secondary infections from one case in a fully susceptible population
