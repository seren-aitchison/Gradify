Lessons in this school

  1. Multivariable Calculus
    Multivariable calculus extends derivatives and integrals to functions of several variables, like temperature across a room or profit depending on price and advertising.
  2. Directional Derivatives
    A directional derivative measures how fast a function changes as you move in any chosen direction, not just along the axes.
  3. Gradient
    The gradient ∇f collects all the partial derivatives of a function into a vector.
  4. Multiple Integrals
    Multiple integrals add up quantities over areas and volumes: mass from density, probability from a joint density, average temperature over a region.
  5. Double Integrals
    Double integrals over general regions need careful limits, and some become easy only after swapping the order of integration or switching to polar coordinates.
  6. Triple Integrals
    Triple integrals add up quantities over solid regions: volume, mass and centre of mass.
  7. Jacobians
    The Jacobian matrix collects all first partial derivatives of a transformation, and its determinant measures how the transformation stretches area or volume.
  8. Change of Variables
    Changing variables in a multiple integral replaces awkward regions or integrands with simple ones, at the cost of a Jacobian factor.
  9. Vector Calculus
    Vector calculus studies vector fields, such as wind velocity, electric fields and fluid flow, using three differential operators (gradient, divergence and curl) and integral theorems that link them.
  10. Divergence
    The divergence of a vector field measures how much it spreads out from a point: positive at sources, negative at sinks, zero for an incompressible flow.
  11. Curl
    The curl of a vector field measures its local rotation: the axis and strength of the swirl a tiny paddle wheel would feel.
  12. Laplacian Operator
    The Laplacian ∇2f is the divergence of the gradient: it compares a function's value at a point with its average nearby.
  13. Line Integrals
    A line integral adds up a quantity along a curve: the mass of a wire, or the work done by a force along a path.
  14. Surface Integrals
    Surface integrals add up quantities over curved surfaces: the area of a dome, the mass of a shell, or the flux of a fluid or field through a membrane.
  15. Volume Integrals
    Volume integrals add a scalar or vector quantity over a three-dimensional region: total mass, charge, energy or the volume itself.
  16. Green's Theorem
    Green's theorem links a line integral around a closed plane curve with a double integral over the region inside.
  17. Stokes' Theorem
    Stokes' theorem says the circulation of a field around a closed curve equals the flux of its curl through any surface bounded by that curve.
  18. Divergence Theorem
    The divergence theorem says that the total outward flux through a closed surface equals the integral of the divergence over the volume inside.
  19. Matrix Calculus
    Matrix calculus differentiates functions of vectors and matrices with respect to those vectors and matrices.
  20. Tensor Calculus
    Tensor calculus extends vector calculus to curved spaces and to quantities with several indices, like stress or curvature.
  21. Calculus of Variations
    The calculus of variations finds functions that make an integral as small or large as possible: the shortest path, the fastest slide, the shape of a hanging chain.
  22. Fractional Calculus
    Fractional calculus defines derivatives and integrals of non-integer order, such as a half-derivative.

Key terms

Partial derivative
∂f/∂x: the rate of change of f as x varies with the other variables held fixed
Directional derivative
Duf = ∇f · u, the rate of change of f in the direction of the unit vector u
Gradient
∇f = (∂f/∂x, ∂f/∂y, ∂f/∂z)
Double integral
∬R f dA, the limit of Riemann sums over small rectangles in R
Type I region
A region a ≤ x ≤ b, g₁(x) ≤ y ≤ g₂(x), integrated dy then dx
Cylindrical coordinates
(r, θ, z) with dV = r dr dθ dz
Jacobian matrix
For (x, y) = T(u, v), the matrix [[∂x/∂u, ∂x/∂v], [∂y/∂u, ∂y/∂v]]
Change of variables formula
∬R f(x, y) dx dy = ∬S f(x(u, v), y(u, v)) |∂(x, y)/∂(u, v)| du dv
Vector field
A function assigning a vector to each point, such as F(x, y, z) = (P, Q, R)
Divergence
∇ · F = ∂P/∂x + ∂Q/∂y + ∂R/∂z for F = (P, Q, R)
Curl
∇ × F = (∂R/∂y − ∂Q/∂z, ∂P/∂z − ∂R/∂x, ∂Q/∂x − ∂P/∂y)
Laplacian
∇2f = ∂2f/∂x2 + ∂2f/∂y2 + ∂2f/∂z2
Line integral of a vector field
∫C F · dr = ∫ₐb F(r(t)) · r′(t) dt, the work done by F along C
Surface element
dS = |ru × rv| du dv for a parametrised surface r(u, v)
