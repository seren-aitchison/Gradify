Key terms and formulas at a glance

Hypotenuse
The longest side of a right-angled triangle, opposite the right angle
SOHCAHTOA
sin = opposite/hypotenuse, cos = adjacent/hypotenuse, tan = opposite/adjacent
Sine function
sin θ, the y-coordinate of the point at angle θ on the unit circle
Cosine function
cos θ, the x-coordinate of the point at angle θ on the unit circle
Tangent function
tan θ = sin θ/cos θ, undefined where cos θ = 0
Radian
The angle at the centre of a circle subtended by an arc equal in length to the radius
Unit circle
The circle x2 + y2 = 1
Angle of elevation
The angle above the horizontal from an observer to an object
Sine rule
a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C (sides a, b, c opposite angles A, B, C)
Cosine rule
a2 = b2 + c2 − 2bc cos A
Included angle
The angle between the two sides used in the formula
Spherical triangle
A triangle on a sphere whose sides are arcs of great circles

Which side is always the longest in a right-angled triangle?

The hypotenuse. From “Basic Trigonometry” →

In plain English: Trigonometry studies the relationships between the angles and sides of triangles. Key idea: Label H, O and A relative to the chosen angle; trigonometry links angles to ratios of sides.

What are the coordinates on the unit circle at 90°?

(0, 1). From “Unit Circle” →

In plain English: The unit circle (radius 1, centred at the origin) defines sine and cosine for every angle: the point at angle θ is (cos θ, sin θ). Key idea: The point at angle θ is (cos θ, sin θ); use reference angles and quadrant signs (CAST) for exact values.

What is the period of y = cos 3x?

120°. From “Trigonometric Graphs” →

In plain English: The graphs of sine, cosine and tangent show their periodic behaviour. Key idea: Amplitude |a|, period 360°/b, phase shift −c, vertical shift d for y = a sin(bx + c) + d.

A wave varies between 3 and 11. What are its amplitude and midline?

Amplitude 4, midline y = 7. From “Sinusoidal Modelling” →

In plain English: Sinusoidal models y = A sin(B(t − C)) + D describe repeating real-world patterns: tides, daylight hours, temperatures, sound and populations. Key idea: Amplitude is half the range, the midline is the average of max and min, B = 2π/period; choose sin or cos to match the start.

How many solutions does sin 3x = 0.2 have in 0° ≤ x < 360°?

6. From “Trigonometric Equations” →

In plain English: Trigonometric equations usually have many solutions, because trig functions repeat. Key idea: Reduce to one function of one angle, factorise, find all solutions in the interval; adjust the interval for multiple angles.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Trigonometry

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. In a right-angled triangle, which side is opposite the right angle? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    The hypotenuse. The hypotenuse is always opposite the right angle and is the longest side.

  2. What is tan 45°? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    1. In an isosceles right-angled triangle the opposite and adjacent sides are equal, so their ratio is 1.

  3. What is the maximum value of 5 sin x? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    5. sin x is at most 1, so 5 sin x is at most 5: the amplitude is 5.

  4. For the other acute angle of the same triangle, what does the "opposite" side become? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    The adjacent side. Swapping the acute angle swaps which leg is opposite and which is adjacent.

  5. In a right-angled triangle, the adjacent side is 9 and the hypotenuse is 15. What is the angle? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    About 53.1°. cos θ = 9/15 = 0.6, so θ = cos−1(0.6) ≈ 53.1°.

  6. Which angle between 0° and 360° has the same sine as 40°? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    140°. sin(180° − θ) = sin θ, so sin 140° = sin 40°.

  7. How many solutions does cos x = 0.3 have for 0° ≤ x < 720°? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    4. Two solutions per 360° period, over two periods, gives four.

  8. What is the exact value of cos 330°? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    √3/2. 330° is in the fourth quadrant, where cosine is positive, with reference angle 30°.

  9. For a = 5, b = 8, A = 30°, how many triangles are possible? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Two. sin B = 8 × 0.5/5 = 0.8, so B ≈ 53.1° or 126.9°; both give valid angle sums.

Key takeaways

  • Label H, O and A relative to the chosen angle; trigonometry links angles to ratios of sides.
  • SOHCAHTOA: choose the ratio with the known and unknown sides; inverse functions give angles; learn the exact values.
  • sin θ is the unit circle's y-coordinate; it repeats every 360°; sin(180° − θ) = sin θ gives a second solution.
  • cos θ is the unit circle's x-coordinate; cosine is even; cos(360° − θ) = cos θ gives the second solution.
  • tan θ = sin θ/cos θ is a gradient; period 180°; asymptotes where cos θ = 0; add 180° to find further solutions.
  • π radians = 180°; multiply by π/180 or 180/π; calculus needs radians.

Common mistakes in Trigonometry

  • Labelling sides before choosing the angle, or calling the hypotenuse the adjacent side.
  • Using degree values in a calculator set to radians.
  • Giving only the calculator's principal value when an equation has several solutions in the range.
  • Assuming cos x = −0.5 means x = −60°, which isn't in the usual range.
  • Adding 360° instead of 180° to find the second solution of a tangent equation.
  • Leaving a calculator in degree mode for calculus or radian questions.

Lessons in this school

  1. Basic Trigonometry
    Trigonometry studies the relationships between the angles and sides of triangles.
  2. Trigonometric Ratios
    The trigonometric ratios sine, cosine and tangent compare pairs of sides in a right-angled triangle.
  3. Sine
    The sine of an angle starts as a ratio in a right-angled triangle and becomes a function defined for every angle, using the unit circle.
  4. Cosine
    The cosine is the x-coordinate of the point at angle θ on the unit circle.
  5. Tangent
    The tangent of an angle is sin θ/cos θ, the gradient of the line at angle θ to the horizontal.
  6. Radians and Degrees
    Radians measure angles by arc length: one radian is the angle subtended by an arc equal to the radius.
  7. Unit Circle
    The unit circle (radius 1, centred at the origin) defines sine and cosine for every angle: the point at angle θ is (cos θ, sin θ).
  8. Trigonometric Geometry
    Trigonometry solves geometric problems: heights, distances, angles of elevation and depression, and 3D problems such as the angle between a line and a plane.
  9. Sine Rule
    The sine rule relates the sides of any triangle to the sines of their opposite angles.
  10. Cosine Rule
    The cosine rule generalises Pythagoras' theorem to any triangle.
  11. Area of a Triangle
    The area of any triangle can be found from two sides and the included angle: ½ab sin C.
  12. Spherical Trigonometry
    Spherical trigonometry solves triangles drawn on a sphere, whose sides are arcs of great circles.
  13. Trigonometric Graphs
    The graphs of sine, cosine and tangent show their periodic behaviour.
  14. Periodic Functions
    A periodic function repeats its values at regular intervals.
  15. Cosecant
    Cosecant is the reciprocal of sine: cosec θ = 1/sin θ.
  16. Secant
    Secant is the reciprocal of cosine: sec θ = 1/cos θ.
  17. Cotangent
    Cotangent is the reciprocal of tangent: cot θ = cos θ/sin θ.
  18. Inverse Trigonometric Functions
    The inverse trigonometric functions arcsin, arccos and arctan give an angle from a ratio.
  19. Sinusoidal Modelling
    Sinusoidal models y = A sin(B(t − C)) + D describe repeating real-world patterns: tides, daylight hours, temperatures, sound and populations.
  20. Harmonic Functions
    Harmonic motion is motion described by sines and cosines: springs, pendulums, vibrating strings and AC circuits.
  21. Trigonometric Identities
    Trigonometric identities are equations true for every angle.
  22. Compound Angle Formulas
    The compound angle formulas give the sine, cosine and tangent of a sum or difference of angles.
  23. Double Angle Formulas
    The double angle formulas express sin 2A, cos 2A and tan 2A in terms of A.
  24. Half Angle Formulas
    The half angle formulas give the trig functions of A/2 in terms of A.
  25. Trigonometric Equations
    Trigonometric equations usually have many solutions, because trig functions repeat.
  26. Trigonometric Inequalities
    Trigonometric inequalities ask where a trig function is above or below a value.
  27. Hyperbolic Functions
    The hyperbolic functions sinh, cosh and tanh are built from exponentials.
  28. Inverse Hyperbolic Functions
    The inverse hyperbolic functions arsinh, arcosh and artanh undo sinh, cosh and tanh.
  29. Fourier Series
    A Fourier series writes a periodic function as a sum of sines and cosines of increasing frequencies.
  30. Fourier Analysis
    Fourier analysis decomposes signals into their frequencies.

Trigonometry: questions students ask

Does the opposite side depend on which angle you choose?

Yes: it's the side across from your chosen angle.

Is cosine odd or even?

Even: cos(−θ) = cos θ.

Where is tan x undefined?

Where cos x = 0: at 90°, 270°, and so on.

How are angles of elevation and depression between two points related?

They're equal (alternate angles).
