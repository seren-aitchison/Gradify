Lessons in this school

  1. Basic Trigonometry
    Trigonometry studies the relationships between the angles and sides of triangles.
  2. Trigonometric Ratios
    The trigonometric ratios sine, cosine and tangent compare pairs of sides in a right-angled triangle.
  3. Sine
    The sine of an angle starts as a ratio in a right-angled triangle and becomes a function defined for every angle, using the unit circle.
  4. Cosine
    The cosine is the x-coordinate of the point at angle θ on the unit circle.
  5. Tangent
    The tangent of an angle is sin θ/cos θ, the gradient of the line at angle θ to the horizontal.
  6. Radians and Degrees
    Radians measure angles by arc length: one radian is the angle subtended by an arc equal to the radius.
  7. Unit Circle
    The unit circle (radius 1, centred at the origin) defines sine and cosine for every angle: the point at angle θ is (cos θ, sin θ).
  8. Trigonometric Geometry
    Trigonometry solves geometric problems: heights, distances, angles of elevation and depression, and 3D problems such as the angle between a line and a plane.
  9. Sine Rule
    The sine rule relates the sides of any triangle to the sines of their opposite angles.
  10. Cosine Rule
    The cosine rule generalises Pythagoras' theorem to any triangle.
  11. Area of a Triangle
    The area of any triangle can be found from two sides and the included angle: ½ab sin C.
  12. Spherical Trigonometry
    Spherical trigonometry solves triangles drawn on a sphere, whose sides are arcs of great circles.
  13. Trigonometric Graphs
    The graphs of sine, cosine and tangent show their periodic behaviour.
  14. Periodic Functions
    A periodic function repeats its values at regular intervals.
  15. Cosecant
    Cosecant is the reciprocal of sine: cosec θ = 1/sin θ.
  16. Secant
    Secant is the reciprocal of cosine: sec θ = 1/cos θ.
  17. Cotangent
    Cotangent is the reciprocal of tangent: cot θ = cos θ/sin θ.
  18. Inverse Trigonometric Functions
    The inverse trigonometric functions arcsin, arccos and arctan give an angle from a ratio.
  19. Sinusoidal Modelling
    Sinusoidal models y = A sin(B(t − C)) + D describe repeating real-world patterns: tides, daylight hours, temperatures, sound and populations.
  20. Harmonic Functions
    Harmonic motion is motion described by sines and cosines: springs, pendulums, vibrating strings and AC circuits.
  21. Trigonometric Identities
    Trigonometric identities are equations true for every angle.
  22. Compound Angle Formulas
    The compound angle formulas give the sine, cosine and tangent of a sum or difference of angles.
  23. Double Angle Formulas
    The double angle formulas express sin 2A, cos 2A and tan 2A in terms of A.
  24. Half Angle Formulas
    The half angle formulas give the trig functions of A/2 in terms of A.
  25. Trigonometric Equations
    Trigonometric equations usually have many solutions, because trig functions repeat.
  26. Trigonometric Inequalities
    Trigonometric inequalities ask where a trig function is above or below a value.
  27. Hyperbolic Functions
    The hyperbolic functions sinh, cosh and tanh are built from exponentials.
  28. Inverse Hyperbolic Functions
    The inverse hyperbolic functions arsinh, arcosh and artanh undo sinh, cosh and tanh.
  29. Fourier Series
    A Fourier series writes a periodic function as a sum of sines and cosines of increasing frequencies.
  30. Fourier Analysis
    Fourier analysis decomposes signals into their frequencies.

Key terms

Hypotenuse
The longest side of a right-angled triangle, opposite the right angle
SOHCAHTOA
sin = opposite/hypotenuse, cos = adjacent/hypotenuse, tan = opposite/adjacent
Sine function
sin θ, the y-coordinate of the point at angle θ on the unit circle
Cosine function
cos θ, the x-coordinate of the point at angle θ on the unit circle
Tangent function
tan θ = sin θ/cos θ, undefined where cos θ = 0
Radian
The angle at the centre of a circle subtended by an arc equal in length to the radius
Unit circle
The circle x2 + y2 = 1
Angle of elevation
The angle above the horizontal from an observer to an object
Sine rule
a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C (sides a, b, c opposite angles A, B, C)
Cosine rule
a2 = b2 + c2 − 2bc cos A
Included angle
The angle between the two sides used in the formula
Spherical triangle
A triangle on a sphere whose sides are arcs of great circles
Period
The length of one complete cycle: 360° for sin and cos, 180° for tan
Periodic function
A function with f(x + T) = f(x) for all x, for some T > 0
