Lessons in this school

  1. Arithmetic
    Arithmetic is calculating with numbers: adding, subtracting, multiplying and dividing.
  2. Order of Operations
    When a calculation has several operations, the order you do them in changes the answer.
  3. Number Systems
    A number system is a way of representing numbers with symbols.
  4. Natural Numbers
    The natural numbers are the counting numbers 1, 2, 3, … (and, in many university courses, 0 as well).
  5. Integers
    The integers extend the natural numbers to include zero and the negatives: …, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, ….
  6. Rational Numbers
    Rational numbers are numbers that can be written as a fraction of two integers, p/q with q not zero.
  7. Irrational Numbers
    An irrational number can't be written as a fraction of two integers.
  8. Real Numbers
    The real numbers are all the rational and irrational numbers together: every point on the number line.
  9. Factors and Multiples
    A factor of a number divides it exactly; a multiple is the result of multiplying it by a whole number.
  10. Divisibility
    Divisibility tests tell you whether one number divides another without doing the division.
  11. Prime Numbers
    A prime number has exactly two factors: 1 and itself.
  12. Composite Numbers
    A composite number is a natural number greater than 1 that isn't prime: it has at least one factor other than 1 and itself.
  13. Prime Factorisation
    Every whole number greater than 1 can be written as a product of primes in exactly one way, apart from order.
  14. Greatest Common Divisor (GCD)
    The greatest common divisor (GCD), called the highest common factor (HCF) at GCSE, is the largest number that divides two numbers exactly.
  15. Least Common Multiple (LCM)
    The least common multiple (LCM), called the lowest common multiple at GCSE, is the smallest number that is a multiple of two numbers.
  16. Fractions
    A fraction represents part of a whole, or a division: 3/4 means 3 ÷ 4.
  17. Decimals
    Decimals extend place value to the right of the units: tenths, hundredths, thousandths.
  18. Percentages
    A percentage is a fraction out of 100.
  19. Percentage Change and Reverse Percentages
    Percentage change measures an increase or decrease relative to the original amount.
  20. Ratios and Proportions
    A ratio compares the sizes of quantities; a proportion says two ratios are equal.
  21. Exponents and Powers
    A power (exponent, index) tells you how many times to multiply a number by itself.
  22. Roots and Radicals
    A root undoes a power: the square root of 49 is 7 because 72 = 49.
  23. Scientific Notation
    Scientific notation (standard form) writes numbers as A × 10n, with 1 ≤ A < 10.
  24. Rounding and Estimation
    Rounding simplifies numbers to a sensible accuracy; estimation uses rounded numbers to predict an answer.
  25. Significant Figures
    Significant figures count the digits that carry meaning about a number's size and precision.
  26. Sequences of Numbers
    A sequence is an ordered list of numbers following a rule.
  27. Number Patterns
    Number patterns (square, triangular, cube and Fibonacci numbers, and patterns in tables) reveal structure in mathematics.
  28. Number Bases
    A number base is the number of digits a positional system uses.
  29. Binary Arithmetic
    Binary (base 2) uses only the digits 0 and 1, matching the off and on states of electronic circuits.

Key terms

Inverse operation
An operation that undoes another: subtraction undoes addition, division undoes multiplication
BIDMAS
Brackets, Indices, Division and Multiplication, Addition and Subtraction: the order for evaluating a calculation
Positional system
A system where a digit's value depends on its position, such as hundreds, tens and units
Natural numbers
The counting numbers 1, 2, 3, …, written ℕ; some definitions include 0
Integers
The whole numbers, positive, negative and zero, written ℤ
Rational number
A number that can be written as p/q, where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0, written ℚ
Irrational number
A real number that can't be written as p/q with integers p and q
Real numbers
All rational and irrational numbers, written ℝ
Factor
A whole number that divides another exactly: the factors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12
Divisible
a is divisible by b if a ÷ b is a whole number, written b
Prime number
A natural number greater than 1 whose only factors are 1 and itself
Composite number
A natural number greater than 1 with more than two factors
Prime factorisation
Writing a number as a product of primes, such as 360 = 23 × 32 × 5
Highest common factor (HCF)
The largest number that is a factor of two or more numbers; also called the greatest common divisor
