Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

Name the four fields of nursing in the UK.

Adult, children's, learning disabilities and mental health nursing. From “Introduction to nursing” →

In plain English: Nursing is a profession that protects, promotes and restores health, prevents illness, and cares for people of all ages through illness, disability and dying. Key idea: Nursing is defined by its knowledge and accountability, not only by tasks. Many tasks can be delegated; the professional judgement behind them cannot.

Name the seven NMC platforms.

1 Being an accountable professional; 2 Promoting health and preventing ill health; 3 Assessing needs and planning care; 4 Providing and evaluating care; 5 Leading and managing nursing care and working in teams; 6 Improving safety and quality of care; 7 Coordinating care. From “The seven NMC platforms” →

In plain English: The NMC's Future nurse: standards of proficiency for registered nurses (2018) describe what every newly qualified nurse must know and be able to do. Key idea: The platforms describe the nurse at the point of registration. After registration, competence is maintained through CPD and revalidation and extended through further training.

Give three lawful grounds for sharing confidential information.

Consent; a legal requirement (such as a court order or statutory duty); public interest to prevent serious harm or serious crime; safeguarding. From “Confidentiality” →

In plain English: Confidentiality is the duty to protect information people share with health professionals and to use it only for their care, unless there is a lawful reason to share it. Key idea: The duty to share information can be as important as the duty to protect it (Caldicott Principle 7). Don't let confidentiality stop you sharing to keep someone safe.

What determines a nurse's scope of practice?

Registration, education and assessed competence, job role, employer policy and the law. From “Scope of practice” →

In plain English: Scope of practice is the range of activities a nurse is educated, competent and authorised to perform. Key idea: Competence in one setting doesn't automatically transfer to another. A procedure you are competent in may need local sign-off in a new trust.

Name four types of adult abuse under the Care Act guidance.

Any four of: physical, sexual, psychological, financial, domestic, modern slavery, discriminatory, organisational, neglect, self-neglect. From “Safeguarding” →

In plain English: Safeguarding means protecting people's health, wellbeing and human rights, enabling them to live free from harm, abuse and neglect. Key idea: You don't need proof to raise a concern. Report what you have seen or been told, and let the process decide.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Foundations of Nursing and Professional Practice

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which is a registered nurse's core professional activity? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Assessing needs, planning and giving care, and evaluating it. Assessment, planning, implementation and evaluation underpin nursing practice in every setting.

  2. Which body has regulated nurses and midwives in the UK since 2002? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    The Nursing and Midwifery Council (NMC). The NMC replaced the UKCC in 2002, under the Nursing and Midwifery Order 2001; the RCN is a professional body and union, and the CQC inspects services.

  3. A nurse wants representation at a disciplinary meeting at work. Who would normally provide it? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Their trade union or the RCN. The NMC regulates; unions and professional bodies represent members.

  4. A student asks why all fields share one set of standards of proficiency. What is the best answer? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    So every nurse meets the same core standards. The NMC sets one set of proficiencies for all registered nurses; field-specific knowledge is built on top.

  5. Nightingale's work in the Crimea is often cited as an early use of what? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Statistics to change practice. Her mortality data and diagrams showed the effect of sanitation, an early form of evidence-based change.

  6. Which statement about healthcare support workers is correct? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    They aren't on the NMC register. Support workers are not NMC-registered; the registrant is accountable for appropriate delegation.

  7. A doctor tells a nurse to give a medicine in a way that the nurse believes is unsafe. What is the nurse's position? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Don't proceed; raise and escalate the concern. Nurses remain accountable for their own practice and must not act in a way they believe is unsafe; they must raise concerns.

  8. A newly qualified nurse attempts a procedure they have not been trained in because the ward is busy, and the patient is harmed. Which statement is correct? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Judged as a competent nurse; inexperience is no defence. Wilsher: the standard attaches to the task undertaken; nurses must work within competence and seek help.

  9. A person with dementia in England seems unable to decide about moving into a care home. What must happen before deciding they lack capacity? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Support them to decide, then assess capacity. The MCA requires support first and a decision-specific, time-specific assessment.

Key takeaways

  • Nursing is defined by its knowledge and accountability, not only by tasks. Many tasks can be delegated; the professional judgement behind them cannot.
  • Registration exists to protect the public: it sets standards and allows unsafe practitioners to be removed.
  • Distinguish the regulator (protects the public), the professional body or union (supports members), and system regulators (inspect services).
  • Professional identity is visible in everyday behaviour: how you introduce yourself, ask for consent, speak about colleagues and record care.
  • Fitness to practise means having the skills, knowledge, health and character to practise safely and effectively. It is about the present and future, not punishing the past.
  • The Code is not optional guidance. Departing from it can bring your fitness to practise into question.

Common mistakes in Foundations of Nursing and Professional Practice

  • Posting about patients, even without names, can breach confidentiality if they could be identified from the details.
  • Silence or a lack of objection isn't consent. Check understanding and agreement.
  • Viewing your own family member's record "just to check" without a care reason is still a breach.
  • Never document care in advance of giving it, or alter a record after an event without making the change clear.

Foundations of Nursing and Professional Practice mind map

Lessons in this school

  1. Introduction to nursing
    Nursing is a profession that protects, promotes and restores health, prevents illness, and cares for people of all ages through illness, disability and dying.
  2. History and development of nursing
    Modern nursing grew from religious and military caring traditions into a regulated, graduate profession.
  3. The nursing profession
    Nursing is the largest professional group in UK health care.
  4. Professional identity
    Professional identity is how nurses understand themselves as nurses: their values, knowledge, behaviour and sense of belonging to the profession.
  5. The NMC and professional regulation
    The Nursing and Midwifery Council regulates nurses and midwives in the UK and nursing associates in England.
  6. The NMC Code
    The Code: Professional standards of practice and behaviour for nurses, midwives and nursing associates (2015, updated 2018) sets out what the public can expect of every NMC registrant.
  7. The seven NMC platforms
    The NMC's Future nurse: standards of proficiency for registered nurses (2018) describe what every newly qualified nurse must know and be able to do.
  8. Professional accountability
    Accountability means being answerable for your actions and omissions, and being able to justify them.
  9. Duty of care
    A duty of care is the legal obligation to take reasonable care to avoid acts or omissions that you can reasonably foresee would harm others.
  10. Duty of candour
    The duty of candour requires health professionals and organisations to be open and honest with people when something goes wrong with their care that causes, or could cause, harm or distress.
  11. Consent
    Valid consent is a person's voluntary, informed agreement, given with capacity, to examination, care or treatment.
  12. Capacity
    Mental capacity is the ability to make a particular decision at the time it needs to be made.
  13. Confidentiality
    Confidentiality is the duty to protect information people share with health professionals and to use it only for their care, unless there is a lawful reason to share it.
  14. Data protection
    Data protection law governs how personal information, including health information, is collected, used, stored and shared.
  15. Documentation
    Documentation is the written or electronic record of assessment, care, decisions and communication.
  16. Record keeping
    Record keeping covers how records are created, stored, shared, retained and disposed of.
  17. Professional boundaries
    Professional boundaries are the limits that keep the relationship between a nurse and the people they care for safe, therapeutic and focused on the person's needs.
  18. Delegation
    Delegation is transferring to another person the authority to carry out a task, while the delegating registrant keeps accountability for the decision to delegate.
  19. Scope of practice
    Scope of practice is the range of activities a nurse is educated, competent and authorised to perform.
  20. Clinical supervision
    Clinical supervision is a formal process of professional support and learning that helps practitioners develop knowledge and skills, reflect on practice and look after their wellbeing.
  21. Reflective practice
    Reflective practice is thinking critically about your experiences to learn from them and improve future practice.
  22. Evidence-based practice
    Evidence-based practice integrates the best available research evidence with clinical expertise and the person's values and preferences to make decisions about care.
  23. Equality, diversity and inclusion
    Equality means fair treatment and opportunity; diversity recognises and values differences; inclusion means everyone can take part and feels they belong.
  24. Human rights
    Human rights are the basic rights and freedoms everyone has.
  25. Safeguarding
    Safeguarding means protecting people's health, wellbeing and human rights, enabling them to live free from harm, abuse and neglect.
  26. Professional resilience
    Professional resilience is the ability to maintain wellbeing and effective practice in the face of stress and adversity.
  27. Patient advocacy
    Advocacy is acting on behalf of a person to make sure their views, wishes and rights are heard and respected, especially when they cannot speak up for themselves.
  28. Ethical decision-making
    Ethical decision-making is working out what you ought to do when values, duties and interests conflict.
  29. Multidisciplinary working
    Multidisciplinary (or interprofessional) working brings together professionals with different skills to plan and deliver coordinated care.
  30. Continuing professional development
    Continuing professional development (CPD) is the ongoing learning that keeps a nurse's knowledge and skills up to date and develops their practice.
