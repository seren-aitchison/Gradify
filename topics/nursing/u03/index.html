Lessons in this school

  1. Introduction to clinical assessment
    Clinical assessment is the systematic gathering and interpretation of information about a person's health to identify needs, problems and risks, and to plan care.
  2. Person-centred assessment
    Person-centred assessment puts the person's own experiences, values, preferences and goals at the centre.
  3. History taking
    History taking is a structured conversation to understand a person's presenting problem and its context.
  4. Subjective and objective data
    Assessment uses two kinds of information: subjective data (what the person tells you) and objective data (what you observe, measure or test).
  5. Physical assessment
    Physical assessment is the systematic examination of the body to gather objective data.
  6. General inspection
    General inspection is the first impression you form when you approach a person: how they look, behave and breathe.
  7. Vital signs
    Vital signs (observations) are measurements of the body's most basic functions: respiratory rate, oxygen saturation, blood pressure, pulse, consciousness level and temperature.
  8. Temperature
    Body temperature reflects the balance between heat production and heat loss.
  9. Pulse
    The pulse is the wave of pressure felt as the heart ejects blood into the arteries.
  10. Respiratory rate
    Respiratory rate is the number of breaths per minute.
  11. Blood pressure
    Blood pressure is the pressure of blood against arterial walls: systolic (when the heart contracts) over diastolic (when it relaxes).
  12. Oxygen saturation
    Oxygen saturation (SpO2) measured by pulse oximetry estimates the percentage of haemoglobin carrying oxygen.
  13. Pain assessment
    Pain is an unpleasant sensory and emotional experience.
  14. Neurological observations
    Neurological observations assess consciousness, pupils, limb power and vital signs to detect changes in brain function.
  15. Cardiovascular assessment
    Cardiovascular assessment examines the heart and circulation to identify problems with cardiac output, perfusion and fluid status.
  16. Respiratory assessment
    Respiratory assessment looks at how well a person is breathing and oxygenating.
  17. Abdominal assessment
    Abdominal assessment explores symptoms and signs from the digestive, urinary and reproductive systems.
  18. Skin assessment
    Skin assessment examines the skin, hair and nails for colour, integrity, temperature, moisture and lesions.
  19. Hydration assessment
    Hydration assessment identifies dehydration and fluid overload by combining history, observation, examination, fluid balance and test results.
  20. Nutritional assessment
    Nutritional assessment identifies people who are malnourished or at risk, so that they get the right support.
  21. Mental state examination
    The mental state examination (MSE) is a structured description of a person's mental state at a point in time, based on observation and conversation.
  22. Mobility assessment
    Mobility assessment considers how a person moves, transfers and walks, and what support they need.
  23. Falls assessment
    Falls are a leading cause of injury in older people and are common in hospitals and care homes.
  24. Pressure ulcer risk assessment
    Pressure ulcers are localised damage to the skin and underlying tissue, usually over a bony prominence, caused by pressure or pressure combined with shear.
  25. Clinical reasoning
    Clinical reasoning is the thinking process nurses use to collect and interpret information, identify problems, decide on actions, and evaluate their effect.
  26. Differential considerations
    Differential considerations are the possible explanations for a person's symptoms and signs.
  27. Recognising deterioration
    Recognising deterioration means noticing early signs that a person is becoming more unwell and acting promptly.
  28. Escalation of concerns
    Escalation is communicating concerns about a person's condition to someone who can provide the right level of help, in time.
  29. Structured handover
    Handover transfers responsibility and information about a person's care from one professional or team to another.
  30. Documentation and reassessment
    Documentation of assessment and reassessment shows what was found, what was done and whether it worked.

Key terms

Nursing process
Assess, plan, implement and evaluate care
Person-centred care
Care that treats the person as an individual and partner in their care
Presenting complaint
The main problem in the person's own words
Subjective data
Information the person reports, such as symptoms and feelings
Palpation
Examining by touch
General inspection
The first overall impression of a person's appearance, behaviour and breathing
NEWS2
National Early Warning Score 2, used to detect deterioration in adults
Pyrexia
A raised body temperature (fever)
Tachycardia
A heart rate above 100 beats per minute in adults
Tachypnoea
An abnormally fast breathing rate
Postural hypotension
A drop in blood pressure on standing
SpO2
Oxygen saturation measured by pulse oximetry
Numerical rating scale
A 0–10 scale for self-reported pain intensity
Glasgow Coma Scale
A 3–15 scale of eye, verbal and motor response
