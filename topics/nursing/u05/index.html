What is the difference between incidence and prevalence?

Incidence is the number of new cases in a period; prevalence is the number of existing cases at a point or period in time. From “Introduction to disease processes” →

How should a person use GTN for angina?

Sit down, use the spray, repeat after 5 minutes if needed, and call 999 if pain persists 5 minutes after the second dose. From “Ischaemic heart disease” →

What is the commonest bacterial cause of community-acquired pneumonia?

Streptococcus pneumoniae. From “Pneumonia” →

Lessons in this school

  1. Introduction to disease processes
    Pathophysiology is the study of how normal body functions are disrupted by disease.
  2. Inflammation
    Inflammation is the body's protective response to injury or infection.
  3. Infection
    Infection is the invasion and multiplication of micro-organisms in the body, causing harm.
  4. Immune dysfunction
    Immune dysfunction occurs when the immune system is underactive (immunodeficiency), overactive (hypersensitivity), or misdirected against the body (autoimmunity).
  5. Cardiovascular disease
    Cardiovascular disease (CVD) is a group of conditions affecting the heart and blood vessels, mostly caused by atherosclerosis.
  6. Hypertension
    Hypertension is persistently raised arterial blood pressure.
  7. Heart failure
    Heart failure is a clinical syndrome in which the heart can't pump enough blood to meet the body's needs, or can only do so at high filling pressures.
  8. Ischaemic heart disease
    Ischaemic heart disease (coronary heart disease) occurs when the coronary arteries are narrowed by atherosclerosis, reducing blood supply to the heart muscle.
  9. Myocardial infarction
    A myocardial infarction (MI, heart attack) is death of heart muscle caused by prolonged ischaemia, usually from a blood clot on a ruptured coronary plaque.
  10. Arrhythmias
    An arrhythmia is an abnormal heart rhythm.
  11. Stroke
    A stroke is a sudden loss of brain function caused by an interruption of blood supply: either a blocked artery (ischaemic stroke) or bleeding (haemorrhagic stroke).
  12. Respiratory disease
    Respiratory diseases affect the airways, lung tissue, blood vessels or breathing muscles.
  13. Asthma
    Asthma is a chronic inflammatory condition of the airways causing variable, reversible airflow obstruction, with symptoms of wheeze, breathlessness, chest tightness and cough.
  14. COPD
    Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is a common, preventable lung condition with persistent breathlessness and airflow obstruction that is not fully reversible, usually caused by smoking.
  15. Pneumonia
    Pneumonia is infection of the lung tissue, causing inflammation and consolidation of the alveoli.
  16. Pulmonary embolism
    A pulmonary embolism (PE) is a blockage of the pulmonary arteries, usually by a blood clot from a deep vein thrombosis (DVT) in the leg.
  17. Diabetes
    Diabetes mellitus is a group of conditions in which blood glucose is persistently raised because the body produces too little insulin, responds poorly to it, or both.
  18. Thyroid disease
    Thyroid disease is common, especially in women.
  19. Renal disease
    The kidneys filter blood, remove waste, balance fluid and electrolytes, regulate acid-base and blood pressure, make erythropoietin and activate vitamin D.
  20. Acute kidney injury
    Acute kidney injury (AKI) is a sudden fall in kidney function over hours to days.
  21. Chronic kidney disease
    Chronic kidney disease (CKD) is long-term reduced kidney function or kidney damage lasting more than 3 months.
  22. Liver disease
    Liver disease is a growing cause of death in the UK, largely preventable and often diagnosed late.
  23. Gastrointestinal disorders
    Gastrointestinal (GI) disorders range from common, mild problems such as reflux and constipation to life-threatening bleeding, obstruction and cancer.
  24. Neurological disorders
    Neurological disorders affect the brain, spinal cord, nerves or muscles.
  25. Epilepsy
    Epilepsy is a condition of recurrent, unprovoked seizures caused by abnormal electrical activity in the brain.
  26. Dementia
    Dementia is a syndrome of progressive decline in memory, thinking, behaviour and the ability to perform everyday activities, caused by brain disease.
  27. Parkinson's disease
    Parkinson's disease is a progressive neurological condition caused by loss of dopamine-producing neurones in the brain.
  28. Cancer
    Cancer is uncontrolled growth of abnormal cells that can invade nearby tissue and spread (metastasise).
  29. Haematological disorders
    Haematological disorders affect the blood and blood-forming organs: red cells, white cells, platelets, clotting and the bone marrow.
  30. Musculoskeletal disease
    Musculoskeletal (MSK) conditions affect bones, joints, muscles and connective tissue.
  31. Rheumatological conditions
    Rheumatological conditions are inflammatory and autoimmune diseases of joints, muscles and connective tissue.
  32. Skin conditions
    The skin is the largest organ, and skin conditions are common causes of discomfort, distress and infection.
  33. Sepsis
    Sepsis is a life-threatening reaction to an infection in which the body's response damages its own tissues and organs.
  34. Shock
    Shock is a life-threatening state of circulatory failure in which tissues don't receive enough oxygen.
  35. Multimorbidity
    Multimorbidity is the presence of two or more long-term health conditions in one person.
  36. Frailty
    Frailty is a long-term condition in which reduced reserve across many body systems makes a person vulnerable to sudden, dramatic changes in health after minor events such as an infection or a new medicine.
  37. Palliative and end-of-life conditions
    Palliative care improves quality of life for people with life-limiting illness and their families, through relief of pain and other symptoms and attention to psychological, social and spiritual needs.

Key terms

Pathophysiology
The study of how normal body functions are disrupted by disease
Acute inflammation
A rapid, short-lived protective response to injury or infection
Chain of infection
The links needed for infection to spread, from agent to susceptible host
Immunodeficiency
Reduced ability of the immune system to fight infection
Atherosclerosis
Build-up of fatty plaques in artery walls
Primary hypertension
Raised blood pressure with no single identifiable cause
Heart failure
A syndrome in which the heart can't pump enough blood to meet the body's needs
Stable angina
Predictable chest pain on exertion relieved by rest or GTN
STEMI
ST-elevation myocardial infarction
Atrial fibrillation
An irregular, often fast heart rhythm caused by chaotic atrial activity
Ischaemic stroke
A stroke caused by a blocked artery
Obstructive lung disease
Disease that limits airflow out of the lungs, such as asthma and COPD
Peak expiratory flow
The maximum speed of breathing out, used to assess asthma
COPD
Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease: persistent, not fully reversible airflow obstruction
