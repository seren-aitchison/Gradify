Lessons in this school

  1. Ageing physiology
    Ageing brings gradual changes in every body system that reduce physiological reserve.
  2. Frailty
    Frailty is a distinct clinical state of vulnerability.
  3. Falls
    Falls are common in older people and can cause injury, fear, loss of independence and death.
  4. Dementia
    Dementia is a progressive syndrome affecting memory, thinking, behaviour and daily function.
  5. Delirium
    Delirium is a sudden, fluctuating disturbance of attention and awareness, usually caused by physical illness.
  6. Polypharmacy
    Many older people take several medicines, often for multiple conditions.
  7. Continence
    Bladder and bowel problems are common in older people but aren't an inevitable part of ageing.
  8. Nutrition and hydration
    Malnutrition and dehydration are common in older people, in hospital, in care homes and at home.
  9. Pressure injuries
    Pressure injuries (pressure ulcers) are localised damage to the skin and underlying tissue, usually over a bony prominence, caused by pressure or pressure with shear.
  10. Mobility
    Mobility underpins independence, dignity and health in older age.
  11. Rehabilitation
    Rehabilitation and intermediate care help older people recover function after illness, injury or surgery, often outside hospital.
  12. Care homes
    Several hundred thousand older people in the UK live in care homes.
  13. Safeguarding older people
    Older people can experience abuse and neglect at home, in care homes and in hospitals, often from people they trust.
  14. Advanced care planning
    Advance care planning is a voluntary process of discussion between a person, those close to them and professionals about future wishes and priorities for care.
  15. Palliative care
    Palliative care for older people often involves non-cancer conditions such as frailty, dementia, heart failure and COPD, with uncertain trajectories.
  16. Symptom management
    Good symptom management relieves suffering and improves quality of life at the end of life.
  17. Pain management
    Pain is one of the most feared symptoms at the end of life.
  18. End-of-life communication
    Conversations about serious illness, dying and death are among the most important nurses have.
  19. Last days of life
    The last days of life are a time when the focus of care shifts to comfort, dignity and the wishes of the dying person and those close to them.
  20. Bereavement support
    Bereavement is the experience of losing someone close; grief is the response.
  21. Family support
    Families and those close to older and dying people are often carers, decision-makers and people in their own distress.
  22. Ethical decision-making
    Caring for older and dying people raises difficult ethical questions about treatment, autonomy, burden and benefit.

Key terms

Homeostenosis
The progressive narrowing of physiological reserve with ageing
Frailty phenotype
Fried's model defining frailty by weight loss, exhaustion, weakness, slow walking and low activity
Orthostatic hypotension
A drop in blood pressure on standing (systolic 20 mmHg or diastolic 10 mmHg within 3 minutes)
Dementia with Lewy bodies
A dementia with fluctuating cognition, visual hallucinations and parkinsonism, with severe antipsychotic sensitivity
Hypoactive delirium
Delirium with reduced activity and alertness, often missed
STOPP/START
Criteria listing potentially inappropriate medicines to stop and beneficial medicines to start in older people
Bladder diary
A record of fluid intake, voiding times and volumes, and leakage over several days
Food first
Increasing energy and protein using ordinary food before supplements
Pressure injury
Localised damage to skin and underlying tissue from pressure or pressure with shear
Hospital-associated deconditioning
Loss of strength and function during a hospital stay
Intermediate care
Short-term services to help people recover, avoid admission or return home sooner
Residential care home
A care home providing personal care without on-site registered nursing
Elder abuse
A single or repeated act, or lack of appropriate action, causing harm or distress to an older person in a relationship of trust
Advance care planning
A voluntary process of discussing and recording future wishes and priorities for care
