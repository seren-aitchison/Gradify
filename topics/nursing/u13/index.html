Lessons in this school

  1. Foundations of learning disability nursing
    Learning disability nursing supports people with learning disabilities and autistic people across their lives to be healthy, safe and included.
  2. Intellectual disability
    Intellectual (learning) disability is a lifelong condition that affects how a person learns, understands and copes independently.
  3. Neurodevelopmental conditions
    Neurodevelopmental conditions affect the development of the brain and nervous system, influencing learning, attention, communication, movement and behaviour.
  4. Autism
    Autism is a lifelong neurodevelopmental difference in how people communicate, interact and experience the world.
  5. Down syndrome
    Down syndrome is the most common chromosomal cause of learning disability.
  6. Communication needs
    Many people with learning disabilities communicate in ways other than speech, or understand less than they appear to.
  7. Accessible healthcare
    People with learning disabilities often face barriers to healthcare: inaccessible information, difficult environments, communication problems and attitudes.
  8. Reasonable adjustments
    Reasonable adjustments are changes to how services are provided so that disabled people, including people with learning disabilities and autistic people, can use them as effectively as others.
  9. Mental capacity
    Supporting people with learning disabilities to make their own decisions is central to learning disability nursing.
  10. Consent
    Valid consent applies to people with learning disabilities just as it does to anyone else.
  11. Advocacy
    Advocacy helps people speak up, be heard and have their rights respected.
  12. Positive behavioural support
    Positive behaviour support (PBS) is a person-centred, evidence-based approach to understanding and reducing behaviour that challenges by improving quality of life and meeting needs.
  13. Challenging behaviour
    "Behaviour that challenges" describes behaviour that puts the person or others at risk or limits their quality of life.
  14. Physical health inequalities
    People with learning disabilities experience more physical health problems and die younger than the general population, often from treatable conditions.
  15. Epilepsy
    Epilepsy is much more common in people with learning disabilities than in the general population, and more so in people with more severe disabilities.
  16. Complex needs
    Some people with learning disabilities have complex health needs, such as profound and multiple learning disabilities, dysphagia, postural difficulties and sensory impairments.
  17. Safeguarding
    People with learning disabilities are at higher risk of abuse, exploitation and neglect, and may find it harder to report it.
  18. Family and carer support
    Families and carers provide much of the support for people with learning disabilities, often for a lifetime.
  19. Community learning disability services
    Community learning disability teams support people to stay healthy, safe and included in their local communities.
  20. Hospital care
    Hospital stays can be frightening and risky for people with learning disabilities.
  21. Health promotion
    Health promotion for people with learning disabilities aims to reduce health inequalities by making healthy living, screening and prevention accessible.
  22. Independent living
    Independent living means people with learning disabilities having choice and control over where and how they live, who supports them and how they spend their time.
  23. Multidisciplinary care
    People with learning disabilities often need support from many professionals.
  24. Rights and inclusion
    People with learning disabilities have the same human rights as everyone else.
  25. Preparing for a learning disability nursing placement
    Learning disability placements take students into homes, communities, hospitals and specialist services.
  26. Reflective learning and case studies in learning disability nursing
    Reflection and case studies help learning disability nursing students and registrants understand people's lives, link theory and law to practice, and develop values-based, person-centred care.

Key terms

Learning disability nurse
A registered nurse in the learning disability field
Learning disability
Reduced intellectual and adaptive functioning, starting before adulthood, with lasting effects
Neurodevelopmental condition
A condition affecting brain development, such as autism, ADHD or learning disability
Masking
Suppressing autistic traits to fit in, which can be exhausting and harmful
Trisomy 21
An extra copy of chromosome 21, the usual cause of Down syndrome
Makaton
A language programme using signs and symbols alongside speech
Annual health check
A yearly health check with a GP for people with a learning disability (aged 14 and over in England)
Reasonable adjustment
A change to how a service is provided so disabled people aren't at a substantial disadvantage
Supported decision-making
Helping a person make their own decision with accessible information and support
Acquiescence
A tendency to say "yes" or agree, regardless of the question
Self-advocacy
People speaking up for themselves, often with group support
Positive behaviour support
A person-centred approach to understanding behaviour that challenges and improving quality of life
Behaviour that challenges
Behaviour that risks safety or limits access to ordinary life, reflecting unmet needs
Aspiration pneumonia
Lung infection caused by inhaling food, drink, saliva or vomit
