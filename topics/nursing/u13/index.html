Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What is LeDeR?

The programme reviewing deaths of people with a learning disability and autistic people in England to drive improvement. From “Foundations of learning disability nursing” →

In plain English: Learning disability nursing supports people with learning disabilities and autistic people across their lives to be healthy, safe and included. Key idea: Learning disability nurses protect rights and health; history teaches the dangers of closed institutions, and LeDeR shows the scale of avoidable early deaths.

What is total communication?

Using every available method of communication together. From “Communication needs” →

In plain English: Many people with learning disabilities communicate in ways other than speech, or understand less than they appear to. Key idea: Find out how each person communicates, use total communication with tools such as Makaton, PECS and easy read, and allow time.

What is self-advocacy?

People speaking up for themselves, often supported by self-advocacy groups. From “Advocacy” →

In plain English: Advocacy helps people speak up, be heard and have their rights respected. Key idea: Know the types of advocacy and when statutory advocates are required, advocate for people's voices, and support self-advocacy.

What signs may suggest aspiration during eating?

Coughing, a wet-sounding voice and recurrent chest infections. From “Complex needs” →

In plain English: Some people with learning disabilities have complex health needs, such as profound and multiple learning disabilities, dysphagia, postural difficulties and sensory impairments. Key idea: People with PMLD and complex needs require individualised care for communication, dysphagia, feeding, posture and sensory needs, coordinated by a team that includes families.

Can a person with a learning disability choose an unhealthy lifestyle?

Yes, if they have capacity for the decision. From “Health promotion” →

In plain English: Health promotion for people with learning disabilities aims to reduce health inequalities by making healthy living, screening and prevention accessible. Key idea: Promote health through accessible information, screening, vaccination and lifestyle support, respecting choice and working with carers.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Learning Disability Nursing

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which 2001 strategy set principles of rights, independence, choice and inclusion? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Valuing People. Valuing People set these four key principles.

  2. Which is required for a diagnosis of learning disability? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Onset before adulthood. The condition must begin before adulthood and have lasting effects.

  3. Which describes ADHD? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Persistent inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity. ADHD involves persistent patterns that affect daily life.

  4. What training became mandatory for health and social care staff in England under the Health and Care Act 2022? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    The Oliver McGowan training. The Oliver McGowan Mandatory Training is required for staff in regulated services.

  5. Which factor is commonly found in avoidable deaths of people with learning disabilities? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Delays in recognising illness and diagnostic overshadowing. LeDeR reports highlight delays, overshadowing and poor adjustments.

  6. What does PMLD stand for? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Profound and multiple learning disabilities. PMLD describes a profound learning disability with other significant disabilities.

  7. A woman with a learning disability needs a dental check, a blood test and an ear examination, all of which distress her. What adjustment could help? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Combining them under one anaesthetic. Combining procedures can reduce distress and improve access.

  8. A woman with a learning disability lacks capacity to decide about a major operation and has no family. Who should be involved? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    An Independent Mental Capacity Advocate. An IMCA must be instructed for serious medical treatment without anyone appropriate to consult.

  9. What is the nurse's advocacy role if a DNACPR decision seems to be based on a person's learning disability? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Challenge it and ensure the decision is individualised and lawful. A learning disability alone is never a reason for DNACPR; nurses should challenge it.

Key takeaways

  • Learning disability nurses protect rights and health; history teaches the dangers of closed institutions, and LeDeR shows the scale of avoidable early deaths.
  • Learning disability means reduced intellectual and adaptive functioning starting before adulthood; it ranges from mild to profound and has many causes.
  • Neurodevelopmental conditions, including ADHD, cerebral palsy and FASD, often co-occur with learning disability; assess holistically and support strengths with adjusted environments.
  • Autism involves differences in communication, sensory processing and routine; reduce sensory load, use clear language, prepare people and recognise masking and burnout.
  • Down syndrome is usually trisomy 21; monitor heart, hearing, vision, thyroid and other risks, and watch for early-onset dementia while excluding treatable causes.
  • Find out how each person communicates, use total communication with tools such as Makaton, PECS and easy read, and allow time.

Common mistakes in Learning Disability Nursing

  • Assuming poorer health outcomes are inevitable in learning disability. Many early deaths are avoidable with good, adjusted care.
  • Diagnosing learning disability from an IQ score alone. Adaptive functioning and onset before adulthood are also required.
  • Attributing all difficulties to one known condition. Co-occurring conditions such as ADHD or sensory impairment may be missed.
  • Interpreting a meltdown as deliberate bad behaviour. It is a response to overwhelm; reduce demands and stimulation.
  • Assuming a change in behaviour in a person with Down syndrome is dementia without checking for hypothyroidism, hearing loss or depression.
  • Assuming someone who speaks well understands complex information. Many people understand less than their speech suggests; check understanding.

Lessons in this school

  1. Foundations of learning disability nursing
    Learning disability nursing supports people with learning disabilities and autistic people across their lives to be healthy, safe and included.
  2. Intellectual disability
    Intellectual (learning) disability is a lifelong condition that affects how a person learns, understands and copes independently.
  3. Neurodevelopmental conditions
    Neurodevelopmental conditions affect the development of the brain and nervous system, influencing learning, attention, communication, movement and behaviour.
  4. Autism
    Autism is a lifelong neurodevelopmental difference in how people communicate, interact and experience the world.
  5. Down syndrome
    Down syndrome is the most common chromosomal cause of learning disability.
  6. Communication needs
    Many people with learning disabilities communicate in ways other than speech, or understand less than they appear to.
  7. Accessible healthcare
    People with learning disabilities often face barriers to healthcare: inaccessible information, difficult environments, communication problems and attitudes.
  8. Reasonable adjustments
    Reasonable adjustments are changes to how services are provided so that disabled people, including people with learning disabilities and autistic people, can use them as effectively as others.
  9. Mental capacity
    Supporting people with learning disabilities to make their own decisions is central to learning disability nursing.
  10. Consent
    Valid consent applies to people with learning disabilities just as it does to anyone else.
  11. Advocacy
    Advocacy helps people speak up, be heard and have their rights respected.
  12. Positive behavioural support
    Positive behaviour support (PBS) is a person-centred, evidence-based approach to understanding and reducing behaviour that challenges by improving quality of life and meeting needs.
  13. Challenging behaviour
    "Behaviour that challenges" describes behaviour that puts the person or others at risk or limits their quality of life.
  14. Physical health inequalities
    People with learning disabilities experience more physical health problems and die younger than the general population, often from treatable conditions.
  15. Epilepsy
    Epilepsy is much more common in people with learning disabilities than in the general population, and more so in people with more severe disabilities.
  16. Complex needs
    Some people with learning disabilities have complex health needs, such as profound and multiple learning disabilities, dysphagia, postural difficulties and sensory impairments.
  17. Safeguarding
    People with learning disabilities are at higher risk of abuse, exploitation and neglect, and may find it harder to report it.
  18. Family and carer support
    Families and carers provide much of the support for people with learning disabilities, often for a lifetime.
  19. Community learning disability services
    Community learning disability teams support people to stay healthy, safe and included in their local communities.
  20. Hospital care
    Hospital stays can be frightening and risky for people with learning disabilities.
  21. Health promotion
    Health promotion for people with learning disabilities aims to reduce health inequalities by making healthy living, screening and prevention accessible.
  22. Independent living
    Independent living means people with learning disabilities having choice and control over where and how they live, who supports them and how they spend their time.
  23. Multidisciplinary care
    People with learning disabilities often need support from many professionals.
  24. Rights and inclusion
    People with learning disabilities have the same human rights as everyone else.
  25. Preparing for a learning disability nursing placement
    Learning disability placements take students into homes, communities, hospitals and specialist services.
  26. Reflective learning and case studies in learning disability nursing
    Reflection and case studies help learning disability nursing students and registrants understand people's lives, link theory and law to practice, and develop values-based, person-centred care.

Learning Disability Nursing: questions students ask

What three criteria define a learning disability?

Reduced intellectual ability, reduced adaptive functioning, and onset before adulthood.

What is fetal alcohol spectrum disorder?

Lifelong effects of alcohol exposure before birth, affecting learning, memory, attention and behaviour.

What is masking?

Suppressing autistic traits to fit in, which can be exhausting and harmful to mental health.

What usually causes Down syndrome?

An extra copy of chromosome 21 (trisomy 21).
