Lessons in this school

  1. Professional regulation
    Professional regulation protects the public by setting standards for education and practice, keeping a register of who may practise, and acting when a professional's fitness to practise is in question.
  2. The NMC Code
    The NMC Code sets out the professional standards that nurses, midwives and nursing associates must uphold.
  3. Duty of care
    A duty of care is a legal obligation to take reasonable care to avoid causing foreseeable harm to others.
  4. Consent
    Valid consent is required before examination, treatment or care.
  5. Mental Capacity Act 2005
    The Mental Capacity Act 2005 (MCA) provides the legal framework in England and Wales for making decisions for, and supporting, people who may lack capacity.
  6. Mental Health Act
    The Mental Health Act 1983 (as amended) allows people in England and Wales with a mental disorder to be assessed and treated in hospital without their consent in defined circumstances, with safeguards.
  7. Safeguarding adults
    Safeguarding adults means protecting an adult's right to live in safety, free from abuse and neglect, while respecting their wishes.
  8. Safeguarding children
    Safeguarding children means protecting them from maltreatment, preventing impairment of their health and development, and ensuring they grow up with safe and effective care.
  9. Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards and relevant reforms
    The Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards (DoLS) protect people who lack capacity to consent to arrangements for their care that amount to a deprivation of liberty.
  10. Human rights
    Human rights are basic rights and freedoms belonging to everyone.
  11. Equality law
    Equality law protects people from discrimination, harassment and victimisation, and requires public bodies to advance equality.
  12. Data protection
    Data protection law governs how personal information is collected, used, stored and shared.
  13. Confidentiality
    Confidentiality is a legal, ethical and professional duty to protect information people share in confidence.
  14. Information governance
    Information governance is the framework of law, policy and practice that ensures information is handled legally, securely, efficiently and effectively.
  15. Record keeping
    Health records are legal documents that support safe care, communication and accountability.
  16. Duty of candour
    The duty of candour requires openness and honesty with people when things go wrong in their care.
  17. Negligence
    Clinical negligence is a civil claim that a healthcare professional or organisation breached their duty of care and caused harm.
  18. Professional accountability
    Accountability means being answerable for your decisions and actions.
  19. Clinical incidents
    A clinical or patient safety incident is any unintended or unexpected event that could have harmed, or did harm, a person receiving care.
  20. Raising concerns
    Raising concerns about care that puts people at risk is a professional duty under the NMC Code.
  21. Whistleblowing
    Whistleblowing is making a disclosure in the public interest about wrongdoing, such as risks to patient safety, in a way that is protected by law.
  22. Professional boundaries
    Professional boundaries are the limits that keep the relationship between a nurse and the people in their care safe, therapeutic and focused on the person's needs.
  23. Ethical dilemmas
    Ethical dilemmas arise when values, duties or principles conflict and there is no clearly right answer.
  24. End-of-life law
    The law at the end of life covers decisions to withhold or withdraw treatment, the limits on hastening death, and what must happen after death.
  25. Advance decisions
    Advance decisions let people refuse specified medical treatment in advance, for a time when they lack capacity.
  26. Best interests
    When a person lacks capacity to make a decision, any decision made for them must be in their best interests.
  27. Professional misconduct
    Misconduct is a serious departure from the standards expected of a registered professional.
  28. Regulatory investigations
    When a concern about a nurse is raised with the NMC, it follows a defined fitness to practise process.

Key terms

Nursing and Midwifery Order 2001
The legislation establishing the NMC and its powers
Fitness to practise
Having the skills, knowledge, health and character to practise safely and effectively
Duty of care
A legal obligation to take reasonable care to avoid causing foreseeable harm
Material risk
A risk a reasonable person in the patient's position, or this particular patient, would attach significance to
Capacity
The ability to make a particular decision at the time it needs to be made
Section 5(4)
The nurse's holding power, allowing a qualified mental health or learning disability nurse to detain an informal inpatient for up to six hours
Adult at risk
An adult with care and support needs who is experiencing or at risk of abuse or neglect and can't protect themselves
Significant harm
The threshold under the Children Act 1989 for compulsory intervention to protect a child
Acid test
Continuous supervision and control, and not free to leave: the Supreme Court's test for deprivation of liberty
Absolute right
A right that can never be limited, such as freedom from torture or degrading treatment
Protected characteristic
A characteristic protected from discrimination under the Equality Act 2010, such as age, disability or race
Special category data
Personal data needing extra protection, including health, ethnicity and religion
Common law duty of confidentiality
The legal duty not to disclose information given in confidence without a lawful basis
Information governance
The framework ensuring information is handled legally, securely, efficiently and effectively
