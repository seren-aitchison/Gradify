Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

Which NEWS2 total triggers an emergency response?

7 or more. From “Adult health assessment” →

In plain English: Adult health assessment brings together history, observation, examination and validated tools to build a full picture of an adult's health and needs. Key idea: Combine history, NEWS2 observations, systematic examination and risk tools; escalate by NEWS2 thresholds and clinical concern; adapt assessment to the person.

How long must a seizure last to be treated as status epilepticus?

Five minutes or more, or repeated seizures without recovery. From “Neurological nursing” →

In plain English: Neurological nursing cares for adults with conditions of the brain, spinal cord and nerves, such as stroke, head injury, epilepsy, Parkinson's disease and multiple sclerosis. Key idea: Record GCS components, pupils and limb power, escalate a fall of two points or new signs, act fast on stroke and long seizures, and give time-critical medicines on time.

When should observations be taken during a transfusion?

Before starting, 15 minutes after starting each unit, at the end, and whenever the patient feels unwell. From “Haematology nursing” →

In plain English: Haematology nursing cares for adults with blood disorders such as anaemia, sickle cell disease, bleeding and clotting disorders, and blood cancers. Key idea: Transfuse safely with positive ID, bedside checks and observations; stop and escalate if a reaction occurs; treat sickle cell pain promptly and respectfully.

Give two frailty syndromes.

Falls, immobility, delirium, incontinence or susceptibility to medicine side effects. From “Frailty” →

In plain English: Frailty is a state of reduced physiological reserve that makes people vulnerable to sudden, disproportionate declines after minor events. Key idea: Frailty is reduced reserve, identified with tools like the Clinical Frailty Scale; care should be proportionate, comprehensive and centred on the person's goals.

What is multimodal analgesia?

Combining analgesics with different mechanisms to improve pain relief and reduce side effects. From “Postoperative care” →

In plain English: Postoperative care spans the recovery room to discharge and beyond. Key idea: Get a structured handover, monitor closely, use multimodal analgesia safely, prevent respiratory, VTE, infection and other complications, and work towards recovery milestones.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Adult Nursing

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. What does a NEWS2 score of 3 in a single parameter usually trigger? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    An urgent clinical review. A single red score of 3 needs an urgent response under the RCP guidance.

  2. Which is a common presentation on an acute medical unit? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Breathlessness. Breathlessness, chest pain, falls, confusion and infection are common acute medical presentations.

  3. What is the usual minimum fasting time for clear fluids before elective anaesthesia in adults? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    About 2 hours. Clear fluids are usually allowed until about two hours before anaesthesia, following local guidance.

  4. A patient's NEWS2 total is 7. What does this require? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    An emergency response, usually from a team with critical care skills. A total of 7 or more triggers an emergency assessment.

  5. An 84-year-old admitted with pneumonia has stayed in bed for four days. Which risk should the nurse address? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Deconditioning, falls and pressure damage. Prolonged bed rest increases deconditioning, pressure damage, VTE and falls.

  6. Which bundle of measures helps prevent delirium on a medical ward? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Orientation, hydration, sleep, mobilisation and reviewing medicines. Multicomponent interventions reduce delirium risk.

  7. An adult with a learning disability is more agitated than usual and holding his abdomen. Staff say "that's just his behaviour." What should the nurse do? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Assess for a physical cause with his carers and escalate. Behaviour change can be a sign of pain or illness; avoiding diagnostic overshadowing saves lives.

  8. On day four after bowel surgery, a patient develops increasing abdominal pain, fever and a heart rate of 115, having been recovering well. What should the nurse suspect and do? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    A possible anastomotic leak or sepsis: escalate urgently. New pain, fever and tachycardia after bowel surgery can signal a leak, which is a surgical emergency.

  9. A patient on a cardiac monitor develops a heart rate of 36, becomes dizzy and pale, and has a blood pressure of 78/40. What should the nurse do? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Call for urgent help: bradycardia with shock. Bradycardia with shock is an emergency covered by the peri-arrest algorithm.

Key takeaways

  • Combine history, NEWS2 observations, systematic examination and risk tools; escalate by NEWS2 thresholds and clinical concern; adapt assessment to the person.
  • Medical nursing combines close monitoring and escalation with preventing hospital-acquired harm and early discharge planning.
  • Prepare patients safely with checklists and appropriate fasting, use enhanced recovery principles, and monitor closely to catch post-operative complications early.
  • Act fast on chest pain with an ECG and the pathway; monitor heart failure with daily weights and electrolytes; teach self-management and secondary prevention.
  • Know the target saturation, manage breathlessness with positioning, oxygen and technique, escalate life-threatening features, and support self-management and rehabilitation.
  • Record GCS components, pupils and limb power, escalate a fall of two points or new signs, act fast on stroke and long seizures, and give time-critical medicines on time.

Common mistakes in Adult Nursing

  • Recording observations without acting on the score. A NEWS2 of 5 or a single red score of 3 needs urgent review.
  • Focusing only on the medical diagnosis. Delirium, falls and deconditioning often decide how a medical patient's stay ends.
  • Fasting patients from midnight regardless of operation time. Prolonged fasting causes dehydration and distress; follow current fasting guidance.
  • Giving high-flow oxygen to every patient with chest pain. Oxygen is given only if saturation is below target.
  • Driving a nebuliser with high-flow oxygen in a patient with COPD and hypercapnia. Use air with separate controlled oxygen as prescribed.
  • Giving a drink to a patient with suspected stroke before a swallow screen. Aspiration is a major risk.

Lessons in this school

  1. Adult health assessment
    Adult health assessment brings together history, observation, examination and validated tools to build a full picture of an adult's health and needs.
  2. Medical nursing
    Medical nursing cares for adults with acute and chronic medical conditions, such as heart failure, COPD exacerbations, infections, stroke and diabetes.
  3. Surgical nursing
    Surgical nursing cares for adults before, during and after surgery.
  4. Cardiovascular nursing
    Cardiovascular nursing cares for adults with heart and circulatory conditions, from acute coronary syndromes to heart failure and arrhythmias.
  5. Respiratory nursing
    Respiratory nursing supports adults with acute and long-term lung conditions such as COPD, asthma, pneumonia and lung cancer.
  6. Neurological nursing
    Neurological nursing cares for adults with conditions of the brain, spinal cord and nerves, such as stroke, head injury, epilepsy, Parkinson's disease and multiple sclerosis.
  7. Gastrointestinal nursing
    Gastrointestinal nursing cares for adults with conditions of the gut, liver and pancreas, such as GI bleeding, inflammatory bowel disease, liver disease and pancreatitis.
  8. Renal nursing
    Renal nursing cares for adults with acute kidney injury and chronic kidney disease, including those on dialysis or with a kidney transplant.
  9. Endocrine nursing
    Endocrine nursing cares for adults with hormone disorders such as thyroid disease, adrenal disorders and pituitary conditions.
  10. Diabetes care
    Diabetes affects millions of adults in the UK, and people with diabetes are frequent users of every health service.
  11. Oncology nursing
    Oncology nursing supports adults through cancer diagnosis, treatment and beyond.
  12. Haematology nursing
    Haematology nursing cares for adults with blood disorders such as anaemia, sickle cell disease, bleeding and clotting disorders, and blood cancers.
  13. Musculoskeletal nursing
    Musculoskeletal nursing cares for adults with bone, joint and muscle conditions, including fractures, joint replacements and arthritis.
  14. Dermatology nursing
    Dermatology nursing cares for adults with skin conditions, from eczema and psoriasis to leg ulcers, cellulitis and serious drug reactions.
  15. Infection and infectious disease
    Adult nurses care for people with a wide range of infections, from urinary and chest infections to HIV, tuberculosis and imported infections.
  16. Older adult care
    Older adults make up most hospital inpatients and many community caseloads.
  17. Frailty
    Frailty is a state of reduced physiological reserve that makes people vulnerable to sudden, disproportionate declines after minor events.
  18. Rehabilitation
    Rehabilitation in adult nursing helps people regain function after acute illness, surgery or injury, and adapt to long-term conditions.
  19. Long-term conditions
    Long-term conditions are health problems that can't currently be cured but can be managed, such as diabetes, COPD, heart failure, arthritis and chronic kidney disease.
  20. Multimorbidity
    Multimorbidity is living with two or more long-term conditions.
  21. Palliative care
    Palliative care improves the quality of life of people with life-limiting illness and their families, by relieving symptoms and supporting physical, psychological, social and spiritual needs.
  22. End-of-life care
    End-of-life care in adult nursing covers the last weeks and days of life, the moment of death and care afterwards.
  23. Postoperative care
    Postoperative care spans the recovery room to discharge and beyond.
  24. Discharge planning
    Discharge planning for adults coordinates the move from hospital to home or another setting, so the person leaves at the right time with the right support.
  25. Community adult nursing
    Community adult nurses care for people in their own homes, care homes and clinics.
  26. Law and professional considerations in adult nursing
    Adult nurses regularly face legal and professional questions, especially when caring for older people, people who lack capacity and people at risk of harm.
  27. Preparing for an adult nursing placement
    Practice placements are where student nurses learn to apply knowledge, develop skills and demonstrate proficiency.
  28. Reflective learning and case studies in adult nursing
    Reflection turns experience into learning.

Adult Nursing: questions students ask

What is diagnostic overshadowing?

Attributing symptoms to an existing disability or diagnosis instead of investigating them.

What is a board round?

A short daily team meeting reviewing each patient's plan and expected discharge.

What are the three stages of the WHO Surgical Safety Checklist?

Sign in, time out and sign out.

Why are daily weights important in heart failure?

A rapid weight gain shows fluid retention, often before symptoms worsen.
