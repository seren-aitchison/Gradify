Which NEWS2 total triggers an emergency response?

How long must a seizure last to be treated as status epilepticus?

Five minutes or more, or repeated seizures without recovery. From “Neurological nursing” →

When should observations be taken during a transfusion?

Before starting, 15 minutes after starting each unit, at the end, and whenever the patient feels unwell. From “Haematology nursing” →

Give two frailty syndromes.

Lessons in this school

  1. Adult health assessment
    Adult health assessment brings together history, observation, examination and validated tools to build a full picture of an adult's health and needs.
  2. Medical nursing
    Medical nursing cares for adults with acute and chronic medical conditions, such as heart failure, COPD exacerbations, infections, stroke and diabetes.
  3. Surgical nursing
    Surgical nursing cares for adults before, during and after surgery.
  4. Cardiovascular nursing
    Cardiovascular nursing cares for adults with heart and circulatory conditions, from acute coronary syndromes to heart failure and arrhythmias.
  5. Respiratory nursing
    Respiratory nursing supports adults with acute and long-term lung conditions such as COPD, asthma, pneumonia and lung cancer.
  6. Neurological nursing
    Neurological nursing cares for adults with conditions of the brain, spinal cord and nerves, such as stroke, head injury, epilepsy, Parkinson's disease and multiple sclerosis.
  7. Gastrointestinal nursing
    Gastrointestinal nursing cares for adults with conditions of the gut, liver and pancreas, such as GI bleeding, inflammatory bowel disease, liver disease and pancreatitis.
  8. Renal nursing
    Renal nursing cares for adults with acute kidney injury and chronic kidney disease, including those on dialysis or with a kidney transplant.
  9. Endocrine nursing
    Endocrine nursing cares for adults with hormone disorders such as thyroid disease, adrenal disorders and pituitary conditions.
  10. Diabetes care
    Diabetes affects millions of adults in the UK, and people with diabetes are frequent users of every health service.
  11. Oncology nursing
    Oncology nursing supports adults through cancer diagnosis, treatment and beyond.
  12. Haematology nursing
    Haematology nursing cares for adults with blood disorders such as anaemia, sickle cell disease, bleeding and clotting disorders, and blood cancers.
  13. Musculoskeletal nursing
    Musculoskeletal nursing cares for adults with bone, joint and muscle conditions, including fractures, joint replacements and arthritis.
  14. Dermatology nursing
    Dermatology nursing cares for adults with skin conditions, from eczema and psoriasis to leg ulcers, cellulitis and serious drug reactions.
  15. Infection and infectious disease
    Adult nurses care for people with a wide range of infections, from urinary and chest infections to HIV, tuberculosis and imported infections.
  16. Older adult care
    Older adults make up most hospital inpatients and many community caseloads.
  17. Frailty
    Frailty is a state of reduced physiological reserve that makes people vulnerable to sudden, disproportionate declines after minor events.
  18. Rehabilitation
    Rehabilitation in adult nursing helps people regain function after acute illness, surgery or injury, and adapt to long-term conditions.
  19. Long-term conditions
    Long-term conditions are health problems that can't currently be cured but can be managed, such as diabetes, COPD, heart failure, arthritis and chronic kidney disease.
  20. Multimorbidity
    Multimorbidity is living with two or more long-term conditions.
  21. Palliative care
    Palliative care improves the quality of life of people with life-limiting illness and their families, by relieving symptoms and supporting physical, psychological, social and spiritual needs.
  22. End-of-life care
    End-of-life care in adult nursing covers the last weeks and days of life, the moment of death and care afterwards.
  23. Postoperative care
    Postoperative care spans the recovery room to discharge and beyond.
  24. Discharge planning
    Discharge planning for adults coordinates the move from hospital to home or another setting, so the person leaves at the right time with the right support.
  25. Community adult nursing
    Community adult nurses care for people in their own homes, care homes and clinics.
  26. Law and professional considerations in adult nursing
    Adult nurses regularly face legal and professional questions, especially when caring for older people, people who lack capacity and people at risk of harm.
  27. Preparing for an adult nursing placement
    Practice placements are where student nurses learn to apply knowledge, develop skills and demonstrate proficiency.
  28. Reflective learning and case studies in adult nursing
    Reflection turns experience into learning.

Key terms

NEWS2
The National Early Warning Score 2, used to detect and respond to deterioration in adults
Acute medical unit
A unit receiving and assessing adults with acute medical illness, usually for a short stay
Enhanced recovery after surgery
An evidence-based programme of perioperative care to speed recovery and reduce complications
Acute coronary syndrome
A spectrum of conditions caused by sudden reduced blood flow to the heart, including unstable angina and myocardial infarction
Non-invasive ventilation
Ventilatory support through a mask, used for hypercapnic respiratory failure
Glasgow Coma Scale
A 3–15 scale assessing eye opening, verbal and motor responses
Haematemesis
Vomiting blood, fresh or like coffee grounds
Acute kidney injury
A sudden fall in kidney function, shown by rising creatinine or falling urine output
Adrenal crisis
Life-threatening cortisol deficiency causing shock, needing immediate hydrocortisone
Hypoglycaemia
Blood glucose below 4.0 mmol/L in a person on glucose-lowering treatment
Neutropenic sepsis
Sepsis in a person with a low neutrophil count, usually after chemotherapy; a medical emergency
Positive patient identification
Actively confirming a person's identity against their ID band and documentation at each step
Neurovascular observations
Checks of colour, warmth, capillary refill, pulses, sensation and movement in a limb
Emollient
A moisturising treatment that soothes and protects dry skin
