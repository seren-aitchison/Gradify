Lessons in this school

  1. Research literacy
    Research literacy is the ability to find, understand, judge and use research.
  2. Quantitative research
    Quantitative research uses numerical data to measure, compare and test relationships.
  3. Qualitative research
    Qualitative research explores people's experiences, meanings and perspectives in depth, using words rather than numbers.
  4. Mixed methods
    Mixed methods research combines quantitative and qualitative approaches in one study, so that each answers questions the other can't.
  5. Study design
    The design of a study determines what questions it can answer and how much its results can be trusted.
  6. Clinical trials
    Clinical trials test whether interventions work and are safe.
  7. Cohort studies
    Cohort studies follow groups of people over time to see who develops an outcome, comparing those with and without an exposure.
  8. Case-control studies
    Case-control studies compare people who have a condition (cases) with people who don't (controls), looking back at their past exposures.
  9. Systematic reviews
    A systematic review uses explicit, reproducible methods to find, appraise and synthesise all relevant studies on a question.
  10. Meta-analysis
    Meta-analysis statistically combines the results of several studies to give a more precise overall estimate of effect.
  11. Critical appraisal
    Critical appraisal is the systematic assessment of a study's trustworthiness, results and relevance.
  12. Research ethics
    Research ethics protects the rights, safety, dignity and wellbeing of research participants.
  13. Informed consent in research
    Informed consent is the foundation of ethical research.
  14. Evidence-based practice
    Evidence-based practice integrates research evidence, clinical expertise and the person's preferences.
  15. Clinical guidelines
    Clinical guidelines are systematically developed recommendations that help professionals and patients make decisions about care.
  16. Quality improvement
    Quality improvement (QI) uses systematic methods to make care safer, more effective, more person-centred and more efficient.
  17. Clinical audit
    Clinical audit is a quality improvement process that measures care against explicit standards and makes changes to close the gap.
  18. Patient safety
    Patient safety is the avoidance of unintended or unexpected harm to people during healthcare.
  19. Human factors
    Human factors (ergonomics) is the science of designing systems, equipment and tasks around how people actually work.
  20. Leadership
    Leadership is about influencing and supporting others towards shared goals.
  21. Management
    Management is about planning, organising and controlling resources to deliver safe, effective care.
  22. Teamwork
    Healthcare is delivered by teams, and how well they work together affects safety and outcomes.
  23. Delegation
    Delegation transfers responsibility for a task to another person while the delegator stays accountable for the decision to delegate.
  24. Supervision
    Clinical supervision and preceptorship support practitioners to reflect, learn and stay well.
  25. Service improvement
    Service improvement means redesigning how care is delivered so it works better for patients and staff.
  26. Clinical governance
    Clinical governance is the framework through which healthcare organisations are accountable for continuously improving quality and safeguarding high standards of care.
  27. Risk management
    Risk management is the systematic identification, assessment and control of risks to patients, staff and organisations.
  28. Healthcare policy
    Health policy shapes what services exist, how they are funded and organised, and what nurses are expected to do.
  29. Digital health
    Digital health uses technology such as electronic records, apps, remote monitoring and artificial intelligence to improve care.
  30. Health informatics
    Health informatics is the use of information, data and technology to improve health and care.

Key terms

PICO
Population, intervention, comparison, outcome: a framework for focused clinical questions
Reliability
The consistency of a measurement
Phenomenology
A qualitative approach exploring people's lived experience
Mixed methods
Research combining quantitative and qualitative approaches in one study
Quasi-experimental design
A study testing an intervention without random allocation
Allocation concealment
Preventing those recruiting from knowing the next allocation
Prospective cohort study
A study following people forward in time from exposure to outcome
Case-control study
A study comparing past exposures in people with and without a condition
Systematic review
A review using explicit, reproducible methods to find, appraise and synthesise all relevant studies
Forest plot
A graph showing each study's effect and confidence interval and the pooled estimate
Critical appraisal
Systematically assessing a study's trustworthiness, results and relevance
Declaration of Helsinki
The World Medical Association's ethical principles for medical research involving humans
Participant information sheet
A plain-language document explaining a study to potential participants
The 5 As
Ask, acquire, appraise, apply and assess: the steps of evidence-based practice
