Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What is the stress-vulnerability model?

A model in which mental illness occurs when stress exceeds a person's vulnerability threshold. From “Foundations of mental health nursing” →

In plain English: Mental health nursing supports people experiencing mental distress and illness to recover, stay safe and live meaningful lives. Key idea: Mental health nursing combines therapeutic relationships, recovery and rights, understands distress through biopsychosocial and stress-vulnerability models, and challenges stigma and inequality.

What are the two core symptoms of depression?

Persistently low mood and loss of interest or pleasure (anhedonia). From “Depression” →

In plain English: Depression is one of the most common mental health conditions. Key idea: Recognise core and associated symptoms, support stepped care including therapies and antidepressants, and focus nursing on risk, activity, physical health and hope.

Why does smoking status matter for clozapine?

Starting or stopping smoking changes clozapine levels, risking toxicity or relapse. From “Schizophrenia” →

In plain English: Schizophrenia is a long-term mental illness involving episodes of psychosis and often ongoing negative and cognitive symptoms. Key idea: Support antipsychotic treatment while watching for side effects and NMS, follow clozapine monitoring and constipation and smoking precautions, and prioritise physical health and recovery.

What does NHS 111 option 2 provide in England?

A route to local 24/7 mental health crisis support. From “Crisis intervention” →

In plain English: A mental health crisis is when a person feels they can't cope or are at risk, and needs urgent help. Key idea: Know local crisis pathways, use crisis intervention principles to stabilise and plan, and understand the role of section 136 and places of safety.

What is a recovery college?

A college offering courses co-produced and co-delivered by people with lived experience and professionals. From “Recovery-oriented practice” →

In plain English: Recovery in mental health means living a satisfying, hopeful and contributing life, even with ongoing symptoms. Key idea: Recovery is personal and hopeful; use CHIME, strengths-based planning, peer support, social inclusion, recovery colleges and WRAP.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Mental Health Nursing

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. What is the biopsychosocial model? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Health explained by interacting biological, psychological and social factors. It integrates biological, psychological and social influences on health.

  2. Which tool screens for depression? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    PHQ-9. The PHQ-9 is a widely used depression questionnaire.

  3. What is a hallucination? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    A perception without an external stimulus. Hallucinations are perceptions, such as voices, without a stimulus.

  4. Why do people with serious mental illness die earlier than average? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Mostly from preventable physical illnesses. Physical illnesses such as heart disease are the main cause, which is why physical health checks matter.

  5. Which is respectful, person-first language? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    A person with schizophrenia. Person-first language reduces stigma and respects identity.

  6. Why should physical health be assessed in a mental health assessment? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Physical illness can cause or mimic mental health symptoms. Conditions such as thyroid disease or delirium can present as mental health problems.

  7. A person says, "Part of me wants to stop drinking, but it's the only thing that helps me sleep." What motivational interviewing approach fits best? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Reflect the ambivalence and explore their own reasons for change. Reflecting ambivalence and eliciting change talk strengthens their motivation.

  8. A man with severe depression suddenly seems calm and gives away his possessions after starting treatment. What should the nurse consider? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Increased suicide risk: explore his thoughts and escalate. Sudden calm and giving away possessions can signal suicidal planning.

  9. A woman of childbearing age with bipolar disorder is prescribed valproate. What should the nurse check? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    That the national pregnancy prevention requirements are in place. Valproate has strict safety rules because of the risk to unborn babies.

Key takeaways

  • Mental health nursing combines therapeutic relationships, recovery and rights, understands distress through biopsychosocial and stress-vulnerability models, and challenges stigma and inequality.
  • Assess holistically and collaboratively, use tools wisely, include physical health and strengths, and build a shared formulation.
  • The MSE describes appearance, speech, mood, affect, thought, perception, cognition and insight at that moment; record observations objectively, using the person's words.
  • Build relationships through Peplau's phases and Rogers' core conditions, be reliable and honest, manage power and boundaries, repair ruptures and use supervision.
  • Use OARS and validation, communicate calmly and honestly in psychosis and distress, ask directly about suicide, and use motivational interviewing to support change.
  • Recognise trauma's impact, apply safety, trust, choice, collaboration and empowerment, and avoid re-traumatising people, especially through restrictive practices.

Common mistakes in Mental Health Nursing

  • Using labels such as "a schizophrenic" or "a self-harmer". Use person-first, respectful language.
  • Assuming new confusion or hallucinations are psychiatric without checking physical causes. Infection, delirium or other illness may be responsible.
  • Recording conclusions such as "psychotic" instead of describing what was observed. Describe specific behaviours and statements.
  • Promising to always be available. It sets up disappointment; be reliable about what you can offer and plan endings.
  • Arguing with a person about their delusional beliefs. Acknowledge their experience and feelings instead.
  • Insisting a person describes their trauma in detail at assessment. Ask sensitively and only when they're ready.

Mental Health Nursing mind map

Lessons in this school

  1. Foundations of mental health nursing
    Mental health nursing supports people experiencing mental distress and illness to recover, stay safe and live meaningful lives.
  2. Mental health assessment
    Mental health assessment gathers information about a person's mental state, history, risks, strengths and circumstances to understand their difficulties and plan care with them.
  3. Mental state examination
    The mental state examination (MSE) is a structured description of a person's mental state at the time of assessment.
  4. Therapeutic relationships
    The therapeutic relationship is the foundation of mental health nursing.
  5. Communication
    Communication in mental health nursing is a therapeutic intervention in itself.
  6. Trauma-informed care
    Many people who use mental health services have experienced trauma, such as abuse, violence or neglect.
  7. Depression
    Depression is one of the most common mental health conditions.
  8. Bipolar disorder
    Bipolar disorder involves episodes of depression and of mania or hypomania, with periods of stability between.
  9. Anxiety disorders
    Anxiety disorders are the most common mental health conditions.
  10. OCD
    Obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD) involves intrusive, distressing obsessions and repetitive compulsions performed to reduce anxiety.
  11. PTSD
    Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) can develop after experiencing or witnessing traumatic events.
  12. Psychosis
    Psychosis is a state in which a person's perception of reality is altered, with hallucinations, delusions or disorganised thinking.
  13. Schizophrenia
    Schizophrenia is a long-term mental illness involving episodes of psychosis and often ongoing negative and cognitive symptoms.
  14. Personality disorders
    Personality disorders describe long-standing patterns of thinking, feeling and relating that cause significant distress and difficulty.
  15. Eating disorders
    Eating disorders are serious mental illnesses with high physical risk; anorexia nervosa has one of the highest mortality rates of any mental illness.
  16. Substance misuse
    Substance misuse involves harmful use of alcohol, drugs or medicines.
  17. Addiction
    Addiction is a chronic, relapsing condition in which a person continues to use a substance or behaviour despite harm.
  18. Self-harm
    Self-harm is any act of self-poisoning or self-injury, whatever the motivation.
  19. Suicide risk assessment
    Suicide is a leading cause of death, especially among men and young people.
  20. Crisis intervention
    A mental health crisis is when a person feels they can't cope or are at risk, and needs urgent help.
  21. Psychiatric medication
    Medicines play an important role in mental health care, alongside psychological and social approaches.
  22. Mental Health Act
    The Mental Health Act 1983 (as amended) shapes daily practice for mental health nurses in England and Wales.
  23. Mental Capacity Act
    The Mental Capacity Act 2005 applies to mental health care as it does elsewhere, but its interaction with the Mental Health Act and with fluctuating capacity raises particular questions.
  24. Safeguarding
    People who use mental health services are at higher risk of abuse, exploitation and neglect, and may also pose risks to others, including children in their family.
  25. De-escalation
    De-escalation uses communication and other approaches to reduce agitation and aggression and prevent harm.
  26. Recovery-oriented practice
    Recovery in mental health means living a satisfying, hopeful and contributing life, even with ongoing symptoms.
  27. Community mental health
    Most mental health care happens in the community.
  28. Inpatient mental health care
    Inpatient mental health wards care for people who need more intensive support and safety than the community can provide.
  29. Forensic mental health
    Forensic mental health services care for people with mental illness who have been in contact with the criminal justice system or who pose a risk of serious harm to others.
  30. Older adult mental health
    Older people experience mental health problems such as depression, anxiety, dementia and delirium, often alongside physical illness.
  31. Preparing for a mental health nursing placement
    Mental health placements offer rich learning in therapeutic relationships, assessment and recovery-focused care.
  32. Reflective learning and case studies in mental health nursing
    Reflection is especially important in mental health nursing, where relationships, emotions and ethical tensions are central.
