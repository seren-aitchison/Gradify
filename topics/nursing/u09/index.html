Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What are the three levels of situational awareness?

Perceiving what is happening, understanding what it means, and anticipating what will happen next. From “Acute nursing principles” →

In plain English: Acute nursing is the care of people whose condition is unstable or could become unstable quickly. Key idea: Acute nursing means anticipating risk, assessing systematically, reassessing, escalating early and working as a team, supported by human factors and systems such as the chain of prevention.

What is source control in sepsis?

Removing or draining the source of infection, such as an abscess or infected line. From “Sepsis” →

In plain English: Sepsis is a life-threatening response to infection and a leading cause of avoidable death. Key idea: Stratify sepsis risk using NEWS2, give time-critical treatment (antibiotics within one hour for the highest risk), achieve source control and recognise septic shock early.

Name four life-threatening causes of chest pain.

Acute coronary syndrome, aortic dissection, pulmonary embolism and tension pneumothorax (also tamponade and oesophageal rupture). From “Chest pain” →

In plain English: Chest pain is one of the most common reasons for emergency attendance. Key idea: Consider the serious causes first; record an ECG promptly, take a careful history, recognise red flags for ACS, dissection, PE and pneumothorax, and escalate.

What is the standard UK hospital emergency number?

2222. From “Emergency escalation” →

In plain English: Emergency escalation is calling for urgent help when a patient is critically ill or arrests. Key idea: Know 2222 in hospital and 999 in the community, communicate with SBAR and closed loops, prepare equipment and documents, and know the patient's treatment escalation plan.

What are signs of local anaesthetic toxicity?

Tingling around the mouth, tinnitus, confusion, seizures and arrhythmias. From “Postoperative monitoring” →

In plain English: Higher-risk patients after surgery often need enhanced monitoring in recovery, post-anaesthesia care, high dependency or enhanced care units. Key idea: Higher-risk surgical patients need enhanced monitoring of arterial and central lines, urine output and lactate; monitor epidurals closely and act on hypotension, high block, toxicity and new leg weakness.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Acute, Emergency and Critical Care Nursing

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which is a principle of acute nursing? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Escalating early, before the patient is in extremis. Early escalation is central to preventing harm.

  2. Which observation is often the most sensitive early sign of deterioration? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Respiratory rate. Respiratory rate changes early in many acute illnesses.

  3. What is the first priority in an ABCDE assessment? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Airway. The airway is assessed first because obstruction kills fastest.

  4. What is situational awareness? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Perceiving, understanding and anticipating what is happening. It describes understanding a situation and predicting what comes next.

  5. A nurse is absorbed in setting up a pump while a nearby patient becomes quiet and grey. Which human factor is at play? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Task fixation reducing situational awareness. Fixation on a task can hide changes; regular scanning helps.

  6. A patient's heart rate has risen steadily from 82 to 108 over 12 hours, each value scoring low. What should the nurse do? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Recognise the trend as possible deterioration and review or escalate. Trends reveal deterioration that single values may hide.

  7. A patient has a snoring noise when breathing and is drowsy. What should the nurse do first? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Open the airway and call for help. Snoring suggests partial airway obstruction, the most urgent problem.

  8. A patient on 2 L/min oxygen has a respiratory rate of 22, SpO2 95%, pulse 105, BP 118/70, alert and temperature 37.9°C. What is the likely response level? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Medium: urgent review, hourly obs. Oxygen scores 2, RR 22 scores 2, SpO2 95% scores 1 and pulse 105 scores 1 (BP, consciousness and a temperature of 37.9°C score 0): a total of 6.

  9. Despite 2 litres of IV fluid, a septic patient's mean arterial pressure stays at 58 mmHg and lactate is 5. What does this indicate? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Septic shock: escalate urgently. Persistent hypotension with high lactate despite fluids indicates septic shock.

Key takeaways

  • Acute nursing means anticipating risk, assessing systematically, reassessing, escalating early and working as a team, supported by human factors and systems such as the chain of prevention.
  • Look for rising respiratory rate, new confusion and soft signs, avoid normalising abnormal values, look at trends, and act on concerns from patients, families and colleagues.
  • Assess A to E in order, treat problems as you find them, reassess often, check glucose in D, and call for help early.
  • Early warning scores track and trigger responses using population-specific tools; they need accurate observations and reliable responses, and clinical concern always overrides the score.
  • NEWS2 scores seven parameters; a red single score of 3 or a total of 5–6 needs urgent review and hourly observations, and 7 or more needs an emergency response and continuous monitoring.
  • Stratify sepsis risk using NEWS2, give time-critical treatment (antibiotics within one hour for the highest risk), achieve source control and recognise septic shock early.

Common mistakes in Acute, Emergency and Critical Care Nursing

  • Fixating on one task while a patient's condition changes around you. Step back regularly and reassess the whole picture.
  • Dismissing a high respiratory rate because the patient "always breathes fast". Changes from baseline matter; never normalise without review.
  • Moving on to breathing while an airway problem is untreated. Treat each problem before moving to the next.
  • Using NEWS2 for a pregnant woman. Use the maternity early warning score, which accounts for the physiological changes of pregnancy.
  • Forgetting that supplemental oxygen adds 2 points. A patient on oxygen with otherwise normal observations still scores.
  • Delaying antibiotics in a high-risk patient while waiting for blood culture results. Take cultures quickly, but don't delay antibiotics.

Lessons in this school

  1. Acute nursing principles
    Acute nursing is the care of people whose condition is unstable or could become unstable quickly.
  2. Recognition of deterioration
    Patients usually show signs of deterioration hours before a cardiac arrest or critical care admission.
  3. ABCDE assessment
    The ABCDE approach is a systematic way to assess and treat an acutely unwell patient, dealing with the most life-threatening problems first.
  4. Early warning scores
    Early warning scores turn routine observations into a score that signals when a patient needs more attention.
  5. NEWS2
    NEWS2 is the national early warning score for adults in the UK.
  6. Sepsis
    Sepsis is a life-threatening response to infection and a leading cause of avoidable death.
  7. Shock
    Shock is a life-threatening failure of the circulation to deliver enough oxygen to the tissues.
  8. Acute respiratory failure
    Acute respiratory failure occurs when the lungs can't maintain adequate oxygen or remove enough carbon dioxide.
  9. Acute cardiac conditions
    Acute cardiac conditions such as acute coronary syndromes, acute heart failure and dangerous arrhythmias can be rapidly fatal.
  10. Acute neurological conditions
    Acute neurological emergencies, such as status epilepticus, raised intracranial pressure, meningitis and spinal cord compression, can cause permanent harm or death within hours.
  11. Chest pain
    Chest pain is one of the most common reasons for emergency attendance.
  12. Stroke recognition
    Stroke is a medical emergency in which time lost is brain lost.
  13. Acute kidney injury
    Acute kidney injury (AKI) is a sudden fall in kidney function, common in acutely ill patients and associated with serious complications and death.
  14. Fluid resuscitation principles
    Fluid resuscitation restores circulating volume in patients who are hypovolaemic or in shock.
  15. Oxygenation and ventilation
    Oxygen is a medicine that must be prescribed and given to a target.
  16. Emergency escalation
    Emergency escalation is calling for urgent help when a patient is critically ill or arrests.
  17. Cardiac arrest
    Cardiac arrest is the sudden loss of effective heart function.
  18. Resuscitation
    Resuscitation includes the whole process around a cardiac arrest: preparation, decision-making, care after the return of circulation, support for families and staff, and learning from events.
  19. Critical care fundamentals
    Critical care provides close monitoring and organ support for people with life-threatening illness.
  20. Intensive care
    Intensive care nursing provides continuous, highly skilled care to critically ill patients.
  21. Postoperative monitoring
    Higher-risk patients after surgery often need enhanced monitoring in recovery, post-anaesthesia care, high dependency or enhanced care units.
  22. Trauma
    Major trauma is a leading cause of death and disability, especially in younger adults, and increasingly in older people after falls.
  23. Emergency triage
    Triage sorts patients by clinical urgency so that the sickest are seen first.
  24. Major incident response
    A major incident is an event that overwhelms the normal capacity of emergency services and hospitals, such as a terrorist attack, transport crash or chemical release.
  25. Deteriorating patient scenarios
    Bringing together recognition, assessment, escalation and treatment, these scenarios show how acute care principles apply in practice.

Acute, Emergency and Critical Care Nursing: questions students ask

Which vital sign is often the earliest indicator of deterioration?

A rising respiratory rate.

Why should blood glucose be checked during the D assessment?

Hypoglycaemia can cause reduced consciousness and is quickly treatable.

What does "track and trigger" mean?

Tracking observations over time and triggering set responses at defined thresholds.

What monitoring is recommended for a NEWS2 total of 7 or more?

Continuous monitoring with an emergency response.
