Five sample flashcards

Name the WHO five moments for hand hygiene.

Before touching a patient; before a clean or aseptic procedure; after body fluid exposure risk; after touching a patient; after touching a patient's surroundings. From “Hand hygiene” →

What is the most effective way to prevent CAUTI?

Avoid unnecessary catheters and remove them as soon as they are no longer needed. From “Catheter care” →

Why use a mouthpiece rather than a mask where possible?

Better delivery to the lungs and less medicine deposited on the face and eyes. From “Nebuliser administration principles” →

Name three time-critical medicines.

Parkinson's medicines, insulin, anticonvulsants, some antimicrobials (any three). From “Medication administration” →

Name three things you can assess during personal care.

Skin and pressure areas, mobility, pain, breathing, mood and cognition, signs of neglect or abuse (any three). From “Personal care” →

Lessons in this school

  1. Hand hygiene
    Hand hygiene is the single most effective way to prevent the spread of infection in health and social care.
  2. Standard infection precautions
    Standard infection control precautions (SICPs) are the basic practices used by all staff, in all settings, with every person, whether or not an infection is known.
  3. Personal protective equipment
    Personal protective equipment (PPE) protects staff and patients from exposure to infection and hazardous substances.
  4. Aseptic technique
    Aseptic technique prevents micro-organisms being introduced into susceptible sites such as wounds, the bloodstream and the urinary tract during clinical procedures.
  5. Wound assessment
    Wound assessment is the systematic evaluation of a wound and the person who has it, so that the right care can be planned, progress monitored and complications spotted early.
  6. Wound dressing principles
    Dressings protect wounds and create conditions for healing.
  7. Pressure area care
    Pressure area care prevents pressure ulcers by reducing pressure and shear, managing moisture, and supporting nutrition and mobility.
  8. Catheter care
    Catheter care keeps an indwelling urinary catheter working and reduces the risk of infection and other harm.
  9. Urinary catheterisation principles
    Urinary catheterisation is an invasive procedure with real risks.
  10. Fluid balance monitoring
    Fluid balance monitoring tracks the fluid a person takes in and loses, alongside clinical signs and weight, so that dehydration and overload are recognised early.
  11. Intake and output recording
    Accurate intake and output recording is the practical skill underneath fluid balance monitoring.
  12. Nutrition support
    Nutrition support helps people who are malnourished, or at risk, to meet their needs: from help with eating and food fortification to oral nutritional supplements, tube feeding and parenteral nutrition.
  13. Enteral feeding principles
    Enteral feeding gives nutrition through a tube into the stomach or small intestine when someone can't eat enough safely by mouth.
  14. Oxygen therapy
    Oxygen is a medicine.
  15. Nebuliser administration principles
    A nebuliser turns a liquid medicine into a fine mist inhaled through a mask or mouthpiece.
  16. Blood glucose monitoring
    Capillary blood glucose monitoring guides the care of people with diabetes and others at risk of high or low glucose.
  17. Insulin administration principles
    Insulin is a high-alert medicine: small errors in type, dose, timing or device can cause serious harm.
  18. Blood sampling
    Blood samples are only useful if they are taken from the right person, into the right tubes, labelled correctly, at the right time and sent promptly.
  19. Venepuncture principles
    Venepuncture is the puncture of a vein to obtain blood.
  20. Cannulation principles
    Peripheral intravenous cannulation provides venous access for fluids and medicines.
  21. Intravenous therapy principles
    Intravenous (IV) therapy delivers fluids, medicines and blood products directly into the circulation.
  22. Medication administration
    This lesson brings together the practical skill of giving medicines in a clinical setting: preparation, checks, administration by different routes, documentation and monitoring, within the law and your competence.
  23. Specimen collection
    Specimens such as urine, stool, sputum and swabs guide diagnosis and treatment.
  24. ECG recording
    A 12-lead electrocardiogram (ECG) records the heart's electrical activity from different angles.
  25. Resuscitation principles
    Resuscitation aims to restore breathing and circulation after cardiac or respiratory arrest.
  26. Basic life support
    Basic life support (BLS) keeps oxygenated blood flowing to the brain and heart until defibrillation and advanced care are available.
  27. Patient positioning
    How a person is positioned affects their breathing, circulation, comfort, skin, swallowing and recovery.
  28. Safe moving and handling
    Moving and handling people safely protects both the person and staff from injury.
  29. Personal care
    Personal care, including washing, dressing, toileting and grooming, is central to dignity, comfort and wellbeing.
  30. Oral care
    Good oral care prevents pain, infection and malnutrition, supports dignity and communication, and reduces the risk of pneumonia in people who are unwell.
  31. Continence care
    Continence care helps people maintain continence where possible and manages incontinence with dignity when it occurs.
  32. Stoma care
    A stoma is a surgically created opening that brings part of the bowel or urinary tract to the abdominal surface.
  33. Drain management
    Surgical and other drains remove fluid, blood or air from a body cavity or wound.
  34. Observations and escalation
    Observations are only valuable if they are accurate, timely and acted on.
  35. Documentation of procedures
    Documenting a procedure records what was done, why, how it went and what happens next.

Key terms

Five moments
The WHO's five points in care when hand hygiene is needed: before patient contact, before an aseptic task, after body fluid exposure risk, after patient contact, after contact with patient surroundings
Standard infection control precautions (SICPs)
The basic infection prevention practices used with everyone, in all care settings, whether or not infection is known
FFP3 respirator
A tight-fitting respiratory protective device that filters airborne particles; must be fit tested and fit checked
Key part
A part of equipment that, if contaminated, is likely to infect the patient
TIME
A framework for assessing the wound bed: Tissue, Infection or inflammation, Moisture, Edge
Moist wound healing
Maintaining a moist, not wet, wound environment to support healing
Shear
Deformation of tissues when skin stays still while deeper tissues move, as in sliding down a bed
CAUTI
Catheter-associated urinary tract infection
Residual volume
The volume of urine drained immediately after catheterisation, or left in the bladder after voiding
Running balance
The cumulative difference between recorded intake and output during a period
Fluid balance chart
A record of all measured intake and output, with running and 24-hour totals
Oral nutritional supplements (ONS)
Prescribed or recommended nutritional products taken by mouth to increase intake
pH indicator paper
CE-marked paper used to test aspirate; pH 5.5 or below supports gastric placement
Target saturation range
The SpO2 range the prescription aims for, usually 94–98% or 88–92%
