Lessons in this school

  1. Drama as performance
    A play is written to be performed, so the text is only part of the work.
  2. Tragedy
    Tragedy shows the fall of a significant figure through a combination of character and circumstance.
  3. Comedy
    Comedy aims to amuse and usually ends happily, often in marriage or reconciliation.
  4. Dramatic structure
    Plays are shaped by structure: how action is divided and how tension builds and resolves.
  5. Character and dialogue in drama
    In drama, character is revealed mainly through dialogue and action.
  6. Stagecraft and stage directions
    Stagecraft is everything that turns a script into a performance: set, lighting, sound, costume, props and movement.
  7. Shakespeare's theatre
    Shakespeare wrote for particular theatres, companies and audiences.
  8. Shakespeare's language
    Shakespeare moves between blank verse, rhyme and prose for dramatic effect.
  9. Soliloquy and aside
    Soliloquies and asides let characters share their thoughts with the audience.
  10. Shakespearean tragedy
    Shakespeare's great tragedies, including Hamlet, Othello, King Lear and Macbeth, centre on a hero whose choices and flaws lead to catastrophe.
  11. Shakespearean comedy
    Shakespeare's comedies blend romance, wit, disguise and festivity.
  12. The history plays and the late romances
    Beyond tragedy and comedy, Shakespeare wrote English history plays and, late in his career, romances.
  13. Marlowe and Shakespeare's contemporaries
    Shakespeare worked alongside other major playwrights.
  14. Restoration and eighteenth-century drama
    After the theatres reopened in 1660, English drama changed: women appeared on stage, indoor theatres dominated, and the witty comedy of manners flourished.
  15. Wilde, Shaw and the Victorian stage
    The late Victorian and Edwardian stage saw a revival of serious and witty drama.
  16. Ibsen, Chekhov and modern realism
    In the late nineteenth century, Ibsen and Chekhov transformed drama with realism: ordinary settings, everyday speech and serious social and psychological themes.
  17. Modernist and absurdist drama
    Twentieth-century dramatists broke with realism.
  18. Post-war and contemporary British drama
    British drama since 1945 has included the "angry young men", Pinter's comedy of menace, political and feminist theatre, and the in-yer-face drama of the 1990s.

Key terms

Stagecraft
The skills and techniques of putting a play on stage
Hamartia
The error or flaw of judgement that leads to the tragic hero's downfall
Comedy of manners
A comedy satirising the manners and pretensions of fashionable society
Exposition
The opening section that introduces characters, setting and situation
Subtext
The underlying meaning beneath what a character says
Stage direction
An instruction in a play script about action, setting, sound or delivery
Groundlings
Spectators who paid the cheapest price to stand in the yard of an open-air playhouse
Antithesis
Placing contrasting ideas side by side for effect
Soliloquy
A speech in which a character alone on stage voices their thoughts
Tragic hero
The central figure of a tragedy, whose fall brings catastrophe
Green world
Frye's term for the forest or dreamlike setting where comic characters are transformed
History play
A play based on the reigns of English kings
Revenge tragedy
A genre in which a wronged hero seeks vengeance, often with a ghost, delay and a bloody ending
Restoration comedy
Witty, often sexually frank comedy of London society after 1660
