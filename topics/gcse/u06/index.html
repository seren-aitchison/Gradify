Lessons in this school

  1. Energy stores and transfers
    Energy is never used up; it is stored in different ways and transferred between stores.
  2. Work, power and efficiency
    Work done, power and efficiency let us measure how much energy is transferred, how fast, and how usefully.
  3. Energy resources
    Our energy comes from renewable and non-renewable resources used for transport, electricity generation and heating.
  4. Circuits, current and potential difference
    Electric circuits let charge flow to transfer energy.
  5. Resistance and I–V characteristics
    Resistance tells us how hard it is for current to flow through a component.
  6. Series and parallel circuits
    Components can be joined in series or in parallel, and each arrangement follows its own rules.
  7. Mains electricity and the National Grid
    Homes in the UK are supplied with alternating current through a three-core cable and the National Grid.
  8. Density and states of matter
    The particle model explains the properties of solids, liquids and gases and the idea of density.
  9. Specific heat capacity and latent heat
    Heating a substance either raises its temperature or changes its state.
  10. Atomic structure and radioactive decay
    Atoms have a tiny dense nucleus surrounded by electrons, and some nuclei are unstable and decay.
  11. Half-life and the uses and dangers of radiation
    Radioactive sources decay at a rate described by their half-life.
  12. Scalars, vectors and resultant forces
    Physical quantities are either scalars or vectors, and forces are vectors that combine into a resultant.
  13. Speed, velocity and acceleration
    Motion can be described with distance, speed, velocity and acceleration, and shown on graphs.
  14. Newton's laws of motion
    Newton's three laws explain how forces change the motion of objects.
  15. Stopping distances and momentum
    A vehicle's stopping distance depends on the driver and on the vehicle and road.
  16. Forces, springs and Hooke's law
    Forces can stretch, squash or bend objects, and springs follow a simple rule up to a limit.
  17. Properties of waves
    Waves transfer energy and information without transferring matter.
  18. The electromagnetic spectrum
    Electromagnetic waves form a continuous spectrum, all travelling at the same speed in a vacuum.
  19. Magnetism and electromagnetism
    Magnets and electric currents both produce magnetic fields, and a current in a field can feel a force.
  20. Physics equations: recall and use
    AQA GCSE Physics expects you to recall some equations and use others from the equation sheet.
  21. Physics required practicals
    AQA GCSE Physics includes ten required practicals that can be examined in written papers.

Key terms

Energy store
A way in which energy is held, such as kinetic, thermal or chemical
Work done
Energy transferred when a force moves an object a distance
Renewable resource
An energy resource that is replenished as it is used
Current
The rate of flow of electrical charge, in amperes
Resistance
Opposition to current, measured in ohms
Series circuit
A circuit with components in a single loop
Alternating current
A current that repeatedly reverses direction
Density
Mass per unit volume, in kg/m³
Specific heat capacity
Energy needed to raise 1 kg of a substance by 1 °C
Isotope
An atom of the same element with a different number of neutrons
Half-life
The time for the number of unstable nuclei or the activity to halve
Vector
A quantity with magnitude and direction
Velocity
Speed in a given direction
Inertia
The tendency of an object to stay at rest or keep moving uniformly
