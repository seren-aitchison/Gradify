Five sample flashcards

Name the four basic tissue types.

Epithelial, connective, muscle and nervous tissue. From “Cells, tissues and organs” →

Which cells release insulin, and which release glucagon?

Beta cells of the islets of Langerhans release insulin; alpha cells release glucagon. From “Blood glucose regulation” →

What is the heart's natural pacemaker?

The sinoatrial node (SAN) in the right atrium. From “The heart” →

What happens to the diaphragm during inspiration?

It contracts and flattens, increasing the volume of the chest. From “Gas exchange and breathing” →

What does ADH do?

It increases the permeability of the collecting ducts to water, so more water is reabsorbed and urine is more concentrated. From “The kidneys and fluid balance” →

Lessons in this school

  1. Cells, tissues and organs
    The body is built in levels: cells form tissues, tissues form organs and organs work together in systems.
  2. Homeostasis
    Homeostasis is the maintenance of a stable internal environment despite changes outside and inside the body.
  3. Body temperature regulation
    The body keeps its core temperature close to 37 °C by balancing heat production and heat loss.
  4. Blood glucose regulation
    Blood glucose must stay within narrow limits because the brain depends on it and high levels damage blood vessels.
  5. The nervous system
    The nervous system detects changes, processes information and coordinates fast responses.
  6. The endocrine system
    The endocrine system controls the body with hormones carried in the blood.
  7. The heart
    The heart is a muscular pump that drives blood through two circulations.
  8. Blood vessels and blood pressure
    Arteries, capillaries and veins carry blood around the body, and blood pressure keeps it moving.
  9. Blood
    Blood transports oxygen, nutrients, hormones and wastes, defends against infection and seals wounds.
  10. The respiratory system
    The respiratory system brings air into the lungs so oxygen can enter the blood and carbon dioxide can leave.
  11. Gas exchange and breathing
    Breathing moves air in and out of the lungs; gas exchange moves oxygen into the blood and carbon dioxide out.
  12. The digestive system
    The digestive system breaks food down into molecules small enough to be absorbed into the blood.
  13. The liver
    The liver is the body's chemical processing centre, with hundreds of functions.
  14. The kidneys and fluid balance
    The kidneys filter the blood, remove wastes and control the body's water and salt balance.
  15. The immune system
    The immune system defends the body against pathogens.
  16. The musculoskeletal system
    Bones, joints and muscles support the body, protect organs and make movement possible.
  17. The skin
    The skin is the body's largest organ, forming a barrier between the body and the outside world.

Key terms

Tissue
A group of similar cells working together to perform a particular function
Homeostasis
Maintenance of a relatively stable internal environment within narrow limits
Vasodilation
Widening of blood vessels; in the skin it increases heat loss
Insulin
A hormone from pancreatic beta cells that lowers blood glucose by promoting uptake and storage
Central nervous system
The brain and spinal cord
Hormone
A chemical messenger secreted into the blood by an endocrine gland and acting on target cells
Cardiac output
The volume of blood pumped by one ventricle per minute; heart rate × stroke volume
Systolic pressure
The highest arterial pressure, when the ventricles contract
Plasma
The liquid part of blood, carrying dissolved nutrients, hormones, wastes and proteins
Alveoli
Tiny air sacs at the end of the bronchioles where gas exchange takes place
Inspiration
Breathing in: the diaphragm and external intercostals contract, chest volume rises and pressure falls
Peristalsis
Waves of muscle contraction that move food along the gut
Deamination
Removal of the amino group from excess amino acids in the liver, forming ammonia that is converted to urea
Nephron
The functional unit of the kidney, where blood is filtered and urine formed
