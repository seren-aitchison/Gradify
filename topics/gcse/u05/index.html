Lessons in this school

  1. Atomic structure and isotopes
    All matter is made of atoms, which contain protons, neutrons and electrons.
  2. The development of the atomic model
    Scientists' ideas about atoms changed as new evidence appeared.
  3. The periodic table and groups
    The periodic table arranges the elements so that patterns in their properties can be seen.
  4. Ionic, covalent and metallic bonding
    Atoms join together in three main ways.
  5. Structure and properties of substances
    The way particles are bonded determines a substance's properties.
  6. Conservation of mass and relative formula mass
    In a chemical reaction, atoms are rearranged but not created or destroyed.
  7. Moles and concentration
    Chemists count particles using the mole.
  8. Reactivity series and extraction of metals
    Metals differ in how readily they react.
  9. Acids, alkalis and salts
    Acids and alkalis are among the most common chemicals in the laboratory and everyday life.
  10. Electrolysis
    Electrolysis uses electricity to break down ionic compounds.
  11. Exothermic and endothermic reactions
    Chemical reactions transfer energy to or from their surroundings.
  12. Rates of reaction
    Some reactions are very fast and others very slow.
  13. Reversible reactions and equilibrium
    Some reactions can go both forwards and backwards.
  14. Crude oil and hydrocarbons
    Crude oil is the source of most fuels and many chemicals.
  15. Chemical analysis and chromatography
    Chemists need to know what substances are present and whether they are pure.
  16. Tests for gases
    Simple tests identify common gases.
  17. The Earth's atmosphere and climate change
    The Earth's atmosphere has changed dramatically over billions of years, and human activity is changing it again.
  18. Using resources: water and life cycle assessment
    Humans use the Earth's resources for food, warmth, shelter and products.
  19. Chemistry required practicals
    GCSE Chemistry includes required practical activities that develop practical skills and can be assessed in exams.

Key terms

Atomic number
The number of protons in an atom
Plum pudding model
Thomson's model of positive charge with embedded electrons
Group
A column of the periodic table
Ion
An atom or group of atoms with an electric charge
Intermolecular forces
Weak forces of attraction between molecules
Conservation of mass
The principle that mass is neither created nor destroyed in a reaction
Mole
The amount of substance containing 6.02 × 10²³ particles
Reactivity series
A list of metals in order of reactivity
pH
A scale measuring how acidic or alkaline a solution is
Electrolyte
A molten or dissolved ionic compound that conducts electricity
Exothermic
A reaction that transfers energy to the surroundings
Rate of reaction
How quickly reactants are used up or products formed
Reversible reaction
A reaction where products can re-form the reactants
Hydrocarbon
A compound containing hydrogen and carbon only
