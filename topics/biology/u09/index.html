Lessons in this school

  1. Introduction to microbiology
    Microbiology is the study of microorganisms.
  2. Bacterial structure
    Bacteria have a simple but highly effective cell structure.
  3. Bacterial classification
    Bacteria are classified using their appearance, biochemistry and genes.
  4. Bacterial growth
    Bacteria multiply by binary fission and can grow very rapidly.
  5. Bacterial metabolism
    Bacteria show enormous metabolic diversity.
  6. Bacterial genetics
    Bacterial genetics explains how bacteria store, express and change their genes.
  7. Horizontal gene transfer
    Bacteria can share genes without reproducing.
  8. Viral structure
    Viruses are tiny infectious particles with a simple structure.
  9. Viral replication
    Viruses hijack host cells to make copies of themselves.
  10. Viral evolution
    Viruses evolve rapidly, producing new strains and variants.
  11. Fungi
    Fungi are eukaryotic organisms including yeasts, moulds and mushrooms.
  12. Protozoa
    Protozoa are single-celled eukaryotes, some of which cause major diseases.
  13. Archaea
    Archaea are prokaryotes that form a separate domain of life.
  14. Microbial ecology
    Microbial ecology studies microbes in their environments.
  15. Microbiomes
    A microbiome is the community of microbes living in a particular environment, including the human body.
  16. Host-microbe interactions
    Microbes and hosts interact along a spectrum from mutualism to parasitism.
  17. Pathogenic microorganisms
    Pathogens are microbes that cause disease.
  18. Virulence
    Virulence is the degree of harm a pathogen causes.
  19. Transmission
    Pathogens spread between hosts by several routes.
  20. Infection
    Infection progresses through stages from exposure to recovery or death.
  21. Antibiotics
    Antibiotics kill or inhibit bacteria by targeting structures they need.
  22. Antimicrobial resistance
    Antimicrobial resistance is a growing global health threat.
  23. Sterilisation
    Sterilisation destroys all microorganisms, including spores.
  24. Disinfection
    Disinfection reduces the number of microbes on surfaces and skin.
  25. Microbial culture
    Microbes are grown in the laboratory to study and identify them.
  26. Microscopy
    Microscopes allow us to see microbes and their structures.
  27. Molecular identification
    Molecular methods identify microbes quickly and accurately from their genes or proteins.
  28. Environmental microbiology
    Environmental microbiology studies microbes in soil, water and air.
  29. Industrial microbiology
    Industrial microbiology uses microbes to make products on a large scale.
  30. Applied microbiology
    Applied microbiology uses microbes to solve problems in health, food, agriculture and the environment.

Key terms

Microorganism
A living organism too small to see without a microscope
Peptidoglycan
The polymer forming bacterial cell walls
Strain
A genetic variant within a bacterial species
Binary fission
Division of one bacterial cell into two
Chemoautotroph
An organism using chemical energy to fix CO₂
Operon
A group of genes transcribed together
Transformation
Uptake of free DNA by a bacterium
Capsid
The protein coat of a virus
Lytic cycle
Viral replication ending in cell lysis
Antigenic drift
Gradual change in viral surface proteins
Hypha
A thread-like filament of a fungus
Protozoan
A single-celled heterotrophic eukaryote
Extremophile
An organism thriving in extreme conditions
Quorum sensing
Bacterial communication based on population density
