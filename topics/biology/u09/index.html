Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

Who first observed microorganisms with a microscope?

Antonie van Leeuwenhoek. From “Introduction to microbiology” →

In plain English: Microbiology is the study of microorganisms. Key idea: Microbiology studies bacteria, archaea, fungi, protists and viruses; key figures include Leeuwenhoek, Pasteur, Koch and Fleming, and microbes drive nutrient cycles and are mostly harmless or beneficial.

What is conjugation?

Direct transfer of DNA, usually a plasmid, through a pilus. From “Horizontal gene transfer” →

In plain English: Bacteria can share genes without reproducing. Key idea: Horizontal gene transfer moves genes between bacteria by transformation, transduction and conjugation, spreading antibiotic resistance and virulence and shaping evolution, as shown first by Griffith's experiment.

What is a methanogen?

An archaeon that produces methane. From “Archaea” →

In plain English: Archaea are prokaryotes that form a separate domain of life. Key idea: Archaea are prokaryotes with ether-linked membrane lipids and no peptidoglycan, with eukaryote-like gene expression; many are extremophiles, methanogens produce methane, and their enzymes are used in biotechnology.

What is a fomite?

A contaminated object that can spread infection. From “Transmission” →

In plain English: Pathogens spread between hosts by several routes. Key idea: Pathogens spread by direct contact, droplets and aerosols, the faecal-oral route, vectors and fomites; reservoirs maintain them, R₀ measures transmissibility, and control measures aim to reduce R below 1.

What is a selective medium?

A medium that allows only certain microbes to grow. From “Microbial culture” →

In plain English: Microbes are grown in the laboratory to study and identify them. Key idea: Microbes are grown in broth or on agar, including selective and differential media; aseptic technique prevents contamination, streak plating gives pure colonies and serial dilution with plate counts estimates numbers in CFU.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Microbiology

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which group of microbes is non-cellular? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Viruses. Viruses are particles that replicate only inside host cells.

  2. What is a bacillus? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    A rod-shaped bacterium. Bacilli are rod-shaped, like Bacillus and E. coli.

  3. Which group contains photosynthetic bacteria? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Cyanobacteria. Cyanobacteria carry out oxygen-producing photosynthesis.

  4. What did Koch's postulates establish? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Criteria for proving a microbe causes a specific disease. Koch linked specific microbes to specific diseases.

  5. Why are Gram-negative bacteria often harder to treat? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Their outer membrane blocks some antibiotics. The extra membrane is a barrier to many drugs.

  6. What has DNA sequencing revealed about environmental bacteria? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Most have never been grown in a laboratory. Sequencing detects many uncultured species.

  7. Why do Koch's postulates not apply to all pathogens? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Some pathogens cannot be grown in pure culture. Unculturable microbes and carriers break the rules.

  8. Why are endospores a problem in hospitals? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    They resist heat, drying and disinfectants. Endospores survive harsh treatments and can later germinate.

  9. Why are bacterial species boundaries hard to define? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Bacteria exchange genes by horizontal transfer. Gene sharing blurs the lines between species.

Key takeaways

  • Microbiology studies bacteria, archaea, fungi, protists and viruses; key figures include Leeuwenhoek, Pasteur, Koch and Fleming, and microbes drive nutrient cycles and are mostly harmless or beneficial.
  • Bacteria have a peptidoglycan cell wall, membrane, 70S ribosomes, a nucleoid and plasmids, sometimes with capsules, flagella and pili; Gram-positive bacteria have thick walls and stain purple, Gram-negative have thin walls with an outer membrane and stain pink.
  • Bacteria are named binomially and classified by shape, Gram stain, oxygen needs and biochemistry, and now by 16S rRNA and genome sequencing; major groups include Proteobacteria, Firmicutes, Actinobacteria and Cyanobacteria.
  • Bacteria multiply by binary fission with generation times as short as about 20 minutes; growth curves have lag, log, stationary and death phases, numbers can be calculated as N × 2ⁿ and conditions such as temperature affect growth.
  • Bacteria are classified as phototrophs or chemotrophs and autotrophs or heterotrophs, and by oxygen needs as obligate aerobes, obligate anaerobes or facultative anaerobes; their metabolism includes nitrogen fixation, chemosynthesis and fermentation.
  • Bacteria have a single circular chromosome with tightly packed genes in operons, plasmids carrying extra genes that transfer between cells, and rapid mutation as haploid organisms; they are key tools in research and biotechnology.

Common mistakes in Microbiology

  • Thinking most microbes cause disease. Only a small proportion of microbes are pathogens.
  • Thinking bacteria have no organised structures. They have walls, ribosomes, flagella and other specialised structures.
  • Thinking bacterial species are defined like animal species. Gene exchange makes species boundaries in bacteria harder to define.
  • Thinking bacteria keep growing exponentially forever. Nutrients run out and wastes accumulate, causing stationary and death phases.
  • Thinking all bacteria need organic food. Autotrophic bacteria make their own organic compounds from CO₂.
  • Thinking all bacterial genes are on the chromosome. Plasmids carry many important genes, such as resistance genes.

Lessons in this school

  1. Introduction to microbiology
    Microbiology is the study of microorganisms.
  2. Bacterial structure
    Bacteria have a simple but highly effective cell structure.
  3. Bacterial classification
    Bacteria are classified using their appearance, biochemistry and genes.
  4. Bacterial growth
    Bacteria multiply by binary fission and can grow very rapidly.
  5. Bacterial metabolism
    Bacteria show enormous metabolic diversity.
  6. Bacterial genetics
    Bacterial genetics explains how bacteria store, express and change their genes.
  7. Horizontal gene transfer
    Bacteria can share genes without reproducing.
  8. Viral structure
    Viruses are tiny infectious particles with a simple structure.
  9. Viral replication
    Viruses hijack host cells to make copies of themselves.
  10. Viral evolution
    Viruses evolve rapidly, producing new strains and variants.
  11. Fungi
    Fungi are eukaryotic organisms including yeasts, moulds and mushrooms.
  12. Protozoa
    Protozoa are single-celled eukaryotes, some of which cause major diseases.
  13. Archaea
    Archaea are prokaryotes that form a separate domain of life.
  14. Microbial ecology
    Microbial ecology studies microbes in their environments.
  15. Microbiomes
    A microbiome is the community of microbes living in a particular environment, including the human body.
  16. Host-microbe interactions
    Microbes and hosts interact along a spectrum from mutualism to parasitism.
  17. Pathogenic microorganisms
    Pathogens are microbes that cause disease.
  18. Virulence
    Virulence is the degree of harm a pathogen causes.
  19. Transmission
    Pathogens spread between hosts by several routes.
  20. Infection
    Infection progresses through stages from exposure to recovery or death.
  21. Antibiotics
    Antibiotics kill or inhibit bacteria by targeting structures they need.
  22. Antimicrobial resistance
    Antimicrobial resistance is a growing global health threat.
  23. Sterilisation
    Sterilisation destroys all microorganisms, including spores.
  24. Disinfection
    Disinfection reduces the number of microbes on surfaces and skin.
  25. Microbial culture
    Microbes are grown in the laboratory to study and identify them.
  26. Microscopy
    Microscopes allow us to see microbes and their structures.
  27. Molecular identification
    Molecular methods identify microbes quickly and accurately from their genes or proteins.
  28. Environmental microbiology
    Environmental microbiology studies microbes in soil, water and air.
  29. Industrial microbiology
    Industrial microbiology uses microbes to make products on a large scale.
  30. Applied microbiology
    Applied microbiology uses microbes to solve problems in health, food, agriculture and the environment.

Microbiology: questions students ask

Why is the 16S rRNA gene used to classify bacteria?

It is in all bacteria and has conserved and variable regions.

What is binary fission?

Division of one bacterial cell into two identical cells.

What is a facultative anaerobe?

A microbe that can grow with or without oxygen.

Why are mutations expressed immediately in bacteria?

Bacteria are haploid, with only one copy of each gene.
