Lessons in this school

  1. Chemistry of life
    Living things are built from a small set of elements arranged into large, complex molecules.
  2. Water and biological systems
    Water makes up most of the mass of cells, and its unusual properties make life possible.
  3. Hydrogen bonding
    Hydrogen bonds are weak attractions, yet they shape water, proteins and DNA.
  4. pH and biological buffers
    The pH of body fluids and cells must stay within narrow limits.
  5. Carbohydrates
    Carbohydrates provide energy, store energy and build structures.
  6. Monosaccharides
    Monosaccharides are the building blocks of all carbohydrates.
  7. Disaccharides
    Disaccharides form when two monosaccharides join by a glycosidic bond.
  8. Polysaccharides
    Polysaccharides are long chains of sugar units used for storage and structure.
  9. Lipids
    Lipids are a diverse group of molecules that are insoluble in water.
  10. Fatty acids
    Fatty acids are the main components of triglycerides and phospholipids.
  11. Triglycerides
    Triglycerides are the main form of stored fat in plants and animals.
  12. Phospholipids
    Phospholipids form the basic structure of all cell membranes.
  13. Steroids
    Steroids are lipids with a ring structure rather than fatty acid chains.
  14. Proteins
    Proteins carry out most of the work in cells, from catalysis to transport and structure.
  15. Amino acids
    Amino acids are the monomers of proteins.
  16. Peptide bonds
    Amino acids join through peptide bonds to form polypeptides.
  17. Protein primary structure
    The primary structure is the sequence of amino acids in a polypeptide.
  18. Protein secondary structure
    Secondary structure is the regular folding of the polypeptide backbone into helices and sheets.
  19. Protein tertiary structure
    Tertiary structure is the overall 3D shape of a single polypeptide.
  20. Protein quaternary structure
    Some proteins are made of more than one polypeptide chain.
  21. Protein folding
    Newly made polypeptides must fold into precise shapes to function.
  22. Protein denaturation
    Denaturation is the loss of a protein's 3D shape and function.
  23. Enzymes
    Enzymes are biological catalysts that speed up reactions in living cells.
  24. Enzyme-substrate interactions
    Enzymes bind their substrates with great specificity.
  25. Enzyme kinetics
    Enzyme kinetics describes how reaction rate depends on substrate concentration.
  26. Enzyme inhibition
    Inhibitors reduce enzyme activity and are important in metabolic control and medicine.
  27. Factors affecting enzyme activity
    Enzyme activity depends on temperature, pH, substrate concentration and enzyme concentration.
  28. Nucleic acids
    Nucleic acids store and transmit genetic information.
  29. DNA and RNA
    DNA and RNA are both nucleic acids but differ in structure and role.
  30. ATP and biological energy
    ATP is the main energy currency of cells.

Key terms

Monomer
A small molecule that joins with others to form a polymer
Polar molecule
A molecule with an uneven distribution of charge
Hydrogen bond
A weak attraction between δ+ hydrogen and a lone pair on O or N
pH
−log₁₀ of the hydrogen ion concentration
Monosaccharide
A single sugar unit
Hexose
A monosaccharide with six carbon atoms
Glycosidic bond
A covalent bond joining two sugar units
Amylose
Unbranched, helical α-glucose polymer in starch
Lipid
A non-polar biological molecule insoluble in water
Saturated fatty acid
A fatty acid with no C=C double bonds
Triglyceride
A lipid made of glycerol and three fatty acids
Amphipathic
Having both hydrophilic and hydrophobic parts
Steroid
A lipid with four fused carbon rings
Globular protein
A compact, usually soluble protein, such as an enzyme
