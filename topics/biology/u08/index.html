Lessons in this school

  1. Plant cell structure
    Plant cells share features with animal cells but have distinctive structures.
  2. Plant tissues
    Plants are built from a few main tissue types.
  3. Roots and root systems
    Roots anchor plants and absorb water and minerals.
  4. Stems
    Stems support plants and transport materials between roots and leaves.
  5. Leaves
    Leaves are the main organs of photosynthesis.
  6. Plant vascular tissue
    Vascular tissue transports water, minerals and sugars through plants.
  7. Xylem
    Xylem is specialised for transporting water and providing support.
  8. Phloem
    Phloem transports sugars and other organic substances.
  9. Water transport
    Water moves from soil to leaves through roots and xylem.
  10. Transpiration
    Transpiration is the loss of water vapour from leaves.
  11. Mineral nutrition
    Plants need mineral ions from the soil for growth.
  12. Photosynthesis
    Photosynthesis converts light energy into chemical energy in sugars.
  13. Light-dependent reactions
    The light-dependent reactions capture light energy to make ATP and NADPH.
  14. Calvin cycle
    The Calvin cycle uses ATP and NADPH to fix carbon dioxide into sugars.
  15. C3 photosynthesis
    Most plants use C3 photosynthesis, named after its first product.
  16. C4 photosynthesis
    C4 plants concentrate CO₂ around RuBisCO to avoid photorespiration.
  17. CAM photosynthesis
    CAM plants separate CO₂ uptake and the Calvin cycle in time.
  18. Cellular respiration in plants
    Plants respire as well as photosynthesise.
  19. Plant hormones
    Plant hormones coordinate growth and responses to the environment.
  20. Auxins
    Auxins control cell elongation and many growth responses.
  21. Gibberellins
    Gibberellins promote stem elongation and seed germination.
  22. Cytokinins
    Cytokinins promote cell division and delay ageing.
  23. Abscisic acid
    Abscisic acid helps plants respond to stress and controls dormancy.
  24. Ethylene
    Ethylene is a gaseous hormone that controls ripening and ageing.
  25. Tropisms
    Tropisms are growth responses to directional stimuli.
  26. Photoperiodism
    Photoperiodism is the response of plants to day length.
  27. Flowering
    Flowers are the reproductive structures of flowering plants.
  28. Pollination
    Pollination is the transfer of pollen to a stigma.
  29. Fertilisation
    In flowering plants, fertilisation follows pollination.
  30. Seed formation
    Seeds contain an embryo and food store protected by a coat.
  31. Germination
    Germination is the start of growth of a seed into a seedling.
  32. Plant reproduction
    Plants reproduce sexually and asexually.
  33. Plant responses to stress
    Plants cannot move away from danger, so they respond to stress in other ways.
  34. Plant biotechnology
    Plant biotechnology uses scientific techniques to improve and propagate plants.

Key terms

Turgor pressure
Pressure of cell contents against the cell wall
Meristem
A region of dividing cells in a plant
Root hair
An extension of a root epidermal cell increasing surface area
Node
The point on a stem where a leaf attaches
Stoma
A pore in the leaf surface for gas exchange
Xylem
Tissue transporting water and minerals
Vessel element
A dead xylem cell forming part of a continuous tube
Translocation
Movement of organic substances in phloem
Cohesion
Attraction between water molecules
Transpiration
Evaporation of water from plant leaves
Chlorosis
Yellowing of leaves due to lack of chlorophyll
Chloroplast
The organelle where photosynthesis occurs
Photolysis
Splitting of water using light energy
RuBisCO
The enzyme fixing CO₂ to RuBP
