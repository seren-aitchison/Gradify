Lessons in this school

  1. What is biology?
    Biology is the scientific study of living things: what they are made of, how they work, how they develop, how they interact and how they came to be.
  2. Characteristics of living organisms
    Living things share a set of properties that, taken together, distinguish them from non-living matter.
  3. Levels of biological organisation
    Living things are organised in a hierarchy, from atoms and molecules through cells, tissues, organs and organisms to populations, communities, ecosystems and the biosphere.
  4. The characteristics of life
    How do we know whether a newly discovered organism, a dormant seed or a frozen bacterium is alive?
  5. Classification and taxonomy
    With an estimated several million species alive today, biologists need a system to name and organise them.
  6. The three domains of life
    All living organisms fall into three domains: Bacteria, Archaea and Eukarya.
  7. The kingdoms of life
    Within the domain Eukarya, organisms are traditionally divided into kingdoms: animals, plants, fungi and protists.
  8. Scientific method
    Science is a way of finding out about the natural world by testing ideas against evidence.
  9. Hypotheses and predictions
    A good investigation starts with a clear hypothesis and a precise prediction.
  10. Variables and experimental design
    A fair experiment changes one factor, measures its effect on another and keeps everything else the same.
  11. Controls and reproducibility
    Controls show that a result is caused by the factor being tested and not by something else.
  12. Accuracy and precision
    Accuracy is how close a measurement is to the true value; precision is how close repeated measurements are to each other.
  13. Reliability and validity
    A result is reliable if it can be obtained consistently, and valid if it actually measures what it claims to and answers the question asked.
  14. Biological measurements
    Biologists measure things across an enormous range of scales, from nanometre-sized molecules to whole ecosystems.
  15. Units and conversions
    Science uses the International System of Units (SI) so that measurements can be compared worldwide.
  16. Scientific notation
    Biology deals with numbers from the 10⁻¹⁰ m of an atom to the 10¹³ cells of a human body.
  17. Graphs and data presentation
    Choosing the right table or graph makes patterns in data clear and honest.
  18. Statistical foundations
    Living things vary, so biologists need statistics to summarise data and decide whether differences are real or due to chance.
  19. Sampling methods
    Biologists can rarely measure every individual, so they study samples.
  20. Ethical considerations in biological research
    Biological research can involve animals, human volunteers, human tissue and data, and organisms that could affect the environment.

Key terms

Biology
The scientific study of living organisms and life processes
Metabolism
All the chemical reactions in an organism, including those that release energy and those that build molecules
Tissue
A group of similar cells that work together to carry out a function
Dormancy
A state of very low metabolic activity from which an organism can resume normal life
Taxonomy
The science of naming, describing and classifying organisms
Domain
The highest rank of classification: Bacteria, Archaea or Eukarya
Heterotroph
An organism that obtains organic molecules by feeding on other organisms or their products
Hypothesis
A testable, falsifiable explanation for an observation
Null hypothesis
The hypothesis that there is no effect or no difference; it is what a statistical test assesses
Independent variable
The variable deliberately changed by the experimenter
Negative control
A treatment expected to produce no effect, used to show that an effect depends on the factor tested
Accuracy
How close a measured value is to the true value
Reliability
The consistency of results when measurements or experiments are repeated
Magnification
How many times larger an image is than the actual object
