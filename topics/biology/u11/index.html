Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What is a community?

All the populations of different species living in an area. From “Introduction to ecology” →

In plain English: Ecology is the study of interactions between organisms and their environment. Key idea: Ecology studies interactions at the levels of individual, population, community, ecosystem and biosphere, shaped by abiotic and biotic factors, with limiting factors restricting populations.

What is a food web?

A network of interlinked food chains. From “Food webs” →

In plain English: Food webs show the many interlinked feeding relationships in an ecosystem. Key idea: Food webs combine interlinked food chains, showing interdependence; removing species causes direct and indirect effects and trophic cascades, and keystone species such as sea otters have large effects on community structure.

Which bacteria convert ammonium to nitrite?

Nitrosomonas. From “Nitrogen cycle” →

In plain English: Nitrogen cycles between the atmosphere, soil and organisms through processes mainly carried out by bacteria. Key idea: The nitrogen cycle includes fixation, ammonification, nitrification, assimilation and denitrification, mostly by bacteria; the Haber process supports food production but fertiliser runoff causes eutrophication.

What is an endoparasite?

A parasite living inside its host. From “Parasitism” →

In plain English: Parasites live on or in a host and harm it. Key idea: Ectoparasites live on and endoparasites in their hosts; many have complex life cycles with intermediate hosts, some alter host behaviour and parasites can regulate host populations and shape ecosystems.

What is an invasive species?

A non-native species that spreads and causes harm. From “Invasive species” →

In plain English: Invasive species are non-native species that spread and cause harm. Key idea: Invasive non-native species are introduced by humans and spread to cause harm, often succeeding through enemy release and rapid reproduction; control relies on biosecurity, early detection, removal, chemicals and carefully tested biological control.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Ecology and Environmental Biology

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which is a biotic factor? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Predation by foxes. Predation involves living organisms, so it is a biotic factor.

  2. Which distribution pattern is most common in nature? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Clumped. Patchy resources and social behaviour cause clumping.

  3. What is a habitat? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    The place where an organism lives. A habitat is a location, while a niche is a role.

  4. What does an ecosystem include that a community does not? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    The non-living physical environment. Ecosystems combine living and non-living parts.

  5. Which assumption does capture-recapture rely on? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Marked animals mix fully with the population. Mixing ensures the recapture sample is representative.

  6. Why is a realised niche often smaller than a fundamental niche? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Competition and other species restrict it. Competitors exclude species from parts of their range.

  7. Why are long-term ecological studies valuable? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    They reveal slow changes that short studies miss. Long records show trends such as climate effects.

  8. Why might a capture-recapture estimate be too high? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Marks are lost, so fewer marked animals are recaptured. Fewer recaptures inflate the calculated total.

  9. What did MacArthur's warbler study show? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Similar species coexist by feeding in different parts of trees. Niche partitioning reduced competition between similar warbler species.

Key takeaways

  • Ecology studies interactions at the levels of individual, population, community, ecosystem and biosphere, shaped by abiotic and biotic factors, with limiting factors restricting populations.
  • Populations are described by size, density, distribution, age structure and sex ratio, and estimated with quadrats, transects and capture-mark-recapture using the Lincoln index.
  • A habitat is where an organism lives and a niche is its role; the fundamental niche is reduced to a realised niche by competition, competitive exclusion prevents identical niches coexisting and niche partitioning allows coexistence.
  • Ecosystems contain producers, consumers, decomposers and abiotic components, and provide provisioning, regulating, supporting and cultural services that benefit people.
  • Biomes are large regions defined by climate and vegetation, including rainforest, desert, grassland, temperate forest, taiga and tundra, mainly determined by temperature and rainfall, while aquatic biomes depend on salinity, depth and light.
  • Food chains start with producers, followed by primary, secondary and tertiary consumers, with arrows showing energy flow; they are usually short because energy is lost at each step.

Common mistakes in Ecology and Environmental Biology

  • Confusing a community with an ecosystem. An ecosystem includes the non-living environment as well as the community.
  • Assuming a capture-recapture estimate is exact. It relies on assumptions and gives an estimate.
  • Using habitat and niche as the same thing. A niche is an organism's role, not just its location.
  • Thinking decomposers are unimportant. Without them, nutrients would not be recycled.
  • Thinking biomes have sharp boundaries. They usually change gradually across transition zones.
  • Drawing arrows from the eater to the food. Arrows point from the food to the eater, showing energy flow.

Ecology and Environmental Biology mind map

Lessons in this school

  1. Introduction to ecology
    Ecology is the study of interactions between organisms and their environment.
  2. Organisms and populations
    Populations have characteristics such as size, density and distribution.
  3. Habitats and niches
    Every species has a habitat and a niche.
  4. Ecosystems
    Ecosystems are communities interacting with their physical environment.
  5. Biomes
    Biomes are large regions with similar climate and vegetation.
  6. Food chains
    Food chains show how energy passes from one organism to another.
  7. Food webs
    Food webs show the many interlinked feeding relationships in an ecosystem.
  8. Energy flow
    Energy flows through ecosystems in one direction and is lost at each step.
  9. Trophic levels
    Trophic levels describe an organism's position in a food chain.
  10. Primary productivity
    Primary productivity measures how fast producers make organic matter.
  11. Nutrient cycles
    Nutrients cycle between living organisms and the environment.
  12. Carbon cycle
    Carbon cycles between the atmosphere, living organisms, oceans and rocks.
  13. Nitrogen cycle
    Nitrogen cycles between the atmosphere, soil and organisms through processes mainly carried out by bacteria.
  14. Water cycle
    Water moves continuously between the oceans, atmosphere and land.
  15. Population dynamics
    Populations change in size over time.
  16. Competition
    Competition occurs when organisms need the same limited resources.
  17. Predation
    Predation shapes populations and drives adaptations in both predators and prey.
  18. Mutualism
    Mutualism is a relationship in which both species benefit.
  19. Parasitism
    Parasites live on or in a host and harm it.
  20. Succession
    Succession is the change in a community over time.
  21. Biodiversity
    Biodiversity is the variety of life at all levels.
  22. Species richness
    Species richness and diversity indices measure biodiversity.
  23. Conservation biology
    Conservation biology aims to protect biodiversity.
  24. Habitat fragmentation
    Habitat fragmentation breaks habitats into small, isolated patches.
  25. Invasive species
    Invasive species are non-native species that spread and cause harm.
  26. Climate change and ecosystems
    Climate change is altering ecosystems worldwide.
  27. Pollution
    Pollution harms organisms and ecosystems.
  28. Environmental monitoring
    Environmental monitoring tracks the state of ecosystems over time.
  29. Conservation strategies
    Conservation uses in-situ and ex-situ strategies to protect species.
  30. Sustainable ecosystems
    Sustainability means meeting present needs without harming future generations.
