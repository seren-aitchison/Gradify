Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check.

State the three principles of cell theory.

All organisms are made of one or more cells; the cell is the basic unit of structure and function; all cells come from pre-existing cells. From “Cell theory” →

Why do Gram-positive bacteria stain purple?

Their thick peptidoglycan layer keeps the crystal violet stain after the decolourising step. From “Bacterial cells” →

Name three functions of smooth ER.

Making lipids (phospholipids, cholesterol, steroid hormones), detoxifying drugs and storing calcium ions. From “Endoplasmic reticulum” →

Which transport processes are passive?

Simple diffusion, facilitated diffusion and osmosis. From “Membrane transport” →

How is a red blood cell adapted for carrying oxygen?

Biconcave shape for a large surface area and flexibility; no nucleus, leaving more room for haemoglobin. From “Cell specialisation” →

Lessons in this school

  1. Cell theory
    Cell theory says that every living thing is made of cells, that the cell is the basic unit of life, and that new cells come only from existing cells.
  2. Prokaryotic cells
    Prokaryotes, the bacteria and archaea, are cells without a nucleus or membrane-bound organelles.
  3. Eukaryotic cells
    Eukaryotic cells, found in animals, plants, fungi and protists, keep their DNA in a nucleus and divide their interior into membrane-bound compartments.
  4. Animal cells
    Animal cells are eukaryotic cells without a cell wall or chloroplasts.
  5. Plant cells
    Plant cells share the eukaryotic plan with animal cells but add three features: a cellulose wall, chloroplasts and a large permanent vacuole.
  6. Fungal cells
    Fungi, including yeasts, moulds and mushrooms, are eukaryotes with cell walls made of chitin rather than cellulose.
  7. Bacterial cells
    Bacteria are the most familiar prokaryotes.
  8. Cell organelles
    Organelles are the specialised compartments and structures inside eukaryotic cells.
  9. Nucleus and nucleolus
    The nucleus holds almost all of a eukaryotic cell's DNA and controls which genes are used.
  10. Mitochondria
    Mitochondria are the organelles where aerobic respiration releases most of the energy in food, storing it as ATP.
  11. Chloroplasts
    Chloroplasts are the organelles of photosynthesis in plants and algae.
  12. Ribosomes
    Ribosomes are the cell's protein factories: they read the code in messenger RNA and join amino acids in the order it specifies.
  13. Endoplasmic reticulum
    The endoplasmic reticulum is a network of membranes spreading through the cytoplasm from the nuclear envelope.
  14. Golgi apparatus
    The Golgi apparatus is the cell's processing and dispatch centre.
  15. Lysosomes and peroxisomes
    Lysosomes and peroxisomes are small membrane-bound organelles that break things down.
  16. Cytoskeleton
    The cytoskeleton is a network of protein filaments that gives the cell its shape, holds organelles in place, moves materials around and lets cells crawl, contract and divide.
  17. Cell membranes
    Every cell is bounded by a plasma membrane, and eukaryotic cells are full of internal membranes too.
  18. Membrane structure
    Membranes are built from a double layer of phospholipids with proteins floating in it, a structure described by the fluid mosaic model.
  19. Membrane transport
    Substances cross membranes in several ways, which differ in whether they need energy and whether they need a protein.
  20. Diffusion
    Diffusion is the net movement of particles from where they are more concentrated to where they are less concentrated, driven by their random motion.
  21. Osmosis
    Osmosis is the diffusion of water across a partially permeable membrane.
  22. Facilitated diffusion
    Facilitated diffusion is passive movement down a concentration gradient through specific membrane proteins.
  23. Active transport
    Active transport moves substances against their concentration gradient using energy, usually from ATP.
  24. Endocytosis and exocytosis
    Some cargo is far too large for channels or carriers.
  25. Cell specialisation
    In a multicellular organism, cells take on different shapes and contents to do particular jobs.
  26. Stem cells
    Stem cells are unspecialised cells that can divide to renew themselves and can also give rise to specialised cells.
  27. Cell differentiation
    Differentiation is the process by which a cell becomes specialised.
  28. Cell signalling
    Cells constantly send and receive chemical signals that coordinate growth, metabolism and defence.
  29. Cell junctions
    In tissues, cells are physically joined to their neighbours.
  30. Cellular organisation
    Multicellular organisms are organised in levels: specialised cells form tissues, tissues form organs, and organs work together in organ systems.

Key terms

Cell theory
All organisms are made of cells, the cell is the basic unit of life, and cells arise only from existing cells
Prokaryote
An organism whose cells have no nucleus or membrane-bound organelles: bacteria and archaea
Eukaryote
An organism whose cells have a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles
Centriole
A small cylinder of microtubules, found in pairs in the animal centrosome
Cell wall
A rigid layer outside the plasma membrane; made of cellulose in plants
Chitin
A tough nitrogen-containing polysaccharide in fungal cell walls and arthropod exoskeletons
Peptidoglycan
The mesh of sugar chains cross-linked by peptides that forms the bacterial cell wall
Endomembrane system
The connected membranes of the nuclear envelope, ER, Golgi, lysosomes, vesicles and plasma membrane
Nuclear pore
A protein complex in the nuclear envelope that controls movement of molecules in and out
Cristae
The folds of the inner mitochondrial membrane, where ATP is made
Thylakoid
A flattened membrane sac in a chloroplast where the light-dependent reactions occur
Ribosome
A structure of rRNA and protein that translates mRNA into a polypeptide
Rough endoplasmic reticulum
ER with ribosomes on its surface, where proteins for secretion and membranes are made
Golgi apparatus
A stack of membrane sacs that modifies, sorts and packages proteins and lipids
