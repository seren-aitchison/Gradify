Lessons in this school

  1. Organisation of the human body
    The human body is organised from cells into tissues, organs and organ systems.
  2. Homeostasis
    Homeostasis keeps the internal environment stable.
  3. Nervous system
    The nervous system provides rapid communication throughout the body.
  4. Neurones
    Neurones are cells specialised to transmit electrical impulses.
  5. Action potentials
    An action potential is a brief electrical impulse along a neurone.
  6. Synaptic transmission
    Synapses pass signals between neurones or to effectors.
  7. Central nervous system
    The central nervous system integrates information and controls the body.
  8. Peripheral nervous system
    The peripheral nervous system connects the CNS to the rest of the body.
  9. Endocrine system
    The endocrine system uses hormones carried in the blood to coordinate the body.
  10. Hormones and signalling
    Hormones signal through receptors on or inside target cells.
  11. Cardiovascular system
    The cardiovascular system transports substances around the body.
  12. Heart anatomy
    The heart is a muscular double pump.
  13. Cardiac cycle
    The cardiac cycle is the sequence of events in one heartbeat.
  14. Blood vessels
    Arteries, capillaries and veins have structures suited to their functions.
  15. Blood composition
    Blood is a tissue made of cells suspended in plasma.
  16. Circulation
    Humans have a double circulation with pulmonary and systemic circuits.
  17. Respiratory system
    The respiratory system takes in oxygen and removes carbon dioxide.
  18. Ventilation
    Ventilation moves air into and out of the lungs.
  19. Gas exchange
    Gas exchange in the alveoli supplies oxygen and removes carbon dioxide.
  20. Oxygen transport
    Haemoglobin carries oxygen from the lungs to the tissues.
  21. Digestive system
    The digestive system breaks down food and absorbs nutrients.
  22. Digestion and absorption
    Food molecules are digested into small units and absorbed in the small intestine.
  23. Liver and pancreas
    The liver and pancreas have essential digestive and metabolic roles.
  24. Renal system
    The renal system removes wastes and controls water and salt balance.
  25. Kidney function
    The nephron filters blood and adjusts the composition of urine.
  26. Urine formation
    Urine composition reflects how the kidneys regulate the body.
  27. Musculoskeletal system
    The musculoskeletal system supports the body and allows movement.
  28. Bone physiology
    Bone is a living tissue that is constantly remodelled.
  29. Muscle contraction
    Skeletal muscle contracts by the sliding of protein filaments.
  30. Immune system
    The immune system defends the body against pathogens.
  31. Reproductive system
    The reproductive systems produce gametes and allow fertilisation.
  32. Human development
    Human development continues from fertilisation through birth, childhood and ageing.
  33. Skin and thermoregulation
    The skin protects the body and helps control temperature.
  34. Fluid and electrolyte balance
    The body controls the volume and composition of its fluids.

Key terms

Tissue
A group of similar cells performing a shared function
Homeostasis
Maintenance of a stable internal environment
Central nervous system
The brain and spinal cord
Axon
A fibre carrying impulses away from the cell body
Resting potential
The membrane potential of a neurone at rest, about −70 mV
Synapse
A junction between a neurone and another cell
Cerebral cortex
The outer layer of the cerebrum, involved in higher functions
Somatic nervous system
The part controlling voluntary skeletal muscles
Hormone
A chemical messenger carried in the blood
Second messenger
An intracellular molecule relaying a hormone's signal
Atherosclerosis
Build-up of fatty plaques in artery walls
Atrium
An upper heart chamber receiving blood
Systole
Contraction of heart chambers
Lumen
The central space inside a vessel
