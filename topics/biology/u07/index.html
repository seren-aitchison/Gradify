Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

Name the four main tissue types.

Epithelial, connective, muscle and nervous tissue. From “Organisation of the human body” →

In plain English: The human body is organised from cells into tissues, organs and organ systems. Key idea: The body is organised as cells, tissues, organs and organ systems; the four tissue types are epithelial, connective, muscle and nervous, and organ systems such as the nervous, cardiovascular and digestive systems work together.

What does white matter contain?

Myelinated axons. From “Central nervous system” →

In plain English: The central nervous system integrates information and controls the body. Key idea: The brain includes the cerebrum with four cortical lobes, the cerebellum for coordination, the brainstem for vital functions and the hypothalamus for homeostasis; the spinal cord relays signals and reflexes, and the CNS is protected by bone, meninges, fluid and the blood-brain barrier.

What drains excess tissue fluid?

The lymphatic system. From “Blood vessels” →

In plain English: Arteries, capillaries and veins have structures suited to their functions. Key idea: Arteries have thick elastic walls for high pressure, capillaries have thin walls for exchange and veins have wide lumens and valves for low-pressure return; tissue fluid forms by hydrostatic pressure and returns by osmosis, with excess drained by lymph.

What is peristalsis?

Waves of muscle contraction that move food along the gut. From “Digestive system” →

In plain English: The digestive system breaks down food and absorbs nutrients. Key idea: The digestive system includes the mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small and large intestines and accessory organs; mechanical and chemical digestion break down food, the stomach uses acid and pepsin and the large intestine absorbs water with help from gut microbiota.

Which cells build new bone?

Osteoblasts. From “Bone physiology” →

In plain English: Bone is a living tissue that is constantly remodelled. Key idea: Bone contains collagen and calcium phosphate in compact and spongy forms; osteoblasts build and osteoclasts break down bone, parathyroid hormone, calcitonin and vitamin D regulate calcium, and load, ageing and nutrition affect bone health.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Human Anatomy and Physiology

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which tissue type covers body surfaces and lines cavities? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Epithelial tissue. Epithelium forms coverings and linings throughout the body.

  2. What is the role of an effector in a control system? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    To carry out the response that changes the variable. Effectors such as muscles or glands make the corrective response.

  3. Which neurone carries impulses from a receptor to the spinal cord? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Sensory neurone. Sensory neurones carry information towards the CNS.

  4. Which tissue type includes bone, blood and adipose tissue? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Connective tissue. Connective tissues support, bind and transport, and include these forms.

  5. Why is insulin release after a meal an example of negative feedback? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    It lowers blood glucose back towards the set point. The response opposes the original rise, returning glucose to the set point.

  6. How does nervous communication differ from hormonal communication? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    It is faster and shorter-lived. Electrical impulses act quickly and briefly.

  7. Why does exercise show that organ systems are interdependent? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Muscles, heart, lungs and nerves must all respond together. Delivering oxygen to working muscle needs several systems at once.

  8. Why do opposing hormones such as insulin and glucagon give finer control? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    The variable can be actively pushed in either direction. Two antagonistic effectors allow rapid correction both ways.

  9. Why can a hand be withdrawn from heat before pain is felt? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    The reflex is processed in the spinal cord before signals reach the brain. The short spinal pathway is faster than conscious processing.

Key takeaways

  • The body is organised as cells, tissues, organs and organ systems; the four tissue types are epithelial, connective, muscle and nervous, and organ systems such as the nervous, cardiovascular and digestive systems work together.
  • Homeostasis maintains a stable internal environment using receptors, coordinators and effectors around set points; negative feedback reverses changes, as with insulin and glucose, while positive feedback amplifies them, as in childbirth.
  • The nervous system provides sensory input, integration and motor output through the CNS (brain and spinal cord) and the PNS; reflex arcs from receptor to effector via sensory, relay and motor neurones produce rapid protective responses.
  • Neurones have a cell body, dendrites, an axon and terminals; sensory, relay and motor neurones carry signals in defined directions, and myelin made by glial cells allows fast saltatory conduction, which is lost in multiple sclerosis.
  • The resting potential of about −70 mV is maintained by the sodium-potassium pump and K⁺ leakage; an action potential involves depolarisation by Na⁺ influx, repolarisation by K⁺ efflux and hyperpolarisation, is all-or-nothing above threshold and is followed by a refractory period.
  • At chemical synapses, an action potential opens Ca²⁺ channels, triggering neurotransmitter release that diffuses across the cleft and binds postsynaptic receptors; transmitters are broken down or reabsorbed, synapses can excite or inhibit, summation integrates signals and drugs alter transmission.

Common mistakes in Human Anatomy and Physiology

  • Thinking each organ is made of a single tissue. Most organs contain several tissue types.
  • Thinking homeostasis keeps variables exactly constant. Variables fluctuate within a narrow range around a set point.
  • Thinking every response goes through conscious thought in the brain. Many reflexes are processed in the spinal cord.
  • Thinking myelin conducts the impulse. Myelin insulates; the impulse jumps between gaps in it.
  • Thinking a stronger stimulus produces a bigger action potential. It produces more frequent action potentials of the same size.
  • Thinking the electrical impulse jumps across the synaptic cleft. At chemical synapses a neurotransmitter carries the signal.

Lessons in this school

  1. Organisation of the human body
    The human body is organised from cells into tissues, organs and organ systems.
  2. Homeostasis
    Homeostasis keeps the internal environment stable.
  3. Nervous system
    The nervous system provides rapid communication throughout the body.
  4. Neurones
    Neurones are cells specialised to transmit electrical impulses.
  5. Action potentials
    An action potential is a brief electrical impulse along a neurone.
  6. Synaptic transmission
    Synapses pass signals between neurones or to effectors.
  7. Central nervous system
    The central nervous system integrates information and controls the body.
  8. Peripheral nervous system
    The peripheral nervous system connects the CNS to the rest of the body.
  9. Endocrine system
    The endocrine system uses hormones carried in the blood to coordinate the body.
  10. Hormones and signalling
    Hormones signal through receptors on or inside target cells.
  11. Cardiovascular system
    The cardiovascular system transports substances around the body.
  12. Heart anatomy
    The heart is a muscular double pump.
  13. Cardiac cycle
    The cardiac cycle is the sequence of events in one heartbeat.
  14. Blood vessels
    Arteries, capillaries and veins have structures suited to their functions.
  15. Blood composition
    Blood is a tissue made of cells suspended in plasma.
  16. Circulation
    Humans have a double circulation with pulmonary and systemic circuits.
  17. Respiratory system
    The respiratory system takes in oxygen and removes carbon dioxide.
  18. Ventilation
    Ventilation moves air into and out of the lungs.
  19. Gas exchange
    Gas exchange in the alveoli supplies oxygen and removes carbon dioxide.
  20. Oxygen transport
    Haemoglobin carries oxygen from the lungs to the tissues.
  21. Digestive system
    The digestive system breaks down food and absorbs nutrients.
  22. Digestion and absorption
    Food molecules are digested into small units and absorbed in the small intestine.
  23. Liver and pancreas
    The liver and pancreas have essential digestive and metabolic roles.
  24. Renal system
    The renal system removes wastes and controls water and salt balance.
  25. Kidney function
    The nephron filters blood and adjusts the composition of urine.
  26. Urine formation
    Urine composition reflects how the kidneys regulate the body.
  27. Musculoskeletal system
    The musculoskeletal system supports the body and allows movement.
  28. Bone physiology
    Bone is a living tissue that is constantly remodelled.
  29. Muscle contraction
    Skeletal muscle contracts by the sliding of protein filaments.
  30. Immune system
    The immune system defends the body against pathogens.
  31. Reproductive system
    The reproductive systems produce gametes and allow fertilisation.
  32. Human development
    Human development continues from fertilisation through birth, childhood and ageing.
  33. Skin and thermoregulation
    The skin protects the body and helps control temperature.
  34. Fluid and electrolyte balance
    The body controls the volume and composition of its fluids.
