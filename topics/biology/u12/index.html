Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What is biotechnology?

The use of organisms or biological molecules to make products or solve problems. From “Foundations of biotechnology” →

In plain English: Biotechnology uses living organisms and their components to make useful products. Key idea: Biotechnology uses organisms and biological molecules to make products, ranging from traditional fermentation and selective breeding to modern genetic engineering, and spans medical, agricultural, industrial and marine areas.

What is a shuttle vector?

A vector that can replicate in two different hosts. From “Cloning vectors” →

In plain English: Different vectors carry different sizes of DNA into different hosts. Key idea: Good vectors replicate in hosts, have restriction sites and markers; plasmids, phages, cosmids, BACs and YACs carry increasingly large inserts, while viral vectors deliver genes to human cells.

What is coverage in sequencing?

The number of reads covering each base. From “Next-generation sequencing” →

In plain English: Next-generation sequencing reads millions of DNA fragments at once. Key idea: Next-generation sequencing reads millions of fragments in parallel; short-read sequencing by synthesis is accurate and cheap, long-read methods such as nanopore span repeats, and reads are aligned or assembled, with coverage indicating reliability.

What is downstream processing?

Purifying the protein after production. From “Recombinant proteins” →

In plain English: Recombinant proteins are made from cloned genes in host cells. Key idea: Recombinant proteins are produced by expressing cloned genes in hosts grown in bioreactors, then purified by centrifugation and chromatography; examples include insulin, growth hormone, EPO, factor VIII, vaccines and industrial enzymes.

What is a biosimilar?

A highly similar version of an approved biological medicine. From “Biopharmaceuticals” →

In plain English: Biopharmaceuticals are medicines made using living systems. Key idea: Biopharmaceuticals are large molecules made in living cells, including recombinant proteins, monoclonal antibodies, vaccines, nucleic acid therapies and cell and gene therapies; most are injected, production must be tightly controlled and biosimilars provide cheaper versions after patents expire.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Biotechnology and Genetic Engineering

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which is an example of modern biotechnology? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Making insulin in engineered bacteria. Inserting a human gene into bacteria is genetic engineering.

  2. Which enzyme joins DNA fragments together? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    DNA ligase. Ligase forms phosphodiester bonds between fragments.

  3. How do bacteria protect their own DNA from restriction enzymes? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    By methylating their recognition sites. Methyl groups block the enzyme from cutting.

  4. Which area of biotechnology includes GM crops? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Green biotechnology. Green biotechnology covers agriculture and plant breeding.

  5. Why is cDNA used when expressing human genes in bacteria? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Bacteria cannot remove introns from human genes. cDNA is made from processed mRNA without introns.

  6. Why are the gene and vector cut with the same restriction enzyme? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    To produce complementary sticky ends that can pair. Matching overhangs base-pair before ligation.

  7. Why was the Cohen and Boyer experiment a turning point? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    It showed genes could be transferred and expressed in bacteria. Recombinant DNA opened the way to genetic engineering.

  8. Why are transformed bacteria grown on antibiotic agar? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Only cells with the resistance marker survive. Selection kills cells that lack the plasmid.

  9. What does "palindromic" mean for a recognition site? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    It reads the same 5′ to 3′ on both strands. Both strands have the same sequence in the same direction.

Key takeaways

  • Biotechnology uses organisms and biological molecules to make products, ranging from traditional fermentation and selective breeding to modern genetic engineering, and spans medical, agricultural, industrial and marine areas.
  • Recombinant DNA is made by isolating a gene, inserting it into a vector cut with the same restriction enzyme, joining with ligase, transforming host cells and selecting transformed cells using marker or reporter genes.
  • Restriction enzymes from bacteria cut DNA at specific, often palindromic, recognition sites, producing sticky or blunt ends; cutting gene and vector with the same enzyme creates complementary sticky ends that ligase can join.
  • DNA ligase forms phosphodiester bonds to seal nicks in the DNA backbone using ATP; it joins Okazaki fragments in replication, completes repair and, as T4 ligase, joins genes into vectors in genetic engineering.
  • Plasmids are small circular DNA molecules that replicate independently and carry genes such as resistance; engineered vectors have an origin of replication, a selectable marker and a multiple cloning site, and are introduced by heat shock or electroporation.
  • Good vectors replicate in hosts, have restriction sites and markers; plasmids, phages, cosmids, BACs and YACs carry increasingly large inserts, while viral vectors deliver genes to human cells.

Common mistakes in Biotechnology and Genetic Engineering

  • Thinking biotechnology is only modern genetic engineering. Brewing and selective breeding are ancient biotechnologies.
  • Thinking bacteria can express any human gene directly. Introns must be removed, so cDNA is often used.
  • Thinking restriction enzymes cut DNA randomly. They cut only at specific recognition sequences.
  • Thinking ligase forms hydrogen bonds between bases. It forms covalent phosphodiester bonds in the backbone.
  • Thinking plasmids are part of the bacterial chromosome. They are separate DNA molecules.
  • Thinking one vector suits every purpose. Vector choice depends on insert size and host cell.
