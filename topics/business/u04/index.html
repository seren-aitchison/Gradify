Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

How does the CIM define marketing?

The management process responsible for identifying, anticipating and satisfying customer requirements profitably. From “What is marketing?” →

In plain English: Marketing is the process of understanding customers and creating value for them profitably. Key idea: Marketing identifies and satisfies customer needs profitably; a marketing orientation starts with the customer, and defining a business by customer needs rather than products avoids marketing myopia.

What is loss aversion?

The tendency for losses to feel larger than equivalent gains. From “Consumer behaviour” →

In plain English: Understanding why and how people buy helps businesses design and market their products. Key idea: Consumers move through need recognition, search, evaluation, purchase and post-purchase stages, influenced by cultural, social, personal and psychological factors and by predictable biases.

What does AIDA stand for?

Attention, Interest, Desire, Action. From “Promotion and marketing communications” →

In plain English: Promotion communicates with customers to inform, persuade and remind them. Key idea: The promotional mix combines advertising, sales promotion, PR, personal selling and direct marketing; AIDA guides messages from attention to action; integration keeps messages consistent; the ASA enforces the CAP and BCAP Codes.

Name two sources of brand equity identified by Aaker.

Two of: brand loyalty, awareness, perceived quality and brand associations. From “Branding and brand equity” →

In plain English: Brands identify products and carry meaning that adds value. Key idea: Brands identify and differentiate products; brand equity is the added value from loyalty, awareness, perceived quality and associations; firms choose between family and individual branding and extensions.

How is customer acquisition cost calculated?

Marketing and sales spend divided by the number of new customers gained. From “Marketing metrics” →

In plain English: Marketers must show that their spending creates value. Key idea: Metrics such as market share, CAC, ROMI and CLV show whether marketing creates value; they should link to objectives and balance short-term sales with long-term brand effects.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Marketing

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which orientation starts by identifying what customers need? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Marketing orientation. A marketing orientation puts customer needs first.

  2. Which is an example of primary research? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    A survey of customers designed for a new product. Primary research collects new data for the purpose.

  3. Segmenting by lifestyle and values is which type of segmentation? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Psychographic. Psychographic segmentation uses lifestyle, values and personality.

  4. A firm makes the best-engineered typewriters but sales are falling as customers move to computers. Which mistake has it made? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Marketing myopia, defining itself by the product, not the need. The customer need is document production, which computers now meet better.

  5. A survey of 1,000 randomly chosen people finds 50% support. Roughly what is the 95% margin of error? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    ±3%. 1.96 × √(0.25 ÷ 1,000) ≈ 0.031, about ±3%.

  6. A small firm makes premium dog food only for owners of working dogs. Which targeting strategy is this? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Concentrated (niche) targeting. One offer aimed at a single segment is a niche strategy.

  7. A fast-food chain adds healthier options and reduces packaging waste while remaining profitable. Which approach does this reflect? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Societal marketing, balancing customers, profit and society. Societal marketing considers long-term wellbeing alongside needs and profit.

  8. A firm surveys only its own website visitors about a new product for the general public. What is the main weakness? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    The sample is biased towards existing customers. Website visitors may differ systematically from the wider target market.

  9. A perceptual map shows no brands in the low-price, high-quality corner. What should a firm consider before entering? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Whether customers believe the position is credible and the firm can deliver it profitably. Gaps may exist because they are unprofitable or not credible to customers.

Key takeaways

  • Marketing identifies and satisfies customer needs profitably; a marketing orientation starts with the customer, and defining a business by customer needs rather than products avoids marketing myopia.
  • Market research uses secondary and primary, quantitative and qualitative methods; good sampling and question design improve reliability, and research must follow data protection law and professional codes.
  • Segmentation divides a market into groups with similar needs; targeting chooses which to serve; positioning defines how the brand is seen relative to rivals, often mapped on two key dimensions.
  • Consumers move through need recognition, search, evaluation, purchase and post-purchase stages, influenced by cultural, social, personal and psychological factors and by predictable biases.
  • B2B markets have derived demand, few large professional buyers and formal processes; marketers must influence every role in the decision-making unit and build long-term relationships.
  • Products have core, actual and augmented levels; the product life cycle suggests different strategies at each stage, extension strategies prolong maturity, and diffusion theory explains how adoption spreads.

Common mistakes in Marketing

  • Equating marketing with advertising or selling. Promotion is one part of marketing, which begins with understanding customers and shapes products, prices and distribution.
  • Assuming a large sample is always reliable. A large convenience sample can be more biased than a small random one.
  • Choosing segments only because they are large. A segment must also be reachable and respond to a distinct offer the firm can deliver profitably.
  • Assuming consumers always act rationally. Habits, emotions and mental shortcuts shape many purchases.
  • Assuming B2B buyers are purely rational. Trust, relationships and personal risk to the buyer also shape decisions.
  • Treating the product life cycle as a forecasting tool. Its stages are clear only in hindsight, and marketing actions shape the curve.

Lessons in this school

  1. What is marketing?
    Marketing is the process of understanding customers and creating value for them profitably.
  2. Market research
    Market research gives businesses the information they need to understand customers and markets.
  3. Segmentation, targeting and positioning
    Few products appeal to everyone.
  4. Consumer behaviour
    Understanding why and how people buy helps businesses design and market their products.
  5. Business-to-business marketing
    Business-to-business (B2B) marketing sells to organisations rather than individual consumers.
  6. Product and the product life cycle
    The product is the core of the marketing mix.
  7. Pricing strategies
    Price is the only element of the marketing mix that directly generates revenue.
  8. Promotion and marketing communications
    Promotion communicates with customers to inform, persuade and remind them.
  9. Place and distribution channels
    Place is about getting products to customers where and when they want them.
  10. The extended marketing mix
    Services and customer experience need more than the four Ps.
  11. Branding and brand equity
    Brands identify products and carry meaning that adds value.
  12. Customer relationship management
    Keeping customers is usually cheaper than winning new ones.
  13. Services marketing
    Services differ from physical goods in ways that shape how they are marketed.
  14. Digital and social media marketing
    Digital channels have transformed how businesses reach and engage customers.
  15. Marketing metrics
    Marketers must show that their spending creates value.
  16. Sustainable and ethical marketing
    Marketing shapes consumption, so it carries responsibilities to society and the environment.
  17. International marketing
    Selling across borders brings opportunities and challenges.
  18. The marketing plan
    A marketing plan turns analysis into action.
