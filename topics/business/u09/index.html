Lessons in this school

  1. What is entrepreneurship?
    Entrepreneurship is the process of discovering, evaluating and exploiting opportunities to create new value.
  2. Opportunity recognition and the entrepreneurial mindset
    Entrepreneurs succeed by spotting and shaping opportunities.
  3. Effectuation and the lean start-up
    Entrepreneurs often cannot predict the future, so they act, learn and adapt.
  4. The business model canvas
    A business model describes how an organisation creates, delivers and captures value.
  5. The business plan
    A business plan sets out what a venture will do, for whom, and how it will make money.
  6. Funding a start-up
    New ventures need money before they earn it.
  7. Protecting ideas: intellectual property
    Intellectual property rights let creators and businesses control and profit from their ideas.
  8. Small business support in the UK
    A range of public and private organisations help people start and grow small businesses.
  9. Types of innovation
    Innovation means turning new ideas into value, and it takes many forms.
  10. Disruptive innovation
    Disruptive innovation explains how small entrants with simpler, cheaper products can displace established leaders.
  11. Diffusion of innovations
    New products spread through a population over time at different speeds.
  12. Open innovation
    Firms no longer rely only on their own laboratories for new ideas.
  13. Design thinking
    Design thinking is a human-centred approach to solving problems and developing innovations.
  14. Scaling a business
    Scaling means growing revenue much faster than costs.
  15. Social entrepreneurship
    Social entrepreneurs use business methods to tackle social and environmental problems.

Key terms

Entrepreneurship
The discovery, evaluation and exploitation of opportunities to create new value
Opportunity
A situation in which a new product, service or process can be introduced profitably
Effectuation
Entrepreneurial reasoning that starts from available means and lets goals emerge
Business model
How an organisation creates, delivers and captures value
Business plan
A document setting out a venture's objectives, strategy, operations and finances
Equity finance
Raising money by selling shares in the business
Patent
A right protecting a new, inventive and industrially applicable invention for a limited period
Unlimited liability
Personal responsibility for all the debts of the business
Innovation
The successful application of new ideas to create value
Disruptive innovation
An innovation that starts with overlooked customers and eventually displaces established firms
Diffusion
The process by which an innovation spreads among members of a social system over time
Open innovation
Using purposive inflows and outflows of knowledge to accelerate innovation
Design thinking
A human-centred, iterative approach to creative problem solving
Scale-up
A firm growing employees or turnover by over 20% a year for three years from at least 10 employees
