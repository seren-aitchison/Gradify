Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

Name three main activities of HRM.

Three of: workforce planning, recruitment, learning and development, performance management, reward, employee relations. From “What is human resource management?” →

In plain English: Human resource management (HRM) is the management of people at work to achieve organisational goals. Key idea: HRM manages people strategically through planning, recruitment, development, performance, reward and relations; the Harvard model links stakeholders and situational factors to HR choices and outcomes, and Ulrich defined strategic, change, administrative and employee roles.

What did the Supreme Court decide in Uber BV v Aslam?

That Uber drivers were workers, entitled to the minimum wage and holiday pay. From “Employment law essentials” →

In plain English: Employment law sets minimum rights for workers and duties for employers. Key idea: UK law distinguishes employees, workers and the self-employed; key rights include written particulars, minimum wage, working time limits and leave, equality and safety; dismissals must have a fair reason and fair procedure.

What is total reward?

All the financial and non-financial rewards employees receive. From “Reward and pay” →

In plain English: Reward systems attract, retain and motivate people. Key idea: Total reward combines pay, variable pay, benefits and non-financial rewards; job evaluation supports fair pay structures and equal pay; employers with 250 or more staff must report their gender pay gap.

What is the pluralist view of the employment relationship?

Different groups have legitimate competing interests, so conflict is normal and managed through negotiation. From “Employment relations and trade unions” →

In plain English: Employment relations concerns the relationship between employers, employees and their representatives. Key idea: Unitarist, pluralist and radical perspectives view conflict differently; trade unions bargain collectively and support members; ACAS conciliates, mediates and arbitrates, and early conciliation precedes most tribunal claims.

Who can accompany an employee to a disciplinary hearing?

A work colleague or a trade union representative. From “Discipline, grievance and conflict” →

In plain English: Fair procedures for discipline and grievances protect employees and employers. Key idea: The ACAS Code sets out fair disciplinary and grievance steps: investigate, inform, meet, allow accompaniment, decide and appeal; tribunals can adjust awards by up to 25% for unreasonable failure to follow it; mediation can resolve conflict informally.

Quick quiz: test yourself on People Management

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which of Ulrich's roles focuses on representing employees' concerns? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Employee champion. The employee champion builds commitment and voice.

  2. Which stage of workforce planning compares future demand with supply? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Gap analysis. Gap analysis identifies shortages and surpluses.

  3. Which document lists the skills and experience needed for a role? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Person specification. The person specification describes the ideal candidate.

  4. In the Harvard model, which outcome refers to employees' skills? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Competence. Applying the standard result: Competence is one of the four Cs.

  5. A firm with an average of 400 staff has 60 leavers in a year. What is labour turnover? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    15%. Applying the standard result: 60 ÷ 400 × 100 = 15%.

  6. Why are structured interviews more valid than unstructured ones? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Standard questions and scoring reduce bias and focus on job-relevant criteria. Consistency improves prediction and fairness.

  7. An HR department spends all its time on payroll and contracts. What does Ulrich's model suggest is missing? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Strategic and change roles linking HR to business goals. Ulrich argued HR should add strategic value beyond administration.

  8. A firm's turnover is high, but its stability index is also high. What does this suggest? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Leavers are mainly recent starters, so onboarding may be the problem. High stability means longer-serving staff are staying.

  9. An employer asks candidates about their health before making a job offer, without a valid reason. What is the issue? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    It may breach section 60 of the Equality Act 2010. Pre-offer health questions are restricted to limited purposes.

Key takeaways

  • HRM manages people strategically through planning, recruitment, development, performance, reward and relations; the Harvard model links stakeholders and situational factors to HR choices and outcomes, and Ulrich defined strategic, change, administrative and employee roles.
  • Workforce planning forecasts demand and supply, identifies gaps and plans actions; turnover and stability indices measure staff movement, and succession planning prepares for key roles.
  • Recruitment starts from job analysis and clear specifications; structured interviews, work samples and ability tests predict performance best; UK law requires fair, non-discriminatory selection and right-to-work checks.
  • UK law distinguishes employees, workers and the self-employed; key rights include written particulars, minimum wage, working time limits and leave, equality and safety; dismissals must have a fair reason and fair procedure.
  • L&D starts with a needs analysis, draws on learning theories such as Kolb's cycle, uses on- and off-the-job methods and apprenticeships, and should be evaluated beyond reactions, up to behaviour and results.
  • Performance management plans, monitors and reviews work against objectives; methods include ratings, 360-degree feedback and, less now, forced ranking; many firms favour continuous coaching conversations over annual appraisals.

Common mistakes in People Management

  • Treating HR as only an administrative function. Its strategic role in capability and culture is central to modern HRM.
  • Relying on turnover alone. A stability index shows whether experienced staff stay or whether turnover is concentrated among new starters.
  • Relying on unstructured interviews and "gut feeling". They predict performance poorly and are prone to bias.
  • Labelling someone self-employed to avoid obligations. Courts look at the reality of the relationship, as in Uber v Aslam.
  • Evaluating training only by "happy sheets". Positive reactions do not show that behaviour or results changed.
  • Saving all feedback for the annual review. Feedback is most useful when timely and specific.

Lessons in this school

  1. What is human resource management?
    Human resource management (HRM) is the management of people at work to achieve organisational goals.
  2. Workforce planning
    Workforce planning ensures an organisation has the right people, with the right skills, in the right place at the right time.
  3. Recruitment and selection
    Recruitment attracts candidates and selection chooses the best.
  4. Employment law essentials
    Employment law sets minimum rights for workers and duties for employers.
  5. Learning and development
    Learning and development (L&D) builds the skills an organisation needs.
  6. Performance management
    Performance management aligns individual work with organisational goals.
  7. Reward and pay
    Reward systems attract, retain and motivate people.
  8. Talent management
    Talent management attracts, develops and retains people with high performance and potential.
  9. Employee engagement and wellbeing
    Engaged and well employees perform better and are more likely to stay.
  10. Employment relations and trade unions
    Employment relations concerns the relationship between employers, employees and their representatives.
  11. Equality, diversity and inclusion
    Equality, diversity and inclusion (EDI) aims to treat people fairly, value differences and ensure everyone can contribute.
  12. Flexible working and the future of work
    Work is changing through flexible and hybrid working, technology and new forms of employment.
  13. Discipline, grievance and conflict
    Fair procedures for discipline and grievances protect employees and employers.
  14. Strategic HRM
    Strategic human resource management aligns people practices with organisational strategy.
  15. HR analytics
    HR analytics uses data to understand and improve people management.

People Management: questions students ask

What are Ulrich's four HR roles?

Strategic partner, change agent, administrative expert and employee champion.

How is labour turnover calculated?

Leavers in a period divided by average number employed, × 100.

What is the difference between a job description and a person specification?

A job description sets out duties; a person specification sets out the skills and qualities needed.

What are Kirkpatrick's four levels of evaluation?

Reaction, learning, behaviour and results.
