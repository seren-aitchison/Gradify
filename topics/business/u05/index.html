Lessons in this school

  1. What is operations management?
    Operations management is about how organisations create and deliver their goods and services.
  2. The transformation model and the four Vs
    All operations transform inputs into outputs.
  3. Process types and layouts
    The way work flows through an operation depends on its process type and layout.
  4. Capacity management
    Capacity management matches an operation's ability to produce with demand.
  5. Quality management
    Quality is central to customer satisfaction and cost.
  6. Total quality management
    Total quality management (TQM) makes quality the responsibility of everyone in an organisation.
  7. Six Sigma
    Six Sigma is a data-driven approach to reducing variation and defects.
  8. Lean production
    Lean production, developed at Toyota, aims to deliver value with minimum waste.
  9. Supply chain management
    Products reach customers through networks of suppliers, manufacturers, distributors and retailers.
  10. Inventory management
    Inventory protects against uncertainty but ties up cash.
  11. Just-in-time
    Just-in-time (JIT) aims to produce and deliver exactly what is needed, when it is needed.
  12. Procurement and supplier relationships
    Procurement obtains the goods and services an organisation needs, and supplier relationships shape cost, quality and risk.
  13. Logistics
    Logistics moves and stores goods through supply chains.
  14. Supply chain risk and resilience
    Supply chains face disruptions from natural disasters, pandemics, conflicts and accidents.
  15. Productivity and efficiency
    Productivity measures how well inputs are turned into outputs.
  16. Project management
    Projects are temporary endeavours with defined goals.

Key terms

Operations management
Managing the resources that create and deliver products and services
Transformed resources
Inputs changed by the operation, such as materials, information or customers
Batch process
Producing groups of identical items together
Effective capacity
The realistic maximum output after planned losses
Conformance quality
Meeting a product's design specification
Total quality management
An organisation-wide approach making quality everyone's responsibility
Six Sigma
A data-driven approach to reducing process variation and defects
Lean production
An approach to delivering value with minimum waste
Supply chain
The network of organisations involved in producing and delivering a product
Safety stock
Extra inventory held to protect against variation in demand or lead time
Just-in-time
Producing and delivering exactly what is needed, when needed
Total cost of ownership
The full cost of acquiring, using and disposing of a purchased item
Logistics
Managing the movement and storage of goods through supply chains
Resilience
The ability of a supply chain to withstand and recover from disruption
