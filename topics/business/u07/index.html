Lessons in this school

  1. Why managers need finance
    Every management decision has a financial side.
  2. The income statement
    The income statement shows whether a business made a profit over a period.
  3. The statement of financial position
    The statement of financial position, or balance sheet, shows what a business owns and owes at one moment.
  4. The statement of cash flows
    The statement of cash flows explains how cash changed over a period.
  5. Profitability ratios
    Ratios turn raw figures into comparable measures.
  6. Liquidity and efficiency ratios
    A business must be able to pay its bills and manage its working capital.
  7. Gearing and investor ratios
    How a business is financed affects its risk and the returns to shareholders.
  8. Costs and break-even analysis
    Knowing how costs behave helps managers set prices and targets.
  9. Contribution and short-term decisions
    Many short-term decisions depend on contribution rather than full cost.
  10. Budgeting
    A budget turns plans into numbers.
  11. Variance analysis
    Variance analysis compares actual results with the budget to explain what went right or wrong.
  12. Cash flow forecasting
    A cash flow forecast predicts when money will come in and go out.
  13. Sources of finance
    Businesses fund themselves from many sources, each with different costs, risks and control implications.
  14. Investment appraisal: payback and ARR
    Capital investment decisions commit large sums for years.
  15. Net present value
    Net present value is the most widely recommended appraisal method because it allows for the time value of money.
  16. Working capital management
    Working capital is the money tied up in day-to-day operations.
  17. The cost of capital
    The cost of capital is the return investors require, and it sets the hurdle rate for new investments.

Key terms

Financial accounting
Reporting past performance and position to external users
Gross profit
Revenue minus cost of sales
Asset
A resource controlled by the business expected to bring future benefits
Operating cash flow
Cash generated from the main trading activities
Gross profit margin
Gross profit as a percentage of revenue
Current ratio
Current assets divided by current liabilities
Gearing
The proportion of long-term finance provided by borrowing
Contribution
Selling price minus variable cost
Relevant cost
A future cash cost that differs between decision options
Master budget
The combined budgeted income statement, cash budget and statement of financial position
Flexed budget
A budget recalculated at the actual level of activity
Cash flow forecast
A projection of future cash receipts and payments
Equity finance
Funds raised by selling a share of ownership
Payback period
Time taken for cash inflows to repay the investment
