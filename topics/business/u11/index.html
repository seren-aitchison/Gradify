Lessons in this school

  1. Ethical theories in business
    Business decisions often raise questions of right and wrong that law alone does not answer.
  2. Ethical decision-making
    Knowing ethical theory does not guarantee ethical behaviour.
  3. Corporate social responsibility
    Corporate social responsibility asks what businesses owe society beyond making profits.
  4. Shareholder and stakeholder theories of the firm
    Whose interests should a company serve?
  5. Principles of corporate governance
    Corporate governance is the system by which companies are directed and controlled.
  6. The UK Corporate Governance Code
    Listed companies in the UK follow the UK Corporate Governance Code on a comply or explain basis.
  7. Directors' duties
    Company directors have legal duties set out in the Companies Act 2006.
  8. Agency theory
    Agency theory explains the conflicts that arise when owners delegate decisions to managers.
  9. Executive pay
    Executive pay is one of the most debated areas of corporate governance.
  10. ESG: environmental, social and governance
    ESG describes how investors and companies assess environmental, social and governance factors.
  11. Sustainability and the triple bottom line
    Sustainable development meets present needs without compromising the future.
  12. Climate change and business
    Climate change creates risks and opportunities for almost every business.
  13. Sustainability reporting
    Companies increasingly report on sustainability alongside their financial results.
  14. Modern slavery and supply chain ethics
    Global supply chains can hide forced labour and serious abuses.
  15. Bribery and corruption
    Bribery distorts markets and damages trust.
  16. Whistleblowing
    Whistleblowers often expose wrongdoing that other controls miss.
  17. Corporate failures: lessons from case studies
    High-profile corporate collapses reveal how ethics and governance fail in practice.

Key terms

Utilitarianism
The theory that the right action produces the greatest good for the greatest number
Moral awareness
Recognising that a situation raises an ethical issue
Corporate social responsibility
Business responsibilities to society beyond legal and economic obligations
Shareholder primacy
The view that a company should be run mainly to maximise value for its shareholders
Corporate governance
The system by which companies are directed and controlled
Comply or explain
A system where companies follow provisions or explain why they do not
Section 174
The duty to exercise reasonable care, skill and diligence
Principal
The party who delegates work or decisions to another
Long-term incentive plan
A scheme awarding shares if performance targets are met over several years
ESG
Environmental, social and governance factors used to assess companies and investments
Sustainable development
Development that meets present needs without compromising future generations' ability to meet theirs
Net zero
Balancing greenhouse gases emitted with those removed, so the net total is zero
Financial materiality
Sustainability information that could affect a company's value and prospects
Modern slavery
Slavery, servitude, forced labour and human trafficking
