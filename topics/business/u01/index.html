Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check.

Name the four factors of production.

Land, labour, capital and enterprise. From “What is a business?” →

What is the third sector?

Independent, not-for-private-profit organisations, such as charities, voluntary and community groups and social enterprises, that reinvest surpluses in a social purpose. From “The private, public and third sectors” →

What is an asset lock?

A legal restriction on a CIC that prevents its assets and profits being transferred away from its community purpose, including a cap on dividends. From “Social enterprises and co-operatives” →

What is a niche market?

A small, specialised segment of a larger market, with distinct needs that a few firms target. From “Markets and competition” →

Give one business opportunity created by an ageing population.

Higher demand for health and care services, accessible products, retirement housing or later-life financial services. From “Technology and social change” →

Lessons in this school

  1. What is a business?
    A business is an organisation that combines resources to produce goods or services that meet customers' needs, usually in exchange for payment.
  2. Business purpose and objectives
    Every organisation needs to know what it is for and what it is trying to achieve.
  3. Stakeholders
    Stakeholders are the individuals and groups who affect or are affected by a business.
  4. The private, public and third sectors
    The UK economy is organised into three broad sectors by who owns organisations and why they exist: the private sector, the public sector and the third (voluntary and community) sector.
  5. Sole traders and partnerships
    Most UK businesses are run by one person or a small group without a separate legal identity.
  6. Private and public limited companies
    A company is a separate legal person, owned by shareholders and run by directors, whose owners have limited liability.
  7. Social enterprises and co-operatives
    Not every business is owned by investors seeking returns.
  8. Franchising
    Franchising lets a business grow by licensing others to run outlets under its brand and system.
  9. PESTLE analysis
    Businesses operate in an external environment they can't control but must anticipate.
  10. Markets and competition
    A market is where buyers and sellers exchange goods or services.
  11. The economic environment
    Every business is affected by the state of the wider economy.
  12. The legal and regulatory environment
    Law sets the rules within which businesses compete.
  13. Technology and social change
    Technological and social change create new markets and destroy old ones.
  14. Globalisation and international business
    Globalisation is the growing integration of the world's economies through trade, investment, technology and people.
  15. Business size and growth
    Businesses range from one-person enterprises to global corporations.
  16. Why businesses fail
    Many new businesses close within a few years, and even large, famous firms collapse.

Key terms

Business
An organisation that combines resources to produce goods or services for customers
Mission statement
A short public statement of an organisation's purpose and values
Stakeholder
Any individual or group that affects or is affected by a business's activities
Private sector
Organisations owned by individuals or shareholders, usually for profit
Sole trader
A business owned by one person, with no separate legal identity from the owner
Incorporation
Registering a company so that it becomes a separate legal person
Social enterprise
A business that trades for a social or environmental purpose and reinvests most surpluses in that purpose
Franchise
A licence to trade under another firm's brand and business system in return for fees
PESTLE analysis
A framework for scanning political, economic, social, technological, legal and environmental factors in the external environment
Market
Any arrangement in which buyers and sellers exchange a good or service
Gross domestic product
The total value of goods and services produced in an economy over a period
Regulation
Rules made by government or regulators that control how businesses operate
Process innovation
A new or improved way of producing or delivering a product
Globalisation
The increasing integration of economies through trade, investment, technology and the movement of people
