Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What are the five components of an information system?

Hardware, software, data, procedures and people. From “Information systems in organisations” →

In plain English: Every organisation runs on information. Key idea: An information system combines hardware, software, data, procedures and people; TPS record transactions, MIS report on them and DSS support analysis, and systems add value only when processes and people adapt.

What is an indirect network effect?

When more users on one side of a platform attract more users on the other side. From “Platforms and network effects” →

In plain English: Some of the world's most valuable firms are platforms that connect users rather than make products. Key idea: Platforms connect two or more user groups; direct and indirect network effects make them more valuable as they grow, which can tip markets to a dominant firm and attract regulation.

What does a correlation coefficient of −0.9 show?

A strong negative linear relationship. From “Correlation and regression for forecasting” →

In plain English: Businesses often want to predict one variable from another, such as sales from advertising. Key idea: Correlation measures the strength of a linear relationship from −1 to +1; a regression line y = a + bx predicts y from x, most reliably with strong correlation, large samples and interpolation.

Who enforces data protection law in the UK?

The Information Commissioner's Office (ICO). From “Data protection and UK GDPR” →

In plain English: Businesses hold large amounts of personal data, and the law controls how they use it. Key idea: The UK GDPR requires personal data to be processed lawfully, for limited purposes, minimally, accurately, securely and for no longer than needed; individuals have rights, serious breaches must be reported within 72 hours, and fines can be large.

What is software as a service?

Ready-to-use applications delivered over the internet, usually by subscription. From “Cloud computing” →

In plain English: Cloud computing lets businesses rent computing power and software over the internet. Key idea: Cloud computing provides elastic, on-demand, pay-per-use computing as IaaS, PaaS or SaaS on public, private or hybrid clouds; it cuts upfront costs and adds flexibility but brings lock-in, outage and security responsibilities.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Digital Business and Information Systems

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which system records day-to-day sales and payroll? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    A transaction processing system. Transaction processing systems capture routine transactions as they happen.

  2. A council lets residents book bin collections online instead of by phone. This is best described as (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Digitalisation. It uses digital technology to improve an existing process.

  3. A streaming service charges £9.99 a month for access. Which revenue model is this? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Subscription. Customers pay regularly for continued access to the service.

  4. A manager tests how a 5% price rise would affect profit. Which system suits this best? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    A decision support system. Decision support systems allow what-if modelling of semi-structured problems.

  5. Which is the most common reason transformation programmes fail? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    They are treated as IT projects without changing culture and processes. Transformation is a business change, not just a technology purchase.

  6. Why does e-commerce often increase price competition? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Customers can compare prices quickly across many sellers. Price comparison is easy online, which squeezes margins.

  7. Why do some expensive information systems fail to add value? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Processes and staff do not change to use them well. Systems create value only when combined with redesigned processes and trained people.

  8. Why are legacy systems a barrier to transformation? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    They are embedded in core processes, so replacing them is costly and risky. Old systems often run critical operations and are expensive to replace.

  9. Which firm benefits most from an omnichannel strategy? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    A retailer whose customers browse online but like to collect or try in store. Omnichannel joins online and physical channels for customers who use both.

Key takeaways

  • An information system combines hardware, software, data, procedures and people; TPS record transactions, MIS report on them and DSS support analysis, and systems add value only when processes and people adapt.
  • Digitisation converts information, digitalisation improves processes, and digital transformation changes the whole business; it is driven by customers, technology and competition, and often fails through weak leadership and resistance to change.
  • E-commerce can be B2C, B2B, C2C or C2B, earning revenue through direct sales, subscriptions, advertising, commission or freemium models; it widens reach and cuts costs but raises competition, delivery and security challenges.
  • Platforms connect two or more user groups; direct and indirect network effects make them more valuable as they grow, which can tip markets to a dominant firm and attract regulation.
  • Data-driven decisions use accurate, timely evidence through descriptive, diagnostic, predictive and prescriptive analytics, but data must be combined with judgement because of bias, false causation and gaming of targets.
  • Business intelligence moves data through ETL into a warehouse and onto dashboards; good dashboards show a few clearly defined KPIs against targets on one screen.

Common mistakes in Digital Business and Information Systems

  • Treating information systems as just computers. Procedures and people are equally part of the system.
  • Thinking transformation is mainly about technology. Culture, skills and processes are usually harder to change than systems.
  • Assuming B2C retail is the largest form of e-commerce. By value, trade between businesses is much larger.
  • Assuming network effects always grow forever. Congestion, poor quality and multi-homing can weaken them.
  • Assuming more data always means better decisions. Poor-quality or irrelevant data can mislead.
  • Packing a dashboard with every available metric. Too much information hides the problems that matter.
