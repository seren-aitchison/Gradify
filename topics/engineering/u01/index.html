Lessons in this school

  1. SI units and dimensional analysis
    Every engineering number carries a unit, and every valid equation balances its dimensions.
  2. Uncertainty, significant figures and estimation
    No measurement is exact, and an engineering result is only useful with an honest statement of how uncertain it is.
  3. Engineering models and assumptions
    Engineers predict how things will behave by building models: simplified representations that can be calculated.
  4. Functions and graphs in engineering
    Engineers describe how one quantity depends on another with functions and read behaviour off graphs.
  5. Exponentials and logarithms in engineering
    Whenever a rate of change is proportional to the amount present, the result is exponential growth or decay.
  6. Trigonometry and sinusoids
    Trigonometry resolves forces into components and describes everything that oscillates: alternating current, vibration and rotating machinery.
  7. Complex numbers and phasors
    Complex numbers make AC circuits, vibrations and control systems solvable with algebra instead of trigonometry.
  8. Vectors and their products
    Forces, velocities, moments and fields all have direction as well as size.
  9. Matrices and simultaneous equations
    Engineering analysis often produces many linear equations at once: forces at the joints of a truss, currents in a circuit, displacements in a finite element model.
  10. Eigenvalues and eigenvectors
    An eigenvector is a direction a matrix only stretches, and its eigenvalue is the stretch factor.
  11. Differentiation and rates of change
    The derivative measures how fast one quantity changes with another.
  12. Partial differentiation and error propagation
    Most engineering quantities depend on several variables.
  13. Integration in engineering
    Integration adds up a quantity that varies continuously: work from a varying force, displacement from velocity, charge from current, and the centroids and second moments that describe a cross-section.
  14. Taylor series and linearisation
    Near any point, a smooth function looks like a polynomial.
  15. First-order differential equations
    A first-order differential equation relates a quantity to its own rate of change.
  16. Second-order differential equations
    Systems with two energy stores, such as a mass and a spring or an inductor and a capacitor, obey second-order differential equations.
  17. Numerical methods
    Most real engineering equations have no neat closed-form answer, so they are solved numerically.
  18. Engineering statistics
    Materials, measurements and manufactured parts all vary.
  19. Probability and reliability
    Reliability engineering asks how likely a component or system is to keep working.

Key terms

Dimensional homogeneity
The requirement that every term in an equation has the same dimensions
Systematic error
An error that shifts every reading in the same direction and is not reduced by repetition
Validation
Checking a model against real measurements to show it represents the physical system adequately
Gradient
The rate of change of one quantity with respect to another, the slope of its graph
Time constant
The time for a first-order exponential response to complete 63.2% of its change
Angular frequency
ω = 2πf, the rate of change of phase in radians per second
Phasor
A complex number giving the amplitude and phase of a sinusoid at a known frequency
Unit vector
A vector of magnitude one giving a direction
Singular matrix
A square matrix with zero determinant and no inverse
Eigenvalue
A scalar λ for which Av = λv has a non-zero solution v
Derivative
The instantaneous rate of change of a function, the limit of the gradient of a chord
Partial derivative
The rate of change of a function with respect to one variable, all others held constant
Definite integral
The limit of a sum of f(x)δx between two limits, equal to F(b) − F(a)
Linearisation
Replacing a function near a point by its tangent: f(a) + f'(a)(x − a)
