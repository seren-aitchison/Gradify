Lessons in this school

  1. Forces as vectors
    A force has size and direction, so it is a vector.
  2. Moments and couples
    A moment is the turning effect of a force about a point or axis.
  3. Equivalent force systems
    Any system of forces and couples on a rigid body can be replaced by a simpler equivalent system.
  4. Free-body diagrams
    A free-body diagram isolates a body and shows every external force and moment acting on it.
  5. Equilibrium of a particle
    A particle is in equilibrium when the resultant force on it is zero.
  6. Equilibrium of a rigid body in two dimensions
    A rigid body is in equilibrium when both the resultant force and the resultant moment are zero.
  7. Equilibrium in three dimensions
    In three dimensions a rigid body has six equilibrium equations: three for forces and three for moments.
  8. Statical determinacy and stability
    A structure is statically determinate if equilibrium alone can find all its reactions and internal forces.
  9. Plane trusses: the method of joints
    A truss is a framework of straight members joined at their ends.
  10. Plane trusses: the method of sections
    The method of sections finds the forces in particular truss members directly, by cutting the truss and applying equilibrium to one part.
  11. Frames and machines
    Frames and machines are structures with at least one multi-force member, so their members carry bending as well as axial force.
  12. Distributed loads
    Many loads are spread along a beam rather than acting at a point: self-weight, floor loads, snow, water pressure.
  13. Centroids and centres of gravity
    The centre of gravity is where a body's weight acts; the centroid is the geometric centre of a shape.
  14. Second moment of area
    The second moment of area measures how a cross-section's area is distributed about an axis, and so how well it resists bending.
  15. Dry friction
    Dry friction resists sliding between surfaces in contact.
  16. Shear force and bending moment diagrams
    Shear force and bending moment diagrams show how internal forces vary along a beam, and so where it is most highly stressed.

Key terms

Resultant
The single force with the same effect as a system of forces acting at a point
Moment
The turning effect of a force, equal to force times perpendicular distance from the point
Equivalent system
A system with the same resultant force and resultant moment about every point
Free-body diagram
A sketch of a body isolated from its surroundings showing all external forces and moments on it
Particle
A body whose size is negligible for the analysis, so all forces act through one point
Simply supported beam
A beam with a pin at one end and a roller at the other
Ball-and-socket joint
A support preventing translation in all three directions but allowing rotation, with three force reactions
Statically determinate
Able to be fully analysed using equilibrium equations alone
Truss
A structure of straight members joined at their ends to form triangles
Method of sections
Finding member forces by cutting a truss and applying equilibrium to one part
Frame
A stationary structure with at least one multi-force member
Distributed load
A load spread over a length or area, described by its intensity
Centroid
The geometric centre of a shape
Second moment of area
A property of a cross-section, ∫y2 dA, measuring its resistance to bending about an axis
