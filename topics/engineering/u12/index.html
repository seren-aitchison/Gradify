Lessons in this school

  1. The project lifecycle and planning
    Engineering work is usually delivered as projects with a defined goal, budget and timescale.
  2. Scheduling: Gantt charts and the critical path
    A schedule shows when each activity happens and which activities control the finish date.
  3. Engineering economics
    Engineers must show that projects are worth their cost.
  4. Risk management in projects
    Every project faces uncertain events that could harm or help its objectives.
  5. Teamwork and roles
    Engineering is almost always a team activity.
  6. Technical report writing
    Technical reports communicate engineering work so that others can understand, check and act on it.
  7. Presentations and technical communication
    Engineers must explain their work to clients, colleagues and the public.
  8. Searching literature and standards
    Engineers need reliable information from journals, books, standards and data.
  9. Laboratory practice and experimental design
    Laboratory work tests ideas and generates data that others rely on.
  10. Professional registration: EngTech, IEng and CEng
    Professional registration shows that an engineer has met recognised standards of competence and commitment.
  11. The UK-SPEC competences
    UK-SPEC describes the competence and commitment expected of registered engineers and technicians.
  12. Continuing professional development
    Engineering knowledge changes quickly, so registered engineers must keep learning throughout their careers.
  13. Intellectual property and contracts
    Engineers create valuable ideas and work under legal agreements.
  14. The individual project
    Most engineering degrees end with an individual project in which students investigate a problem independently.

Key terms

Work breakdown structure
A hierarchical breakdown of a project deliverable into manageable work packages
Critical path
The longest-duration path through an activity network, setting the minimum project duration
Present value
The value today of a future cash flow, found by discounting
Risk register
A record of identified risks, their assessment, owners and responses
Storming
A team stage in which conflicts over goals, roles and methods emerge
Abstract
A short summary of a report's purpose, method, results and conclusions
Key message
The main point the audience should remember, stated in one or two sentences
Peer review
Evaluation of a research paper by independent experts before publication
Control variable
A factor kept constant so it does not affect the result
Chartered Engineer
The professional title for engineers who develop solutions through innovation, creativity and change
UK-SPEC
The Engineering Council's standard of competence and commitment for registration
Continuing professional development
Systematic, lifelong maintenance and improvement of professional knowledge and skills
Patent
A time-limited right to stop others exploiting an invention, granted in exchange for publishing it
Aim
The overall goal of a project
