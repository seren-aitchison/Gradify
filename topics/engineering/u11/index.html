Lessons in this school

  1. The statement of ethical principles
    The Engineering Council and the Royal Academy of Engineering publish a joint Statement of Ethical Principles that guides all UK engineers.
  2. Professional responsibility and whistleblowing
    Engineers have duties to their employers, clients, profession and the public, and these can conflict.
  3. Learning from engineering failures
    Many advances in engineering have come from studying failures.
  4. Codes, standards and regulation
    Engineers work within a framework of laws, regulations, codes of practice and standards.
  5. Health and safety law and the HSE
    The Health and Safety at Work etc.
  6. Risk assessment
    Risk assessment is the legal and practical basis for controlling workplace hazards.
  7. Hazard analysis: FMEA and HAZOP
    Systematic hazard analysis finds problems before they cause harm.
  8. Human factors and safety culture
    Most accidents involve people as well as hardware.
  9. Sustainable development and engineering
    Sustainable development asks engineers to meet today's needs without harming the ability of future generations to meet theirs.
  10. Life-cycle assessment
    Life-cycle assessment measures the environmental impacts of a product or service from raw materials to disposal.
  11. Energy systems and net zero
    The UK has a legal target of net zero greenhouse gas emissions by 2050.
  12. The circular economy and materials efficiency
    A circular economy keeps products and materials in use for as long as possible instead of making, using and throwing away.
  13. Embodied carbon in infrastructure
    Building and maintaining infrastructure releases large amounts of carbon before any user benefits.
  14. Engineering for global development
    Billions of people still lack safe water, sanitation, energy and transport.
  15. Diversity and inclusion in engineering
    Engineering serves everyone, so it benefits from people with different backgrounds and from designs that work for all users.

Key terms

Statement of Ethical Principles
The joint Engineering Council and Royal Academy of Engineering statement of values for UK engineers
Whistleblowing
Disclosing wrongdoing or danger in the public interest, inside or outside an organisation
Root cause
The underlying reason for a failure which, if removed, prevents it recurring
Standard
A consensus document setting out specifications, methods or good practice
Reasonably practicable
Required unless the cost of a measure is grossly disproportionate to the risk reduction
Hazard
Something with the potential to cause harm
Failure mode
A way in which a component or process can fail
Slip
An action carried out differently from what was intended
Sustainable development
Development that meets present needs without compromising future generations' ability to meet theirs
Functional unit
The quantified service used as the basis for comparing options in an LCA
Net zero
A state where remaining greenhouse gas emissions are balanced by removals
Circular economy
An economic system that keeps products and materials in use and designs out waste
Embodied carbon
Greenhouse gas emissions from producing, transporting, constructing, maintaining and disposing of an asset
Appropriate technology
Technology suited to local skills, resources and needs that users can afford and maintain
