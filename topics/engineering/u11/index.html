Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

Which two bodies publish the Statement of Ethical Principles?

The Engineering Council and the Royal Academy of Engineering. From “The statement of ethical principles” →

In plain English: The Engineering Council and the Royal Academy of Engineering publish a joint Statement of Ethical Principles that guides all UK engineers. Key idea: The Statement of Ethical Principles sets out four principles, honesty and integrity, respect for life, law, the environment and public good, accuracy and rigour, and leadership and communication, which underpin every institution's code of conduct.

What is the UK national standards body?

The British Standards Institution (BSI). From “Codes, standards and regulation” →

In plain English: Engineers work within a framework of laws, regulations, codes of practice and standards. Key idea: Acts and regulations are legally binding, codes of practice explain how to comply, and standards from BSI, CEN and ISO are usually voluntary but are the main way to show that designs meet legal requirements.

How is the risk priority number calculated?

Severity times occurrence times detection. From “Hazard analysis: FMEA and HAZOP” →

In plain English: Systematic hazard analysis finds problems before they cause harm. Key idea: FMEA works bottom-up through failure modes, ranking them by RPN = S x O x D; HAZOP uses guide words with process parameters to explore deviations; and fault tree analysis works top-down from an undesired event.

What are the four phases of an LCA?

Goal and scope, inventory analysis, impact assessment and interpretation. From “Life-cycle assessment” →

In plain English: Life-cycle assessment measures the environmental impacts of a product or service from raw materials to disposal. Key idea: LCA follows ISO 14040 and 14044 through goal and scope, inventory, impact assessment and interpretation; the functional unit and system boundary make comparisons fair, and sensitivity analysis tests the conclusions.

Which life-cycle modules cover the product stage?

Modules A1 to A3. From “Embodied carbon in infrastructure” →

In plain English: Building and maintaining infrastructure releases large amounts of carbon before any user benefits. Key idea: Embodied carbon covers emissions from materials, construction, maintenance and end of life, reported in EN 15978 modules A to D; it is estimated as quantity times carbon factor, and PAS 2080 guides reduction through avoid, switch and improve.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Engineering in Society

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which of these is one of the four principles in the Statement of Ethical Principles? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Accuracy and rigour. Accuracy and rigour means working within competence and using reliable evidence.

  2. What is whistleblowing? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Disclosing wrongdoing or danger in the public interest. Whistleblowing concerns wrongdoing such as dangers to health and safety or illegal acts.

  3. Which failure led to UK rules on disproportionate collapse of buildings? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Ronan Point, 1968. A gas explosion at Ronan Point caused progressive collapse of one corner of the block.

  4. How do the professional engineering institutions use the Statement of Ethical Principles? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Their codes of conduct are built on it. Each institution's code of conduct draws on the joint statement and is binding on members.

  5. Under UK law, a disclosure to which of these is normally protected? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    The Health and Safety Executive about a safety risk. The HSE is a prescribed person for health and safety, so such disclosures are protected.

  6. What was the main technical cause of the Tacoma Narrows Bridge collapse? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Wind-driven torsional oscillation of the deck. The slender deck suffered aeroelastic flutter, twisting until it failed.

  7. An engineer is offered an expensive gift by a supplier bidding for a contract she will assess. Which response best follows the principles? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Decline the gift and declare the approach. Honesty and integrity require rejecting inducements and declaring possible conflicts of interest.

  8. An engineer's internal safety concern has been ignored for months and the risk is serious. What is the most appropriate next step? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Report to the relevant regulator, keeping records. When internal routes fail on a serious risk, disclosure to a prescribed regulator is protected and responsible.

  9. In the Hyatt Regency collapse, a change from one continuous rod to two shorter rods mainly (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    doubled the load on the upper connection. With two rods the upper nut carried both walkways, so its load doubled.

Key takeaways

  • The Statement of Ethical Principles sets out four principles, honesty and integrity, respect for life, law, the environment and public good, accuracy and rigour, and leadership and communication, which underpin every institution's code of conduct.
  • Engineers owe duties to employers, the profession and above all the public; the Public Interest Disclosure Act 1998 protects workers who make protected disclosures, ideally raised internally first and then to a prescribed person such as the HSE.
  • Failures such as Tacoma Narrows, the Comet, Ronan Point and the Hyatt Regency walkways have technical, organisational and human causes, and their investigations have reshaped codes, testing and checking of design changes.
  • Acts and regulations are legally binding, codes of practice explain how to comply, and standards from BSI, CEN and ISO are usually voluntary but are the main way to show that designs meet legal requirements.
  • The HSWA 1974 places goal-setting duties on employers, designers and employees to ensure safety so far as is reasonably practicable, and the HSE enforces it with inspections, improvement and prohibition notices and prosecution.
  • Risk combines likelihood and severity; the HSE approach identifies hazards, who is harmed, evaluates and controls risks, records and reviews, using the hierarchy of control from elimination to PPE and reducing tolerable risks ALARP.

Common mistakes in Engineering in Society

  • Thinking that ethics only matters for senior engineers. Every registered engineer and technician, including graduates, is expected to follow the principles from the start of their career.
  • Going straight to the press with a concern. Media disclosures are protected only in limited circumstances; internal routes and prescribed regulators usually come first.
  • Blaming a single person or component. Most failures have several interacting causes, including organisational ones, and investigations look for root causes.
  • Assuming that a standard is the law. Most standards are voluntary; it is the regulations that are binding, although following designated standards is strong evidence of compliance.
  • Thinking that reasonably practicable means doing only what is cheap. Measures must be taken unless their cost is grossly disproportionate to the risk reduction.
  • Reaching for PPE first. PPE is the last line of defence; eliminating, substituting or engineering out the hazard comes first.

Lessons in this school

  1. The statement of ethical principles
    The Engineering Council and the Royal Academy of Engineering publish a joint Statement of Ethical Principles that guides all UK engineers.
  2. Professional responsibility and whistleblowing
    Engineers have duties to their employers, clients, profession and the public, and these can conflict.
  3. Learning from engineering failures
    Many advances in engineering have come from studying failures.
  4. Codes, standards and regulation
    Engineers work within a framework of laws, regulations, codes of practice and standards.
  5. Health and safety law and the HSE
    The Health and Safety at Work etc.
  6. Risk assessment
    Risk assessment is the legal and practical basis for controlling workplace hazards.
  7. Hazard analysis: FMEA and HAZOP
    Systematic hazard analysis finds problems before they cause harm.
  8. Human factors and safety culture
    Most accidents involve people as well as hardware.
  9. Sustainable development and engineering
    Sustainable development asks engineers to meet today's needs without harming the ability of future generations to meet theirs.
  10. Life-cycle assessment
    Life-cycle assessment measures the environmental impacts of a product or service from raw materials to disposal.
  11. Energy systems and net zero
    The UK has a legal target of net zero greenhouse gas emissions by 2050.
  12. The circular economy and materials efficiency
    A circular economy keeps products and materials in use for as long as possible instead of making, using and throwing away.
  13. Embodied carbon in infrastructure
    Building and maintaining infrastructure releases large amounts of carbon before any user benefits.
  14. Engineering for global development
    Billions of people still lack safe water, sanitation, energy and transport.
  15. Diversity and inclusion in engineering
    Engineering serves everyone, so it benefits from people with different backgrounds and from designs that work for all users.

Engineering in Society: questions students ask

Which UK Act protects whistleblowers?

The Public Interest Disclosure Act 1998.

What caused the de Havilland Comet breakups of 1954?

Metal fatigue cracks at stress concentrations in the pressurised cabin.

What does a prohibition notice do?

It stops an activity that risks serious personal injury.

What is the difference between a hazard and a risk?

A hazard can cause harm; risk is how likely harm is and how severe.
