Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What does the area under a velocity–time graph represent?

Displacement. From “Rectilinear motion” →

In plain English: Rectilinear motion is motion along a straight line. Key idea: Velocity and acceleration are derivatives of position; the four constant-acceleration equations apply only when acceleration is constant, otherwise integrate using the appropriate form.

At what launch angle is range greatest on level ground in a vacuum?

45°. From “Projectile motion” →

In plain English: A projectile moves under gravity alone after launch, giving constant downward acceleration and constant horizontal velocity. Key idea: Projectiles have constant horizontal velocity and constant vertical acceleration g; range is u2 sin 2θ/g, maximum at 45° in a vacuum, but air resistance shortens range and lowers the best angle.

What is the power of a torque T at angular velocity ω?

P = Tω, with ω in radians per second. From “Power and efficiency” →

In plain English: Power is the rate of doing work, and efficiency compares useful output with input. Key idea: Power is the rate of doing work (P = Fv or Tω); efficiency is useful output over input, always less than 1, and series efficiencies multiply.

What is the normal acceleration of a point at radius r on a body rotating at ω?

ω2r, towards the axis. From “Rotation about a fixed axis” →

In plain English: Many machine parts rotate about a fixed axis. Key idea: In fixed-axis rotation, ω and α relate to the linear velocity and acceleration of points by v = ωr, at = αr and an = ω2r; constant angular acceleration equations mirror the linear ones.

What is the kinetic energy of a rigid body in plane motion?

½mvG2 + ½IGω2. From “Energy methods for rigid bodies” →

In plain English: The work–energy principle extends to rigid bodies by including rotational kinetic energy. Key idea: Rigid-body kinetic energy is ½mvG2 + ½IGω2; rolling without slipping conserves mechanical energy, and bodies with smaller I/(mr2) roll down slopes faster.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Dynamics

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. A body starts from rest with a = 2 m/s2. What is its speed after 5 s? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    10 m/s. With constant acceleration, v = u + at = 0 + 2 × 5 = 10 m/s.

  2. A particle's velocity is 3i + 4j m/s. What is its speed? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    5 m/s. Speed is the magnitude of velocity: √(32 + 42) = 5 m/s.

  3. A particle moves at constant speed around a circle. Which acceleration does it have? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Normal acceleration only. Constant speed means at = 0, but direction changes, so an = v2/r.

  4. A train accelerates uniformly from 10 m/s to 30 m/s over 400 m. What is its acceleration? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    1.0 m/s2. v2 = u2 + 2as: 900 = 100 + 800a, so a = 1.0 m/s2.

  5. A particle has x = 4t and y = t3. What is its acceleration at t = 2 s? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    12j m/s2. ax = 0 (x is linear); ay = 6t = 12 at t = 2.

  6. A train travels at 30 m/s round a curve of radius 900 m. What is its normal acceleration? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    1.0 m/s2. an = v2/ρ = 900 ÷ 900 = 1.0 m/s2.

  7. A particle's acceleration is a = 6t m/s2, starting from rest at the origin. What is its displacement at t = 2 s? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    8 m. Integrate: v = 3t2, s = t3; at t = 2, s = 8 m. The constant-acceleration equations don't apply.

  8. A drone flies with v = 5i + (2t)j m/s. When is its velocity at 45° to the x-axis? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    t = 2.5 s. At 45°, vy = vx: 2t = 5, so t = 2.5 s.

  9. If a car doubles its speed on the same curve, what happens to the friction force needed to keep it on the curve? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    It quadruples, because normal acceleration depends on v2. an = v2/ρ, so doubling v quadruples an and the required force.

Key takeaways

  • Velocity and acceleration are derivatives of position; the four constant-acceleration equations apply only when acceleration is constant, otherwise integrate using the appropriate form.
  • In curvilinear motion, treat each rectangular component as rectilinear motion; velocity is tangent to the path, and speed is the magnitude of the velocity vector.
  • Tangential acceleration changes speed; normal acceleration v2/ρ changes direction and points to the centre of curvature; total acceleration combines the two.
  • Projectiles have constant horizontal velocity and constant vertical acceleration g; range is u2 sin 2θ/g, maximum at 45° in a vacuum, but air resistance shortens range and lowers the best angle.
  • Relative velocity is the vector difference of two velocities, vB/A = vB − vA; river and wind problems add velocity vectors to find ground velocity.
  • Newton's second law ΣF = ma is applied component by component using free-body and kinetic diagrams; in curved motion, the normal force sum provides mv2/ρ.

Common mistakes in Dynamics

  • Using the constant-acceleration equations when acceleration varies. Check that a is constant before using them; otherwise integrate.
  • Adding speeds instead of velocity components. Speed is √(vx2 + vy2), not vx + vy.
  • Thinking an object moving at constant speed on a curve has no acceleration. Its direction is changing, so it has normal acceleration v2/ρ.
  • Using the level-ground range formula when launch and landing heights differ. Solve the vertical equation for the actual landing height instead.
  • Subtracting speeds instead of velocity vectors. Relative velocity must be found by vector subtraction, accounting for direction.
  • Treating ma as a force on the free-body diagram. ma is the result of the forces, shown on the kinetic diagram; adding it as an extra force double-counts.

Lessons in this school

  1. Rectilinear motion
    Rectilinear motion is motion along a straight line.
  2. Curvilinear motion: rectangular components
    When a particle moves along a curved path, its position, velocity and acceleration are vectors.
  3. Normal and tangential components
    Normal and tangential components describe motion along a curve relative to the path itself.
  4. Projectile motion
    A projectile moves under gravity alone after launch, giving constant downward acceleration and constant horizontal velocity.
  5. Relative motion
    Motion depends on the frame of reference.
  6. Newton's second law for particles
    Newton's second law links force and acceleration and is the basis of kinetics.
  7. Work and energy
    The work–energy principle relates the work done by forces to the change in kinetic energy, avoiding the need to find accelerations.
  8. Power and efficiency
    Power is the rate of doing work, and efficiency compares useful output with input.
  9. Impulse and momentum
    Impulse and momentum methods relate forces acting over time to changes in velocity.
  10. Impacts
    An impact is a short, violent collision between two bodies.
  11. Rotation about a fixed axis
    Many machine parts rotate about a fixed axis.
  12. General plane motion and the instantaneous centre
    General plane motion combines translation and rotation, as in rolling wheels and connecting rods.
  13. Mass moment of inertia
    The mass moment of inertia measures a body's resistance to angular acceleration about an axis.
  14. Kinetics of rigid bodies in plane motion
    Rigid bodies in plane motion obey Newton's second law for translation and a moment equation for rotation.
  15. Energy methods for rigid bodies
    The work–energy principle extends to rigid bodies by including rotational kinetic energy.
  16. Free undamped vibration
    A mass on a spring vibrates at its natural frequency when disturbed.
  17. Damped vibration
    Real vibrations die away because of damping.
  18. Forced vibration and resonance
    When a periodic force drives a system, it vibrates at the driving frequency, and the response can be very large near the natural frequency.

Dynamics: questions students ask

How is velocity found from a position vector r(t)?

By differentiating each component with respect to time.

What is the normal acceleration of a particle moving at speed v on a curve of radius ρ?

v2/ρ, directed towards the centre of curvature.

What is the horizontal acceleration of a projectile in the vacuum model?

Zero: horizontal velocity is constant.

In a river crossing, which component of the boat's velocity sets the crossing time?

The component perpendicular to the river banks.
