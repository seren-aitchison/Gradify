Lessons in this school

  1. What paint is: pigment and binder
    Every paint is a pigment held in a binder, often thinned by a solvent or diluent.
  2. Egg tempera
    Egg tempera, pigment bound with egg yolk, was the main panel painting medium in Europe from the Middle Ages to the late 15th century.
  3. Fresco
    Fresco is painting into wet lime plaster so the colour becomes part of the wall.
  4. Oil paint
    Oil paint, pigment bound in drying oil, became the dominant European easel medium from the 15th century.
  5. Watercolour and gouache
    Watercolour is transparent paint bound with gum arabic, relying on the white of the paper for light; gouache is its opaque cousin.
  6. Acrylic paint
    Acrylic paint, pigment in a polymer emulsion, was developed in the mid-20th century and is now the most common studio and school paint.
  7. Supports and grounds
    A painting's support is what it is painted on; the ground is the prepared layer between support and paint.
  8. Brushes, knives and tools
    Brush shape, fibre and size determine the marks a painter can make.
  9. Underpainting and glazing
    Indirect painting builds a picture in stages: an underpainting establishes drawing and values, then transparent glazes and opaque layers add colour.
  10. Alla prima
    Alla prima (Italian, "at the first attempt") means painting wet-into-wet in one session, without waiting for layers to dry.
  11. Impasto and texture
    Impasto is paint applied thickly enough to stand up from the surface and keep the marks of brush or knife.
  12. Still life
    Still life depicts arranged inanimate objects such as fruit, flowers, vessels and books.
  13. Landscape painting
    Landscape painting depicts natural and built scenery.
  14. Portraiture
    Portraiture represents particular people, but it has always been about more than likeness: status, identity, memory and power.
  15. Abstract painting
    Abstract painting does not depict recognisable objects, or reduces them until they are no longer the subject.
  16. Health and safety in the studio
    Art materials and tools can be hazardous if misused.
  17. Conservation and the life of a painting
    Paintings change over time: varnish yellows, paint cracks, pigments fade and supports move.

Key terms

Binder
The substance that holds pigment particles together and fixes them to the surface
Egg tempera
Paint made of pigment bound with egg yolk and water
Buon fresco
Painting with water-ground pigment into wet lime plaster, which binds it as it sets
Glaze
A thin, transparent layer of oil paint over a dry layer, modifying its colour
Wet-in-wet
Applying paint to wet paper or wet paint so colours blend softly
Acrylic emulsion
A dispersion of acrylic polymer particles in water, which forms a film as the water evaporates
Support
The physical surface on which a painting is made, such as panel, canvas or paper
Filbert
A flat brush with a rounded, oval tip, giving soft-edged strokes
Grisaille
Painting in shades of grey, used as an underpainting or as a finished monochrome
Alla prima
Painting wet-into-wet to completion in one session, without waiting for layers to dry
Impasto
Paint applied thickly so that it stands up from the surface and retains marks
Still life
A depiction of inanimate objects arranged by the artist
Sublime
An aesthetic of awe or terror in the face of vast or overwhelming nature
Attribute
An object included in a portrait or image to identify a person's role, status or identity
