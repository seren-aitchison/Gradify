Lessons in this school

  1. What a print is: editions and proofs
    A fine art print is an original work made by transferring ink from a matrix (block, plate, stone or screen) to paper, usually in a numbered edition.
  2. Relief printing: woodcut and linocut
    In relief printing, the artist cuts away the areas that will print white and inks the raised surface.
  3. Intaglio: etching and engraving
    Intaglio prints are made from lines and tones cut or bitten into a metal plate, inked into the grooves and printed under heavy pressure.
  4. Lithography
    Lithography prints from a flat surface, using the fact that grease and water repel each other.
  5. Screen printing
    Screen printing pushes ink through a fine mesh where a stencil leaves it open.
  6. Monoprinting
    Monoprinting and monotype produce unique prints rather than editions.
  7. Carving
    Carving is a subtractive process: the sculptor removes material from a block of stone, wood or other substance to reveal a form.
  8. Modelling
    Modelling is an additive process in which a soft material such as clay or wax is built up and shaped.
  9. Casting
    Casting reproduces a form by pouring liquid material into a mould.
  10. Construction and assemblage
    Construction builds sculpture by joining parts, and assemblage combines found objects.
  11. Installation art
    Installation art creates an environment or arrangement that the viewer enters or moves around, often for a specific space and time.
  12. Site-specific and land art
    Site-specific art is made for and depends on a particular place; land art uses the landscape itself as material and setting.
  13. Ceramics
    Ceramics are objects made from clay and hardened by firing.
  14. Textiles and fibre art
    Textile art and design work with fibres, fabrics and threads through weaving, printing, dyeing, stitching and construction.
  15. Product and 3D design
    Product design creates objects that people use, balancing function, form, materials, manufacture and sustainability.
  16. Architecture as an art
    Architecture is the art and science of designing buildings and spaces.

Key terms

Matrix
The block, plate, stone or screen from which a print is made
Relief print
A print made from ink on the raised surface of a block, with cut-away areas printing white
Intaglio
A family of printmaking in which ink is held in lines or areas below the plate's surface
Lithography
A planographic printing method based on the repulsion of grease and water
Stencil
A material that blocks ink from passing through parts of the screen
Monotype
A unique print made by transferring an image painted or inked on a smooth, unworked surface
Subtractive process
Making a form by removing material, as in carving
Modelling
An additive process of building and shaping a form in a soft material such as clay or wax
Mould
A negative impression into which material is poured to make a cast
Construction
Making sculpture by joining separate parts and materials
Installation art
Art that transforms a space into an environment experienced by the viewer
Land art
Art made in and from the landscape, often large-scale or ephemeral, from the late 1960s
Bisque firing
The first firing of clay, making it hard but porous before glazing
Warp and weft
The lengthwise (warp) and crosswise (weft) threads in weaving
