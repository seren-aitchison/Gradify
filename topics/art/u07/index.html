Lessons in this school

  1. The anatomy of type
    Letters have named parts and measurements that designers use to choose and set type precisely.
  2. Type classification
    Typefaces are grouped into families by their history and visual features.
  3. Hierarchy and layout
    Good layout tells readers what to look at first, second and third.
  4. Grids
    Grids are invisible structures that help designers place elements consistently.
  5. Visual communication and semiotics
    Semiotics is the study of signs and how they create meaning.
  6. Logos and visual identity
    A logo is the most visible part of a wider visual identity that includes colour, type and imagery.
  7. Poster design
    Posters must grab attention and communicate quickly, often from a distance.
  8. Illustration
    Illustration uses images to explain, decorate or tell stories alongside text or for a client's purpose.
  9. Information design
    Information design makes complex information clear through diagrams, charts, maps and signs.
  10. User interface design
    User interface design shapes how people interact with apps, websites and devices.
  11. Packaging design
    Packaging protects products, informs customers and helps sell goods on the shelf.
  12. Advertising and persuasion
    Advertising uses images, words and psychology to persuade audiences.
  13. The design process: from brief to final
    Designers move from a client's brief to a final outcome through a structured but flexible process.
  14. Sustainable design
    Designers influence how much material, energy and waste a product or campaign creates.

Key terms

X-height
The height of lowercase letters such as x, excluding ascenders and descenders
Serif
A small finishing stroke at the end of a letter's main strokes
Visual hierarchy
The ordering of elements by importance so readers know what to see first
Gutter
The space between columns in a grid
Signifier
The form of a sign, such as a word, sound or image
Wordmark
A logo made from the organisation's name in distinctive lettering
Focal point
The element that first attracts the viewer's attention
Brief
A client's instructions setting out the purpose, audience and requirements of a job
Information design
Organising and presenting information so people can understand and use it
Wireframe
A simple layout drawing showing the structure of a screen without detailed styling
Net
A flat shape that folds to form a three-dimensional package
AIDA
A model of persuasion: attention, interest, desire, action
Design brief
A client's statement of the problem, audience, deliverables and constraints
Life cycle assessment
Analysing environmental impact at every stage of a product's life
