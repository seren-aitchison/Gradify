Key terms and formulas at a glance

Camera obscura
A darkened room or box in which light through a small opening projects an inverted image
Focal length
The distance in millimetres that sets a lens's angle of view and magnification
f-number
The ratio of focal length to aperture diameter; smaller numbers mean wider openings
Leading lines
Lines within an image that guide the viewer's eye towards a point of interest
Documentary photography
Photography that records real people, places and events, often with a social purpose
Pictorialism
A movement that made photographs look like paintings through soft focus and hand-worked prints
Latent image
The invisible image formed on exposed film before development
Histogram

Why is the image in a camera obscura upside down?

Light travels in straight lines, so rays from the top of the scene pass through the hole and land at the bottom. From “The camera obscura and the invention of photography” →

In plain English: Photography grew from the much older camera obscura and a series of nineteenth-century chemical experiments. Key idea: The camera obscura projected images for centuries, but photography began only when Niépce, Daguerre and Talbot found ways to fix them, with Talbot's negative and positive system making multiple prints possible.

Which aperture gives a shallower depth of field, f/2 or f/16?

f/2. From “Exposure: aperture, shutter speed and ISO” →

In plain English: Exposure is the total amount of light that reaches the film or sensor. Key idea: Aperture, shutter speed and ISO each change exposure in stops, and each also has a creative side effect: depth of field, motion and image noise.

What does Barthes mean by the punctum?

A detail in a photograph that personally pierces or moves the viewer. From “Photography as art” →

In plain English: For many years photography had to argue for its place as an art form. Key idea: Photography moved from imitating painting in Pictorialism to valuing its own qualities in straight photography, and contemporary artists now use staging, scale and concept to make it a central art form.

Who photographed a galloping horse in sequence in 1878?

Eadweard Muybridge. From “Moving image and video art” →

In plain English: Moving images began with experiments to capture motion and grew into cinema, television and video art. Key idea: Moving images are sequences of still frames, developed from Muybridge and the Lumières into cinema and then video art, and they are built from shot choices, camera movement, editing and sound.

What are the three parts of a polygon mesh?

Vertices, edges and faces. From “3D modelling and animation” →

In plain English: 3D software lets artists build objects and worlds in virtual space, then light, animate and render them. Key idea: 3D models are meshes of vertices, edges and faces that are textured, lit and rendered, and they are animated with keyframes, rigs and established principles such as squash and stretch.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Photography, Moving Image and Digital Art

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. What does the Latin phrase camera obscura mean? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Dark room. Camera means room and obscura means dark, describing the darkened space where the image forms.

  2. What is the job of the lens in a camera? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    To gather and focus light onto the film or sensor. The lens bends light so it forms a sharp image on the light-sensitive surface; the shutter controls duration.

  3. Which setting mainly controls whether a moving subject is frozen or blurred? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Shutter speed. The length of time the shutter is open decides how much movement is recorded during the exposure.

  4. Which early process produced a single unique image with no negative? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    The daguerreotype. A daguerreotype was formed directly on a silvered plate, so it could not be printed again from a negative.

  5. Which lens would best fit a whole cathedral interior into one frame from inside? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    A wide-angle lens such as 16 mm. A short focal length gives a wide angle of view, so more of a large space fits into the frame.

  6. A student photographs a flower and wants the background to melt into a soft blur. Which choice helps most? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    A wide aperture such as f/2. A wide aperture creates shallow depth of field, isolating the sharp flower from a blurred background.

  7. A student claims Vermeer certainly traced his paintings with a camera obscura. What is the most accurate response? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    It is a debated theory, supported by some visual clues but without direct proof. The camera obscura existed in Vermeer's time and some features suggest optical study, but no documents prove he used one, so the claim is contested.

  8. A photographer wants a distant row of houses to look tightly stacked against hills behind them. What is the best approach? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Stand far away and use a long telephoto lens. Shooting from far away with a long lens crops a narrow view in which distant layers appear compressed together.

  9. A photograph of a waterfall on a tripod at 1 second is badly overexposed even at f/22 and ISO 100. What is the best solution? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Fit a neutral density filter to cut the light. A neutral density filter reduces light reaching the sensor, so the long exposure can be kept for blurred water without overexposure.

Key takeaways

  • The camera obscura projected images for centuries, but photography began only when Niépce, Daguerre and Talbot found ways to fix them, with Talbot's negative and positive system making multiple prints possible.
  • A camera combines a lens, aperture and shutter to control light reaching film or a sensor, and focal length decides the angle of view and the apparent depth of a photograph.
  • Aperture, shutter speed and ISO each change exposure in stops, and each also has a creative side effect: depth of field, motion and image noise.
  • Photographic composition is built from choices of frame, viewpoint and timing, using devices such as leading lines, framing, negative space and the decisive moment.
  • Documentary photography records real life to inform or persuade, but selection, staging, captions and ethics mean it always interprets as well as records.
  • Photography moved from imitating painting in Pictorialism to valuing its own qualities in straight photography, and contemporary artists now use staging, scale and concept to make it a central art form.

Common mistakes in Photography, Moving Image and Digital Art

  • Thinking the daguerreotype could be copied like a modern print. Each daguerreotype was a unique plate with no negative; Talbot's calotype introduced the negative.
  • Believing that a telephoto lens itself changes perspective. Perspective depends on where the camera stands; a longer lens simply crops a narrower view from that position, which makes space look compressed.
  • Thinking a larger f-number means a larger opening. The f-number is a ratio, so f/16 is a much smaller opening than f/2.
  • Treating the rule of thirds as a rule that must always be followed. It is one useful guide; centred, symmetrical compositions can be just as powerful when they suit the idea.
  • Assuming a documentary photograph is a neutral, objective record. The photographer chooses framing, moment and context, and images can be staged or captioned to persuade.
  • Assuming art photography must be manipulated or painterly to count as art. Straight photographers such as Weston argued that sharp, unmanipulated images had their own artistic value.

Lessons in this school

  1. The camera obscura and the invention of photography
    Photography grew from the much older camera obscura and a series of nineteenth-century chemical experiments.
  2. The camera and the lens
    Every camera, from a pinhole box to a smartphone, controls light falling onto a light-sensitive surface.
  3. Exposure: aperture, shutter speed and ISO
    Exposure is the total amount of light that reaches the film or sensor.
  4. Composition in photography
    Photographers compose by choosing what to include, where to stand and when to press the shutter.
  5. Documentary photography
    Documentary photography records real people, places and events, often to inform or to argue for change.
  6. Photography as art
    For many years photography had to argue for its place as an art form.
  7. The darkroom and analogue processes
    Before digital cameras, photographs were developed and printed by hand in a darkroom, and many artists still use these methods.
  8. Editing and post-production
    Most photographs are adjusted after they are taken, from simple crops to complex composites.
  9. Moving image and video art
    Moving images began with experiments to capture motion and grew into cinema, television and video art.
  10. Raster and vector images
    Digital images are stored either as grids of pixels or as mathematical shapes.
  11. Digital painting and illustration
    Digital painting uses tablets and software to create images that can look like traditional media or something entirely new.
  12. 3D modelling and animation
    3D software lets artists build objects and worlds in virtual space, then light, animate and render them.
  13. Generative art and AI
    Artists have long made work by following rules or letting systems produce results, and computers and artificial intelligence have extended this.
  14. Digital colour and resolution
    Screens and printers make colour in different ways, and image size depends on pixels as well as inches.

Photography, Moving Image and Digital Art: questions students ask

What is a prime lens?

A lens with one fixed focal length.

What does a high ISO setting allow, and what is its cost?

Shooting in lower light, at the cost of more noise or grain.

What is negative space?

The empty or quiet area around and between subjects.

What did the Group f/64 promote?

Straight photography with sharp focus and full tonal range.
