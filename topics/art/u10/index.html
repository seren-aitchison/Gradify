Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What did Frank Stella mean by "What you see is what you see"?

That his paintings have no hidden meaning or illusion beyond their physical appearance. From “Minimalism” →

In plain English: Minimalism stripped art down to simple geometric forms, industrial materials and the viewer's experience of space. Key idea: Minimalism used simple geometric forms, repetition and industrial materials, rejecting expression and illusion; Judd, Andre, Flavin, Martin and LeWitt were key figures; critics such as Fried called it theatrical, and Andre's bricks caused a British scandal.

What happened in Abramović's Rhythm 0?

She let the audience use 72 objects on her for six hours, and their actions became increasingly aggressive. From “Performance art” →

In plain English: Performance art uses the artist's body and actions in time as the medium. Key idea: Performance art uses the body and time as the medium, developing from Dada, happenings and Fluxus; key works by Ono, Abramović and Gilbert & George explore vulnerability, audience and identity; its ephemerality raises questions about documentation and re-performance.

What was Freeze?

A 1988 student exhibition organised by Damien Hirst in London's Docklands that launched many YBAs. From “The Young British Artists” →

In plain English: In the 1990s, a group of young London artists became famous for shocking, entrepreneurial and media-savvy art. Key idea: The YBAs emerged from Goldsmiths and Freeze, promoted by Saatchi and Sensation; key works include Hirst's shark, Emin's bed and Whiteread's House; they revitalised British art but were criticised for relying on shock, media and the market.

Why was Lubaina Himid's 2017 Turner Prize significant?

She was the first Black woman to win the prize. From “Global contemporary art” →

In plain English: Contemporary art is now made, shown and sold across the world. Key idea: Biennials, art fairs and exhibitions such as Magiciens de la terre globalised contemporary art; artists such as Ai Weiwei, El Anatsui, Kusama and Himid have international significance; but debates about the dominance of Western centres and representation continue.

What is Baxandall's period eye?

The visual skills and habits of a culture, which shaped how its people looked at paintings. From “The social history of art” →

In plain English: The social history of art studies art in relation to the society that produced it. Key idea: The social history of art studies how patronage, economics, class and institutions shape art; Baxandall used contracts and the period eye, and Clark related modern painting to social change; it enriches understanding but risks reducing art to its context.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Contemporary Art and Ways of Seeing

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which material is typical of Minimalist art? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Industrial materials such as steel and brick. Minimalists used industrial materials and fabrication.

  2. Which work is an early readymade that influenced conceptual art? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Duchamp's Fountain. Fountain presented a manufactured urinal as art.

  3. Which artist invited audiences to cut her clothing in Cut Piece? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Yoko Ono. Ono performed Cut Piece in Kyoto and Tokyo in 1964, and later in New York and London.

  4. Why did Michael Fried call Minimalism "theatrical"? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    It depended on the viewer's presence and experience in space and time. Fried thought art should be complete in itself, not reliant on a viewer's encounter.

  5. What question does Kosuth's One and Three Chairs raise? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    What the relationship is between an object, its image and its definition. The work explores language and representation.

  6. What did Gilbert & George call themselves in The Singing Sculpture? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Living sculptures. They presented themselves as sculptures that moved and sang.

  7. What is the strongest defence of Equivalent VIII as art? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    It makes viewers reconsider what sculpture can be and how objects occupy space. Its value lies in the questions it raises about sculpture and space.

  8. Why did conceptual art challenge the art market? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Its works were often ideas, texts or documents that were hard to own and sell. Dematerialised works resisted being treated as commodities.

  9. Why is documentation a problem for performance art? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    The live, ephemeral event cannot be fully captured by photographs or video. The essence of performance may lie in its live presence.

Key takeaways

  • Minimalism used simple geometric forms, repetition and industrial materials, rejecting expression and illusion; Judd, Andre, Flavin, Martin and LeWitt were key figures; critics such as Fried called it theatrical, and Andre's bricks caused a British scandal.
  • Conceptual art places the idea above the object, developing from Duchamp's readymades into 1960s work by LeWitt, Kosuth, Art & Language and Craig-Martin; it challenged the market, skill and authorship, but critics question whether anything can be art.
  • Performance art uses the body and time as the medium, developing from Dada, happenings and Fluxus; key works by Ono, Abramović and Gilbert & George explore vulnerability, audience and identity; its ephemerality raises questions about documentation and re-performance.
  • Feminist art challenged women's exclusion and representation in art, using strategies such as celebrating women's history (Chicago), exposing constructed femininity (Sherman) and statistical protest (the Guerrilla Girls); it changed institutions, though inequality persists.
  • Postmodernism doubted grand narratives, progress and originality, using appropriation, pastiche, irony and mixing high and low culture, as in Levine and Koons; it widened art's voices but was criticised as superficial and commercial.
  • The YBAs emerged from Goldsmiths and Freeze, promoted by Saatchi and Sensation; key works include Hirst's shark, Emin's bed and Whiteread's House; they revitalised British art but were criticised for relying on shock, media and the market.

Common mistakes in Contemporary Art and Ways of Seeing

  • Assuming Minimalist works required no thought or skill. They involved careful decisions about proportion, material, placement and space.
  • Assuming conceptual art has no physical form. Many conceptual works include objects, texts or photographs, but these serve the idea.
  • Treating photographs of a performance as the same as the performance. Documentation records the work but cannot fully reproduce the live experience.
  • Assuming feminist art is a single style. It includes painting, photography, installation, craft, text and activism.
  • Treating postmodernism as just "anything goes". It was a critical position questioning originality, authority and representation.
  • Assuming all YBA works aimed only to shock. Works such as Whiteread's House explore memory, loss and domestic space with great subtlety.

Contemporary Art and Ways of Seeing: questions students ask

What did Sol LeWitt mean by "the idea becomes a machine that makes the art"?

That the idea or plan determines the artwork, so its execution is a secondary, almost mechanical process.

What was Fluxus?

An international network of artists from the early 1960s who staged simple events blurring art and everyday life.

What does The Dinner Party commemorate?

Women from history, with 39 place settings around a triangular table.

How did Lyotard define postmodernism?

As incredulity toward metanarratives, doubt about grand stories that claim to explain everything.
