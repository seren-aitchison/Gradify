What did Frank Stella mean by "What you see is what you see"?

That his paintings have no hidden meaning or illusion beyond their physical appearance. From “Minimalism” →

What happened in Abramović's Rhythm 0?

She let the audience use 72 objects on her for six hours, and their actions became increasingly aggressive. From “Performance art” →

What was Freeze?

A 1988 student exhibition organised by Damien Hirst in London's Docklands that launched many YBAs. From “The Young British Artists” →

Why was Lubaina Himid's 2017 Turner Prize significant?

She was the first Black woman to win the prize. From “Global contemporary art” →

What is Baxandall's period eye?

The visual skills and habits of a culture, which shaped how its people looked at paintings. From “The social history of art” →

Lessons in this school

  1. Minimalism
    Minimalism stripped art down to simple geometric forms, industrial materials and the viewer's experience of space.
  2. Conceptual art
    In conceptual art, the idea is more important than the finished object.
  3. Performance art
    Performance art uses the artist's body and actions in time as the medium.
  4. Feminist art
    From the late 1960s, feminist artists challenged how women were represented in art and excluded from the art world.
  5. Postmodernism
    Postmodernism rejected the modernist belief in progress, originality and pure forms.
  6. The Young British Artists
    In the 1990s, a group of young London artists became famous for shocking, entrepreneurial and media-savvy art.
  7. Street art
    Street art moved from illegal graffiti to galleries and auction houses.
  8. Global contemporary art
    Contemporary art is now made, shown and sold across the world.
  9. Formalism
    Formalism analyses art through its visual form rather than its subject or context.
  10. Iconography and iconology
    Iconography studies the subjects and symbols of art.
  11. The social history of art
    The social history of art studies art in relation to the society that produced it.
  12. Feminist and postcolonial art history
    From the 1970s, feminist and postcolonial art historians questioned whose art was studied and how others were represented.
  13. John Berger and ways of seeing
    John Berger's 1972 BBC series and book Ways of Seeing changed how many people think about images.

Key terms

Minimalism
A 1960s art movement using simple geometric forms and industrial materials
Readymade
An ordinary manufactured object presented as art
Performance art
Art in which the artist's actions in time are the medium
Male gaze
A way of depicting women as objects for a presumed male viewer
Postmodernism
A movement questioning modernist ideas of progress, originality and universal truth
Young British Artists
A group of artists who rose to fame in Britain in the late 1980s and 1990s
Graffiti
Writing or drawings made on public surfaces, often without permission
Biennial
A large international exhibition held every two years
Formalism
An approach to art that focuses on visual form rather than content or context
Iconography
The identification and study of subjects and symbols in art
Patronage
Support or commissioning of art by individuals or institutions
Orientalism
Western representations of the East that created stereotypes and supported colonial power
Aura
Benjamin's term for the unique presence of an original artwork
