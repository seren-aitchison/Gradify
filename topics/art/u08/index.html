Which French cave, discovered in 1940, contains paintings about 17,000 years old?

Lascaux. From “Prehistoric art” →

In plain English: The earliest surviving art includes cave paintings, carved figures and monuments made tens of thousands of years ago. Key idea: Prehistoric art includes cave paintings at sites such as Sulawesi, Chauvet, Lascaux and Altamira, carved figures like the Lion-man and Venus of Willendorf, and monuments such as Stonehenge, made with natural pigments and tools for purposes that remain debated.

What is verism?

A Roman portrait style showing age and imperfections realistically. From “Roman art” →

In plain English: Roman art borrowed heavily from Greece but developed its own realism, engineering and use of images for power. Key idea: Roman art combined Greek ideals with veristic portraiture, used sculpture and monuments as political propaganda, and pioneered concrete architecture such as the Pantheon, while Pompeian painting and mosaics decorated private spaces.

What is a minaret?

A tower from which the call to prayer is made. From “Islamic art” →

In plain English: Islamic art covers the art of lands where Islam was the main religion or culture, from Spain to India. Key idea: Islamic art includes mosques with mihrab, minbar and minaret, monuments such as the Dome of the Rock, Córdoba and the Alhambra, and an ornamental language of calligraphy, geometry and arabesque, with figurative images common in secular art.

Which emperor commissioned Hagia Sophia?

Justinian. From “Early Christian and Byzantine art” →

In plain English: Early Christian artists adapted Roman forms to new beliefs, and the Byzantine Empire developed a rich art of mosaics and icons. Key idea: Early Christian art adapted Roman images and basilicas, while Byzantine art produced Hagia Sophia, gold mosaics such as those at Ravenna and icons, surviving the iconoclasm of the eighth and ninth centuries.

Which pope commissioned the Sistine Chapel ceiling?

Pope Julius II. From “The High Renaissance” →

In plain English: Around 1490 to 1527, Leonardo, Michelangelo, Raphael and Venetian painters brought Renaissance ideals to their height. Key idea: The High Renaissance, from the 1490s to 1527, produced Leonardo's studies, Michelangelo's Sistine ceiling, Raphael's School of Athens and Venetian colour painting by Titian, debated through disegno and colorito and celebrated through the idea of genius.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Art History: The Ancient World to the Renaissance

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which pigment gave prehistoric painters their reds and yellows? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Ochre. Ochre is an iron-rich earth that was widely available and used from the earliest times.

  2. In Egyptian painting, how is the eye usually shown on a profile face? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    From the front. The composite view shows the eye frontally, its most recognisable form, even in a profile head.

  3. In red-figure vase painting, what colour are the figures? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    The red of the clay. Red-figure painters left the figures in the colour of the clay and painted the background black.

  4. Why do many researchers think deep cave paintings had a ritual purpose? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    They are in dark, hard-to-reach places rather than in living areas. Their location deep inside caves suggests special use rather than everyday decoration.

  5. Why are pharaohs often shown much larger than other figures? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Hierarchical scale showed their importance. Size indicated status, so the king or tomb owner dominates the composition.

  6. Which work is often cited as an early example of contrapposto, around 480 BCE? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    The Kritios Boy. The Kritios Boy rests its weight on one leg, breaking the rigid symmetry of the kouros type.

  7. A student writes that cave art was definitely made to bring luck in hunting. What is the best response? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    It is one theory among several, since no written evidence explains the images. Hunting magic is a possible explanation, but many images show no hunting, and other theories are equally plausible.

  8. What does the art of the Amarna period suggest about the link between art and religion in Egypt? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    A religious change under Akhenaten brought a sharp change of style, later reversed. The new Aten cult coincided with new forms, and restoring old gods restored old conventions.

  9. Why do some museums now show painted reconstructions of Greek statues? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Scientific study has revealed traces of original pigment. Ultraviolet and other analysis shows the works were coloured, so reconstructions test how they first looked.

Key takeaways

  • Prehistoric art includes cave paintings at sites such as Sulawesi, Chauvet, Lascaux and Altamira, carved figures like the Lion-man and Venus of Willendorf, and monuments such as Stonehenge, made with natural pigments and tools for purposes that remain debated.
  • Egyptian art served the afterlife, gods and kings using composite views, hierarchical scale and grid-based proportions that stayed stable for millennia, apart from the brief, more naturalistic Amarna period.
  • Greek sculpture moved from stiff Archaic kouroi to balanced Classical contrapposto and dramatic Hellenistic works, alongside black-figure and red-figure vases and Doric, Ionic and Corinthian temples that were originally brightly painted.
  • Roman art combined Greek ideals with veristic portraiture, used sculpture and monuments as political propaganda, and pioneered concrete architecture such as the Pantheon, while Pompeian painting and mosaics decorated private spaces.
  • Chinese art ranges from Shang ritual bronzes and the Terracotta Army to calligraphy, scroll painting guided by principles such as Xie He's, Song landscape painting and porcelain.
  • South Asian art includes Indus Valley seals, Ashokan pillars, Buddhist stupas and images, Hindu temples and Chola bronzes, and Mughal painting and architecture, with images central to religious practices such as darshan.

Common mistakes in Art History: The Ancient World to the Renaissance

  • Describing prehistoric artists as crude or primitive. Works such as Chauvet show skilled observation, confident line and use of the rock's form.
  • Thinking Egyptian artists could not draw realistically. The composite view was a deliberate convention showing each part clearly, and works such as the Nefertiti bust and detailed animals show great observational skill.
  • Assuming Greek statues were meant to be pure white. Traces of pigment show they were painted, and the white ideal came later.
  • Dismissing Roman art as merely copying the Greeks. Romans developed new forms, including veristic portraits, narrative columns and concrete architecture.
  • Judging Chinese landscape painting by the rules of Western perspective. It uses shifting viewpoints, mist and empty space deliberately to suggest scale and movement through a landscape.
  • Treating religious images as just decorative sculpture. Many were made to be worshipped and seen in ritual, so their gestures and attributes carry precise meanings.

Lessons in this school

  1. Prehistoric art
    The earliest surviving art includes cave paintings, carved figures and monuments made tens of thousands of years ago.
  2. Ancient Egyptian art
    Ancient Egyptian art served religion, kingship and the afterlife, and kept a remarkably stable style for about three thousand years.
  3. Ancient Greek art
    Greek artists developed a naturalistic, idealised image of the human body that shaped Western art for centuries.
  4. Roman art
    Roman art borrowed heavily from Greece but developed its own realism, engineering and use of images for power.
  5. Art of ancient China
    Chinese art has a continuous history of thousands of years, from ritual bronzes to ink landscape painting.
  6. Art of South Asia
    The art of South Asia spans the Indus Valley, Buddhist, Hindu, Jain and Mughal traditions.
  7. Islamic art
    Islamic art covers the art of lands where Islam was the main religion or culture, from Spain to India.
  8. Art of Africa
    Africa has one of the longest and most varied art histories in the world.
  9. Art of the ancient Americas
    Before European contact, civilisations across Mesoamerica and the Andes created monumental sculpture, cities, murals and textiles.
  10. Early Christian and Byzantine art
    Early Christian artists adapted Roman forms to new beliefs, and the Byzantine Empire developed a rich art of mosaics and icons.
  11. Romanesque and Gothic
    Medieval Europe produced two great architectural styles, Romanesque and Gothic, filled with sculpture, stained glass and manuscripts.
  12. The Early Renaissance in Florence
    In fifteenth-century Florence, artists revived classical ideas and developed new ways to represent space and the body.
  13. The High Renaissance
    Around 1490 to 1527, Leonardo, Michelangelo, Raphael and Venetian painters brought Renaissance ideals to their height.
  14. The Northern Renaissance
    In Flanders, Germany and the Netherlands, artists developed detailed oil painting, printmaking and new subjects.
  15. Mannerism
    After the High Renaissance, some artists deliberately broke its rules of balance and natural proportion.
