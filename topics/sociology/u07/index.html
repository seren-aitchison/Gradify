Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What does it mean to call race a social construct?

Racial categories are created by societies rather than given by biology. From “Race as a social construct” →

In plain English: Sociologists treat race as a social construct: a way of classifying people that has no firm biological basis but has powerful social consequences. Key idea: Race has no firm biological basis, but socially constructed racial categories shape treatment and life chances.

How does structural racism differ from institutional racism?

Structural racism concerns interacting institutions and history; institutional racism concerns one organisation. From “Structural racism” →

In plain English: Structural racism refers to the way racial inequalities are produced and reinforced across interconnected institutions over time. Key idea: Structural racism describes inequality produced across interconnected institutions; disadvantages accumulate, as in housing and the Windrush scandal, though explanations are debated.

What did Spivak ask?

Whether the most marginalised colonised people (the subaltern) could be heard. From “Postcolonial theory” →

In plain English: Postcolonial theory examines how colonialism shaped knowledge, identity and culture, and how its effects continue after formal independence. Key idea: Postcolonial theory examines how colonialism shapes knowledge and identity, through Orientalism, subalternity and hybridity, and challenges Eurocentric sociology.

How did Roy Jenkins describe integration in 1966?

Equal opportunity with cultural diversity in an atmosphere of mutual tolerance. From “Multiculturalism and migration” →

In plain English: Migration has made societies more diverse, and governments have responded with different policies. Key idea: Britain combined immigration control with multicultural and race relations policies; politicians proclaimed a "retreat", but evidence suggests policies changed less than rhetoric.

Why is disaggregated data important?

It reveals differences between groups hidden by broad categories. From “Race and inequality” →

In plain English: Ethnic inequalities appear across income, wealth, housing, education, work, health and justice. Key idea: Ethnic inequality in Britain varies greatly between groups; racism, class, migration history and geography combine to explain it, and interpretation needs care.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Race, Ethnicity and Migration

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Most human genetic variation is found… (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Within groups. Lewontin found about 85% of variation within groups.

  2. According to the Census 2021, what share of England and Wales identified as White? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    81.7%. The ONS reported that 81.7% of residents identified as White in the Census 2021.

  3. Who defined racialisation in his 1989 book Racism? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Robert Miles. Miles shifted focus to the process of racial meaning-making.

  4. A group seen as "non-white" in one century is later seen as "white". This supports the view that… (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Categories are constructed. Changing classification reflects social construction.

  5. A community emphasises its ethnic identity to win funding for a cultural centre. This fits which view? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Instrumental. Ethnicity is mobilised to pursue interests.

  6. A newspaper describes a religious group as having a fixed, incompatible culture. This is an example of… (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Cultural racism. Cultural incompatibility claims are cultural racism.

  7. Why do sociologists still study race if it is socially constructed? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Perceived race shapes treatment and inequality. Racism operates on perceived race, so studying race helps explain inequality.

  8. What does "symbolic ethnicity" describe? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Occasional, voluntary expression. Gans described ethnicity expressed through symbols and occasions.

  9. Why might a group be racialised at a particular time? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Economic competition, politics or media coverage. Racialisation responds to social and political conditions.

Key takeaways

  • Race has no firm biological basis, but socially constructed racial categories shape treatment and life chances.
  • Ethnicity is shared cultural identity, measured by self-identification; primordial, instrumental and constructionist views explain it differently.
  • Racialisation is the process of defining groups in racial terms; it can target groups by religion, culture or migration status, including through cultural racism.
  • Racism takes individual, institutional and structural forms; field experiments show persisting discrimination, and theories differ on its causes.
  • Institutional racism describes racism embedded in organisations' processes and cultures; the Macpherson Report defined it, and its extent in Britain remains debated.
  • Structural racism describes inequality produced across interconnected institutions; disadvantages accumulate, as in housing and the Windrush scandal, though explanations are debated.

Common mistakes in Race, Ethnicity and Migration

  • Thinking "socially constructed" means "imaginary" or unimportant. Constructed categories have real effects.
  • Using "ethnic" to mean "minority". Majority groups also have ethnicities.
  • Assuming racism only concerns skin colour. Religion, culture and nationality can be racialised too.
  • Thinking racism exists only where there is conscious hatred. Much racism is covert or built into systems.
  • Thinking institutional racism means every individual in an organisation is racist. It concerns collective processes and outcomes.
  • Assuming structural racism requires racist individuals. It can arise from interacting policies and histories.

Lessons in this school

  1. Race as a social construct
    Sociologists treat race as a social construct: a way of classifying people that has no firm biological basis but has powerful social consequences.
  2. Ethnicity
    Ethnicity refers to shared cultural identity based on ancestry, language, religion or traditions.
  3. Racialisation
    Racialisation is the process by which groups come to be defined and treated in racial terms.
  4. Racism
    Racism is prejudice, discrimination or structural disadvantage based on perceived race or ethnicity.
  5. Institutional racism
    Institutional racism is racism built into the practices and cultures of organisations.
  6. Structural racism
    Structural racism refers to the way racial inequalities are produced and reinforced across interconnected institutions over time.
  7. Prejudice and discrimination
    Prejudice is a negative attitude towards a group; discrimination is unfair treatment.
  8. Ethnic identity and belonging
    Ethnic identity shapes people's sense of belonging, and belonging can be affirmed or denied by others.
  9. Minority groups
    In sociology, a minority group is defined by disadvantage and subordination rather than simply by numbers.
  10. Colonialism
    Colonialism is the domination of one people or territory by another, involving political control, economic exploitation and cultural imposition.
  11. Postcolonial theory
    Postcolonial theory examines how colonialism shaped knowledge, identity and culture, and how its effects continue after formal independence.
  12. Decolonisation
    Decolonisation refers both to the political end of colonial rule and to efforts to challenge colonial legacies in knowledge, culture and institutions.
  13. International migration
    International migration is the movement of people across national borders to live.
  14. Forced migration
    Forced migration is movement caused by conflict, persecution, disaster or development projects.
  15. Refugees and asylum
    Refugees are protected under international law.
  16. Integration and assimilation
    Integration and assimilation describe how migrants and their descendants become part of a receiving society.
  17. Multiculturalism and migration
    Migration has made societies more diverse, and governments have responded with different policies.
  18. Diaspora
    A diaspora is a community dispersed from an original homeland that maintains a collective identity and links with it.
  19. Transnationalism
    Transnationalism describes migrants' ongoing connections across national borders.
  20. Borders
    Borders are the lines and practices that control movement between states.
  21. Citizenship
    Citizenship is legal membership of a state, with rights and duties, and a sense of belonging.
  22. Race and inequality
    Ethnic inequalities appear across income, wealth, housing, education, work, health and justice.
  23. Race and education
    Educational outcomes differ widely between ethnic groups.
  24. Race and employment
    Ethnic minorities in Britain face different employment rates, occupations and pay.
  25. Race and criminal justice
    Ethnic minorities, especially Black people, are over-represented at several stages of the criminal justice system in England and Wales.
  26. Race and health
    Health outcomes differ between ethnic groups in Britain.
  27. Debates about migration
    Migration is one of the most debated issues in British politics.

Race, Ethnicity and Migration: questions students ask

How is ethnicity measured in the Census?

People choose their own ethnic group from a list (self-identification).

What is racialisation?

The process of attributing racial meaning to features and defining groups as distinct "races".

What is the difference between individual and structural racism?

Individual racism is personal prejudice and acts; structural racism is inequality reinforced across institutions.

How did the Macpherson Report define institutional racism?

The collective failure of an organisation to provide an appropriate service because of colour, culture or ethnic origin.
