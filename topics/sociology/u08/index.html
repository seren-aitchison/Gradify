Lessons in this school

  1. What is a family?
    Defining the family is harder than it seems.
  2. Family structures
    Family structure refers to the composition of families: who lives together and how they are related.
  3. Nuclear and extended families
    The nuclear family is parents and their children; the extended family includes wider kin.
  4. Lone-parent families
    Lone-parent families are headed by one parent living with at least one child.
  5. Blended families
    Blended families, or step-families, form when partners bring children from previous relationships.
  6. Same-sex families
    Same-sex families are headed by same-sex couples, with or without children.
  7. Marriage
    Marriage is a legally and socially recognised union.
  8. Cohabitation
    Cohabitation is an unmarried couple living together.
  9. Divorce
    Divorce is the legal ending of a marriage.
  10. Fertility and family size
    Fertility refers to the number of children born.
  11. Parenting
    Parenting is shaped by class, culture, gender and policy.
  12. Childhood as a social construction
    Sociologists argue childhood is socially constructed: what it means to be a child varies across time and cultures.
  13. Domestic labour
    Domestic labour is the unpaid work of running a home and caring for its members.
  14. Power in the family
    Power in families concerns who makes decisions, controls resources and shapes others' lives.
  15. Domestic violence
    Domestic violence and abuse is harmful behaviour between people who are or have been intimate partners or family members.
  16. Family diversity
    Families in the UK are increasingly diverse.
  17. Intergenerational relationships
    Intergenerational relationships link grandparents, parents, children and grandchildren.
  18. Ageing and care
    An ageing population increases the need for care, much of it provided unpaid by families.
  19. Family policy
    Family policies are laws and measures that affect families, from benefits and parental leave to childcare and child protection.
  20. Global families
    Families around the world differ in structure, marriage practices and roles, and globalisation is changing family life.
  21. Family change
    Family life has changed greatly since the mid-20th century.

Key terms

Family
A couple with or without children, or a lone parent with a child, living together (ONS definition)
Family structure
The composition of a family and how members are related
Nuclear family
Two parents and their dependent children
Lone-parent family
One parent living with at least one child
Blended family
A family including a partner's children from a previous relationship
Civil partnership
A legal union recognising a couple's relationship, introduced in the UK in 2005
Remarriage
A marriage in which at least one partner has been married before
Cohabitation
An unmarried couple living together
No-fault divorce
Divorce without needing to blame a partner, introduced in 2022
Total fertility rate
The average number of children per woman over a lifetime at current rates
Intensive mothering
The expectation that mothers devote extensive time and resources to children
Social construction of childhood
The view that the meaning of childhood is created by society
Domestic labour
Unpaid work running a home and caring for its members
Allowance system
One partner gives the other a fixed amount for household spending
