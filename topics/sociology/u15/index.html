What is urbanisation?

Growth in the share of a population living in towns and cities. From “Urbanisation” →

What is the index of dissimilarity?

The share of a group that would need to move for it to be evenly spread across areas. From “Segregation” →

Name two causes of suburbanisation in Britain.

Railways, mass housebuilding, new towns, car ownership, rising incomes (any two). From “Suburbanisation” →

Why do death rates fall in stage 2?

Lessons in this school

  1. Urbanisation
    Urbanisation is the growth in the share of people living in towns and cities.
  2. Cities and modernity
    Classical sociologists saw the modern city as a new form of social life.
  3. Urban ecology and the Chicago School
    The Chicago School of sociology studied the city as an ecosystem, where groups compete for space.
  4. Urban inequality
    Cities concentrate both wealth and poverty.
  5. Segregation
    Residential segregation is the separation of groups into different neighbourhoods by income, ethnicity or religion.
  6. Gentrification
    Gentrification is the movement of wealthier residents and investment into working-class areas, changing their character.
  7. Housing
    Housing shapes health, wealth, family life and opportunity.
  8. Homelessness
    Homelessness ranges from sleeping rough to living in temporary accommodation or sofa-surfing.
  9. Suburbanisation
    Suburbanisation is the growth of residential areas on the edges of cities.
  10. Rural sociology
    Rural sociology studies life in the countryside, including agriculture, community and rural change.
  11. Community
    Community refers to people bound by shared place, identity or interest.
  12. Social networks in places
    Social networks link people to resources, information and support.
  13. Population change
    Population size changes through births, deaths and migration.
  14. The demographic transition
    The demographic transition model describes how birth and death rates change as societies develop.
  15. Fertility
    Fertility refers to the number of children people have.
  16. Mortality
    Mortality refers to deaths in a population.
  17. Population ageing
    Population ageing means older people make up a growing share of the population.
  18. Migration and population
    Migration changes the size, age structure and diversity of populations.
  19. Urban planning
    Urban planning shapes how cities are designed and developed.
  20. Sustainable cities
    Sustainable cities aim to reduce environmental harm while improving residents' quality of life.
  21. Global cities
    Global cities are command centres of the world economy.
  22. Urban futures
    Cities face challenges from climate change, technology, inequality and demographic change.

Key terms

Urbanisation
Growth in the share of people living in urban areas
Gemeinschaft
Close community based on kinship and tradition
Concentric zone model
Burgess's model of the city as rings around a central business district
Index of Multiple Deprivation
An official ranking of small areas by deprivation in England
Residential segregation
Separation of groups into different neighbourhoods
Gentrification
Wealthier residents and investment moving into working-class areas
Tenure
The legal basis on which a home is occupied, such as owning or renting
Hidden homelessness
Homelessness not counted in official figures, such as sofa-surfing
Suburbanisation
Growth of residential areas on the edges of cities
Rural–urban continuum
A spectrum of places rather than a sharp rural–urban divide
Community
People bound by shared place, interest or identity
Weak ties
Connections to acquaintances that link to new information
Natural change
Births minus deaths
Demographic transition
Change from high to low birth and death rates as societies develop
