Lessons in this school

  1. Functionalism
    Functionalism sees society as a system of interrelated parts, each contributing to the stability and survival of the whole.
  2. Parsons
    Talcott Parsons built the most ambitious functionalist theory of society, the social system.
  3. Merton: manifest and latent functions
    Robert Merton revised functionalism into a more flexible, testable approach.
  4. Conflict theory
    Conflict theory sees society as shaped by inequality and struggle between groups with different interests and power.
  5. Neo-Marxism
    Neo-Marxists revised Marx to explain why revolution didn't happen in advanced capitalist societies, giving more weight to culture, ideology and the state.
  6. Critical theory and the Frankfurt School
    Critical theory, developed by the Frankfurt School, combined Marx, Weber and Freud to criticise modern capitalist society, its culture and its forms of reason.
  7. Symbolic interactionism: Mead and Blumer
    Symbolic interactionism explains society as built from interaction, in which people create and act on shared meanings.
  8. Goffman and dramaturgy
    Erving Goffman studied the fine detail of everyday interaction, from self-presentation to stigma and total institutions.
  9. Ethnomethodology
    Ethnomethodology studies the methods people use to make sense of everyday life and produce social order moment by moment.
  10. Phenomenology
    Phenomenological sociology studies how people experience and understand the everyday world, using shared, taken-for-granted categories.
  11. Social constructionism
    Social constructionism argues that much of what we treat as natural or objective, from gender to mental illness, is created through social processes.
  12. Liberal feminism
    Liberal feminism seeks equal rights and opportunities for women and men through legal and policy reform, education and changing attitudes.
  13. Radical feminism
    Radical feminism argues that patriarchy, men's domination of women, is the most fundamental form of inequality, rooted in family, sexuality and violence as well as public life.
  14. Marxist feminism
    Marxist feminism links women's oppression to capitalism: women's unpaid domestic labour and position in the labour market serve capitalist interests.
  15. Black feminism
    Black feminism analyses how racism, sexism and class oppression combine in Black women's lives, and criticises feminisms that treat white middle-class women's experience as universal.
  16. Intersectionality
    Intersectionality analyses how categories such as race, gender, class, sexuality and disability combine to shape distinctive experiences of privilege and disadvantage.
  17. Postmodernism
    Postmodernism argues that contemporary societies have moved beyond modernity: grand theories have lost credibility, identities are fluid and media images blur with reality.
  18. Poststructuralism
    Poststructuralism holds that meaning is unstable and produced through language and discourse, and that subjects are shaped by the discourses that describe them.
  19. Foucault
    Michel Foucault analysed how power operates through knowledge, institutions and everyday practices, shaping how people are seen and how they see themselves.
  20. Bourdieu: habitus, capital and field
    Pierre Bourdieu explained how social inequality is reproduced through taste, education and everyday dispositions, using the concepts of habitus, capital and field.
  21. Giddens and structuration theory
    Anthony Giddens' structuration theory argues that structure and agency are not opposites but two sides of the same process.
  22. Bauman
    Zygmunt Bauman analysed how modern society has become "liquid": relationships, identities and institutions are fluid, temporary and uncertain.
  23. Beck
    Ulrich Beck argued that late modern societies are "risk societies", organised around manufactured risks produced by modernisation itself.
  24. Theoretical synthesis
    Contemporary sociologists often try to combine insights from different theories rather than choose one.

Key terms

Functionalism
A perspective that explains social institutions by the functions they serve for society as a whole
AGIL
Parsons' four functional prerequisites: adaptation, goal attainment, integration and latency
Manifest function
An intended and recognised consequence of a social practice
Conflict theory
A perspective explaining society through inequality, power and struggle between groups
Hegemony
Gramsci's term for domination achieved through consent, by shaping culture and common sense
Critical theory
Social theory aimed at understanding society in order to free people from domination
Generalised other
Mead's term for the internalised attitudes of the community as a whole
Total institution
A place where all aspects of life are organised under one authority, cut off from wider society
Ethnomethodology
The study of the methods people use to make sense of and produce everyday social order
Typification
A shared category used to understand situations and people quickly
Social construction
The process by which categories and realities are created and sustained through social action
Liberal feminism
Feminism seeking equal rights and opportunities through legal and cultural reform
Patriarchy
A social system in which men dominate women
Reproduction of labour power
Domestic work that sustains and replaces the workforce
