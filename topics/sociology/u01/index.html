Lessons in this school

  1. What is sociology?
    Sociology is the systematic study of social life: how societies are organised, how they change and how they shape, and are shaped by, the people in them.
  2. Sociology as a discipline
    Sociology is an academic discipline with its own concepts, theories, methods and professional bodies.
  3. The sociological imagination
    C. Wright Mills called the ability to connect personal experience to wider social forces "the sociological imagination".
  4. Micro and macro sociology
    Sociologists study social life at different scales: micro sociology studies face-to-face interaction; macro sociology studies large-scale structures and whole societies.
  5. The individual and society
    Is society just a collection of individuals, or something more?
  6. Structure and agency
    How far are our lives shaped by social structures, and how far by our own choices?
  7. Social institutions
    Institutions are the established patterns of social life that organise how societies meet their needs: the family, education, religion, the economy, politics and law, health and the media.
  8. Social relationships
    Social life is made of relationships: between friends, family members, colleagues, strangers and citizens.
  9. Norms and values
    Values are shared ideas about what is good and desirable; norms are the expected behaviours that follow from them.
  10. Culture
    Culture is the shared way of life of a group: its values, norms, beliefs, language, knowledge and material objects.
  11. Primary socialisation
    Primary socialisation is the first and most intensive stage of learning the culture of a society, usually within the family.
  12. Secondary socialisation
    Secondary socialisation continues throughout life as people learn the norms of wider society through school, peer groups, the media, religion and work.
  13. Roles and status
    Status is a social position; a role is the behaviour expected of someone in that position.
  14. Identity
    Identity is our sense of who we are and how others see us.
  15. Groups and communities
    Much of social life happens in groups: families, friendship circles, teams, organisations and communities.
  16. Social interaction
    Social interaction is how people act towards and respond to one another.
  17. Sociological research: an introduction
    Sociologists study society using a range of methods, from large surveys to long-term participant observation.
  18. Contemporary sociology
    Contemporary sociology studies a world reshaped by globalisation, digital technology, climate change, migration and changing inequalities.
  19. Applying sociology
    Sociology is used in policy, public services, business, campaigning and everyday citizenship.

Key terms

Sociology
The systematic study of social life, social groups, institutions and societies
Discipline
A field of study with its own concepts, theories, methods and institutions
Sociological imagination
The ability to see the connection between individual lives and wider social forces and history (Mills)
Micro sociology
The study of small-scale, face-to-face social interaction and meaning
Social fact
A way of acting, thinking or feeling that exists outside individuals and constrains them (Durkheim)
Social structure
Relatively stable patterns of relationships and institutions that shape opportunities and constraints
Social institution
An enduring set of norms, roles and practices organised around a central social activity
Primary relationship
A personal, emotionally close and lasting relationship, such as with family or close friends
Values
General beliefs about what is good, right or important
Culture
The learned, shared way of life of a group, including values, norms, language and material objects
Socialisation
The lifelong process of learning the culture of a society
Secondary socialisation
Socialisation that continues beyond the family, through school, peers, media, religion and work
Status
A social position within a group or society
Identity
A person's sense of who they are and how they are seen by others
