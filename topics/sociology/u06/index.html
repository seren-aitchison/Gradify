Lessons in this school

  1. Sex and gender
    Sociologists distinguish sex, usually referring to biological characteristics, from gender, the social meanings attached to being a woman or a man.
  2. The social construction of gender
    Social constructionists argue that gender is produced through social processes rather than simply expressing biology.
  3. Gender socialisation
    Gender socialisation is the process through which people learn gender norms and roles.
  4. Gender roles
    Gender roles are the behaviours and responsibilities expected of women and men.
  5. Gender stereotypes
    Gender stereotypes are generalised beliefs about the characteristics of women and men.
  6. Gender identity in society
    Gender identity is a person's internal sense of their own gender.
  7. Gender performativity: Butler
    Judith Butler argued that gender is performative: it is produced through repeated acts rather than expressing an inner essence.
  8. Masculinities
    Sociologists study masculinities: the different ways of being a man.
  9. Hegemonic masculinity
    Connell's concept of hegemonic masculinity explains how one form of masculinity becomes dominant and legitimates men's power.
  10. Feminist theory
    Feminist theory explains gender inequality and how to change it.
  11. Patriarchy
    Patriarchy is a system of social structures and practices in which men dominate women.
  12. Gender inequality
    Gender inequality is unequal access to resources, power and opportunities based on gender.
  13. Gender and work
    Work is a central site of gender inequality, from pay and segregation to harassment and care.
  14. Gender and education
    Gender shapes educational achievement, subject choices and experiences.
  15. Gender and the family
    Families are central sites where gender roles, power and care are organised.
  16. Gender and politics
    Gender shapes who holds political power, how political issues are defined and how people participate.
  17. Gender and the media
    Media representations shape and reflect ideas about gender.
  18. Sexuality as a social phenomenon
    Sociologists study sexuality not only as biology but as something shaped by society, culture, law and history.
  19. Sexual identities
    Sexual identity is how people understand and describe their own sexuality.
  20. Heteronormativity
    Heteronormativity is the assumption that heterosexuality is normal and natural, and the social arrangements built on it.
  21. LGBTQ+ sociology
    LGBTQ+ sociology studies the lives, communities, movements and inequalities of lesbian, gay, bisexual, trans and queer people.
  22. Gender and intersectionality
    Intersectionality examines how gender combines with race, class, sexuality, disability and other social divisions.
  23. Gender-based violence
    Gender-based violence is violence directed at people because of their gender, or that affects one gender disproportionately.
  24. Current debates in gender and sexuality
    Gender and sexuality remain areas of active social and political debate.

Key terms

Sex
Biological characteristics such as chromosomes, hormones and anatomy
Doing gender
Producing gender through everyday behaviour and interaction
Gender socialisation
Learning gender norms and roles
Instrumental role
Parsons's term for the breadwinner role
Gender stereotype
A generalised belief about women's or men's characteristics
Cisgender
Having a gender identity that matches one's sex registered at birth
Performativity
The production of gender through repeated acts
Masculinities
The different ways of being a man
Hegemonic masculinity
The culturally dominant form of masculinity legitimating men's power
Liberal feminism
Feminism focusing on legal reform and equal opportunities
Patriarchy
A system in which men as a group dominate women
Motherhood penalty
The fall in mothers' earnings after having children
Glass ceiling
An invisible barrier preventing women reaching senior positions
Laddish culture
Peer culture in which appearing to work hard is seen as uncool or feminine
