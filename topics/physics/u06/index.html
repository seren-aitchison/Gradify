Lessons in this unit

  1. Periodic motion
    Periodic motion repeats itself after a fixed time, the period.
  2. Simple harmonic motion
    Simple harmonic motion (SHM) is oscillation in which the acceleration is proportional to the displacement and directed towards equilibrium.
  3. Amplitude, frequency, period and phase
    An oscillation x = A cos(ωt + φ) is fully specified by its amplitude A, angular frequency ω and phase constant φ.
  4. Mass-spring systems
    A mass on a spring is the model simple harmonic oscillator.
  5. Simple and physical pendulums
    A simple pendulum is a point mass on a light string; a physical (compound) pendulum is any rigid body swinging about a pivot.
  6. Energy in oscillatory systems
    An undamped oscillator continually exchanges kinetic and potential energy while the total stays constant.
  7. Damped oscillations
    Real oscillators lose energy to friction, air resistance or electrical resistance, so their amplitude decays.
  8. Underdamping, critical damping and overdamping
    How strongly an oscillator is damped decides whether it oscillates as it settles.
  9. Driven oscillations
    A periodic driving force makes an oscillator vibrate at the driving frequency, not its natural one.
  10. Resonance
    Resonance is the large response of a system driven at (or near) its natural frequency.
  11. Quality factor
    The quality factor Q measures how lightly damped an oscillator is: how many cycles it rings for, and how sharp its resonance peak is.
  12. Coupled oscillators
    When oscillators are linked, energy flows between them.
  13. Normal modes
    A normal mode is a pattern of motion in which every part of a system oscillates at the same frequency with fixed relative amplitudes.
  14. Beat frequencies
    When two oscillations of slightly different frequency add, the result swells and fades at the difference frequency: beats.
  15. Anharmonic oscillations
    An anharmonic oscillator has a restoring force that isn't exactly proportional to displacement.
  16. Nonlinear oscillators
    Nonlinear oscillators obey equations where superposition fails.
  17. Parametric resonance
    Parametric resonance grows oscillations by periodically varying a parameter of the system (length, stiffness) rather than pushing it.
  18. Mechanical vibration analysis
    Vibration analysis measures and models how machines and structures vibrate, to prevent fatigue, noise and failure and to diagnose faults early.
  19. Frequency response
    A system's frequency response describes how it amplifies (or attenuates) and phase-shifts sinusoidal inputs at each frequency.
  20. Fourier analysis of oscillations
    Fourier analysis breaks any oscillation into a sum of pure sinusoids.
