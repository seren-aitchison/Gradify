What two ingredients does an oscillator need?

A restoring force and inertia. From “Periodic motion” →

In plain English: Periodic motion repeats itself after a fixed time, the period.

What is the period of a physical pendulum?

T = 2π√(I/(Mgd)). From “Simple and physical pendulums” →

In plain English: A simple pendulum is a point mass on a light string; a physical (compound) pendulum is any rigid body swinging about a pivot.

At what frequency does a driven oscillator vibrate in steady state?

The driving frequency. From “Driven oscillations” →

In plain English: A periodic driving force makes an oscillator vibrate at the driving frequency, not its natural one.

What is a normal mode?

A motion in which all parts oscillate at one frequency with fixed relative amplitude and phase. From “Normal modes” →

In plain English: A normal mode is a pattern of motion in which every part of a system oscillates at the same frequency with fixed relative amplitudes.

At what frequency is parametric resonance strongest?

At twice the natural frequency. From “Parametric resonance” →

In plain English: Parametric resonance grows oscillations by periodically varying a parameter of the system (length, stiffness) rather than pushing it.

  1. A pendulum makes 20 swings in 30 s. Its period is: (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    1.5 s. T = 30/20.

  2. In SHM, acceleration is greatest: (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    At maximum displacement. |a| = ω2|x|.

  3. x = 0.2 cos(5t + π/3). The amplitude and ω are: (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    0.2 m, 5 rad s-1. Read from the standard form.

  4. ω = 50 rad s-1 corresponds to f = ? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    8.0 Hz. f = ω/2π.

  5. An oscillator has A = 4 cm, ω = 10 rad s-1. Its speed at x = 2 cm is: (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    0.35 m s-1. ω√(A2 − x2) = 10√(0.0016 − 0.0004).

  6. What defines simple harmonic motion? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Acceleration proportional to displacement and directed towards equilibrium. In SHM, a = −ω²x: the restoring acceleration is proportional to displacement.

Common mistakes in Oscillations and Vibrations

  • Frequency f and angular frequency ω differ by 2π; mixing them is one of the commonest errors.
  • SHM requires a ∝ −x exactly. A bouncing ball has periodic motion but isn't SHM (its acceleration is constant while airborne).
  • Phase is often quoted in degrees or fractions of a cycle; check which, and remember 2π rad = 360° = 1 cycle.
  • Cutting a spring in half doubles its spring constant; each half stretches half as much under the same force.
  • The small-angle formula underestimates the period at large amplitude: 0.5% too short at 16°, 1.7% at 30°.
  • Doubling the amplitude quadruples the energy, not doubles it.

Lessons in this unit

  1. Periodic motion
    Periodic motion repeats itself after a fixed time, the period.
  2. Simple harmonic motion
    Simple harmonic motion (SHM) is oscillation in which the acceleration is proportional to the displacement and directed towards equilibrium.
  3. Amplitude, frequency, period and phase
    An oscillation x = A cos(ωt + φ) is fully specified by its amplitude A, angular frequency ω and phase constant φ.
  4. Mass-spring systems
    A mass on a spring is the model simple harmonic oscillator.
  5. Simple and physical pendulums
    A simple pendulum is a point mass on a light string; a physical (compound) pendulum is any rigid body swinging about a pivot.
  6. Energy in oscillatory systems
    An undamped oscillator continually exchanges kinetic and potential energy while the total stays constant.
  7. Damped oscillations
    Real oscillators lose energy to friction, air resistance or electrical resistance, so their amplitude decays.
  8. Underdamping, critical damping and overdamping
    How strongly an oscillator is damped decides whether it oscillates as it settles.
  9. Driven oscillations
    A periodic driving force makes an oscillator vibrate at the driving frequency, not its natural one.
  10. Resonance
    Resonance is the large response of a system driven at (or near) its natural frequency.
  11. Quality factor
    The quality factor Q measures how lightly damped an oscillator is: how many cycles it rings for, and how sharp its resonance peak is.
  12. Coupled oscillators
    When oscillators are linked, energy flows between them.
  13. Normal modes
    A normal mode is a pattern of motion in which every part of a system oscillates at the same frequency with fixed relative amplitudes.
  14. Beat frequencies
    When two oscillations of slightly different frequency add, the result swells and fades at the difference frequency: beats.
  15. Anharmonic oscillations
    An anharmonic oscillator has a restoring force that isn't exactly proportional to displacement.
  16. Nonlinear oscillators
    Nonlinear oscillators obey equations where superposition fails.
  17. Parametric resonance
    Parametric resonance grows oscillations by periodically varying a parameter of the system (length, stiffness) rather than pushing it.
  18. Mechanical vibration analysis
    Vibration analysis measures and models how machines and structures vibrate, to prevent fatigue, noise and failure and to diagnose faults early.
  19. Frequency response
    A system's frequency response describes how it amplifies (or attenuates) and phase-shifts sinusoidal inputs at each frequency.
  20. Fourier analysis of oscillations
    Fourier analysis breaks any oscillation into a sum of pure sinusoids.

Oscillations and Vibrations: questions students ask

Where is the speed greatest in SHM?

At the equilibrium position.

What determines the amplitude and phase of an oscillator?

Its initial conditions (initial displacement and velocity).

Does gravity change the period of a vertical spring oscillator?

No, it only shifts the equilibrium position.

What is the centre of percussion?

The point where an impact produces no reaction at the pivot (the sweet spot).
