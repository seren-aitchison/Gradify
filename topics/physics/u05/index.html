Five sample flashcards

What are the rotational equivalents of v = u + at and s = ut + ½at2?

ω = ω0 + αt and θ = ω0t + ½αt2. From “Angular displacement, velocity and acceleration” →

In plain English: Rotation is described by the angle turned (angular displacement θ), its rate (angular velocity ω) and the rate of change of that (angular acceleration α).

What is the power delivered by a torque?

P = τω. From “Rotational kinetic energy” →

In plain English: A spinning body stores kinetic energy because each part of it moves.

Which reaches the bottom of a slope first: a solid sphere or a hoop?

The solid sphere (smaller I/MR2). From “Rolling without slipping” →

In plain English: When a wheel rolls without slipping, its contact point is momentarily at rest, linking translation and rotation by v = ωR.

Why does a torque on a spinning gyroscope cause precession rather than toppling?

The torque changes L in its own direction, which is perpendicular to L, so L rotates sideways. From “Gyroscopic motion” →

In plain English: A spinning gyroscope resists changes in the direction of its axis.

Which fictitious forces appear in a uniformly rotating frame?

Centrifugal and Coriolis. From “Rotating reference frames” →

In plain English: In a frame rotating at angular velocity ω, Newton's laws need fictitious forces: centrifugal, Coriolis and (if ω changes) Euler forces.

  1. 300 rpm in rad s-1 is about: (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    31.4. 300 × 2π/60.

  2. A disc spins at 10 rad s-1. A point 0.3 m from the axis moves at: (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    3 m s-1. v = ωr.

  3. A 20 N force acts 0.5 m from a pivot along a line through the pivot. Torque? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    0. The moment arm is zero.

  4. A wheel decelerates uniformly from 20 rad s-1 to rest in 10 revolutions. α = ? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    −3.2 rad s-2. ω2 = ω02 + 2αθ with θ = 20π: α = −400/(40π).

  5. A 10-tooth gear drives a 40-tooth gear at 400 rpm. The large gear turns at: (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    100 rpm. Speed ratio is the inverse of the teeth ratio.

  6. Why is a door handle placed far from the hinges? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    A larger moment arm gives more torque for the same force. τ = Fd⊥.

Common mistakes in Rotational Mechanics

  • Rotational formulas need radians; using degrees in v = ωr or s = rθ gives wrong answers.
  • For a wheel rolling along the ground, the contact point is momentarily at rest; v = ωr gives the speed of the centre, not of every point.
  • Torque depends on where the axis is; always say about which point you take moments.
  • I depends on the axis chosen. The same rod has I = ML2/12 about its centre and ML2/3 about an end.
  • For rolling objects, don't forget the rotational term; ignoring it makes them seem faster than they are.
  • L = Iω is only parallel to ω for rotation about a principal axis; for a lopsided body, L and ω point in different directions (see rigid-body dynamics).

Lessons in this unit

  1. Angular displacement, velocity and acceleration
    Rotation is described by the angle turned (angular displacement θ), its rate (angular velocity ω) and the rate of change of that (angular acceleration α).
  2. Angular and linear motion relationships
    Each point on a rotating rigid body moves in a circle, with linear quantities equal to the angular ones times the radius.
  3. Torque and moment of force
    Torque is the turning effect of a force: force times its perpendicular distance from the axis.
  4. Moment of inertia
    Moment of inertia measures how hard it is to change an object's rotation.
  5. Rotational kinetic energy
    A spinning body stores kinetic energy because each part of it moves.
  6. Angular momentum
    Angular momentum is the rotational counterpart of linear momentum.
  7. Conservation of angular momentum
    With no net external torque, a system's total angular momentum stays constant.
  8. Rotational dynamics
    Rotational dynamics applies τ = Iα (for a fixed axis) to find how torques change rotation.
  9. Rolling without slipping
    When a wheel rolls without slipping, its contact point is momentarily at rest, linking translation and rotation by v = ωR.
  10. Rotational equilibrium
    A body is in rotational equilibrium when the net torque on it is zero about every point.
  11. Parallel-axis theorem
    The parallel-axis theorem gives the moment of inertia about any axis from the moment about a parallel axis through the centre of mass.
  12. Perpendicular-axis theorem
    For a flat (planar) object, the moment of inertia about an axis perpendicular to its plane equals the sum of the moments about two perpendicular axes lying in the plane and crossing at the same point.
  13. Gyroscopic motion
    A spinning gyroscope resists changes in the direction of its axis.
  14. Precession and nutation
    Precession is the slow circling of a spinning body's axis under a torque; nutation is a nodding wobble superimposed on it.
  15. Rigid-body dynamics
    A rigid body's motion is the translation of its centre of mass plus rotation about it.
  16. Euler's equations of rotation
    Euler's equations give the rotational dynamics of a rigid body in its own rotating principal-axis frame.
  17. Rotating reference frames
    In a frame rotating at angular velocity ω, Newton's laws need fictitious forces: centrifugal, Coriolis and (if ω changes) Euler forces.
  18. Centrifugal and Coriolis forces
    Centrifugal and Coriolis forces are fictitious forces seen in rotating frames.
  19. Top motion and stability
    A spinning top on a table combines spin, precession and nutation.
  20. Coupled translational and rotational motion
    Many objects translate and rotate at once, with friction or constraints coupling the two.

Rotational Mechanics: questions students ask

How do the linear speeds of two meshing gears compare?

They're equal at the point of contact.

What is the moment arm?

The perpendicular distance from the axis to the line of action of the force.

Why does a hoop have a larger I than a disc of the same mass and radius?

All its mass is at the maximum radius.

What limits the energy a flywheel can store?

The material's strength, since rim stress is proportional to ρv2.
