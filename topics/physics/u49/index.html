Lessons in this unit

  1. Ancient Greek natural philosophy
    Ancient Greek thinkers sought natural, rational explanations for the world instead of myths.
  2. Early astronomy and mechanics
    Early astronomers across Babylon, Greece, India, China and the Islamic world tracked the heavens with growing precision.
  3. Medieval scientific traditions
    Between antiquity and the Scientific Revolution, scholars in the Islamic world, Byzantium, India, China and medieval Europe preserved, translated and extended ancient knowledge.
  4. Scientific Revolution
    The Scientific Revolution (roughly 1543–1687) transformed how people understood nature, replacing Aristotelian ideas with a Sun-centred cosmos, mathematical laws and experiment.
  5. Galileo and experimental mechanics
    Galileo Galilei combined careful experiments, mathematics and telescope observations to overturn Aristotelian physics.
  6. Newtonian mechanics
    Isaac Newton's Principia (1687) presented three laws of motion and universal gravitation, explaining falling apples, tides, planetary orbits and comets with one framework.
  7. Development of thermodynamics
    Thermodynamics grew from efforts to improve steam engines into a universal science of energy, heat and entropy.
  8. Electromagnetism and Maxwell
    In the 19th century electricity and magnetism were unified into one theory, culminating in Maxwell's equations, which predicted electromagnetic waves travelling at the speed of light.
  9. Discovery of radioactivity
    In just a few years around 1900, the discovery of X-rays and radioactivity revealed that atoms have internal structure and can transform.
  10. Origins of quantum theory
    Quantum theory began with puzzles classical physics couldn't solve: blackbody radiation, the photoelectric effect and atomic spectra.
  11. Development of special and general relativity
    Einstein's relativity overturned absolute space and time.
  12. Nuclear physics and the atomic age
    Nuclear physics advanced rapidly from the neutron's discovery in 1932 to fission in 1938 and the first reactor and atomic bombs in the 1940s.
  13. Development of quantum mechanics
    In the mid-1920s, a new quantum mechanics replaced the old quantum theory.
  14. Particle physics and the Standard Model
    Particle physics grew from cosmic-ray discoveries in the 1930s to the Standard Model of the 1970s and the Higgs boson in 2012.
  15. Space exploration and modern astrophysics
    The 20th century transformed astronomy: galaxies, the expanding universe, the cosmic microwave background, pulsars, black holes and exoplanets were discovered, while spacecraft visited every planet.
  16. History of computing in physics
    Physics and computing grew together: physicists built early computers, invented simulation methods and created the World Wide Web.
  17. Women and underrepresented groups in physics
    People of many backgrounds have shaped physics, though women and other underrepresented groups often faced exclusion and saw their work overlooked.
  18. Scientific institutions and funding
    Physics depends on institutions, from universities and national laboratories to international collaborations and funding agencies.
  19. Physics, technology and society
    Physics drives technology, and technology changes society: steam engines, electricity, radio, nuclear power, semiconductors, lasers, GPS and the internet.
  20. Philosophy and history of scientific revolutions
    Philosophers and historians of science ask how scientific knowledge grows and changes.
