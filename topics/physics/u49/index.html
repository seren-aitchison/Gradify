What did Archimedes discover about floating bodies?

The buoyant force equals the weight of fluid displaced. From “Ancient Greek natural philosophy” →

In plain English: Ancient Greek thinkers sought natural, rational explanations for the world instead of myths. Key idea: Greek philosophers sought rational natural explanations; Aristotle's physics dominated for centuries, while Archimedes pioneered mathematical physics.

What did Galileo's inclined-plane experiments show?

Distance travelled under uniform acceleration is proportional to time squared. From “Galileo and experimental mechanics” →

In plain English: Galileo Galilei combined careful experiments, mathematics and telescope observations to overturn Aristotelian physics. Key idea: Galileo quantified motion through experiment and mathematics, and telescope evidence undermined the geocentric model.

What did Becquerel discover in 1896?

Uranium emits radiation spontaneously, fogging photographic plates. From “Discovery of radioactivity” →

In plain English: In just a few years around 1900, the discovery of X-rays and radioactivity revealed that atoms have internal structure and can transform. Key idea: X-rays and radioactivity revealed atomic structure and transmutation, launching nuclear physics within about 15 years.

What did Born propose about the wavefunction?

|ψ|2 gives the probability density of finding the particle. From “Development of quantum mechanics” →

In plain English: In the mid-1920s, a new quantum mechanics replaced the old quantum theory. Key idea: Matrix and wave mechanics, probability amplitudes and uncertainty created modern quantum theory in a few years.

What is the Matilda effect?

The systematic under-recognition of women scientists, with credit given to male colleagues. From “Women and underrepresented groups in physics” →

In plain English: People of many backgrounds have shaped physics, though women and other underrepresented groups often faced exclusion and saw their work overlooked. Key idea: Physics has always drawn on diverse contributors; acknowledging overlooked work corrects history and strengthens the field.

Key takeaways

  • Greek philosophers sought rational natural explanations; Aristotle's physics dominated for centuries, while Archimedes pioneered mathematical physics.
  • Ancient astronomy combined precise observation with mathematical models; Ptolemy's epicycles predicted well despite a wrong cosmology.
  • Medieval scholars preserved and extended knowledge, especially in optics, algebra and the analysis of motion.
  • The Scientific Revolution established heliocentrism, mathematical laws and experiment, culminating in Newton's synthesis.
  • Galileo quantified motion through experiment and mathematics, and telescope evidence undermined the geocentric model.
  • Newton unified terrestrial and celestial mechanics with three laws and universal gravitation, creating classical physics.

Common mistakes in Historical Development of Physics

  • Ancient atomism was philosophical speculation, not an experimentally grounded theory like modern atomic physics.
  • A model can predict accurately yet describe reality wrongly; predictive success isn't proof of physical truth.
  • Calling the Middle Ages scientifically empty ignores major work in the Islamic world and medieval Europe.
  • The revolution wasn't instant or purely rational; it involved gradual change, institutions and debates.
  • Galileo's inertia was not exactly Newton's; he thought natural inertial motion was circular around Earth.
  • Newtonian mechanics isn't "wrong"; it's an excellent approximation at everyday speeds and gravity.

Lessons in this unit

  1. Ancient Greek natural philosophy
    Ancient Greek thinkers sought natural, rational explanations for the world instead of myths.
  2. Early astronomy and mechanics
    Early astronomers across Babylon, Greece, India, China and the Islamic world tracked the heavens with growing precision.
  3. Medieval scientific traditions
    Between antiquity and the Scientific Revolution, scholars in the Islamic world, Byzantium, India, China and medieval Europe preserved, translated and extended ancient knowledge.
  4. Scientific Revolution
    The Scientific Revolution (roughly 1543–1687) transformed how people understood nature, replacing Aristotelian ideas with a Sun-centred cosmos, mathematical laws and experiment.
  5. Galileo and experimental mechanics
    Galileo Galilei combined careful experiments, mathematics and telescope observations to overturn Aristotelian physics.
  6. Newtonian mechanics
    Isaac Newton's Principia (1687) presented three laws of motion and universal gravitation, explaining falling apples, tides, planetary orbits and comets with one framework.
  7. Development of thermodynamics
    Thermodynamics grew from efforts to improve steam engines into a universal science of energy, heat and entropy.
  8. Electromagnetism and Maxwell
    In the 19th century electricity and magnetism were unified into one theory, culminating in Maxwell's equations, which predicted electromagnetic waves travelling at the speed of light.
  9. Discovery of radioactivity
    In just a few years around 1900, the discovery of X-rays and radioactivity revealed that atoms have internal structure and can transform.
  10. Origins of quantum theory
    Quantum theory began with puzzles classical physics couldn't solve: blackbody radiation, the photoelectric effect and atomic spectra.
  11. Development of special and general relativity
    Einstein's relativity overturned absolute space and time.
  12. Nuclear physics and the atomic age
    Nuclear physics advanced rapidly from the neutron's discovery in 1932 to fission in 1938 and the first reactor and atomic bombs in the 1940s.
  13. Development of quantum mechanics
    In the mid-1920s, a new quantum mechanics replaced the old quantum theory.
  14. Particle physics and the Standard Model
    Particle physics grew from cosmic-ray discoveries in the 1930s to the Standard Model of the 1970s and the Higgs boson in 2012.
  15. Space exploration and modern astrophysics
    The 20th century transformed astronomy: galaxies, the expanding universe, the cosmic microwave background, pulsars, black holes and exoplanets were discovered, while spacecraft visited every planet.
  16. History of computing in physics
    Physics and computing grew together: physicists built early computers, invented simulation methods and created the World Wide Web.
  17. Women and underrepresented groups in physics
    People of many backgrounds have shaped physics, though women and other underrepresented groups often faced exclusion and saw their work overlooked.
  18. Scientific institutions and funding
    Physics depends on institutions, from universities and national laboratories to international collaborations and funding agencies.
  19. Physics, technology and society
    Physics drives technology, and technology changes society: steam engines, electricity, radio, nuclear power, semiconductors, lasers, GPS and the internet.
  20. Philosophy and history of scientific revolutions
    Philosophers and historians of science ask how scientific knowledge grows and changes.

Historical Development of Physics: questions students ask

What did Hipparchus discover?

Precession of the equinoxes.

What did Ibn al-Haytham establish about vision?

Light travels from objects into the eye in straight lines; he emphasised experimental proof.

What were Kepler's three laws?

Elliptical orbits with the Sun at a focus; equal areas in equal times; T2 ∝ a3.

What was Newton's "Moon test"?

Showing the Moon's centripetal acceleration matches gravity weakened by the inverse square of distance.
