Lessons in this unit

  1. Lagrangian mechanics
    Lagrangian mechanics reformulates Newton's laws using energy rather than forces.
  2. Hamiltonian mechanics
    Hamiltonian mechanics describes motion with coordinates and momenta treated as equal partners.
  3. Principle of least action
    The principle of least (stationary) action says a system follows the path that makes the action S = ∫L dt stationary.
  4. Generalised coordinates
    Generalised coordinates are any set of independent variables that fully specify a system's configuration.
  5. Canonical transformations
    Canonical transformations change phase-space variables (q, p) to new ones (Q, P) while preserving the form of Hamilton's equations.
  6. Hamilton–Jacobi theory
    Hamilton–Jacobi theory seeks a canonical transformation that makes the new Hamiltonian zero, so all new variables are constant.
  7. Symplectic geometry
    Symplectic geometry is the mathematical language of Hamiltonian mechanics.
  8. Noether's theorem
    Noether's theorem states that every continuous symmetry of the action gives a conserved quantity.
  9. Continuous and discrete symmetries
    Symmetries are transformations that leave physical laws unchanged.
  10. Lie groups and Lie algebras
    Lie groups are continuous symmetry groups, like rotations, whose elements depend smoothly on parameters.
  11. Representation theory
    A representation assigns matrices to group elements so that the group's multiplication is preserved.
  12. Tensor calculus
    Tensors are quantities that transform in a definite way under coordinate changes, so equations written with them hold in every coordinate system.
  13. Differential geometry
    Differential geometry studies curves, surfaces and higher-dimensional spaces using calculus.
  14. Manifolds and curvature
    A manifold is a space that looks flat locally but may be curved or twisted globally, like the Earth's surface.
  15. Classical field theory
    Classical field theory treats physical quantities as continuous fields spread through space and time, such as electromagnetic, gravitational and fluid fields.
  16. Quantum field theory
    Quantum field theory combines quantum mechanics and special relativity by treating particles as excitations of underlying fields.
  17. Path integrals
    Feynman's path integral says a quantum amplitude is the sum over every possible path, each weighted by eiS/ħ.
  18. Feynman diagrams
    Feynman diagrams are pictorial shorthand for terms in perturbative calculations of particle interactions.
  19. Renormalisation
    Renormalisation handles infinities that appear in quantum field theory loop calculations by absorbing them into measurable parameters like mass and charge.
  20. Gauge theories
    Gauge theories describe forces arising from local symmetries: demanding that a phase or internal rotation can vary from point to point forces the existence of gauge fields.
  21. Effective field theories
    Effective field theories describe physics at a given energy scale using only the relevant degrees of freedom, with unknown high-energy effects packaged into a few parameters.
  22. Scattering theory
    Scattering theory describes what happens when particles collide and fly apart.
  23. Green's functions
    A Green's function is the response of a linear system to a point source.
  24. Perturbative and non-perturbative methods
    Perturbation theory expands answers in powers of a small parameter, giving most of physics' precise predictions.
  25. Solvable models
    Exactly solvable models are idealised systems whose behaviour can be computed completely.
  26. Integrable systems
    Integrable systems have as many independent conserved quantities as degrees of freedom, so their motion is regular and solvable rather than chaotic.
  27. Mathematical methods for boundary-value problems
    Boundary-value problems ask for solutions of differential equations that satisfy conditions on the edges of a region.
