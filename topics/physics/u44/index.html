Lessons in this unit

  1. Instrumentation and sensor design
    Instrumentation turns physical quantities such as temperature, force, light and field into electrical signals we can record.
  2. Precision measurement
    Precision measurement pushes uncertainties down to the limits set by noise and fundamental physics.
  3. Vacuum technology
    Vacuum technology removes gas from enclosed volumes so particles can travel freely, surfaces stay clean and heat doesn't conduct away.
  4. Cryogenic systems
    Cryogenic systems cool apparatus to temperatures from 77 K down to microkelvin, where superconductivity, quantum effects and ultra-low noise appear.
  5. High-field magnets
    High-field magnets create intense magnetic fields for MRI, particle accelerators, fusion reactors and materials research.
  6. Laser systems
    Lasers produce intense, directional, coherent light by stimulated emission in a gain medium inside an optical cavity.
  7. Optical benches and alignment
    Optical benches provide stable, vibration-isolated platforms where lenses, mirrors and lasers are mounted and aligned.
  8. Spectroscopy
    Spectroscopy measures how matter absorbs, emits or scatters light as a function of wavelength.
  9. Interferometry
    Interferometry splits light (or matter waves) into paths, recombines them, and reads tiny path differences from the resulting fringes.
  10. Microwave and radio-frequency measurement
    RF and microwave measurements characterise signals and components from kilohertz to hundreds of gigahertz.
  11. Electronics and signal processing
    Electronics amplify, filter and digitise signals from sensors, while signal processing extracts information from noise.
  12. Materials characterisation
    Materials characterisation measures a material's structure, composition and properties, linking how atoms are arranged to how the material behaves.
  13. Scanning probe microscopy
    Scanning probe microscopes image surfaces by scanning a sharp tip just above or on them, measuring currents, forces or other interactions.
  14. Electron microscopy
    Electron microscopes use beams of electrons, with wavelengths thousands of times shorter than light, to image materials and biological specimens down to individual atoms.
  15. X-ray diffraction
    X-ray diffraction reveals how atoms are arranged in crystals by measuring the angles and intensities of X-rays scattered from lattice planes.
  16. Neutron scattering
    Neutron scattering probes materials with neutrons from reactors or spallation sources.
  17. Synchrotron methods
    Synchrotrons accelerate electrons around rings to produce extremely bright, tunable X-rays and other light.
  18. Particle accelerator operation
    Operating a particle accelerator means injecting, accelerating, focusing, steering and colliding or extracting beams reliably for hours at a time.
  19. Radiation measurement
    Radiation measurement quantifies activity, dose and contamination to protect people and support research, medicine and industry.
  20. Experimental control systems
    Control systems keep experiments at their set points: temperatures, positions, laser frequencies, beam orbits.
  21. Automation and data acquisition
    Data acquisition (DAQ) systems capture signals from instruments, timestamp and store them, while automation runs experiments without constant human intervention.
  22. Laboratory safety and risk assessment
    Laboratory safety protects people, equipment and the environment from hazards such as lasers, high voltage, radiation, cryogens, chemicals and magnetic fields.
