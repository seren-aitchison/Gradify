Lessons in this unit

  1. Geometrical optics
    Geometrical (ray) optics treats light as rays travelling in straight lines, bending at interfaces.
  2. Reflection and refraction
    At a boundary, light partly reflects and partly refracts.
  3. Snell's law
    Snell's law gives the angle of a refracted ray: the product of refractive index and the sine of the angle is the same on both sides of an interface.
  4. Total internal reflection
    When light travelling in a dense medium meets a less dense one at an angle above the critical angle, none refracts out: it's totally reflected.
  5. Mirrors and image formation
    Mirrors form images by reflection.
  6. Thin lenses
    A thin lens refracts light at two curved surfaces to bring rays to a focus (converging) or spread them (diverging).
  7. Lens equations
    The thin lens equation relates object distance, image distance and focal length; magnification gives image size.
  8. Optical instruments
    Optical instruments combine lenses and mirrors to see things too small, too far or too faint for the eye.
  9. Microscopes and telescopes
    Microscopes magnify small, near objects; telescopes magnify the angular size of distant ones and collect their faint light.
  10. Aberrations
    Aberrations are departures from perfect imaging in real lenses and mirrors.
  11. Dispersion and prisms
    A prism refracts different colours by different amounts because glass's refractive index depends on wavelength.
  12. Physical optics
    Physical (wave) optics treats light as a wave to explain what rays can't: interference, diffraction, coherence and polarisation.
  13. Young's double-slit experiment
    Young's double-slit experiment sends light through two narrow slits and shows bright and dark fringes on a screen: interference of waves from the two slits.
  14. Interference and coherence
    Interference needs coherence: a stable phase relationship between waves.
  15. Diffraction
    Diffraction is the spreading and bending of light around obstacles and through apertures, producing patterns of light and dark.
  16. Single-slit and multiple-slit diffraction
    A single slit produces a broad central maximum with weaker side fringes.
  17. Diffraction gratings
    A diffraction grating has thousands of closely spaced slits or grooves.
  18. Michelson interferometer
    A Michelson interferometer splits a beam, sends the halves along two arms, and recombines them.
  19. Fabry–Pérot interferometer
    A Fabry–Pérot interferometer is two parallel, highly reflective surfaces.
  20. Polarisation
    Polarisation describes the orientation of light's electric field oscillation.
  21. Brewster's angle
    At Brewster's angle, light polarised in the plane of incidence (p) isn't reflected at all, so reflected light is perfectly polarised.
  22. Birefringence
    Birefringent crystals have two refractive indices depending on the polarisation of light.
  23. Optical activity
    Optically active materials rotate the plane of polarised light as it passes through.
  24. Lasers
    A laser produces intense, coherent, highly directional, nearly monochromatic light by stimulated emission in a gain medium inside an optical cavity.
  25. Optical cavities
    An optical cavity (resonator) traps light between mirrors so it circulates many times.
  26. Fibre optics
    Optical fibres guide light along thin glass strands by total internal reflection.
  27. Nonlinear optics
    At high intensities, a material's response to light is no longer proportional to the field.
  28. Ultrafast optics
    Ultrafast optics creates and uses light pulses lasting femtoseconds to attoseconds.
  29. Integrated photonics
    Integrated photonics puts optical components (waveguides, modulators, detectors, lasers) on chips, as electronics did for circuits.
  30. Adaptive optics
    Adaptive optics corrects in real time for distortions in light, mainly from atmospheric turbulence, using a deformable mirror controlled by a wavefront sensor.
