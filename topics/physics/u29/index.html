What is quasineutrality?

Near-equality of positive and negative charge densities over scales larger than the Debye length. From “Plasma as a state of matter” →

In plain English: A plasma is an ionised gas of free electrons and ions that behaves collectively through long-range electric and magnetic forces.

What is the plasma frequency?

The natural oscillation frequency of electrons in a plasma, ωp = √(ne2/ε0m). From “Plasma frequency” →

In plain English: The plasma frequency is the natural oscillation frequency of electrons displaced in a plasma.

What does MHD describe?

Plasma as a conducting fluid coupled to magnetic fields. From “Magnetohydrodynamics” →

In plain English: Magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) treats a plasma as an electrically conducting fluid interacting with magnetic fields.

Why is toroidal confinement needed?

To close field lines so particles don't escape along them at the ends. From “Magnetic confinement” →

In plain English: Magnetic confinement holds a hot plasma away from material walls using magnetic fields, since charged particles spiral along field lines.

What is the coronal heating problem?

Explaining why the corona is millions of degrees hotter than the photosphere. From “Solar and astrophysical plasmas” →

In plain English: Almost all visible matter in the universe is plasma.

Common mistakes in Plasma Physics

  • Ionisation alone doesn't make a plasma; collective behaviour does.
  • Ionisation fraction depends on density too: lower density favours ionisation at a given temperature.
  • Shielding is statistical; individual charges still move through the plasma and interact.
  • The Debye length uses the electron temperature (in most cases); hot electrons and cold ions are common in labs.
  • Metals' shininess and the ionosphere's radio reflection are the same physics at very different frequencies.
  • The E × B drift is perpendicular to both fields and independent of charge: electrons and ions drift together, so no current flows from it.

Lessons in this unit

  1. Plasma as a state of matter
    A plasma is an ionised gas of free electrons and ions that behaves collectively through long-range electric and magnetic forces.
  2. Ionisation and recombination
    Plasmas form when atoms lose electrons through collisions, radiation or strong fields, and they're maintained by a balance between ionisation and recombination.
  3. Debye shielding
    In a plasma, free charges rearrange to screen out any electric potential, so the field of a charge falls off exponentially beyond a short distance.
  4. Debye length
    The Debye length is the distance over which charges in a plasma screen each other.
  5. Plasma frequency
    The plasma frequency is the natural oscillation frequency of electrons displaced in a plasma.
  6. Charged-particle motion
    Understanding how individual charged particles move in electric and magnetic fields is the starting point of plasma physics.
  7. Plasma oscillations
    Displace electrons slightly in a plasma and the resulting charge separation pulls them back, so they oscillate at the plasma frequency.
  8. Magnetised plasmas
    In a magnetic field, plasma behaviour becomes strongly directional: particles move freely along field lines but are tied to them across.
  9. Magnetohydrodynamics
    Magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) treats a plasma as an electrically conducting fluid interacting with magnetic fields.
  10. Plasma waves
    Plasmas support a rich variety of waves: electrostatic and electromagnetic, involving electrons, ions or both, with magnetic fields adding more modes.
  11. Alfvén waves
    Alfvén waves are transverse oscillations of magnetic field lines and plasma, like waves on a plucked string, with the field's tension providing the restoring force.
  12. Plasma instabilities
    Plasmas store energy in gradients of density, temperature, current and velocity, and often release it through instabilities that grow rapidly.
  13. Magnetic confinement
    Magnetic confinement holds a hot plasma away from material walls using magnetic fields, since charged particles spiral along field lines.
  14. Tokamaks and stellarators
    Tokamaks and stellarators are the two main toroidal magnetic confinement designs for fusion.
  15. Inertial confinement fusion
    Inertial confinement fusion compresses a tiny fuel capsule so fast and hard that it fuses before it can fly apart.
  16. Fusion energy
    Fusion energy aims to generate electricity from the same reactions that power the Sun, using abundant fuels and producing no carbon emissions or long-lived high-level waste.
  17. Solar and astrophysical plasmas
    Almost all visible matter in the universe is plasma.
  18. Space plasma physics
    Space plasma physics studies plasmas in the solar system: the solar wind, planetary magnetospheres and ionospheres.
  19. Plasma diagnostics
    Plasma diagnostics measure temperatures, densities, fields and flows in plasmas that are too hot or remote for ordinary instruments.
  20. Industrial and technological plasmas
    Low-temperature plasmas are essential industrial tools: they etch and deposit layers on every computer chip, light our streets, cut and weld metals, sterilise medical equipment and treat surfaces.

Plasma Physics: questions students ask

What does the Saha equation describe?

The degree of ionisation of a gas in thermal equilibrium.

What is Debye shielding?

The screening of a charge's field by the surrounding plasma charges, making it decay exponentially.

What is the plasma parameter?

The number of particles in a Debye sphere; must be large for collective behaviour.

What is the E × B drift?

A drift of all charged particles perpendicular to both E and B at speed E/B.
