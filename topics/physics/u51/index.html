Lessons in this unit

  1. Explaining physical concepts clearly
    Explaining physics clearly means connecting abstract ideas to what the listener already knows, using precise language, concrete examples and well-chosen analogies.
  2. Scientific diagrams and representations
    Diagrams turn physical situations into visual models: free-body diagrams, field lines, ray diagrams, circuit diagrams, energy-level diagrams and graphs.
  3. Deriving equations from first principles
    Deriving equations from first principles means starting with fundamental laws and logically building to a result.
  4. Translating physical situations into mathematical models
    Modelling turns a real situation into mathematics: deciding what matters, what can be neglected, which laws apply and which variables describe the system.
  5. Estimation and order-of-magnitude reasoning
    Order-of-magnitude estimation gives quick, approximate answers using rough numbers and simple physics.
  6. Solving multi-step problems
    Real physics problems usually need several steps, combining principles in sequence.
  7. Interpreting graphs and experimental data
    Graphs reveal relationships hidden in tables of numbers.
  8. Demonstrations and practical investigations
    Demonstrations and hands-on investigations make physics tangible, letting learners see principles in action and practise scientific methods.
  9. Laboratory report writing
    A laboratory report communicates what you did, what you found and what it means, clearly enough that someone else could reproduce and judge your work.
  10. Scientific presentations
    Scientific presentations, from class talks to conference posters, communicate results to audiences quickly.
  11. Peer review and research critique
    Peer review is how scientists evaluate each other's work before publication, checking methods, analysis and conclusions.
  12. Communicating uncertainty
    Every measurement and prediction has uncertainty, and communicating it well is essential for honest science and good decisions.
  13. Public engagement with physics
    Public engagement shares physics with society through talks, festivals, museums, media and citizen science, and listens to public concerns.
  14. Science communication and misinformation
    Misinformation about physics, such as claims about radiation, 5G, climate or free energy, spreads quickly online.
  15. Teaching and assessing conceptual understanding
    Understanding physics means more than plugging numbers into formulas: it means grasping concepts deeply enough to explain and apply them in new situations.
  16. Identifying misconceptions
    Learners bring intuitive ideas about the physical world, many of them reasonable but incorrect.
  17. Interdisciplinary communication
    Physicists increasingly work with biologists, engineers, clinicians, economists, data scientists and policymakers.
