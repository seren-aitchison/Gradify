Lessons in this unit

  1. Crystalline and amorphous solids
    In crystalline solids, atoms sit in a regular repeating pattern extending over long distances; in amorphous solids like glass, they have only short-range order.
  2. Crystal lattices and unit cells
    A crystal is a lattice (an infinite array of points with identical surroundings) plus a basis (the group of atoms attached to each point).
  3. Bravais lattices
    In three dimensions there are exactly 14 distinct ways to arrange points so every point has identical surroundings: the Bravais lattices.
  4. Miller indices
    Miller indices (hkl) label planes and directions in a crystal using whole numbers.
  5. Crystal symmetry
    Crystal symmetry describes the operations (rotations, reflections, inversions, translations, screw axes and glide planes) that map a crystal onto itself.
  6. Reciprocal lattices
    The reciprocal lattice is the Fourier transform of the crystal lattice: a lattice in wavevector space.
  7. X-ray diffraction
    X-rays have wavelengths similar to atomic spacings, so crystals diffract them into sharp beams.
  8. Phonons and lattice vibrations
    Atoms in a crystal vibrate collectively in waves.
  9. Specific heat of solids
    The heat capacity of solids is constant at high temperatures but falls towards zero as temperature drops.
  10. Thermal conductivity
    Heat in solids is carried by phonons and, in metals, mostly by electrons.
  11. Electronic band structure
    In a crystal, electrons' allowed energies form bands separated by gaps, because their waves interact with the periodic lattice.
  12. Metals, insulators and semiconductors
    Whether a material conducts depends on how electrons fill its bands.
  13. Fermi energy and Fermi surfaces
    The Fermi energy is the highest occupied electron energy at absolute zero; the Fermi surface is the surface in momentum space separating filled from empty states.
  14. Density of states
    The density of states counts how many electron (or phonon) states there are per unit energy.
  15. Electron transport
    Electron transport describes how electrons carry current and heat through materials under fields and temperature gradients.
  16. Electrical resistivity
    Resistivity measures how strongly a material opposes current.
  17. Hall effect
    When current flows through a conductor in a perpendicular magnetic field, a voltage appears across it.
  18. Magnetic ordering
    In many materials, electron spins order spontaneously below a critical temperature into patterns: all parallel (ferromagnets), alternating (antiferromagnets), unequal antiparallel (ferrimagnets) or mo
  19. Ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic materials
    Ferromagnets and antiferromagnets are the workhorses of magnetic technology: permanent magnets, transformer cores, hard drives and new spintronic memories.
  20. Dielectric properties
    Dielectrics are insulators that polarise in electric fields, storing energy and slowing light.
  21. Piezoelectricity
    Piezoelectric materials produce a voltage when squeezed and change shape when a voltage is applied.
  22. Ferroelectricity
    Ferroelectrics have a spontaneous electric polarisation that can be reversed by an applied field.
  23. Superconductivity
    Below a critical temperature, superconductors carry current with exactly zero resistance and expel magnetic fields.
  24. Conventional and unconventional superconductors
    Conventional superconductors are explained by BCS theory, with electrons paired by lattice vibrations.
  25. Cooper pairs
    Cooper pairs are bound pairs of electrons with opposite momenta and spins, formed by a weak effective attraction in a metal.
  26. Josephson effect
    When two superconductors are separated by a thin barrier, Cooper pairs tunnel through it, creating a supercurrent with no voltage.
  27. Semiconductor heterostructures
    Heterostructures stack different semiconductors in nanometre-thin layers, engineering band edges to confine electrons.
  28. Quantum Hall effect
    In a two-dimensional electron gas at low temperature and high magnetic field, the Hall resistance takes exactly quantised values h/(νe2).
  29. Topological insulators
    Topological insulators are insulating inside but conduct along their surfaces or edges through special states protected by symmetry and topology.
  30. Strongly correlated electron systems
    In strongly correlated materials, electron–electron interactions dominate over kinetic energy, so simple band theory fails.
  31. Low-dimensional materials
    When electrons or atoms are confined to two, one or zero dimensions, new quantum effects emerge.
  32. Graphene and two-dimensional materials
    Graphene is a single layer of carbon atoms in a honeycomb lattice: the first true 2D material, isolated in 2004 with sticky tape (Geim and Novoselov, Nobel 2010).
  33. Nanomaterials
    Nanomaterials have structures between about 1 and 100 nm.
  34. Soft condensed matter
    Soft condensed matter covers materials easily deformed by thermal fluctuations or small forces: polymers, colloids, gels, foams, liquid crystals and biological matter.
  35. Polymers, colloids and liquid crystals
    Polymers are long chain molecules; colloids are particles suspended in a fluid; liquid crystals flow like liquids but have orientational order like crystals.
