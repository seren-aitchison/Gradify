Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What is the damping ratio?

The ratio of actual damping to critical damping, ζ = c/(2√(km)). From “Mechanical vibration analysis” →

In plain English: Vibration analysis studies how machines and structures oscillate when disturbed or driven. Key idea: Natural frequency and damping ratio describe vibration; driving near resonance amplifies response by ~1/(2ζ).

What is operational modal analysis?

Identifying modes from ambient vibration without measuring the input. From “Modal analysis” →

In plain English: Modal analysis identifies the natural frequencies, mode shapes and damping of a structure. Key idea: Modal analysis extracts frequencies, damping and shapes from FRFs, and validates models through MAC correlation.

What is Lighthill's eighth-power law?

Jet noise power scales with the eighth power of jet velocity. From “Aeroacoustics” →

In plain English: Aeroacoustics studies sound generated by moving air: jet noise, fan and propeller noise, turbulence around wings and wind noise on cars. Key idea: Flow noise rises steeply with speed (U6–U8); reducing velocities and smoothing flows is the main lever.

Why do S-waves cause more damage than P-waves?

They have larger amplitudes and shear motion that structures resist poorly. From “Seismic engineering physics” →

In plain English: Seismic engineering applies wave physics and structural dynamics to design buildings and infrastructure that survive earthquakes. Key idea: Seismic design avoids resonance, adds ductility, and uses isolation and damping to survive strong shaking.

What is added mass?

The extra effective inertia from fluid that must accelerate with a body. From “Fluid-structure interaction” →

In plain English: Fluid–structure interaction describes how flowing fluids and flexible structures affect each other: wind making bridges sway, blood flow deforming arteries, flags flapping and pipes vibrating. Key idea: Fluids and structures can feed back on each other; resonance with flow phenomena and flutter must be designed out.

Key takeaways

  • Natural frequency and damping ratio describe vibration; driving near resonance amplifies response by ~1/(2ζ).
  • Structures have many modes; dynamic loads near those frequencies must be avoided or damped.
  • Resonance amplifies response by ~Q; damping lowers Q and broadens the peak.
  • Modal analysis extracts frequencies, damping and shapes from FRFs, and validates models through MAC correlation.
  • Use dB arithmetic correctly and tackle noise at source first, then path, then receiver.
  • Absorbers reduce reflections and reverberation; heavy, decoupled, airtight constructions block transmission.

Common mistakes in Acoustics, Vibrations and Engineering Physics

  • Light damping makes resonance peaks sharp and dangerous; small changes in speed can produce large vibrations.
  • A structure strong enough statically can still fail dynamically through resonance or flutter.
  • Adding damping reduces peak response but also reduces the efficiency of resonant devices like sensors and clocks.
  • Accelerometer mass and poor boundary conditions can shift measured frequencies; light sensors and realistic supports matter.
  • Decibels don't add arithmetically; two 80 dB sources give 83 dB, not 160 dB.
  • Absorbent foam does little to stop sound passing through walls; insulation needs mass and airtightness.

Lessons in this unit

  1. Mechanical vibration analysis
    Vibration analysis studies how machines and structures oscillate when disturbed or driven.
  2. Structural dynamics
    Structural dynamics analyses how buildings, bridges, aircraft and towers respond to time-varying loads such as wind, traffic, earthquakes and machinery.
  3. Resonance and damping
    Resonance occurs when a system is driven at its natural frequency, building large amplitudes from small inputs.
  4. Modal analysis
    Modal analysis identifies the natural frequencies, mode shapes and damping of a structure.
  5. Noise control
    Noise control reduces unwanted sound at its source, along its path or at the receiver.
  6. Acoustic materials
    Acoustic materials control sound by absorbing, reflecting, transmitting or diffusing it.
  7. Sound propagation in structures
    Sound also travels through solids as structure-borne vibration, carried by longitudinal, shear and bending waves.
  8. Aeroacoustics
    Aeroacoustics studies sound generated by moving air: jet noise, fan and propeller noise, turbulence around wings and wind noise on cars.
  9. Underwater acoustics
    Sound travels far in water where light and radio cannot, making acoustics the main way to sense and communicate underwater.
  10. Vibration isolation
    Vibration isolation prevents vibrations passing between a source and its surroundings, protecting sensitive instruments or keeping machinery vibrations out of buildings.
  11. Seismic engineering physics
    Seismic engineering applies wave physics and structural dynamics to design buildings and infrastructure that survive earthquakes.
  12. Shock waves
    Shock waves are abrupt, nearly discontinuous jumps in pressure, density and temperature that travel faster than sound.
  13. Impact mechanics
    Impact mechanics studies collisions where large forces act over short times: car crashes, sports, bird strikes, ballistic protection and dropped phones.
  14. Tribology and friction
    Tribology is the science of friction, wear and lubrication of surfaces in contact.
  15. Fluid-structure interaction
    Fluid–structure interaction describes how flowing fluids and flexible structures affect each other: wind making bridges sway, blood flow deforming arteries, flags flapping and pipes vibrating.
  16. Engineering measurement systems
    Engineering measurement systems monitor strain, displacement, force, pressure, temperature, flow and vibration in tests and operating equipment.
  17. Failure analysis
    Failure analysis investigates why components and structures break, using physics, materials science and detective work.
  18. Reliability and physical modelling
    Reliability engineering predicts and improves how long systems work before failing.

Acoustics, Vibrations and Engineering Physics: questions students ask

What caused the Tacoma Narrows Bridge collapse?

Aeroelastic flutter, a self-excited torsional oscillation driven by wind.

What is the quality factor Q?

A measure of resonance sharpness: centre frequency divided by bandwidth.

What is a frequency response function?

The ratio of response to input force as a function of frequency.

What does A-weighting do?

Adjusts levels to match human hearing sensitivity, reducing low and very high frequencies.
