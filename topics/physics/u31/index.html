Common mistakes in Cosmology

  • Distant galaxies receding faster than light don't violate relativity; it's space expanding, not motion through space.
  • The universe is very inhomogeneous on small scales (galaxies, voids); the principle applies only on very large scales.
  • The expansion isn't an explosion into pre-existing space; space itself grows.
  • Hubble's law is linear only at small distances; at high redshift, the relation depends on the expansion history.
  • "Distance" is ambiguous in cosmology; always specify which one, especially at high redshift.
  • FLRW models assume perfect homogeneity; real structure is treated as perturbations on top.

Lessons in this unit

  1. The observable universe
    The observable universe is the region from which light has had time to reach us since the Big Bang.
  2. Cosmological principles
    The cosmological principle states that, on large enough scales, the universe looks the same everywhere (homogeneous) and in every direction (isotropic).
  3. Expansion of the universe
    The universe is expanding: distant galaxies are moving apart, not through space but because space itself stretches.
  4. Hubble's law
    Hubble's law states that galaxies recede at speeds proportional to their distance.
  5. Redshift and distance measurements
    Redshift measures how much the universe has expanded since light left a source; turning redshift into distance requires a cosmological model.
  6. Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker models
    FLRW models are the family of general-relativistic universes that are homogeneous and isotropic.
  7. Big Bang cosmology
    The Big Bang theory states that the universe began about 13.8 billion years ago in an extremely hot, dense state and has been expanding and cooling ever since.
  8. Cosmic microwave background
    The cosmic microwave background is the afterglow of the Big Bang: light released when the universe became transparent 380 000 years after it began.
  9. Primordial nucleosynthesis
    In the first few minutes after the Big Bang, protons and neutrons fused into deuterium, helium and a little lithium.
  10. Structure formation
    Galaxies, clusters and the cosmic web grew from tiny density fluctuations in the early universe, amplified by gravity over billions of years.
  11. Dark matter
    Dark matter is invisible matter making up about 27% of the universe's energy (85% of its matter).
  12. Dark energy
    Dark energy is the mysterious component causing the universe's expansion to accelerate.
  13. Cosmological constant
    The cosmological constant Λ is a term in Einstein's equations representing a constant energy density of empty space.
  14. Inflationary cosmology
    Inflation is a proposed period of extremely rapid exponential expansion in the first ~10-32 s.
  15. Baryon acoustic oscillations
    Before recombination, sound waves rippled through the hot plasma of photons and baryons.
  16. Large-scale structure
    On scales of millions of light-years, galaxies form a cosmic web: dense clusters linked by filaments and sheets surrounding vast empty voids.
  17. Galaxy surveys
    Galaxy surveys map the positions, distances and properties of millions to billions of galaxies.
  18. Cosmic distance ladder
    The cosmic distance ladder measures distances step by step: each method calibrates the next, reaching from the Solar System to the edge of the observable universe.
  19. Age and geometry of the universe
    Combining the expansion rate and the universe's contents gives its age, 13.8 billion years, and its geometry, flat to within 0.2%.
  20. Open, flat and closed cosmologies
    The overall geometry of the universe depends on its total density compared with the critical density.
  21. Matter-energy density parameters
    The universe's contents are summarised by density parameters: each component's density as a fraction of the critical density.
  22. Modified gravity
    Modified gravity theories change general relativity on large scales or at low accelerations, aiming to explain cosmic acceleration or galaxy dynamics without dark energy or dark matter.
  23. Cyclic and alternative cosmological models
    Besides the standard inflationary Big Bang, physicists explore alternatives: cyclic universes, bouncing cosmologies and steady-state theories.
  24. Observational constraints on cosmology
    Modern cosmology combines many independent observations, each constraining different parameters.

Cosmology: questions students ask

What causes the CMB dipole?

Our motion relative to the CMB rest frame.

What is the scale factor?

The function a(t) describing how distances between unbound objects grow with time.

What is the Hubble tension?

The disagreement between early-universe (~67) and local (~73) measurements of H0.

What is luminosity distance?

The distance inferred from observed flux and known luminosity, F = L/(4πdL2).
