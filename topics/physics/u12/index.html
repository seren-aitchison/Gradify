Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What is thermal equilibrium?

The state in which bodies in thermal contact exchange no net energy, because they're at the same temperature. From “Temperature and thermal equilibrium” →

In plain English: Temperature is the property that decides which way heat flows: from hot to cold, until the two are in thermal equilibrium at the same temperature.

State the first law of thermodynamics.

ΔU = Q − W: internal energy change equals heat supplied minus work done by the system. From “First law of thermodynamics” →

In plain English: The first law is energy conservation for thermodynamic systems: the change in internal energy equals the heat supplied to the system minus the work it does.

Why must a heat engine reject heat?

The second law forbids converting heat entirely into work in a cycle. From “Heat engines” →

In plain English: A heat engine takes heat from a hot source, converts part of it to work, and rejects the rest to a cold sink.

State Carnot's theorem.

No engine operating between two reservoirs can be more efficient than a reversible one, and all reversible engines between them are equally efficient. From “Carnot cycle” →

In plain English: The Carnot cycle is the ideal reversible engine: two isothermal and two adiabatic steps between a hot and a cold reservoir.

What does (∂S/∂V)T = (∂p/∂T)V let you do?

Find entropy changes from measurable p–V–T data. From “Maxwell relations” →

In plain English: Maxwell relations are equalities between partial derivatives of thermodynamic variables, following from the potentials being state functions.

Common mistakes in Thermodynamics

  • Temperature is not heat. A bath of warm water contains more internal energy than a red-hot spark, though the spark is hotter.
  • Thermal equilibrium means no net heat flow, not no microscopic energy exchange: molecules still collide constantly.
  • "Heat content" is a misleading phrase. A body has internal energy; heat is energy crossing a boundary.
  • Specific heat capacity isn't constant; it changes with temperature, especially at low temperatures.
  • During a phase change, adding heat doesn't raise the temperature; a pan of boiling water stays at 100 °C (at 1 atm) however high the flame.
  • Holes expand too when a plate is heated, as if the hole were made of the same material: heating a metal lid helps loosen it.

Lessons in this unit

  1. Temperature and thermal equilibrium
    Temperature is the property that decides which way heat flows: from hot to cold, until the two are in thermal equilibrium at the same temperature.
  2. Zeroth law of thermodynamics
    The zeroth law says that if two systems are each in thermal equilibrium with a third, they're in equilibrium with each other.
  3. Heat and internal energy
    Internal energy is the total microscopic energy of a system's particles.
  4. Specific heat capacity
    Specific heat capacity is the energy needed to raise the temperature of 1 kg of a substance by 1 K.
  5. Latent heat
    Latent heat is the energy absorbed or released during a change of phase at constant temperature.
  6. Thermal expansion
    Most materials expand when heated because their atoms vibrate with larger amplitude in an asymmetric potential, increasing the average spacing.
  7. First law of thermodynamics
    The first law is energy conservation for thermodynamic systems: the change in internal energy equals the heat supplied to the system minus the work it does.
  8. Work done by thermodynamic systems
    A gas does work when it expands against an external pressure.
  9. Pressure-volume diagrams
    A p–V diagram plots a gas's state as it changes.
  10. Isothermal processes
    In an isothermal process the temperature stays constant.
  11. Adiabatic processes
    In an adiabatic process no heat enters or leaves.
  12. Isobaric and isochoric processes
    In an isobaric process the pressure is constant; in an isochoric one the volume is constant.
  13. Heat engines
    A heat engine takes heat from a hot source, converts part of it to work, and rejects the rest to a cold sink.
  14. Refrigerators and heat pumps
    Refrigerators and heat pumps run a heat engine backwards: work drives heat from a cold place to a hot one.
  15. Thermal efficiency
    Thermal efficiency measures how much of the heat put into an engine becomes useful work.
  16. Second law of thermodynamics
    The second law says heat flows spontaneously only from hot to cold, and no cyclic process can turn heat entirely into work.
  17. Entropy
    Entropy measures how many microscopic arrangements correspond to a system's macroscopic state, or equivalently how much energy is dispersed and unavailable for work.
  18. Reversible and irreversible processes
    A reversible process can be undone exactly, leaving no change anywhere; it happens through a sequence of equilibrium states and creates no entropy.
  19. Carnot cycle
    The Carnot cycle is the ideal reversible engine: two isothermal and two adiabatic steps between a hot and a cold reservoir.
  20. Thermodynamic potentials
    Thermodynamic potentials (U, H, F, G) are energy-like state functions, each natural for different controlled conditions.
  21. Enthalpy
    Enthalpy H = U + pV is the energy-like quantity whose change equals the heat absorbed at constant pressure.
  22. Helmholtz free energy
    The Helmholtz free energy F = U − TS is the maximum work a system can do at constant temperature.
  23. Gibbs free energy
    The Gibbs free energy G = H − TS decides whether a process happens spontaneously at constant temperature and pressure, the conditions of most chemistry and biology.
  24. Chemical and phase equilibrium
    At equilibrium, matter has no tendency to move between phases or react further: each species has the same chemical potential everywhere it can go.
  25. Maxwell relations
    Maxwell relations are equalities between partial derivatives of thermodynamic variables, following from the potentials being state functions.
  26. Third law of thermodynamics
    The third law says the entropy of a perfect crystal approaches zero as the temperature approaches absolute zero, and that absolute zero can't be reached in a finite number of steps.
  27. Thermodynamic cycles
    Practical engines and refrigerators follow particular cycles chosen for their working fluid and hardware.
  28. Equations of state
    An equation of state relates pressure, volume and temperature (and amount) for a substance.
  29. Critical points
    A critical point is where the distinction between two phases disappears: above the critical temperature, liquid and gas merge into one supercritical fluid.
  30. Phase transitions
    A phase transition is an abrupt change in a system's properties as a control parameter (temperature, pressure, field) crosses a critical value: melting, boiling, magnetisation, superconductivity.
  31. Non-equilibrium thermodynamics
    Most of the world is out of equilibrium: heat flows, currents run, chemical reactions proceed, organisms live.

Thermodynamics: questions students ask

Why is the zeroth law needed for thermometers?

It ensures that a thermometer's reading can compare the temperatures of separate bodies.

What's the difference between heat and internal energy?

Internal energy is a property of a system; heat is energy transferred because of a temperature difference.

Why does water moderate coastal climates?

Its high specific heat capacity means it warms and cools slowly.

What is specific latent heat?

The energy per kg to change phase at constant temperature.
