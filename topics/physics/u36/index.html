Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What is the photon energy in eV at wavelength λ in nm?

E ≈ 1240/λ. From “Light-matter interactions” →

In plain English: Every photonic device relies on a handful of ways light and matter exchange energy: absorption, spontaneous emission and stimulated emission, along with scattering and refraction. Key idea: Stimulated emission produces identical photons; a population inversion lets it outpace absorption and give gain.

What is Vπ?

The voltage needed to shift the optical phase by π, switching an MZM from fully on to fully off. From “Optical modulators” →

In plain English: Optical modulators impose information on light by changing its intensity, phase or polarisation at high speed. Key idea: Modulators convert electrical signals into changes of optical phase or absorption, then often into intensity via interference.

Which nonlinear order occurs in all materials?

Third order, χ(3). From “Nonlinear optical processes” →

In plain English: At high intensities, a material's response to light is no longer proportional to the field, so new frequencies appear and beams change their own propagation. Key idea: Nonlinear optics appears when intense light changes the material's response, mixing frequencies and modifying the beam itself.

What is the Hong–Ou–Mandel effect?

Two indistinguishable photons meeting at a 50:50 beam splitter always exit together. From “Quantum optics” →

In plain English: Quantum optics treats light as made of photons with quantum states, revealing effects classical waves cannot explain: antibunching, squeezing, entanglement and two-photon interference. Key idea: Photon statistics, not just intensity, reveal the quantum nature of light.

Name an advantage of fibre sensors.

Immune to electromagnetic interference, intrinsically safe and multiplexable. From “Optical sensors” →

In plain English: Optical sensors use light to measure temperature, strain, chemistry, rotation, pressure and more. Key idea: Optical sensors convert physical changes into measurable shifts in light, often along a fibre over long distances.

Key takeaways

  • Stimulated emission produces identical photons; a population inversion lets it outpace absorption and give gain.
  • Light emission needs a direct band gap; the gap energy sets the wavelength.
  • An LED is spontaneous emission from a junction; a laser diode adds feedback so stimulated emission dominates above threshold.
  • Responsivity rises with wavelength until photons no longer exceed the band gap.
  • Modulators convert electrical signals into changes of optical phase or absorption, then often into intensity via interference.
  • PICs miniaturise optical systems onto chips; the choice of platform trades lasers, loss, speed and cost.

Common mistakes in Optoelectronics and Photonics

  • A two-level system cannot reach steady-state inversion by optical pumping; at best it saturates to equal populations.
  • Silicon is a poor light emitter because its gap is indirect, not because its gap is the wrong size.
  • LED efficiency is often limited by light extraction, not internal recombination.
  • More gain does not always mean better SNR: APD excess noise grows with gain.
  • Phase modulation alone changes no intensity; an interferometer is needed to see it on a simple detector.
  • Silicon cannot easily make its own lasers; light sources must usually be added from III–V materials.

Lessons in this unit

  1. Light-matter interactions
    Every photonic device relies on a handful of ways light and matter exchange energy: absorption, spontaneous emission and stimulated emission, along with scattering and refraction.
  2. Semiconductor light emission
    Semiconductors emit light when electrons in the conduction band recombine with holes in the valence band.
  3. LEDs and laser diodes
    LEDs and laser diodes are forward-biased p–n junctions that turn current into light.
  4. Photodetectors
    Photodetectors convert light into an electrical signal.
  5. Optical modulators
    Optical modulators impose information on light by changing its intensity, phase or polarisation at high speed.
  6. Photonic integrated circuits
    Photonic integrated circuits put many optical components, such as lasers, waveguides, modulators, filters and detectors, on a single chip, as electronic chips did for transistors.
  7. Optical waveguides
    Optical waveguides confine and guide light along a path using total internal reflection or more subtle interference effects.
  8. Fibre-optic communications
    Fibre-optic communication sends data as light pulses through glass fibres thinner than a hair, across oceans and into homes.
  9. Nonlinear optical processes
    At high intensities, a material's response to light is no longer proportional to the field, so new frequencies appear and beams change their own propagation.
  10. Frequency conversion
    Frequency conversion uses nonlinear crystals to make new colours of laser light: doubling, tripling, summing and splitting photons.
  11. Ultrafast lasers
    Ultrafast lasers produce pulses lasting picoseconds to femtoseconds, the shortest controlled events humans can make.
  12. Femtosecond optics
    Femtosecond optics deals with how ultrashort pulses are measured, shaped and used.
  13. Quantum optics
    Quantum optics treats light as made of photons with quantum states, revealing effects classical waves cannot explain: antibunching, squeezing, entanglement and two-photon interference.
  14. Single-photon sources
    Single-photon sources emit exactly one photon on demand, ideally indistinguishable from every other.
  15. Photonic crystals
    Photonic crystals are materials with periodic refractive index on the scale of the wavelength of light.
  16. Plasmonics
    Plasmonics uses collective oscillations of electrons at metal surfaces, surface plasmons, to squeeze light into regions far smaller than its wavelength.
  17. Optical sensors
    Optical sensors use light to measure temperature, strain, chemistry, rotation, pressure and more.
  18. Lidar
    Lidar measures distance by timing laser pulses or measuring frequency shifts of returning light, building precise 3D maps.
  19. Optical communications
    Optical communications covers the whole system that moves information with light: transmitters, channels, receivers and the coding that squeezes in more bits.
  20. Optical metrology
    Optical metrology uses light to measure length, shape, surface finish, vibration and time with extraordinary precision.
  21. Silicon photonics
    Silicon photonics builds optical circuits in silicon using the same foundries that make computer chips.

Optoelectronics and Photonics: questions students ask

What property makes stimulated emission special?

The emitted photon is identical in frequency, phase, direction and polarisation to the stimulating photon.

Why is GaAs a good light emitter but Si is not?

GaAs has a direct gap; Si's is indirect, needing a phonon for each photon.

How is white light made in most LEDs?

A blue InGaN chip excites a yellow phosphor; blue plus yellow looks white.

What is the cutoff wavelength of silicon?

About 1.1 μm, set by its 1.12 eV band gap.
