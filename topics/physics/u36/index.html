Lessons in this unit

  1. Light-matter interactions
    Every photonic device relies on a handful of ways light and matter exchange energy: absorption, spontaneous emission and stimulated emission, along with scattering and refraction.
  2. Semiconductor light emission
    Semiconductors emit light when electrons in the conduction band recombine with holes in the valence band.
  3. LEDs and laser diodes
    LEDs and laser diodes are forward-biased p–n junctions that turn current into light.
  4. Photodetectors
    Photodetectors convert light into an electrical signal.
  5. Optical modulators
    Optical modulators impose information on light by changing its intensity, phase or polarisation at high speed.
  6. Photonic integrated circuits
    Photonic integrated circuits put many optical components, such as lasers, waveguides, modulators, filters and detectors, on a single chip, as electronic chips did for transistors.
  7. Optical waveguides
    Optical waveguides confine and guide light along a path using total internal reflection or more subtle interference effects.
  8. Fibre-optic communications
    Fibre-optic communication sends data as light pulses through glass fibres thinner than a hair, across oceans and into homes.
  9. Nonlinear optical processes
    At high intensities, a material's response to light is no longer proportional to the field, so new frequencies appear and beams change their own propagation.
  10. Frequency conversion
    Frequency conversion uses nonlinear crystals to make new colours of laser light: doubling, tripling, summing and splitting photons.
  11. Ultrafast lasers
    Ultrafast lasers produce pulses lasting picoseconds to femtoseconds, the shortest controlled events humans can make.
  12. Femtosecond optics
    Femtosecond optics deals with how ultrashort pulses are measured, shaped and used.
  13. Quantum optics
    Quantum optics treats light as made of photons with quantum states, revealing effects classical waves cannot explain: antibunching, squeezing, entanglement and two-photon interference.
  14. Single-photon sources
    Single-photon sources emit exactly one photon on demand, ideally indistinguishable from every other.
  15. Photonic crystals
    Photonic crystals are materials with periodic refractive index on the scale of the wavelength of light.
  16. Plasmonics
    Plasmonics uses collective oscillations of electrons at metal surfaces, surface plasmons, to squeeze light into regions far smaller than its wavelength.
  17. Optical sensors
    Optical sensors use light to measure temperature, strain, chemistry, rotation, pressure and more.
  18. Lidar
    Lidar measures distance by timing laser pulses or measuring frequency shifts of returning light, building precise 3D maps.
  19. Optical communications
    Optical communications covers the whole system that moves information with light: transmitters, channels, receivers and the coding that squeezes in more bits.
  20. Optical metrology
    Optical metrology uses light to measure length, shape, surface finish, vibration and time with extraordinary precision.
  21. Silicon photonics
    Silicon photonics builds optical circuits in silicon using the same foundries that make computer chips.
