Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What does a wave transfer?

Energy and momentum (and information), not the medium itself. From “Wave properties” →

In plain English: A wave is a travelling disturbance that carries energy and momentum from place to place without carrying the medium along with it.

What is group velocity?

dω/dk: the speed of the envelope of a wave packet, which carries energy. From “Phase and group velocity” →

In plain English: The phase velocity is how fast individual wave crests move; the group velocity is how fast the envelope of a wave packet, and hence energy and information, moves.

When is a reflected pulse on a string inverted?

When it reflects from a fixed end or a denser (higher impedance) medium. From “Reflection and transmission” →

In plain English: When a wave meets a boundary between two media, part is reflected and part transmitted.

Why must a short pulse contain many frequencies?

Because Δt Δω ≳ 1: localising in time requires spreading in frequency. From “Wave packets” →

In plain English: A wave packet is a localised wave made by superposing many waves with a range of wavelengths.

Why do large-amplitude waves steepen?

Higher-amplitude parts travel faster and catch up with lower parts. From “Nonlinear waves” →

In plain English: In nonlinear media, a wave's speed depends on its amplitude, so waves steepen, break, generate harmonics and interact with each other.

Common mistakes in Waves and Wave Motion

  • Particles of the medium oscillate about fixed points; only energy and the pattern travel.
  • Don't confuse a graph of displacement against position with a picture of the wave; for sound, the "peaks" are where molecules are displaced most, not where pressure is highest (pressure peaks are 90° out of phase).
  • Frequency doesn't change at a boundary; wavelength and speed do. The colour we see depends on frequency.
  • kx − ωt moves right; kx + ωt moves left. Sign errors reverse the direction of travel.
  • Inverse-square spreading assumes a point source and no reflections; indoors or near large sources it doesn't hold.
  • Phase velocity can exceed c without breaking relativity; no information travels with the crests. Signal speed is bounded by c.

Lessons in this unit

  1. Wave properties
    A wave is a travelling disturbance that carries energy and momentum from place to place without carrying the medium along with it.
  2. Transverse and longitudinal waves
    In a transverse wave the oscillation is perpendicular to the direction of travel; in a longitudinal wave it's along it, forming compressions and rarefactions.
  3. Wavelength, frequency, period and speed
    For any periodic wave, the speed equals frequency times wavelength.
  4. Wave equations
    The one-dimensional wave equation says the acceleration of each bit of medium is proportional to the curvature of the wave there.
  5. Wave propagation
    As waves travel they spread out, lose energy to the medium, and may change speed and shape.
  6. Phase and group velocity
    The phase velocity is how fast individual wave crests move; the group velocity is how fast the envelope of a wave packet, and hence energy and information, moves.
  7. Superposition
    When waves overlap in a linear medium, the total displacement at each point is the sum of the individual displacements.
  8. Interference
    Interference is the pattern of reinforcement and cancellation when coherent waves overlap.
  9. Constructive and destructive interference
    Waves interfere constructively where they arrive in phase (path difference a whole number of wavelengths) and destructively where they arrive in antiphase (an odd number of half wavelengths).
  10. Standing waves
    A standing wave forms when two identical waves travel in opposite directions, usually a wave and its reflection.
  11. Reflection and transmission
    When a wave meets a boundary between two media, part is reflected and part transmitted.
  12. Refraction
    Refraction is the change in direction of a wave when it enters a medium where its speed is different, at an angle.
  13. Diffraction
    Diffraction is the spreading of waves when they pass through gaps or around obstacles.
  14. Dispersion
    Dispersion is the dependence of wave speed on frequency.
  15. Polarisation
    Polarisation describes the direction of oscillation of a transverse wave.
  16. Wave packets
    A wave packet is a localised wave made by superposing many waves with a range of wavelengths.
  17. Beats
    Beats are the slow periodic swelling and fading of loudness when two waves of slightly different frequencies combine.
  18. Boundary conditions
    Boundary conditions specify what a wave must do at the edges of its medium: fixed, free, or matched to another medium.
  19. Resonant modes
    A bounded system can only sustain standing waves whose shapes fit its boundary conditions: its resonant modes.
  20. Coupled waves
    When two wave systems are linked, energy can pass between them.
  21. Nonlinear waves
    In nonlinear media, a wave's speed depends on its amplitude, so waves steepen, break, generate harmonics and interact with each other.
  22. Solitons
    A soliton is a solitary wave that keeps its shape while travelling at constant speed, because nonlinear steepening exactly balances dispersive spreading.
  23. Wave energy and intensity
    Waves carry energy at a rate set by their amplitude, frequency and medium.
  24. Huygens' principle
    Huygens' principle says every point on a wavefront acts as a source of secondary wavelets; the new wavefront is the envelope of these wavelets.
  25. Fourier decomposition of waves
    Any wave can be written as a sum (or integral) of sinusoidal waves of different frequencies, amplitudes and phases.

Waves and Wave Motion: questions students ask

What is a longitudinal wave?

A wave in which the oscillations are parallel to the direction of energy transfer.

What is the wavenumber?

k = 2π/λ, radians per metre.

What does d'Alembert's solution show?

Any shape travelling unchanged in either direction at speed v solves the wave equation.

What is the SOFAR channel?

A layer in the ocean where sound speed is lowest, trapping and guiding low-frequency sound over huge distances.
