Lessons in this unit

  1. Position, displacement, distance and time
    Position locates an object relative to an origin; displacement is the change in position (a vector); distance is the total path length travelled (a scalar).
  2. Speed and velocity
    Speed is the rate of change of distance; velocity is the rate of change of displacement, with direction.
  3. Acceleration
    Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity.
  4. Motion in one dimension
    Straight-line motion is described by signed position, velocity and acceleration along one axis.
  5. Motion in two and three dimensions
    In two and three dimensions position, velocity and acceleration are vectors.
  6. Kinematic equations
    For constant acceleration, five quantities (s, u, v, a, t) are linked by the suvat equations.
  7. Projectile motion
    A projectile moves under gravity alone: constant velocity horizontally and constant acceleration g vertically.
  8. Relative motion
    Velocities are measured relative to a frame.
  9. Reference frames
    A reference frame is a viewpoint with its own clocks and rulers from which motion is measured.
  10. Newton's laws of motion
    Newton's three laws connect force and motion.
  11. Inertial and non-inertial frames
    Newton's laws hold as written only in inertial frames.
  12. Free-body diagrams
    A free-body diagram shows one object, isolated, with every external force acting on it drawn as an arrow from its point of action.
  13. Normal force and tension
    The normal force is the perpendicular push a surface exerts to stop objects passing through it; tension is the pull transmitted along a rope or string.
  14. Friction and drag
    Friction opposes relative sliding between surfaces; drag opposes motion through a fluid.
  15. Inclined planes
    On a slope, resolve forces parallel and perpendicular to the surface.
  16. Circular motion
    An object moving in a circle constantly changes direction, so it accelerates towards the centre even at constant speed.
  17. Centripetal acceleration and force
    Centripetal means "centre-seeking": the acceleration v2/r towards the centre of a circular path, and the net force mv2/r that causes it.
  18. Work and energy
    Work is energy transferred by a force acting through a displacement.
  19. Kinetic and potential energy
    Kinetic energy is energy of motion; potential energy is energy stored by position or configuration in a field or a deformed body.
  20. Conservative and non-conservative forces
    A conservative force does work that depends only on start and end points, so a potential energy can be defined for it.
  21. Power and efficiency
    Power is the rate of transferring energy or doing work.
  22. Momentum and impulse
    Momentum p = mv measures the quantity of motion.
  23. Conservation of momentum
    If no net external force acts on a system, its total momentum is constant.
  24. Collisions and explosions
    In collisions and explosions, large internal forces act for a short time, so momentum is conserved.
  25. Elastic and inelastic collisions
    The coefficient of restitution e measures how bouncy a collision is: e = 1 for elastic, 0 for perfectly inelastic.
  26. Centre of mass
    The centre of mass is the mass-weighted average position of a system.
  27. Systems of particles
    Treating many particles as a system, internal forces cancel in pairs, so the system's total momentum responds only to external forces.
  28. Variable-mass systems
    When a system gains or loses mass, as rockets, conveyor belts and raindrops do, F = ma isn't enough.
  29. Motion under resistive forces
    Real objects move against drag and friction, which depend on speed.
  30. Terminal velocity
    A falling object accelerates until drag equals its weight; then the net force is zero and it falls at constant terminal velocity.
  31. Equilibrium and statics
    A body is in static equilibrium when it neither translates nor rotates: the net force and net torque on it are both zero.
  32. Mechanical advantage
    Mechanical advantage (MA) is the ratio of the output force a machine delivers to the input force you apply.
  33. Simple machines
    The six classical simple machines (lever, wheel and axle, pulley, inclined plane, wedge, screw) all trade force against distance.
