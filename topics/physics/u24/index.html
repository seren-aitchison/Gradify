Lessons in this unit

  1. Fundamental particles
    Fundamental particles have no known internal structure.
  2. Quarks and leptons
    Quarks feel the strong force and are always confined inside hadrons; leptons don't feel it and can exist freely.
  3. Fermions and bosons
    Particles are either fermions (half-integer spin, obeying Pauli exclusion) or bosons (integer spin, able to share states).
  4. Fundamental interactions
    All known physics arises from four interactions: gravity, electromagnetism, the weak force and the strong force.
  5. Electromagnetic interaction
    The electromagnetic interaction acts between electrically charged particles through the exchange of photons.
  6. Weak interaction
    The weak interaction changes one type of quark or lepton into another, causing beta decay and powering the Sun's first fusion step.
  7. Strong interaction
    The strong interaction binds quarks into protons, neutrons and other hadrons, and indirectly binds nuclei.
  8. Gravitational interaction
    Gravity is the weakest fundamental interaction but dominates on large scales because it's always attractive and acts on all energy.
  9. Standard Model
    The Standard Model is the quantum field theory of all known particles and three of the four forces.
  10. Particle generations
    Quarks and leptons come in three generations, each a heavier copy of the first with the same charges and interactions.
  11. Quark flavours and colour charge
    Quarks come in six flavours (types) and three colours.
  12. Hadrons, baryons and mesons
    Hadrons are composite particles made of quarks held by gluons.
  13. Antimatter and antiparticles
    Every particle has an antiparticle with the same mass and opposite charges.
  14. Particle decay
    Unstable particles decay into lighter ones, at rates set by the interaction involved and the energy available.
  15. Conservation laws in particle reactions
    Particle reactions obey strict conservation laws: some absolute (energy, momentum, charge, baryon and lepton number), others respected only by some interactions (strangeness, parity).
  16. Feynman diagrams
    Feynman diagrams are pictures of particle interactions that stand for precise mathematical terms in quantum field theory calculations.
  17. Quantum electrodynamics
    Quantum electrodynamics (QED) is the quantum field theory of electrons, positrons and photons.
  18. Quantum chromodynamics
    Quantum chromodynamics (QCD) is the theory of the strong interaction between quarks and gluons.
  19. Electroweak theory
    Electroweak theory unifies electromagnetism and the weak force into a single interaction, broken at low energies by the Higgs field.
  20. Higgs mechanism
    The Higgs mechanism explains how the W and Z bosons, quarks and charged leptons acquire mass without breaking the underlying gauge symmetry of the theory.
  21. Higgs boson
    The Higgs boson is the particle excitation of the Higgs field.
  22. Neutrino oscillations
    Neutrinos change flavour as they travel: an electron neutrino can later be detected as a muon or tau neutrino.
  23. CP violation
    CP symmetry combines swapping particles with antiparticles (C) and mirror reflection (P).
  24. Symmetry breaking
    Symmetry breaking occurs when a system's lowest-energy state lacks a symmetry that its laws possess.
  25. Particle accelerators
    Particle accelerators use electric fields to speed up charged particles and magnetic fields to steer and focus them.
  26. Collider experiments
    Colliders smash particle beams head-on so all their energy is available to create new particles.
  27. Particle detectors
    Particle detectors reconstruct what happened in a collision by measuring the tracks, energies and identities of the particles produced.
  28. Scattering experiments
    Scattering experiments fire particles at targets and measure how they deflect, lose energy or create new particles.
  29. Beyond the Standard Model
    Physics beyond the Standard Model seeks to explain what it leaves out: dark matter, neutrino masses, the matter–antimatter asymmetry, gravity, and the puzzling values of its parameters.
  30. Supersymmetry
    Supersymmetry (SUSY) proposes that every boson has a fermion partner and vice versa.
  31. Grand unified theories
    Grand unified theories (GUTs) propose that the strong, weak and electromagnetic forces merge into one at extremely high energies (~1016 GeV).
  32. Dark matter candidates
    Dark matter makes up about 85% of the universe's matter but emits no light.
