Quick quiz: test yourself on Mathematics for Physics

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Rearrange T = 2π√(l/g) for g. (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    g = 4π2l/T2. Square both sides: T2 = 4π2l/g, so g = 4π2l/T2.

  2. Solve 3 − 2x > 7. (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    x < −2. −2x > 4; dividing by −2 reverses: x < −2.

  3. Solve x + y = 10, x − y = 4. (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    x = 7, y = 3. Add: 2x = 14.

  4. Simplify (x2y-1)3 × y4. (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    x6y. (x2y-1)3 = x6y-3; times y4 gives x6y.

  5. For which k does x2 + kx + 9 = 0 have exactly one root? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    k = ±6. Discriminant k2 − 36 = 0.

  6. Currents satisfy I1 = I2 + I3, 11 = 4I1 + 2I2 and 2I2 = 6I3. What is I1? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    2.0 A. From the third, I2 = 3I3, so I1 = 4I3. Then 11 = 16I3 + 6I3, so I3 = 0.5 A and I1 = 2.0 A.

  7. On a log-log plot, data give a straight line of gradient −2. What is the relationship? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    y ∝ x-2. log y = log A + n log x with n = −2: an inverse-square law.

  8. What is the impedance of a capacitor in complex form? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    1/(iωC) = −i/(ωC). The capacitor current leads the voltage by 90°, giving a factor −i.

  9. What is the mean value of sin2(ωt) over a full cycle? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    1/2. sin2 = (1 − cos 2ωt)/2, and the cosine averages to zero.

Common mistakes in Mathematics for Physics

  • √(a2 + b2) is not a + b, and 1/(a + b) is not 1/a + 1/b.
  • Don't divide both sides by an expression that could be zero; you can lose solutions (x2 = x gives x = 0 as well as x = 1).
  • Check that equations are independent: three Kirchhoff loop equations in a two-loop circuit include one that's redundant.
  • Calculators default to degrees on many devices; calculus results like d(sin x)/dx = cos x only hold in radians.
  • ln is log base e; log on many calculators is base 10. ln x = 2.303 log10 x.
  • When you use complex notation for real signals, take the real part only at the end, and never multiply two complex representations to get a physical product (like power) without care.

Lessons in this unit

  1. Algebraic manipulation
    Rearranging equations is the most-used skill in physics.
  2. Equations and inequalities
    An equation states that two expressions are equal; an inequality states that one is bigger.
  3. Simultaneous equations
    When several unknowns are linked by several equations, solve them together.
  4. Trigonometry and circular functions
    Sine, cosine and tangent relate angles to ratios of sides, and as functions of a continuous angle they describe every oscillation and wave.
  5. Exponential and logarithmic functions
    Exponentials describe anything whose rate of change is proportional to its size: radioactive decay, capacitor discharge, population growth, absorption.
  6. Complex numbers
    A complex number z = x + iy, with i2 = −1, is a point in a plane.
  7. Vectors and vector decomposition
    Vectors are added, scaled and multiplied using components.
  8. Matrices and linear algebra
    A matrix is a rectangular array that represents a linear map: rotation, scaling, a system of equations, a set of coupled oscillators, a quantum operator.
  9. Coordinate systems
    A coordinate system assigns numbers to points in space.
  10. Cartesian, polar, cylindrical and spherical coordinates
    Cartesian coordinates suit boxes and straight-line motion; polar, cylindrical and spherical coordinates suit circles, wires and spheres.
  11. Differentiation
    The derivative is the instantaneous rate of change: the gradient of a graph at a point.
  12. Integration
    Integration adds up infinitely many small pieces and reverses differentiation.
  13. Partial derivatives
    When a quantity depends on several variables, a partial derivative measures how it changes with one while the others are held fixed.
  14. Ordinary differential equations
    An ordinary differential equation (ODE) relates a function of one variable to its derivatives.
  15. Partial differential equations
    A partial differential equation (PDE) involves partial derivatives in several variables, such as space and time.
  16. First- and second-order differential equations
    Most of physics reduces to first- and second-order linear ODEs.
  17. Boundary and initial conditions
    Differential equations have families of solutions; conditions on the boundaries of the region, or at the starting time, pick out the one that describes the actual system.
  18. Taylor and Fourier series
    A Taylor series approximates a smooth function near a point by a polynomial; a Fourier series represents a periodic function as a sum of sines and cosines.
  19. Fourier transforms
    The Fourier transform breaks a (not necessarily periodic) signal into a continuous spectrum of frequencies.
  20. Laplace transforms
    The Laplace transform turns linear ODEs with initial conditions into algebra.
  21. Multivariable calculus
    Multivariable calculus extends derivatives and integrals to functions of several variables: fields in 3D space, thermodynamic states, probability distributions.
  22. Gradient, divergence and curl
    The operator ∇ (del) builds three derivatives of fields: the gradient of a scalar field, and the divergence and curl of a vector field.
  23. Line, surface and volume integrals
    Integrals along curves, over surfaces and through volumes add up field quantities: work along a path, flux through a surface, total charge in a region.
  24. Vector calculus theorems
    The divergence theorem and Stokes' theorem connect what happens inside a region to what happens on its boundary.
  25. Linear algebra eigenvalues and eigenvectors
    An eigenvector of a matrix keeps its direction when the matrix acts on it, only scaling by the eigenvalue.
  26. Tensor algebra
    A tensor is a quantity whose components transform in a definite way when the coordinates change.
  27. Probability distributions
    A probability distribution describes how likely each outcome is.
  28. Statistics and error propagation
    Statistics turns repeated measurements into a best estimate and an honest uncertainty, and error propagation carries those uncertainties through calculations into the final result.
  29. Numerical methods
    Most real problems have no closed-form solution.
  30. Optimisation
    Optimisation finds the maximum or minimum of a function, often under constraints.
  31. Dimensional analysis
    In mathematical physics, dimensional analysis is a tool for checking work and finding scaling laws.
  32. Complex analysis
    Complex analysis studies functions of a complex variable.
  33. Group theory and mathematical symmetry
    A group is the mathematics of symmetry: a set of transformations that can be combined and undone.
  34. Variational calculus
    Variational calculus finds the function, rather than the number, that makes a quantity stationary.

Mathematics for Physics: questions students ask

Why solve symbolically before substituting numbers?

It allows dimension checks, limiting-case checks and reuse, and avoids rounding errors.

What does a negative discriminant mean?

The quadratic has no real roots (two complex conjugate roots).

What does it mean geometrically when two linear equations have no solution?

Their lines are parallel.

Why are oscillations sinusoidal?

They are projections of uniform circular motion, and they solve ẍ = −ω2x.
