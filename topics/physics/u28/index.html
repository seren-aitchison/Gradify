Lessons in this unit

  1. Energy bands in solids
    When atoms join into a solid, their discrete energy levels broaden into bands separated by gaps.
  2. Intrinsic and extrinsic semiconductors
    An intrinsic semiconductor is pure: its few carriers come from electrons thermally excited across the gap.
  3. Doping
    Doping adds tiny amounts of impurity atoms to a semiconductor to control its conductivity and carrier type.
  4. Electrons and holes
    In semiconductors current is carried by electrons in the conduction band and by holes in the valence band.
  5. Carrier concentration
    The number of free electrons and holes in a semiconductor depends on temperature, band gap and doping.
  6. Fermi level
    The Fermi level is the energy at which the probability of finding an electron is one half; it's the electrochemical potential of electrons.
  7. Drift and diffusion
    Charge carriers move in two ways: drift, pushed by an electric field, and diffusion, spreading from high to low concentration.
  8. Mobility and conductivity
    Mobility measures how quickly carriers move in an electric field; with carrier density, it sets conductivity.
  9. p-n junctions
    A p–n junction forms where p-type and n-type semiconductor meet.
  10. Diodes
    A diode lets current flow easily in one direction and blocks it in the other.
  11. Transistors
    A transistor uses a small signal to control a larger current, acting as an amplifier or a switch.
  12. Bipolar junction transistors
    A bipolar junction transistor (BJT) has three regions, emitter, base and collector, forming two p–n junctions.
  13. Field-effect transistors
    A field-effect transistor controls current through a channel using the electric field from a gate voltage.
  14. MOSFETs
    The metal–oxide–semiconductor FET is the most manufactured device in history.
  15. Semiconductor heterojunctions
    A heterojunction joins two different semiconductors with different band gaps.
  16. Photodiodes
    Photodiodes convert light into current: photons absorbed in or near the depletion region create electron–hole pairs, which the built-in field separates.
  17. Solar cells
    Solar cells are large-area p–n junctions that turn sunlight directly into electricity.
  18. LEDs
    Light-emitting diodes produce light when electrons and holes recombine across a direct band gap in a forward-biased junction.
  19. Semiconductor lasers
    Semiconductor (diode) lasers create coherent light from a forward-biased p–n junction with an optical cavity.
  20. Integrated circuits
    An integrated circuit puts billions of transistors, resistors and interconnects on one chip of silicon.
  21. Quantum wells, wires and dots
    Shrinking semiconductors to nanometre sizes in one, two or three dimensions confines electrons, quantising their energies.
  22. Tunnel diodes
    A tunnel diode is a very heavily doped p–n junction so thin that electrons tunnel directly through it.
  23. Spintronics
    Spintronics uses the electron's spin as well as its charge to store and process information.
  24. Semiconductor fabrication principles
    Chips are made by repeatedly depositing, patterning and modifying thin layers on silicon wafers in ultra-clean factories.
  25. Nanoscale electronic devices
    As electronic devices shrink to nanometre sizes, quantum effects, single electrons and individual atoms matter.
