Lessons in this unit

  1. Measurement instruments and calibration
    An instrument converts a physical quantity into a reading.
  2. Accuracy, precision and resolution
    Accuracy is how close a result is to the true value; precision is how closely repeated results agree; resolution is the smallest change an instrument can register.
  3. Random and systematic errors
    Random errors scatter readings unpredictably either side of the true value; systematic errors shift all readings the same way.
  4. Measurement uncertainty
    Uncertainty is the range within which the true value is expected to lie, at a stated confidence.
  5. Absolute and relative uncertainty
    Absolute uncertainty has the units of the quantity (± 0.2 cm); relative (fractional or percentage) uncertainty is the absolute uncertainty divided by the value.
  6. Significant figures in experimental results
    In experimental work significant figures follow the uncertainty, not the calculator.
  7. Propagation of uncertainty
    When a result is calculated from measured quantities, their uncertainties propagate into the result.
  8. Error bars and confidence intervals
    Error bars show the uncertainty of each data point on a graph; confidence intervals state the range within which a parameter lies with a given probability.
  9. Repeatability and reproducibility
    Repeatability is agreement between results under the same conditions (same person, apparatus, lab, short time); reproducibility is agreement under changed conditions (different people, methods, labs).
  10. Experimental controls
    A control removes alternative explanations for a result.
  11. Independent, dependent and controlled variables
    In an experiment you change the independent variable, measure the dependent variable, and keep the controlled variables constant.
  12. Experimental design
    Experimental design plans what to measure, how, and how many times so that the result answers the question with the smallest uncertainty for the effort.
  13. Calibration curves
    A calibration curve maps an instrument's raw output to the true quantity using measurements of known standards.
  14. Linearisation of experimental relationships
    Straight lines are easy to test and fit.
  15. Least-squares fitting
    Least squares finds the line (or curve) that minimises the sum of squared vertical distances between data and model.
  16. Regression analysis
    Regression models how a dependent variable depends on one or more predictors, estimates the model's parameters, and quantifies how well it fits.
  17. Residual analysis
    Residuals are the differences between data and the fitted model.
  18. Hypothesis testing
    Hypothesis testing asks whether data are consistent with a null hypothesis (no effect, or the standard model).
  19. Statistical significance
    A result is statistically significant when chance alone is an unlikely explanation.
  20. Instrument response and sensitivity
    An instrument's response describes how its output follows its input: how much (sensitivity), how linearly, how fast (time response) and over which frequencies (bandwidth).
  21. Detection limits
    The detection limit is the smallest signal an instrument or method can reliably distinguish from noise and background.
  22. Signal-to-noise ratio
    The signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) compares the strength of a wanted signal with the random fluctuations hiding it.
  23. Data acquisition systems
    A data acquisition (DAQ) system converts physical signals into digital numbers a computer can store: sensor, signal conditioning, sampling and analogue-to-digital conversion, then software for logging and analysis.
  24. Sensors and transducers
    A transducer converts energy from one form into another; a sensor is a transducer that produces a measurable signal (usually electrical) from a physical quantity.
  25. Oscilloscopes and signal generators
    An oscilloscope displays voltage against time, so you can see waveforms, measure amplitudes, periods and phase, and catch glitches.
  26. Lock-in amplifiers
    A lock-in amplifier extracts a tiny signal buried in noise by modulating it at a known frequency and detecting only what varies at exactly that frequency and phase.
  27. Vacuum systems
    Many experiments need low pressure: to let particles travel without collisions, to keep surfaces clean, or to insulate thermally.
  28. Cryogenic measurement
    Cryogenics studies matter at very low temperatures, where superconductivity, superfluidity and quantum effects appear and thermal noise falls.
  29. High-voltage and high-current safety
    Electricity becomes dangerous through current passing through the body, burns, arcs, fire and stored energy.
  30. Laboratory notebooks and reproducibility
    A lab notebook is the permanent, dated record of what you did, saw and thought.
  31. Experimental validation of physical models
    A model is validated when its predictions agree with independent measurements within uncertainties across its claimed range.
