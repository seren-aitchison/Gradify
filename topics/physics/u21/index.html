Lessons in this unit

  1. Atomic structure
    An atom is a tiny, dense, positively charged nucleus surrounded by a cloud of electrons.
  2. Rutherford scattering
    Geiger and Marsden fired alpha particles at gold foil; a few bounced back at large angles.
  3. Bohr model
    Bohr's 1913 model put hydrogen's electron in circular orbits with quantised angular momentum.
  4. Quantum mechanical atomic models
    In the quantum model, electrons occupy orbitals: wavefunctions giving probability clouds around the nucleus.
  5. Atomic orbitals
    Atomic orbitals are the shapes of electron probability clouds, set by the quantum numbers l and m.
  6. Electron configurations
    An electron configuration lists how an atom's electrons fill its orbitals.
  7. Atomic energy levels
    Each atom has a characteristic set of energy levels determined by its electron configuration and the interactions between electrons.
  8. Hydrogen and hydrogen-like atoms
    Hydrogen and ions with a single electron (He+, Li2+…) can be solved exactly.
  9. Multi-electron atoms
    In atoms with more than one electron, electron–electron repulsion makes the Schrödinger equation unsolvable exactly.
  10. Electron spin
    The electron's intrinsic spin gives it a magnetic moment and two possible orientations in a field.
  11. Fine structure
    Fine structure is the small splitting of atomic energy levels caused by relativistic effects and the coupling between an electron's spin and its orbital motion.
  12. Hyperfine structure
    Hyperfine structure is a tiny splitting of atomic levels from the interaction between the electron and the nucleus's magnetic moment (and electric quadrupole).
  13. Spin-orbit coupling
    Spin–orbit coupling is the interaction between an electron's spin magnetic moment and the magnetic field it feels from its orbital motion.
  14. Zeeman effect
    In a magnetic field, atomic energy levels split according to their magnetic quantum number, so spectral lines split into several components.
  15. Stark effect
    The Stark effect is the shifting and splitting of atomic and molecular energy levels in an electric field.
  16. Atomic spectra
    Every element emits and absorbs light at its own set of wavelengths.
  17. Selection rules
    Selection rules state which transitions between atomic states can occur by emitting or absorbing a photon.
  18. Spontaneous and stimulated emission
    An excited atom can emit a photon spontaneously at a random time, or be stimulated to emit by an incoming photon, producing an identical copy.
  19. Atomic transitions
    An atomic transition is a jump between energy levels, absorbing or emitting energy.
  20. Lasers and atomic transitions
    Lasers depend on atomic (or molecular, or semiconductor) transitions arranged so that population inversion can be maintained.
  21. Atomic clocks
    Atomic clocks keep time by locking an oscillator to an atomic transition frequency.
  22. Laser cooling
    Laser cooling slows atoms using the momentum of photons.
  23. Magneto-optical trapping
    A magneto-optical trap (MOT) combines laser cooling with a magnetic field gradient so the light both cools atoms and pushes them to the centre.
  24. Bose–Einstein condensation in atomic gases
    When a dilute gas of bosonic atoms is cooled to nanokelvin temperatures, a large fraction collapses into the lowest quantum state, forming a Bose–Einstein condensate: a macroscopic matter wave.
  25. Precision spectroscopy
    Precision spectroscopy measures atomic and molecular transition frequencies to extraordinary accuracy, testing fundamental physics: QED, the constancy of nature's constants, and the symmetry between matter and antimatter.
