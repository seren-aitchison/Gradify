Lessons in this unit

  1. Energy conversion and conservation
    Energy can't be created or destroyed, only converted between forms, but every conversion wastes some as low-grade heat.
  2. Solar photovoltaic physics
    Solar cells convert sunlight directly into electricity using the photovoltaic effect in semiconductors.
  3. Solar thermal systems
    Solar thermal systems capture sunlight as heat, for hot water, space heating, industrial processes or electricity generation.
  4. Wind energy
    Wind turbines convert the kinetic energy of moving air into electricity.
  5. Hydroelectric power
    Hydroelectric power converts the gravitational potential energy of water into electricity using turbines.
  6. Tidal and wave energy
    Tidal and wave energy harness the ocean's motion: tides driven by the Moon and Sun, and waves driven by wind.
  7. Geothermal energy
    Geothermal energy uses heat from Earth's interior, originally from formation and ongoing radioactive decay, for electricity and direct heating.
  8. Nuclear fission energy
    Nuclear fission power splits heavy nuclei like uranium-235, releasing about 200 MeV per fission, millions of times more energy per kilogram than burning fuel.
  9. Nuclear fusion energy
    Fusion power aims to reproduce the Sun's energy source on Earth by fusing light nuclei, mainly deuterium and tritium, at temperatures above 100 million kelvin.
  10. Hydrogen energy systems
    Hydrogen is an energy carrier, not a source: it must be produced using energy, then stored, transported and converted back.
  11. Fuel cells
    Fuel cells convert chemical energy directly into electricity by electrochemical reactions, without combustion.
  12. Batteries and electrochemical energy storage
    Batteries store energy chemically and release it as electricity through redox reactions.
  13. Supercapacitors
    Supercapacitors store energy electrostatically at electrode–electrolyte interfaces, delivering power quickly and surviving hundreds of thousands of cycles.
  14. Thermoelectric energy conversion
    Thermoelectric devices convert heat directly into electricity (Seebeck effect) or use electricity to pump heat (Peltier effect), with no moving parts.
  15. Heat pumps
    Heat pumps move heat from a cold place to a warm one using work, delivering three to four units of heat per unit of electricity.
  16. Energy efficiency
    Energy efficiency means getting the same service, such as warmth, light, motion or computation, with less energy.
  17. Grid storage
    Grid storage stores electricity when supply exceeds demand and releases it later, balancing variable wind and solar.
  18. Energy transmission and losses
    Electricity travels from power plants to users through transmission and distribution networks.
  19. Energy systems modelling
    Energy systems models simulate how energy is supplied, converted, stored and used across a region over time.
  20. Life-cycle energy analysis
    Life-cycle analysis counts energy use and emissions over a product's whole life, from extracting raw materials to manufacturing, operation and disposal.
  21. Energy security and sustainability
    Energy security means reliable, affordable access to energy; sustainability means meeting today's needs without harming future generations or the climate.
