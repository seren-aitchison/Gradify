Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What is the superposition principle?

For linear systems, any sum of solutions is also a solution. From “Linear and nonlinear systems” →

In plain English: A linear system's response is proportional to its input, and solutions can be added together. Key idea: Nonlinear systems cannot be understood by adding up simple pieces; interactions create new behaviour.

Which bifurcation creates a limit cycle from a fixed point?

A Hopf bifurcation. From “Bifurcation theory” →

In plain English: A bifurcation is a qualitative change in a system's behaviour as a parameter crosses a critical value: equilibria appear, disappear or swap stability, or oscillations begin. Key idea: Bifurcations are where systems change character; their type predicts smooth change or sudden jumps.

What does a zero Lyapunov exponent indicate in a flow?

The direction along the trajectory, which neither stretches nor shrinks on average. From “Lyapunov exponents” →

In plain English: Lyapunov exponents measure the average exponential rate at which nearby trajectories separate or converge. Key idea: λ1 > 0 means chaos; the full spectrum describes stretching, shrinking and the attractor's dimension.

How is the Mandelbrot set defined?

The set of c for which iterating z → z2 + c from z = 0 stays bounded. From “Fractals” →

In plain English: Fractals are shapes with detail at every scale, often self-similar, and with non-integer dimensions. Key idea: Fractal dimension measures how detail fills space as you zoom in; non-integer values are the signature of fractals.

What equation describes amplitudes of patterns near onset universally?

The (complex) Ginzburg–Landau equation. From “Pattern formation” →

In plain English: Pattern formation is how uniform states break into spatial structures such as stripes, spots, spirals and hexagons. Key idea: Patterns arise when instabilities pick a length scale and nonlinearity chooses a shape.

Key takeaways

  • Nonlinear systems cannot be understood by adding up simple pieces; interactions create new behaviour.
  • Nonlinear ODEs rarely have formulas, but uniqueness, geometry and numerics reveal their behaviour.
  • Eigenvalues of the Jacobian classify equilibria: real parts decide stability, imaginary parts decide spiralling.
  • Phase portraits show the full qualitative behaviour: equilibria, cycles and which initial states go where.
  • Bifurcations are where systems change character; their type predicts smooth change or sudden jumps.
  • Period-doubling cascades converge geometrically with the universal ratio δ ≈ 4.669.

Common mistakes in Nonlinear Dynamics and Chaos

  • A good small-amplitude linear model can give badly wrong predictions at large amplitude.
  • Nonlinear equations can blow up in finite time, which never happens in linear ones with constant coefficients.
  • A linear centre may become a spiral when nonlinear terms are included; check further.
  • 2D autonomous flows cannot be chaotic; you need three dimensions or a driven (time-dependent) system.
  • Subcritical bifurcations cause abrupt, hysteretic transitions that small-signal models fail to warn about.
  • Universality applies to maps with a quadratic maximum; different shapes give different constants.

Lessons in this unit

  1. Linear and nonlinear systems
    A linear system's response is proportional to its input, and solutions can be added together.
  2. Nonlinear differential equations
    Nonlinear differential equations describe how systems change when rates depend nonlinearly on the state.
  3. Fixed points and stability
    Fixed points are states where a system stays put.
  4. Phase-space analysis
    Phase-space analysis pictures every possible state of a system as a point and its evolution as a trajectory.
  5. Bifurcation theory
    A bifurcation is a qualitative change in a system's behaviour as a parameter crosses a critical value: equilibria appear, disappear or swap stability, or oscillations begin.
  6. Period-doubling
    Period-doubling is a route to chaos in which an oscillation repeatedly doubles its period as a parameter increases: period 2, 4, 8 and so on, faster and faster, until chaos appears.
  7. Deterministic chaos
    Deterministic chaos is complex, unpredictable-looking behaviour arising from simple rules with no randomness.
  8. Sensitivity to initial conditions
    Sensitivity to initial conditions, the butterfly effect, means that two almost identical starting states end up completely different.
  9. Lyapunov exponents
    Lyapunov exponents measure the average exponential rate at which nearby trajectories separate or converge.
  10. Strange attractors
    A strange attractor is the set a chaotic dissipative system settles onto: bounded, with a fractal structure and chaotic motion on it.
  11. Logistic map
    The logistic map xn+1 = rxn(1 − xn) is a one-line model of population growth that shows the whole route from steady behaviour to chaos.
  12. Lorenz system
    The Lorenz system is three simple equations derived from atmospheric convection that became the icon of chaos.
  13. Fractals
    Fractals are shapes with detail at every scale, often self-similar, and with non-integer dimensions.
  14. Self-organisation
    Self-organisation is the spontaneous emergence of order from local interactions, without a designer or external template.
  15. Synchronisation
    Synchronisation is the tendency of coupled oscillators to fall into step.
  16. Coupled nonlinear oscillators
    Coupled nonlinear oscillators exchange energy and phase in rich ways: normal modes, beats, localisation, resonance and chaos.
  17. Pattern formation
    Pattern formation is how uniform states break into spatial structures such as stripes, spots, spirals and hexagons.
  18. Reaction-diffusion systems
    Reaction-diffusion systems combine local chemical reactions with diffusion, producing waves, spirals and stationary Turing patterns.
  19. Solitons and nonlinear waves
    Solitons are solitary waves that keep their shape while travelling and survive collisions intact, because nonlinearity exactly balances dispersion.
  20. Chaos in mechanical and electrical systems
    Chaos is not just theory: it appears in pendulums, rotating machines, electronic circuits, lasers and orbits.
  21. Complex systems and emergent behaviour
    Complex systems consist of many interacting parts whose collective behaviour is not obvious from the parts alone.

Nonlinear Dynamics and Chaos: questions students ask

What does the Van der Pol oscillator model?

Self-sustained oscillations with nonlinear damping, such as in valve circuits and heartbeats.

What makes a fixed point a saddle?

Real eigenvalues of opposite sign (determinant negative).

What does Liouville's theorem say?

Phase-space volume is conserved in Hamiltonian systems.

What is critical slowing down?

Near a bifurcation, recovery from perturbations becomes very slow.
