What explains the structure of the periodic table?

Quantum numbers, orbital energies and the Pauli exclusion principle. From “Physics and chemistry” →

In plain English: Physics and chemistry share foundations: quantum mechanics explains chemical bonds, thermodynamics governs reactions and spectroscopy reveals molecular structure. Key idea: Quantum mechanics explains bonding, thermodynamics governs reactions, and spectroscopy probes structure: physics and chemistry are deeply intertwined.

What is Wolff's law?

Bone remodels to adapt its structure to the loads placed on it. From “Biomechanics” →

In plain English: Biomechanics applies mechanics to living bodies: how bones bear loads, muscles generate force, joints move and organisms walk, swim and fly. Key idea: Biomechanics treats bodies as mechanical systems of levers, springs and pendulums, explaining forces, movement and adaptation.

What physics discovery enabled high-capacity hard drives?

Giant magnetoresistance. From “Physics and computer science” →

In plain English: Physics and computer science are deeply linked: physics provides the hardware (transistors, lasers, magnetic storage), computation powers simulation and data analysis, and ideas flow both ways in info Key idea: Physics builds computers; computers advance physics; information theory, quantum computing and machine learning unite them.

Who first modelled stock prices as a random walk?

Louis Bachelier, in 1900. From “Physics and economics” →

In plain English: Econophysics applies tools from statistical physics to economic and financial systems: random walks, power laws, network models and agent-based simulations. Key idea: Statistical physics tools reveal fat tails, power laws and network effects in economics, complementing traditional theory.

Why is the ice–albedo feedback positive?

Melting ice reveals darker surfaces that absorb more sunlight, causing further warming. From “Climate and energy systems” →

In plain English: Climate and energy are tightly linked: burning fossil fuels emits greenhouse gases, while low-carbon energy systems need careful design to keep power reliable. Key idea: Climate change follows from energy physics, and physics-based energy systems with renewables, storage and electrification offer solutions.

Key takeaways

  • Quantum mechanics explains bonding, thermodynamics governs reactions, and spectroscopy probes structure: physics and chemistry are deeply intertwined.
  • Physical chemistry quantifies reactions through thermodynamics, kinetics, quantum mechanics and statistical mechanics.
  • Chemical physics resolves reactions in time, space and quantum state, using lasers, beams and quantum theory.
  • Physics explains biological processes through fluctuations, diffusion, mechanics and energetics; biology inspires new physics of active, living matter.
  • Biomechanics treats bodies as mechanical systems of levers, springs and pendulums, explaining forces, movement and adaptation.
  • Physics provides medicine's imaging, therapy and devices; medical physicists ensure safety and accuracy.

Common mistakes in Interdisciplinary Physics

  • Chemical intuition (octet rule, hybridisation) is a useful model, not fundamental physics; it breaks down for many molecules.
  • Thermodynamics tells you if a reaction can occur, not how fast; kinetics determines the rate.
  • The Born–Oppenheimer approximation fails near conical intersections, where electronic and nuclear motions couple strongly.
  • Intuition from everyday inertia fails for microorganisms; at low Reynolds number, motion stops instantly when propulsion stops.
  • Muscle forces are often far larger than the loads they lift, because of short lever arms.
  • Different imaging modalities see different properties; no single modality is best for everything.

Lessons in this unit

  1. Physics and chemistry
    Physics and chemistry share foundations: quantum mechanics explains chemical bonds, thermodynamics governs reactions and spectroscopy reveals molecular structure.
  2. Physical chemistry
    Physical chemistry applies physics to chemical systems: thermodynamics, kinetics, quantum chemistry and spectroscopy.
  3. Chemical physics
    Chemical physics studies chemical processes with the tools and concepts of physics: molecular dynamics, quantum scattering, laser spectroscopy and statistical mechanics.
  4. Physics and biology
    Physics brings quantitative tools and principles to biology: from the mechanics of cells and the thermodynamics of metabolism to imaging and the information processing of neurons.
  5. Biomechanics
    Biomechanics applies mechanics to living bodies: how bones bear loads, muscles generate force, joints move and organisms walk, swim and fly.
  6. Physics and medicine
    Physics transforms medicine through imaging, radiation therapy, diagnostics and devices.
  7. Physics and neuroscience
    The brain is an electrical, chemical and computational system that physics helps measure and model.
  8. Physics and materials science
    Materials science and physics together explain and design materials: metals, semiconductors, polymers, ceramics and composites.
  9. Physics and computer science
    Physics and computer science are deeply linked: physics provides the hardware (transistors, lasers, magnetic storage), computation powers simulation and data analysis, and ideas flow both ways in info
  10. Physics and mathematics
    Mathematics is the language of physics, and physics has inspired some of the deepest mathematics.
  11. Physics and engineering
    Engineering applies physics to design and build: bridges, engines, circuits, aircraft, power grids and communication systems.
  12. Physics and environmental science
    Environmental science relies on physics to understand the atmosphere, oceans, pollution, energy flows and climate.
  13. Physics and Earth science
    Geophysics applies physics to the Earth: its interior, gravity, magnetism, earthquakes, tectonics and resources.
  14. Physics and economics
    Econophysics applies tools from statistical physics to economic and financial systems: random walks, power laws, network models and agent-based simulations.
  15. Complex systems
    Complex systems contain many interacting parts whose collective behaviour can't be predicted easily from the parts alone.
  16. Network science
    Network science studies systems as nodes connected by links: social networks, the internet, power grids, food webs, neurons and proteins.
  17. Statistical modelling of social systems
    Statistical physics models help understand social phenomena: opinion formation, segregation, crowd motion, voting and cultural spread.
  18. Climate and energy systems
    Climate and energy are tightly linked: burning fossil fuels emits greenhouse gases, while low-carbon energy systems need careful design to keep power reliable.
  19. Nanotechnology
    Nanotechnology manipulates matter at scales of 1–100 nm, where quantum effects and surfaces dominate.
  20. Quantum technologies
    Quantum technologies exploit superposition, entanglement and quantum measurement for practical uses: computing, secure communication, ultra-precise sensing and timing.
  21. Scientific machine learning
    Scientific machine learning combines data-driven methods with physical knowledge.
  22. Cross-disciplinary research design
    Cross-disciplinary research tackles problems no single field can solve alone, such as climate change, pandemics, energy and brain science.

Interdisciplinary Physics: questions students ask

What does the Nernst equation give?

The electrode potential under non-standard concentrations.

What is a potential energy surface?

Energy of a molecular system as a function of nuclear coordinates.

What is active matter?

Systems whose components consume energy to move or exert forces, like bacteria or cytoskeletal filaments.

What is the advantage of proton therapy?

The Bragg peak deposits dose at a precise depth with little exit dose.
