Lessons in this unit

  1. Microstates and macrostates
    A microstate specifies the exact state of every particle; a macrostate specifies only bulk properties like energy, volume and particle number.
  2. Statistical ensembles
    An ensemble is an imagined collection of many copies of a system, each in a different microstate consistent with the macroscopic constraints.
  3. Microcanonical, canonical and grand canonical ensembles
    The three main ensembles each have their own probability rule and their own link to thermodynamics.
  4. Boltzmann distribution
    In thermal equilibrium at temperature T, the probability of finding a system in a state of energy E is proportional to e−E/kBT.
  5. Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution
    The Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution gives the spread of molecular speeds in a gas at temperature T.
  6. Partition functions
    The partition function Z is a weighted sum over all states.
  7. Entropy and probability
    Entropy measures how probable a macrostate is: the logarithm of its number of microstates.
  8. Multiplicity and state counting
    Multiplicity is the number of microstates in a macrostate.
  9. Equipartition theorem
    In classical thermal equilibrium, every quadratic term in a system's energy has an average value of ½kBT.
  10. Classical ideal gases
    An ideal gas is a collection of non-interacting point particles.
  11. Mean free path
    The mean free path is the average distance a molecule travels between collisions.
  12. Molecular speed distributions
    Molecular speeds in a gas follow the Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution, but what a measurement sees depends on how it samples them: molecules effusing through a hole, hitting a wall, or colliding are wei
  13. Chemical potential
    The chemical potential is the energy cost of adding one particle to a system.
  14. Fermi–Dirac statistics
    Fermions (electrons, protons, neutrons, 3He) obey the Pauli exclusion principle: no two can share a quantum state.
  15. Bose–Einstein statistics
    Bosons (photons, phonons, 4He atoms, many other atoms) can pile into the same quantum state.
  16. Quantum gases
    When particles are close compared with their thermal de Broglie wavelength, quantum statistics take over and gases behave very differently from classical ones.
  17. Degenerate matter
    Degenerate matter is so dense that the Pauli exclusion principle, not temperature, provides most of its pressure.
  18. Blackbody radiation
    A blackbody absorbs all radiation and emits a spectrum fixed only by its temperature.
  19. Fluctuations and correlations
    Thermodynamic quantities fluctuate about their averages.
  20. Phase transitions
    Statistical mechanics explains phase transitions as collective behaviour: interactions between many particles produce sudden changes in macroscopic order.
  21. Critical phenomena
    Near a continuous phase transition, physical quantities follow power laws in the distance from the critical point, with exponents that are the same for whole classes of different systems.
  22. Ising model
    The Ising model places spins that can point up or down on a lattice, with neighbouring spins preferring to align.
  23. Renormalisation group
    The renormalisation group (RG) studies how a system's description changes as you zoom out, averaging over small-scale details.
  24. Statistical field theory
    Statistical field theory describes systems near critical points by continuous fields instead of individual particles.
  25. Monte Carlo methods
    Monte Carlo methods use random sampling to estimate averages in systems with too many states to enumerate.
  26. Nonequilibrium statistical mechanics
    Nonequilibrium statistical mechanics describes systems driven away from equilibrium: how they relax, transport heat and particles, and fluctuate.
