Why are metals good thermal conductors?

State Newton's law of cooling.

Why is counterflow more effective than parallel flow?

Lessons in this unit

  1. Conduction
    Conduction transfers heat through a material without bulk motion, by collisions between neighbouring particles and, in metals, by free electrons.
  2. Convection
    Convection transfers heat by bulk movement of a fluid.
  3. Thermal radiation
    Every object above absolute zero emits electromagnetic radiation because of the thermal motion of its charges.
  4. Fourier's law of heat conduction
    Fourier's law states that heat flux is proportional to the temperature gradient and flows down it.
  5. Newton's law of cooling
    Newton's law of cooling says an object's rate of heat loss is proportional to the temperature difference between it and its surroundings.
  6. Stefan–Boltzmann law
    The Stefan–Boltzmann law gives the total power radiated by a blackbody: proportional to its surface area and the fourth power of its absolute temperature.
  7. Wien's displacement law
    Wien's law says the wavelength at which a blackbody's emission peaks is inversely proportional to its temperature.
  8. Thermal diffusivity
    Thermal diffusivity measures how quickly temperature changes spread through a material.
  9. Heat exchangers
    Heat exchangers move heat from one fluid to another without mixing them.
  10. Insulation and thermal resistance
    Insulation slows heat flow.
  11. Blackbody and greybody radiation
    A blackbody absorbs and emits perfectly at every wavelength; real surfaces don't.
  12. Emissivity and absorptivity
    Emissivity is how well a surface emits thermal radiation compared with a blackbody; absorptivity is the fraction of incoming radiation it absorbs.
  13. Greenhouse effect
    Greenhouse gases are transparent to most incoming sunlight but absorb and re-emit outgoing infrared.
  14. Thermoelectric effects
    Thermoelectric effects convert directly between heat and electricity in conducting materials.
  15. Peltier and Seebeck effects
    Seebeck and Peltier effects are two faces of thermoelectricity.
  16. Thermal contact resistance
    When two solid surfaces touch, they really meet only at tiny high points, so heat flow across the interface is impeded.
  17. Cryogenics
    Cryogenics is the production and use of very low temperatures, typically below about 120 K.
  18. Low-temperature physics
    Cooling matter towards absolute zero reveals quantum behaviour on a macroscopic scale: superconductivity, superfluidity, quantum Hall effects and Bose–Einstein condensates.
  19. Heat transport in solids and fluids
    Heat moves through matter by different carriers: phonons and electrons in solids, molecules in gases, and both conduction and convection in liquids.
  20. Thermal management in engineering
    Thermal management keeps devices within safe temperatures: removing heat from chips, batteries, engines and spacecraft, or keeping things warm.
