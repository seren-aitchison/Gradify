State Newton's law of gravitation.

F = Gm1m2/r2, attractive, along the line between centres. From “Newton's law of universal gravitation” →

In plain English: Every pair of masses attracts with a force proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of their separation.

What is the escape velocity from Earth's surface?

About 11.2 km s-1. From “Escape velocity” →

In plain English: Escape velocity is the minimum launch speed for an unpowered object to escape a body's gravity completely.

What happens to a LEO satellite's speed as drag lowers its orbit?

It increases. From “Satellite motion” →

In plain English: Satellites are placed in orbits chosen for their purpose: low orbits for imaging and the ISS, sun-synchronous polar orbits for Earth observation, medium orbits for navigation, and geostationary orbits

Why is JWST at L2?

It keeps Sun, Earth and Moon on one side for shielding and cooling, with constant geometry and continuous communications. From “Lagrange points” →

In plain English: In a system of two orbiting masses there are five points where a small third body can orbit in step with them, the gravitational and centrifugal forces balancing in the rotating frame.

What is J2?

The coefficient describing a planet's oblateness, the main perturbation on low satellite orbits. From “Gravitational perturbations” →

In plain English: Real orbits are perturbed by other bodies, by the non-spherical shapes of planets, by drag and by radiation pressure.

Common mistakes in Gravitation and Orbital Mechanics

  • r is measured between centres, not between surfaces.
  • The real Earth isn't uniform: its dense core makes g increase slightly with depth at first, peaking at the core–mantle boundary.
  • Weightlessness in orbit doesn't mean zero g; it means everything around you falls with the same acceleration.
  • Potential is negative and becomes less negative (increases) with height. "Higher potential" means closer to zero.
  • Rockets don't need to reach escape velocity at launch; with continuous thrust they can climb slowly. vesc applies to an unpowered launch.
  • Higher orbits are slower, though they have more total energy. To catch up with a spacecraft ahead of you in orbit, you first slow down to drop lower.

Lessons in this unit

  1. Newton's law of universal gravitation
    Every pair of masses attracts with a force proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of their separation.
  2. Gravitational fields
    A gravitational field is the region around a mass where other masses feel a force.
  3. Gravitational field strength
    Gravitational field strength g is the force per unit mass at a point.
  4. Gravitational potential and potential energy
    Gravitational potential is the work done per unit mass to bring a mass from infinity to a point.
  5. Escape velocity
    Escape velocity is the minimum launch speed for an unpowered object to escape a body's gravity completely.
  6. Orbital velocity
    A satellite in a circular orbit falls continuously around the planet: gravity supplies exactly the centripetal force needed at its speed.
  7. Circular and elliptical orbits
    Bound orbits under an inverse-square force are ellipses with the central body at one focus; a circle is the special case of zero eccentricity.
  8. Kepler's laws
    Kepler found three laws in Tycho Brahe's planetary data; Newton showed they follow from an inverse-square gravitational force.
  9. Satellite motion
    Satellites are placed in orbits chosen for their purpose: low orbits for imaging and the ISS, sun-synchronous polar orbits for Earth observation, medium orbits for navigation, and geostationary orbits
  10. Orbital energy and angular momentum
    An orbit's total energy fixes its size (semi-major axis); its angular momentum fixes its shape.
  11. Two-body gravitational problem
    Two bodies orbiting under mutual gravity both circle their common centre of mass.
  12. Tidal forces
    Tidal forces arise because gravity varies across an extended body: the near side is pulled more strongly than the far side.
  13. Roche limits
    The Roche limit is the distance within which a body held together only by its own gravity is torn apart by a planet's tides.
  14. Lagrange points
    In a system of two orbiting masses there are five points where a small third body can orbit in step with them, the gravitational and centrifugal forces balancing in the rotating frame.
  15. Restricted three-body problem
    The general three-body problem has no closed-form solution and can be chaotic.
  16. Orbital transfers
    Changing orbit means changing velocity with rocket burns (Δv).
  17. Hohmann transfers
    A Hohmann transfer moves a spacecraft between two circular orbits using two burns and half an elliptical orbit that touches both.
  18. Gravitational perturbations
    Real orbits are perturbed by other bodies, by the non-spherical shapes of planets, by drag and by radiation pressure.
  19. Precession of orbits
    Under a pure inverse-square force around a point mass, an orbit's ellipse stays fixed.
  20. General relativistic corrections
    General relativity treats gravity as curved spacetime.
  21. Gravitational lensing
    Mass bends light, so a galaxy or cluster in front of a distant source acts as a lens, distorting, magnifying and multiplying its images.
  22. Gravitational waves
    Gravitational waves are ripples in spacetime that travel at the speed of light, produced by accelerating masses.

Gravitation and Orbital Mechanics: questions students ask

What does the shell theorem say?

A spherically symmetric mass attracts outside bodies as if all its mass were at its centre (and exerts no net force inside a hollow shell).

How does g vary inside a uniform solid sphere?

Proportionally to distance from the centre.

Why are astronauts on the ISS weightless?

They and the station are in free fall together; g there is still about 90% of surface g.

Why is gravitational potential negative?

Zero is defined at infinity, and gravity is attractive, so work must be done to move away.
