Lessons in this unit

  1. Qubits and quantum states
    A qubit is the quantum version of a bit: a two-level system that can be in 0, 1 or any superposition of both.
  2. Bloch sphere
    The Bloch sphere represents any pure single-qubit state as a point on a unit sphere.
  3. Quantum gates
    Quantum gates are unitary operations that transform qubit states reversibly.
  4. Quantum circuits
    Quantum circuits are the standard language for describing quantum computations: qubits are wires, gates act on them in time order from left to right, and measurements read out results.
  5. Quantum measurement
    Measurement is how quantum information becomes classical.
  6. Entanglement
    Entanglement is a correlation between quantum systems so strong that the state of the whole cannot be described by states of the parts.
  7. Bell inequalities
    Bell inequalities test whether nature can be explained by local hidden variables, pre-existing values with no faster-than-light influences.
  8. Quantum teleportation
    Quantum teleportation transfers an unknown quantum state from one place to another using a shared entangled pair and two bits of classical communication.
  9. Quantum cryptography
    Quantum cryptography uses the laws of physics, rather than the difficulty of maths problems, to secure communication.
  10. Quantum key distribution
    Quantum key distribution (QKD) lets two parties share a secret random key over an insecure channel, with any eavesdropping revealed by errors.
  11. Quantum algorithms
    Quantum algorithms use superposition, interference and entanglement to solve certain problems faster than any known classical method.
  12. Deutsch–Jozsa algorithm
    The Deutsch–Jozsa algorithm decides with a single query whether a function is constant or balanced, where a deterministic classical algorithm may need exponentially many queries.
  13. Grover's algorithm
    Grover's algorithm searches an unstructured list of N items for a marked one using about √N queries instead of N.
  14. Shor's algorithm
    Shor's algorithm factors large integers in polynomial time on a quantum computer, exponentially faster than the best known classical algorithms.
  15. Quantum error correction
    Quantum error correction protects fragile quantum information by spreading one logical qubit across many physical qubits.
  16. Quantum noise and decoherence
    Decoherence is the loss of quantum behaviour as a qubit becomes entangled with its environment.
  17. Fault-tolerant quantum computing
    Fault-tolerant quantum computing performs arbitrarily long computations reliably using error-corrected logical qubits, even though every physical component is imperfect.
  18. Quantum complexity
    Quantum complexity theory classifies problems by the resources a quantum computer needs to solve them.
  19. Quantum simulation
    Quantum simulation uses controllable quantum systems to model other quantum systems, such as molecules, materials and field theories, that are exponentially hard for classical computers.
  20. Quantum communication
    Quantum communication sends quantum states between distant parties, enabling secure key distribution, teleportation and distributed quantum computing.
  21. Quantum networks
    Quantum networks connect quantum processors and sensors by distributing entanglement between nodes.
  22. Physical implementations of qubits
    Many physical systems can act as qubits, each with distinct strengths in coherence, speed, connectivity and scalability.
  23. Superconducting qubits
    Superconducting qubits are tiny electrical circuits cooled near absolute zero that behave like artificial atoms.
  24. Trapped-ion systems
    Trapped-ion quantum computers store qubits in the internal states of individual ions held by electromagnetic fields in vacuum.
  25. Neutral-atom quantum computing
    Neutral-atom quantum computers hold individual atoms in arrays of tightly focused laser beams called optical tweezers.
  26. Photonic quantum computing
    Photonic quantum computing encodes qubits in single photons or squeezed light, which travel at the speed of light, barely decohere and work at room temperature.
