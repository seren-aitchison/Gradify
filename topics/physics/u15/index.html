Key terms and formulas at a glance

F = kq1q2/r2, k = 1/(4πε0) = 8.99 × 109 N m2 C-2
ε0 = 8.854 × 10-12 F m-1
E = F/q (N C-1 = V m-1)
E = Q/(4πε0r2)
point charge
E = σ/(2ε0)
infinite sheet; E = σ/ε0 between parallel plates
E = λ/(2πε0r)
infinite line charge
V = U/q (volts, J C-1)
V = Q/(4πε0r)
point charge, zero at infinity

What is the charge on an electron?

−1.602 × 10-19 C. From “Electric charge” →

In plain English: Electric charge is a basic property of matter that makes particles exert electric and magnetic forces on each other.

What is the field between charged parallel plates?

Uniform, E = V/d = σ/ε0. From “Electric fields” →

In plain English: An electric field is the force per unit charge that a test charge would feel at a point.

Why do dipole fields fall faster than point-charge fields?

The opposite charges' fields largely cancel at large distances (E ∝ 1/r3). From “Electric dipoles” →

In plain English: An electric dipole is a pair of equal and opposite charges separated by a small distance.

Define capacitance.

Charge stored per unit potential difference, C = Q/V. From “Capacitance” →

In plain English: Capacitance is the ability of a conductor arrangement to store charge per volt.

Which component of E is continuous across any boundary?

The tangential component. From “Boundary conditions for electric fields” →

In plain English: At the boundary between two materials, electric fields obey simple matching rules derived from Maxwell's equations.

Common mistakes in Electricity and Electrostatics

  • Rubbing doesn't create charge; it transfers electrons from one material to the other.
  • Quarks have fractional charge but are never seen free; observed charges remain integer multiples of e.
  • Dielectrics don't conduct, but they still respond to fields; that response is what makes capacitors and microwave heating work.
  • Coulomb's law is for point charges (or spherically symmetric ones); extended shapes need integration.
  • Field direction is the direction of force on a positive charge; electrons accelerate against the field.
  • Field lines aren't paths of charges in general; a charge's velocity isn't always along the line.

Lessons in this unit

  1. Electric charge
    Electric charge is a basic property of matter that makes particles exert electric and magnetic forces on each other.
  2. Conservation and quantisation of charge
    Electric charge is conserved: the total in an isolated system never changes.
  3. Conductors, insulators and dielectrics
    In conductors, charges move freely; in insulators, they're bound.
  4. Coulomb's law
    Coulomb's law gives the force between two point charges: proportional to the product of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance.
  5. Electric fields Interactive lesson
    An electric field is the force per unit charge that a test charge would feel at a point.
  6. Electric field lines Interactive lesson
    Field lines map an electric field: their direction gives the field's direction, and their density gives its strength.
  7. Electric potential
    Electric potential is the potential energy per unit charge at a point, measured relative to a reference (often infinity or earth).
  8. Electric potential energy
    Electric potential energy is the energy stored in a configuration of charges because of their positions.
  9. Voltage and potential difference
    Voltage, or potential difference, is the energy transferred per unit charge between two points.
  10. Electric dipoles
    An electric dipole is a pair of equal and opposite charges separated by a small distance.
  11. Electric flux
    Electric flux measures how much electric field passes through a surface: field strength times area, counting only the component perpendicular to the surface.
  12. Gauss's law
    Gauss's law states that the electric flux out of any closed surface equals the enclosed charge divided by ε0.
  13. Charge distributions
    Real charges are spread over lines, surfaces and volumes.
  14. Conductors in electrostatic equilibrium
    In electrostatic equilibrium, charges in a conductor have stopped moving.
  15. Capacitance
    Capacitance is the ability of a conductor arrangement to store charge per volt.
  16. Parallel-plate and spherical capacitors
    Simple geometries give simple formulas for capacitance.
  17. Capacitors in series and parallel
    Capacitors combine opposite to resistors: in parallel their capacitances add; in series their reciprocals add.
  18. Energy stored in electric fields
    Charging a capacitor stores energy, and that energy lives in the electric field between the plates.
  19. Dielectric polarisation
    In an electric field, a dielectric's bound charges shift, creating tiny dipoles throughout the material.
  20. Boundary conditions for electric fields
    At the boundary between two materials, electric fields obey simple matching rules derived from Maxwell's equations.
  21. Electrostatic shielding
    A closed conducting shell blocks external static electric fields from its interior: a Faraday cage.
  22. Method of images
    The method of images solves electrostatics problems near conductors by replacing the conductor with imaginary charges that reproduce the same boundary conditions.
  23. Poisson's equation
    Poisson's equation relates the electric potential to the charge density producing it.
  24. Laplace's equation
    In regions without charge, the electric potential obeys Laplace's equation.

Electricity and Electrostatics: questions students ask

How does charging by induction work?

A nearby charged object pushes charges within a conductor; earthing removes one kind, leaving the conductor charged.

What did Millikan's experiment show?

Charge comes in whole-number multiples of the elementary charge e.

What is relative permittivity?

The factor by which a material reduces the electric field (and increases capacitance) compared with vacuum.

Why can't field lines cross?

The field has a unique direction at each point.
