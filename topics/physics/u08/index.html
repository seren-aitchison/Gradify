What is gauge pressure?

Pressure measured relative to atmospheric pressure. From “Density and pressure” →

In plain English: Density is mass per unit volume; pressure is force per unit area acting perpendicular to a surface.

What is a streamline?

A line everywhere tangent to the flow velocity. From “Fluid dynamics” →

In plain English: Fluid dynamics studies fluids in motion: rivers, blood, weather, aircraft, stars.

What distinguishes laminar from turbulent flow?

Laminar flow moves in smooth layers; turbulent flow has chaotic eddies and strong mixing. From “Laminar and turbulent flow” →

In plain English: In laminar flow, fluid moves in smooth parallel layers; in turbulent flow, it churns in irregular eddies at many scales.

Why are small droplets spherical?

Surface tension minimises surface area, and a sphere has the least area for its volume. From “Surface tension” →

In plain English: Molecules at a liquid's surface are pulled inward by their neighbours, so the surface behaves like a stretched membrane.

What is vorticity?

The curl of the velocity field, measuring local rotation of fluid elements. From “Vorticity and circulation” →

In plain English: Vorticity measures local spinning of a fluid; circulation measures the total rotation around a loop.

Common mistakes in Fluid Mechanics

  • Pressure isn't a vector; force on a surface is p times area, directed along the surface normal.
  • The pressure on a dam depends on the water depth, not on how far the reservoir stretches behind it.
  • Hydraulics don't give free energy: the large piston moves a much smaller distance.
  • Buoyant force depends on the displaced volume, not on the object's weight or depth (for incompressible fluids).
  • The force on a vertical wall isn't ρghA with the full depth; use the centroid depth.
  • "Steady" flow doesn't mean no acceleration; fluid particles can accelerate as they move to places of different velocity.

Lessons in this unit

  1. Density and pressure
    Density is mass per unit volume; pressure is force per unit area acting perpendicular to a surface.
  2. Hydrostatic equilibrium
    In a fluid at rest, pressure increases with depth because each layer supports the weight of the fluid above it.
  3. Pascal's principle
    Pascal's principle states that a pressure change applied to an enclosed incompressible fluid is transmitted undiminished to every part of the fluid and its container walls.
  4. Buoyancy and Archimedes' principle
    A body immersed in a fluid feels an upward buoyant force equal to the weight of fluid it displaces.
  5. Fluid statics
    Fluid statics covers fluids at rest: pressure distributions, forces on submerged surfaces, manometers and the stability of floating bodies.
  6. Fluid dynamics
    Fluid dynamics studies fluids in motion: rivers, blood, weather, aircraft, stars.
  7. Continuity equation
    The continuity equation expresses conservation of mass in a flow.
  8. Conservation of mass in fluid flow
    Mass can't be created or destroyed in a flow; it can only move.
  9. Bernoulli's equation
    Along a streamline in steady, incompressible, frictionless flow, pressure energy, kinetic energy and potential energy per unit volume add to a constant.
  10. Viscosity
    Viscosity is a fluid's internal friction: its resistance to layers sliding past each other.
  11. Laminar and turbulent flow
    In laminar flow, fluid moves in smooth parallel layers; in turbulent flow, it churns in irregular eddies at many scales.
  12. Reynolds number
    The Reynolds number is the ratio of inertial to viscous forces in a flow.
  13. Poiseuille flow
    Poiseuille flow is steady laminar flow through a pipe.
  14. Drag and lift
    A body moving through a fluid feels a force with two components: drag, opposing the relative motion, and lift, perpendicular to it.
  15. Boundary layers
    At a solid surface a real fluid sticks (no-slip), so a thin boundary layer forms in which the velocity rises from zero to the free-stream value.
  16. Surface tension
    Molecules at a liquid's surface are pulled inward by their neighbours, so the surface behaves like a stretched membrane.
  17. Capillary action
    Capillary action is the rise or fall of a liquid in a narrow tube because of the balance between adhesion to the walls, cohesion within the liquid and surface tension.
  18. Compressible and incompressible fluids
    Liquids barely change volume under pressure; gases compress easily.
  19. Navier–Stokes equations
    The Navier–Stokes equations are Newton's second law for a viscous fluid.
  20. Euler equations for fluids
    The Euler equations describe an inviscid fluid: Navier–Stokes without viscosity.
  21. Vorticity and circulation
    Vorticity measures local spinning of a fluid; circulation measures the total rotation around a loop.
  22. Flow instabilities
    A steady flow is unstable if small disturbances grow instead of dying out.
  23. Shock waves
    When a disturbance moves through a fluid faster than sound, pressure waves can't get ahead of it and pile up into a shock: an extremely thin jump in pressure, density, temperature and velocity.
  24. Turbulence modelling
    Turbulence spans a huge range of scales, so simulating every eddy is usually impossible.
  25. Computational fluid dynamics
    Computational fluid dynamics (CFD) solves the equations of fluid flow numerically.

Fluid Mechanics: questions students ask

Why does pressure in a liquid not depend on the container's shape?

Pressure at a depth is set by ρgh plus surface pressure; walls supply whatever extra force balance is needed.

Why does air in brake lines reduce braking?

Air compresses, so pedal movement compresses gas instead of transmitting pressure.

What fraction of a floating object is submerged?

The ratio of its density to the fluid's density.

What does a U-tube manometer measure?

A pressure difference, Δp = ρgΔh.
