Define capital expenditure.

Spending on acquiring or improving non-current assets whose benefits last more than one accounting period. From “Capital and revenue expenditure” →

How is the profit or loss on disposal calculated?

Proceeds minus the carrying amount (cost less accumulated depreciation) at the date of disposal. From “Disposal of non-current assets” →

What are the four pairs of columns in an extended trial balance?

Ledger balances, adjustments, statement of profit or loss, statement of financial position. From “The extended trial balance” →

How is profit found from statements of affairs?

Closing capital − opening capital + drawings − capital introduced. From “Incomplete records” →

Name the items in a partnership appropriation account.

Profit for the year, interest on drawings (added), salaries and interest on capital (deducted), and residual profit shared in the profit-sharing ratio. From “The appropriation account” →

Lessons in this school

  1. Capital and revenue expenditure
    Spending that brings benefits over several years is capital expenditure and goes to the statement of financial position; spending used up in the year is revenue expenditure and goes to profit.
  2. Accruals and prepayments
    The accruals concept matches income and expenses to the period they belong to, not to when cash moves.
  3. Depreciation
    Depreciation spreads the cost of a non-current asset over the years it is used.
  4. Disposal of non-current assets
    When a non-current asset is sold or scrapped, its cost and accumulated depreciation are removed and any profit or loss on disposal is calculated.
  5. Irrecoverable debts and allowances for receivables
    Some customers never pay.
  6. Inventory valuation
    Closing inventory directly changes cost of sales and profit.
  7. The extended trial balance
    The extended trial balance is a worksheet that takes the trial balance, adds year-end adjustments and sorts every figure into the statement of profit or loss or the statement of financial position.
  8. The statement of profit or loss
    The statement of profit or loss shows a business's income and expenses for a period and the profit that results.
  9. The statement of financial position
    The statement of financial position shows what a business owns and owes, and the owner's capital, at one date.
  10. Incomplete records
    Many small businesses don't keep full double-entry records.
  11. Profit and cash
    A profitable business can run out of cash, and a loss-making one can have plenty.
  12. Partnership agreements and the Partnership Act 1890
    A partnership's accounts follow its partnership agreement, and where there is none, the default rules of the Partnership Act 1890.
  13. The appropriation account
    A partnership's profit is shared out in an appropriation account: interest on drawings is added, salaries and interest on capital are deducted, and the residue is split in the profit-sharing ratio.
  14. Partners' capital and current accounts
    Partners usually keep fixed capital accounts for the money they have invested and current accounts for their shares of profit and their drawings.
  15. Goodwill and changes in partners
    When a partner joins or leaves, or the profit-sharing ratio changes, the existing partners must be credited with the value they have built up.

Key terms

Capital expenditure
Spending on acquiring or improving non-current assets that benefit more than one period
Accrual
An expense incurred but not yet paid at the year end, shown as a current liability
Depreciable amount
The cost of an asset less its residual value
Disposal account
A temporary ledger account that brings together cost, accumulated depreciation and proceeds of an asset disposed of
Irrecoverable debt
A receivable that will not be collected and is written off as an expense
Net realisable value
Estimated selling price less estimated costs of completion and of making the sale
Extended trial balance
A worksheet that adjusts the trial balance and sorts each figure into the statement of profit or loss or of financial position
Gross profit
Revenue less cost of sales: the profit from trading before overheads
Net current assets
Current assets less current liabilities; also called working capital
Statement of affairs
A list of assets and liabilities at a date, used to calculate capital when full records are not kept
Overtrading
Expanding sales faster than the working capital available to finance them, leading to cash shortages
Partnership agreement
A contract, written or oral, setting out the partners' rights and duties, including how profits are shared
Appropriation account
The account showing how a partnership's profit for the year is divided among the partners
Fixed capital account
A partner's capital account that records only agreed long-term capital and doesn't change with profits or drawings
