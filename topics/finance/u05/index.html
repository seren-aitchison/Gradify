Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

Who are the main users of management accounting information?

Internal managers. From “The role of management accounting” →

In plain English: Management accounting provides information to help managers plan, control and make decisions. Key idea: Management accounting gives internal managers forward-looking, detailed and timely information for planning, control and decisions, unconstrained by external reporting rules.

Name a more statistically reliable alternative to the high-low method.

Least-squares regression analysis. From “The high-low method” →

In plain English: Semi-variable costs must be split into fixed and variable elements for planning. Key idea: The high-low method splits a mixed cost using the highest and lowest activity points: variable rate from the change in cost over the change in activity, fixed cost from the remainder.

What is over-absorption?

When the overhead absorbed is more than the actual overhead incurred. From “Absorption costing” →

In plain English: Absorption costing charges production overheads to cost units using a predetermined rate. Key idea: Absorption costing charges production overheads using a predetermined rate based on budgeted figures; differences between absorbed and actual overheads are under- or over-absorption adjustments to profit.

Give an example of a business that uses job costing.

A builder, a printer or a garage carrying out repairs. From “Job and batch costing” →

In plain English: Job and batch costing are used where work is done to customer order or in distinct batches. Key idea: Job costing collects costs for each unique order on a job card; batch costing does the same for a batch and divides by units; prices add a mark-up on cost or a margin on price.

How is a target cost calculated?

Target selling price minus the required profit. From “Target costing” →

In plain English: Target costing starts with the price customers will pay and works back to the cost a product must achieve. Key idea: Target costing sets cost from the market price minus required profit; the cost gap is closed by value engineering and design changes before production begins.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Management Accounting: Costing

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which feature distinguishes management accounting from financial accounting? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    It has no legally prescribed format. Management accounts are internal and unregulated, so their format suits the business.

  2. Which of these is usually a direct cost of a manufactured table? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    The timber used to make it. Timber can be traced to each table; the others are overheads.

  3. Which cost is variable in relation to the number of units produced? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Raw materials used in each unit. Raw material cost rises in proportion to the number of units made.

  4. A manager compares this month's actual costs with the budget and investigates overspends. Which role is this? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Control. Comparing actual with plan and acting on differences is control.

  5. Direct materials are £30, direct labour £20, production overhead £15 and selling overhead £10 per unit. What is the production cost? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    £65. Production cost = prime cost £50 + production overhead £15 = £65; selling overhead is excluded.

  6. Fixed costs are £12,000 and variable costs £3 per unit. What is the total cost of 4,000 units? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    £24,000. Total cost = 12,000 + (3 × 4,000) = £24,000.

  7. A report costing £20,000 a year to produce saves managers about £5,000 a year. Which quality of good information does it fail? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Cost-effectiveness, because its cost exceeds its benefit. Information should only be produced when its benefits exceed its costs.

  8. A company includes advertising costs in its closing inventory valuation. What is the effect? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Inventory and profit are overstated, contrary to IAS 2. Advertising is a period cost; capitalising it defers expense and inflates current profit.

  9. A firm adds a second supervisor whenever its workforce passes 25 people. What type of cost is supervision? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    A stepped fixed cost that rises in jumps. Supervision is fixed within each band of 25 workers, then steps up.

Key takeaways

  • Management accounting gives internal managers forward-looking, detailed and timely information for planning, control and decisions, unconstrained by external reporting rules.
  • Costs are classified as direct or indirect, and by function; prime cost plus production overhead gives production cost, which values inventory, while other costs are period costs.
  • Variable costs are constant per unit and fixed costs constant in total, so unit cost falls as output rises; these patterns hold only within the relevant range.
  • The high-low method splits a mixed cost using the highest and lowest activity points: variable rate from the change in cost over the change in activity, fixed cost from the remainder.
  • Material issues are valued by FIFO or weighted average; the EOQ balances ordering and holding costs; control levels avoid stock-outs and excess inventory.
  • Basic pay of direct workers is direct labour; overtime premium and idle time are usually overheads; efficiency and turnover ratios help control labour costs.

Common mistakes in Management Accounting: Costing

  • Treating management information as useful just because it is precise. Information that arrives too late, or costs more than it saves, fails the test.
  • Including selling or administration costs in inventory. IAS 2 limits inventory cost to the costs of purchase, conversion and bringing inventory to its present location and condition.
  • Treating unit cost as constant at all output levels. Because fixed cost per unit falls with volume, a unit cost calculated at one level misleads at another.
  • Choosing the highest and lowest costs instead of the highest and lowest activity levels. The method is driven by activity.
  • Assuming FIFO and AVCO give the same profit. When prices change, they give different cost of sales and inventory values.
  • Charging overtime premium to the job worked in overtime. Unless the customer asked for the rush, the premium is caused by general workload and belongs in overheads.

Lessons in this school

  1. The role of management accounting
    Management accounting provides information to help managers plan, control and make decisions.
  2. Cost classification
    Costs can be grouped in several ways, depending on the purpose.
  3. Cost behaviour
    Costs respond differently to changes in activity.
  4. The high-low method
    Semi-variable costs must be split into fixed and variable elements for planning.
  5. Accounting for materials
    Materials are often the largest cost in manufacturing.
  6. Accounting for labour
    Labour costs must be measured, controlled and charged to products.
  7. Overhead allocation, apportionment and reapportionment
    Before overheads can be charged to products, they must be shared out among cost centres.
  8. Absorption costing
    Absorption costing charges production overheads to cost units using a predetermined rate.
  9. Marginal costing
    Marginal costing charges only variable costs to products and treats fixed costs as period costs.
  10. Marginal and absorption costing compared
    Marginal and absorption costing can report different profits when inventory levels change.
  11. Activity-based costing
    Activity-based costing (ABC) assigns overheads to products according to the activities they consume.
  12. Job and batch costing
    Job and batch costing are used where work is done to customer order or in distinct batches.
  13. Process costing
    Process costing is used where identical units are produced continuously through a series of processes.
  14. Process losses and equivalent units
    Process accounts must deal with losses that differ from expectations and with work still in progress at the period end.
  15. Service costing
    Service organisations need cost information as much as manufacturers, but their output is intangible.
  16. Target costing
    Target costing starts with the price customers will pay and works back to the cost a product must achieve.
  17. Life-cycle costing
    Life-cycle costing tracks all the costs of a product or asset from development to disposal.
  18. Throughput accounting
    Throughput accounting, based on the theory of constraints, focuses on maximising the rate at which a business earns money through its bottleneck.
  19. Environmental management accounting
    Environmental management accounting (EMA) identifies and manages environmental costs that conventional systems often hide.
