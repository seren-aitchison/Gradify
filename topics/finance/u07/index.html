Lessons in this school

  1. The financial management function
    Financial management makes decisions about raising and using funds to create value.
  2. Financial objectives and stakeholders
    Companies pursue financial objectives on behalf of shareholders, but managers and other stakeholders have their own interests.
  3. The financial environment
    Companies raise and invest funds through financial markets and institutions.
  4. The time value of money
    Money received today is worth more than the same amount later, because it can be invested.
  5. Accounting rate of return and payback
    Accounting rate of return (ARR) and payback are simple appraisal methods still widely used.
  6. Net present value
    Net present value (NPV) is the theoretically preferred method of investment appraisal.
  7. Internal rate of return
    The internal rate of return (IRR) is the discount rate at which a project's NPV is zero.
  8. Inflation and taxation in investment appraisal
    Inflation and tax affect project cash flows and discount rates.
  9. Risk and uncertainty in investment appraisal
    Investment cash flows are uncertain, so appraisal must consider risk.
  10. Capital rationing and asset replacement
    When capital is limited, firms must choose the best combination of projects.
  11. Working capital and its financing
    Working capital is the capital tied up in day-to-day operations.
  12. Managing inventory
    Inventory management balances the costs of holding stock against the risks of running out.
  13. Managing receivables and payables
    Credit given to customers and taken from suppliers has a major effect on cash.
  14. Managing cash
    Cash management keeps enough cash to meet obligations while investing any surplus.
  15. Sources of finance
    Businesses can raise finance from many sources, each with different costs and risks.
  16. Islamic finance
    Islamic finance follows Sharia principles, notably the prohibition of interest.
  17. Dividend policy
    Dividend policy decides how much of its profit a company pays to shareholders.
  18. The cost of equity
    The cost of equity is the return shareholders require.
  19. The cost of debt and WACC
    The weighted average cost of capital (WACC) combines the costs of equity and debt into one discount rate.
  20. Capital structure
    Capital structure is the mix of debt and equity finance.
  21. Business valuation
    Businesses are valued for takeovers, share issues and investment decisions.
  22. Foreign exchange risk
    Companies trading internationally face risks from exchange rate movements.
  23. Interest rate risk
    Changes in interest rates affect borrowing costs and the value of investments.

Key terms

Investment decision
Choosing which assets and projects to invest in
Shareholder wealth maximisation
Maximising the value of shareholders' investment over time
Capital market
A market for long-term finance through shares and bonds
Present value
The value today of a future cash flow, discounted at a required rate
Payback period
The time taken for cash inflows to repay the initial investment
Net present value
The present value of cash inflows minus the initial investment
Internal rate of return
The discount rate at which a project's NPV is zero
Real rate
A rate of return excluding inflation
Sensitivity analysis
Testing how changes in a variable affect NPV
Capital rationing
A limit on the funds available for investment
Working capital
Current assets minus current liabilities
Inventory days
The average number of days inventory is held
Receivables days
The average time customers take to pay
Precautionary motive
Holding cash to meet unexpected needs
