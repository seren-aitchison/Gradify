Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check.

What makes a tax indirect?

It is collected by an intermediary, usually a business, and passed on to the person who bears it in the price. From “The UK tax system” →

What is the difference between tax avoidance and tax evasion?

Evasion is illegal non-payment by concealment or deception; avoidance is legal in form but uses the law in ways Parliament did not intend. From “Tax avoidance and tax evasion” →

How is a company car benefit calculated?

List price, less any capital contribution, multiplied by the appropriate percentage for its CO2 emissions. From “Employment income and benefits” →

Who pays Class 1A NIC?

Employers, on the value of most taxable benefits in kind provided to employees. From “National Insurance contributions” →

What happens when assets pass between spouses?

The transfer is at no gain, no loss: the recipient takes over the original cost. From “Capital gains tax” →

Lessons in this school

  1. The UK tax system
    Tax pays for public services and shapes economic behaviour.
  2. Tax administration and self-assessment
    Most individuals with untaxed income and all companies calculate and report their own tax under self-assessment.
  3. Tax avoidance and tax evasion
    Tax evasion is illegal; tax avoidance uses the law in ways Parliament did not intend.
  4. The income tax computation
    Income tax is charged on an individual's taxable income for a tax year.
  5. Employment income and benefits
    Employees are taxed on their earnings, including most benefits in kind.
  6. Trading income
    The profits of a trade carried on by a sole trader or partner are taxed as trading income.
  7. Capital allowances
    Capital allowances give tax relief for spending on plant and machinery and some structures, in place of depreciation.
  8. National Insurance contributions
    National Insurance contributions are charged on earnings and self-employed profits and build entitlement to some state benefits.
  9. Corporation tax
    UK resident companies pay corporation tax on their worldwide taxable total profits.
  10. Capital gains tax
    Individuals pay capital gains tax when they dispose of chargeable assets at a gain.
  11. Inheritance tax
    Inheritance tax is charged on a person's estate at death and on some lifetime gifts.
  12. Value added tax
    VAT is a tax on consumer spending collected by businesses at each stage of supply.

Key terms

Direct tax
A tax paid by the person on whom it falls, assessed on income, profits or wealth
Self-assessment
The system under which taxpayers calculate, report and pay their own tax
Tax evasion
Illegally failing to pay tax that is due, by concealment or deception
Net income
Total income less reliefs such as qualifying interest and loss relief
Benefit in kind
A non-cash reward from employment, taxable unless an exemption applies
Badges of trade
Indicators used by the courts to decide whether an activity is a trade
Plant and machinery
Assets used to carry on a business, as opposed to the setting in which it is carried on
Primary threshold
The level of earnings above which employees pay Class 1 contributions
Taxable total profits
A company's income and gains from all sources, less qualifying charitable donations
Chargeable disposal
A sale, gift, exchange or loss of an asset that can give rise to a chargeable gain
Potentially exempt transfer
A lifetime gift to an individual that becomes exempt if the donor survives seven years
Output tax
VAT charged by a registered business on its taxable supplies
