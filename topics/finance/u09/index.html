Key terms and formulas at a glance

Direct tax
A tax paid by the person on whom it falls, assessed on income, profits or wealth
Self-assessment
The system under which taxpayers calculate, report and pay their own tax
Tax evasion
Illegally failing to pay tax that is due, by concealment or deception
Net income
Total income less reliefs such as qualifying interest and loss relief
Benefit in kind
A non-cash reward from employment, taxable unless an exemption applies
Badges of trade
Indicators used by the courts to decide whether an activity is a trade
Plant and machinery
Assets used to carry on a business, as opposed to the setting in which it is carried on
Primary threshold
The level of earnings above which employees pay Class 1 contributions
Taxable total profits
A company's income and gains from all sources, less qualifying charitable donations
Chargeable disposal
A sale, gift, exchange or loss of an asset that can give rise to a chargeable gain
Potentially exempt transfer
A lifetime gift to an individual that becomes exempt if the donor survives seven years
Output tax
VAT charged by a registered business on its taxable supplies

Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What makes a tax indirect?

It is collected by an intermediary, usually a business, and passed on to the person who bears it in the price. From “The UK tax system” →

In plain English: Tax pays for public services and shapes economic behaviour. Key idea: UK tax law is mainly statute, interpreted by the courts; HMRC guidance explains how HMRC applies it but does not bind the courts.

What is the difference between tax avoidance and tax evasion?

Evasion is illegal non-payment by concealment or deception; avoidance is legal in form but uses the law in ways Parliament did not intend. From “Tax avoidance and tax evasion” →

In plain English: Tax evasion is illegal; tax avoidance uses the law in ways Parliament did not intend. Key idea: Evasion breaks the law; avoidance complies with its letter but defeats its purpose and is increasingly counteracted; planning uses reliefs as intended.

How is a company car benefit calculated?

List price, less any capital contribution, multiplied by the appropriate percentage for its CO2 emissions. From “Employment income and benefits” →

In plain English: Employees are taxed on their earnings, including most benefits in kind. Key idea: Most benefits are taxable on their cost to the employer, with special rules for cars, fuel, vans, accommodation and loans; expenses must be wholly, exclusively and necessarily incurred.

Who pays Class 1A NIC?

Employers, on the value of most taxable benefits in kind provided to employees. From “National Insurance contributions” →

In plain English: National Insurance contributions are charged on earnings and self-employed profits and build entitlement to some state benefits. Key idea: Employees pay 8% and 2%, employers 15% above a low threshold, and the self-employed 6% and 2% under Class 4, for 2025/26.

What happens when assets pass between spouses?

The transfer is at no gain, no loss: the recipient takes over the original cost. From “Capital gains tax” →

In plain English: Individuals pay capital gains tax when they dispose of chargeable assets at a gain. Key idea: Taxable gains sit on top of taxable income, at 18% in the basic band and 24% above, after a £3,000 annual exempt amount (2025/26).

Quick quiz: test yourself on Taxation

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which of these is an indirect tax? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Value added tax. VAT is charged by businesses and paid over to HMRC, so the person who bears it (the customer) does not pay it directly.

  2. When does the 2025/26 tax year end? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    5 April 2026. The UK tax year for individuals runs from 6 April to 5 April.

  3. A shopkeeper deliberately omits cash takings from his tax return. What is this? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Tax evasion. Deliberately concealing income to avoid tax due is evasion, a criminal offence.

  4. A taxpayer relies on an HMRC manual that conflicts with the wording of the statute. What will a tribunal follow? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    The statute as interpreted by the courts. HMRC guidance is not law. The tribunal applies the legislation, though the guidance may matter to other remedies.

  5. Last year Ravi had £6,000 of income tax not deducted at source. What is each payment on account? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    £3,000. Each payment on account is half of the previous year's liability not deducted at source.

  6. Which principle lets courts disregard pre-ordained steps with no commercial purpose? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    The Ramsay principle. Ramsay and Furniss v Dawson allow the courts to look at the composite transaction as a whole.

  7. A government raises tobacco duty sharply. Which description fits best? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    An indirect tax used partly to discourage harmful activity. Excise duty is collected from producers and passed on, and its high rate aims to reduce smoking as well as raise revenue.

  8. A taxpayer deliberately understates income but does not hide it, and makes no disclosure. What maximum penalty applies? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    70% of the potential lost revenue. Deliberate but not concealed errors attract up to 70%; 100% is for deliberate and concealed, 30% for careless.

  9. A client refuses to disclose an underpayment you have found. What should the accountant do? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Cease to act and consider a report to the MLRO. The accountant cannot be associated with the evasion, must cease to act, tell HMRC they no longer act, and consider an internal money laundering report without tipping off.

Key takeaways

  • UK tax law is mainly statute, interpreted by the courts; HMRC guidance explains how HMRC applies it but does not bind the courts.
  • For individuals, the online return and the balancing payment are both due on 31 January after the tax year, with payments on account on 31 January and 31 July.
  • Evasion breaks the law; avoidance complies with its letter but defeats its purpose and is increasingly counteracted; planning uses reliefs as intended.
  • Tax non-savings income first, then savings, then dividends; the allowances at 0% still use up the band.
  • Most benefits are taxable on their cost to the employer, with special rules for cars, fuel, vans, accommodation and loans; expenses must be wholly, exclusively and necessarily incurred.
  • Taxable trading profit is accounting profit adjusted for disallowed items, non-trading income and capital allowances, taxed on a tax-year basis.

Common mistakes in Taxation

  • Treating HMRC guidance as law. A manual or concession shows HMRC's view and practice; a tribunal decides on the statute itself.
  • Basing payments on account on the current year's tax. They are based on the previous year's liability not deducted at source, and the difference is settled by the balancing payment.
  • Assuming avoidance is safe because it is legal. Schemes can be defeated under Ramsay or the GAAR, with the tax, interest, penalties and accelerated payments to follow.
  • Leaving the savings or dividend allowance out of the band. Income covered by these allowances is taxed at 0% but still counts toward the basic and higher rate limits.
  • Treating commuting as business travel. Travel between home and a permanent workplace is ordinary commuting and is not deductible.
  • Deducting depreciation for tax. Depreciation is always added back; capital allowances take its place.

Lessons in this school

  1. The UK tax system
    Tax pays for public services and shapes economic behaviour.
  2. Tax administration and self-assessment
    Most individuals with untaxed income and all companies calculate and report their own tax under self-assessment.
  3. Tax avoidance and tax evasion
    Tax evasion is illegal; tax avoidance uses the law in ways Parliament did not intend.
  4. The income tax computation
    Income tax is charged on an individual's taxable income for a tax year.
  5. Employment income and benefits
    Employees are taxed on their earnings, including most benefits in kind.
  6. Trading income
    The profits of a trade carried on by a sole trader or partner are taxed as trading income.
  7. Capital allowances
    Capital allowances give tax relief for spending on plant and machinery and some structures, in place of depreciation.
  8. National Insurance contributions
    National Insurance contributions are charged on earnings and self-employed profits and build entitlement to some state benefits.
  9. Corporation tax
    UK resident companies pay corporation tax on their worldwide taxable total profits.
  10. Capital gains tax
    Individuals pay capital gains tax when they dispose of chargeable assets at a gain.
  11. Inheritance tax
    Inheritance tax is charged on a person's estate at death and on some lifetime gifts.
  12. Value added tax
    VAT is a tax on consumer spending collected by businesses at each stage of supply.

Taxation: questions students ask

When is an online self-assessment return due?

31 January after the end of the tax year.

What is the personal allowance for 2025/26?

£12,570, reduced by £1 for every £2 of adjusted net income above £100,000.

What is the test for deducting an employee's expenses?

Wholly, exclusively and necessarily incurred in the performance of the duties of the employment.

Is depreciation deductible for tax?

No. It is added back, and capital allowances are given instead.
