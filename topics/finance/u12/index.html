Charge and discharge accounting
A stewardship system in which an agent accounts to an owner for what was received and paid out
Normative theory

Financial Reporting Manual

Restricted fund

Financial materiality
Information about sustainability matters that could affect a company's cash flows, finance or cost of capital
Public interest
The net benefits to, and procedural rigour on behalf of, all society in relation to any action or decision
CCAB
The Consultative Committee of Accountancy Bodies, linking the six main UK and Irish chartered bodies

Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

Which transaction cycle covers ordering from suppliers and paying them?

The expenditure cycle. From “Accounting information systems” →

In plain English: An accounting information system collects, stores and processes transaction data and turns it into information for decisions and reports. Key idea: An accounting information system combines people, procedures, data, software, infrastructure and controls to turn transactions in the revenue, expenditure, payroll, production and financing cycles into reliable information, and ERP systems integrate these cycles but bring cost, access and dependence risks.

Which technology suits high-volume, rule-based tasks with structured data?

Robotic process automation. From “Automation, AI and the accountant” →

In plain English: Robotic process automation and artificial intelligence are changing what accountants do. Key idea: RPA automates fixed rule-based tasks while machine learning and generative AI learn and generate but can err unpredictably, so automation speeds routine work but needs governance and human review, and the accountant remains responsible for the output.

When did the IASB replace the IASC?

In 2001. From “The history of accounting” →

In plain English: Accounting has developed over thousands of years in response to trade, industry and regulation. Key idea: Accounting grew from ancient records and stewardship accounts to Italian double entry, described in print by Pacioli in 1494, then developed through industrial costing and British company law into a chartered profession and, from the 1970s, national and international standard setting.

Who issues the FReM?

HM Treasury. From “Public sector accounting” →

In plain English: Government bodies account for taxpayers' money under rules adapted from IFRS. Key idea: Public sector accounting stresses stewardship, authorised spending and value for money; UK central government uses IFRS adapted by the FReM and local government the CIPFA/LASAAC Code, with the NAO auditing central government and the Public Accounts Committee holding accounting officers to account.

Which scope covers emissions from purchased electricity?

Scope 2. From “Sustainability and integrated reporting” →

In plain English: Investors and regulators now expect companies to report on climate and other sustainability matters. Key idea: ISSB's IFRS S1 and S2 build on the TCFD pillars using financial materiality, UK companies already make climate disclosures under FCA rules and the Companies Act, integrated reporting explains value creation through six capitals, and emissions are reported by Scope 1, 2 and 3 in tCO2e.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Accounting, Technology and Society

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which transaction cycle includes invoicing customers and collecting cash from them? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    The revenue cycle. Applying the standard result: The revenue cycle runs from customer order to cash receipt.

  2. A dashboard showing last month's sales by region is an example of which type of analytics? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Descriptive. Applying the standard result: Descriptive analytics reports what has already happened.

  3. Software that logs into the bank portal each morning and downloads statements by following fixed steps is (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Robotic process automation. Applying the standard result: RPA bots copy fixed, rule-based human actions on screen.

  4. An invoice from a supplier does not agree with the goods received note. What should a well-controlled system do? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Hold the invoice for review before payment. Three-way matching exists to stop payment for goods not received or not ordered.

  5. Before analysing a ledger extract, why should the analyst reconcile its total to the trial balance? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    To confirm the extract is complete. Reconciling totals and record counts shows no transactions were lost in extraction.

  6. A model learns from five years of coded invoices to suggest the ledger code for new ones. This is (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Machine learning. The model learns patterns from historical data rather than following fixed rules.

  7. After an ERP implementation, one clerk can create suppliers, approve invoices and release payments. What is the main risk? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Lost segregation of duties allowing fictitious supplier fraud. One person controlling the whole payment process could set up and pay a false supplier.

  8. A retailer's prices mostly end in .99 and its sales data deviate from Benford's law. What is the best conclusion? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    The deviation is expected and is not a sign of fraud. Data shaped by pricing policies or limits are not expected to follow Benford's law.

  9. A junior uses a public generative AI tool to draft a client's tax memo, pasting in the client's full records. What is the main professional concern? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Breach of confidentiality and data protection. Client data entered into public tools may be stored or reused, breaching confidentiality.

Key takeaways

  • An accounting information system combines people, procedures, data, software, infrastructure and controls to turn transactions in the revenue, expenditure, payroll, production and financing cycles into reliable information, and ERP systems integrate these cycles but bring cost, access and dependence risks.
  • Descriptive, diagnostic, predictive and prescriptive analytics answer what happened, why, what will happen and what to do, but only reliable, complete and well-documented data give useful results, and tests such as Benford's law flag anomalies that need investigation rather than proving wrongdoing.
  • RPA automates fixed rule-based tasks while machine learning and generative AI learn and generate but can err unpredictably, so automation speeds routine work but needs governance and human review, and the accountant remains responsible for the output.
  • Finance functions face phishing, mandate fraud, ransomware and insider threats, which are reduced by controls such as Cyber Essentials, multi-factor authentication, call-back checks and backups, while UK GDPR requires personal data to be handled on its seven principles and serious breaches reported to the ICO within 72 hours.
  • Accounting grew from ancient records and stewardship accounts to Italian double entry, described in print by Pacioli in 1494, then developed through industrial costing and British company law into a chartered profession and, from the 1970s, national and international standard setting.
  • Normative theories prescribe how accounting should be done while positive theories explain and predict practice; positive accounting theory uses agency costs to predict policy choices through the bonus, debt covenant and political cost hypotheses, and legitimacy, stakeholder and institutional theories explain disclosures as responses to social pressures.

Common mistakes in Accounting, Technology and Society

  • Thinking an AIS is just the accounting software. The software is only one component; procedures, people and controls determine whether the information is reliable.
  • Treating a Benford's law deviation as evidence of fraud. Some data sets, such as prices set at £9.99 or amounts with fixed limits, do not follow Benford's law, so deviations need explaining before conclusions are drawn.
  • Assuming AI output is correct because it is fluent. Generative AI can invent figures, standards or citations, so every output used professionally must be checked against reliable sources.
  • Thinking cyber security is purely an IT matter. Many losses come from staff acting on fraudulent emails, so finance procedures such as independent call-back verification are key controls.
  • Saying Pacioli invented double entry. He described a method that Italian merchants were already using; earlier double-entry records, such as Genoa's 1340 accounts, survive.
  • Treating positive theory as more "correct" than normative theory. They answer different questions: positive theory explains what happens, and normative theory argues about what should happen.

Lessons in this school

  1. Accounting information systems
    An accounting information system collects, stores and processes transaction data and turns it into information for decisions and reports.
  2. Data analytics in accounting
    Accountants increasingly analyse whole populations of data rather than samples.
  3. Automation, AI and the accountant
    Robotic process automation and artificial intelligence are changing what accountants do.
  4. Cyber security and data protection
    Finance teams hold valuable data and control payments, so they are prime targets for cyber attack.
  5. The history of accounting
    Accounting has developed over thousands of years in response to trade, industry and regulation.
  6. Accounting theory
    Accounting theory tries to explain and guide accounting practice.
  7. Critical perspectives on accounting
    Critical accounting research argues that accounting is not a neutral technical activity but shapes organisations and society.
  8. Public sector accounting
    Government bodies account for taxpayers' money under rules adapted from IFRS.
  9. Charity accounting
    Charities must show donors and regulators how funds have been raised and spent.
  10. Sustainability and integrated reporting
    Investors and regulators now expect companies to report on climate and other sustainability matters.
  11. Ethics and the public interest
    Accountants have a duty to act in the public interest, not only for their clients or employers.
  12. The accountancy profession and its qualifications
    The UK has several professional accountancy bodies with different qualifications and specialisms.
