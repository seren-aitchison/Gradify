Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What are Tuckman's four original stages of team development?

Forming, storming, norming and performing. From “Creative collaboration” →

In plain English: Most design work is done in teams, so designers need to collaborate well. Key idea: Teams typically move through forming, storming, norming and performing, and psychological safety is a key condition for learning and creativity; clear goals, roles, working agreements, visible work, retrospectives and a balance of solo and group work help teams collaborate.

What does RACI stand for?

Responsible, accountable, consulted and informed. From “Working with stakeholders” →

In plain English: Design projects succeed only if stakeholders understand, support and act on the work. Key idea: Stakeholders are anyone who affects or is affected by a project; power and interest grids prioritise them, RACI and decision rights clarify roles, and early involvement, shared success measures, evidence, clear rationale and tests help align stakeholders and resolve disagreement.

What does Minto's Pyramid Principle recommend?

Lead with the main message, then give supporting arguments and evidence. From “Presenting and pitching” →

In plain English: Designers regularly present work and pitch ideas to clients, managers and investors. Key idea: Effective pitches start from the audience, lead with the main message, use structures such as situation, complication, question and problem, insight, solution, evidence, ask, apply clear slide principles such as one idea per slide, coherence and signalling, and are rehearsed, with questions anticipated and the ask restated.

What is a design token?

A named value storing a design decision, such as a colour, spacing or type size. From “Design systems” →

In plain English: A design system is a shared set of reusable components, patterns, standards and guidance. Key idea: A design system combines components, patterns, tokens, guidance, code and governance into a shared design language; atomic design structures components from atoms to pages, tokens store design decisions, and systems bring consistency, speed and accessibility but need active governance and contribution to avoid rigidity and decay.

How long can a UK registered design last?

Up to 25 years, renewed every 5 years. From “Registered designs” →

In plain English: Registered designs protect the appearance of products. Key idea: A registered design protects a product's appearance, such as shape, lines, colour and texture, if it is new and has individual character; it lasts up to 25 years with renewals every 5 years and has a 12-month grace period, while unregistered design rights are automatic but shorter and protect only against copying.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Collaboration, Communication and Intellectual Property

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which researcher is most associated with the concept of team psychological safety? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Amy Edmondson. Edmondson's 1999 study linked psychological safety to team learning.

  2. What should a facilitator stay neutral on? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    The content of the group's decisions. Facilitators manage the process but do not impose their own answers.

  3. Which comment is critique rather than reaction or direction? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    The button is low contrast, so users may miss the main action. Critique links a specific element to its effect on the objective.

  4. A team member says nothing about a usability problem because the lead dislikes bad news. What is lacking? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Psychological safety. Without safety, people hide problems and the team cannot learn.

  5. Which activity helps a group converge quickly on preferred ideas? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Dot voting. Dot voting prioritises options, while the others generate ideas.

  6. What should a designer do first when presenting work for critique? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Explain the goal and what feedback is needed. Setting context lets reviewers judge the work against its objectives.

  7. A design team spends every meeting brainstorming aloud and the quietest members rarely contribute. What is the best change? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Add solo idea time before group discussion. Individual thinking first reduces production blocking and includes quieter voices.

  8. Halfway through a workshop, a manager keeps raising an unrelated budget issue. What should the facilitator do? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Record it in the parking lot and return to the agenda. A parking lot respects the issue while keeping the group on purpose.

  9. In a critique, a senior colleague says "Just copy what our competitor does." What is the best response? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Ask which problem that would solve and how it fits the objective. Asking about the objective turns direction into a discussion of effectiveness.

Key takeaways

  • Teams typically move through forming, storming, norming and performing, and psychological safety is a key condition for learning and creativity; clear goals, roles, working agreements, visible work, retrospectives and a balance of solo and group work help teams collaborate.
  • Facilitators guide groups to their goal while staying neutral on content, managing time, energy and participation through divergent, groan and convergent zones; good workshops have a clear purpose, the right people, an opening, exploring and closing structure, timeboxed activities and captured outcomes.
  • Critique analyses whether a design's elements achieve its objectives and why, unlike reactions or direction; givers should be specific, objective-led and constructive, receivers should set context, listen, ask questions and decide what to act on, and structured methods such as Lerman's process help.
  • Stakeholders are anyone who affects or is affected by a project; power and interest grids prioritise them, RACI and decision rights clarify roles, and early involvement, shared success measures, evidence, clear rationale and tests help align stakeholders and resolve disagreement.
  • Sketches are quick, cheap, plentiful and open, inviting discussion; designers use thumbnails, perspective, isometric, oblique, orthographic and exploded drawings for products, and wireframes, storyboards and flows for interfaces and services, choosing the format and finish to suit the audience and decision.
  • Stories help designers understand users and persuade others; good design stories have a character, context, conflict and resolution, use structures such as Duarte's what is and what could be or the story spine, rely on tools like scenarios, storyboards and user stories, and must be honest and evidence-based.

Common mistakes in Collaboration, Communication and Intellectual Property

  • Avoiding all disagreement to keep the peace. Constructive conflict about ideas improves designs; the aim is to keep it about the work, not the person.
  • Running a workshop without a clear purpose or outcome. Without them, activities fill time but decisions are not made.
  • Treating critique as a personal attack or a list of instructions. It is an analysis of the work against its goals, and the designer decides how to respond.
  • Presenting finished designs to stakeholders for the first time at the end. Late involvement leads to surprises, rework and resistance.
  • Making early sketches too polished. Polish signals that an idea is finished and discourages useful feedback.
  • Using stories as decoration or exaggeration. Design stories should be grounded in real research and paired with data that shows how common the problem is.

Collaboration, Communication and Intellectual Property: questions students ask

What is the groan zone?

The uncomfortable stage between divergent and convergent thinking when views clash.

What four questions does Connor and Irizarry's critique approach ask?

What is the objective, which elements relate to it, are they effective, and why or why not.

Which projection method does the UK standard BS 8888 prefer for technical drawings?

First-angle projection.

What pattern did Nancy Duarte find in great speeches?

They move between what is and what could be, ending with a call to action.
