Key terms and formulas at a glance

SCAMPER
A checklist of seven prompts: substitute, combine, adapt, modify, put to another use, eliminate, reverse
Mind map
A diagram radiating from one central topic through branches of associated keywords and images
Analogy

Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

Name Osborn's four rules of brainstorming.

Go for quantity, withhold criticism, welcome wild ideas, combine and improve. From “Brainstorming and its rules” →

In plain English: Brainstorming is the best-known idea generation method, devised by Alex Osborn in the 1940s. Key idea: Groups are good for building on and selecting ideas, but people generate more unique ideas working alone first. Combine individual and group phases.

Why is SCAMPER better for incremental than radical innovation?

It starts from an existing product and transforms it, so ideas tend to stay close to what exists. From “SCAMPER” →

In plain English: SCAMPER is a checklist of seven prompts for changing an existing product or idea: substitute, combine, adapt, modify, put to another use, eliminate and reverse. Key idea: SCAMPER works because each prompt forces a specific transformation, which breaks fixation on the current form while keeping ideas grounded in something real.

Who developed morphological analysis?

Fritz Zwicky. From “Morphological analysis” →

In plain English: Morphological analysis breaks a design into its functions or parameters and lists options for each, then combines them systematically. Key idea: The number of possible concepts is the product of the number of options for each parameter, so even a small chart contains many combinations, including ones nobody would think of directly.

What is an affinity diagram?

A diagram grouping many items into clusters by similarity, building themes from the bottom up. From “Clustering and affinity diagrams” →

In plain English: After generating many ideas or research notes, teams need to find patterns. Key idea: Affinity diagrams build categories from the data (bottom-up), so unexpected themes emerge instead of being forced into the team's existing assumptions.

What does design development resolve?

The details of form, function, materials, components, manufacture, ergonomics and cost. From “Concept development” →

In plain English: A chosen concept is still only a direction. Key idea: Each development decision should link to a reason: a test result, user feedback or a specification point. That is what turns a sequence of drawings into a justified design.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Ideation Methods

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which is one of Osborn's rules of brainstorming? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Withhold criticism until later. Deferring judgement is a core rule; the others contradict the method.

  2. In the 6-3-5 method, how long is each round? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Five minutes. Each of six participants writes three ideas in five minutes per round.

  3. In SCAMPER, what does the E stand for? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Eliminate. E is eliminate: remove parts, features or steps.

  4. In a team brainstorm, several people say they had ideas but forgot them while waiting to speak. What is this? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Production blocking. Waiting for a turn causes ideas to be lost: production blocking (Diehl and Stroebe, 1987).

  5. A 6-3-5 session runs for all six rounds with every box filled. How many ideas are written? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    108. Six people write three ideas in each of six rounds: 6 × 3 × 6 = 108.

  6. A designer turns an old bicycle wheel into a ceiling light. Which SCAMPER prompt is this? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Put to another use. Using the wheel for a new purpose is "put to another use".

  7. Which change would most improve the idea output of a group brainstorm? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Start with silent individual idea writing, then share and build in the group. Individual generation removes blocking and apprehension; the group phase keeps combination.

  8. In which situation would brainwriting be most clearly better than spoken brainstorming? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    A mixed-seniority team where juniors rarely speak in front of the director. Written, parallel and anonymous generation reduces apprehension and dominance in mixed-seniority groups.

  9. Which is the most important limitation of SCAMPER? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    It tends to produce variations on what exists rather than radically new concepts. Starting from an existing product grounds ideas but tends to keep them incremental.

Key takeaways

  • Groups are good for building on and selecting ideas, but people generate more unique ideas working alone first. Combine individual and group phases.
  • Brainwriting keeps the benefit of building on others' ideas while removing the turn-taking that limits spoken brainstorming.
  • SCAMPER works because each prompt forces a specific transformation, which breaks fixation on the current form while keeping ideas grounded in something real.
  • The value of a mind map is in the links: the most interesting ideas often appear where two distant branches connect.
  • Abstracting the problem to a function ("how does nature attach, cool, absorb?") is the key step that makes far analogies usable.
  • The number of possible concepts is the product of the number of options for each parameter, so even a small chart contains many combinations, including ones nobody would think of directly.

Common mistakes in Ideation Methods

  • Assuming a lively, noisy meeting means a productive one. Energy is not output; production blocking means many ideas are never voiced.
  • Treating the 108 ideas as 108 good ideas. Brainwriting produces quantity; convergence, clustering and evaluation are still needed.
  • Using SCAMPER without a problem in mind. The prompts generate variations; you still need research and criteria to know which variations matter to users.
  • Producing a decorative mind map as a portfolio requirement and never using it. A mind map is a thinking tool; its ideas should feed into research questions and design directions.
  • Copying a natural form without understanding the principle. A design that looks like a leaf is not biomimicry unless it uses how the leaf works.
  • Trying to evaluate every combination. A chart with six parameters of five options each has 15,625 combinations; the method is a generator, not a list to work through.

Lessons in this school

  1. Brainstorming and its rules
    Brainstorming is the best-known idea generation method, devised by Alex Osborn in the 1940s.
  2. Brainwriting and 6-3-5
    Brainwriting replaces talking with writing: people generate ideas silently and pass them on for others to build on.
  3. SCAMPER
    SCAMPER is a checklist of seven prompts for changing an existing product or idea: substitute, combine, adapt, modify, put to another use, eliminate and reverse.
  4. Mind mapping
    Mind maps radiate ideas outwards from a central theme, making associations and structure visible.
  5. Analogies and biomimicry
    Many inventions come from seeing how a problem in one domain was solved in another.
  6. Morphological analysis
    Morphological analysis breaks a design into its functions or parameters and lists options for each, then combines them systematically.
  7. How might we questions
    "How might we...?" questions turn research insights into prompts for ideas.
  8. Reversal and the worst possible idea
    Thinking about how to make a problem worse can be a fast route to better ideas.
  9. Clustering and affinity diagrams
    After generating many ideas or research notes, teams need to find patterns.
  10. Dot voting and its limits
    Dot voting lets a group quickly show which ideas it prefers by placing sticky dots.
  11. Evaluation matrices
    Evaluation matrices compare design concepts against criteria in a table, making selection systematic and transparent.
  12. Concept development
    A chosen concept is still only a direction.
  13. TRIZ basics
    TRIZ is a systematic method of inventive problem solving developed by Genrich Altshuller from the study of patents.
  14. Sketching to think
    For designers, sketching is a way of thinking, not just a way of showing.

Ideation Methods: questions students ask

What is production blocking?

Losing ideas in a group because only one person can speak at a time, so others wait, forget or stop thinking.

What do the numbers in 6-3-5 mean?

Six people, three ideas each, five minutes per round.

What do the letters of SCAMPER stand for?

Substitute, combine, adapt, modify, put to another use, eliminate, reverse (or rearrange).

How does a mind map differ from a concept map?

A mind map radiates from one centre with unlabelled branches; a concept map links concepts with labelled relationships and can have several centres.
