Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check.

Why can't designers rely on their own assumptions about users?

They know the product too well, differ in skills and background from users, and are not the users. From “Why research users” →

What makes consent "informed"?

The participant understands the purpose, what is involved, how data will be used and that taking part is voluntary. From “Research ethics and consent” →

What is the master-apprentice model?

In contextual inquiry, the participant is the expert and the researcher learns by watching and asking as an apprentice would. From “Contextual inquiry and observation” →

What are cultural probes?

Kits of materials such as cameras and postcards given to participants to record their lives for designers. From “Ethnography in design” →

What should be at the core of a persona?

Goals and behaviours from research, rather than demographics. From “Personas” →

Lessons in this school

  1. Why research users
    Designers research users because their own assumptions are unreliable guides to what other people need and do.
  2. Research questions and plans
    Good research starts with clear questions and a plan that matches methods to them.
  3. Qualitative and quantitative research
    Qualitative research explores why and how; quantitative research measures how many and how much.
  4. Research ethics and consent
    Research with people must protect them from harm, respect their choices and handle their data lawfully.
  5. Recruiting participants
    Research is only as good as the people in it.
  6. User interviews
    Interviews are the most common user research method.
  7. Contextual inquiry and observation
    Contextual inquiry combines observation and interviewing in the place where people actually do the work or activity.
  8. Surveys
    Surveys collect standardised answers from many people quickly.
  9. Diary studies
    Diary studies ask participants to record their experiences over days or weeks, capturing behaviour that unfolds over time or in private settings.
  10. Ethnography in design
    Ethnography is the study of people's everyday lives and cultures through immersion.
  11. Desk research and competitor analysis
    Before talking to users, designers learn what is already known and what already exists.
  12. Empathy maps
    Empathy maps summarise what a team has learned about a user in four quadrants: what they say, think, do and feel.
  13. Personas
    A persona is a fictional but research-based profile representing a group of users with similar goals and behaviours.
  14. Customer journey maps
    A journey map visualises the steps a person goes through to achieve a goal, with their actions, thoughts and emotions at each step.
  15. Jobs to be done
    Jobs to be done is a way of understanding why people "hire" a product: to make progress in a particular situation.
  16. Synthesis and insight
    Research only helps if it is turned into insight: a new understanding that changes what the team will do.

Key terms

User research
The systematic study of the people a design is for: their goals, behaviour, contexts and needs
Research question
A statement of what the team needs to learn to make a design decision
Qualitative research
Research gathering rich, non-numerical data to understand reasons, contexts and experiences
Informed consent
Voluntary agreement to take part, given with an understanding of what the research involves
Screener
A short questionnaire used to check that potential participants meet recruitment criteria
Discussion guide
A flexible list of topics and questions used to structure an interview
Contextual inquiry
A research method combining observation and interviewing where an activity actually happens
Likert scale
A rating scale measuring agreement or satisfaction across ordered, balanced options
Diary study
A research method in which participants record their experiences over time, near the moment they happen
Ethnography
Research that studies people's everyday lives and cultures through sustained immersion
Desk research
Gathering and analysing existing information rather than collecting new data
Empathy map
A four-quadrant summary of what a user says, thinks, does and feels
Persona
A fictional, research-based profile representing a group of users with similar goals and behaviours
Customer journey map
A visualisation of a person's steps, touchpoints, thoughts and emotions while pursuing a goal
