When does incubation help most?

Lessons in this school

  1. What is creativity?
    Creativity researchers broadly agree that a creative idea must be both original and effective.
  2. The four Ps of creativity
    Rhodes (1961) organised creativity research into four strands: the person, the process, the press (environment) and the product.
  3. Divergent thinking and its measurement
    Divergent thinking tests ask people to produce many answers to open problems, such as unusual uses for a brick.
  4. Stages of the creative process
    Graham Wallas (1926) proposed four stages of creative thought: preparation, incubation, illumination and verification.
  5. Insight and incubation
    Insight is the sudden reorganisation of a problem that makes a solution obvious; incubation is the effect of stepping away.
  6. Knowledge, expertise and creativity
    Creativity draws on deep knowledge, but expertise can also trap people in familiar solutions.
  7. Motivation and creativity
    People tend to be most creative when they are interested in the work itself.
  8. Design fixation and how to break it
    Designers often get stuck on the first ideas they see or have, copying features without realising it.
  9. Creative confidence
    Many people believe they are "not creative" and stop trying.
  10. Constraints and creativity
    A blank page can paralyse, while the right limits can spark invention.
  11. Creative climate in teams
    Most design happens in teams, and the climate of a team strongly affects its creativity.
  12. Myths about creativity
    Popular beliefs about creativity, such as right-brained creative people, lone geniuses and the mad artist, are widespread but often unsupported.

Key terms

Creativity
The production of ideas or products that are both original and effective
Press
The environment and social pressures that influence creativity, in Rhodes's framework
Fluency
The number of relevant responses produced in a divergent thinking task
Incubation
A period away from a problem, after preparation, which can help a solution emerge
Insight
A sudden restructuring of a problem's representation that reveals the solution
Domain-relevant skills
Knowledge, technical skills and talent in a particular field (Amabile)
Intrinsic motivation
Motivation from interest, enjoyment or meaning in the task itself
Functional fixedness
Seeing an object only in terms of its usual function
Creative self-efficacy
A person's belief in their ability to produce creative outcomes
Constraint
A limit on what an acceptable solution can be, such as cost, time, material or regulation
Psychological safety
A shared belief that the team is safe for interpersonal risk-taking
Lone genius myth
The belief that breakthroughs come from isolated individuals rather than collaboration and accumulated work
