Key terms and formulas at a glance

Creativity
The production of ideas or products that are both original and effective
Press
The environment and social pressures that influence creativity, in Rhodes's framework
Fluency
The number of relevant responses produced in a divergent thinking task
Incubation
A period away from a problem, after preparation, which can help a solution emerge
Insight
A sudden restructuring of a problem's representation that reveals the solution
Domain-relevant skills
Knowledge, technical skills and talent in a particular field (Amabile)
Intrinsic motivation
Motivation from interest, enjoyment or meaning in the task itself
Functional fixedness
Seeing an object only in terms of its usual function
Creative self-efficacy
A person's belief in their ability to produce creative outcomes
Constraint
A limit on what an acceptable solution can be, such as cost, time, material or regulation
Psychological safety
A shared belief that the team is safe for interpersonal risk-taking
Lone genius myth
The belief that breakthroughs come from isolated individuals rather than collaboration and accumulated work

Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What are the two parts of the standard definition of creativity?

Originality (novelty) and effectiveness (usefulness or appropriateness). From “What is creativity?” →

In plain English: Creativity researchers broadly agree that a creative idea must be both original and effective. Key idea: Creativity = original + effective. Most valuable design creativity is P-creative and little-c or Pro-c: new to the team and the problem, and useful.

What is measured by flexibility in a divergent thinking test?

The number of different categories the responses fall into. From “Divergent thinking and its measurement” →

In plain English: Divergent thinking tests ask people to produce many answers to open problems, such as unusual uses for a brick. Key idea: Fluency and originality are correlated: people who produce more ideas tend to produce more original ones, because unusual ideas often come after the obvious ones.

When does incubation help most?

After substantial preparation, with a break filled by an undemanding activity. From “Insight and incubation” →

In plain English: Insight is the sudden reorganisation of a problem that makes a solution obvious; incubation is the effect of stepping away. Key idea: Work hard on a problem first, then take a genuine break with an undemanding activity: incubation helps most after preparation.

What is functional fixedness?

Seeing an object only in terms of its usual function, as in Duncker's candle problem. From “Design fixation and how to break it” →

In plain English: Designers often get stuck on the first ideas they see or have, copying features without realising it. Key idea: Seeing an example, or having a first idea, narrows what designers produce next, often without their noticing. Fixation affects experts as well as students.

How can a constraint help creativity?

By ruling out familiar solutions, so people must search for new ones. From “Constraints and creativity” →

In plain English: A blank page can paralyse, while the right limits can spark invention. Key idea: Neither total freedom nor tight control is best for creativity. Moderate, meaningful constraints push people beyond the obvious.

Quick quiz: test yourself on The Psychology of Creativity

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. According to the standard definition, which idea is creative? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    An unusual design that also solves the user's problem. Creativity needs originality and effectiveness together; random or copied ideas lack one of them.

  2. In Rhodes's framework, which P refers to the environment? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Press. Press covers the social and physical environment; the others refer to people, methods and outcomes.

  3. Which score counts the total number of relevant ideas a person produces? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Fluency. Fluency is the count of relevant responses; the others measure variety, rarity and detail.

  4. A Year 10 student invents a folding mechanism, unaware that an identical one was patented in 1950. How would Boden describe it? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    P-creative but not H-creative. It is new to the student (P-creative) but not new in history (not H-creative).

  5. A company introduces a creativity questionnaire about trust, freedom and debate in teams. Which P is it measuring? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Press. Trust, freedom and debate describe the climate a person works in, which is press.

  6. Responses for a brick: "doorstop, paperweight, bookend, weapon, plant pot". How many categories, roughly, does this show? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Three: holding things in place, attack, containing a plant. Doorstop, paperweight and bookend share one category (weight that holds), so flexibility is about three despite fluency of five.

  7. A critic argues a wildly unusual but unusable chair is "the most creative chair ever made". Which reply is strongest? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Originality alone is not enough; a creative design must also be effective for its purpose. Under the standard definition, novelty without effectiveness is not creativity, though as an art object its purpose might differ.

  8. Which is the strongest criticism of the four Ps? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    The Ps interact so closely that studying one alone can mislead. The framework is useful but the strands overlap; for instance, products are judged within a social press.

  9. Which is the strongest criticism of using divergent thinking tests to select designers? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    They measure idea generation potential, not the knowledge and judgement design achievement needs. Divergent thinking is one component; selection would need evidence of domain skill, judgement and track record.

Key takeaways

  • Creativity = original + effective. Most valuable design creativity is P-creative and little-c or Pro-c: new to the team and the problem, and useful.
  • Creativity happens when a person uses a process within a press to produce a product. Improving creativity can target any of the four, but they interact.
  • Fluency and originality are correlated: people who produce more ideas tend to produce more original ones, because unusual ideas often come after the obvious ones.
  • Insight rarely arrives without preparation. The "aha" moment is built on knowledge and effort put in beforehand, and must be followed by verification.
  • Work hard on a problem first, then take a genuine break with an undemanding activity: incubation helps most after preparation.
  • Creativity needs knowledge and flexibility together. Teams combine experts with fresh perspectives, and experts use methods that help them question their assumptions.

Common mistakes in The Psychology of Creativity

  • Thinking creativity is only for geniuses or artists. Research treats creativity as a normal capacity found at every level, from mini-c to Big-C.
  • Studying only the person. Treating creativity as an individual gift ignores the methods and environments that research shows make a large difference.
  • Treating a divergent thinking score as "your creativity". It measures one component, idea generation, not the knowledge, judgement and persistence that creative achievement also needs.
  • Waiting for inspiration. The model shows illumination follows preparation; doing nothing without first working on the problem rarely produces insight.
  • Believing insight comes from nowhere. Laboratory and historical evidence show insights depend on prior knowledge and engagement with the problem.
  • Assuming that knowing less makes you more creative. Novices produce more unusual ideas sometimes, but most creative achievement requires deep domain knowledge to make ideas effective.

Lessons in this school

  1. What is creativity?
    Creativity researchers broadly agree that a creative idea must be both original and effective.
  2. The four Ps of creativity
    Rhodes (1961) organised creativity research into four strands: the person, the process, the press (environment) and the product.
  3. Divergent thinking and its measurement
    Divergent thinking tests ask people to produce many answers to open problems, such as unusual uses for a brick.
  4. Stages of the creative process
    Graham Wallas (1926) proposed four stages of creative thought: preparation, incubation, illumination and verification.
  5. Insight and incubation
    Insight is the sudden reorganisation of a problem that makes a solution obvious; incubation is the effect of stepping away.
  6. Knowledge, expertise and creativity
    Creativity draws on deep knowledge, but expertise can also trap people in familiar solutions.
  7. Motivation and creativity
    People tend to be most creative when they are interested in the work itself.
  8. Design fixation and how to break it
    Designers often get stuck on the first ideas they see or have, copying features without realising it.
  9. Creative confidence
    Many people believe they are "not creative" and stop trying.
  10. Constraints and creativity
    A blank page can paralyse, while the right limits can spark invention.
  11. Creative climate in teams
    Most design happens in teams, and the climate of a team strongly affects its creativity.
  12. Myths about creativity
    Popular beliefs about creativity, such as right-brained creative people, lone geniuses and the mad artist, are widespread but often unsupported.

The Psychology of Creativity: questions students ask

What is the consensual assessment technique?

Independent expert judges rate the creativity of products; agreement among them is taken as the measure.

What does the Geneplore model describe?

Creative cognition as cycles of generating idea structures and exploring their implications.

What is an impasse in problem solving?

Being stuck because the problem is represented in an unhelpful way, before insight restructures it.

What is the ten-year rule?

The finding that major creative achievement usually follows about ten years of intensive preparation in a field.
