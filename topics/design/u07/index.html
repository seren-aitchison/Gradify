What is a readymade?

An everyday manufactured object selected and presented as art, as in Duchamp's Fountain (1917). From “What is art and design?” →

What did Hogarth call his S-shaped curve?

The "line of beauty", in The Analysis of Beauty (1753). From “Line” →

What is frottage?

A technique of rubbing a drawing material over paper placed on a textured surface, developed by Max Ernst from 1925. From “Texture” →

Name three things that increase an element's visual weight.

Any three: size, darkness or contrast, saturation, texture or detail, isolation, human interest, distance from the centre. From “Balance and symmetry” →

What does "unity in variety" mean?

A work holds together as a whole while containing enough difference to stay interesting. From “Unity and variety” →

Lessons in this school

  1. What is art and design?
    Art and design are the making of things that are looked at, used and thought about.
  2. How art and design are assessed: the four assessment objectives
    GCSE and A level Art and Design are marked against the same four assessment objectives: develop, refine, record and present.
  3. Looking closely: an approach to visual analysis
    Visual analysis is the disciplined description and interpretation of what a work looks like.
  4. Line
    Line is the most basic visual element: a mark with length and direction, or an edge the eye perceives.
  5. Shape and form
    Shape is a flat, two-dimensional area; form is three-dimensional, with height, width and depth.
  6. Tone and value
    Tone (or value) is the lightness or darkness of a colour or surface.
  7. Colour as a visual element
    Colour is the visual element with the most immediate emotional impact.
  8. Texture
    Texture is the surface quality of a work, how it feels or looks as if it would feel.
  9. Space
    Space is the area around, between and within forms, real in sculpture and architecture and illusory in pictures.
  10. Pattern
    Pattern is the regular repetition of a motif across a surface.
  11. Balance and symmetry
    Balance is the distribution of visual weight in a composition.
  12. Contrast and emphasis
    Emphasis makes one part of a work dominant, and contrast is the main way to achieve it.
  13. Rhythm, repetition and movement
    Rhythm is created by repeating visual elements at intervals, and movement is the path the eye takes through a work or the illusion of motion within it.
  14. Proportion and scale
    Proportion is the size relationship of parts to each other and to the whole; scale is size relative to a standard, usually the human body.
  15. Unity and variety
    Unity is the sense that all parts of a work belong together; variety is the difference that keeps it interesting.
  16. Composition and the picture plane
    Composition is the arrangement of elements within the format of a work.
  17. The rule of thirds and the golden ratio
    The rule of thirds and the golden ratio are proportional systems often recommended for composition.
  18. Gestalt principles in art and design
    Gestalt psychology studies how we perceive wholes rather than separate parts.

Key terms

Fine art
Art made chiefly to be looked at and contemplated rather than used, such as painting, sculpture and installation
Assessment objective
A statement of the skill or understanding an exam rewards; Art and Design has four (develop, refine, record, present)
Formal analysis
Analysis of how a work's visual elements and principles are organised, independent of its subject's meaning
Contour line
A line that follows the edges of a form, including internal edges, to describe its shape and volume
Organic shape
An irregular, often curving shape associated with natural forms
Tone
The relative lightness or darkness of a colour or surface; also called value
Hue
The name of a colour family, such as red, blue or yellow-green
Actual texture
Texture that can be felt, such as thick paint, carved surfaces or woven fibre
Picture plane
The flat surface of a picture, imagined as a window through which pictorial space is seen
Motif
The repeated unit in a pattern
Symmetrical balance
Balance in which elements on either side of a central axis mirror each other
Focal point
The area of a work that attracts the eye first
Rhythm
A visual beat created by repeating elements at intervals
Proportion
The size relationship of parts to each other and to the whole
