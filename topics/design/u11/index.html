What is greenwashing?

Making misleading claims about the environmental benefits of a product or organisation. From “Design and the environment” →

What does nesting mean in manufacturing?

Arranging parts closely on a sheet to minimise offcuts. From “Designing out waste” →

What is the central principle of cradle to cradle?

Waste equals food. From “Cradle to cradle” →

What is a roach motel pattern?

A design that is easy to get into, such as a subscription, but hard to get out of. From “Dark patterns” →

Lessons in this school

  1. Design and the environment
    Design decisions shape how much energy, material and waste a product creates over its whole life.
  2. Life cycle assessment
    Life cycle assessment measures the environmental impact of a product from raw materials to disposal.
  3. The circular economy
    The circular economy keeps products and materials in use instead of throwing them away.
  4. Designing out waste
    The best way to deal with waste is to stop it being created.
  5. Design for disassembly and repair
    Products that can be taken apart can be repaired, upgraded and recycled.
  6. Choosing materials sustainably
    Material choice shapes a product's footprint, durability and end of life.
  7. Cradle to cradle
    Cradle to cradle is a design philosophy in which materials are designed to cycle safely forever.
  8. The waste hierarchy
    The waste hierarchy ranks ways of managing waste from most to least preferred.
  9. Design ethics
    Design shapes how people live, so designers face ethical choices.
  10. Dark patterns
    Dark patterns, also called deceptive patterns, are design tricks that push people into choices they did not intend.
  11. Persuasive technology
    Many digital products are designed to change what people do and how often they return.
  12. Privacy by design
    Privacy by design builds data protection into products from the start rather than adding it later.
  13. Bias in design and data
    Designs and data sets can systematically disadvantage some groups.
  14. Designing with AI
    Artificial intelligence is changing both how designers work and what they design.
  15. Social and cultural responsibility
    Design affects workers, communities and cultures as well as users.
  16. Professional codes of conduct
    Professional bodies set out what is expected of designers.

Key terms

Planetary boundaries
Limits on Earth system processes within which humanity can operate safely
Life cycle assessment
A method for quantifying environmental impacts across a product's whole life
Circular economy
An economic system in which materials never become waste and nature is regenerated
Material efficiency
Achieving the same function with less material
Design for disassembly
Designing products so they can be taken apart easily for repair, reuse or recycling
Embodied energy
The energy used to extract, process and make a material
Eco-effectiveness
Designing products to have positive effects, with materials cycling safely, not just reduced harm
Waste hierarchy
A ranking of waste management options from prevention to disposal
Value sensitive design
An approach that accounts for human values throughout the design process
Dark pattern
An interface design that tricks or pressures users into unintended choices
Persuasive technology
Technology designed to change attitudes or behaviours
Privacy by design
An approach that builds privacy into products and systems from the start
Sampling bias
Bias caused when some groups are missing or under-represented in data
Generative AI
AI that produces new content such as text, images or code
