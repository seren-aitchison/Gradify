Lessons in this school

  1. The skull and facial skeleton
    The skull protects the brain and carries the teeth, the airway and the special senses.
  2. The maxilla and palate
    The paired maxillae carry the upper teeth, form most of the hard palate and enclose the largest paranasal sinus.
  3. The mandible
    The mandible is the largest and strongest bone of the face and the only one that moves.
  4. The temporomandibular joint
    The temporomandibular joint (TMJ) is a synovial joint that both hinges and slides, with an articular disc dividing it into two compartments.
  5. Muscles of mastication
    Four paired muscles move the mandible in chewing: masseter, temporalis and the medial and lateral pterygoids.
  6. Mandibular movements
    The mandible opens, closes, protrudes, retrudes and moves from side to side, and the teeth guide it as they meet.
  7. Muscles of facial expression
    The muscles of facial expression lie in the superficial fascia and move the skin of the face.
  8. The tongue and floor of the mouth
    The tongue is a muscular organ of taste, speech, chewing and swallowing, with a complex nerve supply that reflects its development.
  9. The trigeminal nerve
    The trigeminal nerve (V) is the great sensory nerve of the face, mouth and teeth and the motor nerve of mastication.
  10. Nerve supply of the teeth and gingivae
    Each tooth and each area of gingiva has its own named nerve, and the choice of local anaesthetic technique follows directly from this map.
  11. The facial nerve and other cranial nerves in dentistry

  12. Blood supply and lymphatic drainage of the head and neck
    The external carotid artery and its maxillary branch supply the jaws and teeth, the pterygoid venous plexus links the face to the cavernous sinus, and lymph from the mouth drains through predictable node groups.
  13. The oral cavity: anatomy and landmarks
    A dentist examines the whole mouth at every visit, so the normal landmarks and the common normal variants must be known well enough that anything unusual stands out.
  14. The salivary glands
    Three paired major salivary glands and hundreds of minor glands produce saliva.
  15. The pharynx and larynx
    The pharynx is shared by the airway and the food passage, and the larynx guards the lower airway.
  16. Fascial spaces of the head and neck
    Fascial layers divide the soft tissues of the head and neck into potential spaces, and pus from a dental infection spreads along them.

Key terms

Viscerocranium
The facial skeleton: the 14 bones that form the face and jaws
Maxillary tuberosity
The rounded posterior end of the maxillary alveolar process behind the last molar
Lingula
The bony tongue at the front of the mandibular foramen, attachment of the sphenomandibular ligament
Articular disc
The fibrous plate dividing the TMJ into upper (translation) and lower (rotation) compartments
Trismus
Restricted mouth opening due to spasm, inflammation or injury of the muscles of mastication
Intercuspal position
The position of maximum intercuspation of the teeth
Buccinator
The cheek muscle from the pterygomandibular raphe and alveolar bone to the modiolus, keeping food between the teeth
Sulcus terminalis
The V-shaped groove dividing the anterior two-thirds from the posterior third of the tongue
Trigeminal ganglion
The sensory ganglion of V, in Meckel's cave on the petrous temporal bone
Nasopalatine nerve
The branch of V2 supplying the anterior palatal gingiva through the incisive foramen
Chorda tympani
The facial nerve branch carrying taste from the anterior two-thirds of the tongue and secretomotor fibres to the submandibular and sublingual glands
Maxillary artery
Terminal branch of the external carotid, supplying the jaws, teeth, muscles of mastication and palate
Vestibule
The space between the lips and cheeks and the teeth and alveolar processes
Otic ganglion
The parasympathetic ganglion below foramen ovale relaying IX fibres to the parotid
