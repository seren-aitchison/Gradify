Lessons in this school

  1. Production of X-rays
    Dental radiographs depend on X-rays generated in a small tube head.
  2. Interaction of X-rays with matter
    An X-ray image is a map of how much radiation passes through each part of the patient.
  3. Biological effects of ionising radiation
    Ionising radiation can damage DNA and living cells.
  4. Radiation doses in dentistry
    Comparing doses helps dentists explain risk and choose the right examination.
  5. IRR17 and IR(ME)R in dentistry
    Two sets of UK regulations govern the use of X-rays in dental practice.
  6. Justification and optimisation
    Justification and optimisation are the core principles of patient radiation protection.
  7. Radiation protection in the dental surgery
    Good radiation protection keeps staff and public doses tiny and patient doses as low as reasonably practicable.
  8. Quality assurance in dental radiography
    Quality assurance makes sure radiographs are diagnostic, doses are low and equipment works safely.
  9. Image receptors: film and digital
    The receptor captures the X-ray pattern and turns it into an image.
  10. Intraoral techniques: bitewing and periapical
    Bitewings and periapicals are the most common dental radiographs.
  11. Occlusal radiographs
    Occlusal radiographs show larger areas of the jaws than periapicals.
  12. Panoramic radiography
    A panoramic radiograph shows both jaws and all teeth in one image.
  13. Cone beam CT
    Cone beam computed tomography gives three-dimensional images of the teeth and jaws.
  14. Selection criteria for dental radiographs
    Selection criteria help dentists decide which radiograph to take and when.
  15. Radiographic interpretation: caries and periodontal bone
    Reading bitewings and periapicals systematically finds caries and bone loss that are hidden clinically.
  16. Radiographic interpretation: periapical and jaw lesions
    Periapical and jaw lesions range from common inflammatory changes to rare tumours.
  17. Radiographic faults and artefacts
    Faulty radiographs give extra dose without benefit.

Key terms

Thermionic emission
Release of electrons from a heated filament
Attenuation
Reduction of beam intensity as it passes through matter
Deterministic effect
A tissue reaction with a threshold dose whose severity increases with dose
Gray
The unit of absorbed dose, one joule per kilogram
Radiation Protection Adviser
A qualified expert who advises the employer on compliance with IRR17
Justification
Deciding an exposure gives a net benefit to the patient
Controlled area
An area around X-ray equipment where special procedures apply to restrict exposure
Quality assurance
A planned programme ensuring consistent, high-quality and safe radiography
Latent image
The invisible image formed in film before processing
Bitewing
An intraoral view of upper and lower posterior crowns and crestal bone
Occlusal radiograph
An intraoral view with the receptor in the occlusal plane, showing a large area
Focal trough
The curved zone within which structures are sharply imaged in panoramic radiography
Voxel
A three-dimensional pixel, the unit of a CBCT volume
Selection criteria
Evidence-based guidance on when a radiograph is likely to change management
