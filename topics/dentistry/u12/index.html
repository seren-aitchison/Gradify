Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What is the main purpose of the General Dental Council?

To protect patients and maintain public confidence in dental services. From “The GDC and its Standards for the Dental Team” →

In plain English: The General Dental Council regulates the dental team in the UK. Key idea: The GDC regulates UK dental professionals to protect patients, keeping a register and setting standards; its nine principles cover patients' interests, communication, consent, confidentiality, complaints, teamwork, competence, raising concerns and personal behaviour.

What are the four parts of the functional test for capacity?

The ability to understand, retain, use or weigh information, and communicate a decision. From “Mental capacity and consent” →

In plain English: Some patients may lack the capacity to make decisions about their dental care. Key idea: The Mental Capacity Act 2005 presumes capacity, requires support to decide, accepts unwise decisions, and requires best-interests, least restrictive decisions; capacity is decision-specific and assessed by the ability to understand, retain, weigh and communicate.

What does contemporaneous mean for records?

Made at the time of the treatment or event. From “Record keeping and data protection” →

In plain English: Good records support patient care and protect both patients and professionals. Key idea: Records must be accurate, contemporaneous and complete, including consent and treatment details; health data are special category data under the UK GDPR, requiring security and confidentiality; patients can access their records, normally within a month.

What are the three components of evidence-based dentistry?

Best research evidence, clinical expertise and patients' values and preferences. From “Evidence-based dentistry” →

In plain English: Evidence-based dentistry combines research evidence with clinical expertise and patients' values. Key idea: Evidence-based dentistry combines research evidence, clinical expertise and patient values; systematic reviews and RCTs are high in the evidence hierarchy; PICO frames questions; Cochrane, NICE, SDCEP and Delivering Better Oral Health are key UK sources.

What do COSHH regulations cover?

Control of substances hazardous to health, such as chemicals and mercury. From “Health and safety in dental practice” →

In plain English: Dental practices contain many hazards, from sharps to radiation. Key idea: Health and safety in dentistry is governed by the 1974 Act, COSHH, RIDDOR and radiation regulations; HTM 01-05 sets decontamination standards; sharps, hazardous substances and amalgam waste must be managed safely.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Professionalism, Ethics and Law

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which law established the GDC's current framework? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    The Dentists Act 1984. The Dentists Act 1984 sets out the GDC's role and powers.

  2. Which team member can provide complete dentures directly to patients? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    A clinical dental technician. Clinical dental technicians can fit complete dentures directly.

  3. Which option must always be discussed when gaining consent? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    The option of no treatment, with its consequences. Patients must understand all reasonable options.

  4. Who must follow the Standards for the Dental Team? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    All GDC registrants, including dental care professionals. The standards apply to the whole registered team.

  5. What should a hygienist do if they find a problem outside their scope? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Refer the patient to a dentist and record it. Working within scope and referring when needed protects patients.

  6. When is a risk material under Montgomery? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    When a reasonable person in the patient's position would consider it significant. The Montgomery test is patient-centred, not based on what clinicians think.

  7. Which principle applies when a dental nurse sees a colleague working while impaired by alcohol? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Raise concerns if patients are at risk. Principle 8 requires action when patients are at risk.

  8. Why must a dental therapist be competent as well as within scope? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Being within the group's scope does not guarantee an individual has the skill. Each registrant is accountable for their own competence.

  9. A patient's relative offers to interpret during a consent discussion. What is best practice? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Use a professional interpreter where possible to ensure accurate, confidential communication. Professional interpreters reduce errors and protect confidentiality.

Key takeaways

  • The GDC regulates UK dental professionals to protect patients, keeping a register and setting standards; its nine principles cover patients' interests, communication, consent, confidentiality, complaints, teamwork, competence, raising concerns and personal behaviour.
  • The dental team includes dentists, dental nurses, hygienists, therapists, orthodontic therapists, technicians and clinical dental technicians; each works within the GDC's scope of practice and their own competence, and hygienists and therapists can be seen by direct access.
  • Valid consent must be voluntary, informed and given by someone with capacity; Montgomery requires discussion of material risks and reasonable alternatives; clear communication of options, risks and costs, with records, is essential.
  • The Mental Capacity Act 2005 presumes capacity, requires support to decide, accepts unwise decisions, and requires best-interests, least restrictive decisions; capacity is decision-specific and assessed by the ability to understand, retain, weigh and communicate.
  • Registrants must raise concerns when patients are at risk, protected by whistleblowing law; the duty of candour requires openness, an apology and putting things right when harm occurs, with statutory requirements for CQC-registered providers.
  • Fitness to practise covers skills, knowledge, health and character; the GDC assesses concerns through case examiners and Practice Committees, with interim orders if needed; sanctions range from reprimand to conditions, suspension and erasure, aiming to protect the public.

Common mistakes in Professionalism, Ethics and Law

  • Thinking the standards apply only to dentists. They apply to all registered members of the dental team.
  • Thinking anything in a group's scope can be done by every member of it. Each registrant must also be trained and competent for the task.
  • Treating a signed form as proof of valid consent. Consent depends on the quality of the discussion and the patient's understanding.
  • Assuming someone with dementia or a learning disability lacks capacity. Capacity must be assessed for each decision.
  • Believing that apologising means admitting legal liability. An apology is part of candour and is not itself an admission of liability.
  • Thinking fitness to practise is about punishing professionals. Its purpose is to protect the public and confidence in the profession.

    The General Dental Council regulates the dental team in the UK.

    The dental team includes several registered professions, each with defined roles.

    Valid consent is a legal and ethical requirement before any dental treatment.

    Some patients may lack the capacity to make decisions about their dental care.

  7. Record keeping and data protection
    Good records support patient care and protect both patients and professionals.

    Handling complaints well can resolve problems and improve care.

    Evidence-based dentistry combines research evidence with clinical expertise and patients' values.
  11. Safeguarding adults
    Dental teams may be the first to notice signs that an adult is being abused or neglected.
  12. Equality, diversity and inclusion
    Dental teams must treat everyone fairly and make care accessible.
  13. Health and safety in dental practice
    Dental practices contain many hazards, from sharps to radiation.
  14. Continuing professional development
    Dental professionals must keep their knowledge and skills up to date.
  15. Ethics in dental practice
    Dental professionals face ethical dilemmas in everyday practice.
