Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check.

What are the four interacting factors in caries?

Biofilm, fermentable carbohydrates (free sugars), a susceptible host and time. From “What dental caries is” →

What did the Vipeholm study show?

Sugar between meals, especially sticky sugar, increased caries far more than sugar at meals. From “Diet, sugars and caries” →

What is the main way fluoride prevents caries?

Topically: inhibiting demineralisation and enhancing remineralisation at the tooth surface. From “How fluoride works” →

When should toothbrushing start, according to DBOH?

As soon as the first tooth erupts. From “Delivering Better Oral Health” →

What is the social gradient in oral health?

The stepwise worsening of oral health at each lower level of social position, across the whole population. From “Oral health inequalities” →

Lessons in this school

  1. What dental caries is
    Dental caries is a biofilm-mediated, sugar-driven, dynamic disease in which the dental hard tissues lose and regain mineral many times a day.
  2. The ecological plaque hypothesis
    Which bacteria cause caries?
  3. Demineralisation and remineralisation
    Every time sugar is eaten, the pH of plaque falls and the tooth surface loses mineral; between intakes, saliva helps it regain mineral.
  4. Diet, sugars and caries
    Free sugars are the essential dietary cause of caries.
  5. Caries detection and diagnosis
    Detecting a caries lesion, judging its extent and deciding whether it is active are three separate steps, and all three guide treatment.
  6. Caries risk assessment
    Caries risk assessment predicts who is likely to develop new lesions, so that prevention, recall intervals and radiographs can be matched to need.
  7. Root caries
    Root caries affects exposed root surfaces, usually in older adults with gingival recession, and is becoming more common as people keep their teeth for longer.
  8. How fluoride works
    Fluoride is the cornerstone of caries prevention.
  9. Fluoride toothpaste, varnish and other vehicles
    Fluoride reaches teeth through toothpaste, varnish, mouthrinses, water, milk and salt.
  10. Fissure sealants
    Pits and fissures on molars trap plaque and are where most caries occurs in children's permanent teeth.
  11. Delivering Better Oral Health
    Delivering Better Oral Health (DBOH) is the evidence-based toolkit for prevention used by dental teams in England.
  12. Minimal intervention dentistry
    Minimal intervention dentistry (MID) manages caries by detecting it early, controlling its causes, remineralising early lesions and, when restoration is needed, removing as little tooth as possible.
  13. Epidemiology of oral disease
    Epidemiology describes how common oral diseases are, who has them and why, and it underpins planning of services and prevention.
  14. Measuring oral disease: indices
    An index turns oral disease into a number that can be compared between people, places and times.
  15. Oral health inequalities
    Oral disease follows a social gradient: at every step down the social ladder, oral health gets worse.
  16. Community water fluoridation
    Community water fluoridation adjusts the fluoride in public water supplies to a level that helps prevent caries.
  17. Oral health promotion
    Oral health promotion enables people to increase control over their oral health.
  18. NHS dental services
    Most dental care in the UK is delivered through the NHS by general dental practitioners under contract, alongside community and hospital services and private care.

Key terms

Dental caries
A biofilm-mediated, sugar-driven, dynamic disease causing phasic loss and gain of mineral in teeth
Ecological plaque hypothesis
The theory that environmental changes such as frequent sugar shift the biofilm towards a cariogenic community
Critical pH
The pH below which saliva and plaque fluid are undersaturated with respect to tooth mineral, about 5.5 for enamel
Free sugars
Sugars added to foods and drinks plus those in honey, syrups and fruit juices; not those in intact fruit or milk
ICDAS
International Caries Detection and Assessment System, grading coronal lesions from 0 (sound) to 6 (extensive cavity)
Caries risk assessment
A structured judgement of a person's likelihood of developing new caries lesions
Root caries
Caries of the exposed root surface of a tooth, usually after gingival recession
Fluorapatite
Apatite in which fluoride replaces hydroxyl, less soluble than hydroxyapatite
ppm F
Parts per million fluoride, the unit for fluoride concentration in toothpaste and water
Fissure sealant
A material bonded into the pits and fissures of a tooth to prevent plaque stagnation and caries
Delivering Better Oral Health
The evidence-based prevention toolkit for dental teams in England, 4th edition 2021
Minimal intervention dentistry
Caries management that prioritises prevention, early detection and minimal tissue removal
Prevalence
The proportion of a population with a condition at a given time
DMFT
The number of decayed, missing (due to caries) and filled permanent teeth
