Lessons in this school

  1. Child development and behaviour management
    Successful dental care for children depends on understanding how they develop and communicate.
  2. Caries in children
    Caries remains the most common chronic disease of childhood in the UK.
  3. Pulp therapy in primary teeth
    Primary teeth with pulp involvement can often be kept until they exfoliate.
  4. Dental trauma in children
    Dental injuries are common in children and may affect both primary teeth and their developing successors.
  5. Molar–incisor hypomineralisation
    Molar–incisor hypomineralisation is a common developmental enamel defect that causes sensitivity, breakdown and caries.
  6. Safeguarding children in dentistry
    Dental professionals see children regularly and are well placed to recognise abuse and neglect.
  7. Children with additional needs
    Children with medical, physical, learning or sensory needs often have higher oral disease risk and need adapted care.
  8. Growth of the face and jaws
    Facial growth shapes the occlusion and affects the timing of orthodontic treatment.
  9. Development of occlusion
    The occlusion develops from the gum pads of infancy to the permanent dentition.
  10. Classification of malocclusion
    Malocclusions are described with standard classifications so clinicians can communicate and plan care.
  11. The Index of Orthodontic Treatment Need
    The Index of Orthodontic Treatment Need ranks malocclusions by their effect on dental health and appearance.
  12. Orthodontic assessment
    A structured orthodontic assessment gathers the information needed for diagnosis and treatment planning.
  13. Removable and functional appliances
    Removable appliances tip teeth and hold space, while functional appliances use muscle forces to correct jaw relationships.
  14. Fixed appliances and tooth movement
    Fixed appliances give precise, three-dimensional control of tooth movement.
  15. Retention and stability
    Teeth tend to move back after orthodontic treatment, so retention is part of every plan.
  16. Interceptive orthodontics
    Some developing malocclusions can be prevented or reduced by simple, timely treatment.
  17. Cleft lip and palate
    Cleft lip and palate is the most common craniofacial birth anomaly and needs coordinated care from birth to adulthood.

Key terms

Tell-show-do
Explaining, demonstrating, then performing a procedure
Early childhood caries
Caries in children under six years
Pulpotomy
Removal of the coronal pulp leaving radicular pulp vital
Partial pulpotomy
Removal of a few millimetres of inflamed pulp at an exposure, preserving the rest
Hypomineralisation
A qualitative enamel defect with reduced mineral content
Safeguarding
Protecting children from harm and promoting their welfare
Reasonable adjustment
A change made so a disabled person is not at a disadvantage
Intramembranous ossification
Bone formation directly in fibrous tissue
Primate space
Natural spaces mesial to upper and distal to lower primary canines
Overjet
The horizontal distance between upper and lower incisors
Dental Health Component
The IOTN part grading the worst occlusal feature from 1 to 5
Problem list
A summary of patient concerns and skeletal, dental and soft tissue problems
Adams clasp
A retentive clasp on molars or premolars for removable appliances
Hyalinisation
A cell-free, compressed area of PDL caused by heavy force
