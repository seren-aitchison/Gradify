Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What distinguishes mixed methods from multimethod research?

Mixed methods combines qualitative and quantitative strands; multimethod may use several methods of one type. From “What is mixed methods research?” →

In plain English: Mixed methods research combines quantitative and qualitative approaches within one study or programme. Key idea: Mixed methods research combines and integrates qualitative and quantitative approaches for purposes such as triangulation, complementarity, development, initiation and expansion, often grounded in pragmatism, critical realism or transformative frameworks.

What is the role of the qualitative phase in an explanatory sequential design?

To help explain the quantitative results. From “Explanatory sequential designs” →

In plain English: An explanatory sequential design starts with quantitative research and follows it with qualitative research to explain the results. Key idea: Explanatory sequential designs use qualitative follow-up to explain quantitative results, connecting the phases through participant selection and question design, and offer a clear structure at the cost of extra time and follow-up challenges.

Name the four methods-level integration approaches described by Fetters and colleagues.

Connecting, building, merging and embedding. From “Integration and joint displays” →

In plain English: Integration is the defining feature of mixed methods research. Key idea: Integration in mixed methods occurs at design, methods and interpretation levels through connecting, building, merging and embedding, and joint displays present quantitative and qualitative results side by side with meta-inferences.

Who developed meta-ethnography?

George Noblit and R. Dwight Hare. From “Qualitative evidence synthesis” →

In plain English: Qualitative evidence synthesis brings together findings from multiple qualitative studies. Key idea: Qualitative evidence synthesis integrates findings from qualitative studies through methods such as meta-ethnography and thematic synthesis, producing interpretations beyond single studies, and GRADE-CERQual assesses confidence in each finding.

What are the Five Safes?

Safe projects, safe people, safe settings, safe data and safe outputs. From “Secondary data analysis” →

In plain English: Secondary data analysis uses data collected by others to answer new questions. Key idea: Secondary data analysis reuses existing surveys, longitudinal studies, administrative records and archived qualitative data, offering large samples and efficiency but limited control, and requires careful use of documentation, weighting and the Five Safes for secure data.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Mixed Methods and Evidence Synthesis

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which purpose for mixing seeks convergence of results from different methods? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Triangulation. Triangulation compares results from different methods to see whether they agree.

  2. In a convergent design, when are the two strands brought together? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    After each has been analysed separately. Each strand is analysed on its own terms, then results are merged and compared.

  3. Which phase comes first in an explanatory sequential design? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Quantitative. The design begins with quantitative data, which the qualitative phase then explains.

  4. Survey and interview findings unexpectedly contradict each other, leading to a new research question. Which of Greene's purposes does this reflect? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Initiation. Initiation uses paradox and contradiction between methods to open new lines of inquiry.

  5. Qualitative codes are counted and compared with survey percentages. What merging strategy is this? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Data transformation. Quantifying qualitative codes converts one data type into the other for comparison.

  6. A survey identifies some participants with unexpectedly high scores. How might they be used? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Selected for follow-up interviews. Extreme or surprising cases are often chosen for qualitative follow-up to explain results.

  7. A study uses interviews and participant observation and calls itself mixed methods. What is the most accurate judgement? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    It is multimethod qualitative research. Both methods are qualitative, so it is multimethod rather than mixed methods.

  8. A convergent study finds survey scores show no change but interviews describe major benefits. What is the most appropriate response? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Investigate and report possible reasons for the divergence. Exploring measurement, sampling or conceptual reasons for divergence adds insight.

  9. A student's ethics approval covers only the survey phase of an explanatory sequential study. What should she do? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Seek an amendment before interviews. Ethics approval must cover each phase, and phase 2 details may need an amendment.

Key takeaways

  • Mixed methods research combines and integrates qualitative and quantitative approaches for purposes such as triangulation, complementarity, development, initiation and expansion, often grounded in pragmatism, critical realism or transformative frameworks.
  • Convergent designs collect and analyse quantitative and qualitative data in parallel and then merge them through side-by-side comparison, joint displays or data transformation, treating convergence, complementarity and divergence as informative results.
  • Explanatory sequential designs use qualitative follow-up to explain quantitative results, connecting the phases through participant selection and question design, and offer a clear structure at the cost of extra time and follow-up challenges.
  • Exploratory sequential designs begin with qualitative exploration and build a quantitative feature, such as a scale, intervention or hypotheses, which is then tested on a larger sample, ensuring that measures fit the context but requiring time and expertise.
  • Integration in mixed methods occurs at design, methods and interpretation levels through connecting, building, merging and embedding, and joint displays present quantitative and qualitative results side by side with meta-inferences.
  • Mixed methods quality depends on the rigour of each strand and of their integration, and frameworks such as GRAMMS for reporting, MMAT for appraisal and inference quality and legitimation help judge whether combined conclusions are warranted.

Common mistakes in Mixed Methods and Evidence Synthesis

  • Treating any study using two methods as mixed methods. Mixed methods requires both qualitative and quantitative strands and their integration.
  • Treating divergence between strands as a failure. Disagreement often reveals something important and should be explored and reported.
  • Planning the qualitative phase in full before seeing quantitative results. The second phase should be shaped by what the first phase finds.
  • Using the small qualitative sample to validate a new instrument. Validation requires a separate, larger quantitative sample.
  • Leaving integration to the final discussion paragraph. Integration should be planned and documented throughout design, methods and reporting.
  • Assuming a strong survey can compensate for a weak qualitative strand. Each strand must meet its own standards, and integration must be done well.

Lessons in this school

  1. What is mixed methods research?
    Mixed methods research combines quantitative and qualitative approaches within one study or programme.
  2. Convergent designs
    In a convergent design, quantitative and qualitative data are collected and analysed separately and then merged.
  3. Explanatory sequential designs
    An explanatory sequential design starts with quantitative research and follows it with qualitative research to explain the results.
  4. Exploratory sequential designs
    An exploratory sequential design starts with qualitative research and uses the findings to build a quantitative phase.
  5. Integration and joint displays
    Integration is the defining feature of mixed methods research.
  6. Quality in mixed methods research
    Mixed methods studies must meet quality standards for each strand and for their integration.
  7. Meta-analysis
    Meta-analysis statistically combines the results of several studies to estimate an overall effect.
  8. Qualitative evidence synthesis
    Qualitative evidence synthesis brings together findings from multiple qualitative studies.
  9. Realist reviews
    Realist reviews synthesise evidence to explain how and why complex interventions work in different contexts.

    Secondary data analysis uses data collected by others to answer new questions.
  11. Big data and computational methods
    Big data and computational methods allow researchers to analyse very large, often digital, datasets.

    Generative AI tools are increasingly used in research, from searching literature to drafting text.

Mixed Methods and Evidence Synthesis: questions students ask

When are the two strands collected in a convergent design?

In roughly the same phase.

What is built in the middle phase of an exploratory sequential design?

A quantitative feature such as an instrument, intervention or hypotheses.

What is a meta-inference?

A conclusion drawn from integrating quantitative and qualitative findings.

What does GRAMMS stand for?

Good Reporting of A Mixed Methods Study.
