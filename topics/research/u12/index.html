Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

Why give notice of intention to submit?

So that examiners can be appointed in time. From “Preparing to submit” →

In plain English: Submitting a thesis is the culmination of years of work. Key idea: Plan submission with supervisors, allowing time for feedback, revisions and formatting; check argument, regulations, word count, references and declarations; give notice of intention to submit and follow electronic submission and repository steps.

What is a mock viva?

A practice viva with supervisors or colleagues. From “Preparing for the viva” →

In plain English: Preparing well for the viva builds confidence and helps candidates show their expertise. Key idea: Prepare by rereading and annotating the thesis, summarising chapters, noting errors, checking recent literature, anticipating questions on contribution, methods and limitations, reading examiners' work and practising in a mock viva.

Who usually approves corrections?

The internal examiner. From “Viva outcomes and corrections” →

In plain English: The viva ends with a recommendation about the thesis. Key idea: Viva outcomes range from a pass with no corrections to minor or major corrections, revise and resubmit, a lower award or fail; corrections follow a written list, are tracked and documented, and approval leads to the award and repository deposit.

What is a case for support?

The main part of a grant application explaining the research and why it matters. From “Fellowships and grant writing” →

In plain English: Winning research funding is central to many research careers. Key idea: UK early-career fellowships include UKRI, research council, British Academy, Leverhulme and Wellcome schemes; applications include a summary, case for support, impact, budget, data plan and CVs, and should be clear, feasible and tailored to criteria.

Which RDF domain covers public engagement?

Domain D: Engagement, influence and impact. From “Transferable skills and the RDF” →

In plain English: The Researcher Development Framework describes the knowledge, behaviours and attributes of successful researchers. Key idea: The Vitae RDF has four domains: knowledge and intellectual abilities, personal effectiveness, research governance and organisation, and engagement, influence and impact; it supports self-assessment, goal setting and describing transferable skills.

Quick quiz: test yourself on The Viva and Beyond

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. What should students agree with supervisors before submitting? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    A realistic submission date with time for feedback. Agreeing a plan allows full-draft feedback and revisions.

  2. What is one purpose of the viva? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    To confirm the thesis is the candidate's own work. The viva checks authorship and understanding.

  3. Which is useful preparation for the viva? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Rereading and annotating the thesis. Rereading refreshes details and annotations help in the viva.

  4. Which is a final check before submission? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Formatting follows university regulations. Regulations set formatting requirements that must be met.

  5. When are two external examiners usually appointed? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    When the candidate is a member of university staff. Two externals ensure independence for staff candidates.

  6. Which question is very likely in a viva? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    What is your contribution to knowledge?. Contribution is central to doctoral examination.

  7. Why might a student request an embargo on her deposited thesis? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    To allow publication of papers first or protect confidential material. Embargoes delay public access for valid reasons.

  8. What is the role of an independent chair in a viva? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    To ensure the viva is fair and follows procedure. The chair oversees fairness without examining.

  9. Why might reading some of the examiners' published work help? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    It shows their perspectives and likely interests. Understanding examiners' work helps anticipate questions.

Key takeaways

  • Plan submission with supervisors, allowing time for feedback, revisions and formatting; check argument, regulations, word count, references and declarations; give notice of intention to submit and follow electronic submission and repository steps.
  • The viva confirms authorship and assesses understanding and contribution; it involves internal and external examiners, sometimes an independent chair, usually lasts one and a half to three hours and is a conversation about the research.
  • Prepare by rereading and annotating the thesis, summarising chapters, noting errors, checking recent literature, anticipating questions on contribution, methods and limitations, reading examiners' work and practising in a mock viva.
  • In the viva, listen fully, think, answer directly with evidence and page references, defend choices while acknowledging limitations, treat criticism as discussion and manage nerves with pauses, breaks and adjustments.
  • Viva outcomes range from a pass with no corrections to minor or major corrections, revise and resubmit, a lower award or fail; corrections follow a written list, are tracked and documented, and approval leads to the award and repository deposit.
  • UK academic careers include postdoctoral posts, fellowships and lecturer, teaching-focused and research-focused roles; employers look for research, teaching, funding, impact and fit, and the Researcher Development Concordat supports career development.

Common mistakes in The Viva and Beyond

  • Leaving formatting and references to the last day. These checks take longer than expected.
  • Seeing the viva as an attack. Examiners usually want candidates to succeed and to discuss the research in depth.
  • Not rereading the thesis because you wrote it. Months may pass between submission and viva, and details fade.
  • Becoming defensive when challenged. Examiners value thoughtful engagement with criticism.
  • Assuming corrections mean failure. Minor and major corrections are the most common outcomes.
  • Focusing only on publications. Employers also value teaching, funding, impact and citizenship.

The Viva and Beyond: questions students ask

What is an external examiner?

An expert from another institution who examines the thesis.

What should a candidate do if a viva question is unclear?

Ask for clarification.

What is the most common type of viva outcome?

A pass with minor or major corrections.

What does the Researcher Development Concordat commit employers to?

Supporting researchers' professional development, including at least 10 days per year pro rata.
