Lessons in this school

  1. What is research?
    Research is systematic, original investigation that produces new knowledge and shares it so others can check and use it.
  2. An original contribution to knowledge
    A doctorate must make an original contribution to knowledge; a Master's dissertation usually needs to show advanced understanding and some independent analysis.
  3. Disciplines and interdisciplinarity
    Every researcher works within a discipline's questions, methods and standards of evidence.
  4. The UK research system and its funders
    UK university research is funded through a "dual support" system: block funding allocated by research assessment, and competitive project grants from UK Research and Innovation, charities, government and industry.
  5. Research degrees: Master's, MPhil and PhD
    UK postgraduate study includes taught Master's degrees with a dissertation, research Master's (MRes, MPhil) and doctorates.
  6. Professional doctorates and the PhD by publication
    Not every doctorate is a traditional PhD by thesis.
  7. Doctoral training: DTPs, CDTs and studentships
    Much UK doctoral study is funded through studentships, many awarded by UKRI's councils through Doctoral Training Partnerships and Centres for Doctoral Training.
  8. Progression: upgrade and confirmation
    Doctoral students are formally reviewed during their degree, most importantly at the upgrade (also called transfer or confirmation), when an independent panel decides whether the project is on track for a PhD.
  9. Researcher development and the Vitae RDF
    The Vitae Researcher Development Framework (RDF) describes the knowledge, behaviours and attributes of effective researchers in four domains.
  10. The supervisory relationship
    The relationship with your supervisors is the strongest single influence on a research degree experience.
  11. Managing your supervisor
    "Managing your supervisor" means taking responsibility for making supervision work: preparing meetings, asking for the feedback you need and keeping a record.
  12. Regulations, responsibilities and expectations
    Every research degree is governed by the awarding university's regulations and code of practice, which set out registration periods, supervision, progression, examination and the responsibilities of s
  13. Research culture and community
    Research culture is the behaviours, values, expectations and norms of research communities, and it shapes how research is done and who thrives.
  14. Research project management
    A research degree is a long, uncertain project with a fixed deadline, so project management skills matter as much as subject knowledge.
  15. Writing a research proposal
    A research proposal persuades a reader (an admissions panel, a funder or an upgrade panel) that a question matters, can be answered and can be answered by you in the time available.
  16. Planning time: milestones and Gantt charts
    A research degree has a fixed end date and many dependent tasks.
  17. Wellbeing and resilience in research
    Research degrees can be intellectually rewarding and personally demanding.

Key terms

Research
A process of investigation leading to new insights, effectively shared (REF definition)
Original contribution to knowledge
New knowledge, method, evidence or synthesis that extends a discipline, required of a doctorate
Interdisciplinary research
Research that integrates concepts, methods or theories from two or more disciplines
UKRI
UK Research and Innovation: the body, created by the Higher Education and Research Act 2017, that houses the seven research councils, Innovate UK and Research England
MPhil
A research Master's examined by thesis and viva; often the initial registration for doctoral students
Professional doctorate
A level 8 doctorate combining taught elements with research in or about professional practice, such as the EdD or DBA
Doctoral Training Partnership
A UKRI council's block award to a university or consortium to recruit and train doctoral students across a broad area
Upgrade (transfer, confirmation)
The formal review at which a probationary or MPhil-registered student is assessed for continuation to PhD
Vitae RDF
The Researcher Development Framework: four domains, twelve sub-domains and 63 descriptors of effective researchers' knowledge, behaviours and attributes
Principal supervisor
The supervisor with lead responsibility for a research student's academic guidance and progress
Supervision record
A written note of a supervision meeting's decisions and actions, shared by the student
Code of practice
A university's document setting out how research degree supervision, progression and support work, and each party's responsibilities
Research culture
The behaviours, values, expectations, attitudes and norms of research communities
Work package
A defined part of a project with its own tasks, outputs and timescale
