Lessons in this school

  1. Variables and levels of measurement
    Quantitative research measures variables, and the level of measurement determines which statistics are appropriate.
  2. Questionnaire design
    A good questionnaire turns concepts into clear, unbiased questions that respondents can answer accurately.
  3. Measurement scales and psychometrics
    Psychometrics is the science of measuring psychological and social constructs.
  4. Data screening and cleaning
    Before analysis, data must be checked for errors, outliers and violations of assumptions.
  5. Missing data
    Almost every dataset has missing values, and how they are handled can bias results.
  6. Descriptive statistics
    Descriptive statistics summarise the main features of a dataset.
  7. Distributions and the normal curve
    Probability distributions describe how values of a variable are spread.
  8. Visualising data
    Good graphs reveal patterns, outliers and uncertainty that tables can hide.
  9. Sampling distributions and confidence intervals
    A statistic calculated from a sample varies from sample to sample, and its sampling distribution describes that variation.
  10. Null hypothesis significance testing
    Null hypothesis significance testing (NHST) is the most widely used framework for statistical inference.
  11. Effect sizes
    Effect sizes quantify how large an effect or relationship is, independent of sample size.
  12. Statistical power and sample size
    Underpowered studies are common and can mislead even when results are significant.
  13. Type I and Type II errors
    Every significance test risks two kinds of wrong decision.
  14. t-tests
    The t-test compares means between two groups or conditions.
  15. Analysis of variance (ANOVA)
    ANOVA compares means across three or more groups or conditions in a single test.
  16. Chi-square tests
    Chi-square tests analyse categorical data by comparing observed and expected frequencies.
  17. Correlation
    Correlation measures the strength and direction of a relationship between two variables.
  18. Linear regression
    Simple linear regression predicts an outcome from one predictor using a straight line.
  19. Multiple regression
    Multiple regression predicts an outcome from several predictors at once.
  20. Logistic regression
    Logistic regression models binary outcomes such as pass or fail, using odds and odds ratios.
  21. Non-parametric tests
    Non-parametric tests make fewer assumptions about distributions, often by analysing ranks.
  22. Bayesian statistics
    Bayesian statistics updates beliefs about parameters in the light of data using Bayes' theorem.
  23. Statistical software: SPSS, R and Stata
    Postgraduate researchers can choose among several statistical packages.
  24. Reporting statistics
    Clear, complete statistical reporting allows readers to judge and reproduce findings.

Key terms

Confounding variable
A variable related to both predictor and outcome that can distort their apparent relationship
Operationalisation
Turning an abstract concept into measurable indicators
Latent construct
An unobservable concept inferred from multiple indicators
Data screening
Checking data for errors, outliers, missing values and assumption violations
Missing at random
Missingness that depends only on observed variables
Median
The middle value of ordered data
Normal distribution
A symmetric bell-shaped distribution defined by its mean and standard deviation
Data-ink ratio
The proportion of a graph's ink that presents data
Sampling distribution
The distribution of a statistic across many repeated samples
Null hypothesis
A statement of no effect or no difference that a test evaluates
Effect size
A measure of the magnitude of an effect
Statistical power
The probability of detecting an effect of a specified size if it exists
Type I error
Rejecting a true null hypothesis
Degrees of freedom
The number of values free to vary when estimating a statistic
