Lessons in this school

  1. What is research design?
    Research design is the overall plan that connects a research question to the evidence needed to answer it.
  2. Aligning question, philosophy and method
    A coherent study aligns its research question, philosophical assumptions, methodology and methods.
  3. Quantitative, qualitative and mixed approaches
    Research approaches are often grouped as quantitative, qualitative or mixed.
  4. Experimental designs
    Experiments manipulate a variable and use comparison to test causal claims.
  5. Quasi-experimental designs
    Quasi-experiments test interventions without random allocation, often in real-world settings.
  6. Survey research design
    Surveys collect standardised data from a sample to describe a population or examine relationships.
  7. Cross-sectional and longitudinal designs
    Cross-sectional designs collect data at one point in time, while longitudinal designs follow cases over time.
  8. Case study research
    Case study research examines one or a small number of cases in depth within their real-life context.
  9. Ethnography
    Ethnography studies people's everyday lives and cultures through sustained immersion in a setting.
  10. Action research
    Action research combines inquiry with deliberate action to improve practice, usually with participants as co-researchers.
  11. Evaluation research
    Evaluation research judges the design, implementation and effects of programmes, policies and interventions.
  12. Sampling strategies
    Sampling is how researchers select cases from a wider population or setting.
  13. Sample size and saturation
    Deciding how many participants or cases to include depends on the design.
  14. Validity and reliability
    Validity and reliability are central criteria for judging the quality of research, especially quantitative research.
  15. Trustworthiness in qualitative research
    Qualitative researchers often use trustworthiness criteria rather than validity and reliability.
  16. Pilot studies
    Pilot and feasibility studies test whether a study can be carried out as planned before the main study.

Key terms

Research design
The overall plan linking a research question to the evidence needed to answer it
Methodology
The strategy or plan of action that shapes the choice and use of methods
Mixed methods research
Research that combines and integrates quantitative and qualitative strands
Independent variable
The variable the researcher manipulates
Quasi-experiment
A study that tests an intervention without random allocation
Sampling frame
The list or source from which a sample is drawn
Cross-sectional design
Data collected from many cases at one point in time
Case
A bounded unit, such as a person, organisation or event, studied in context
Ethnography
Sustained, immersive study of people's everyday lives and culture
Action research
Research that combines inquiry with deliberate action to improve practice
Formative evaluation
Evaluation aimed at improving a programme during its development
Probability sampling
Sampling in which every case has a known, non-zero chance of selection
Statistical power
The probability of detecting an effect if it truly exists
Reliability
The consistency of a measure
