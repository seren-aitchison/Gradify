Lessons in this school

  1. Asthma in adults
    Asthma is a common inflammatory airways disease causing variable breathlessness, wheeze, cough and chest tightness.
  2. Asthma in children
    Diagnosing and treating asthma in children needs care: many young children wheeze with viral infections without having asthma.
  3. Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease
    COPD causes persistent breathlessness and cough from airflow obstruction, usually due to smoking.
  4. Inhaler devices and technique
    An inhaler only works if the drug reaches the lungs.
  5. Bronchodilators
    Bronchodilators relax airway smooth muscle.
  6. Inhaled corticosteroids
    Inhaled corticosteroids (ICS) control airway inflammation and prevent asthma attacks.
  7. Acute exacerbations
    Asthma attacks and COPD exacerbations can be life-threatening.
  8. Allergic rhinitis and allergy
    Allergic rhinitis (hay fever) is very common and is mostly managed in pharmacies.
  9. Anaphylaxis
    Anaphylaxis is a severe, life-threatening allergic reaction.
  10. Cough and respiratory infections
    Most acute coughs and respiratory infections are viral and self-limiting.
  11. Smoking cessation
    Stopping smoking is the most effective way for smokers to improve their health.

Key terms

Anti-inflammatory reliever (AIR) therapy
Using a combined inhaled corticosteroid and formoterol inhaler as needed for symptom relief
Paediatric low-dose ICS
The lowest inhaled corticosteroid dose band for children, as defined in national guidance
COPD
Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease: persistent, progressive airflow obstruction, confirmed by spirometry
Pressurised metered-dose inhaler (pMDI)
An aerosol inhaler that needs a slow, steady breath coordinated with actuation
LABA
Long-acting beta-2 agonist, such as salmeterol or formoterol
ICS dose band
The low, moderate or high dose category of an inhaled corticosteroid, defined in national guidance
Life-threatening asthma
An attack with features such as SpO₂ below 92%, peak flow below 33% of best, silent chest, cyanosis, exhaustion or confusion
Intranasal corticosteroid
A nasal spray corticosteroid such as beclometasone, fluticasone or mometasone, the most effective treatment for persistent rhinitis
Anaphylaxis
A severe, life-threatening systemic hypersensitivity reaction with airway, breathing or circulation problems
Self-limiting infection
An infection that resolves without specific treatment, such as most colds and acute coughs
Combination NRT
Using a nicotine patch with a short-acting product (gum, lozenge, inhalator, spray) for cravings
