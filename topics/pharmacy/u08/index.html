Key terms and formulas at a glance

Anti-inflammatory reliever (AIR) therapy
Using a combined inhaled corticosteroid and formoterol inhaler as needed for symptom relief
Paediatric low-dose ICS
The lowest inhaled corticosteroid dose band for children, as defined in national guidance
COPD
Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease: persistent, progressive airflow obstruction, confirmed by spirometry
Pressurised metered-dose inhaler (pMDI)
An aerosol inhaler that needs a slow, steady breath coordinated with actuation
LABA
Long-acting beta-2 agonist, such as salmeterol or formoterol
ICS dose band
The low, moderate or high dose category of an inhaled corticosteroid, defined in national guidance
Life-threatening asthma
An attack with features such as SpO₂ below 92%, peak flow below 33% of best, silent chest, cyanosis, exhaustion or confusion
Intranasal corticosteroid
A nasal spray corticosteroid such as beclometasone, fluticasone or mometasone, the most effective treatment for persistent rhinitis
Anaphylaxis
A severe, life-threatening systemic hypersensitivity reaction with airway, breathing or circulation problems
Self-limiting infection
An infection that resolves without specific treatment, such as most colds and acute coughs
Combination NRT
Using a nicotine patch with a short-acting product (gum, lozenge, inhalator, spray) for cravings

Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What is AIR therapy?

Using a combined ICS and formoterol inhaler as needed for relief. From “Asthma in adults” →

In plain English: Asthma is a common inflammatory airways disease causing variable breathlessness, wheeze, cough and chest tightness. Key idea: Under NG245, adults and young people use ICS/formoterol (AIR or MART) rather than SABA alone; frequent reliever use signals poor control.

Which intervention most changes the course of COPD?

Stopping smoking. From “Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease” →

In plain English: COPD causes persistent breathlessness and cough from airflow obstruction, usually due to smoking. Key idea: Smoking cessation, pulmonary rehabilitation and vaccination first; then LAMA plus LABA, or LABA plus ICS if asthmatic features, escalating to triple therapy.

Why is formoterol used for AIR and MART?

It has a fast onset as well as a long duration. From “Bronchodilators” →

In plain English: Bronchodilators relax airway smooth muscle. Key idea: SABAs for relief, LABAs and LAMAs for maintenance; in asthma a LABA is always paired with an ICS. Theophylline needs level and interaction checks.

What is the oxygen target for people with COPD at risk of hypercapnia?

88 to 92%. From “Acute exacerbations” →

In plain English: Asthma attacks and COPD exacerbations can be life-threatening. Key idea: Recognise severe and life-threatening features and call 999; treat with bronchodilators, oxygen to target and oral steroids, and follow up promptly.

How many auto-injectors should people at risk carry?

Two. From “Anaphylaxis” →

In plain English: Anaphylaxis is a severe, life-threatening allergic reaction. Key idea: Anaphylaxis: call 999 and give IM adrenaline promptly, repeating after 5 minutes if needed; people at risk carry two auto-injectors.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Respiratory Therapeutics

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which device is most suitable for a 3-year-old with asthma? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Metered-dose inhaler with spacer and face mask. Young children can't coordinate or inhale forcefully enough for other devices.

  2. How is COPD confirmed? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Post-bronchodilator spirometry. Spirometry shows persistent airflow obstruction.

  3. How should someone breathe in through a pMDI? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Slowly and steadily. A slow, steady breath carries the aerosol into the lungs.

  4. What is first-line treatment for newly diagnosed asthma in a 30-year-old under NG245? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    As-needed low-dose ICS/formoterol. NG245 recommends AIR therapy with ICS/formoterol for people aged 12 and over.

  5. Someone with asthma buys a salbutamol inhaler every two weeks. What should the pharmacist do? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Refer for an urgent asthma review. Frequent reliever use indicates poor control and risk of a severe attack.

  6. What adverse effects of montelukast should parents be told about? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Sleep disturbance and mood or behaviour changes. The MHRA has highlighted neuropsychiatric reactions with montelukast.

  7. Before stepping up asthma treatment, what should be checked first? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Adherence and inhaler technique. Poor technique and adherence are common reasons for apparent treatment failure.

  8. A person on theophylline is prescribed ciprofloxacin. What's the concern? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Theophylline levels may rise to toxic levels. Ciprofloxacin inhibits CYP1A2, raising theophylline levels.

  9. What oxygen saturation target applies to someone with COPD at risk of hypercapnia? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    88 to 92%. Over-oxygenation can worsen carbon dioxide retention.

Key takeaways

  • Under NG245, adults and young people use ICS/formoterol (AIR or MART) rather than SABA alone; frequent reliever use signals poor control.
  • Children's asthma uses paediatric ICS doses, age-appropriate devices with spacers, growth monitoring and a written action plan.
  • Smoking cessation, pulmonary rehabilitation and vaccination first; then LAMA plus LABA, or LABA plus ICS if asthmatic features, escalating to triple therapy.
  • pMDIs need slow, steady breaths (ideally with a spacer); DPIs need quick, deep breaths. Check technique every time and prescribe by brand.
  • SABAs for relief, LABAs and LAMAs for maintenance; in asthma a LABA is always paired with an ICS. Theophylline needs level and interaction checks.
  • ICS reduce asthma attacks; use the lowest effective dose, prescribe beclometasone by brand, and watch for local and high-dose systemic effects.

Common mistakes in Respiratory Therapeutics

  • Supplying repeated salbutamol inhalers to someone without an inhaled corticosteroid, without referring them for review.
  • Giving a child a dry powder inhaler they can't inhale through forcefully enough to use.
  • Starting an ICS in COPD without asthmatic features or frequent exacerbations, adding pneumonia risk without clear benefit.
  • Assuming someone knows how to use an inhaler because they've had it for years.
  • Supplying a LABA-only inhaler for asthma without an inhaled corticosteroid.
  • Switching between beclometasone brands as if equivalent, when extra-fine products are more potent.

Lessons in this school

  1. Asthma in adults
    Asthma is a common inflammatory airways disease causing variable breathlessness, wheeze, cough and chest tightness.
  2. Asthma in children
    Diagnosing and treating asthma in children needs care: many young children wheeze with viral infections without having asthma.
  3. Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease
    COPD causes persistent breathlessness and cough from airflow obstruction, usually due to smoking.
  4. Inhaler devices and technique
    An inhaler only works if the drug reaches the lungs.
  5. Bronchodilators
    Bronchodilators relax airway smooth muscle.
  6. Inhaled corticosteroids
    Inhaled corticosteroids (ICS) control airway inflammation and prevent asthma attacks.
  7. Acute exacerbations
    Asthma attacks and COPD exacerbations can be life-threatening.
  8. Allergic rhinitis and allergy
    Allergic rhinitis (hay fever) is very common and is mostly managed in pharmacies.
  9. Anaphylaxis
    Anaphylaxis is a severe, life-threatening allergic reaction.
  10. Cough and respiratory infections
    Most acute coughs and respiratory infections are viral and self-limiting.
  11. Smoking cessation
    Stopping smoking is the most effective way for smokers to improve their health.

Respiratory Therapeutics: questions students ask

What device is usual for a 4-year-old?

A pressurised metered-dose inhaler with a spacer and face mask.

Why is ICS used selectively in COPD?

It increases pneumonia risk and benefits mainly people with asthmatic features or frequent exacerbations.

Why prescribe inhalers by brand?

Devices and doses aren't interchangeable.

Drug card: Beclometasone dipropionate (inhaled). Class, mechanism and main uses?

Class and mechanism: Inhaled corticosteroid: anti-inflammatory action in the airways via glucocorticoid receptors Uses: Asthma (alone or combined with formoterol); COPD in combination
