Lessons in this school

  1. Hypertension
    High blood pressure is the commonest treatable risk factor for heart attack, stroke, kidney disease and dementia.
  2. Antihypertensive drugs
    NICE's step-wise approach chooses the first drug by age, ethnicity and diabetes, then adds drugs from the main classes.
  3. Lipid management
    Lowering cholesterol, especially LDL and non-HDL cholesterol, reduces heart attacks and strokes.
  4. Cardiovascular risk assessment
    Risk tools estimate a person's chance of a heart attack or stroke over 10 years, helping decide who benefits from treatment.
  5. Stable angina
    Stable angina is chest pain on exertion from narrowed coronary arteries.
  6. Acute coronary syndromes
    Acute coronary syndromes (ACS) are emergencies: unstable angina and heart attacks (NSTEMI and STEMI).
  7. Secondary prevention after a heart attack
    After a heart attack, a set of medicines and lifestyle changes greatly reduces the risk of another event.
  8. Chronic heart failure
    In heart failure the heart can't pump enough blood for the body's needs.
  9. Atrial fibrillation
    Atrial fibrillation (AF) is the commonest heart rhythm disorder and a major cause of stroke.
  10. Antiarrhythmic drugs
    Antiarrhythmic drugs restore or maintain a normal rhythm, but many can also cause arrhythmias and have serious adverse effects.
  11. Venous thromboembolism
    Venous thromboembolism (VTE), meaning deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism, is common and can be fatal.
  12. Anticoagulants
    Anticoagulants prevent and treat clots by acting on the clotting cascade.
  13. Warfarin management
    Warfarin has a narrow therapeutic range, many interactions and is affected by diet and illness, so it needs regular INR monitoring and careful counselling.
  14. Direct oral anticoagulants
    DOACs have largely replaced warfarin for atrial fibrillation and VTE.
  15. Antiplatelet drugs
    Antiplatelet drugs reduce arterial clots, which cause heart attacks and strokes.
  16. Stroke and transient ischaemic attack
    Stroke is a medical emergency: rapid recognition and treatment limit brain damage.
  17. Peripheral arterial disease
    Peripheral arterial disease (PAD) narrows the arteries to the legs, causing pain on walking and, in severe cases, threatening the limb.
  18. Cardiovascular adverse drug reactions
    Many drugs affect the heart and circulation: slowing the heart, prolonging the QT interval, causing fluid retention or raising potassium.

Key terms

Ambulatory blood pressure monitoring (ABPM)
Automatic readings over a day, used to confirm a diagnosis of hypertension
ACE inhibitor
A drug that blocks angiotensin-converting enzyme, reducing angiotensin II (such as ramipril)
Non-HDL cholesterol
Total cholesterol minus HDL cholesterol: a measure of atherogenic lipoproteins
QRISK3
A UK tool estimating 10-year risk of cardiovascular disease from factors such as age, blood pressure, cholesterol, smoking and conditions
Stable angina
Predictable chest pain on exertion or stress, relieved by rest or GTN
STEMI
ST-elevation myocardial infarction: a heart attack with complete coronary occlusion, needing urgent reperfusion
Secondary prevention
Measures to prevent further events in people who already have cardiovascular disease
Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF)
Heart failure with a left ventricular ejection fraction of 40% or less
CHA₂DS₂-VASc
A score estimating stroke risk in atrial fibrillation
Proarrhythmia
An arrhythmia caused or worsened by an antiarrhythmic drug
Deep vein thrombosis (DVT)
A clot in a deep vein, usually of the leg
Vitamin K antagonist
An anticoagulant such as warfarin that reduces production of vitamin K-dependent clotting factors
INR
International normalised ratio: a measure of how long blood takes to clot, used to monitor warfarin
Factor Xa inhibitor
A DOAC inhibiting activated factor X: apixaban, rivaroxaban and edoxaban
