Key terms and formulas at a glance

Registration assessment
The GPhC's national exam taken during or after foundation training, which must be passed to register as a pharmacist
Assessment framework
The GPhC document setting out the outcomes, topics and weightings for the registration assessment
Single best answer (SBA)
A question with a scenario, a lead-in question and several options, where more than one may be partly right but one is best
Extended matching question (EMQ)
A set of questions sharing a theme and an option list, each needing the best match
BNF monograph
The BNF entry for a drug: indications, doses, cautions, interactions, side effects and more
Retrieval practice
Testing yourself to strengthen memory, rather than rereading
Foundation trainee pharmacist
A pharmacy graduate completing the supervised training year before registration
Supervised learning event
A structured activity (such as a case-based discussion or observed consultation) used to give feedback and evidence progress
Feedback
Information about performance aimed at improvement
Fitness to practise declaration
A declaration about health, character and conduct required for registration
Revalidation
The annual process for maintaining GPhC registration by submitting records of learning and reflection

Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

How many questions are in Part 1 of the assessment?

40 calculation questions. From “The GPhC registration assessment” →

In plain English: The registration assessment is the General Pharmaceutical Council's exam that trainees must pass to join the register. Key idea: The assessment has a 40-question calculations paper and a 120-question clinical paper; you get up to three attempts and the pass mark comes from standard setting.

What is a lead-in?

The specific question after the scenario. From “Single best answer questions” →

In plain English: Single best answer (SBA) questions ask you to choose the most appropriate option from several plausible ones. Key idea: Read the lead-in first, answer before looking at options, eliminate, and pick the best answer for the scenario given; answer every question.

Where would you find a drug's renal impairment advice?

In its BNF monograph. From “Using the BNF and resources efficiently” →

In plain English: Fast, accurate use of the BNF and other references is essential in practice and in revision. Key idea: Know the BNF layout (monographs, treatment summaries, appendices), choose the right reference, and read provided extracts carefully.

Who supervises a trainee's prescribing practice?

A designated prescribing practitioner. From “The foundation training year” →

In plain English: The foundation training year is the final stage of initial education and training: a supervised year in practice before registration. Key idea: Foundation training is a 52-week supervised year with progress reviews and a final declaration; from 2025/26 it includes prescribing training.

Where can a trainee go if the supervisory relationship isn't working?

The training lead, NHS England's training team, or the GPhC. From “Working with your designated supervisor” →

In plain English: Your designated supervisor guides, assesses and supports you through the foundation year. Key idea: Supervisors plan, observe, feed back and sign off; trainees should agree expectations, seek feedback, raise issues early and be open about mistakes.

  1. Which paper of the registration assessment covers calculations? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Part 1. Part 1 has 40 calculation questions, answered with an on-screen calculator.

  2. Which document sets out the topics and weightings for the registration assessment? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    The registration assessment framework. The GPhC framework defines what's assessed.

  3. How many options does a typical SBA question have? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Five. Registration assessment SBAs have five options.

  4. How is the pass mark for the assessment decided? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    By a standard-setting process for each sitting. Standard setting reflects the minimum safe standard.

  5. What should a candidate with a disability do before the assessment? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Apply for reasonable adjustments in good time. The GPhC considers adjustment requests made in advance.

  6. What's the best use of the framework during revision? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Map revision to topics and weightings, focusing on weak areas. Planning against the framework covers the whole syllabus.

  7. Which question style should you expect most in Part 2? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Applying knowledge to a patient scenario. The assessment tests applied clinical and professional judgement.

  8. Which revision approach has strong evidence? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Spaced retrieval practice mixed across topics. Spacing, retrieval and interleaving improve long-term learning.

  9. What is a mini-CEX? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    An observed clinical encounter with feedback. It's a supervised learning event observing practice.

Key takeaways

  • The assessment has a 40-question calculations paper and a 120-question clinical paper; you get up to three attempts and the pass mark comes from standard setting.
  • The framework lists outcomes, clinical and professional topics and weightings; map revision to it and expect applied questions.
  • Read the lead-in first, answer before looking at options, eliminate, and pick the best answer for the scenario given; answer every question.
  • EMQs share an option list across scenarios; decide the answer first, remember options can repeat, and revise by themes.
  • Know the BNF layout (monographs, treatment summaries, appendices), choose the right reference, and read provided extracts carefully.
  • Start early, plan with the framework, use retrieval practice, spacing and mocks, and learn from mistakes while protecting your wellbeing.

Common mistakes in Registration Assessment and Foundation Training

  • Assuming the pass mark is always the same percentage between sittings.
  • Revising only favourite topics instead of covering the framework.
  • Choosing an option that's true but doesn't answer the specific lead-in.
  • Ruling out the right answer because it was used in the previous question.
  • Answering from memory when the question provides an extract with different information.
  • Rereading notes repeatedly instead of testing yourself.

Lessons in this school

  1. The GPhC registration assessment
    The registration assessment is the General Pharmaceutical Council's exam that trainees must pass to join the register.
  2. The assessment framework
    The registration assessment framework sets out what can be assessed and how much weight each area carries.
  3. Single best answer questions
    Single best answer (SBA) questions ask you to choose the most appropriate option from several plausible ones.
  4. Extended matching questions
    Extended matching questions (EMQs) share a list of options across several scenarios.
  5. Using the BNF and resources efficiently
    Fast, accurate use of the BNF and other references is essential in practice and in revision.
  6. Revision strategy
    Passing the assessment depends on steady, active revision over months rather than cramming.
  7. The foundation training year
    The foundation training year is the final stage of initial education and training: a supervised year in practice before registration.
  8. Learning outcomes and evidence
    Trainees must show they meet the GPhC learning outcomes by the end of initial training.
  9. Working with your designated supervisor
    Your designated supervisor guides, assesses and supports you through the foundation year.
  10. From trainee to registered pharmacist
    After passing the assessment and completing training, you apply to join the GPhC register.
  11. GPhC revalidation
    Registered pharmacists must revalidate every year to show they keep their practice up to date.
