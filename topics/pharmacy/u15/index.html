Lessons in this school

  1. Children and young people
    Children aren't small adults: their bodies handle medicines differently and change rapidly as they grow.
  2. Neonates
    Neonates, especially premature babies, are the most vulnerable patients.
  3. Pregnancy
    Medicines in pregnancy can affect the fetus, but untreated illness also causes harm.
  4. Breastfeeding
    Most medicines are compatible with breastfeeding, and stopping breastfeeding unnecessarily has harms.
  5. Older people
    Older people take the most medicines and are most vulnerable to their harms.
  6. Prescribing in renal impairment
    Kidney impairment causes many medicines to accumulate, and some worsen kidney function further.
  7. Prescribing in hepatic impairment
    Liver disease alters drug metabolism, protein binding and clotting, but there's no simple test to adjust doses.
  8. Polypharmacy and deprescribing
    Taking many medicines increases the risk of harm, interactions and burden.
  9. Frailty
    Frailty is a state of reduced reserve, making people vulnerable to sudden decline from minor stresses.
  10. People with learning disabilities
    People with learning disabilities have poorer health outcomes and are often overmedicated with psychotropic medicines.
  11. Swallowing difficulties and covert administration
    Many people struggle to swallow tablets, and some lack capacity to agree to medicines.
  12. Care homes
    Care home residents are frail, take many medicines and depend on others to give them.

Key terms

BNF for Children (BNFC)
The UK reference for paediatric medicines, doses and licensing
Neonate
A baby in the first 28 days of life
Teratogen
A substance causing birth defects
Milk to plasma ratio
The ratio of drug concentration in milk to plasma, one indicator of transfer
STOPP/START
Criteria listing potentially inappropriate medicines (STOPP) and potentially omitted medicines (START) in older people
Cockcroft-Gault equation
A formula estimating creatinine clearance from age, weight, sex and serum creatinine
First-pass metabolism
Metabolism of a drug by the liver before it reaches the circulation
Problematic polypharmacy
Multiple medicines where harms or burden outweigh benefits
Frailty
A long-term condition of reduced physiological reserve, increasing vulnerability to minor stressors
Reasonable adjustments
Changes to services that remove barriers for disabled people, required by the Equality Act 2010
Dysphagia
Difficulty swallowing, which affects medicines as well as food
Medication administration record (MAR)
The record of each dose given, refused or omitted
