Lessons in this school

  1. Microbiology for pharmacists
    Choosing an antimicrobial well starts with understanding the organisms that cause infection.
  2. Antibiotic classes and mechanisms
    Antibiotics act on bacterial cell walls, protein synthesis, nucleic acids or metabolism.
  3. Antimicrobial resistance
    Antimicrobial resistance (AMR) makes infections harder to treat and threatens modern medicine.
  4. Antimicrobial stewardship
    Antimicrobial stewardship is a coordinated programme to use antimicrobials well: the right drug, dose, route and duration, reviewed regularly.
  5. Choosing an antibiotic
    Choosing an antibiotic means matching the likely organism and site of infection with the person's factors: allergy, kidney function, pregnancy, interactions and route.
  6. Urinary tract infections
    Urinary tract infections are among the commonest infections managed in primary care and pharmacies.
  7. Respiratory tract infections
    Most respiratory tract infections are viral and self-limiting, but pneumonia and some bacterial infections need antibiotics.
  8. Skin and soft tissue infections
    Skin infections range from impetigo and minor wound infections to cellulitis and bites.
  9. Sepsis
    Sepsis is a life-threatening response to infection that damages the body's own organs.
  10. Clostridioides difficile infection
    C. difficile infection causes diarrhoea and colitis, usually after antibiotics disrupt the gut flora.
  11. Tuberculosis
    Tuberculosis needs months of combination treatment to cure it and prevent resistance.
  12. Fungal infections
    Fungal infections range from common skin, nail and vaginal infections to serious systemic infections in immunocompromised people.
  13. Antiviral drugs
    Antivirals treat a growing range of infections, including herpes viruses, influenza and COVID-19.
  14. HIV
    HIV is now a manageable long-term condition: effective antiretroviral therapy (ART) gives a near-normal life expectancy and prevents transmission.
  15. Viral hepatitis
    Hepatitis B and C cause chronic liver disease, but hepatitis C can now be cured with short courses of tablets and hepatitis B is preventable by vaccination.
  16. Vaccines and immunisation
    Vaccines prevent serious diseases and pharmacies give millions of doses every year.
  17. Travel health
    Travellers may need advice on malaria, vaccines, food and water safety and existing medicines.

Key terms

Gram stain
A staining method dividing bacteria into Gram-positive (thick peptidoglycan wall) and Gram-negative (thin wall with an outer membrane)
Beta-lactam
An antibiotic with a beta-lactam ring that blocks cell wall synthesis: penicillins, cephalosporins, carbapenems
Antimicrobial resistance (AMR)
The ability of microorganisms to survive medicines that would normally kill them or stop their growth
Start Smart then Focus
UKHSA's antimicrobial stewardship toolkit for hospitals
Empirical treatment
Treatment started before the organism is known, based on the likely cause and local resistance
Lower UTI
Infection of the bladder (cystitis), causing frequency, urgency and dysuria
FeverPAIN score
A score (fever, purulence, attend rapidly, inflamed tonsils, no cough) predicting streptococcal sore throat
Cellulitis
A bacterial infection of the deeper skin and tissues, usually streptococcal or staphylococcal
Sepsis
Life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated response to infection
Clostridioides difficile
A spore-forming anaerobic bacterium causing antibiotic-associated diarrhoea and colitis
Active TB
Tuberculosis causing symptoms and disease, needing treatment
Dermatophyte
A fungus infecting skin, hair or nails, such as the causes of athlete's foot and ringworm
Neuraminidase inhibitor
An influenza antiviral such as oseltamivir that stops new virus particles leaving infected cells
Antiretroviral therapy (ART)
Combination treatment that suppresses HIV replication
