Lessons in this school

  1. Depression
    Depression is common, disabling and treatable.
  2. Antidepressants
    Antidepressant classes differ in side effects, interactions and safety in overdose.
  3. Anxiety disorders
    Generalised anxiety disorder, panic disorder and related conditions are common.
  4. Bipolar disorder and lithium
    Bipolar disorder involves episodes of mania or hypomania and depression.
  5. Schizophrenia and psychosis
    Psychosis involves losing touch with reality through hallucinations, delusions or disordered thinking.
  6. Antipsychotics
    Antipsychotics block dopamine D2 receptors and other receptors.
  7. Attention deficit hyperactivity disorder
    ADHD affects attention, impulsivity and activity from childhood, often into adulthood.
  8. Dementia
    Dementia causes progressive decline in memory and thinking.
  9. Insomnia
    Insomnia is common and often linked to stress, health problems or habits.
  10. Substance misuse and harm reduction
    Pharmacies are central to treatment and harm reduction for people who use drugs or alcohol: supervised consumption of opioid substitution therapy, needle and syringe programmes, naloxone and alcohol support.
  11. Epilepsy
    Epilepsy is a tendency to recurrent seizures.
  12. Antiepileptic drugs
    Antiseizure medicines have narrow margins, many interactions and specific serious adverse effects.
  13. Parkinson's disease
    Parkinson's disease causes slowness, stiffness and tremor from dopamine loss.
  14. Migraine
    Migraine causes disabling headaches, often with nausea and sensitivity to light.
  15. Multiple sclerosis
    Multiple sclerosis is an immune-mediated condition damaging the central nervous system.
  16. Pain physiology and assessment
    Pain is a personal experience shaped by biology, psychology and circumstances.
  17. Analgesic ladder and simple analgesics
    Paracetamol and NSAIDs are the foundation of treating acute pain, used at the right dose and duration.
  18. Opioids
    Opioids relieve severe acute and cancer pain but have limited benefit and real harms in long-term non-cancer pain.
  19. Neuropathic pain
    Neuropathic pain responds poorly to standard analgesics.
  20. Palliative care symptom control
    In palliative and end-of-life care, the aim is comfort and dignity.

Key terms

Less severe depression
Depression with fewer or milder symptoms (formerly mild and subthreshold), as categorised by NICE
Serotonin syndrome
A potentially serious reaction to excess serotonergic activity: agitation, tremor, clonus, sweating, fever
Generalised anxiety disorder (GAD)
Excessive, persistent worry about everyday matters, for at least several months
Mood stabiliser
A medicine reducing the frequency and severity of mood episodes, such as lithium
Treatment-resistant schizophrenia
Schizophrenia not responding to adequate trials of at least two antipsychotics
Extrapyramidal side effects (EPSEs)
Movement effects of dopamine blockade: dystonia, parkinsonism, akathisia and tardive dyskinesia
Stimulant
An ADHD medicine increasing dopamine and noradrenaline, such as methylphenidate or lisdexamfetamine
Acetylcholinesterase inhibitor
A drug increasing acetylcholine, such as donepezil, used in mild to moderate Alzheimer's disease
CBT for insomnia (CBT-I)
A structured psychological treatment for chronic insomnia, the first-line treatment
Opioid substitution therapy (OST)
Treatment of opioid dependence with methadone or buprenorphine
Focal seizure
A seizure starting in one area of the brain
Enzyme-inducing antiseizure medicine
A drug such as carbamazepine or phenytoin that induces liver enzymes and lowers levels of other drugs
Levodopa
The precursor of dopamine, given with a decarboxylase inhibitor (co-careldopa or co-beneldopa)
Triptan
A 5-HT1B/1D agonist used for acute migraine, such as sumatriptan
