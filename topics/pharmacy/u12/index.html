Lessons in this school

  1. Dyspepsia and gastro-oesophageal reflux
    Dyspepsia and reflux are very common and often managed in pharmacies.
  2. Peptic ulcer disease and Helicobacter pylori
    Peptic ulcers are mostly caused by H.
  3. Nausea and vomiting
    Nausea and vomiting have many causes, from infections and pregnancy to medicines and raised intracranial pressure.
  4. Constipation
    Constipation is common, especially in older people and those taking opioids.
  5. Diarrhoea
    Acute diarrhoea is usually viral and self-limiting, but dehydration is dangerous, especially in infants and older people.
  6. Inflammatory bowel disease
    Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis are long-term inflammatory conditions with flares and remissions.
  7. Irritable bowel syndrome
    IBS causes abdominal pain with altered bowel habit and bloating, without structural disease.
  8. Acute kidney injury
    Acute kidney injury (AKI) is a sudden fall in kidney function, often caused by illness plus medicines.
  9. Chronic kidney disease
    Chronic kidney disease (CKD) affects millions of people and raises cardiovascular risk.
  10. Electrolyte disorders
    Abnormal sodium, potassium, calcium and magnesium levels can be life-threatening and are often caused or worsened by medicines.
  11. Fluid therapy
    IV fluids are medicines: the wrong type or amount causes harm.
  12. Liver disease and drug handling
    The liver metabolises many drugs, so liver disease changes how medicines work and how safe they are.
  13. Drug-induced liver injury
    Many medicines can damage the liver, from paracetamol overdose to idiosyncratic reactions.
  14. Alcohol-related liver disease
    Alcohol is the commonest cause of liver disease in the UK.

Key terms

Dyspepsia
Upper abdominal pain or discomfort, often with bloating, early fullness or nausea
Helicobacter pylori
A bacterium living in the stomach lining that causes most peptic ulcers
Chemoreceptor trigger zone
An area of the brainstem sensing toxins and drugs in the blood, rich in dopamine and 5-HT3 receptors
Bulk-forming laxative
A laxative such as ispaghula husk that increases stool mass and needs good fluid intake
Oral rehydration salts (ORS)
A glucose and electrolyte solution replacing fluid and salts lost in diarrhoea
Ulcerative colitis
Inflammation of the colon lining, starting at the rectum
Irritable bowel syndrome (IBS)
A long-term functional bowel disorder with pain, bloating and change in bowel habit
Acute kidney injury (AKI)
A rapid fall in kidney function, shown by rising creatinine or falling urine output
eGFR
Estimated glomerular filtration rate, a measure of kidney function from creatinine
Hyperkalaemia
A high blood potassium level, which can cause dangerous heart rhythms
Maintenance fluids
Fluids replacing normal daily losses in people unable to drink enough
Hepatic encephalopathy
Confusion caused by toxins the failing liver can't clear, worsened by some medicines
Drug-induced liver injury (DILI)
Liver damage caused by a medicine, herbal or dietary supplement
Alcohol withdrawal
Symptoms (tremor, anxiety, sweating, seizures, delirium tremens) when a dependent person stops drinking
