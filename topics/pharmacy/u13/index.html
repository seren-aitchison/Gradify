Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

Which enzyme do NSAIDs inhibit?

Cyclo-oxygenase (COX). From “Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs” →

In plain English: NSAIDs relieve pain and inflammation and are among the most widely used medicines, but they cause significant gastrointestinal, kidney and cardiovascular harm. Key idea: NSAIDs cause gastrointestinal, kidney and cardiovascular harm; use the lowest dose for the shortest time, consider topical forms and PPIs, and check interactions.

Which DMARD is usually first line in RA?

Methotrexate. From “Rheumatoid arthritis” →

In plain English: Rheumatoid arthritis is an autoimmune disease causing joint inflammation and damage. Key idea: Refer suspected RA urgently; start DMARDs early, use short steroid courses, and escalate to biologics to reach remission.

How often is methotrexate taken for arthritis?

Once a week. From “Disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs” →

In plain English: DMARDs control inflammatory disease but need careful monitoring. Key idea: DMARDs need regular blood monitoring; methotrexate is weekly with folic acid on another day and one tablet strength; check results before supply.

Name a medicine that can worsen psoriasis.

For example lithium or beta-blockers. From “Psoriasis” →

In plain English: Psoriasis is a long-term immune-mediated skin disease with raised, scaly plaques. Key idea: Use potent steroids with vitamin D analogues for plaques; systemic and biologic treatments are specialist; screen for arthritis, cardiovascular risk and depression.

When should head lice be treated?

Only if live lice are found. From “Skin infections and infestations” →

In plain English: Many skin infections and infestations are treated in pharmacies, including fungal infections, cold sores, warts, scabies and head lice. Key idea: Treat scabies with permethrin for the whole body and all contacts, and head lice only if live lice are seen; avoid wart treatments in diabetes.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Musculoskeletal, Skin, Eye and Inflammatory Conditions

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which COX isoform protects the stomach lining? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    COX-1. COX-1 produces protective prostaglandins in the stomach.

  2. What is a core treatment for osteoarthritis? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Therapeutic exercise. Exercise reduces pain and improves function.

  3. Which drug is usually first-line DMARD in RA? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Methotrexate. Methotrexate is the anchor DMARD and usually the first one offered.

  4. Which person is most at risk of NSAID-induced bleeding? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    A 75-year-old taking an SSRI and warfarin. Age, anticoagulants and SSRIs all increase bleeding risk.

  5. Which medicine should be tried first for knee osteoarthritis pain? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Topical NSAID. NICE recommends topical NSAIDs first for knee osteoarthritis, alongside exercise.

  6. Which joint symptom needs urgent referral? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    A hot, swollen, very painful joint. Septic arthritis or gout must be excluded.

  7. Which NSAID is associated with the highest cardiovascular risk? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Diclofenac. Diclofenac has cardiovascular risk similar to COX-2 inhibitors.

  8. Why is colchicine or an NSAID given when starting allopurinol? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Gout attacks can increase early in urate-lowering therapy. Changing urate levels mobilise crystals, triggering flares.

  9. Which DMARD needs regular eye screening? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Hydroxychloroquine. Hydroxychloroquine can cause retinal toxicity.

Key takeaways

  • NSAIDs cause gastrointestinal, kidney and cardiovascular harm; use the lowest dose for the shortest time, consider topical forms and PPIs, and check interactions.
  • Exercise and weight loss are core; use topical NSAIDs first, then oral NSAIDs with PPI; NICE advises against routine paracetamol alone and strong opioids.
  • Refer suspected RA urgently; start DMARDs early, use short steroid courses, and escalate to biologics to reach remission.
  • Treat attacks with NSAIDs, colchicine or steroids; offer allopurinol treat-to-target with flare cover; review diuretics and lifestyle.
  • DMARDs need regular blood monitoring; methotrexate is weekly with folic acid on another day and one tablet strength; check results before supply.
  • Emollients liberally, topical steroids by potency and fingertip units; warn about fire risk; refer infection and eczema herpeticum.

Common mistakes in Musculoskeletal, Skin, Eye and Inflammatory Conditions

  • Selling ibuprofen to someone with heart failure on furosemide and ramipril.
  • Recommending glucosamine supplements instead of exercise.
  • Treating swollen hand joints for months with ibuprofen alone without referral.
  • Starting allopurinol in someone taking azathioprine without specialist advice.
  • Supplying methotrexate labelled "take 4 tablets daily".
  • Advising someone to use emollient "sparingly" so it lasts longer.

Lessons in this school

  1. Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs
    NSAIDs relieve pain and inflammation and are among the most widely used medicines, but they cause significant gastrointestinal, kidney and cardiovascular harm.
  2. Osteoarthritis
    Osteoarthritis is the commonest joint condition, causing pain and stiffness, mostly in knees, hips and hands.
  3. Rheumatoid arthritis
    Rheumatoid arthritis is an autoimmune disease causing joint inflammation and damage.
  4. Gout
    Gout causes sudden severe joint pain from urate crystals.
  5. Disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs
    DMARDs control inflammatory disease but need careful monitoring.
  6. Eczema
    Atopic eczema causes itchy, dry, inflamed skin and is very common in children.
  7. Psoriasis
    Psoriasis is a long-term immune-mediated skin disease with raised, scaly plaques.
  8. Acne
    Acne affects most teenagers and many adults, and can cause scarring and distress.
  9. Skin infections and infestations
    Many skin infections and infestations are treated in pharmacies, including fungal infections, cold sores, warts, scabies and head lice.
  10. Eye conditions
    Many eye problems are minor, but some threaten sight.
  11. Ear conditions
    Ear problems such as wax, otitis externa and otitis media are common in pharmacy.

Musculoskeletal, Skin, Eye and Inflammatory Conditions: questions students ask

Are strong opioids recommended for osteoarthritis?

No, NICE advises against them.

What monitoring does hydroxychloroquine need?

Eye screening for retinal toxicity.

What is a fingertip unit?

Cream from the fingertip to the first crease, covering two adult palm areas.

What topical combination is used for trunk and limb plaques?

A potent corticosteroid with a vitamin D analogue.
