Lessons in this school

  1. Type 1 diabetes
    Type 1 diabetes is autoimmune destruction of insulin-producing beta cells, so insulin is lifelong and essential.
  2. Insulins
    Insulin is a high-risk medicine: errors with type, strength, device or dose can cause severe hypoglycaemia or ketoacidosis.
  3. Type 2 diabetes
    Type 2 diabetes results from insulin resistance and reduced insulin secretion.
  4. Glucose-lowering drugs
    Each class of glucose-lowering drug has its own benefits, risks and monitoring.
  5. Hypoglycaemia
    Hypoglycaemia is the commonest acute complication of diabetes treatment and can be dangerous.
  6. Diabetes in special situations
    Illness, steroids, pregnancy, older age, fasting and surgery all change glucose control and medicine needs.
  7. Diabetic emergencies
    Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) and hyperosmolar hyperglycaemic state (HHS) are life-threatening emergencies.
  8. Thyroid disorders
    Underactive and overactive thyroid disease are common and treatable.
  9. Adrenal insufficiency and corticosteroids
    Adrenal insufficiency, whether from Addison's disease or long-term steroid use, can lead to a life-threatening adrenal crisis.
  10. Osteoporosis
    Osteoporosis makes bones fragile, so fractures happen after minor falls.
  11. Contraception
    Pharmacists supply, advise on and increasingly provide contraception, including emergency contraception.
  12. Menopause and hormone replacement therapy
    Menopause symptoms affect many people's health and work.
  13. Obesity and weight management
    Obesity increases the risk of type 2 diabetes, heart disease, some cancers and joint problems.

Key terms

Basal-bolus regimen
A long-acting (basal) insulin once or twice daily plus rapid-acting (bolus) insulin with meals
Analogue insulin
A modified insulin with altered absorption, such as rapid-acting aspart or long-acting glargine
HbA1c
Glycated haemoglobin: a measure of average blood glucose over about 2 to 3 months
GLP-1 receptor agonist
A drug mimicking glucagon-like peptide 1, increasing insulin release and satiety, such as semaglutide
Hypoglycaemia
Blood glucose below 4 mmol/L in a person with diabetes
Steroid-induced hyperglycaemia
Raised glucose caused by corticosteroids, typically in the afternoon and evening after a morning dose
Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)
An emergency with high ketones, acidosis and usually high glucose, caused by insulin deficiency
TSH
Thyroid-stimulating hormone: the main blood test for monitoring thyroid replacement
Adrenal crisis
A life-threatening emergency from cortisol deficiency, with vomiting, low blood pressure and collapse
Fragility fracture
A fracture from a fall from standing height or less
UKMEC
UK Medical Eligibility Criteria for Contraceptive Use: categories 1 to 4 for each method and condition
Combined HRT
Oestrogen with a progestogen, needed to protect the womb lining in people with a uterus
Body mass index (BMI)
Weight in kg divided by height in metres squared; obesity is 30 or above, with lower thresholds for some ethnic groups
