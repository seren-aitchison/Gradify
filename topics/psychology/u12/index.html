Lessons in this school

  1. Adaptation
    An adaptation is a trait shaped by natural selection because it helped ancestors survive or reproduce.
  2. Natural selection
    Natural selection is the process by which heritable traits that improve survival and reproduction become more common.
  3. Sexual selection
    Sexual selection is selection for traits that improve mating success.
  4. Evolution of cognition
    Evolutionary psychology asks how the human mind was shaped by selection.
  5. Evolution of emotion
    Emotions may have evolved to help organisms respond to recurrent challenges and opportunities.
  6. Kin selection and inclusive fitness
    Kin selection explains why organisms help relatives, even at a cost to themselves.
  7. Reciprocal altruism
    Reciprocal altruism explains helping non-relatives when help is likely to be returned.
  8. Evolution of cooperation
    Humans cooperate on a scale unusual among animals.
  9. Mating strategies
    Mating strategies are patterns of behaviour for finding and keeping mates.
  10. Parental investment
    Parental investment is the time, energy and resources parents devote to offspring.
  11. Attachment in evolutionary perspective
    Bowlby's attachment theory was built on evolutionary ideas.
  12. Aggression in evolutionary perspective
    Evolutionary psychology explains some aggression as a strategy that helped ancestors gain or defend resources, status and mates.
  13. Social behaviour in evolutionary perspective
    Humans are an intensely social species.
  14. Behavioural ecology
    Behavioural ecology studies how behaviour is adapted to current ecological conditions.
  15. Cultural evolution
    Cultural evolution studies how culture changes through processes of variation, transmission and selection.
  16. Evolutionary developmental psychology
    Evolutionary developmental psychology studies how evolved mechanisms unfold during development and why childhood itself might be adaptive.
  17. Critiques of evolutionary psychology
    Evolutionary psychology is influential but controversial.

Key terms

Adaptation
A trait shaped by natural selection because it solved an ancestral problem
Natural selection
The process by which heritable traits that improve reproduction become more common
Intrasexual selection
Competition within one sex for mates
Modularity
The idea that the mind contains specialised mechanisms for specific problems
Basic emotions
A proposed small set of universal emotions with distinct expressions
Inclusive fitness
Fitness from one's own reproduction plus effects on relatives' reproduction
Reciprocal altruism
Helping non-relatives when help is likely to be returned
Prisoner's Dilemma
A game where individual incentives favour defection but mutual cooperation benefits both
Sexual strategies theory
Buss and Schmitt's theory of sex-differentiated short- and long-term mating strategies
Parental investment
Investment in an offspring that reduces a parent's ability to invest in others
Environment of evolutionary adaptedness
The ancestral environment in which adaptations evolved
Young male syndrome
The high rate of risky and violent behaviour among young men, linked to status competition
Social brain hypothesis
The idea that large brains evolved to manage complex social groups
Behavioural ecology
The study of how behaviour is adapted to current ecological conditions
