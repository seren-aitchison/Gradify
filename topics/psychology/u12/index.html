What is an adaptation?

An inherited trait shaped by natural selection because it solved an ancestral problem of survival or reproduction. From “Adaptation” →

In plain English: An adaptation is a trait shaped by natural selection because it helped ancestors survive or reproduce. Key idea: Adaptations are traits shaped by selection for ancestral problems; not all traits are adaptations, and claims need evidence of special design.

What is the EEA?

The ancestral environment in which human adaptations evolved. From “Evolution of cognition” →

In plain English: Evolutionary psychology asks how the human mind was shaped by selection. Key idea: Evolutionary psychology proposes specialised evolved mechanisms such as cheater detection; critics stress flexible, general and culturally learned cognition.

How do emotions help regulate reciprocity?

Gratitude, guilt and anger encourage returning help and punishing cheaters. From “Reciprocal altruism” →

In plain English: Reciprocal altruism explains helping non-relatives when help is likely to be returned. Key idea: Reciprocal altruism explains helping non-kin when help is returned; it needs repeated interaction and protection against cheaters, which emotions and reputation provide.

Why did Bowlby think attachment evolved?

Staying close to caregivers increased infants' survival. From “Attachment in evolutionary perspective” →

In plain English: Bowlby's attachment theory was built on evolutionary ideas. Key idea: Attachment evolved to keep infants close to caregivers; life history approaches suggest attachment variation may calibrate children to their environments, though evidence is mixed.

How does human behavioural ecology differ from evolutionary psychology?

HBE tests fitness maximisation in current environments; evolutionary psychology studies mechanisms adapted to ancestral ones. From “Behavioural ecology” →

In plain English: Behavioural ecology studies how behaviour is adapted to current ecological conditions. Key idea: Behavioural ecology tests whether behaviour maximises fitness under current conditions, using optimality models; HBE and evolutionary psychology differ in emphasis.

Key takeaways

  • Adaptations are traits shaped by selection for ancestral problems; not all traits are adaptations, and claims need evidence of special design.
  • Natural selection requires variation, inheritance and differential reproduction; fitness is reproductive success; behaviour can evolve if heritable and fitness-relevant.
  • Sexual selection works through competition and choice; parental investment explains sex differences; human evidence is consistent but shaped by social roles.
  • Evolutionary psychology proposes specialised evolved mechanisms such as cheater detection; critics stress flexible, general and culturally learned cognition.
  • Emotions may have evolved functions such as escape, contamination avoidance and negotiation; evidence supports some universality, but constructionists stress cultural shaping.
  • Kin selection explains helping relatives through inclusive fitness; Hamilton's rule (rB > C) predicts when helping evolves; human evidence is consistent but shaped by culture.

Common mistakes in Evolutionary Psychology and Behavioural Ecology

  • Assuming every human trait is an adaptation. Many are by-products, exaptations or culturally learned.
  • Thinking "survival of the fittest" means the strongest win. Fitness is about reproductive success.
  • Assuming sexual selection predicts fixed differences in all individuals. It describes average tendencies with large overlap.
  • Assuming evolved means fixed or present at birth. Evolved mechanisms can develop through experience.
  • Assuming all emotional expressions are identical across cultures. Display rules and concepts vary.
  • Thinking kin selection requires individuals to calculate relatedness. Selection shapes tendencies; no conscious calculation is needed.

Lessons in this school

  1. Adaptation
    An adaptation is a trait shaped by natural selection because it helped ancestors survive or reproduce.
  2. Natural selection
    Natural selection is the process by which heritable traits that improve survival and reproduction become more common.
  3. Sexual selection
    Sexual selection is selection for traits that improve mating success.
  4. Evolution of cognition
    Evolutionary psychology asks how the human mind was shaped by selection.
  5. Evolution of emotion
    Emotions may have evolved to help organisms respond to recurrent challenges and opportunities.
  6. Kin selection and inclusive fitness
    Kin selection explains why organisms help relatives, even at a cost to themselves.
  7. Reciprocal altruism
    Reciprocal altruism explains helping non-relatives when help is likely to be returned.
  8. Evolution of cooperation
    Humans cooperate on a scale unusual among animals.
  9. Mating strategies
    Mating strategies are patterns of behaviour for finding and keeping mates.
  10. Parental investment
    Parental investment is the time, energy and resources parents devote to offspring.
  11. Attachment in evolutionary perspective
    Bowlby's attachment theory was built on evolutionary ideas.
  12. Aggression in evolutionary perspective
    Evolutionary psychology explains some aggression as a strategy that helped ancestors gain or defend resources, status and mates.
  13. Social behaviour in evolutionary perspective
    Humans are an intensely social species.
  14. Behavioural ecology
    Behavioural ecology studies how behaviour is adapted to current ecological conditions.
  15. Cultural evolution
    Cultural evolution studies how culture changes through processes of variation, transmission and selection.
  16. Evolutionary developmental psychology
    Evolutionary developmental psychology studies how evolved mechanisms unfold during development and why childhood itself might be adaptive.
  17. Critiques of evolutionary psychology
    Evolutionary psychology is influential but controversial.

Evolutionary Psychology and Behavioural Ecology: questions students ask

What three conditions does natural selection require?

Variation, inheritance and differential reproduction.

What is the difference between intrasexual and intersexual selection?

Intrasexual is competition within a sex; intersexual is choice by the other sex.

What evolutionary function has been proposed for disgust?

Avoiding contamination and disease.

What is Hamilton's rule?

A gene for helping spreads if rB > C.
