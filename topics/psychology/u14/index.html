What do educational psychologists do?

What did Melby-Lervåg and Hulme find about working memory training?

It showed little transfer to other skills such as classroom learning. From “Individual differences in learning” →

What are Schein's three levels of culture?

What is the prevention paradox?

Lessons in this school

  1. Educational psychology
    Educational psychology applies psychology to learning and teaching.
  2. Achievement and motivation in education
    Pupils' motivation strongly influences their learning.
  3. Behaviour in schools
    Classroom behaviour affects learning and wellbeing.
  4. Assessment in education
    Assessment measures and supports learning.
  5. Individual differences in learning
    Pupils differ in prior knowledge, working memory, attention, language and motivation.
  6. Inclusion and special educational needs
    Inclusion aims to ensure all pupils, including those with special educational needs and disabilities, can learn and participate.
  7. Occupational psychology
    Occupational psychology applies psychology to work and organisations.
  8. Work motivation
    Work motivation is what drives people to put effort into their jobs.
  9. Leadership at work
    Leadership strongly shapes employees' motivation, wellbeing and performance.
  10. Organisational behaviour
    Organisational behaviour studies how people act in organisations: in teams, cultures and during change.
  11. Job satisfaction
    Job satisfaction is how positively people feel about their jobs.
  12. Wellbeing at work
    Work can support or harm wellbeing.
  13. Burnout
    Burnout is a response to chronic workplace stress characterised by exhaustion, cynicism and reduced efficacy.
  14. Human factors and ergonomics
    Human factors and ergonomics design systems, tools and environments to fit human abilities and limits.
  15. Mental workload
    Mental workload is the demand a task places on a person's limited mental resources.
  16. Human–computer interaction
    Human–computer interaction (HCI) studies how people use digital technology and how to design it to be usable, accessible and safe.
  17. Consumer and marketing psychology
    Consumer psychology studies how people choose, buy and use products.
  18. Health psychology
    Health psychology studies how psychological and social factors affect health, illness and healthcare.
  19. Behaviour change
    Behaviour change interventions apply psychology to help people change habits for health, environment or wellbeing.
  20. Psychology and public health
    Psychology contributes to public health by understanding behaviour at population level.
  21. Environmental psychology
    Environmental psychology studies how people and their physical environments affect each other.
  22. Forensic psychology
    Forensic psychology applies psychology to crime and the criminal justice system.
  23. Psychology and the law
    Psychology contributes to the legal system by studying eyewitnesses, interviews, juries and false confessions.
  24. Sport and performance psychology
    Sport and performance psychology applies psychology to improve performance and wellbeing in sport, music and other high-pressure activities.

Key terms

Educational psychologist
A registered practitioner who assesses and supports learning and wellbeing
Expectancy-value theory
The theory that motivation depends on expected success and perceived value
Token economy
A system where tokens earned for desired behaviour can be exchanged for rewards
Formative assessment
Assessment during learning used to guide teaching
Prior knowledge
What learners already know, which supports new learning
EHC plan
A legal document setting out a child's education, health and care needs and provision
Scientific management
Taylor's approach of simplifying and timing tasks to maximise efficiency
Hygiene factors
Features such as pay and conditions that prevent dissatisfaction but don't motivate
Leader–member exchange
A theory focusing on the quality of individual leader–employee relationships
Psychological safety
A shared belief that it is safe to take interpersonal risks in a team
Job satisfaction
A positive evaluation of one's job
Demand–control model
Karasek's model that high demands with low control cause strain
Burnout
A response to chronic workplace stress involving exhaustion, cynicism and reduced efficacy
Ergonomics
Designing systems and tools to fit human abilities and limits
