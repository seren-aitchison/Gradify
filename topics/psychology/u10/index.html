Lessons in this school

  1. Scientific reasoning in psychology
    Scientific reasoning is the way psychologists move from ideas to evidence and back: making claims testable, collecting data systematically and drawing conclusions that go no further than the data allow.
  2. Research questions and hypotheses
    A good study starts with a clear research question and a precise hypothesis.
  3. Operationalisation
    Operationalisation turns an abstract concept, such as stress or aggression, into something that can be measured.
  4. Independent and dependent variables
    In an experiment, the independent variable is what the researcher changes and the dependent variable is what they measure.
  5. Confounding variables
    A confounding variable changes along with the independent variable, so it offers an alternative explanation for the results.
  6. Experimental design
    Experimental design is how participants are allocated to conditions: independent groups, repeated measures or matched pairs.
  7. Laboratory experiments
    Laboratory experiments manipulate an IV in a controlled setting, allowing strong causal conclusions at a possible cost to realism.
  8. Field experiments
    Field experiments manipulate an IV in a natural setting, often without participants knowing they are in a study.
  9. Natural experiments
    In a natural experiment, the independent variable changes naturally, without the researcher manipulating it, and the researcher studies its effects.
  10. Quasi-experiments
    A quasi-experiment compares groups that differ on a pre-existing characteristic, such as age, sex or diagnosis, which the researcher can't assign.
  11. Correlational research
    Correlational research measures two or more variables to see whether they are related, without manipulating anything.
  12. Cross-sectional and longitudinal research
    To study change over time, researchers either compare different age groups at one point (cross-sectional) or follow the same people over years (longitudinal).
  13. Case studies
    A case study is an in-depth investigation of one person, group or event, usually using several methods.
  14. Naturalistic observation
    Naturalistic observation records behaviour as it happens in its everyday setting, without interfering.
  15. Structured observation
    Structured observation uses predefined behavioural categories and sampling methods so that observations are systematic, countable and reliable.
  16. Surveys and questionnaires
    Surveys collect self-report data from many people using questionnaires.
  17. Interviews
    Interviews collect self-report data through conversation, from fully structured to unstructured.
  18. Focus groups
    A focus group is a guided discussion with a small group about a topic, used to explore views and how people talk about an issue together.
  19. Quantitative, qualitative and mixed methods
    Quantitative research measures and counts; qualitative research explores meanings in words and experiences; mixed methods combine them.
  20. Random and stratified sampling
    Sampling decides who takes part.
  21. Opportunity and volunteer sampling
    Opportunity and volunteer samples are quick and common in psychology, but they are rarely representative.
  22. Sample size
    How many participants a study needs depends on the size of the effect it is looking for and how sure the researchers want to be of detecting it.
  23. Reliability
    Reliability is the consistency of a measure: whether it gives the same results under the same conditions.
  24. Validity
    Validity is whether a study or measure actually captures what it claims to.
  25. Generalisability
    Generalisability is how far findings extend beyond the specific people, place and time studied.
  26. Bias in research
    Bias is systematic error that pushes results in a particular direction.
  27. Replication
    Replication means repeating a study to see whether its findings hold.
  28. Preregistration
    Preregistration means publicly recording a study's hypotheses, design and analysis plan before collecting or analysing data.
  29. Open science
    Open science is a set of practices that make research transparent, accessible and checkable: sharing data, materials, code and papers, and making plans public.
  30. Informed consent
    Informed consent means participants agree to take part knowing what the research involves, its risks and their rights.
  31. Confidentiality
    Confidentiality means protecting participants' personal information and ensuring they can't be identified in reports.
  32. Deception and debriefing
    Deception means misleading participants about a study, and debriefing means explaining the study fully afterwards.
  33. Vulnerable participants
    Some participants need extra protection because their capacity to consent is limited or they face greater risk: children, people with some cognitive impairments or mental health conditions, prisoners
  34. Research governance and ethics committees
    Research governance is the system of rules, review and accountability that oversees research.

Key terms

Deduction
Reasoning from a general theory to a specific, testable prediction
Directional hypothesis
A prediction that states the direction of a difference or relationship
Operationalisation
Defining a variable in terms of how it will be measured or manipulated
Independent variable
The variable the researcher manipulates
Confounding variable
A variable that changes systematically with the IV, offering an alternative explanation
Independent groups design
Different participants take part in each condition
Laboratory experiment
An experiment in a controlled setting, with a manipulated IV and standardised procedures
Field experiment
An experiment in a natural setting in which the researcher manipulates the IV
Natural experiment
A study in which the IV varies naturally, not manipulated by the researcher
Quasi-experiment
A study comparing groups that differ on a pre-existing characteristic not assigned by the researcher
Correlation coefficient
A number from −1 to +1 showing the strength and direction of a linear relationship
Cohort effect
Differences between age groups caused by the different times in which they grew up
Case study
An in-depth investigation of a single individual, group or event
Naturalistic observation
Recording behaviour in its normal setting without intervention
