What are the aims of clinical assessment?

To understand difficulties, history, context, strengths, goals and risks in order to agree a plan. From “Clinical assessment” →

In plain English: Clinical assessment gathers information to understand a person's difficulties and decide how to help. Key idea: Clinical assessment aims to understand difficulties, context, strengths and risks collaboratively; methods include interviews, questionnaires, observation, testing and information from others.

What share of referrals finishing treatment in NHS Talking Therapies moved to recovery in 2024–25?

50.5%. From “Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT)” →

In plain English: Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) is a structured psychological therapy that helps people change unhelpful patterns of thinking and behaviour. Key idea: CBT links thoughts, emotions, sensations and behaviours; it is structured, time-limited and collaborative; techniques include thought records, behavioural experiments and activation; evidence is strong for many conditions.

What are Rogers's three core conditions?

Empathy, unconditional positive regard and congruence. From “Humanistic and person-centred therapy” →

In plain English: Person-centred therapy, developed by Carl Rogers, holds that people can grow when offered a supportive relationship. Key idea: Person-centred therapy offers empathy, unconditional positive regard and congruence; it is non-directive; its emphasis on the relationship is widely influential, though the conditions may not be sufficient for all problems.

How many referrals did NHS Talking Therapies receive in 2024–25?

1.81 million. From “Psychological treatment of depression” →

In plain English: Several psychological therapies help people with depression. Key idea: NICE recommends a range of therapies for depression; comparative studies find small differences, supporting common factors; Talking Therapies reported 50.5% recovery in 2024–25; stepped care and choice matter.

What are the three parts of Bordin's working alliance?

Agreement on goals, agreement on tasks, and the emotional bond. From “The therapeutic relationship” →

In plain English: The therapeutic relationship is a key ingredient of effective therapy across approaches. Key idea: Bordin's working alliance involves goals, tasks and bond; meta-analyses show a consistent moderate link between alliance and outcome; ruptures can be repaired; relationship and techniques both matter.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Clinical Psychology and Interventions

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. A plan agreed with someone for staying safe in a crisis is a… (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Safety plan. Safety plans are developed collaboratively.

  2. An interview with set questions asked in a fixed order is… (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Structured. Structured interviews standardise questions.

  3. A person's understanding of their own situation is recorded in the MSE as… (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Insight. Insight concerns understanding of one's situation.

  4. A psychologist uses an interview, the PHQ-9 and a conversation with a GP (with consent). This shows… (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Combining assessment methods. Combining several sources of information gives a fuller and more reliable picture.

  5. In the 5 Ps model, what is a precipitating factor? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Something that triggered the current difficulty. Precipitating factors are recent triggers; perpetuating factors maintain problems.

  6. A clinician asks "What has been most difficult for you?" and then "Have you lost weight?" This shows… (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Combining open and closed questions. Skilled interviewers mix open questions for depth with closed questions for detail.

  7. Why is cultural humility important in assessment? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Misreading culture can bias how difficulties are interpreted. Assessment must understand the person's context.

  8. What is a weakness of fully structured interviews in practice? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    They can feel impersonal and miss the person's priorities. Fixed questions may overlook what matters most.

  9. Why should the MSE be read alongside history? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    It captures one moment that context can affect. A snapshot of one moment needs the longer history to be interpreted properly.

Key takeaways

  • Clinical assessment aims to understand difficulties, context, strengths and risks collaboratively; methods include interviews, questionnaires, observation, testing and information from others.
  • Clinical interviews may be unstructured, semi-structured or structured; structured interviews such as the SCID improve reliability; skills include active listening, open and closed questions, reflecting and summarising.
  • The MSE describes appearance, speech, mood and affect, thought, perception, cognition, insight and risk at one point in time; it uses descriptive language and complements history.
  • Clinical tests include cognitive tests, symptom measures and personality measures; good tests are reliable, valid and standardised with appropriate norms; scores support but don't replace clinical judgement.
  • Formulation is a collaborative, revisable explanation of difficulties; the 5 Ps framework covers presenting, predisposing, precipitating, perpetuating and protective factors; it complements diagnosis.
  • CBT links thoughts, emotions, sensations and behaviours; it is structured, time-limited and collaborative; techniques include thought records, behavioural experiments and activation; evidence is strong for many conditions.

Common mistakes in Clinical Psychology and Interventions

  • Assuming assessment is a one-off event. Understanding develops throughout care.
  • Assuming structured interviews are always best. They increase reliability but can miss the person's priorities.
  • Treating an MSE as a complete assessment. It is a snapshot to be read with the history.
  • Treating a questionnaire score as a diagnosis. Scores support clinical judgement.
  • Treating formulation as fixed. It is a working hypothesis revised over time.
  • Assuming CBT is just "positive thinking". It tests thoughts against evidence and changes behaviour.

Lessons in this school

  1. Clinical assessment
    Clinical assessment gathers information to understand a person's difficulties and decide how to help.
  2. Clinical interviews
    The clinical interview is the main way clinicians learn about a person's difficulties.
  3. Mental state assessment
    A mental state examination (MSE) is a structured description of a person's presentation at the time of assessment.
  4. Psychological testing in clinical practice
    Psychological tests measure aspects of cognition, personality and symptoms in a standardised way.
  5. Formulation
    A formulation is a shared explanation of how a person's difficulties developed and are maintained.
  6. Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT)
    Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) is a structured psychological therapy that helps people change unhelpful patterns of thinking and behaviour.
  7. Behavioural therapy
    Behavioural therapies change behaviour directly using principles of learning.
  8. Exposure therapy
    Exposure therapy helps people reduce fear by gradually and safely facing what they fear.
  9. Acceptance and commitment therapy (ACT)
    Acceptance and commitment therapy (ACT) helps people relate differently to difficult thoughts and feelings while acting on their values.
  10. Dialectical behaviour therapy (DBT)
    Dialectical behaviour therapy (DBT) was developed for people with intense emotions and self-harm.
  11. Psychodynamic therapy
    Psychodynamic therapy explores unconscious feelings, relationship patterns and the past to help people understand and change current difficulties.
  12. Humanistic and person-centred therapy
    Person-centred therapy, developed by Carl Rogers, holds that people can grow when offered a supportive relationship.
  13. Family therapy
    Family therapy works with families and relationships rather than only with individuals.
  14. Group therapy
    Group therapy brings together several people with shared difficulties, guided by one or more therapists.
  15. Interpersonal therapy (IPT)
    Interpersonal therapy (IPT) is a time-limited therapy that focuses on the links between mood and relationships.
  16. Trauma-focused therapies
    Trauma-focused therapies help people process traumatic memories and change the meanings they attach to trauma.
  17. Psychological treatment of depression
    Several psychological therapies help people with depression.
  18. Psychological treatment of anxiety disorders
    Anxiety disorders are among the most treatable mental health conditions.
  19. Psychological treatment of OCD
    Obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD) responds well to specific psychological treatments.
  20. Psychological treatment of PTSD
    Psychological treatment is the first-line approach for PTSD.
  21. Psychological interventions for psychosis
    Psychological interventions can help people with psychosis alongside medication and other support.
  22. Relapse prevention
    Relapse prevention helps people maintain progress after therapy.
  23. The therapeutic relationship
    The therapeutic relationship is a key ingredient of effective therapy across approaches.
  24. Clinical supervision
    Clinical supervision is a formal relationship in which an experienced practitioner supports and oversees another's clinical work.
  25. Measuring therapy outcomes
    Measuring outcomes shows whether therapy is helping.
  26. Evidence-based practice
    Evidence-based practice combines the best research evidence with clinical expertise and the person's values and preferences.
  27. Ethics in clinical practice
    Clinical psychologists follow ethical principles to protect clients and the public.
  28. Professional boundaries
    Professional boundaries keep the therapeutic relationship safe and focused on the client.

Clinical Psychology and Interventions: questions students ask

What is the advantage of a structured clinical interview?

It improves reliability by asking set questions in a fixed order.

What is the difference between mood and affect in an MSE?

Mood is the person's own description; affect is the clinician's observation of emotional expression.

What are norms in psychological testing?

Scores from a large reference group used to interpret an individual's result.

What is a formulation?

A shared, revisable explanation of how a person's difficulties developed and are maintained.
