Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What is a script?

A schema for a familiar sequence of events, such as eating at a restaurant. From “Social cognition” →

In plain English: Social cognition is how people think about themselves, other people and social situations. Key idea: Social cognition relies on schemas and a mix of automatic and controlled thinking, which is efficient but can bias judgement.

What are the two routes in the ELM?

The central route (careful thought) and the peripheral route (superficial cues). From “Attitude change and persuasion” →

In plain English: Persuasion is deliberately changing attitudes through communication. Key idea: Persuasion works through careful thought (central route) or cues (peripheral route); source, message and audience factors shape it.

What is the difference between descriptive and injunctive norms?

Descriptive norms describe what people do; injunctive norms describe what people approve of. From “Social norms” →

In plain English: Social norms are shared expectations about how people behave. Key idea: Descriptive norms say what people do and injunctive norms what they should do; combining them can change behaviour without boomerang effects.

What is social creativity?

Achieving a positive identity by comparing on a different dimension or with a different group. From “Social identity theory” →

In plain English: Social identity theory explains how group memberships become part of who we are and shape intergroup behaviour. Key idea: Social identity comes from group memberships; even minimal groups produce in-group favouritism as people seek positive distinctiveness.

What did Philpot et al. find?

In about 90% of filmed public conflicts, at least one bystander intervened. From “Bystander behaviour” →

In plain English: Bystander behaviour is how people respond when they witness an emergency. Key idea: The presence of others can reduce each person's likelihood of helping, but in real incidents someone usually intervenes.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Social Psychology

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Overestimating how many people share your opinions is the… (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    False consensus effect. We assume others think as we do more often than they do.

  2. In Asch's study, which pair of traits changed impressions most? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Warm and cold. Swapping warm for cold changed the whole impression, so they were central traits.

  3. "He failed because he didn't revise" is an attribution to… (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Effort. Effort is internal, unstable and controllable.

  4. A new employee assumes meetings will start with small talk, then an agenda. Which concept is this? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    A script. Expectations about event sequences are scripts.

  5. An interviewer rates an attractive candidate as more competent with the same CV. This is the… (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Halo effect. Attractiveness spilled over into competence judgements.

  6. Everyone dislikes a restaurant, Jo always dislikes it, and Jo likes most restaurants. Kelley's model suggests… (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    An external attribution to the restaurant. High consensus, consistency and distinctiveness point to the restaurant.

  7. Why is the "motivated tactician" view more complete than the "cognitive miser" view? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    People switch strategy depending on goals. People choose effortful or quick thinking as needed.

  8. Which is a fair conclusion about teacher expectancy effects? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    They are real but usually small. Reviews suggest small, real effects, larger in some groups.

  9. Why might attributional retraining help struggling students? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Effort attributions encourage persistence. Seeing failure as changeable through effort supports trying again.

Key takeaways

  • Social cognition relies on schemas and a mix of automatic and controlled thinking, which is efficient but can bias judgement.
  • Impressions form quickly, are shaped by central traits, order and halo effects, and expectations can be partly self-fulfilling.
  • Attributions explain behaviour as internal or external; covariation information and Weiner's dimensions shape conclusions and motivation.
  • People overweight dispositions when explaining others, favour themselves in explaining outcomes, and these biases vary across cultures.
  • Attitudes have affective, behavioural and cognitive parts, form through exposure, conditioning and learning, and predict behaviour under specific conditions.
  • Persuasion works through careful thought (central route) or cues (peripheral route); source, message and audience factors shape it.

Common mistakes in Social Psychology

  • Assuming famous priming findings are solid. Several high-profile social priming effects haven't replicated.
  • Treating fast impressions as accurate. Confidence in snap judgements often exceeds their accuracy.
  • Thinking people always use covariation information systematically. People often have incomplete information and use shortcuts.
  • Calling the FAE universal. It is weaker in some cultures and situations, which is one reason "fundamental" has been questioned.
  • Assuming attitudes always predict behaviour. Norms, control and the situation also matter.
  • Assuming more fear always persuades more. Fear works best paired with clear steps people believe they can take.

Lessons in this school

  1. Social cognition
    Social cognition is how people think about themselves, other people and social situations.
  2. Social perception and impressions
    People form impressions of others quickly from small amounts of information.
  3. Attribution
    Attribution is how people explain the causes of behaviour.
  4. The fundamental attribution error and self-serving bias
    Attributions are often biased in predictable ways.
  5. Attitude formation
    An attitude is an evaluation of a person, object or idea.
  6. Attitude change and persuasion
    Persuasion is deliberately changing attitudes through communication.
  7. Cognitive dissonance
    Cognitive dissonance is the discomfort felt when beliefs and actions conflict.
  8. Conformity
    Conformity is changing behaviour or beliefs to match a group.
  9. Obedience
    Obedience is following orders from an authority figure.
  10. Compliance
    Compliance is going along with a direct request.
  11. Social norms
    Social norms are shared expectations about how people behave.
  12. Group dynamics
    Group dynamics are the processes that operate within groups.
  13. Group decision-making
    Groups often make decisions together, sometimes better and sometimes worse than individuals.
  14. Leadership
    Leadership is influencing a group towards shared goals.
  15. Power
    Power is the capacity to influence others and control resources.
  16. Social identity theory
    Social identity theory explains how group memberships become part of who we are and shape intergroup behaviour.
  17. Self-categorisation
    Self-categorisation theory explains when people see themselves as individuals and when as group members.
  18. In-groups and out-groups
    An in-group is a group we belong to; an out-group is one we don't.
  19. Prejudice, stereotyping and discrimination
    Stereotypes are beliefs about groups, prejudice is an attitude towards them, and discrimination is unfair behaviour.
  20. Intergroup relations and conflict
    Intergroup conflict arises between groups competing for resources, status or identity.
  21. Bystander behaviour
    Bystander behaviour is how people respond when they witness an emergency.
  22. Prosocial behaviour and altruism
    Prosocial behaviour is any action that benefits others; altruism is helping without expectation of reward.
  23. Aggression
    Aggression is behaviour intended to harm someone who wants to avoid it.
  24. Attraction
    Interpersonal attraction is the liking that draws people together.
  25. Close relationships
    Close relationships include friendships, romantic partnerships and family bonds.
  26. Culture and social behaviour
    Culture shapes how people think, feel and behave.

Internal explains behaviour by the person; external by the situation.
