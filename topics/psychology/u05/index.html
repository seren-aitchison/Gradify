Lessons in this school

  1. Prenatal development
    Prenatal development runs from conception to birth.
  2. Infancy
    Infancy is the first two years of life, a time of rapid physical, perceptual and social development.
  3. Childhood
    Childhood, from about 2 to 11 years, brings major growth in thinking, self-control and social understanding.
  4. Adolescence
    Adolescence is the transition from childhood to adulthood, marked by puberty, brain development and social change.
  5. Adulthood and ageing
    Adulthood brings continued development in relationships, work, personality and well-being.
  6. Lifespan perspectives
    Lifespan perspectives treat development as continuing from conception to death and shaped by many interacting influences.
  7. Piaget's theory of cognitive development
    Piaget's theory proposes that children actively construct understanding through four stages.
  8. Vygotsky and sociocultural development
    Vygotsky argued that children's thinking develops through social interaction and cultural tools.
  9. Information-processing approaches to development
    Information-processing approaches explain cognitive development as gradual improvements in speed, capacity, strategies and knowledge.
  10. Language development
    Language development runs from early speech perception to complex grammar and conversation.
  11. Emotional development
    Emotional development is how children come to express, understand and regulate emotions.
  12. Social development
    Social development is how children learn to relate to others.
  13. Bowlby's attachment theory
    Attachment is a deep, lasting emotional bond to a caregiver.
  14. Ainsworth and the Strange Situation
    Ainsworth developed the Strange Situation to assess the quality of infant attachment.
  15. Measuring attachment
    Attachment can be measured at different ages using observation, interviews and questionnaires.
  16. Caregivers and parenting styles
    Caregivers shape development through warmth, responsiveness and control.
  17. Peers and friendship
    Peers become increasingly important through childhood and adolescence.
  18. Family influences
    Families shape development through relationships, conflict, structure and economic circumstances.
  19. Identity development
    Identity is a coherent sense of who you are, what you value and where you are going.
  20. Erikson's psychosocial stages
    Erikson proposed eight stages of psychosocial development across the lifespan, each centred on a conflict.
  21. Moral development: Kohlberg
    Kohlberg proposed that moral reasoning develops through six stages grouped into three levels.
  22. Risk and resilience
    Resilience is positive adaptation despite significant adversity.
  23. Neurodevelopment
    Neurodevelopment is how the brain develops from before birth to adulthood.
  24. Neurodevelopmental conditions
    Neurodevelopmental conditions begin in development and affect how the brain processes information.
  25. Developmental psychopathology
    Developmental psychopathology studies how mental health difficulties emerge and change across development.
  26. Developmental research methods
    Developmental researchers study change over time, which needs special designs.

Key terms

Embryonic stage
Weeks 3–8 of pregnancy, when major organs form
Reflex
An automatic response present at birth
Theory of mind
Understanding that others have beliefs and desires different from one's own
Puberty
Biological changes leading to sexual maturity
Emerging adulthood
Arnett's proposed stage of identity exploration from about 18 to 29
Multidirectional
Development involving both gains and losses
Equilibration
Restoring balance between schemas and experience, driving development
Cultural tools
Language, number systems and other resources passed on by a culture
Processing speed
How quickly cognitive operations are carried out
Statistical learning
Detecting patterns, such as syllable sequences, from input
Social referencing
Using others' emotional reactions to guide one's own behaviour
Joint attention
Sharing attention with another person on the same object
Monotropy
Bowlby's idea that one primary attachment is more important than others
Strange Situation
Ainsworth's procedure for assessing infant attachment
