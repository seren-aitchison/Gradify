Lessons in this school

  1. Organisation of the nervous system
    The nervous system is the body's network for sending, receiving and processing signals.
  2. The central nervous system
    The central nervous system is the brain and spinal cord.
  3. The peripheral nervous system: somatic and autonomic
    The peripheral nervous system links the central nervous system with the rest of the body.
  4. Sympathetic and parasympathetic divisions
    The autonomic nervous system has two branches that usually work in opposition: the sympathetic prepares the body for action and the parasympathetic restores calm.
  5. Neurone structure and types
    Neurones are the cells that carry information in the nervous system.
  6. The resting potential
    A neurone at rest holds an electrical charge across its membrane.
  7. Action potentials
    An action potential is the brief electrical impulse that travels along an axon.
  8. Synaptic transmission
    Synaptic transmission is how one neurone passes a signal to another, usually by releasing a chemical across a tiny gap.
  9. Neurotransmitters and receptors
    Neurotransmitters are the chemical messengers of the nervous system.
  10. Excitatory and inhibitory signalling
    Every neurone receives a mix of excitatory and inhibitory input and fires only when the balance tips past threshold.
  11. Brain anatomy: an overview
    The brain is organised into regions with broadly different roles, working together as networks.
  12. The cerebral cortex and the four lobes
    The cerebral cortex is divided into four lobes, each linked to broad functions.
  13. The limbic system: hippocampus and amygdala
    The limbic system is a set of structures involved in emotion, motivation and memory.
  14. Hypothalamus and thalamus
    The hypothalamus and thalamus are small structures in the middle of the brain with large roles.
  15. Basal ganglia and cerebellum
    The basal ganglia and cerebellum both help control movement, in different ways.
  16. Lateralisation of function
    Some functions are handled more by one cerebral hemisphere than the other.
  17. Neuroplasticity
    Neuroplasticity is the brain's ability to change its structure and function through experience and after injury.
  18. Neurogenesis
    Neurogenesis is the creation of new neurones.
  19. Hormones and the endocrine system
    The endocrine system uses hormones carried in the blood to send slower, longer-lasting signals than the nervous system.
  20. Circadian rhythms
    Circadian rhythms are cycles of about 24 hours in body and behaviour, such as the sleep–wake cycle.
  21. Sleep
    Sleep is a regular state of reduced awareness with distinct stages.
  22. Genetics and behaviour
    Genes influence behaviour, but always in interaction with the environment.
  23. Epigenetics
    Epigenetics studies changes in how genes are expressed without changes to the DNA sequence.
  24. Evolution and the brain
    Evolutionary psychology explains brain and behaviour in terms of natural selection.
  25. Brain imaging: EEG, MRI, fMRI and PET
    Brain imaging lets researchers study the living brain's structure and activity.
  26. Lesion studies
    Lesion studies examine what happens when part of the brain is damaged or temporarily disrupted.
  27. Psychopharmacology
    Psychopharmacology studies how drugs affect mind and behaviour.
  28. Biological explanations of mental disorders
    Biological explanations link mental disorders to genes, brain structure and neurochemistry.

Key terms

Central nervous system
The brain and spinal cord
Meninges
Three protective membranes around the brain and spinal cord
Somatic nervous system
The part of the PNS controlling voluntary movement and carrying sensation
Sympathetic division
The autonomic branch that prepares the body for action
Dendrite
A branch that receives signals from other neurones
Resting potential
The charge across a neurone's membrane at rest, about −70 mV
Threshold
The membrane potential at which an action potential is triggered, about −55 mV
Synaptic cleft
The small gap between presynaptic and postsynaptic neurones
Agonist
A substance that activates a receptor, mimicking a neurotransmitter
EPSP
An excitatory postsynaptic potential that depolarises the membrane
Cerebral cortex
The folded outer layer of the cerebrum
Motor cortex
The frontal lobe strip controlling voluntary movement
Hippocampus
A medial temporal lobe structure for forming memories and navigation
Hypothalamus
A small structure regulating homeostasis and the pituitary
