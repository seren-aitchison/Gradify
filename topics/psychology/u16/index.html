Lessons in this school

  1. Cognitive neuroscience today
    Cognitive neuroscience links mental processes to brain activity.
  2. Computational psychology
    Computational psychology uses mathematical and computer models to explain how the mind works.
  3. Computational psychiatry
    Computational psychiatry uses models of brain and behaviour to understand mental health conditions.
  4. Artificial intelligence and psychology
    Psychology and artificial intelligence (AI) influence each other: psychology inspired AI, and AI is now a tool and a subject for psychological research.
  5. Digital mental health
    Digital mental health uses apps, online therapy and other technologies to support mental health.
  6. Social media and psychology
    Social media are part of daily life for most young people.
  7. The psychology of technology
    Technology changes how we remember, pay attention and relate to others.
  8. Human–computer interaction today
    Human–computer interaction (HCI) applies psychology to the design of technology.
  9. Neuroeconomics
    Neuroeconomics combines neuroscience, psychology and economics to study how the brain makes decisions.
  10. Behavioural economics
    Behavioural economics uses psychology to explain why people's economic decisions depart from traditional models of rational choice.
  11. Decision science
    Decision science studies how people and organisations make decisions and how decisions can be improved.
  12. Predictive processing
    Predictive processing proposes that the brain constantly predicts incoming sensory information and updates its predictions when errors occur.
  13. Embodied cognition
    Embodied cognition proposes that thinking depends on the body and its interaction with the environment, not just on abstract processing in the brain.
  14. Bayesian cognition
    Bayesian models describe the mind as combining prior knowledge with new evidence according to probability theory.
  15. Network approaches to mental health
    Network approaches see mental health conditions as systems of symptoms that influence each other, rather than as hidden underlying diseases.
  16. Psychoneuroimmunology
    Psychoneuroimmunology (PNI) studies how psychological processes, the nervous system and the immune system interact.
  17. Behavioural genetics
    Behavioural genetics studies how genetic and environmental differences contribute to differences in behaviour.
  18. Epigenetics and behaviour
    Epigenetics studies changes in how genes are expressed without changes to the DNA sequence.
  19. Open science and the replication crisis
    In the 2010s, psychology faced a replication crisis when many well-known findings failed to replicate.
  20. Current debates in psychology
    Psychology is a lively, contested science.
  21. Future directions
    Psychology is changing through large-scale collaboration, new technology and broader participation.

Key terms

Statistical power
The probability that a study detects a real effect
Computational model
A theory specified precisely enough to run as a program
Computational psychiatry
Using computational models to understand mental health conditions
Connectionism
Modelling cognition with networks of simple units
iCBT
Cognitive behavioural therapy delivered online
Upward social comparison
Comparing oneself with people who seem better off
Transactive memory
A shared memory system in which others or tools store information
Affordance
What an object allows a user to do
Neuroeconomics
The study of the brain processes underlying decisions
Loss aversion
Losses feeling larger than equivalent gains
Dual-process theory
The view that thinking involves fast intuitive and slow deliberative systems
Predictive processing
The view that the brain predicts input and updates on prediction errors
Embodied cognition
The view that thinking depends on the body and its interaction with the world
Prior
A belief held before new evidence
