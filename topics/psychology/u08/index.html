Lessons in this school

  1. Defining abnormality
    Psychologists have proposed several ways to define abnormality, each with strengths and limits.
  2. A history of psychopathology
    Ideas about mental distress have changed greatly over time.
  3. The biological model
    The biological model explains psychological difficulties in terms of genes, brain structure and neurochemistry.
  4. The cognitive model
    The cognitive model explains psychological difficulties in terms of thoughts, beliefs and information processing.
  5. The behavioural model
    The behavioural model explains psychological difficulties as learned through conditioning.
  6. The psychodynamic model
    The psychodynamic model explains psychological difficulties in terms of unconscious conflicts and early experiences.
  7. The humanistic model
    The humanistic model sees psychological difficulties as arising when people's needs for growth and self-acceptance are blocked.
  8. The diathesis–stress model
    The diathesis–stress model proposes that psychological difficulties arise when an underlying vulnerability meets stressful circumstances.
  9. The biopsychosocial model
    The biopsychosocial model explains psychological difficulties through the interaction of biological, psychological and social factors.
  10. Classification: DSM and ICD
    Diagnostic classification systems group symptoms into named conditions.
  11. Reliability and validity of diagnosis
    A good diagnosis should be reliable (consistent between clinicians) and valid (meaningful and useful).
  12. Generalised anxiety disorder
    Generalised anxiety disorder (GAD) involves persistent, hard-to-control worry about many areas of life.
  13. Panic disorder
    Panic disorder involves recurrent, unexpected panic attacks and worry about further attacks.
  14. Specific phobias
    A specific phobia is an intense, persistent fear of a particular object or situation.
  15. Social anxiety disorder
    Social anxiety disorder involves intense fear of being judged or embarrassed in social situations.
  16. Obsessive-compulsive disorder
    Obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD) involves intrusive, distressing thoughts and repetitive behaviours performed to reduce distress.
  17. Trauma and post-traumatic stress disorder
    Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) can develop after exposure to a traumatic event.
  18. Depression
    Depression involves persistent low mood or loss of interest, with effects on thinking, energy and daily life.
  19. Bipolar disorder
    Bipolar disorder involves episodes of mania or hypomania, usually alongside episodes of depression.
  20. Schizophrenia and psychosis
    Psychosis involves losing touch with shared reality, through hallucinations or delusions.
  21. Personality disorders
    Personality disorders describe long-standing patterns of thinking, feeling and relating that cause significant difficulties.
  22. Eating disorders
    Eating disorders involve serious disturbances in eating and in thoughts about weight and shape.
  23. Substance use disorders
    Substance use disorders involve continued use of alcohol or drugs despite significant harm.
  24. Autism
    Autism is a lifelong neurodevelopmental difference affecting social communication and how people experience the world.
  25. ADHD
    Attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) is a neurodevelopmental condition involving inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity.
  26. Sleep disorders
    Sleep disorders affect the quality, timing or amount of sleep and have wide effects on health.
  27. Dissociative disorders
    Dissociation involves disruptions in the normal integration of memory, identity, perception and awareness.
  28. Somatic symptom disorders
    Somatic symptom disorders involve distressing physical symptoms with excessive thoughts, feelings or behaviours about them.
  29. Risk and protective factors
    Risk factors increase the likelihood of psychological difficulties, while protective factors reduce it.
  30. Comorbidity
    Comorbidity is the occurrence of two or more conditions in the same person.
  31. Stigma
    Mental health stigma involves negative stereotypes, prejudice and discrimination towards people with mental health difficulties.
  32. Culture and psychopathology
    Culture shapes how psychological difficulties are expressed, understood and responded to.

Key terms

Statistical infrequency
Defining abnormality as rare behaviour
Moral treatment
Humane, respectful care with routine and activity
Reductionism
Explaining complex behaviour by its simplest parts
Cognitive triad
Negative views of the self, the world and the future
Two-process model
Fear acquired through classical conditioning and maintained through operant conditioning
Unconscious
Mental processes outside awareness
Incongruence
A gap between self-concept and experience or ideal self
Diathesis
An underlying vulnerability to difficulties
Biopsychosocial model
A model combining biological, psychological and social factors
DSM-5-TR
The American Psychiatric Association's diagnostic manual, text revision (2022)
Inter-rater reliability
Agreement between clinicians diagnosing the same person
Intolerance of uncertainty
Finding uncertainty very hard to bear
Panic attack
A sudden surge of intense fear with physical symptoms
Specific phobia
An intense, persistent fear of a particular object or situation
