Lessons in this school

  1. Intraspecific and interspecific competition
    Competition occurs when organisms use the same limited resource and harm each other in the process.
  2. The competitive exclusion principle
    The competitive exclusion principle states that two species competing for exactly the same limiting resources can't coexist indefinitely.
  3. Resource partitioning and niche differentiation
    Species that coexist often divide up resources between them.
  4. Predation
    Predation is the consumption of one organism by another.
  5. Predator–prey dynamics
    Predator and prey populations affect each other and can cycle together.
  6. Herbivory and plant defences
    Herbivores eat plants, and plants have evolved many defences in response.
  7. Parasitism and disease
    Parasites live on or in a host, taking resources and usually harming it without killing it quickly.
  8. Mutualism
    Mutualism is an interaction in which both species benefit.
  9. Commensalism and facilitation
    Not all interactions involve both costs and benefits.
  10. What is an ecological community?
    An ecological community is all the populations of different species living and interacting in an area.
  11. Keystone species and trophic cascades
    Some species have effects on their communities far greater than their abundance would suggest.
  12. Species richness and evenness
    Biodiversity within a community has two parts: how many species there are and how evenly individuals are spread among them.
  13. Island biogeography
    The theory of island biogeography explains why larger, closer islands hold more species.
  14. Ecological succession
    Succession is the change in the species composition of a community over time.
  15. Primary and secondary succession
    Succession can begin on bare ground with no soil or on land where soil remains after disturbance.
  16. Climax communities and plagioclimax
    Succession tends towards a relatively stable climax community, but human activity often holds communities at earlier stages.
  17. Disturbance and the intermediate disturbance hypothesis
    Disturbances such as storms, fire and grazing disrupt communities and reset succession.
  18. Invasive non-native species
    Invasive non-native species are introduced outside their natural range and cause harm to ecosystems, the economy or health.

Key terms

Interspecific competition
Competition between individuals of different species for a limiting resource
Competitive exclusion principle
Two species competing for the same limiting resources can't coexist indefinitely
Fundamental niche
The full range of conditions and resources a species could use without competitors
Aposematism
Warning coloration signalling that prey is toxic or dangerous
Numerical response
The change in predator population size in response to changing prey density
Secondary metabolite
A plant chemical not needed for basic metabolism, often used in defence
Macroparasite
A large parasite that grows but usually doesn't multiply within its host
Mutualism
An interaction in which both species gain a net benefit
Commensalism
An interaction where one species benefits and the other is unaffected
Ecological community
All the populations of different species in an area that interact with each other
Keystone species
A species with a disproportionately large effect on its community relative to its abundance
Species richness
The number of different species in a community
Species–area relationship
The increase in number of species with the area sampled, often S = cA^z
Succession
Directional change in community composition over time
