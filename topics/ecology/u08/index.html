Lessons in this school

  1. Weather and climate
    Weather is what happens in the atmosphere from day to day; climate is the long-term pattern of weather.
  2. The atmosphere
    Earth's atmosphere is a thin layer of gases that regulates temperature and redistributes heat.
  3. Earth's energy balance
    Earth's temperature depends on the balance between energy arriving from the Sun and energy leaving as infrared radiation.
  4. The greenhouse effect
    Greenhouse gases keep Earth habitable by trapping outgoing infrared radiation.
  5. Greenhouse gases
    Different greenhouse gases come from different sources, last for different times and trap different amounts of heat.
  6. Radiative forcing
    Radiative forcing measures how much a change, such as more CO2 or more aerosols, alters Earth's energy balance.
  7. The instrumental record
    Thermometers, tide gauges, satellites and ocean floats provide direct measurements of a changing climate.
  8. Past climates and proxies
    Natural archives such as ice cores, tree rings and sediments record climate long before thermometers.
  9. Attribution of climate change
    Attribution is the science of identifying the causes of observed climate change.
  10. Climate feedbacks
    Feedbacks amplify or dampen the warming caused by a forcing.
  11. Tipping points
    Some parts of the climate system may change abruptly and irreversibly once a threshold is crossed.
  12. Climate models and scenarios
    Climate models use physics to simulate the climate system and project how it will respond to future emissions.
  13. Sea-level rise
    Global sea level is rising and will keep rising for centuries.
  14. Ocean warming and acidification
    The oceans have absorbed most of the extra heat and a quarter or more of the carbon dioxide released by humans.
  15. Phenology and mismatch
    Phenology is the timing of seasonal events such as flowering, breeding and migration.
  16. Range shifts
    As climate warms, many species are moving towards the poles, uphill and into deeper water.
  17. Coral reefs under climate change
    Coral reefs are among the most diverse and most threatened ecosystems on Earth.
  18. Climate change and extinction risk
    Climate change is becoming a major driver of extinction alongside habitat loss, exploitation and invasive species.
  19. Mitigation
    Mitigation means reducing the causes of climate change by cutting greenhouse gas emissions and increasing removals.
  20. Adaptation
    Adaptation means adjusting to actual or expected climate change to reduce harm or exploit opportunities.
  21. Nature-based solutions
    Nature-based solutions use the protection, management and restoration of ecosystems to tackle societal challenges such as climate change.

Key terms

Weather
The state of the atmosphere at a given time and place
Troposphere
The lowest layer of the atmosphere, where weather occurs
Albedo
The fraction of incoming sunlight reflected by a surface or planet
Greenhouse gas
A gas that absorbs and emits infrared radiation
Global warming potential
The warming caused by a tonne of a gas relative to a tonne of CO2 over a set period
Radiative forcing
A change in Earth's energy balance caused by an external factor, in W/m2
Temperature anomaly
The difference between a measured temperature and a baseline average
Proxy
A natural record, such as an ice core or tree ring, used to infer past climate
Attribution
Assessing the relative contributions of different causes to an observed change or event
Feedback
A process in which a change causes effects that alter the original change
Tipping point
A threshold beyond which a system changes abruptly and often irreversibly
Climate model
A computer simulation of the climate system based on physical laws
Thermal expansion
The increase in volume of water as it warms
Ocean acidification
The decline in ocean pH caused by absorption of atmospheric CO2
