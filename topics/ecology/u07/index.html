Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check.

Why did Soulé call conservation biology a crisis discipline?

Because it must act before complete evidence is available, as delay risks irreversible loss. From “What is conservation biology?” →

What is an Allee effect?

Reduced population growth at low density, for example from difficulty finding mates. From “Minimum viable populations” →

Why is yellow rattle sown in grassland restoration?

It is a semi-parasite that weakens vigorous grasses, letting wildflowers establish. From “Habitat restoration” →

Why does climate change make connectivity more important?

Species need to move to track suitable conditions, which isolated patches prevent. From “Wildlife corridors and landscape connectivity” →

What does NEOLI stand for?

No-take, well enforced, old, large and isolated. From “Marine conservation” →

Lessons in this school

  1. What is conservation biology?
    Conservation biology is the science of protecting biodiversity.
  2. Conservation values and ethics
    Why should we conserve nature?
  3. Conservation genetics
    Small populations lose genetic variation, which can threaten their survival.
  4. Minimum viable populations
    How many individuals does a species need to survive?
  5. Protected areas
    Protected areas are the cornerstone of conservation.
  6. UK protected sites: SSSIs, SACs and SPAs
    The UK protects its most important wildlife sites through national and international designations.
  7. Habitat management
    Many valuable habitats depend on continuing management to stop them changing.
  8. Habitat restoration
    Restoration aims to return degraded ecosystems towards a healthier state.
  9. Rewilding
    Rewilding aims to restore natural processes rather than manage for particular species.
  10. Species reintroductions
    Reintroductions return species to places where they were lost.
  11. Wildlife corridors and landscape connectivity
    Fragmented habitats leave wildlife isolated.
  12. Zoos and captive breeding
    Some species survive only because of captive breeding.
  13. Seed banks and botanic gardens
    Plants can be conserved as seeds, living collections and tissue cultures.
  14. Conservation on farmland
    Farmland covers about 70% of the UK's land, so how it is farmed shapes much of its wildlife.
  15. Marine conservation
    The oceans face overfishing, pollution, habitat damage and climate change.
  16. International conservation agreements
    Biodiversity crosses borders, so conservation needs international cooperation.
  17. Evidence-based conservation
    Conservation resources are limited, so actions should be chosen on the basis of evidence of what works.
  18. Community-based conservation
    Conservation succeeds or fails with the people who live alongside wildlife.

Key terms

Conservation biology
The applied science of protecting and restoring biodiversity
Intrinsic value
Value that something has in itself
Genetic drift
Random change in allele frequencies between generations
Minimum viable population
The smallest population with a high probability of long-term survival
Protected area
A defined space managed for long-term conservation of nature
SSSI
Site of Special Scientific Interest, the main UK site designation
Succession
The gradual change in community composition over time
Ecological restoration
Assisting recovery of a degraded ecosystem
Rewilding
Restoring natural processes and letting ecosystems develop with minimal intervention
Reintroduction
Returning a species to an area of its former range
Fragmentation
The breaking up of habitat into smaller, isolated patches
In situ conservation
Protecting species in their natural habitat
Seed bank
A facility storing seeds under dry, cold conditions to conserve plant diversity
Agri-environment scheme
A payment scheme rewarding farmers for environmental management
