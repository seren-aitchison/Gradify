Lessons in this school

  1. The scientific method in ecology
    Ecologists answer questions about nature by observing, forming hypotheses, testing them and revising their ideas.
  2. Hypotheses and null hypotheses
    Statistical tests in ecology start with a null hypothesis of no effect.
  3. Experimental and observational studies
    Ecologists can either manipulate the system or observe natural variation.
  4. Replication and pseudoreplication
    Replication lets ecologists distinguish real effects from natural variation.
  5. Fieldwork ethics and safety
    Fieldwork must be safe for people and responsible towards wildlife, habitats and landowners.
  6. Random, systematic and stratified sampling
    Ecologists rarely count every organism, so they take samples that should represent the whole area.
  7. Quadrats
    Quadrats are frames used to sample plants and slow-moving animals in a defined area.
  8. Transects
    Transects are lines along which samples are taken to show how organisms change across an environmental gradient.
  9. Measuring abundance: frequency, cover and ACFOR
    Ecologists use several measures of abundance, from precise counts to quick abundance scales.
  10. Sampling animals
    Mobile and hidden animals need special sampling methods.
  11. Freshwater sampling and biotic indices
    Freshwater invertebrates are sampled to assess water quality, because different groups tolerate pollution differently.
  12. Measuring abiotic factors
    Abiotic factors help explain where organisms live.
  13. Types of data and variables
    The type of data collected determines how it can be presented and analysed.
  14. Descriptive statistics
    Descriptive statistics summarise a set of data.
  15. Standard deviation and standard error
    Standard deviation describes how spread out data are, and standard error describes how precisely a sample mean estimates the population mean.
  16. Choosing a statistical test
    The right statistical test depends on the question, the type of data and its distribution.
  17. The chi-squared test
    The chi-squared test compares observed frequencies with those expected under a null hypothesis.
  18. Spearman's rank correlation
    Spearman's rank correlation tests whether two variables measured on the same samples tend to increase or decrease together.
  19. The t-test
    The t-test compares the means of two groups of normally distributed data.
  20. The Mann–Whitney U test
    The Mann–Whitney U test compares two independent samples without assuming normal distributions.
  21. Remote sensing and GIS
    Satellites, aircraft and drones collect data on ecosystems across whole landscapes, and geographic information systems (GIS) analyse them.
  22. Environmental DNA
    Organisms shed DNA into water, soil and air, and ecologists can detect species from these traces.
  23. Citizen science
    Members of the public collect large amounts of ecological data through citizen science projects.

Key terms

Hypothesis
A testable explanation for an observation
Null hypothesis
A statement that there is no difference, association or correlation
Manipulative experiment
A study in which the researcher deliberately changes a factor
Replicate
An independent experimental unit receiving a treatment
Risk assessment
A process identifying hazards and measures to reduce the risk of harm
Random sampling
Selecting sample positions so that each point has an equal chance of being chosen
Frame quadrat
A square frame marking a sample area
Belt transect
Quadrats placed continuously along a line to record change across a habitat
ACFOR scale
An abundance scale: abundant, common, frequent, occasional, rare
Pitfall trap
A container sunk into the ground to catch walking invertebrates
Indicator species
A species whose presence or absence reflects environmental conditions
Data logger
An electronic device that records measurements automatically over time
Ordinal data
Data that can be ranked but without equal intervals between ranks
Median
The middle value of ordered data
