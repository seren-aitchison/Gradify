Lessons in this school

  1. Defining biodiversity
    Biodiversity is the variety of life at every level.
  2. Genetic, species and ecosystem diversity
    Biodiversity exists at three linked levels.
  3. Species concepts
    Defining what a species is turns out to be difficult.
  4. Classification and naming
    Classification organises the diversity of life, and scientific names allow precise communication.
  5. Variation and natural selection
    Natural selection is the main mechanism of adaptive evolution.
  6. Adaptation and fitness
    Adaptations are traits shaped by natural selection that improve survival and reproduction.
  7. Speciation
    Speciation is the formation of new species.
  8. Coevolution
    Coevolution occurs when interacting species drive each other's evolution.
  9. The history of life and mass extinctions
    Life has evolved over more than 3.5 billion years, punctuated by mass extinctions.
  10. The latitudinal diversity gradient
    For most groups of organisms, species richness rises from the poles to the tropics.
  11. Biodiversity hotspots
    Biodiversity hotspots are regions rich in endemic species and under severe threat.
  12. Endemism
    Endemic species are found nowhere else.
  13. Measuring biodiversity: diversity indices
    Diversity indices combine species richness and evenness into a single number.
  14. Biodiversity in the UK
    The UK is one of the most nature-depleted countries in the world.
  15. The global state of biodiversity
    Biodiversity is declining worldwide at unprecedented rates.
  16. Drivers of biodiversity loss
    Human activities drive biodiversity loss through a small number of major pathways.
  17. Extinction risk and the IUCN Red List
    The IUCN Red List is the world's most comprehensive assessment of extinction risk.
  18. Why biodiversity matters
    People value biodiversity for many reasons, from practical benefits to moral and cultural values.

Key terms

Biodiversity
The variety of life, including diversity within species, between species and of ecosystems
Genetic diversity
The variety of genes and alleles within a species
Biological species concept
Species as interbreeding populations reproductively isolated from others
Binomial nomenclature
The two-part scientific naming system of genus and species
Natural selection
Differential survival and reproduction of individuals due to heritable traits
Adaptation
A heritable trait that improves survival or reproduction, shaped by selection
Reproductive isolation
Barriers that prevent gene flow between populations
Coevolution
Reciprocal evolutionary change between interacting species
Mass extinction
The loss of a large proportion of species in a geologically short time
Latitudinal diversity gradient
The increase in species richness from the poles to the tropics
Biodiversity hotspot
A region with at least 1,500 endemic plant species and at least 70% habitat loss
Endemism
The occurrence of species found naturally in only one place
Simpson's index
A diversity index based on the probability that two individuals belong to different species
State of Nature
A regular UK report on the status of wildlife
