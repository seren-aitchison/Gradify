Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What three levels does the CBD definition of biodiversity include?

Diversity within species, between species and of ecosystems. From “Defining biodiversity” →

In plain English: Biodiversity is the variety of life at every level. Key idea: Biodiversity, defined by the CBD as variability within species, between species and of ecosystems, was popularised by E. O. Wilson; about 2 million species are described out of perhaps 8.7 million eukaryotes.

What are the three domains of life?

Bacteria, Archaea and Eukarya. From “Classification and naming” →

In plain English: Classification organises the diversity of life, and scientific names allow precise communication. Key idea: Linnaeus's binomial system gives each species a genus and species name; organisms are classified in ranks from domain to species, in three domains, and modern classification reflects common ancestry.

What is coevolution?

Reciprocal evolutionary change in interacting species. From “Coevolution” →

In plain English: Coevolution occurs when interacting species drive each other's evolution. Key idea: Coevolution is reciprocal adaptation between interacting species, seen in arms races between predators, prey, parasites and hosts, and in mutualisms such as orchids and moths; the Red Queen hypothesis says species must keep evolving to keep pace.

What share of the Earth's land did Myers's 25 hotspots cover?

About 1.4%. From “Biodiversity hotspots” →

In plain English: Biodiversity hotspots are regions rich in endemic species and under severe threat. Key idea: Hotspots combine high plant endemism (at least 1,500 species) with severe habitat loss (at least 70%); they hold a disproportionate share of endemic species, guiding conservation investment, though they leave out some groups and intact areas.

About how many species did IPBES say are threatened with extinction?

About 1 million. From “The global state of biodiversity” →

In plain English: Biodiversity is declining worldwide at unprecedented rates. Key idea: The IPBES assessment warns that about 1 million species face extinction and most land is altered; the Living Planet Index shows an average 73% decline in monitored populations; the Kunming–Montreal framework sets 2030 targets including 30 by 30.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Biodiversity and Evolution

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Who popularised the term biodiversity? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    E. O. Wilson. Wilson edited the 1988 book BioDiversity.

  2. Which level of biodiversity is the variety of habitats in a landscape? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Ecosystem diversity. Ecosystem diversity spans habitats and communities.

  3. Which species concept is used for fossils? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Morphological. Applying the standard result: Fossils can be compared only by form.

  4. Roughly how many species have been formally described? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    About 2 million. Applying the standard result: Around 2 million species are described.

  5. Why did reliance on one potato variety worsen the Irish famine? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    All plants were equally susceptible to blight. Genetic uniformity meant no plants resisted the disease.

  6. Two bat populations look identical but use different call frequencies and do not interbreed. What are they? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Cryptic species. They are distinct species that look alike.

  7. Why are estimates of total species numbers uncertain? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Many groups, especially insects, microbes and deep-sea life, are poorly sampled. Undersampled groups make extrapolation difficult.

  8. A population of 5,000 animals descends from 20 survivors of a crash. What is likely? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Low genetic diversity despite its current size. Bottlenecks reduce genetic variation that later growth does not restore.

  9. Why do hybridising plants challenge the biological species concept? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Gene flow between recognised species blurs reproductive isolation. The concept relies on isolation, which hybrids break.

Key takeaways

  • Biodiversity, defined by the CBD as variability within species, between species and of ecosystems, was popularised by E. O. Wilson; about 2 million species are described out of perhaps 8.7 million eukaryotes.
  • Genetic diversity allows adaptation and resistance to disease, species diversity combines richness and evenness, and ecosystem diversity covers the variety of habitats; loss at any level weakens the others.
  • The biological species concept defines species by reproductive isolation, while morphological, phylogenetic and ecological concepts use form, ancestry or niche; each has limits, especially for asexual organisms, hybrids and cryptic species.
  • Linnaeus's binomial system gives each species a genus and species name; organisms are classified in ranks from domain to species, in three domains, and modern classification reflects common ancestry.
  • Natural selection requires heritable variation, overproduction and differential survival; it shifts allele frequencies over generations, as shown in peppered moths and Darwin's finches.
  • Adaptations are heritable traits shaped by selection; fitness is reproductive success; trade-offs, constraints and time lags mean adaptations are rarely perfect, and some traits are co-opted for new uses.

Common mistakes in Biodiversity and Evolution

  • Equating biodiversity with the number of species. It also includes genetic variation within species and the diversity of ecosystems.
  • Assuming a large population always has high genetic diversity. Populations that passed through bottlenecks can remain genetically uniform even after recovery.
  • Assuming species always look different. Cryptic species can be almost identical in appearance.
  • Writing scientific names without italics or with a capitalised species name. The convention is Genus species.
  • Saying organisms evolve "because they need to". Variation arises randomly; selection favours individuals that happen to be better suited.
  • Equating fitness with physical strength. Evolutionary fitness means leaving more surviving offspring.

  8. Coevolution
    Coevolution occurs when interacting species drive each other's evolution.

    Biodiversity hotspots are regions rich in endemic species and under severe threat.

    Endemic species are found nowhere else.

    The UK is one of the most nature-depleted countries in the world.
  15. The global state of biodiversity
    Biodiversity is declining worldwide at unprecedented rates.
  16. Drivers of biodiversity loss
    Human activities drive biodiversity loss through a small number of major pathways.
  17. Extinction risk and the IUCN Red List
    The IUCN Red List is the world's most comprehensive assessment of extinction risk.
  18. Why biodiversity matters
    People value biodiversity for many reasons, from practical benefits to moral and cultural values.

Biodiversity and Evolution: questions students ask

What is species evenness?

How equally individuals are distributed among species in a community.

How did Mayr define a species?

As groups of interbreeding natural populations reproductively isolated from other groups.

What are the four conditions for natural selection?

Variation, inheritance, overproduction and differential survival and reproduction.

What is evolutionary fitness?

An organism's success in passing on its genes, measured by surviving offspring.
