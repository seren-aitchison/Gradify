Key terms and formulas at a glance

Half
One of two equal parts of a whole, written ½
Numerator
The top number of a fraction: how many equal parts you have
Equivalent fractions
Fractions that are worth the same amount, such as ½ and 3/6
Common denominator
A denominator shared by two or more fractions, found from a common multiple
Common multiple
A number that is in the times tables of two or more numbers
Bar model
A diagram that shows a whole as a bar split into equal parts
Mixed number
A whole number and a fraction together, such as 3½
Proper fraction
A fraction less than one, with the numerator smaller than the denominator
Tenth
One of ten equal parts of a whole, 1/10 or 0.1
Decimal point
The dot that separates the whole number part from the parts of a whole
Decimal place
A place to the right of the decimal point: tenths are the first, hundredths the second
Per cent
Out of a hundred, shown by the sign %

Five sample flashcards

How many quarters make a whole?

Four. From “Halves and quarters” →

In plain English: A fraction is an equal part of a whole. Key idea: A half is one of two equal parts, and a quarter is one of four equal parts of the same whole.

What is 4/12 in its simplest form?

1/3. From “Equivalent fractions” →

In plain English: Equivalent fractions look different but are worth the same amount, like ½ and 2/4. Key idea: Multiplying or dividing the numerator and denominator by the same number gives an equivalent fraction: the same amount cut into more or fewer pieces.

What is 2/3 of 27?

18. From “Fractions of amounts” →

In plain English: Finding a fraction of an amount, such as ¾ of 20, is a skill used every day with money, time and measures. Key idea: To find a fraction of an amount, divide by the denominator to find one part, then multiply by the numerator to find that many parts.

How is 9/10 written as a decimal?

0.9. From “Tenths and hundredths” →

In plain English: When one whole is split into ten equal parts, each part is a tenth; split into a hundred equal parts, each is a hundredth. Key idea: One tenth is 1/10 or 0.1, and one hundredth is 1/100 or 0.01: ten hundredths make one tenth.

What fraction is 25%?

One quarter. From “Percentages” →

In plain English: Per cent means "out of a hundred", so 30% means 30 out of every 100. Key idea: Per cent means out of a hundred: 100% is the whole, 50% is a half, 25% is a quarter and 10% is a tenth.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Fractions, Decimals and Percentages

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. A sandwich is cut into 4 equal pieces. What is each piece called? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    A quarter. Four equal parts are quarters. Two equal parts would be halves.

  2. What is the numerator of 3/7? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    3. The numerator is the top number, 3. The denominator is the bottom number, 7.

  3. Which fraction is equivalent to 1/2? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    3/6. Multiply top and bottom of 1/2 by 3 to get 3/6. Three of six equal parts is half.

  4. What is half of 16? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    8. Share 16 into 2 equal groups and each has 8. 4 would be a quarter of 16, and 32 is double.

  5. What is a quarter of 20? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    5. Halve 20 to get 10, then halve 10 to get 5. Or share 20 into 4 equal groups of 5.

  6. A shape is split into 6 equal parts and 5 are shaded. What fraction is shaded? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    5/6. There are 6 equal parts, so the denominator is 6, and 5 are shaded, so the numerator is 5.

  7. A ribbon is cut into two pieces, one 10 cm long and one 6 cm long. Is each piece a half? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    No, because the pieces are not equal. Halves must be equal. Each half of the 16 cm ribbon would be 8 cm, so these pieces are not halves.

  8. Mo says 1/10 of a pizza is more than 1/6 because 10 is more than 6. Who is right? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    1/6 is more, so Mo is wrong. Cutting into 10 pieces gives smaller slices than cutting into 6, so 1/6 is bigger than 1/10.

  9. Which fraction is NOT equivalent to 2/5? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    4/15. 4/10, 6/15 and 20/50 all come from multiplying 2/5 by 2, 3 and 10. 4/15 has the top doubled but the bottom tripled, so it is smaller.

Key takeaways

  • A half is one of two equal parts, and a quarter is one of four equal parts of the same whole.
  • The denominator tells you how many equal parts the whole is cut into; the numerator tells you how many of those parts you have.
  • Multiplying or dividing the numerator and denominator by the same number gives an equivalent fraction: the same amount cut into more or fewer pieces.
  • To compare fractions, make the denominators the same; then the fraction with the bigger numerator is bigger.
  • Add or subtract the numerators and keep the denominator, because the size of the pieces doesn't change.
  • To find a fraction of an amount, divide by the denominator to find one part, then multiply by the numerator to find that many parts.

Common mistakes in Fractions, Decimals and Percentages

  • Calling any one of two pieces "a half" even when the pieces are different sizes. Check the parts are equal first.
  • Thinking ⅕ is bigger than ⅓ because 5 is bigger than 3. Sharing a cake between 5 people gives smaller slices than sharing it between 3.
  • Adding the same number to top and bottom. 1/2 + 1 on each part gives 2/3, which isn't equal to 1/2. Only multiplying or dividing keeps the value.
  • Comparing only the denominators, or only the numerators, when both are different. 2/3 and 3/5 need a common denominator: 10/15 and 9/15.
  • Adding the denominators as well: 1/3 + 1/4 is not 2/7. 2/7 is less than a half, but a third plus a quarter is more than a half.
  • Dividing by the numerator instead of the denominator. For 3/4 of 28, divide by 4 (the number of equal parts), not by 3.

Lessons in this school

  1. Halves and quarters
    A fraction is an equal part of a whole.
  2. Unit and non-unit fractions
    Every fraction has a numerator on top and a denominator underneath.
  3. Equivalent fractions
    Equivalent fractions look different but are worth the same amount, like ½ and 2/4.
  4. Comparing fractions
    To decide which of two fractions is bigger, it helps to give them the same denominator.
  5. Adding and subtracting fractions
    Fractions can be added and subtracted when their denominators are the same.
  6. Fractions of amounts
    Finding a fraction of an amount, such as ¾ of 20, is a skill used every day with money, time and measures.
  7. Mixed numbers and improper fractions
    Some amounts are more than a whole, like two and a half pizzas.
  8. Multiplying and dividing fractions
    In Years 5 and 6 children learn to multiply fractions by whole numbers, multiply two fractions together, and divide a fraction by a whole number.
  9. Tenths and hundredths
    When one whole is split into ten equal parts, each part is a tenth; split into a hundred equal parts, each is a hundredth.
  10. Decimal place value
    Decimal numbers use the same place value system as whole numbers, carrying on to the right of the decimal point into tenths, hundredths and thousandths.
  11. Rounding decimals
    Decimals can be rounded to the nearest whole number or to one, two or more decimal places.
  12. Percentages
    Per cent means "out of a hundred", so 30% means 30 out of every 100.
  13. Fractions, decimals and percentages together
    Fractions, decimals and percentages are three ways of writing the same amount.
  14. Ratio and proportion
    Ratio compares one part with another, such as 2 red beads for every 3 blue beads.

Fractions, Decimals and Percentages: questions students ask

What is a unit fraction?

A fraction with 1 as the numerator, such as ¼.

Which is bigger, 2/5 or 2/9?

2/5 (fifths are bigger pieces than ninths).

What must you do before adding 1/2 and 1/3?

Change them to the same denominator: 3/6 + 2/6.

What proportion of that bag is green?

1/5 (one out of five).
