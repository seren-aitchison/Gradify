Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What do roots do?

Hold the plant in the soil and take in water and nutrients. From “Parts of a plant” →

In plain English: Flowering plants are made of parts that each do an important job. Key idea: Roots anchor the plant and take in water, the stem supports it and carries water, leaves make food using sunlight and flowers make seeds.

What is pollination?

Moving pollen from the anther to the stigma. From “Pollination and seed dispersal” →

In plain English: Flowers make seeds through pollination, and plants spread their seeds in clever ways. Key idea: Pollen moves from anther to stigma by insects or wind, fertilisation turns ovules into seeds, and seeds are dispersed by wind, animals, water or explosion.

How many milk teeth do children have?

20. From “Teeth and eating” →

In plain English: Our teeth help us bite and chew food, and we need to look after them. Key idea: Incisors cut, canines tear and molars grind; sugar feeds bacteria that make acid and cause decay, so brush twice a day with fluoride toothpaste and cut down on sugar.

How do you work out beats per minute from a 15-second count?

Multiply the number of beats by 4. From “The heart and circulation” →

In plain English: The heart pumps blood around the body through blood vessels. Key idea: The heart pumps blood through arteries, capillaries and veins, carrying oxygen and nutrients to the body, and your pulse rises during exercise.

What is always at the start of a food chain?

A producer, which is a green plant. From “Food chains” →

In plain English: A food chain shows what eats what in a habitat. Key idea: Food chains start with a producer, the arrows mean "is eaten by", and changing the number of one living thing affects the others in the chain.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Plants, Animals and Habitats

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which part of a plant takes in water from the soil? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    The roots. Roots grow into the soil and take in water and nutrients for the plant.

  2. What does a plant do when it does not have enough water? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    It wilts. Without water the plant cannot stay firm, so its leaves and stem droop.

  3. Which of these is a bulb? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    An onion. An onion is made of fleshy layers that store food underground, so it is a bulb.

  4. What is the main job of a plant's leaves? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Making food using sunlight. Leaves catch sunlight and use it, with air and water, to make food for the plant.

  5. A plant kept in a dark cupboard grows tall and pale. What was missing? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Light. Plants stretch towards light and lose their green colour when they do not get enough light.

  6. What does a seed need to germinate? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Water and warmth. Water makes the seed swell and warmth lets it start growing, using its stored food.

  7. Celery left in coloured water turns colour at the top. What does this show? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    The stem carries water upwards. The colour travels with the water, showing the stem carries water up through the plant.

  8. Ellie tests whether plants need water. Which test is fair? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Same plant, soil and light, one watered and one not. Only the water is changed, so any difference must be caused by the water.

  9. Why can a seed start growing in a dark cupboard? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    It uses food stored inside the seed. Seeds carry their own food store, so they do not need light until their leaves appear.

Key takeaways

  • Roots anchor the plant and take in water, the stem supports it and carries water, leaves make food using sunlight and flowers make seeds.
  • Plants need water, light, warmth, air, nutrients and space, and a fair test changes only one of these at a time to find out which matters.
  • Seeds germinate when they have water and warmth, growing a root then a shoot, while bulbs store food underground to grow new leaves and flowers each year.
  • Pollen moves from anther to stigma by insects or wind, fertilisation turns ovules into seeds, and seeds are dispersed by wind, animals, water or explosion.
  • Vertebrates have a backbone and form five groups (fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds and mammals), and animals can also be sorted into carnivores, herbivores and omnivores.
  • We have five senses (sight, hearing, smell, taste and touch), each linked to a sense organ, and our brain uses their messages to understand the world and stay safe.

Common mistakes in Plants, Animals and Habitats

  • Thinking plants take in food through their roots. Roots take in water and nutrients, but the plant makes its own food in its leaves using sunlight.
  • Changing two things at once, such as light and water, in a test. Then you cannot tell which one made the difference, so change only one.
  • Thinking seeds need soil to start growing. Seeds need water and warmth to germinate, and can start growing on damp kitchen roll.
  • Mixing up pollination and seed dispersal. Pollination is moving pollen to make seeds; dispersal is spreading the seeds once they are made.
  • Grouping animals by where they live, such as calling dolphins fish because they swim. Look at features such as how they breathe and how they feed their young.
  • Thinking we only use one sense at a time. We often use several together, for example smelling and tasting food at the same time.

Lessons in this school

  1. Parts of a plant
    Flowering plants are made of parts that each do an important job.
  2. What plants need to grow
    Plants need the right conditions to grow well.
  3. How seeds and bulbs grow
    Many plants grow from seeds and some grow from bulbs.
  4. Pollination and seed dispersal
    Flowers make seeds through pollination, and plants spread their seeds in clever ways.
  5. Animal groups
    Animals can be sorted into groups by their features and what they eat.
  6. The human body and senses
    Our bodies have many parts, and our five senses help us find out about the world.
  7. Teeth and eating
    Our teeth help us bite and chew food, and we need to look after them.
  8. Skeletons and muscles
    Skeletons and muscles help bodies move, stay upright and stay protected.
  9. The digestive system
    The digestive system breaks food down so the body can use it.
  10. The heart and circulation
    The heart pumps blood around the body through blood vessels.
  11. Healthy living
    Eating well, being active, sleeping enough and keeping clean help us stay healthy.
  12. Habitats
    A habitat is the natural home of a living thing.
  13. Food chains
    A food chain shows what eats what in a habitat.
  14. Classifying living things
    Scientists classify living things into groups based on their features.
  15. Life cycles
    All living things go through a life cycle of birth, growth, reproduction and death.
  16. Evolution and inheritance
    Living things change over very long periods of time through evolution.
