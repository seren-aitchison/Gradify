Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What is dark?

The absence of light. From “Light and dark” →

In plain English: We need light to see things, and dark is simply the absence of light. Key idea: We need light to see, dark is the absence of light, light sources give out their own light while other objects reflect it, and we must never look directly at the Sun.

What does a prism do to white light?

Splits it into a spectrum of colours. From “How we see” →

In plain English: We see things because light travels in straight lines into our eyes. Key idea: Light travels in straight lines; we see light sources because their light enters our eyes, and we see other objects because light reflects off them into our eyes.

Why should you never touch a switch with wet hands?

Water conducts electricity and could give you a shock. From “Electrical safety” →

In plain English: Electricity is useful but can be very dangerous. Key idea: Mains electricity is powerful and dangerous, batteries are safer for experiments, and we stay safe by keeping water away, never touching sockets or damaged wires, and keeping clear of power lines and substations.

Why are wires covered in plastic?

Plastic is an insulator, so it keeps us safe from shocks. From “Conductors and insulators” →

In plain English: Some materials let electricity flow through them and others do not. Key idea: Conductors such as metals let electricity flow and insulators such as plastic, rubber and wood do not, and electrical objects use both to work safely.

How long does the Earth take to spin once?

About 24 hours. From “Day and night” →

In plain English: Day and night happen because the Earth spins. Key idea: The Earth spins on its axis once about every 24 hours, so each place has day when it faces the Sun and night when it faces away, and the Sun appears to rise in the east and set in the west.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Light, Sound, Electricity, Earth and Space

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which of these is a light source? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    A candle flame. A candle flame gives out its own light, while the others only reflect light.

  2. What makes a shadow? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    An opaque object blocking light. Light cannot pass through an opaque object, so a dark area forms behind it.

  3. Why can we see a lit lamp? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Light travels from the lamp into our eyes. A lamp is a light source, so its light travels straight into our eyes.

  4. Why do cyclists wear reflective strips at night? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    They reflect car headlights so drivers see them. Reflective strips bounce light from headlights back towards drivers, making cyclists easier to spot.

  5. How can you make a puppet's shadow bigger? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Move the puppet closer to the torch. Moving the object nearer the light source blocks more of the light, so the shadow gets bigger.

  6. Which order shows how we see a tree on a sunny day? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Sun, then tree, then eye. Sunlight hits the tree, reflects off it and travels into our eyes.

  7. Why is it unsafe to look at the Sun through binoculars? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    They focus strong sunlight into your eyes. Binoculars gather and focus even more sunlight into the eye, which can cause permanent damage.

  8. Which material would make the faintest shadow? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Clear plastic. Clear plastic is transparent and lets almost all the light through, so it makes a very faint shadow.

  9. Why does a periscope need two mirrors? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    To reflect light twice so it reaches the eye. The top mirror reflects light down the tube, and the bottom mirror reflects it into the eye.

Key takeaways

  • We need light to see, dark is the absence of light, light sources give out their own light while other objects reflect it, and we must never look directly at the Sun.
  • Shadows form when an opaque object blocks light travelling in straight lines, they get bigger as the object moves closer to the light, and Sun shadows are shortest at midday.
  • Light travels in straight lines; we see light sources because their light enters our eyes, and we see other objects because light reflects off them into our eyes.
  • Sounds are made by vibrations that travel through solids, liquids and gases to our ears, they cannot travel through a vacuum, and they get fainter with distance.
  • Pitch is how high or low a sound is and depends on how fast something vibrates, which changes with length, tightness and thickness, while volume depends on the strength of the vibrations.
  • Mains electricity is powerful and dangerous, batteries are safer for experiments, and we stay safe by keeping water away, never touching sockets or damaged wires, and keeping clear of power lines and substations.

Common mistakes in Light, Sound, Electricity, Earth and Space

  • Thinking the Moon is a light source. The Moon gives out no light of its own; it reflects light from the Sun.
  • Thinking a shadow is a reflection or shows colours and faces. A shadow is just a dark area where light is blocked, so it only shows the outline shape.
  • Drawing arrows from the eye to the object, as if eyes send out light. Light travels from the source, to the object and then into the eye.
  • Thinking sound travels through empty space, as in films where spaceships roar. With no particles to vibrate, space is silent.
  • Mixing up pitch and volume, such as calling a loud sound "high". Pitch is high or low; volume is loud or quiet, and a sound can be low and loud at the same time.
  • Thinking a quick touch of mains electricity is harmless. Even a brief shock from mains electricity can cause serious injury or death.
