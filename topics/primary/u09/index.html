Five sample flashcards

Answer each from memory, then open it to check. Still foggy? Each card has the plain-English version.

What is the difference between an object and a material?

An object is a thing; a material is what it is made from. From “Everyday materials” →

In plain English: Everything around us is made from materials such as wood, plastic, metal and glass. Key idea: An object is a thing and a material is what it is made from, and we choose materials whose properties suit the object's job.

At what temperature does water boil?

100 °C. From “Solids, liquids and gases” →

In plain English: Materials can be solids, liquids or gases. Key idea: Solids keep their shape, liquids flow and take the shape of their container, and gases spread out to fill a space; heating and cooling change materials from one state to another.

What is soil made from?

Tiny pieces of rock mixed with organic matter, water and air. From “Rocks and soils” →

In plain English: Rocks are natural materials that make up the Earth's surface, and soils are made partly from rocks. Key idea: Rocks are igneous, sedimentary or metamorphic, fossils form when living things are buried in sediment that becomes rock, and soil is made from rock pieces and organic matter.

In which direction does friction act?

Opposite to the direction of movement. From “Friction” →

In plain English: Friction is a force that acts when two surfaces rub together. Key idea: Friction acts between surfaces against the direction of movement, slowing things down; it is useful for grip and braking but causes wear and heat, which lubricants reduce.

Why does a parachute slow a fall?

Its large surface area increases air resistance. From “Air and water resistance” →

In plain English: Air resistance and water resistance are forces that slow down objects moving through air and water. Key idea: Air and water resistance act against objects moving through air or water, larger surfaces increase them, and streamlined shapes reduce them.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Materials, Forces and Magnets

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which of these is a material? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Wood. Wood is what things are made from, while a table, spoon and window are objects.

  2. Which material is transparent? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Clear glass. Clear glass lets light straight through so you can see through it, which means it is transparent.

  3. Which of these is a liquid? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    Orange juice. Orange juice flows and takes the shape of the glass, so it is a liquid.

  4. Which material would be best for a pair of wellies? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Rubber. Rubber is waterproof and bendy, so it keeps feet dry and can be worn comfortably.

  5. Why are electrical wires coated in plastic? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Plastic is an electrical insulator. The metal inside conducts electricity, and the plastic coating stops it flowing into anyone who touches the wire.

  6. What happens when water vapour touches a cold window? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    It condenses into water droplets. The vapour cools on the glass and turns back into liquid water, which is condensing.

  7. Why would a chocolate teapot be a bad idea? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Hot tea would melt it. A teapot must hold hot liquid without changing shape, and chocolate melts when heated.

  8. Which is the best material for a saucepan handle? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Wood, because it is a thermal insulator. A handle needs to stay cool to hold, so a thermal insulator such as wood or plastic is best.

  9. Why can sand be poured even though it is a solid? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    Each grain is a tiny solid that can move. Every grain keeps its own shape, which makes it a solid, but the separate grains can slide past each other when poured.

Key takeaways

  • An object is a thing and a material is what it is made from, and we choose materials whose properties suit the object's job.
  • Materials have properties such as hardness, flexibility, transparency, conductivity and solubility, and fair tests let us compare them to choose the best material for a job.
  • Solids keep their shape, liquids flow and take the shape of their container, and gases spread out to fill a space; heating and cooling change materials from one state to another.
  • In the water cycle, water evaporates when heated, condenses into clouds when cooled, falls as precipitation and collects again, and higher temperatures speed up evaporation.
  • Rocks are igneous, sedimentary or metamorphic, fossils form when living things are buried in sediment that becomes rock, and soil is made from rock pieces and organic matter.
  • Dissolving, mixing and changes of state are reversible and mixtures can be separated by sieving, filtering and evaporating, while burning, rusting and reactions such as vinegar with bicarbonate of soda make new materials and are irreversible.

Common mistakes in Materials, Forces and Magnets

  • Calling an object a material, such as saying "the material is a chair". The chair is the object; wood or plastic is the material.
  • Thinking all metals are magnetic. Iron, nickel and cobalt are magnetic, but aluminium, copper and gold are not.
  • Thinking that steam you can see is a gas. Water vapour is invisible: the white cloud is tiny droplets of liquid water that have condensed.
  • Thinking water only evaporates when it boils. Puddles and washing dry at normal temperatures because water evaporates slowly all the time.
  • Thinking all fossils are dinosaur bones. Fossils include shells, leaves, footprints and even burrows, and most come from sea creatures.
  • Thinking dissolved sugar has disappeared. It is still in the water, which tastes sweet, and it can be recovered by evaporating the water.

Lessons in this school

  1. Everyday materials
    Everything around us is made from materials such as wood, plastic, metal and glass.
  2. Properties of materials
    Materials have properties such as hardness, flexibility and transparency.
  3. Solids, liquids and gases
    Materials can be solids, liquids or gases.
  4. The water cycle
    The water cycle is the journey water takes between the land, the sea and the sky.
  5. Rocks and soils
    Rocks are natural materials that make up the Earth's surface, and soils are made partly from rocks.
  6. Changes of materials
    Materials can change in reversible and irreversible ways.
  7. Pushes and pulls
    A force is a push or a pull.
  8. Friction
    Friction is a force that acts when two surfaces rub together.
  9. Gravity
    Gravity is the force that pulls things towards the Earth.
  10. Air and water resistance
    Air resistance and water resistance are forces that slow down objects moving through air and water.
  11. Magnets
    Magnets can attract or repel each other and attract some materials.
  12. Levers, pulleys and gears
    Levers, pulleys and gears are simple machines that let a small force have a bigger effect.
