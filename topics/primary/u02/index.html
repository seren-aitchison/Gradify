What goes with 7 to make 10?

What is the difference between 15 and 9?

6. From “Subtracting in your head” →

In plain English: Subtraction can mean taking away or finding the difference between two numbers. Key idea: Subtraction is both taking away and finding the difference; you can count back from the bigger number or count up from the smaller one.

What is 400 − 1?

399. From “Column subtraction” →

In plain English: Column subtraction is the written method for taking away bigger numbers. Key idea: When the top digit is smaller than the bottom digit, exchange 1 from the next column to the left, which gives 10 more in this column.

What is 7 × 8?

56. From “Times tables” →

In plain English: Knowing the times tables by heart, up to 12 × 12, makes almost every other kind of maths easier. Key idea: Learn tables a few facts at a time, every day, and use facts you already know to build the ones you don't.

Which times table fact helps with 35 ÷ 5?

7 × 5 = 35, so the answer is 7. From “What division means” →

In plain English: Division means splitting into equal parts. Key idea: Division is the inverse of multiplication, so the times tables give you the division facts: 4 × 5 = 20, so 20 ÷ 5 = 4.

Quick quiz: test yourself on The Four Operations and Times Tables

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which pair is a number bond to 10? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    6 and 4. 6 + 4 = 10. The other pairs make 11, so they are bonds to 11 instead.

  2. What is 42 + 30? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    72. Adding 30 adds 3 tens, so 4 tens become 7 tens: 72. Only the tens digit changes.

  3. What is 85 − 40? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    45. Taking away 4 tens changes the 8 tens to 4 tens; the ones stay at 5. So the answer is 45.

  4. What must you add to 13 to make 20? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    7. 3 + 7 = 10, so 13 + 7 = 20. You use the bond to 10 and keep the extra ten.

  5. What goes with 46 to make 100? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    54. 46 + 4 = 50 and 50 + 50 = 100, so you add 4 + 50 = 54. Picking 64 makes 110.

  6. Which is the quickest way to work out 63 + 29? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    63 + 30, then take away 1. 29 is one less than 30, so add 30 and take away 1: 93 − 1 = 92. Counting on in ones takes far longer.

  7. Which fact is wrong, and so NOT in the same family as 30 + 70 = 100? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    100 − 30 = 30. The family uses 30, 70 and 100. 100 − 30 is 70, not 30, so that fact is false.

  8. Zara works out 358 + 99 as 358 + 100 + 1. What is wrong? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    She should take away 1, not add 1. 99 is 1 less than 100, so after adding 100 she has added 1 too many and must take 1 away: 457, not 459.

  9. Which calculation is easiest to do by counting up? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    2,001 − 1,996. 2,001 and 1,996 are very close, so counting up from 1,996 gives the difference of 5 quickly. The others are easy to count back.

Key takeaways

  • If you know the bonds to 10 by heart, you can quickly work out bonds to 20 and to 100.
  • Adding in your head is easiest when you break a number into tens and ones, or round it to a friendly number first.
  • Subtraction is both taking away and finding the difference; you can count back from the bigger number or count up from the smaller one.
  • Line digits up by place value, add from the right, and carry a ten into the next column whenever a column makes 10 or more.
  • When the top digit is smaller than the bottom digit, exchange 1 from the next column to the left, which gives 10 more in this column.
  • To check a calculation, do the inverse with your answer: if you get back to the number you started with, your answer is right.

Common mistakes in The Four Operations and Times Tables

  • Thinking the bond to 100 for 58 is 52, by making the tens 10 and the ones 10. The ones make 10 (8 + 2), which adds an extra ten, so the tens only need to make 90: 50 + 40.
  • Rounding up and then forgetting to adjust, or adjusting the wrong way. If you add 20 instead of 19, you have added 1 too many, so take 1 away.
  • Thinking subtraction can be done in any order. 9 − 4 is 5, but 4 − 9 is not 5: the order matters, unlike in addition.
  • Lining the numbers up on the left instead of the right. In 3,507 + 46, the 46 must go under the 07, or you will add tens to thousands.
  • Taking the smaller digit from the bigger one in every column, whichever is on top. For 52 − 17, that gives 45 (7 − 2 = 5) instead of the correct 35.
  • Checking by doing the same calculation again. If you made a slip the first time, you may make it again. The inverse is a different calculation, so it catches more mistakes.

Lessons in this school

  1. Number bonds
    Number bonds are pairs of numbers that add up to a total, such as 7 and 3 making 10.
  2. Adding in your head
    Many additions are quicker in your head than on paper.
  3. Subtracting in your head
    Subtraction can mean taking away or finding the difference between two numbers.
  4. Column addition
    Column addition is the written method for adding bigger numbers.
  5. Column subtraction
    Column subtraction is the written method for taking away bigger numbers.
  6. Checking with the inverse
    Addition and subtraction undo each other, and so do multiplication and division.
  7. What multiplication means
    Multiplication is a quick way of adding equal groups.
  8. Times tables
    Knowing the times tables by heart, up to 12 × 12, makes almost every other kind of maths easier.
  9. Factors and multiples
    Multiples are the numbers in a times table; factors are the numbers that divide exactly into a number.
  10. What division means
    Division means splitting into equal parts.
  11. Written multiplication
    To multiply bigger numbers on paper we use short multiplication (by a one-digit number) and long multiplication (by a two-digit number).
  12. Short division and remainders
    Short division is the written method for dividing a big number by a one-digit number, sometimes called the bus stop method.

Which column do you start with in column addition?

The ones column, on the right.

In column subtraction, what do you do if the top digit is smaller?

Exchange 1 from the next column to the left, making 10 more in this column.
