What is a proper noun?

The name of a particular person, place or thing, starting with a capital letter. From “Nouns and noun phrases” →

In plain English: A noun is a word for a person, place, thing or idea. Key idea: A noun names a person, place, thing or idea; proper nouns always start with a capital letter.

Which relative pronoun is used for people?

who. From “Pronouns” →

In plain English: Pronouns take the place of nouns so we don't have to keep repeating them. Key idea: Pronouns replace nouns to avoid repetition, but the reader must always be able to tell which noun a pronoun means.

In "Sara kicked the ball", what is the subject?

Sara. From “Active and passive voice” →

In plain English: In an active sentence the subject does the action; in a passive sentence the subject has the action done to it. Key idea: Active: the subject does the action. Passive: the subject has the action done to it, using "was" or "were" plus a past participle.

What is a comma splice?

Two sentences wrongly joined with just a comma. From “Commas” →

In plain English: Commas help readers by separating parts of a sentence. Key idea: Commas separate list items, follow fronted adverbials and subordinate clauses at the start, and make the meaning clear.

What are the five parts of a story mountain?

Opening, build-up, problem, resolution, ending. From “Planning your writing” →

In plain English: Good writing starts before you write a single sentence. Key idea: Plan with your purpose and audience in mind, and organise ideas into a clear order before you start writing.

Quick quiz: test yourself on Grammar, Punctuation and Writing

9 questions from the course, from foundation recall to stretch. Pick an answer, then say whether you knew it or guessed.

  1. Which word is a noun in "The cat slept on the warm mat"? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    mat. "Mat" names a thing. "Slept" is a verb, "warm" an adjective and "on" a preposition.

  2. Which word is the verb in "The baby laughed loudly"? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    laughed. "Laughed" is what the baby did, so it is the verb. "Loudly" is an adverb.

  3. Which word is an adjective in "The huge whale swam slowly"? (Foundation recall)
    Show the answer

    huge. "Huge" describes the noun "whale", so it is an adjective. "Slowly" is an adverb.

  4. Which is a proper noun? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    Scotland. Scotland names a particular place, so it is a proper noun with a capital letter. The others are common nouns.

  5. Which is an expanded noun phrase? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    the tiny spider in the corner. "The tiny spider in the corner" is built around the noun "spider" with an adjective and a prepositional phrase.

  6. Which sentence is in the present perfect? (Exam style)
    Show the answer

    She has painted a picture.. The present perfect uses has or have with the past participle: "has painted".

  7. Which sentence uses capital letters for proper nouns correctly? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    On Friday, Leo visited Cardiff.. Days of the week, names and places are proper nouns, so Friday, Leo and Cardiff all need capitals.

  8. Which sentence keeps the tense consistent? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    We ran to the bus stop and caught the bus.. Both verbs, "ran" and "caught", are in the simple past, so the tense is consistent.

  9. Which sentence uses an adverb correctly? (Stretch)
    Show the answer

    He ran quickly to catch the bus.. "Quickly" is an adverb describing how he ran. "Quick" is an adjective and can't describe the verb.

Key takeaways

  • A noun names a person, place, thing or idea; proper nouns always start with a capital letter.
  • Keep the tense consistent within a piece of writing unless there is a reason to change time.
  • Adjectives describe nouns; adverbs describe verbs and tell you how, when, where or how often.
  • Pronouns replace nouns to avoid repetition, but the reader must always be able to tell which noun a pronoun means.
  • Coordinating conjunctions join equal parts; subordinating conjunctions add a clause that depends on the main one.
  • A preposition links a noun or pronoun to the rest of the sentence, often showing where or when.

Common mistakes in Grammar, Punctuation and Writing

  • Forgetting capital letters on proper nouns such as days, months, people's names and places: "on monday we went to wales" should be "On Monday we went to Wales."
  • Switching tense by accident: "I went to the shop and I buy some milk." Keep both verbs in the past: went, bought.
  • Using an adjective where an adverb is needed: "She sang beautiful." The word describes how she sang, so it needs the adverb "beautifully".
  • Writing "me and Josh went" in formal writing. Use "Josh and I went" as the subject, and "with Josh and me" after a preposition.
  • Starting lots of sentences with "and then". Choose a conjunction that shows the real link: because (reason), although (contrast), when (time), if (condition).
  • Confusing prepositions with adverbs. In "She looked up", "up" is an adverb; in "She looked up the chimney", "up" is a preposition because it is followed by a noun.

Lessons in this school

  1. Nouns and noun phrases
    A noun is a word for a person, place, thing or idea.
  2. Verbs and tenses
    Verbs are doing or being words, and their tense shows when something happens.
  3. Adjectives and adverbs
    Adjectives describe nouns and adverbs describe verbs, adjectives or other adverbs.
  4. Pronouns
    Pronouns take the place of nouns so we don't have to keep repeating them.
  5. Conjunctions
    Conjunctions are joining words that link words, phrases and clauses.
  6. Prepositions
    Prepositions show how one thing relates to another, often in place or time: under the table, before lunch.
  7. Clauses and sentences
    A sentence is a group of words that makes complete sense.
  8. Active and passive voice
    In an active sentence the subject does the action; in a passive sentence the subject has the action done to it.
  9. Capital letters and full stops
    Capital letters and full stops are the first punctuation children learn: a capital starts a sentence and a full stop ends it.
  10. Question marks and exclamation marks
    Question marks end sentences that ask something; exclamation marks show strong feeling or surprise.
  11. Commas
    Commas help readers by separating parts of a sentence.
  12. Apostrophes
    Apostrophes have two jobs: showing where letters are missing in a contraction, and showing that something belongs to someone.
  13. Inverted commas
    Inverted commas, also called speech marks, show the exact words someone says.
  14. Brackets, dashes, colons and semicolons
    In upper Key Stage 2, children learn more advanced punctuation.
  15. Planning your writing
    Good writing starts before you write a single sentence.
  16. Writing a story
    A good story has interesting characters, a vivid setting and a plot with a problem that gets solved.
  17. Non-fiction writing
    Non-fiction writing gives real information, instructions or opinions.
  18. Editing and improving
    Good writers never stop at their first draft.

What is a prepositional phrase?

A preposition followed by a noun or pronoun, such as "under the table".

Do days of the week need capital letters?

Yes, for example Monday.

What do commas separate in a list?
