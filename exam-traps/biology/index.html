Biology: common exam mistakes

These are the mistakes our Biology lessons warn about most, grouped by topic. Read each trap, then cover it and explain the right idea from memory.

Foundations of Biology

  • Thinking biology is just memorising names. The names matter less than the mechanisms: how and why living things work as they do. From “What is biology?”
  • Saying plants don't respire because they photosynthesise. Plant cells respire all the time; in light, photosynthesis also runs, usually faster. From “Characteristics of living organisms”
  • Confusing a tissue with an organ. A tissue is one type of cell working together; an organ contains several tissues. From “Levels of biological organisation”
  • Concluding that a dormant seed is dead because it shows no growth. It is alive; its metabolism is very slow until conditions allow germination. From “The characteristics of life”

Cell Biology

  • Confusing magnification with resolution. Enlarging a blurry image more doesn't reveal more detail; only better resolution does. From “Cell theory”
  • Saying prokaryotes have no DNA organisation or no ribosomes. They have a chromosome and many ribosomes; they lack a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles. From “Prokaryotic cells”
  • Thinking "eukaryotic" means "multicellular". Many eukaryotes, such as yeast and amoebae, are single cells. From “Eukaryotic cells”
  • Drawing a large central vacuole in an animal cell. Animal cells have small vesicles and vacuoles, not one large permanent vacuole. From “Animal cells”

Biochemistry and Biomolecules

  • Thinking hydrolysis means "breaking with heat". Hydrolysis breaks bonds by adding water, usually catalysed by enzymes. From “Chemistry of life”
  • Saying water molecules are held together by hydrogen bonds within the molecule. Covalent bonds join O and H; hydrogen bonds form between molecules. From “Water and biological systems”
  • Calling a hydrogen bond a covalent bond to hydrogen. It is a weaker attraction between molecules or parts of molecules. From “Hydrogen bonding”
  • Thinking pH 4 is twice as acidic as pH 5. It has ten times the hydrogen ion concentration. From “pH and biological buffers”

Genetics and Molecular Biology

  • Thinking all variation is genetic. Many characteristics are affected by both genes and environment. From “Introduction to genetics”
  • Overlooking Rosalind Franklin's role. Her X-ray data were essential evidence for the double helix. From “DNA structure”
  • Saying DNA polymerase unwinds DNA. Helicase unwinds the helix; DNA polymerase builds new strands. From “DNA replication”
  • Assuming all DNA damage becomes a mutation. Most damage is repaired before it can be passed on. From “DNA repair”

Cell Division and Development

  • Thinking interphase is a resting phase. Cells are very active in interphase, growing and copying DNA. From “Cell cycle”
  • Saying chromosomes are visible as condensed structures during interphase. They are uncondensed chromatin until mitosis. From “Interphase”
  • Saying chromosomes pair up in mitosis. Homologous pairing happens in meiosis, not mitosis. From “Mitosis”
  • Saying sister chromatids separate in meiosis I. Homologous chromosomes separate in meiosis I; chromatids separate in meiosis II. From “Meiosis”

Evolution and Natural Selection

  • Describing Darwin's theory as "animals adapt because they need to". Selection acts on existing variation; individuals do not change their genes by need. From “Origins of evolutionary thought”
  • Saying there is no evidence because transitional fossils are missing. Many transitional fossils, such as Tiktaalik, have been found. From “Evidence for evolution”
  • Saying individuals evolve during their lifetimes. Populations evolve as allele frequencies change over generations. From “Natural selection”
  • Assuming every feature is an adaptation. Some traits are by-products, inherited from ancestors or neutral. From “Adaptation”

Human Anatomy and Physiology

  • Thinking each organ is made of a single tissue. Most organs contain several tissue types. From “Organisation of the human body”
  • Thinking homeostasis keeps variables exactly constant. Variables fluctuate within a narrow range around a set point. From “Homeostasis”
  • Thinking every response goes through conscious thought in the brain. Many reflexes are processed in the spinal cord. From “Nervous system”
  • Thinking myelin conducts the impulse. Myelin insulates; the impulse jumps between gaps in it. From “Neurones”

Plant Biology

  • Thinking all plant cells contain chloroplasts. Root cells and many internal cells lack them. From “Plant cell structure”
  • Thinking plants stop growing when they reach adult size. Meristems allow growth throughout life. From “Plant tissues”
  • Thinking roots absorb water through the root cap. Most absorption occurs through root hairs in the maturation zone. From “Roots and root systems”
  • Calling a potato a root. A potato is a stem tuber with buds at its nodes. From “Stems”

Microbiology

  • Thinking most microbes cause disease. Only a small proportion of microbes are pathogens. From “Introduction to microbiology”
  • Thinking bacteria have no organised structures. They have walls, ribosomes, flagella and other specialised structures. From “Bacterial structure”
  • Thinking bacterial species are defined like animal species. Gene exchange makes species boundaries in bacteria harder to define. From “Bacterial classification”
  • Thinking bacteria keep growing exponentially forever. Nutrients run out and wastes accumulate, causing stationary and death phases. From “Bacterial growth”

Immunology

  • Thinking the immune system only fights infections. It also removes abnormal cells and maintains tolerance to self. From “Introduction to immunology”
  • Thinking innate immunity is unimportant because it is non-specific. It controls most infections and starts adaptive responses. From “Innate immunity”
  • Thinking adaptive immunity acts immediately. On first exposure it takes days to develop. From “Adaptive immunity”
  • Thinking barriers are passive walls only. They include active chemicals, flushing and competing microbes. From “Physical barriers”

Ecology and Environmental Biology

  • Confusing a community with an ecosystem. An ecosystem includes the non-living environment as well as the community. From “Introduction to ecology”
  • Assuming a capture-recapture estimate is exact. It relies on assumptions and gives an estimate. From “Organisms and populations”
  • Using habitat and niche as the same thing. A niche is an organism's role, not just its location. From “Habitats and niches”
  • Thinking decomposers are unimportant. Without them, nutrients would not be recycled. From “Ecosystems”

Biotechnology and Genetic Engineering

  • Thinking biotechnology is only modern genetic engineering. Brewing and selective breeding are ancient biotechnologies. From “Foundations of biotechnology”
  • Thinking bacteria can express any human gene directly. Introns must be removed, so cDNA is often used. From “Recombinant DNA”
  • Thinking restriction enzymes cut DNA randomly. They cut only at specific recognition sequences. From “Restriction enzymes”
  • Thinking ligase forms hydrogen bonds between bases. It forms covalent phosphodiester bonds in the backbone. From “DNA ligase”

Bioinformatics and Computational Biology

  • Thinking bioinformatics is only about programming. It needs biological knowledge to ask and interpret the right questions. From “Introduction to bioinformatics”
  • Thinking database entries are always error-free. Primary data can contain errors, so curated databases add checks. From “Biological databases”
  • Thinking every ORF is a real gene. Many short ORFs occur by chance and need further evidence. From “DNA sequence analysis”
  • Thinking two proteins with similar functions always have similar sequences. Unrelated proteins can perform similar functions. From “Protein sequence analysis”

Experimental Biology and Research Methods

  • Thinking more measurements of the same sample count as replicates. Independent biological samples are needed. From “Experimental design”
  • Saying an experiment "proved" a hypothesis. Evidence supports or fails to support it; it does not prove it. From “Hypothesis development”
  • Thinking a control just means "the normal condition". Different controls answer different questions. From “Controls”
  • Thinking a large sample is always unbiased. A large biased sample is still biased. From “Sampling”
