Spanish: common exam mistakes

These are the mistakes our Spanish lessons warn about most, grouped by topic. Read each trap, then cover it and explain the right idea from memory.

First Steps in Spanish

  • Pronouncing the silent letters of English habits: in Spanish h is always silent (hola is "OH-la"), and the u in que, qui, gue and gui isn't pronounced. From “The Spanish alphabet and its sounds”
  • Saying English vowels: no pronounced "noh-oo" or de as "day" sounds foreign at once. Keep each vowel short, steady and unglided. From “Vowels and diphthongs”
  • Dropping accents as optional. Papa is "potato" or "Pope", papá is "dad"; esta is "this", está is "is". The accent changes the word. From “Stress and written accents”
  • Pronouncing pero (but) with a trill makes it perro (dog). The tap and trill distinguish words, so practise both. From “Consonants that differ from English”

The Present Tense, Family and Friends

  • Using jugar for instruments: you tocas la guitarra (play an instrument) but juegas al tenis (play a sport or game). From “Regular -ar verbs in the present”
  • Giving -er and -ir verbs -ar endings: Yo como is right, but él coma or ellos viván are wrong in the present. -er and -ir verbs use e in the endings. From “Regular -er and -ir verbs in the present”
  • Translating "I have been living here for a year" with a past tense: Spanish says Vivo aquí desde hace un año, in the present, because the action continues. From “Using the present tense”
  • Saying Estoy caliente for "I'm hot": a person who feels hot tiene calor. Estar caliente refers to objects (La sopa está caliente) and, of people, has a sexual meaning. From “Tener and expressions with tener”

Daily Life, School and Food

  • Leaving out the pronoun: Levanto a las siete means "I lift at seven". The reflexive form is Me levanto a las siete. From “Reflexive verbs”
  • Using a conjugated verb or gerund after después de: después de comiendo and después de como are wrong; Spanish needs the infinitive: después de comer. From “Daily routine”
  • Writing Nunca no voy: with nunca first, there is no no. Use Nunca voy or No voy nunca. From “Frequency and time expressions”
  • Saying Me gusta las ciencias: plural subjects need gustan. And El inglés es útil but Las ciencias son útiles. From “School subjects and opinions”

Town, Travel and Directions

  • Translating "library" as librería: a librería sells books; a biblioteca lends them. From “Places in town”
  • Mixing up derecho and derecha: a la derecha is "on the right", while todo derecho (like todo recto) is "straight on". From “Asking for and giving directions”
  • Using the affirmative form in the negative: No habla means "He doesn't speak"; "Don't speak" is No hables. From “Tú commands”
  • Adding the normal present ending: Usted habla states a fact ("You speak"); the command is Hable. From “Usted and ustedes commands”

Talking about the Past

  • Dropping the accent: Hablo con mi madre ayer means nothing correct; the preterite is Hablé. Without its accent, habló becomes hablo, "I speak". From “The regular preterite”
  • Applying the yo change to other forms: buscó and llegaste are regular. Only the yo form changes. From “Spelling changes in the preterite”
  • Writing hació or tenió: the forms are hizo and tuvo. And fué, dió and vió no longer carry accents. From “Irregular preterites: ser, ir, hacer and tener”
  • Writing dijieron or trajieron: after j the ending is -eron: dijeron, trajeron. From “More irregular preterites: estar, poder, poner, querer, venir and decir”

The Future, the Conditional and Work

  • Removing the -ar, -er or -ir before adding the endings. The future uses the whole infinitive: hablaré, not hablé. From “The future tense”
  • Writing "teneré" or "haceré". Irregular verbs must use their irregular stems: tendré and haré. From “Irregular futures”
  • Always translating the future as "will". In context it can mean "probably" or "I wonder". From “The future of probability”
  • Confusing the conditional with the imperfect. Comería (I would eat) uses the whole infinitive; comía (I used to eat) does not. From “The conditional”

The Subjunctive

  • Starting from the infinitive instead of the yo form, as in "que tena". Always start from yo: tengo gives tenga. From “Forming the present subjunctive”
  • Writing "que voy" or "que soy" after a subjunctive trigger. Use the subjunctive forms: que vaya, que sea. From “Irregular present subjunctives”
  • Translating "I want you to come" as "quiero tú venir". Spanish needs que + subjunctive: quiero que vengas. From “The subjunctive after wishes and influence”
  • Writing "me molesta que la gente tira basura". After an emotion trigger the verb must be subjunctive: tire. From “The subjunctive after emotion”

Advanced Grammar and Accuracy

  • Writing "gracias para el regalo". Thanks are given in exchange for something, so use por: gracias por el regalo. From “Por and para”
  • Writing "conozco tu hermana". A specific person as direct object needs the personal a: conozco a tu hermana. From “Personal a”
  • Writing "busco para mis llaves" or "espero para el autobús". Buscar and esperar already include "for": busco mis llaves, espero el autobús. From “Verbs with prepositions”
  • Forgetting agreement, as in "la casa fue construido". The participle must agree with the subject: la casa fue construida. From “The passive with ser”

The Spanish-Speaking World

  • Thinking that everyone in Spain speaks only Spanish. Many people are bilingual in Spanish and a co-official language such as Catalan or Basque. From “Spain's regions and languages”
  • Saying children in Spain only get presents on 25 December. Many receive them from the Reyes Magos on 6 January. From “Festivals and traditions in Spain”
  • Thinking Spain became a democracy immediately in 1939. The war ended in 1939, but democracy came only after Franco's death in 1975. From “The Civil War, Franco and the transition to democracy”
  • Confusing the Día de Muertos with Halloween. It is a separate tradition on 1 and 2 November that honours and remembers the dead. From “Mexico: history and culture”

Skills: Listening, Reading, Speaking, Writing and Translation

  • Writing down the first matching word you hear. Wait for the whole sentence, as pero or no can change the answer. From “Listening strategies”
  • Confusing sesenta (60) and setenta (70). Listen for the s or t sound at the start of the second syllable. From “Understanding numbers, dates and details”
  • Choosing P because you heard "me gusta" in "no me gusta nada". Listen for no and nada, which make it strongly negative. From “Listening for opinions and attitudes”
  • Translating every word of a text before answering. Read the questions first and scan for the relevant section. From “Reading strategies”
