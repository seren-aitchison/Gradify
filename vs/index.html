Concept comparisons

The pairs of ideas students mix up most, each answered in two sentences with a side-by-side table and the common exam mix-up.

  • Active recall vs Spaced repetitionActive recall is how you study (testing yourself from memory); spaced repetition is when you study (reviews spread out at growing gaps).
  • Molarity vs MolalityMolarity is moles of solute per litre (dm³) of solution, so it changes slightly with temperature; molality is moles of solute per kilogram of solvent, so it doesn't.
  • Correlation vs CausationCorrelation means two things change together; causation means one actually makes the other happen.
  • Mitosis vs MeiosisMitosis is one division that makes two genetically identical diploid cells, for growth and repair; meiosis is two divisions that make four genetically different haploid gametes, for sexual reproduction..
  • Essay outline vs Mind mapA mind map is best for getting ideas out and seeing connections; an essay outline is best for putting them in an argued order with a word budget.
  • Blurting vs FlashcardsBlurting tests a whole topic at once and shows how ideas connect; flashcards test single facts and definitions precisely.
  • Pomodoro vs Time-blockingPomodoro splits work into short timed focus blocks with breaks (25/5 or 50/10); time-blocking assigns tasks to calendar slots across the day or week.
  • Accuracy vs PrecisionAccuracy is how close a measurement is to the true value; precision is how close repeated measurements are to each other.
  • Validity vs ReliabilityValidity is whether a method measures what it's meant to; reliability is whether it gives consistent results when repeated.
  • Aerobic respiration vs Anaerobic respirationAerobic respiration uses oxygen and releases much more energy per glucose; anaerobic respiration doesn't use oxygen, releases far less, and in human muscle produces lactate..
