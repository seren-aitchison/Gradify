Correlation vs Causation

Correlation means two things change together; causation means one actually makes the other happen. A correlation can come from chance, reverse causation or a third factor, so causation needs a mechanism and a controlled comparison.

CorrelationCausation
MeaningTwo variables vary togetherA change in one produces a change in the other
Shown byObservational data, scatter graphsControlled experiments (ideally randomised)
Classic trapIce-cream sales and drownings (both rise in summer)Assuming the correlation proves the cause

When should I use each?

Say 'is associated with' for correlation; save 'causes' for evidence from controlled comparisons with a plausible mechanism.

What's the common exam mix-up?

Reverse causation (which way round?) and confounding variables (a third factor driving both).
