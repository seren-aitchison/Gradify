## Required practical: observing cells with a light microscope
LV: GCSE · Foundation
S: Looking at real plant and animal cells under a light microscope, then drawing and labelling what you see, is one of the required practicals in AQA GCSE Biology and builds the skills examiners test on microscopy.
O: Use a light microscope in the right order to bring cells into clear focus
O: Make a clear, labelled biological drawing that includes a magnification scale

T: magnification | How many times bigger an image appears than the real object, found by dividing image size by real size
T: field of view | The circular area you can see when you look down the eyepiece of a microscope
T: biological drawing | A clear scientific record of a specimen, drawn with single lines and labels rather than shading
H: Using the microscope
P: In this required practical you look at several kinds of plant and animal cell under a light microscope, then draw and label them with a magnification scale. AQA links it to its apparatus and technique skills 1 and 7.
- Start with the **lowest-power objective lens**.
- Focus the image, then move to a **higher-power** objective lens to see more detail.
- You can compare what you see with micrographs, photographs or video of the same kinds of cell.
K: Always begin on the lowest-power objective lens and only then move to higher power.
SAFE: Follow your teacher's instructions and your school's risk assessment when handling slides, glass and any stains or specimens.
H: Drawing what you see
P: A biological drawing is a scientific record, not a piece of art. Examiners want to see what is really there, clearly.
- Draw large, using most of the space on the page.
- Use clear, single, continuous lines.
- Do not shade or colour in.
- Label the structures you can see.
- Add a **magnification scale** to every drawing; this is required for the practical.
!: Shading and sketchy lines make a drawing look artistic but lose marks. Use clean single lines and labels only.

H: From drawing to real size
E: real size = image size ÷ magnification :: rearranged from magnification = image size ÷ real size
X: An onion cell viewed at ×400 is 14.8 mm wide in your image. How wide is the real cell?
> Divide image size by magnification: 14.8 ÷ 400 = 0.037, so the real cell is 0.037 mm wide.
> Convert to micrometres: 0.037 × 1000 = 37.
> The real cell is about 37 micrometres wide, or 3.7 × 10^{-2} mm in standard form.
!: Forgetting to convert units. Put both lengths in the same unit before you divide, then give the answer in the unit asked for.
