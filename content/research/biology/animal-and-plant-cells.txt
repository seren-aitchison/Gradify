F: Most animal cells contain a nucleus, cytoplasm, cell membrane, mitochondria and ribosomes | s1,s3 | conf=high
F: Plant cells often add chloroplasts and a permanent vacuole filled with cell sap; plant and algal cells also have a cellulose cell wall that strengthens the cell | s1,s3 | conf=high
F: Students must explain how nucleus, cell membrane, mitochondria, chloroplasts (plant) and plasmids (bacterial) relate to their functions | s1 | conf=high
F: Nucleus holds the DNA and so directs cell activity; it is a membrane-bound compartment, which is what defines a eukaryote | s1,s3,s4 | conf=high
F: Cell membrane is selective, controlling which substances move in and out | s4,s3 | conf=high
F: Mitochondria hold the enzymes for aerobic respiration, where most energy is released; they make ATP | s4,s3 | conf=high
F: Chloroplasts contain chlorophyll that absorbs light, plus the enzymes for photosynthesis; they have their own DNA and ribosomes and stacked thylakoid membranes | s4,s3 | conf=high
F: Ribosomes are the tiny sites of protein synthesis, seen only with an electron microscope | s4,s3 | conf=high
F: Permanent vacuole of cell sap helps keep plant cells turgid; animal cells may have small temporary vacuoles for storing or moving substances | s4,s3 | conf=med
F: Cytoplasm is a jelly-like medium holding dissolved nutrients and organelles where many reactions occur | s4,s3 | conf=high
F: Bacterial cells are much smaller, lack a nucleus, have a single DNA loop plus possibly small DNA rings called plasmids, and a cell wall | s1 | conf=high
F: Typical animal cells are about 0.01 to 0.05 mm and plant cells 0.01 to 0.10 mm; the naked eye resolves about 0.05 mm | s4 | conf=med
F: Micrometre (1/1000 mm, 1 x 10^-6 m) is the sensible unit for cells; nanometre (1 x 10^-9 m) for ribosomes and viruses | s4 | conf=high
F: AQA expects order-of-magnitude calculations, standard form, and the prefixes centi, milli, micro, nano | s1 | conf=high
F: Students must be able to make rough size estimates for structures inside cells, and say when a rough figure is good enough to compare how big or how much area one structure takes against another; micrographs and photographs can be compared with own drawings | s1 | conf=high
F: Relative size is found by dividing one size by the other after converting both to one unit; an order of magnitude is a factor of 10 | s4 | conf=high
F: Magnification = size of image / size of real object; answers in standard form where suitable | s1 | conf=high
F: Microscope total magnification = eyepiece magnification x objective magnification | s4 | conf=high
F: Light microscope tops out near x1500 and resolves about 0.2 micrometres; TEM goes to about x1 000 000 and resolves under 1 nm | s4,s1 | conf=med
F: Electron microscopes revealed many more sub-cellular structures; AQA limits this to differences in magnification and resolution | s1,s4 | conf=high
F: Required practical 1: observe, draw and label plant and animal cells with a light microscope; magnification scale must be included; covers AT 1 and AT 7 | s1,s2 | conf=high
F: Practical method: start on low power (eg x10), coarse focus then fine focus, thin specimen under a coverslip lowered gently to avoid air bubbles, stain for contrast, then move to high power (eg x40) | s4,s2 | conf=high
F: Typical specimens are onion epidermis (plant) and cheek cells (animal) | s4,s2 | conf=med
F: Drawings: a low-power plan shows tissue regions; a high-power drawing shows detail of a single cell; all must be labelled | s4 | conf=high
F: An eyepiece graticule is calibrated against a stage micrometer for each objective; one division's length differs between objectives | s4,s2 | conf=high
F: Risks to note: too-bright illumination, stains, glass coverslips and slides, mounted needles | s4 | conf=high
DEF: magnification | how many times larger an image is than the real object | GCSE
DEF: resolution | ability to see two close points as separate; more detail at higher resolution | GCSE
DEF: order of magnitude | difference in size expressed as a factor of ten | GCSE
DEF: standard form | A x 10^n with A between 1 and 10 | GCSE
DEF: eyepiece graticule | scale in the eyepiece, calibrated with a stage micrometer, used to measure cells | GCSE
MIS: Magnification and resolution are the same | Magnification enlarges; resolution is how much fine detail shows, and limits the useful magnification | s4
MIS: Only plant cells have mitochondria | Plant and animal cells both respire, so both have mitochondria; plants have chloroplasts as well | s4,s3
MIS: Cell wall and cell membrane are the same | Wall is rigid cellulose outside; membrane is thin and selective | s4,s1
EXAM: Convert image and real size to the same unit before dividing | s4
EXAM: Include a magnification scale and full labels on drawings for the required practical | s1,s4
NUM: Graticule worked example: 250 micrometres spans 51 divisions, so 1 division = 4.9 micrometres; a 12-division cell is about 59 micrometres | s4
NUM: Leaf cell 70 micrometres vs red blood cell 7 micrometres = 10 times; cheek cell 70 micrometres vs bacterium 0.5 micrometres = 140 times | s4
VAR: AQA numbers microscopy content 4.1.1.5 and the practical under 4.1.1.2; check the current spec if a resource disagrees | s1
