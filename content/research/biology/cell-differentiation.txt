F: A multicellular organism begins as one fertilised egg cell (zygote) that divides many times and whose daughter cells also differentiate into cells with different roles | aqa,bbc | conf=high
F: Differentiation means an unspecialised cell becoming a more specialised cell type, with features that suit a specific job | aqa,bbc | conf=high
F: A differentiating cell gains different sub-cellular structures, which let it carry out its particular function; once it has done so it is a specialised cell | aqa | conf=high
F: Importance: differentiation is what lets a multicellular organism have cells, tissues and organ systems each doing a distinct job (transport, movement, nerve signalling, reproduction, photosynthesis and so on) | aqa,bbc | conf=med
F: Most types of animal cell differentiate early in development | aqa | conf=high
F: Many plant cells keep the ability to differentiate throughout life; meristem cells near shoot and root tips divide and can become any plant cell type at any time | aqa,bbc | conf=high
F: In plants, new cells made near a shoot tip become differentiated as they age and move away from the tip (they enlarge and develop vacuoles) | bbc | conf=med
F: In mature animals, cell division is mainly limited to repair and replacement | aqa | conf=high
F: Adult stem cells exist in limited numbers in some tissues and form only related cell types (e.g. bone marrow gives blood and immune cells) | aqa,bbc | conf=high
F: Cells do not all make every protein: each cell expresses only a subset of its genes, which saves energy and space, so differences between cell types come from which genes are used, not from which genes are present | os | conf=med
DEF: differentiation | process by which an unspecialised cell becomes a specialised cell type with structures suited to a function | GCSE
DEF: specialised cell | a cell that has differentiated and has sub-cellular structures that let it do a particular job | GCSE
DEF: undifferentiated cell | a cell not yet specialised, e.g. a stem cell | GCSE
DEF: stem cell | undifferentiated cell that can produce more cells of its own type and cells that go on to differentiate | GCSE
DEF: meristem | plant region (near shoot and root tips) where cells divide and can differentiate into any plant cell type | GCSE
MIS: differentiated cells in an organism have different DNA | all body cells carry the same genetic information; they differ in which genes are expressed (os; mitosis gives cells with the same genetic information, bbc)
MIS: animals and plants keep differentiating equally | most animal cell types differentiate early; many plant cells keep the ability for life (aqa)
MIS: specialised animal cells keep dividing freely in adults | adult division is mostly repair and replacement (aqa)
EXAM: AQA lists examples to know: sperm, nerve and muscle cells in animals; root hair, xylem and phloem cells in plants; answers should link a structure to a function | aqa
EXAM: command word explain the importance of differentiation: link specialisation to carrying out a function in an organism with many cell types | aqa
