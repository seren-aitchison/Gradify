F: Light microscope history, simplified: Janssen spectacle makers tried lenses in tubes in 1590s giving roughly x3 to x9; Hooke drew cells with a compound microscope in 1650 (BBC date); van Leeuwenhoek used a single spherical lens up to about x275; lens quality improved through the 1800s | bbc-cellmeas | conf=med
F: Hooke named "cell" from cork in his 1665 Micrographia; van Leeuwenhoek found bacteria and protozoa in the 1670s; Schleiden and Schwann proposed cell theory in the late 1830s | os-bio2e | conf=high
F: Staining adds contrast to colourless cells in light microscopy; staining usually kills cells; a coverslip prevents drying and protects the lens | bbc-cellmeas,os-bio2e | conf=high
F: Electron microscopes use electron beams not light; electrons have much shorter wavelength than light so resolve finer detail | os-bio2e,os-micro | conf=high
F: Transmission EM (TEM) beam passes through very thin sections and shows internal structure; scanning EM (SEM) scans surfaces and gives 3D surface views | bbc-cellmeas,os-bio2e,os-micro | conf=high
F: Electron microscope specimens are prepared in ways that kill them and need a vacuum, so living cells cannot be viewed | os-bio2e,os-micro | conf=high
F: Electron microscopes are bulkier and more expensive than light microscopes | os-bio2e | conf=high
F: Ribosomes are visible only with an electron microscope; mitochondria are visible with light microscope but not in detail | bbc-cellmeas | conf=high
F: Light microscope stops being useful beyond about x1000 to x1500 because enlarging further adds no detail | bbc-cellmeas,os-micro | conf=high
DEF: magnification | how many times larger an image appears than the real object | GCSE
DEF: resolution (resolving power) | ability to see two close points as separate; higher means finer detail and a clearer image | GCSE
DEF: micrograph | photograph of a microscope image | GCSE
DEF: sub-cellular structure | structure smaller than a cell found inside it, e.g. ribosome | GCSE
MIS: more magnification always gives more detail | detail needs resolution; enlarging beyond resolution only gives a bigger blur
MIS: electron microscopes simply magnify more, nothing else | they also have far better resolving power, which is what reveals new structures
MIS: electron microscopes can look at live cells | preparation and vacuum kill the specimen
EXAM: Mixing units in magnification = image / real size loses marks; convert both to same unit first (mm to um multiply by 1000) | bbc-cellmeas
