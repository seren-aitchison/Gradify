## Changes of materials
LV: Year 5 to Year 6 · Evaluation
S: Materials can change in reversible and irreversible ways. This lesson explains dissolving, separating mixtures and changes that make new materials, such as burning and rusting.
O: Explain dissolving and separate mixtures by filtering, sieving and evaporating
O: Distinguish reversible from irreversible changes

H: Mixing and dissolving
P: When some solids, such as salt or sugar, are stirred into water they **dissolve**, forming a **solution**. The solid seems to disappear, but it is still there: you can get it back by **evaporating** the water. A material that dissolves is **soluble**; one that does not, such as sand, is **insoluble**. Mixtures can be separated in different ways: **sieving** separates large solids from small ones, **filtering** separates an insoluble solid from a liquid, and **evaporating** gets a dissolved solid back from a solution. Dissolving, mixing and changes of state are **reversible changes**. In Year 5 the national curriculum asks pupils to explain that some changes result in the formation of **new materials** and are usually **irreversible**, such as burning and the action of acid on bicarbonate of soda.
H: Reversible and irreversible changes
- **Reversible**: melting, freezing, dissolving, evaporating, mixing sand and water.
- **Irreversible**: burning wood, cooking an egg, baking a cake, rusting iron.
- Mixing vinegar and bicarbonate of soda makes a new gas, carbon dioxide.
- Signs of a new material: bubbles of gas, a colour change, light or heat given out, a smell.
- Pupils learn about scientists such as **Spencer Silver**, whose adhesive was used for sticky notes, and **Ruth Benerito**, who developed wrinkle-free cotton.
H: Separating mixtures
- **Sieve**: separates gravel from sand, or flour from lumps.
- **Filter paper**: separates sand from water.
- **Evaporate**: leave salty water in a warm place to get the salt back.
- **Magnet**: separates iron filings from sand.
- Choose the method by thinking about the properties of the materials.
X: Separating sand, salt and water.
> A pupil mixes sand, salt and water together.
> Pour the mixture through filter paper: the sand stays behind, and salty water drips through.
> Leave the salty water in a warm place: the water evaporates and salt crystals are left.
> All three materials are recovered, showing these were reversible changes.
K: Dissolving, mixing and changes of state are reversible and mixtures can be separated by sieving, filtering and evaporating, while burning, rusting and reactions such as vinegar with bicarbonate of soda make new materials and are irreversible.
!: Thinking dissolved sugar has disappeared. It is still in the water, which tastes sweet, and it can be recovered by evaporating the water.
