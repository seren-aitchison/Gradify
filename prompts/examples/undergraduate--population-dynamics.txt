## Population dynamics
LV: Year 1 · Application
S: Populations change in size over time. This lesson explains exponential and logistic growth and the factors that regulate populations.
O: Describe exponential and logistic population growth
O: Define carrying capacity
O: Distinguish density-dependent and density-independent factors
H: Growth models
- Population change = births + immigration − deaths − emigration.
- **Exponential growth**: J-shaped curve when resources are unlimited.
- **Logistic growth**: S-shaped curve as growth slows near the **carrying capacity** (K).
- Carrying capacity is the maximum population an environment can support.
D: bio-logistic-growth
H: Regulating factors
- **Density-dependent factors** have stronger effects at higher density: competition, predation, disease.
- **Density-independent factors** act regardless of density: weather, floods, fires.
- Populations may overshoot K and crash.
- **r-selected species** reproduce quickly; **K-selected species** have few offspring with much care.
P: Predator-prey cycles, such as those of lynx and snowshoe hares in Canada, show linked oscillations. Human population has grown from about 1 billion in 1800 to over 8 billion today. Understanding population dynamics is essential for managing fisheries, pests and endangered species.
X: Yeast in a flask.
> A few yeast cells are added to a sugar solution.
> At first they multiply slowly, then rapidly in an exponential phase.
> As sugar runs out and ethanol builds up, growth slows.
> The population levels off at the carrying capacity.
K: Population size changes with births, deaths and migration; exponential growth occurs with unlimited resources and logistic growth levels at carrying capacity, regulated by density-dependent and density-independent factors.
!: Thinking populations stay exactly at carrying capacity. They fluctuate around it and can overshoot.
