## The European Reformation
LV: GCSE and A level · Application
S: In 1517 Martin Luther's protest against indulgences began a movement that split Western Christianity. This lesson explains Luther's ideas, the spread of Protestantism through print and princes, the work of Calvin, and the Catholic response.
O: Explain why Luther challenged the Catholic Church
O: Describe how Protestantism spread across Europe
O: Evaluate the success of the Catholic Reformation
H: Luther
P: In 1517 the friar Johann Tetzel was selling **indulgences**, which promised to reduce time in purgatory, to raise money for St Peter's in Rome. Martin Luther, a monk and professor at Wittenberg, published his **Ninety-Five Theses** on 31 October 1517. He came to argue that salvation came through faith alone, that the Bible was the only authority, and that all believers were priests. At the **Diet of Worms** in 1521 he refused to recant.
H: Spread
- **Printing** spread Luther's pamphlets and his German Bible quickly and widely.
- **Princes** protected reformers and took control of churches in their lands.
- **Huldrych Zwingli** led reform in Zurich, and **John Calvin** in Geneva, whose Institutes (1536) shaped Reformed churches across Europe.
- The **Peace of Augsburg** (1555) allowed each German prince to choose Lutheranism or Catholicism for his lands.
H: The Catholic Reformation
P: The **Council of Trent** (1545 to 1563) reaffirmed Catholic doctrine and reformed the training of priests. The **Jesuits**, founded by Ignatius of Loyola in 1540, built schools and sent missionaries across Europe and the world. The Inquisition and the Index of forbidden books tried to stop Protestant ideas.
X: How successful was the Catholic Reformation?
> Success: southern Europe, Poland and much of central Europe stayed or became Catholic.
> Limits: northern Germany, Scandinavia, the Dutch Republic and Britain remained Protestant.
> Judgement: it halted and partly reversed Protestant expansion, but could not restore a united Western Church.
K: Luther's Ninety-Five Theses (1517) against indulgences led to a doctrine of faith alone; print and princes spread Protestantism, Calvin shaped Reformed churches, and the Council of Trent (1545 to 1563) and the Jesuits led a Catholic revival.
!: Thinking Luther meant to split the Church in 1517. He sought reform; division followed from conflict over authority.
