## Solid-state inorganic chemistry
LV: Year 1 · Chemical reasoning
S: Solid-state chemistry studies extended inorganic solids. This lesson explores structures, defects and properties of inorganic solids.

H: Structure types
- **Rock salt** (NaCl): 6:6 coordination.
- **Caesium chloride**: 8:8 coordination.
- **Fluorite** (CaF₂): 8:4 coordination.
- **Perovskite** (CaTiO₃): ABX₃, important in electronics and solar cells.
D: chem-perovskite
H: Defects
- **Schottky** defects: missing cation-anion pairs.
- **Frenkel** defects: an ion moved to an interstitial site.
- **Non-stoichiometric** compounds such as Fe₀.₉₅O have variable composition.
- Defects allow **ionic conduction** in solid electrolytes.
P: Radius ratio helps predict structure: larger cations fit more anions around them. Perovskite oxides show ferroelectric and superconducting properties, while lead halide perovskites are promising solar cell materials. Zirconia doped with yttria conducts oxide ions in fuel cells and oxygen sensors.
X: Ionic conduction in doped zirconia.
> Y³⁺ replaces some Zr⁴⁺ in ZrO₂.
> To balance charge, some oxide sites are left vacant.
> Oxide ions hop between vacancies at high temperature.
> The solid conducts electricity through moving O²⁻ ions.
K: Inorganic solids adopt structure types such as rock salt, caesium chloride, fluorite and perovskite, contain Schottky and Frenkel defects and non-stoichiometry and these features give useful properties such as ionic conduction.
!: Thinking all crystals are perfect. Real solids always contain defects, which control many properties.
