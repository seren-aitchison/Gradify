Study glossary

The learning-science terms behind good revision, in plain English.

Active recall
Active recall is studying by retrieving information from memory, for example answering a flashcard or writing what you know on a blank page, instead of re-reading it.
Cornell notes
Cornell notes divide a page into a wide notes column, a narrow cue column for questions or keywords, and a summary at the bottom.
Desirable difficulties
Desirable difficulties are study conditions that make learning feel harder in the moment but improve long-term memory, such as testing, spacing and interleaving.
Dual coding
Dual coding is combining words with visuals, such as diagrams, timelines and mind maps, so the same idea is stored both verbally and visually.
Elaborative interrogation
Elaborative interrogation is asking yourself why and how a fact is true, and answering from what you already know.
Feynman technique
The Feynman technique is learning by explaining a topic in simple words, as if teaching a beginner, then going back to the source wherever the explanation breaks down.
Forgetting curve
The forgetting curve shows how quickly memory fades after learning: steeply in the first hours and days, then more slowly.
FSRS (Free Spaced Repetition Scheduler)
FSRS is an open-source spaced repetition algorithm that predicts how likely you are to remember each card and schedules the next review for when that chance drops to a target, typically 90%.
Interleaving
Interleaving is mixing different topics or problem types in one study session (A, B, C, A, C, B) instead of practising one type at a time (A, A, A, B, B, B).
Leitner system
The Leitner system is a paper flashcard method using numbered boxes: a card you answer correctly moves up a box and is reviewed less often; a card you miss goes back to box 1.
Metacognition
Metacognition is thinking about your own thinking: judging what you know, planning how to learn and checking whether it's working.
Pomodoro technique
The Pomodoro technique is working in focused 25-minute blocks with a 5-minute break after each, and a longer break after four.
Retrieval practice
Retrieval practice is the research term for learning by recalling information, also called the testing effect: being tested on material improves later memory of it more than restudying it.
Spaced repetition
Spaced repetition is reviewing material at increasing intervals, such as after 1, 3, 7 and 14 days, timed so each review comes just before you'd forget.
Spacing effect
The spacing effect is the finding that we remember more when study is spread over several sessions than when the same time is spent in one block (cramming).
