University Study Skills practice questions

36 questions from Gradify's free University Study Skills course, with the answer and why behind each reveal. Answer before you open it: that's what makes it stick.

Starting University and Independent Learning

  1. Which statement best describes how university study differs from school? (Foundation recall)
    • Attendance is checked in every class by every tutor
    • Contact hours usually increase
    • Students take responsibility for most of their own learning time
    • Teachers cover everything that will be assessed
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Students take responsibility for most of their own learning time. Contact hours usually fall and much of the week is independent study; teaching guides rather than covers everything that will be assessed. Revise Starting University and Independent Learning →

  2. A 15-credit module has 30 contact hours. About how many hours of independent study does it expect? (Exam style)
    • About 120
    • About 15
    • About 30
    • About 300
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    About 120. 15 credits × 10 hours = 150 notional hours; 150 − 30 contact hours leaves about 120 hours of independent study. Revise Starting University and Independent Learning →

  3. A student says: "University is just A level but harder." Which response is most accurate? (Stretch)
    • The main change is responsibility for independent learning and new academic conventions, not only harder content
    • The main difference is that there are no deadlines at university
    • They are right: the only difference is the level of difficulty of the content
    • They are wrong: university content is usually easier than A level content
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The main change is responsibility for independent learning and new academic conventions, not only harder content. Content is more advanced, but the bigger adjustment is managing independent study and learning a discipline's ways of reading and arguing; deadlines certainly exist. Revise Starting University and Independent Learning →

How Pupils Learn

  1. Which statement best captures learning as the ITTECF describes it? (Foundation recall)
    • A lasting change in pupils' capabilities or understanding
    • Being able to repeat what the teacher has just said
    • Completing all the tasks set in a lesson
    • Showing high engagement throughout a lesson
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A lasting change in pupils' capabilities or understanding. The framework defines learning by what lasts. Completion, repetition and engagement can happen without any lasting change in long-term memory. Revise How Pupils Learn →

  2. A trainee notes that every pupil answered the plenary questions correctly. What is the most accurate conclusion? (Exam style)
    • No further practice of the skill is needed
    • Pupils can perform the skill now; retention needs checking later
    • Pupils have learned the skill securely
    • The lesson was too easy and should be harder next time
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Pupils can perform the skill now; retention needs checking later. Correct answers immediately after teaching show current performance. Whether it has become learning is only visible after a delay, so the next step is planned retrieval. Revise How Pupils Learn →

  3. Which lesson feature is the strongest evidence that pupils have learned last month's content? (Stretch)
    • Accurate answers to unprompted questions on it at the start of today's lesson
    • Every pupil completing last month's worksheet
    • Pupils saying they found the topic easy
    • Pupils working quietly on the topic for a whole hour
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Accurate answers to unprompted questions on it at the start of today's lesson. Unprompted retrieval after a delay is direct evidence of a change in long-term memory. Quiet work, completion and self-report are proxies that can occur without retention. Revise How Pupils Learn →

Time Management, Lectures and Note-making

  1. Roughly how many notional hours does a 120-credit year represent? (Foundation recall)
    • About 1,200 hours
    • About 12,000 hours
    • About 120 hours
    • About 400 hours
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    About 1,200 hours. Applying the standard result: 120 credits × 10 hours. Revise Time Management, Lectures and Note-making →

  2. Where does most learning on a UK degree take place? (Exam style)
    • In exams
    • In independent study outside classes
    • In timetabled lectures only
    • On placement only
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    In independent study outside classes. Teaching hours are a fraction of the total. Revise Time Management, Lectures and Note-making →

  3. A student's audit shows four hours a day lost to unplanned scrolling. What is the most useful response? (Stretch)
    • Drop all social activities
    • Feel guilty and change nothing
    • Plan specific study blocks and limit phone use
    • Stop attending lectures
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Plan specific study blocks and limit phone use. Applying the standard result: Use the audit to plan. Revise Time Management, Lectures and Note-making →

Reading Academic Texts and Critical Thinking

  1. Which of these is usually a tertiary source? (Foundation recall)
    • A dataset published by the ONS
    • A historical letter in an archive
    • A peer-reviewed research article
    • An encyclopaedia entry
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    An encyclopaedia entry. Tertiary sources compile and summarise existing knowledge, as encyclopaedias and handbooks do. Revise Reading Academic Texts and Critical Thinking →

  2. A student wants to know the current state of research on a topic. Which text is the most efficient starting point? (Exam style)
    • A newspaper report of one study
    • A recent review article
    • A single chapter in a general textbook from 2005
    • The oldest article on the topic
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A recent review article. A recent review article surveys and evaluates current research, so it maps the field quickly and points to key studies. Revise Reading Academic Texts and Critical Thinking →

  3. A media historian studies how newspapers reported the 1984 miners' strike. For this project, what are the newspaper reports? (Stretch)
    • Grey literature that should be avoided
    • Primary sources
    • Secondary sources because journalists wrote them
    • Tertiary sources
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Primary sources. Whether a source is primary depends on the research question: here the reports themselves are the material being analysed. Revise Reading Academic Texts and Critical Thinking →

Academic Writing

  1. Which feature is most characteristic of academic writing? (Foundation recall)
    • Claims supported by referenced evidence
    • Dramatic headlines to grab attention
    • Informal language to engage the reader
    • Personal anecdotes as the main evidence
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Claims supported by referenced evidence. Academic writing builds a reasoned argument from evidence that readers can trace through references. Revise Academic Writing →

  2. Which sentence is the most appropriate in academic style? (Exam style)
    • Attendance has gone through the roof this year
    • Attendance is up, which is absolutely brilliant
    • Everyone knows attendance has improved a lot
    • The data indicate a modest rise in attendance (Author, year)
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The data indicate a modest rise in attendance (Author, year). It is precise, cautious and referenced, without informal or exaggerated language. Revise Academic Writing →

  3. Lea and Street (1998) argue that student writing is best seen as academic literacies. What does this imply for a joint-honours student? (Stretch)
    • Only English students need to learn about writing
    • She can use one writing style for every module
    • She may need to adapt her writing to each subject's conventions
    • Writing conventions are the same everywhere in a university
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    She may need to adapt her writing to each subject's conventions. If conventions differ between disciplines, students working across subjects must learn the expectations of each. Revise Academic Writing →

Referencing and Academic Integrity

  1. Which is a purpose of referencing? (Foundation recall)
    • Letting readers trace and check your evidence
    • Making an essay reach its word count
    • Proving that every claim is true
    • Showing which sources the marker should read
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Letting readers trace and check your evidence. References let readers find the sources behind your claims and judge them for themselves. Revise Referencing and Academic Integrity →

  2. A student cites (Jones, 2019) in her essay, but Jones is missing from the reference list. What is the problem? (Exam style)
    • Jones should have been in a footnote instead
    • Only books need to be in the reference list
    • Readers cannot find the full details of the source
    • The citation is in the wrong referencing style
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Readers cannot find the full details of the source. Each in-text citation must match a full entry so that the source can be identified and checked. Revise Referencing and Academic Integrity →

  3. Why does referencing a strong source make an argument more persuasive? (Stretch)
    • A longer reference list automatically earns higher marks
    • Citing anyone makes a claim true
    • Readers can see the claim rests on traceable, credible evidence
    • References show the marker how hard the student worked
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Readers can see the claim rests on traceable, credible evidence. The persuasive force comes from evidence the reader can check, not from the number of references. Revise Referencing and Academic Integrity →

Research and Information Literacy

  1. Which stage involves judging whether a source is reliable? (Foundation recall)
    • Communicating your findings
    • Evaluating the information found
    • Identifying what you need to know
    • Organising files on your laptop
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Evaluating the information found. Evaluation checks quality, relevance and bias. Revise Research and Information Literacy →

  2. Why does information literacy matter more today than in the past? (Exam style)
    • Information never changes once it is published
    • Libraries no longer hold any books at all
    • Search engines now always rank the best sources first
    • There is far more information, and much of it is unreliable or misleading
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    There is far more information, and much of it is unreliable or misleading. Abundance makes critical judgement essential. Revise Research and Information Literacy →

  3. A student finds a convincing blog post that supports her argument. What should she do next? (Stretch)
    • Check who wrote it, what evidence it uses and whether better sources exist
    • Cite it immediately because it agrees with her view
    • Copy it into her essay without referencing it
    • Ignore all sources that disagree with her argument
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Check who wrote it, what evidence it uses and whether better sources exist. Evaluation comes before use, however well a source fits your argument. Revise Research and Information Literacy →

Numeracy and Data Skills

  1. Where can students often get help with maths at university? (Foundation recall)
    • A maths and statistics support centre run by the university
    • Nowhere, as help is not available
    • Only by paying a private tutor
    • Only from friends studying the same course
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A maths and statistics support centre run by the university. Most universities offer free maths and statistics support. Revise Numeracy and Data Skills →

  2. Why does maths anxiety lower performance? (Exam style)
    • Anxiety makes numbers change on the page
    • Anxious students always have lower ability
    • Calculators stop working for anxious people
    • Worry uses up working memory needed for calculations
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Worry uses up working memory needed for calculations. Anxiety competes for limited mental resources. Revise Numeracy and Data Skills →

  3. Which strategy best builds number confidence? (Stretch)
    • Avoiding all numbers until the exam
    • Copying answers from classmates
    • Doing every calculation in your head at speed
    • Regular, low-pressure practice starting with familiar problems
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Regular, low-pressure practice starting with familiar problems. Gradual practice builds skill and confidence. Revise Numeracy and Data Skills →

Professional Communication

  1. In the transmission model, what does "noise" refer to? (Foundation recall)
    • Anything that distorts or blocks the message on its way to the receiver
    • The channel chosen to carry the message, such as email or a meeting
    • The reply the receiver sends to show they understood the message
    • The sender's process of turning an idea into words or images
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Anything that distorts or blocks the message on its way to the receiver. Noise is interference of any kind: physical, semantic, psychological or cultural. Feedback is the reply; encoding is the sender's process; the channel is the medium. Revise Professional Communication →

  2. You must tell a busy operations director that a supplier will deliver late. What should come first in your email? (Exam style)
    • A full history of the supplier relationship and earlier problems
    • An apology for writing, followed by a summary of your own workload
    • The detailed explanation of what went wrong in the supplier's factory
    • The new delivery date, its impact and any decision you need from them
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The new delivery date, its impact and any decision you need from them. A senior reader needs the news and its consequence first so they can act; background can follow or be attached for those who want it. Revise Professional Communication →

  3. A project update is accurate, complete and grammatically perfect, yet the steering group takes no decision. What is the most likely reason? (Stretch)
    • It contained too much information about the project budget
    • It never made clear what decision was needed from them, or by when
    • It used too few technical terms to sound credible to senior readers
    • It was sent by email rather than printed and circulated on paper
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It never made clear what decision was needed from them, or by when. Accuracy isn't enough: a message without a clear purpose and call to action leaves the reader unsure what to do. Channel and jargon are secondary here. Revise Professional Communication →

Exams and Revision

  1. Which exam type uses selected answers from a list of options? (Foundation recall)
    • A multiple-choice question exam
    • A take-home dissertation
    • An oral viva exam
    • An unseen essay exam
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A multiple-choice question exam. MCQ exams ask students to choose answers from options. Revise Exams and Revision →

  2. Why are open-book exams not an excuse to skip revision? (Exam style)
    • Notes are always confiscated at the start
    • Open-book exams do not count towards the degree
    • Questions are always identical to past papers
    • There is too little time to look everything up without good understanding
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    There is too little time to look everything up without good understanding. Understanding and organised notes are essential under time limits. Revise Exams and Revision →

  3. Which preparation best suits an unseen essay exam? (Stretch)
    • Memorising single facts without context
    • Only reading the textbook once
    • Practising multiple-choice questions only
    • Practising planning and writing timed essay answers
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Practising planning and writing timed essay answers. Practice in the exam format builds the needed skills. Revise Exams and Revision →

Sleep, Stress and Rest

  1. Which hormone is released mainly during deep sleep and supports growth and repair? (Foundation recall)
    • Adrenaline
    • Glucagon
    • Growth hormone
    • Insulin
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Growth hormone. Growth hormone secretion peaks during slow-wave (deep) sleep. Revise Sleep, Stress and Rest →

  2. A driver has slept 5 hours a night all week and feels "fine". What does research suggest? (Exam style)
    • Feeling fine always means performance is normal
    • He has fully adapted and is at no extra risk
    • His reaction time and attention are likely impaired, increasing crash risk
    • Short sleep improves alertness after a week
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    His reaction time and attention are likely impaired, increasing crash risk. Chronic restriction impairs performance despite little feeling of sleepiness. Revise Sleep, Stress and Rest →

  3. Which is the best evaluation of the glymphatic theory of sleep? (Stretch)
    • It explains every known function of sleep on its own
    • It has been disproved by recent research and abandoned
    • It is proven beyond doubt by large trials in human volunteers
    • It is supported by animal studies, but evidence in humans is still developing
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It is supported by animal studies, but evidence in humans is still developing. Brain clearance during sleep has been shown in mice; human evidence is promising but limited. Revise Sleep, Stress and Rest →

Knowing Yourself

  1. Which is the best description of a career as careers professionals use the word? (Foundation recall)
    • A profession that needs a degree and a formal licence
    • The highest job title a person reaches in one employer
    • The lifelong pattern of a person's learning, work and life roles
    • The paid jobs a person holds after leaving full-time study
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The lifelong pattern of a person's learning, work and life roles. Careers professionals use "career" broadly: education, work, caring and volunteering all form part of it, not just paid graduate roles or promotions. Revise Knowing Yourself →

  2. Sam has a strong degree, internships and good references but can't find a job during a recession. What does this show about employability? (Exam style)
    • Being employable makes work more likely but does not guarantee it
    • Employability is only measured by whether a person is in work
    • Sam must lack the skills that employers really value
    • Sam's degree subject must be the main cause of the problem
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Being employable makes work more likely but does not guarantee it. Employability describes a person's assets; the labour market also matters, so an employable person can be unemployed when demand for workers is weak. Revise Knowing Yourself →

  3. Which is the strongest reason the CDI framework treats career development as lifelong? (Stretch)
    • Employers no longer offer any permanent contracts to new graduate employees
    • Most careers advisers prefer to work with adults rather than with school pupils
    • People change roles and sectors and return to learning, so they must keep managing transitions
    • Skills learned at school stop being useful after the age of about twenty-five
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    People change roles and sectors and return to learning, so they must keep managing transitions. Non-linear careers with repeated transitions are the norm, so the ability to manage change is needed throughout life, not just at one decision point. Revise Knowing Yourself →
