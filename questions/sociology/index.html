Foundations of Sociology

  1. Which is the best definition of sociology? (Foundation recall)
    • The study of how markets set prices
    • The study of individual personality and the mind
    • The study of past events and documents
    • The systematic study of social life, groups and institutions
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The systematic study of social life, groups and institutions. Sociology studies social life and institutions; psychology, economics and history have different main focuses. Revise Foundations of Sociology →

  2. Drop-out rates are higher among students who work over 20 hours a week. Which explanation is most sociological? (Exam style)
    • Financial pressure linked to background shapes students' ability to study
    • It is pure coincidence with no wider explanation
    • Those students are simply less intelligent than others
    • Those students lack the willpower to keep studying
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Financial pressure linked to background shapes students' ability to study. A sociological explanation links the pattern to social conditions, here financial circumstances, rather than to individual traits alone. Revise Foundations of Sociology →

  3. Which is the strongest reply to the claim that sociology is "just common sense"? (Stretch)
    • Common sense is always wrong about how society works
    • Common sense is contradictory and untested, while sociology checks claims against systematic evidence
    • Sociologists have degrees, so their views must be correct
    • Sociology uses longer and more technical words than common sense
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Common sense is contradictory and untested, while sociology checks claims against systematic evidence. The difference is method: sociology tests claims and reveals patterns invisible from individual experience. Revise Foundations of Sociology →

Classical Sociological Theory

  1. In which century did sociology emerge as a discipline? (Foundation recall)
    • The 16th century
    • The 17th century
    • The 19th century
    • The 20th century
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The 19th century. Sociology emerged in 19th-century Europe as thinkers responded to industrialisation and revolution. Revise Classical Sociological Theory →

  2. A thinker in 1860 studies why people moving from villages to cities feel cut off from community. Which change is this thinker responding to? (Exam style)
    • The internet
    • The invention of printing
    • The Roman Empire
    • Urbanisation
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Urbanisation. Rapid movement from countryside to cities was a defining change of the 19th century. Revise Classical Sociological Theory →

  3. Which is the strongest criticism of the standard story of sociology's origins? (Stretch)
    • It claims sociology was invented in the 20th century by Americans
    • It focuses on cities rather than on rural life in every case
    • It gives too much attention to the role of industrialisation in Europe
    • It treats European development as universal and ignores colonialism and non-European thinkers
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It treats European development as universal and ignores colonialism and non-European thinkers. Postcolonial critics argue the canon misrepresents how modernity was made and who contributed to social thought. Revise Classical Sociological Theory →

Contemporary Sociological Theory

  1. Which assumption is central to functionalism? (Foundation recall)
    • Power works through discourse
    • Reality is constructed in conversation
    • Social order rests on shared values
    • Society is driven by class conflict
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Social order rests on shared values. Functionalists see value consensus as the basis of order. Revise Contemporary Sociological Theory →

  2. A sociologist argues that religion persists because it binds people to shared values. Which perspective is this? (Exam style)
    • Ethnomethodology
    • Functionalism
    • Marxism
    • Postmodernism
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Functionalism. Explaining an institution by its integrative function is functionalist. Revise Contemporary Sociological Theory →

  3. What is the teleological criticism of functionalism? (Stretch)
    • It explains an institution's existence by its effects
    • It focuses on individual meanings
    • It ignores the functions of institutions
    • It relies only on interviews
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It explains an institution's existence by its effects. Saying something exists because it is useful confuses consequences with causes. Revise Contemporary Sociological Theory →

Culture, Identity and Socialisation

  1. Which is an example of non-material culture? (Foundation recall)
    • A belief in fairness
    • A cathedral
    • A pair of jeans
    • A smartphone
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A belief in fairness. Beliefs are ideas rather than physical objects, so they are non-material culture. Revise Culture, Identity and Socialisation →

  2. Laws on online privacy develop years after social media spreads. This illustrates… (Exam style)
    • Counterculture
    • Cultural lag
    • Material culture only
    • Socialisation
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Cultural lag. Norms and laws catching up slowly with new technology is cultural lag. Revise Culture, Identity and Socialisation →

  3. Which is a criticism of Ogburn's idea of cultural lag? (Stretch)
    • Culture has no material side
    • Ideas can drive technological change
    • Norms always change first
    • Technology never changes
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Ideas can drive technological change. Values and beliefs can also lead change, rather than always lagging behind. Revise Culture, Identity and Socialisation →

Social Stratification and Inequality

  1. Who argued that stratification motivates talented people to fill important roles? (Foundation recall)
    • Davis and Moore
    • Karl Marx
    • Max Weber
    • Melvin Tumin
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Davis and Moore. Davis and Moore offered the classic functionalist theory in 1945. Revise Social Stratification and Inequality →

  2. A society where most people stay in the layer they were born into is… (Exam style)
    • A classless society
    • A closed system
    • A meritocracy
    • An open system
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A closed system. Little movement between layers marks a closed system. Revise Social Stratification and Inequality →

  3. Which is the strongest criticism of functionalist stratification theory? (Stretch)
    • It assumes rewards match importance
    • It denies inequality exists
    • It focuses only on gender
    • It ignores the economy entirely
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It assumes rewards match importance. Rewards may reflect power rather than functional importance. Revise Social Stratification and Inequality →

Gender and Sexuality

  1. Who popularised the sex–gender distinction in British sociology? (Foundation recall)
    • Ann Oakley
    • Judith Butler
    • Margaret Mead
    • Raewyn Connell
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Ann Oakley. Oakley's 1972 book set out the distinction. Revise Gender and Sexuality →

  2. In one society both men and women are expected to be gentle and nurturing. What does this suggest? (Exam style)
    • All societies are the same
    • Gender expectations are culturally shaped
    • Gender is purely biological
    • Sex doesn't exist
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Gender expectations are culturally shaped. Variation across societies points to social shaping. Revise Gender and Sexuality →

  3. Why has Mead's fieldwork been criticised? (Stretch)
    • Her findings may be overstated
    • She found no differences
    • She studied only one society
    • She used experiments
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Her findings may be overstated. Later anthropologists questioned the neatness of her contrasts. Revise Gender and Sexuality →

Race, Ethnicity and Migration

  1. Most human genetic variation is found… (Foundation recall)
    • Between racial groups
    • Only in Africa
    • Only in Europe
    • Within groups
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Within groups. Lewontin found about 85% of variation within groups. Revise Race, Ethnicity and Migration →

  2. A group seen as "non-white" in one century is later seen as "white". This supports the view that… (Exam style)
    • Categories are constructed
    • Census data are simply false
    • Genes changed very quickly
    • Race is fixed biology
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Categories are constructed. Changing classification reflects social construction. Revise Race, Ethnicity and Migration →

  3. Why do sociologists still study race if it is socially constructed? (Stretch)
    • Construction makes it unimportant
    • It has no effects
    • Perceived race shapes treatment and inequality
    • Race is biological after all
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Perceived race shapes treatment and inequality. Racism operates on perceived race, so studying race helps explain inequality. Revise Race, Ethnicity and Migration →

Family and Relationships

  1. Who argued the nuclear family is universal? (Foundation recall)
    • David Morgan
    • George Murdock
    • Jeffrey Weeks
    • Talcott Parsons
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    George Murdock. George Murdock studied 250 societies and claimed the nuclear family was universal in 1949. Revise Family and Relationships →

  2. Three friends rent a flat together and share a kitchen. In ONS terms they are… (Exam style)
    • A family but not a household
    • A household but not a family
    • A nuclear family
    • An extended family
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A household but not a family. Sharers form a household without being a family. Revise Family and Relationships →

  3. Why do definitions of the family matter for policy? (Stretch)
    • They are fixed by law forever
    • They decide eligibility
    • They have no practical effect
    • They only matter to researchers
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    They decide eligibility. Who counts as family affects access to support. Revise Family and Relationships →

Crime and Deviance

  1. Crime that is never reported or recorded is called… (Foundation recall)
    • Deviance amplification
    • Recorded crime
    • The dark figure of crime
    • White-collar crime
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The dark figure of crime. It is the hidden crime outside official statistics. Revise Crime and Deviance →

  2. Police recorded crime rises after new recording rules are introduced, while the CSEW is stable. What is the likely explanation? (Exam style)
    • Crime doubled
    • Recording practices changed
    • Reporting stopped
    • The survey is wrong
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Recording practices changed. Changes in recording can raise police figures without more crime. Revise Crime and Deviance →

  3. Why do sociologists ask whose crimes are counted? (Stretch)
    • All crimes are counted equally
    • Only street crime exists
    • Some crimes are under-recorded
    • Statistics are always complete
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Some crimes are under-recorded. Official data can hide crimes of the powerful. Revise Crime and Deviance →

Politics and Power

  1. Power accepted as legitimate is called… (Foundation recall)
    • Authority
    • Coercion
    • Hegemony only
    • Non-decision-making
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Authority. Weber distinguished legitimate authority from coercion. Revise Politics and Power →

  2. A government avoids ever debating a proposal for a wealth tax by keeping it out of parliamentary business. This is… (Exam style)
    • Charismatic authority
    • Non-decision-making
    • Open coercion
    • Pluralist bargaining
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Non-decision-making. Keeping issues off the agenda is the second dimension of power. Revise Politics and Power →

  3. Why is Lukes's third dimension criticised? (Stretch)
    • It denies that power exists at all
    • It ignores ideology completely
    • It is hard to observe and assumes false consciousness
    • It only counts visible decisions
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It is hard to observe and assumes false consciousness. Claims of hidden power can be hard to test. Revise Politics and Power →

Religion

  1. Durkheim defined religion in terms of beliefs and practices relating to… (Foundation recall)
    • Economic production
    • Individual choice
    • Sacred things
    • The state
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Sacred things. Durkheim centred religion on sacred things set apart from the everyday. Revise Religion →

  2. A researcher counts only groups that believe in a god as religious. This uses a… (Exam style)
    • Constructionist definition
    • Functional definition
    • Pluralist definition
    • Substantive definition
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Substantive definition. Defining by belief content is substantive. Revise Religion →

  3. Why does the choice of definition affect secularisation research? (Stretch)
    • All definitions give the same results
    • Definitions never affect data
    • Narrow definitions may find decline while broad ones find change
    • Only functional definitions measure belief
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Narrow definitions may find decline while broad ones find change. What counts as religion shapes the trend found. Revise Religion →

Work and Economy

  1. Taylor's method of breaking jobs into simple, timed tasks is called… (Foundation recall)
    • Emotional labour
    • Flexible specialisation
    • Post-Fordism
    • Scientific management
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Scientific management. Taylor called his approach scientific management. Revise Work and Economy →

  2. A factory introduces bells, clocking-in and fines for lateness to replace flexible working rhythms. Thompson would call this… (Exam style)
    • Deskilling only
    • Emotional labour
    • Flexible specialisation
    • Imposing time discipline
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Imposing time discipline. Clock-based control of workers' activity is what Thompson called time discipline. Revise Work and Economy →

  3. Why have feminists criticised classical accounts of industrial work? (Stretch)
    • They denied that factories existed
    • They focused only on women's paid work
    • They ignored unpaid domestic labour, mostly done by women
    • They studied only agricultural labour
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    They ignored unpaid domestic labour, mostly done by women. Domestic labour was left out of many accounts. Revise Work and Economy →

    • Hyperglobalist
    • Postcolonial
    • Sceptical
    • Transformationalist
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Hyperglobalist. Hyperglobalists see states losing power to markets. Revise Globalisation and Social Change →

  2. A researcher shows most trade happens within regions, not globally, and states still set key rules. This supports… (Exam style)
    • Cultural homogenisation
    • The hyperglobalist view
    • The sceptical view
    • Time–space compression
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The sceptical view. Sceptics doubt the world economy is truly global. Revise Globalisation and Social Change →

  3. What is distinctive about contemporary globalisation compared with earlier eras? (Stretch)
    • It has no historical roots at all
    • It involves no international trade
    • It reduced connections between places
    • Its speed, scale and density of connections
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Its speed, scale and density of connections. Digital technology intensifies older connections. Revise Globalisation and Social Change →

Urban Sociology and Population

  1. A city with more than 10 million people is called a… (Foundation recall)
    • Conurbation only
    • Market town
    • Megacity
    • Suburb
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Megacity. A megacity is defined as an urban area with more than 10 million residents. Revise Urban Sociology and Population →

  2. Families leave drought-hit farmland for a city where relatives say there is work. This illustrates… (Exam style)
    • Counter-urbanisation
    • Gentrification
    • Rural–urban migration
    • Suburbanisation

    • It denies that slums exist at all
    • It exaggerates the quality of rural life
    • It ignores cities altogether
    • It may overlook residents' agency and improvements
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

Health and Medicine

  1. Smoking, diet and exercise are examples of… (Foundation recall)
    • General socio-economic conditions
    • Genetic factors
    • Lifestyle factors
    • Social networks
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Lifestyle factors. Individual behaviours are lifestyle factors. Revise Health and Medicine →

  2. A worker in insecure night shifts eats irregularly and sleeps poorly. The rainbow model would explain this through… (Exam style)
    • Genetic factors alone
    • Medicalisation of sleep
    • The sick role in action
    • Working conditions shaping lifestyle
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Working conditions shaping lifestyle. Outer conditions shape individual behaviour. Revise Health and Medicine →

  3. Why do critics say the social determinants approach can be hard to act on? (Stretch)
    • It focuses only on genetic causes
    • It ignores social factors entirely
    • It is so broad that priorities are unclear
    • It rejects public health action
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It is so broad that priorities are unclear. Its breadth can make it hard to decide which policies to target first. Revise Health and Medicine →

Research Methods

  1. Following the same group of people over several years is a… (Foundation recall)
    • Case study design
    • Content analysis
    • Cross-sectional design
    • Longitudinal design
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Longitudinal design. Repeated measurement of the same units over time defines a longitudinal design. Revise Research Methods →

  2. A researcher with little funding wants to understand how members of one food bank volunteer team see their work. Which design fits best? (Exam style)
    • A cross-national comparison
    • A laboratory experiment
    • A national longitudinal survey
    • A qualitative case study
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A qualitative case study. A single setting, a question about meaning and limited resources suit a case study. Revise Research Methods →

  3. Why is starting from a favourite method a weak way to design research? (Stretch)
    • It always produces biased samples
    • It makes ethical approval impossible
    • Methods never affect findings
    • The method may not fit the question being asked
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The method may not fit the question being asked. The question should determine the design; a mismatched method gives weak evidence. Revise Research Methods →

Social Statistics and Data Science

  1. Housing tenure (owner, private renter, social renter) is… (Foundation recall)
    • Interval
    • Nominal
    • Ordinal
    • Ratio
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Nominal. The tenure categories have no inherent order, so the variable is nominal. Revise Social Statistics and Data Science →

  2. A researcher attaches each respondent's local area poverty rate to their survey record. What kind of variable is the poverty rate? (Exam style)
    • Aggregate data
    • Digital trace data
    • Individual-level data
    • Nominal data
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Aggregate data. It describes the area, not the individual. Revise Social Statistics and Data Science →

  3. Why shouldn't social class categories be averaged? (Stretch)
    • Averages are always misleading
    • Class can only be measured by income
    • Class has no effect on outcomes
    • They are categorical, not quantities
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    They are categorical, not quantities. Class categories classify positions; averaging them treats labels as numbers. Revise Social Statistics and Data Science →
