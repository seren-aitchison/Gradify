Medicine practice questions

36 questions from Gradify's free Medicine course, with the answer and why behind each reveal. Answer before you open it: that's what makes it stick.

Foundations of Medical Science

  1. Which organelle is the main site of ATP production by oxidative phosphorylation? (Foundation recall)
    • Golgi apparatus
    • Mitochondrion
    • Peroxisome
    • Rough endoplasmic reticulum
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Mitochondrion. Oxidative phosphorylation takes place on the inner mitochondrial membrane; the Golgi sorts proteins, the RER makes them and peroxisomes oxidise very-long-chain fatty acids. Revise Foundations of Medical Science →

  2. A drug used for gout and the vinca alkaloids used in chemotherapy share a target. What is it? (Exam style)
    • DNA topoisomerase
    • The bacterial-type ribosome
    • The Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase pump
    • Tubulin, the protein of microtubules
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Tubulin, the protein of microtubules. Colchicine and vincristine both stop microtubule polymerisation: one reduces neutrophil migration, the other arrests dividing cells in mitosis. Revise Foundations of Medical Science →

  3. A child inherits a mitochondrial DNA mutation. Which statement about the family is most accurate? (Stretch)
    • Each child of an affected parent has a 50% risk regardless of the parent's sex
    • It cannot be inherited because mitochondria are made fresh in each cell
    • It passes from father to son only, like a Y-linked gene
    • The mother passes it to all her children, but severity varies with the proportion of mutant mitochondria
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The mother passes it to all her children, but severity varies with the proportion of mutant mitochondria. Mitochondria come from the egg, so transmission is maternal; heteroplasmy (a mix of normal and mutant copies) explains the variable severity. Revise Foundations of Medical Science →

Clinical Method and Reasoning

  1. Which part of the history records the reaction a patient had to a drug? (Foundation recall)
    • Drug history, as the allergy and its reaction
    • Family history
    • Past surgical history
    • Systems review
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Drug history, as the allergy and its reaction. Allergies are documented with the drug history, always with the nature of the reaction so allergy can be distinguished from intolerance. Revise Clinical Method and Reasoning →

  2. A patient says she is "allergic to amoxicillin" because it gave her loose stools. How should this be recorded? (Exam style)
    • As a penicillin allergy, so all penicillins are avoided
    • As an intolerance (diarrhoea), not an allergy, with the reaction described
    • As anaphylaxis to be safe
    • It needn't be recorded at all
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    As an intolerance (diarrhoea), not an allergy, with the reaction described. Gastrointestinal upset is a predictable side effect, not an immune reaction. Labelling it as allergy would needlessly exclude penicillins; recording the reaction lets prescribers decide. Revise Clinical Method and Reasoning →

  3. Which question adds the most diagnostic value in a patient with a headache? (Stretch)
    • Did it reach its maximum intensity within a minute or so?
    • Do you drink coffee?
    • Have you ever had a headache before?
    • Is there anyone else at home?
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Did it reach its maximum intensity within a minute or so?. A thunderclap onset is a red flag for subarachnoid haemorrhage and changes management immediately. Discriminating questions shift probability; generic ones rarely do. Revise Clinical Method and Reasoning →

Cardiovascular Medicine

  1. What is normally heard at the start of isovolumetric contraction? (Foundation recall)
    • S1, from closure of the mitral and tricuspid valves
    • S2, from closure of the aortic and pulmonary valves
    • S3, from rapid early ventricular filling
    • S4, from atrial contraction into a stiff ventricle
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    S1, from closure of the mitral and tricuspid valves. Ventricular contraction closes the AV valves, producing S1, before the semilunar valves open. Revise Cardiovascular Medicine →

  2. A patient has heart rate 90/min, EDV 150 mL and ESV 90 mL. What is the cardiac output? (Exam style)
    • 13.5 L/min
    • 3.6 L/min
    • 5.4 L/min
    • 8.1 L/min
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    5.4 L/min. SV = 150 − 90 = 60 mL; CO = 90 × 60 = 5,400 mL/min = 5.4 L/min (EF 40%). Revise Cardiovascular Medicine →

  3. A 75-year-old with long-standing hypertension has a fourth heart sound. What does it most likely indicate? (Stretch)
    • A normal finding in a healthy older adult
    • Atrial contraction into a stiff, hypertrophied left ventricle
    • Rapid filling of a dilated ventricle in systolic failure
    • Severe aortic regurgitation with a dilated ventricle
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Atrial contraction into a stiff, hypertrophied left ventricle. S4 occurs in late diastole when the atrium pushes blood into a non-compliant ventricle, as in left ventricular hypertrophy. Revise Cardiovascular Medicine →

Respiratory Medicine

  1. What happens to oxygen release from haemoglobin when the curve shifts to the right? (Foundation recall)
    • Carbon dioxide transport stops
    • Haemoglobin can no longer carry oxygen
    • Oxygen is held more tightly by haemoglobin
    • Oxygen is released more easily to the tissues
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Oxygen is released more easily to the tissues. A right shift lowers haemoglobin's affinity for oxygen, helping unloading in active tissues. Revise Respiratory Medicine →

  2. In lobar pneumonia, consolidated alveoli are perfused but not ventilated. What is this called? (Exam style)
    • Dead space
    • Diffusion limitation
    • Hypoventilation
    • Shunt
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Shunt. Perfused but unventilated alveoli create a shunt, causing hypoxaemia that responds poorly to oxygen. Revise Respiratory Medicine →

  3. A patient on 60% oxygen has a PaO2 of 12 kPa. What is the best interpretation? (Stretch)
    • A normal result, since 12 kPa is within the normal range
    • An error, because PaO2 can't be measured on oxygen
    • Hyperoxia needing the oxygen to be stopped
    • Severe impairment of gas exchange, as this value is far below what 60% oxygen should give
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Severe impairment of gas exchange, as this value is far below what 60% oxygen should give. On high inspired oxygen, PaO2 should be much higher; the result shows a large A–a gradient. Revise Respiratory Medicine →

Gastroenterology and Hepatology

  1. Where is iron mainly absorbed? (Foundation recall)
    • The colon
    • The duodenum and proximal jejunum
    • The stomach
    • The terminal ileum
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The duodenum and proximal jejunum. Iron absorption is concentrated in the proximal small intestine. Revise Gastroenterology and Hepatology →

  2. Which enzyme deficiency causes bloating and diarrhoea after drinking milk? (Exam style)
    • Lactase
    • Lipase
    • Pepsin
    • Trypsin
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Lactase. Lactase in the brush border breaks down lactose; deficiency leaves it to be fermented. Revise Gastroenterology and Hepatology →

  3. A patient has had a total gastrectomy. Which deficiency should be anticipated and monitored? (Stretch)
    • Sodium, due to loss of colonic absorption
    • Vitamin B12, due to loss of intrinsic factor
    • Vitamin C, due to loss of gastric acid alone
    • Vitamin K, due to loss of bile production
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Vitamin B12, due to loss of intrinsic factor. Without the stomach, intrinsic factor is absent, so B12 cannot be absorbed. Revise Gastroenterology and Hepatology →

Renal, Endocrine and Metabolic Medicine

  1. Where is most filtered sodium and glucose reabsorbed? (Foundation recall)
    • The bladder
    • The collecting duct
    • The glomerulus
    • The proximal tubule
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The proximal tubule. The proximal tubule reabsorbs the bulk of filtered solutes. Revise Renal, Endocrine and Metabolic Medicine →

  2. Why do patients with chronic kidney disease often become anaemic? (Exam style)
    • Excess renin release
    • Increased vitamin D activation
    • Loss of red cells in the filtrate
    • Reduced erythropoietin production
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Reduced erythropoietin production. Damaged kidneys make less erythropoietin. Revise Renal, Endocrine and Metabolic Medicine →

  3. A very muscular athlete has a slightly raised creatinine but normal urine tests. Why should eGFR be interpreted cautiously? (Stretch)
    • Creatinine is not produced by skeletal muscle
    • eGFR is always exact, whatever a person's build
    • High muscle mass raises creatinine, so eGFR may underestimate true GFR
    • Muscle mass has no effect at all on creatinine levels
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    High muscle mass raises creatinine, so eGFR may underestimate true GFR. Creatinine reflects muscle mass as well as filtration. Revise Renal, Endocrine and Metabolic Medicine →

Neurology and Psychiatry

  1. Which headache is typically bilateral and pressing, without nausea? (Foundation recall)
    • Cluster headache
    • Migraine with aura
    • Subarachnoid haemorrhage
    • Tension-type headache
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Tension-type headache. Tension-type headache is mild to moderate and bilateral. Revise Neurology and Psychiatry →

  2. Which features suggest cluster headache? (Exam style)
    • A gradual headache that is worse only on lying down
    • A headache accompanied by fever and a non-blanching rash
    • Bilateral pressing pain lasting all day without nausea
    • Severe pain around one eye with tearing and nasal congestion on the same side
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Severe pain around one eye with tearing and nasal congestion on the same side. Autonomic features on the painful side are typical. Revise Neurology and Psychiatry →

  3. A 70-year-old has a new headache, scalp tenderness and jaw pain when chewing. What is the priority? (Stretch)
    • Prescribe a triptan and treat it as new migraine
    • Reassurance and a routine review in about a month
    • Refer for neck physiotherapy and posture advice
    • Same-day assessment for giant cell arteritis to prevent vision loss
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Same-day assessment for giant cell arteritis to prevent vision loss. Giant cell arteritis needs urgent treatment. Revise Neurology and Psychiatry →

Infection, Haematology and Oncology

  1. Gram-positive cocci in clusters most likely indicate which organism? (Foundation recall)
    • Escherichia coli
    • Pseudomonas
    • Staphylococci
    • Streptococci
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Staphylococci. Applying the standard result: Clusters suggest staphylococci. Revise Infection, Haematology and Oncology →

  2. What does the minimum inhibitory concentration describe? (Exam style)
    • The highest safe antibiotic dose for a patient
    • The lowest antibiotic concentration preventing visible growth
    • The number of bacteria in a blood sample
    • The time an organism takes to grow in culture
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The lowest antibiotic concentration preventing visible growth. Applying the standard result: MIC measures susceptibility. Revise Infection, Haematology and Oncology →

  3. A single blood culture bottle grows coagulase-negative staphylococci in a well patient without a line. What is most likely? (Stretch)
    • A Gram-negative bacteraemia
    • Infective endocarditis
    • Severe staphylococcal sepsis
    • Skin contamination
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Skin contamination. A single positive bottle with a skin commensal is often a contaminant. Revise Infection, Haematology and Oncology →

Musculoskeletal, Skin and the Special Senses

  1. Which finding suggests osteoarthritis rather than rheumatoid arthritis? (Foundation recall)
    • A raised CRP with fatigue
    • Heberden's nodes
    • Morning stiffness of two hours
    • Symmetrical swelling of the MCP joints
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Heberden's nodes. Heberden's nodes at the DIP joints are typical of OA; prolonged stiffness and MCP synovitis suggest inflammatory arthritis. Revise Musculoskeletal, Skin and the Special Senses →

  2. A 58-year-old has typical activity-related knee pain and 15 minutes of morning stiffness. What does NICE recommend? (Exam style)
    • A clinical diagnosis without imaging
    • A plain X-ray before any treatment
    • An urgent knee MRI scan
    • Blood tests for rheumatoid factor
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A clinical diagnosis without imaging. NICE advises diagnosing OA clinically in people aged 45 or over with typical features. Revise Musculoskeletal, Skin and the Special Senses →

  3. Which treatment does NICE recommend for knee osteoarthritis? (Stretch)
    • A tailored strengthening exercise programme
    • Arthroscopic washout of the knee
    • Long-term glucosamine supplements
    • Regular paracetamol as sole treatment
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A tailored strengthening exercise programme. Exercise is core treatment; NICE advises against arthroscopic lavage, glucosamine and paracetamol alone. Revise Musculoskeletal, Skin and the Special Senses →

Acute, Surgical and Palliative Care

  1. What is the first step in the ABCDE approach? (Foundation recall)
    • Assess the airway
    • Check blood glucose
    • Examine the abdomen
    • Take blood pressure
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Assess the airway. Airway problems kill fastest, so they are assessed first. Revise Acute, Surgical and Palliative Care →

  2. A patient has stridor and is struggling to breathe. What is the priority? (Exam style)
    • Arrange a chest X-ray
    • Call for urgent anaesthetic help and manage the airway
    • Check capillary blood glucose
    • Measure the blood pressure
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Call for urgent anaesthetic help and manage the airway. Stridor suggests upper airway obstruction, a life-threatening A problem. Revise Acute, Surgical and Palliative Care →

  3. A patient with COPD and known hypercapnic respiratory failure is hypoxic. What oxygen saturation target is appropriate? (Stretch)
    • 100%
    • 80 to 85%
    • 88 to 92%
    • 94 to 98%
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    88 to 92%. Patients at risk of hypercapnia have a lower target to avoid worsening CO2 retention. Revise Acute, Surgical and Palliative Care →

Obstetrics, Gynaecology and Paediatrics

  1. What daily dose of folic acid is advised for most women in early pregnancy? (Foundation recall)
    • 10 milligrams
    • 400 micrograms
    • 5 grams
    • 50 micrograms
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    400 micrograms. 400 micrograms reduces neural tube defects; higher-risk women take 5 mg. Revise Obstetrics, Gynaecology and Paediatrics →

  2. Why does haemoglobin often fall in normal pregnancy? (Exam style)
    • Iron absorption falls to zero
    • Plasma volume rises more than red cell mass
    • Red cell production stops in pregnancy
    • The fetus destroys maternal red cells
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Plasma volume rises more than red cell mass. The dilutional effect causes a physiological anaemia. Revise Obstetrics, Gynaecology and Paediatrics →

  3. A woman is RhD negative. What should be offered? (Stretch)
    • Anti-D prophylaxis
    • Aspirin from 12 weeks
    • Early induction at 37 weeks
    • Routine iron infusion
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Anti-D prophylaxis. Anti-D prevents RhD sensitisation that could harm future pregnancies. Revise Obstetrics, Gynaecology and Paediatrics →

Professional Practice, Prescribing and Population Health

  1. Which body keeps the UK register of medical practitioners? (Foundation recall)
    • The British Medical Association
    • The Care Quality Commission
    • The General Medical Council
    • The Royal College of Physicians of London
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The General Medical Council. The GMC holds the register and sets professional standards for doctors. Revise Professional Practice, Prescribing and Population Health →

  2. A student posts a photo from theatre in which a patient's tattoo is visible. What is the main problem? (Exam style)
    • Students may never mention placements online
    • The patient could be identified without consent
    • The photo shows poor theatre lighting
    • The post does not name the hospital directly
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The patient could be identified without consent. Identifiable features breach confidentiality even when no name is given. Revise Professional Practice, Prescribing and Population Health →

  3. Which of these is most likely to lead to a student fitness to practise referral? (Stretch)
    • Asking a consultant to repeat an instruction
    • Failing one written examination at first sitting
    • Forging a supervisor's signature on a logbook
    • Taking an approved interruption for illness
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Forging a supervisor's signature on a logbook. Dishonesty such as forging signatures is among the most serious concerns. Revise Professional Practice, Prescribing and Population Health →
