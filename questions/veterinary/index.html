Veterinary Science practice questions

36 questions from Gradify's free Veterinary Science course, with the answer and why behind each reveal. Answer before you open it: that's what makes it stick.

Comparative Anatomy

  1. Which term describes the surface of a dog's tail base that faces the ground? (Foundation recall)
    • Caudal
    • Palmar
    • Plantar
    • Ventral
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Ventral. Ventral means towards the belly side, and applies to the tail as to the trunk. Palmar and plantar are only for the distal limbs; caudal means towards the tail tip along the body's long axis. Revise Comparative Anatomy →

  2. A dog is lying on its back on the X-ray table and the beam enters the belly and leaves through the back. How is the thoracic radiograph named? (Exam style)
    • Dorsoventral
    • Lateromedial
    • Right lateral
    • Ventrodorsal
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Ventrodorsal. Projections are named entry to exit: the beam enters the ventral surface and exits dorsally, so it is ventrodorsal (VD). A dorsoventral (DV) view has the animal in sternal recumbency with the beam entering the back. Revise Comparative Anatomy →

  3. A referral letter describes a wound "on the anterior aspect of the horse's knee". Which correction is most accurate? (Stretch)
    • On the cranial aspect of the fetlock
    • On the cranial aspect of the stifle
    • On the dorsal aspect of the carpus
    • On the palmar aspect of the carpus
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    On the dorsal aspect of the carpus. The equine "knee" is the carpus, and its front surface is called dorsal (the distal-limb convention begins at the carpus). The stifle is the true knee, so the most tempting distractor changes the joint. Revise Comparative Anatomy →

Veterinary Physiology

  1. Which ion is most concentrated in the intracellular fluid? (Foundation recall)
    • Calcium
    • Chloride
    • Potassium
    • Sodium
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Potassium. The Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase keeps potassium high inside cells and sodium high outside; chloride and calcium are mainly extracellular. Revise Veterinary Physiology →

  2. A ewe with a heavy liver fluke burden develops a soft swelling under the jaw. What is the most likely mechanism? (Exam style)
    • High plasma albumin raises oncotic pressure in the tissues, drawing water out of the vessels
    • Low plasma albumin reduces oncotic pressure, so fluid leaves the capillaries faster than lymph returns it
    • Lymphatic vessels are blocked by fluke eggs
    • Raised plasma sodium causes water to move into cells
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Low plasma albumin reduces oncotic pressure, so fluid leaves the capillaries faster than lymph returns it. Fluke and Haemonchus cause protein loss, and hypoalbuminaemia lowers oncotic pressure, giving dependent oedema ("bottle jaw"). Revise Veterinary Physiology →

  3. A male cat with urethral obstruction has a plasma potassium far above normal and a slow heart rate. Which explanation is best? (Stretch)
    • High potassium increases sodium channel opening, causing rapid firing
    • Potassium blocks acetylcholine release at the sinoatrial node
    • Potassium hyperpolarises cardiac cells so they cannot reach threshold
    • Sustained depolarisation of cardiac cells inactivates sodium channels, reducing excitability and conduction
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Sustained depolarisation of cardiac cells inactivates sodium channels, reducing excitability and conduction. Raised extracellular potassium makes the resting potential less negative; the resulting inactivation of sodium channels slows conduction and can cause bradycardia, arrhythmias and arrest. Revise Veterinary Physiology →

Biochemistry, Cell Biology and Genetics

  1. Which organelle makes ATP by aerobic respiration? (Foundation recall)
    • The Golgi apparatus
    • The lysosome
    • The mitochondrion
    • The rough endoplasmic reticulum
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The mitochondrion. Mitochondria carry out the Krebs cycle and oxidative phosphorylation; the others package, digest and make proteins. Revise Biochemistry, Cell Biology and Genetics →

  2. Glucose is absorbed from the gut lumen into enterocytes against its concentration gradient. Which mechanism is used? (Exam style)
    • Facilitated diffusion down its gradient
    • Phagocytosis
    • Secondary active transport coupled to sodium
    • Simple diffusion through the lipid bilayer
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Secondary active transport coupled to sodium. The sodium–glucose cotransporter uses the sodium gradient made by the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, which is why oral rehydration solutions contain both sodium and glucose. Revise Biochemistry, Cell Biology and Genetics →

  3. A Springer Spaniel puppy from a line with a known inherited disease develops progressive neurological signs, and neurones on biopsy contain swollen vacuoles. Which mechanism best explains this? (Stretch)
    • Deficiency of a lysosomal enzyme, so undigested substrate accumulates in lysosomes
    • Excessive mitochondrial ATP production
    • Failure of ribosomes to make proteins in the neurone cytoplasm
    • Loss of the nuclear envelope
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Deficiency of a lysosomal enzyme, so undigested substrate accumulates in lysosomes. Vacuolated, swollen neurones in a young dog of a predisposed breed suggest a lysosomal storage disease such as fucosidosis. Revise Biochemistry, Cell Biology and Genetics →

Animal Husbandry, Nutrition and Welfare

  1. According to the RCVS, what is a vet's primary consideration? (Foundation recall)
    • The owner's convenience
    • The profit of the practice
    • The speed of treatment
    • The welfare of animals in their care
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The welfare of animals in their care. Applying the standard result: The Code puts welfare first. Revise Animal Husbandry, Nutrition and Welfare →

  2. Which view of welfare focuses on feelings such as pain and pleasure? (Exam style)
    • Affective states
    • Biological functioning
    • Natural living
    • Productivity
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Affective states. Applying the standard result: Feelings are affective states. Revise Animal Husbandry, Nutrition and Welfare →

  3. A dairy cow is healthy and high-yielding but kept indoors all year. Which view of welfare raises most concern? (Stretch)
    • Biological functioning
    • Feed efficiency
    • Natural living
    • Productivity
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Natural living. Applying the standard result: She cannot perform grazing behaviour. Revise Animal Husbandry, Nutrition and Welfare →

Veterinary Pathology

  1. Which form of cell death occurs without inflammation? (Foundation recall)
    • Apoptosis
    • Caseous necrosis
    • Coagulative necrosis
    • Liquefactive necrosis
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Apoptosis. Apoptotic bodies are phagocytosed quietly. Revise Veterinary Pathology →

  2. Which type of necrosis is typical of the brain? (Exam style)
    • Caseous
    • Coagulative
    • Fat
    • Liquefactive
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Liquefactive. Applying the standard result: Brain tissue liquefies. Revise Veterinary Pathology →

  3. A liver biopsy shows hepatocytes full of fat droplets, but nuclei are intact. What does this suggest? (Stretch)
    • Apoptosis of all cells
    • Caseous necrosis
    • Irreversible coagulative necrosis
    • Reversible fatty change
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Reversible fatty change. Applying the standard result: Lipidosis can recover. Revise Veterinary Pathology →

Microbiology, Parasitology and Immunology

  1. Which feature is found only in Gram-negative bacteria? (Foundation recall)
    • A thick peptidoglycan wall
    • A waxy mycolic acid wall
    • An outer membrane with LPS
    • Purple staining after Gram
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    An outer membrane with LPS. The outer membrane carries endotoxin, the hallmark of Gram-negative bacteria. Revise Microbiology, Parasitology and Immunology →

  2. How do spores help Clostridium species cause disease? (Exam style)
    • They attach the bacteria to gut cells
    • They secrete endotoxin into the blood
    • They stop antibodies from binding
    • They survive for years in soil
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    They survive for years in soil. Spores resist heat, drying and disinfectants, so they persist in the environment. Revise Microbiology, Parasitology and Immunology →

  3. A colicky horse with a strangulating lesion becomes shocked with toxic mucous membranes. Which bacterial product is most likely involved? (Stretch)
    • Capsule from Streptococcus equi
    • Endotoxin from Gram-negative gut bacteria
    • Spores from Bacillus anthracis
    • Tetanus neurotoxin from clostridia
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Endotoxin from Gram-negative gut bacteria. Damaged gut lets LPS from Gram-negative bacteria enter the circulation, causing endotoxaemia. Revise Microbiology, Parasitology and Immunology →

Veterinary Pharmacology and Therapeutics

  1. Which process converts a drug into a more water-soluble form? (Foundation recall)
    • Absorption
    • Distribution
    • Metabolism
    • Protein binding
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Metabolism. Hepatic metabolism makes drugs easier to excrete in urine or bile. Revise Veterinary Pharmacology and Therapeutics →

  2. Why does an oral drug sometimes have low bioavailability? (Exam style)
    • First-pass metabolism by the liver
    • Giving it straight into a vein
    • Its passage through the kidneys
    • Rapid binding to red blood cells
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    First-pass metabolism by the liver. Oral drugs pass through the liver before reaching the systemic circulation. Revise Veterinary Pharmacology and Therapeutics →

  3. A cat with reduced kidney function is given a drug excreted unchanged in urine. What is most likely? (Stretch)
    • Bioavailability will fall to zero
    • The drug will act more briefly
    • The drug will skip distribution
    • The half-life will lengthen
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The half-life will lengthen. Reduced renal clearance slows elimination, so the drug persists longer. Revise Veterinary Pharmacology and Therapeutics →

Clinical Method and Reasoning

  1. Which part of the history records the reaction a patient had to a drug? (Foundation recall)
    • Drug history, as the allergy and its reaction
    • Family history
    • Past surgical history
    • Systems review
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Drug history, as the allergy and its reaction. Allergies are documented with the drug history, always with the nature of the reaction so allergy can be distinguished from intolerance. Revise Clinical Method and Reasoning →

  2. A patient says she is "allergic to amoxicillin" because it gave her loose stools. How should this be recorded? (Exam style)
    • As a penicillin allergy, so all penicillins are avoided
    • As an intolerance (diarrhoea), not an allergy, with the reaction described
    • As anaphylaxis to be safe
    • It needn't be recorded at all
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    As an intolerance (diarrhoea), not an allergy, with the reaction described. Gastrointestinal upset is a predictable side effect, not an immune reaction. Labelling it as allergy would needlessly exclude penicillins; recording the reaction lets prescribers decide. Revise Clinical Method and Reasoning →

  3. Which question adds the most diagnostic value in a patient with a headache? (Stretch)
    • Did it reach its maximum intensity within a minute or so?
    • Do you drink coffee?
    • Have you ever had a headache before?
    • Is there anyone else at home?
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Did it reach its maximum intensity within a minute or so?. A thunderclap onset is a red flag for subarachnoid haemorrhage and changes management immediately. Discriminating questions shift probability; generic ones rarely do. Revise Clinical Method and Reasoning →

Small Animal Medicine and Surgery

  1. Which sign suggests large bowel diarrhoea? (Foundation recall)
    • Large volumes of faeces
    • Mucus and straining
    • Normal frequency
    • Weight loss
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Mucus and straining. Large bowel disease causes frequent small amounts with mucus, blood and tenesmus. Revise Small Animal Medicine and Surgery →

  2. A dog brings up undigested, tube-shaped food with no effort. What is this? (Exam style)
    • Melaena
    • Regurgitation
    • Tenesmus
    • Vomiting
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Regurgitation. Passive return of food without abdominal effort is regurgitation. Revise Small Animal Medicine and Surgery →

  3. A cat is vomiting and has lost weight despite a big appetite. Which extra-intestinal cause should be checked? (Stretch)
    • Cruciate rupture
    • Dental tartar
    • Hip dysplasia
    • Hyperthyroidism
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Hyperthyroidism. Hyperthyroidism commonly causes vomiting and weight loss with polyphagia in older cats. Revise Small Animal Medicine and Surgery →

Equine Practice

  1. What does colic mean? (Foundation recall)
    • A gut infection
    • A type of worm
    • Abdominal pain
    • Diarrhoea
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Abdominal pain. Colic is a sign of abdominal pain with many causes. Revise Equine Practice →

  2. Why are horses especially prone to colic? (Exam style)
    • Their stomachs are very large
    • They cannot vomit and have a large, mobile hindgut
    • They chew food very little
    • They have a short intestine
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    They cannot vomit and have a large, mobile hindgut. Gut anatomy makes horses vulnerable to displacement, impaction and gas build-up. Revise Equine Practice →

  3. A horse with colic has nasogastric reflux and a rising heart rate despite pain relief. What is most appropriate? (Stretch)
    • Feeding a bran mash
    • Hand walking for another day
    • Turning it out to grass
    • Urgent referral for possible surgery
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Urgent referral for possible surgery. Persistent pain, tachycardia and reflux suggest a critical surgical case. Revise Equine Practice →

Farm Animal Practice and Herd Health

  1. Which pathogen is a classic contagious cause of mastitis? (Foundation recall)
    • Clostridium perfringens
    • Escherichia coli from bedding
    • Salmonella Dublin in calves
    • Staphylococcus aureus
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Staphylococcus aureus. Contagious pathogens spread between cows during milking. Revise Farm Animal Practice and Herd Health →

  2. What does an individual cow SCC of 450,000 cells/mL suggest? (Exam style)
    • Likely intramammary infection
    • Low milk fat content
    • Normal, healthy udder
    • Recent vaccination
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Likely intramammary infection. Counts above about 200,000 cells/mL suggest infection. Revise Farm Animal Practice and Herd Health →

  3. Records show most new infections occur in early lactation with environmental pathogens. What should be the main focus? (Stretch)
    • Changing the breed of bull used
    • Improving hygiene of calving and early-lactation housing
    • Increasing antibiotic use at every milking
    • Selling all the cows in the herd
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Improving hygiene of calving and early-lactation housing. Environmental infections around calving point to housing hygiene. Revise Farm Animal Practice and Herd Health →

Veterinary Public Health and Professional Practice

  1. Which organisation is one of the One Health quadripartite partners? (Foundation recall)
    • The European Space Agency
    • The International Olympic Committee
    • The World Organisation for Animal Health
    • The World Trade Organization
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The World Organisation for Animal Health. WOAH joins WHO, FAO and UNEP in the quadripartite. Revise Veterinary Public Health and Professional Practice →

  2. Why is antimicrobial resistance a One Health issue? (Exam style)
    • It has no link to farming at all
    • It is caused only by human antibiotic use
    • It only affects patients in hospitals, not animals or farms
    • Resistant bacteria and genes move between animals, people and the environment
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Resistant bacteria and genes move between animals, people and the environment. Resistance crosses species and environments. Revise Veterinary Public Health and Professional Practice →

  3. Which factor most increases the risk of new diseases spilling over from animals to people? (Stretch)
    • Changes in land use and closer contact with wildlife
    • Improving farm biosecurity standards
    • Reducing global trade in wildlife
    • Vaccinating domestic animals regularly
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Changes in land use and closer contact with wildlife. Habitat change and contact drive spillover. Revise Veterinary Public Health and Professional Practice →
