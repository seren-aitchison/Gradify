Teaching practice questions

36 questions from Gradify's free Teaching course, with the answer and why behind each reveal. Answer before you open it: that's what makes it stick.

How Pupils Learn

  1. Which statement best captures learning as the ITTECF describes it? (Foundation recall)
    • A lasting change in pupils' capabilities or understanding
    • Being able to repeat what the teacher has just said
    • Completing all the tasks set in a lesson
    • Showing high engagement throughout a lesson
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A lasting change in pupils' capabilities or understanding. The framework defines learning by what lasts. Completion, repetition and engagement can happen without any lasting change in long-term memory. Revise How Pupils Learn →

  2. A trainee notes that every pupil answered the plenary questions correctly. What is the most accurate conclusion? (Exam style)
    • No further practice of the skill is needed
    • Pupils can perform the skill now; retention needs checking later
    • Pupils have learned the skill securely
    • The lesson was too easy and should be harder next time
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Pupils can perform the skill now; retention needs checking later. Correct answers immediately after teaching show current performance. Whether it has become learning is only visible after a delay, so the next step is planned retrieval. Revise How Pupils Learn →

  3. Which lesson feature is the strongest evidence that pupils have learned last month's content? (Stretch)
    • Accurate answers to unprompted questions on it at the start of today's lesson
    • Every pupil completing last month's worksheet
    • Pupils saying they found the topic easy
    • Pupils working quietly on the topic for a whole hour
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Accurate answers to unprompted questions on it at the start of today's lesson. Unprompted retrieval after a delay is direct evidence of a change in long-term memory. Quiet work, completion and self-report are proxies that can occur without retention. Revise How Pupils Learn →

Classroom Practice

  1. Which is one of Rosenshine's ten principles? (Foundation recall)
    • Keep teacher talk to under five minutes per lesson
    • Let pupils discover new material for themselves before any teaching
    • Match each task to pupils' learning styles
    • Present new material in small steps, with pupil practice after each step
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Present new material in small steps, with pupil practice after each step. Small steps with practice is principle 2. The others are not among Rosenshine's principles, and learning styles lack evidence. Revise Classroom Practice →

  2. Rosenshine drew on studies observing the teachers whose classes made the most progress. What were these called? (Exam style)
    • Case studies of individual pupils
    • Neuroimaging studies
    • Process-product studies
    • Randomised controlled trials
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Process-product studies. Process-product research related observed teaching behaviours to pupils' progress; it was one of his three sources. Revise Classroom Practice →

  3. A school observes trainees against all ten principles in every 50-minute lesson. What is the strongest criticism? (Stretch)
    • Observation can never judge teaching
    • Rosenshine said the principles apply only to primary schools
    • The principles are not based on research
    • The principles describe teaching over time, so one lesson need not show all ten
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The principles describe teaching over time, so one lesson need not show all ten. Weekly and monthly review, for example, cannot happen in every lesson, and a lesson may focus on some stages. The principles are research-based and not limited to primary. Revise Classroom Practice →

Adaptive Teaching and SEND

  1. Which is the best description of adaptive teaching? (Foundation recall)
    • Giving each ability group a different, easier or harder task
    • Keeping the same high goal for all pupils while adjusting support in response to their needs
    • Letting pupils choose the level of work they want
    • Teaching to the middle of the class
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Keeping the same high goal for all pupils while adjusting support in response to their needs. The ITTECF describes responsive adjustment of support with high expectations; separate tasks risk lowering expectations. Revise Adaptive Teaching and SEND →

  2. Which practice is most clearly adaptive teaching? (Exam style)
    • Giving the bottom table a colouring task while others write
    • Moving struggling pupils to an easier set
    • Re-explaining a step with a new example for pupils who got a hinge question wrong
    • Setting the same worksheet to everyone and marking it next week
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Re-explaining a step with a new example for pupils who got a hinge question wrong. Adaptive teaching responds to evidence of understanding with targeted support towards the same goal. Revise Adaptive Teaching and SEND →

  3. A trainee says her lesson is adaptive because it has "all, most, some" objectives, with "some" reserved for the top table. What is the best evaluation? (Stretch)
    • It is a good example of adaptive teaching, because the objectives are tiered
    • It is adaptive only if the objectives are displayed
    • It is fine as long as the top table finishes the core objectives first
    • It risks capping expectations for pupils not on the top table, which the ITTECF warns against
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It risks capping expectations for pupils not on the top table, which the ITTECF warns against. Pre-deciding that only some pupils will reach the full goal can lower expectations; adaptive teaching aims for all to succeed with varied support. Revise Adaptive Teaching and SEND →

Assessment and Feedback

  1. Which is the main purpose of summative assessment? (Foundation recall)
    • To decide what to teach in the next five minutes
    • To identify a single misconception in one pupil
    • To motivate pupils by giving them stickers
    • To sum up what pupils have learned at a particular point
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    To sum up what pupils have learned at a particular point. Summative assessment sums up learning, often for reporting; formative and diagnostic uses guide next steps. Revise Assessment and Feedback →

  2. A teacher uses a Friday quiz to decide which topics to reteach on Monday. How is the quiz being used? (Exam style)
    • For accountability, because the head sees it
    • For certification, because it is marked
    • Formatively, because the results change what is taught next
    • Summatively, because it happens at the end of the week
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Formatively, because the results change what is taught next. Use, not timing or format, makes assessment formative. Revise Assessment and Feedback →

  3. A school uses one half-termly GCSE-style grade both to plan teaching and to report progress. What is the strongest criticism? (Stretch)
    • GCSE papers should never be used before Year 11 in any form
    • Grades are not allowed to be shared with parents
    • Pupils find exam papers too long to complete
    • The grade is too broad to guide teaching and too unreliable on a few topics to report progress
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The grade is too broad to guide teaching and too unreliable on a few topics to report progress. Christodoulou argues that formative and summative needs differ; one broad grade serves neither well. Revise Assessment and Feedback →

Learning and Behaviour

  1. In Pavlov's experiment, salivation to the bell after conditioning is the… (Foundation recall)
    • Conditioned response
    • Neutral stimulus
    • Unconditioned response
    • Unconditioned stimulus
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Conditioned response. It is learned, and triggered by the bell, so it is the conditioned response. Revise Learning and Behaviour →

  2. A child is stung by a wasp in a garden and afterwards feels anxious in that garden. What is the conditioned stimulus? (Exam style)
    • The anxiety
    • The garden
    • The pain of the sting
    • The sting
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The garden. The garden was neutral until paired with the sting (UCS); now it triggers anxiety (CR). Revise Learning and Behaviour →

  3. Which is the strongest methodological criticism of the Little Albert study? (Stretch)
    • It studied adults rather than infants
    • It took place outside a laboratory
    • It used too many participants
    • It was a single, poorly measured case
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It was a single, poorly measured case. One infant, informal observations and no follow-up limit what can be concluded. Revise Learning and Behaviour →

Curriculum and Subject Knowledge

  1. Which best distinguishes curriculum from pedagogy? (Foundation recall)
    • Curriculum and pedagogy mean exactly the same thing
    • Curriculum is assessment; pedagogy is the timetable
    • Curriculum is how pupils are taught; pedagogy is what they learn
    • Curriculum is what pupils learn; pedagogy is how it is taught
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Curriculum is what pupils learn; pedagogy is how it is taught. Curriculum concerns content and sequence; pedagogy concerns teaching methods. Revise Curriculum and Subject Knowledge →

  2. A planned unit includes historians' debates, but a teacher skips them for time. What does this show? (Exam style)
    • A gap between the planned and enacted curriculum
    • That pupils experienced the full curriculum anyway
    • That the hidden curriculum replaced the planned one
    • That the planned curriculum was too short for the unit
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A gap between the planned and enacted curriculum. The taught curriculum differed from the intended one. Revise Curriculum and Subject Knowledge →

  3. Which is the strongest evidence of the quality of a school's curriculum? (Stretch)
    • The length and detail of its written curriculum policies
    • The number of topics listed on its long-term plans
    • The variety of activities used in lessons each week
    • What pupils know, remember and can do across subjects over time
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    What pupils know, remember and can do across subjects over time. The experienced curriculum, what pupils actually learn, is the real test. Revise Curriculum and Subject Knowledge →

Planning Lessons and Sequences

  1. In backward design, what is planned first? (Foundation recall)
    • The homework task
    • The intended learning outcomes
    • The seating plan
    • The starter activity
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The intended learning outcomes. Wiggins and McTighe begin with desired results, before evidence or activities. Revise Planning Lessons and Sequences →

  2. A trainee plans a lesson around a card sort but cannot say what pupils will learn from it. Which fault is this? (Exam style)
    • Activity-focused planning
    • Coverage-focused planning
    • Excessive retrieval practice
    • Over-reliance on assessment
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Activity-focused planning. Building the lesson around an engaging task with no link to an outcome is the activity sin. Revise Planning Lessons and Sequences →

  3. A head of department says every lesson must have pupils producing lots of written work, as proof of learning. What is the best response? (Stretch)
    • Pupils should produce no written work in lessons
    • Volume of work is a poor proxy; plan the evidence that shows the outcome
    • Volume of work reliably shows deep learning in every subject
    • Written work matters only in English lessons
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Volume of work is a poor proxy; plan the evidence that shows the outcome. Coe and colleagues (2014) note that lots of work produced is a poor proxy, so evidence should be chosen to match the outcome. Revise Planning Lessons and Sequences →

Literacy and Oracy Across the Curriculum

  1. Who is responsible for promoting literacy in a secondary school, according to the Teachers' Standards? (Foundation recall)
    • Every teacher
    • Only English teachers
    • Only teaching assistants
    • Only the SENCO
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Every teacher. Standard 3 applies to all teachers, whatever their subject. Revise Literacy and Oracy Across the Curriculum →

  2. A geography lesson asks pupils to read a dense report on climate change. Which is a literacy demand of this lesson? (Exam style)
    • Drawing a neat map
    • Reading complex text with subject vocabulary
    • Remembering last week's homework
    • Working quietly in pairs
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Reading complex text with subject vocabulary. Reading the report and its vocabulary is a literacy demand built into the subject. Revise Literacy and Oracy Across the Curriculum →

  3. A school asks every teacher to correct the same ten spellings in all subjects. What is the main limitation? (Stretch)
    • It helps every pupil read complex texts
    • It ignores each subject's own literacy demands
    • It is required by the national curriculum
    • It replaces the need for interventions
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It ignores each subject's own literacy demands. The EEF recommends subject-specific approaches over generic ones applied identically everywhere. Revise Literacy and Oracy Across the Curriculum →

Safeguarding and Pupil Welfare

  1. What should a trainee do first if they are worried about a pupil's welfare? (Foundation recall)
    • Ask the pupil's friends about it
    • Mention it at the next staff meeting
    • Speak to the DSL straight away
    • Wait to see if it happens again
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Speak to the DSL straight away. KCSIE expects staff to act on concerns immediately by speaking to the DSL. Revise Safeguarding and Pupil Welfare →

  2. Which is an example of safeguarding rather than child protection specifically? (Exam style)
    • A child protection plan for a named child
    • A police investigation into a child's injuries
    • A section 47 enquiry into suspected abuse
    • Teaching pupils about online safety
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Teaching pupils about online safety. Teaching online safety promotes welfare for all; child protection concerns specific children at risk of significant harm. Revise Safeguarding and Pupil Welfare →

  3. A teaching assistant says a concern is probably nothing, so she will not mention it. What is the best response? (Stretch)
    • Agree, as minor worries are not important
    • Ask the pupil's parents directly first
    • Record it privately and keep it to yourself
    • Share it with the DSL, who can see the wider picture
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Share it with the DSL, who can see the wider picture. Small concerns can form part of a bigger picture that only the DSL can see. Revise Safeguarding and Pupil Welfare →

Sleep, Stress and Rest

  1. Which hormone is released mainly during deep sleep and supports growth and repair? (Foundation recall)
    • Adrenaline
    • Glucagon
    • Growth hormone
    • Insulin
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Growth hormone. Growth hormone secretion peaks during slow-wave (deep) sleep. Revise Sleep, Stress and Rest →

  2. A driver has slept 5 hours a night all week and feels "fine". What does research suggest? (Exam style)
    • Feeling fine always means performance is normal
    • He has fully adapted and is at no extra risk
    • His reaction time and attention are likely impaired, increasing crash risk
    • Short sleep improves alertness after a week
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    His reaction time and attention are likely impaired, increasing crash risk. Chronic restriction impairs performance despite little feeling of sleepiness. Revise Sleep, Stress and Rest →

  3. Which is the best evaluation of the glymphatic theory of sleep? (Stretch)
    • It explains every known function of sleep on its own
    • It has been disproved by recent research and abandoned
    • It is proven beyond doubt by large trials in human volunteers
    • It is supported by animal studies, but evidence in humans is still developing
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It is supported by animal studies, but evidence in humans is still developing. Brain clearance during sleep has been shown in mice; human evidence is promising but limited. Revise Sleep, Stress and Rest →

Research Methods

  1. Deriving a specific prediction from a general theory is… (Foundation recall)
    • Deduction
    • Induction
    • Operationalisation
    • Replication
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Deduction. Deduction moves from the general to the specific. Revise Research Methods →

  2. A researcher only reports the three participants whose results fit her theory. Which error is this? (Exam style)
    • Confirmation bias
    • Counterbalancing
    • Induction
    • Random allocation
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Confirmation bias. Selecting supportive evidence is confirmation bias, here as selective reporting. Revise Research Methods →

  3. Which design feature best protects against confirmation bias in analysis? (Stretch)
    • Analysing data before deciding the hypothesis
    • Preregistering predictions and analyses
    • Testing only people who agree
    • Using a bigger font in questionnaires
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Preregistering predictions and analyses. Committing to predictions and analyses in advance limits choosing results that fit. Revise Research Methods →

Education

  1. Who described school as a bridge between family and society? (Foundation recall)
    • Émile Durkheim
    • Karl Marx
    • Paul Willis
    • Talcott Parsons
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Talcott Parsons. Parsons saw school bridging particularistic and universalistic standards. Revise Education →

  2. A country expands university places to boost economic growth. Which theory supports this? (Exam style)
    • Correspondence principle
    • Feminism
    • Human capital theory
    • Labelling theory
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Human capital theory. Education is treated as investment in productivity. Revise Education →

  3. Which is the main criticism of functionalist meritocracy? (Stretch)
    • Background strongly shapes outcomes
    • Education has no functions
    • Schools have no rules
    • Skills aren't needed
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Background strongly shapes outcomes. Class, ethnicity and gender influence achievement. Revise Education →
