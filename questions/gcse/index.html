Revision and Exam Skills

  1. Which memory store holds the knowledge you build up over years of school? (Foundation recall)
    • Long-term memory
    • Sensory memory
    • Short-term rehearsal
    • Working memory
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Long-term memory. Long-term memory is the large, lasting store. Working memory holds only a few items for seconds. Revise Revision and Exam Skills →

  2. A student says, "I read my notes three times and they looked really familiar, so I'm ready." What is the problem? (Exam style)
    • Familiar notes prove the topic is already in long-term memory
    • Familiarity is recognition, but the exam needs recall from memory
    • Notes should be read only once, otherwise you get confused
    • Reading notes three times is always too many for one topic
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Familiarity is recognition, but the exam needs recall from memory. Recognising material on a page is easier than recalling it without prompts. The student should test themselves before deciding they are ready. Revise Revision and Exam Skills →

  3. Which revision plan best fits what is known about memory? (Stretch)
    • Copying out notes neatly once, then never looking again
    • Highlighting every textbook page in a different colour
    • Rereading all notes the night before each exam
    • Short self-tests on each topic spread across several weeks
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Short self-tests on each topic spread across several weeks. Spaced, active retrieval slows forgetting. Cramming, highlighting and copying feel productive but give weaker long-term recall. Revise Revision and Exam Skills →

Foundations of Literary Study

  1. Which critic argued that art "defamiliarises" familiar things so that we perceive them afresh? (Foundation recall)
    • Matthew Arnold
    • Raymond Williams
    • Samuel Johnson
    • Viktor Shklovsky
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Viktor Shklovsky. Shklovsky's essay "Art as Technique" (1917) is the classic statement of defamiliarisation, a key idea in Russian Formalism. Revise Foundations of Literary Study →

  2. A student says a cereal advert that uses rhyme and alliteration must be literature. Which reply is best? (Exam style)
    • Adverts cannot use literary techniques, so the claim is wrongly described
    • Only texts written before 1900 can be literature
    • Patterned language is one feature of literature but not enough on its own to make a text literary
    • The advert is literature because any rhymed text counts as poetry
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Patterned language is one feature of literature but not enough on its own to make a text literary. The Formalist test identifies literariness, but literature is also defined by purpose and value; many patterned texts are not literature. Revise Foundations of Literary Study →

  3. Which is the strongest objection to defining literature purely as "writing a culture values"? (Stretch)
    • It cannot explain why some texts reward close reading more than others, so it may make value seem arbitrary
    • It excludes all poetry from the definition
    • It is wrong because cultures never change their minds about value
    • It was invented by the Russian Formalists, who rejected it
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It cannot explain why some texts reward close reading more than others, so it may make value seem arbitrary. A purely institutional definition captures how canons are formed, but critics argue it leaves out the qualities of texts that make readers return to them. Revise Foundations of Literary Study →

Drama and Shakespeare

  1. Which feature most distinguishes drama from prose fiction? (Foundation recall)
    • It always includes a narrator who explains the action
    • It is always shorter than a novel or a poem
    • It is always written in rhyming verse throughout
    • It is written to be performed before an audience
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It is written to be performed before an audience. Drama's meaning is completed in performance, usually without a narrator. Revise Drama and Shakespeare →

  2. Two productions of the same play present one character as comic and as tragic, without changing the text. What does this show? (Exam style)
    • One production must be wrong
    • Performance choices shape meaning beyond the words
    • Plays have no fixed words
    • The text must have been altered secretly
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Performance choices shape meaning beyond the words. Delivery, casting and staging can produce different interpretations of the same lines. Revise Drama and Shakespeare →

  3. A student writes, "The reader feels tension as Macbeth looks at his hands." What is the best improvement? (Stretch)
    • Discuss the audience and how staging, pauses and sound effects create tension
    • Remove any reference to tension, since plays can't create it
    • Replace the word "reader" with "narrator" throughout the essay
    • Summarise the plot of the whole play instead of one moment
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Discuss the audience and how staging, pauses and sound effects create tension. Drama analysis should consider the audience and theatrical effects, not just the reader. Revise Drama and Shakespeare →

The Human Body

  1. Which cell structure controls what enters and leaves the cell? (Foundation recall)
    • The cell membrane
    • The Golgi apparatus
    • The nucleus
    • The ribosome
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The cell membrane. The phospholipid membrane is selectively permeable; the nucleus holds DNA and ribosomes make proteins. Revise The Human Body →

  2. Blood is classed as which type of tissue? (Exam style)
    • Connective
    • Epithelial
    • Muscle
    • Nervous
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Connective. Blood is a connective tissue: cells in a fluid matrix (plasma), like bone and cartilage, which have cells in a solid matrix. Revise The Human Body →

  3. Why does damage to the small blood vessels in diabetes cause problems in so many different organs? (Stretch)
    • Diabetes infects each organ separately
    • Every organ depends on small vessels for its supply, so damage at the tissue level affects eyes, kidneys and nerves alike
    • Insulin is made in every organ
    • Small vessels exist only in the eyes and kidneys
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Every organ depends on small vessels for its supply, so damage at the tissue level affects eyes, kidneys and nerves alike. The endothelium lines vessels throughout the body; when it is damaged, organs with dense small-vessel networks show the effects first. Revise The Human Body →

Chemistry

  1. What is the relative charge of a neutron? (Foundation recall)
    • +1
    • +2
    • −1
    • 0
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    0. Applying the standard result: Neutrons are neutral. Revise Chemistry →

  2. An atom of sodium has atomic number 11 and mass number 23. How many neutrons does it have? (Exam style)
    • 11
    • 12
    • 23
    • 34
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    12. Applying the standard result: 23 − 11 = 12. Revise Chemistry →

  3. Why do isotopes of the same element react in the same way? (Stretch)
    • They have different numbers of protons
    • They have no neutrons
    • They have the same electron arrangement
    • They have the same mass number
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    They have the same electron arrangement. Applying the standard result: Chemistry depends on electrons. Revise Chemistry →

Physics

  1. Which energy store increases when a spring is stretched? (Foundation recall)
    • Chemical
    • Elastic potential
    • Gravitational potential
    • Nuclear
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Elastic potential. Stretching or squashing an object increases its elastic potential store. Revise Physics →

  2. How is energy transferred when a current flows through a heater? (Exam style)
    • By electrical work
    • By mechanical work on air
    • By radiation only
    • By the nuclear store
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    By electrical work. A current flowing transfers energy by electrical work, which then heats the element. Revise Physics →

  3. A car's speed doubles from 10 m/s to 20 m/s. What happens to its kinetic energy? (Stretch)
    • It becomes eight times larger
    • It becomes four times larger
    • It becomes twice as large
    • It stays exactly the same
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It becomes four times larger. Kinetic energy depends on speed squared, so doubling the speed multiplies Ek by 2² = 4. Revise Physics →

Foundations of Ecology

  1. Which of these is a biotic factor affecting a population of rabbits? (Foundation recall)
    • The average winter temperature
    • The number of foxes in the area
    • The pH of the soil
    • The slope of the hillside
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The number of foxes in the area. Foxes are living organisms (predators), so they are a biotic factor; the others are physical or chemical conditions. Revise Foundations of Ecology →

  2. A campaign leaflet says "ecology tells us we must ban all new building on farmland". What is the best response? (Exam style)
    • Ecology can predict effects on wildlife, but whether to ban building is a value judgement
    • Ecology has nothing to say about land use
    • Ecology proves that all new building harms the environment
    • The leaflet is right, because ecology is the same as environmentalism
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Ecology can predict effects on wildlife, but whether to ban building is a value judgement. Ecology provides evidence about consequences; deciding what to do also involves values, costs and other interests. Revise Foundations of Ecology →

  3. Why is the red squirrel's decline in Britain a good example of a typical ecological explanation? (Stretch)
    • A single abiotic factor, climate, explains it fully
    • It proves that introduced species always replace native ones
    • It shows that biotic factors never matter when habitat is suitable
    • Several interacting causes, competition, disease and history, combine to explain it
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Several interacting causes, competition, disease and history, combine to explain it. Ecological patterns usually arise from several interacting biotic and abiotic causes; single-factor explanations are rarely enough. Revise Foundations of Ecology →

Geography: Human Environments and Skills

  1. Which of these is a push factor encouraging people to leave rural areas? (Foundation recall)
    • A new city hospital
    • Better-paid factory jobs
    • More secondary schools in towns
    • Repeated crop failure
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Repeated crop failure. Push factors are problems in the place people leave, such as crop failure, while the others are pull factors. Revise Geography: Human Environments and Skills →

  2. Why do cities with many young migrants also have high natural increase? (Exam style)
    • Young adults are at the age when most people have children
    • Young adults have the highest death rates of any group
    • Young adults rarely settle in one place for long
    • Young migrants usually return quickly to rural areas
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Young adults are at the age when most people have children. Migrants are mostly young adults of child-bearing age, so the birth rate in the city rises. Revise Geography: Human Environments and Skills →

  3. Which statement best explains why most new megacities are in LICs and NEEs? (Stretch)
    • Counter-urbanisation from city centres
    • Falling birth rates and an ageing population
    • Rapid rural–urban migration and high natural increase
    • Strict limits on building in urban areas
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Rapid rural–urban migration and high natural increase. LICs and NEEs combine high migration with high natural increase, so their cities grow fastest. Revise Geography: Human Environments and Skills →

Foundations of Health and Wellbeing

  1. Which statement is a negative definition of health? (Foundation recall)
    • Health is a reserve of strength to draw on
    • Health is complete physical, mental and social wellbeing
    • Health is the ability to adapt and self-manage
    • Health is the absence of disease or illness
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Health is the absence of disease or illness. A negative definition says what health is not (disease); the others describe something present, so they are positive or functional definitions. Revise Foundations of Health and Wellbeing →

  2. A man with well-controlled epilepsy works full time, plays football and says he feels well. Which definition is least likely to class him as healthy? (Exam style)
    • A functional lay definition
    • A psychosocial wellbeing definition
    • Huber's ability-to-adapt definition
    • The negative (absence of disease) definition
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The negative (absence of disease) definition. He has a diagnosed condition, so a strictly negative definition excludes him, while adaptive, functional and wellbeing definitions focus on how he copes and lives. Revise Foundations of Health and Wellbeing →

  3. What is the strongest criticism of the word "complete" in the WHO definition? (Stretch)
    • Almost nobody meets it for long, so it risks medicalising ordinary life and excludes people living well with chronic conditions
    • It defines health only as the absence of disease
    • It leaves out physical health altogether
    • It was written by doctors rather than by politicians
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Almost nobody meets it for long, so it risks medicalising ordinary life and excludes people living well with chronic conditions. "Complete" wellbeing is an ideal few reach; with an ageing population and common long-term conditions, it would label most people unhealthy. Revise Foundations of Health and Wellbeing →
