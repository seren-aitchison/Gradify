Careers and Employability practice questions

33 questions from Gradify's free Careers and Employability course, with the answer and why behind each reveal. Answer before you open it: that's what makes it stick.

Knowing Yourself

  1. Which is the best description of a career as careers professionals use the word? (Foundation recall)
    • A profession that needs a degree and a formal licence
    • The highest job title a person reaches in one employer
    • The lifelong pattern of a person's learning, work and life roles
    • The paid jobs a person holds after leaving full-time study
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The lifelong pattern of a person's learning, work and life roles. Careers professionals use "career" broadly: education, work, caring and volunteering all form part of it, not just paid graduate roles or promotions. Revise Knowing Yourself →

  2. Sam has a strong degree, internships and good references but can't find a job during a recession. What does this show about employability? (Exam style)
    • Being employable makes work more likely but does not guarantee it
    • Employability is only measured by whether a person is in work
    • Sam must lack the skills that employers really value
    • Sam's degree subject must be the main cause of the problem
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Being employable makes work more likely but does not guarantee it. Employability describes a person's assets; the labour market also matters, so an employable person can be unemployed when demand for workers is weak. Revise Knowing Yourself →

  3. Which is the strongest reason the CDI framework treats career development as lifelong? (Stretch)
    • Employers no longer offer any permanent contracts to new graduate employees
    • Most careers advisers prefer to work with adults rather than with school pupils
    • People change roles and sectors and return to learning, so they must keep managing transitions
    • Skills learned at school stop being useful after the age of about twenty-five
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    People change roles and sectors and return to learning, so they must keep managing transitions. Non-linear careers with repeated transitions are the norm, so the ability to manage change is needed throughout life, not just at one decision point. Revise Knowing Yourself →

Exploring Sectors and Roles

  1. Which is an example of qualitative labour market information? (Foundation recall)
    • A nurse describing a typical shift
    • Median salaries for accountants
    • Projected jobs growth by 2035
    • The national unemployment rate
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A nurse describing a typical shift. A first-hand description of work is qualitative; rates, salaries and projections are statistical, so quantitative. Revise Exploring Sectors and Roles →

  2. A job profile gives a national average salary of £38,000 for an occupation. What should a student in the North East also check? (Exam style)
    • Nothing, as national averages apply to everyone
    • Regional pay and entry-level salaries for the role
    • The salary of the most senior person in the field
    • Whether the figure is printed in a bold font
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Regional pay and entry-level salaries for the role. National averages hide differences by region and experience; entry salaries locally are what matter for a new graduate. Revise Exploring Sectors and Roles →

  3. Which combination gives the most reliable picture of a career option? (Stretch)
    • A friend's opinion based on a television drama about the job
    • A single social media post by someone who enjoys the job
    • Official data on pay and demand plus first-hand accounts from people in the job
    • One company's recruitment brochure about its own graduate scheme
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Official data on pay and demand plus first-hand accounts from people in the job. Combining independent statistics with several first-hand accounts balances breadth and depth; the others are single, partial or promotional views. Revise Exploring Sectors and Roles →

Work Experience, Internships and Placements

  1. Which Gatsby Benchmark covers experiences of workplaces? (Foundation recall)
    • Benchmark 2
    • Benchmark 4
    • Benchmark 6
    • Benchmark 8
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Benchmark 6. Benchmark 6 is experiences of workplaces; 2 is labour market information, 4 links curriculum to careers and 8 is personal guidance. Revise Work Experience, Internships and Placements →

  2. Why is work shadowing common in medicine and law courts? (Exam style)
    • Doctors and judges are not allowed to supervise any young people
    • Employers in these fields only take people who have finished degrees
    • Shadowing always lasts much longer than any other kind of work experience
    • Untrained people can't safely or legally do the work, but can observe it
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Untrained people can't safely or legally do the work, but can observe it. Where tasks need qualifications or carry risk, observing is the safe way to learn about the job. Revise Work Experience, Internships and Placements →

  3. Which description of work experience would most impress an admissions tutor or employer? (Stretch)
    • A description of the building and the size of the organisation
    • A list of five organisations with dates but no further details
    • A statement that the student completed over two hundred hours in total
    • What the student observed, a task they did and what it taught them
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    What the student observed, a task they did and what it taught them. Reflection on specific observations and learning shows insight and motivation; lists and hours don't show what was gained. Revise Work Experience, Internships and Placements →

Networking and Your Professional Presence

  1. Who wrote "The strength of weak ties" (1973)? (Foundation recall)
    • Donald Super
    • Edgar Schein
    • John Holland
    • Mark Granovetter
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Mark Granovetter. Granovetter's sociological study found job information often came through acquaintances; the others are career theorists. Revise Networking and Your Professional Presence →

  2. Which contact is most likely to tell a student about an opportunity she hasn't heard of? (Exam style)
    • An alumna she met once at a careers event
    • Her best friend on the same course
    • Her flatmate who knows the same people
    • Her sister who studies the same subject
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    An alumna she met once at a careers event. Weak ties connect to different circles and information; close contacts tend to know what she already knows. Revise Networking and Your Professional Presence →

  3. Which is the strongest reason networks contribute to unequal access to professional careers? (Stretch)
    • Employers are legally required to recruit only through personal networks
    • Networking events are banned for students at most UK universities
    • Opportunities shared informally reach those already connected to professionals
    • People from professional families are always better qualified than others
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Opportunities shared informally reach those already connected to professionals. Informal sharing advantages those whose strong and weak ties already include professionals, which access schemes try to counter. Revise Networking and Your Professional Presence →

CVs and Cover Letters

  1. How long should a typical UK graduate CV be? (Foundation recall)
    • As long as possible
    • At least five pages long
    • Exactly half a page
    • No more than two sides of A4
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    No more than two sides of A4. Applying the standard result: Two sides is the usual maximum. Revise CVs and Cover Letters →

  2. What does a recruiter usually look for in the first scan of a CV? (Exam style)
    • A full list of every hobby
    • Evidence matching the job's essential criteria
    • The candidate's photograph and age
    • The font size used on the page
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Evidence matching the job's essential criteria. Applying the standard result: Relevance decides shortlisting. Revise CVs and Cover Letters →

  3. A candidate wants to include their marital status and nationality on a UK CV. What is the best advice? (Stretch)
    • Add a photo alongside them
    • Include them only if married
    • Leave them out, as they are not needed
    • Put them at the very top of the CV
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Leave them out, as they are not needed. Applying the standard result: Personal details are unnecessary. Revise CVs and Cover Letters →

Applications and Psychometric Tests

  1. What usually happens to equal opportunities monitoring information on an application form? (Foundation recall)
    • It is kept separate from assessment and used for monitoring
    • It is passed to interviewers to help them choose
    • It is published on the employer's website by name
    • It is scored as part of the candidate's application
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It is kept separate from assessment and used for monitoring. Monitoring data is separated from selection and used to check that processes are fair. Revise Applications and Psychometric Tests →

  2. An employer recruits on a rolling basis with a deadline of 30 November. When should a student apply? (Exam style)
    • As early as possible once she has prepared a strong application
    • In December, after the deadline, to show extra persistence
    • On 30 November, so that her application is the most recent
    • Only after hearing whether her other applications succeed
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    As early as possible once she has prepared a strong application. With rolling recruitment, places fill as applications arrive, so a strong early application is best. Revise Applications and Psychometric Tests →

  3. Which is the strongest reason employers prefer forms for large graduate schemes? (Stretch)
    • Consistent questions allow fair, criteria-based scoring across many applicants
    • Forms are required by law for all employers with over 50 staff
    • Forms guarantee the best candidates always apply to the employer
    • Forms let employers avoid reading any details about candidates at all
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Consistent questions allow fair, criteria-based scoring across many applicants. Standardised answers can be scored consistently against criteria, which improves fairness and efficiency. Revise Applications and Psychometric Tests →

Interviews and Assessment Centres

  1. Which interview format asks every candidate the same questions and scores them against set criteria? (Foundation recall)
    • Informal coffee chat
    • Speculative meeting
    • Structured interview
    • Unstructured interview
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Structured interview. Structured interviews use standard questions and scoring, which improves fairness and prediction. Revise Interviews and Assessment Centres →

  2. Why do employers often start with a phone or video screening interview? (Exam style)
    • It allows candidates to choose the questions they answer
    • It filters large numbers of applicants quickly and cheaply
    • It is the stage where final job offers are always made
    • It replaces the need to check right to work in the UK
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It filters large numbers of applicants quickly and cheaply. Screening formats are efficient early filters before more costly, in-depth stages. Revise Interviews and Assessment Centres →

  3. A firm's interviews are free-flowing chats where each manager asks whatever they like. What is the main weakness? (Stretch)
    • Candidates are compared unfairly, with weaker prediction of performance
    • Candidates will have too much time to prepare their answers
    • Interviews will be too short to show any of a candidate's skills
    • Managers will be unable to ask any follow-up questions at all
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Candidates are compared unfairly, with weaker prediction of performance. Unstructured interviews are less consistent, more open to bias and predict performance less well. Revise Interviews and Assessment Centres →

Graduate Schemes, Apprenticeships and Other Routes

  1. Which source surveys graduates 15 months after they finish their course? (Foundation recall)
    • Graduate Outcomes
    • ISE Student Recruitment Survey
    • Labour Force Survey only
    • UCAS end of cycle report
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Graduate Outcomes. HESA's Graduate Outcomes survey asks graduates about their activities around 15 months after finishing. Revise Graduate Schemes, Apprenticeships and Other Routes →

  2. Why might a salary figure from a survey of large employers be higher than one from Graduate Outcomes? (Exam style)
    • It covers only large recruiters rather than all graduate jobs
    • It includes graduates who have stayed unemployed for years
    • It measures salaries ten years after graduation instead
    • It records salaries in a different currency from the other
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It covers only large recruiters rather than all graduate jobs. Large employer surveys cover a well-paid segment, while Graduate Outcomes covers graduates in all jobs. Revise Graduate Schemes, Apprenticeships and Other Routes →

  3. A news story says "graduates earn more, so a degree always pays off." What is the best evaluation? (Stretch)
    • Averages hide large variation, and causation is debated
    • It is correct, because earnings data never differ by subject studied
    • It is correct, because every graduate earns more than every non-graduate
    • It is wrong, because graduates earn less than non-graduates on average
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Averages hide large variation, and causation is debated. Average gains are real, but they vary widely and may partly reflect prior characteristics. Revise Graduate Schemes, Apprenticeships and Other Routes →

Starting Work: Contracts and Rights

  1. Which status gives the widest set of employment rights? (Foundation recall)
    • Employee
    • Self-employed
    • Volunteer
    • Worker
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Employee. Employees have extra rights such as unfair dismissal protection and redundancy pay on top of worker rights. Revise Starting Work: Contracts and Rights →

  2. Which right do workers have but self-employed people do not? (Exam style)
    • Choosing to run their own business
    • Sending a substitute to do the work
    • Setting their own prices for clients
    • The National Minimum Wage
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The National Minimum Wage. Workers are entitled to the minimum wage and paid holiday, unlike the genuinely self-employed. Revise Starting Work: Contracts and Rights →

  3. A contract calls Asha "self-employed", but she must work set shifts, cannot send anyone else and uses the firm's equipment. What is most likely? (Stretch)
    • She has no employment rights of any kind at all
    • She is automatically a director of the business
    • She is self-employed because the contract says so
    • She may legally be a worker or employee despite the label
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    She may legally be a worker or employee despite the label. Status depends on the reality, and personal service, control and the firm's equipment point away from self-employment. Revise Starting Work: Contracts and Rights →

Marketing

  1. Which orientation starts by identifying what customers need? (Foundation recall)
    • Marketing orientation
    • Product orientation
    • Production orientation
    • Sales orientation
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Marketing orientation. A marketing orientation puts customer needs first. Revise Marketing →

  2. A firm makes the best-engineered typewriters but sales are falling as customers move to computers. Which mistake has it made? (Exam style)
    • Excessive focus on reducing its prices
    • Marketing myopia, defining itself by the product, not the need
    • Over-reliance on customer research and feedback
    • Too broad a view of its market and customers
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Marketing myopia, defining itself by the product, not the need. The customer need is document production, which computers now meet better. Revise Marketing →

  3. A fast-food chain adds healthier options and reduces packaging waste while remaining profitable. Which approach does this reflect? (Stretch)
    • Product orientation, focusing on technical features
    • Production orientation, focusing on cheap output
    • Sales orientation, pushing existing products harder
    • Societal marketing, balancing customers, profit and society
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Societal marketing, balancing customers, profit and society. Societal marketing considers long-term wellbeing alongside needs and profit. Revise Marketing →

Career Planning and Resilience

  1. Who developed the DOTS model of career learning? (Foundation recall)
    • Bill Law and Tony Watts
    • Frank Parsons alone
    • John Holland and Donald Super
    • Mark Granovetter
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Bill Law and Tony Watts. Law and Watts set out the DOTS model in 1977 as a framework for careers education. Revise Career Planning and Resilience →

  2. Researching what jobs a geography degree can lead to belongs to which part of DOTS? (Exam style)
    • Decision learning
    • Opportunity awareness
    • Self awareness
    • Transition learning
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Opportunity awareness. Finding out what options exist is opportunity awareness. Revise Career Planning and Resilience →

  3. A student knows her values well and has researched options but keeps putting off choosing. Which area needs most work? (Stretch)
    • Decision learning
    • Nothing in DOTS
    • Opportunity awareness
    • Self awareness
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Decision learning. She has self and opportunity awareness, so the gap is in making and acting on decisions. Revise Career Planning and Resilience →
