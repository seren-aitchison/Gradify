Historical Thinking and Methods

  1. Which statement best describes history as a discipline? (Foundation recall)
    • A complete record of everything that happened
    • A list of important dates to be memorised
    • An evidence-based account of the past that answers questions
    • Opinions about the past, all equally valid
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    An evidence-based account of the past that answers questions. History is enquiry: questions answered with evidence. It is selective, not complete, and interpretations are judged by evidence. Revise Historical Thinking and Methods →

  2. Two historians use the same documents but reach different conclusions about why a war began. What is the most likely reason? (Exam style)
    • Historical evidence is always forged
    • One of them must have invented evidence
    • Only one of them read the documents
    • They weigh the evidence and its significance differently
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    They weigh the evidence and its significance differently. Interpretation involves judging which evidence matters most, so careful historians can disagree without either being dishonest. Revise Historical Thinking and Methods →

  3. Which is the strongest reply to the claim that history is "just facts and dates"? (Stretch)
    • Dates matter more than explanations, because they can't be disputed
    • Facts matter, but history selects and explains them to answer questions, which involves argument
    • Historians now agree on the main facts, so argument is no longer needed
    • History is mostly opinion, since every historian has a different view of events
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Facts matter, but history selects and explains them to answer questions, which involves argument. Facts constrain history, but explaining and judging significance requires argument; the reply that keeps both is strongest. Revise Historical Thinking and Methods →

Medieval Britain

  1. Which kingdom eventually unified England under its kings? (Foundation recall)
    • East Anglia
    • Kent
    • Northumbria
    • Wessex
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Wessex. The kings of Wessex, from Alfred's family, led the creation of a single English kingdom. Revise Medieval Britain →

  2. What does the Sutton Hoo ship burial suggest about early Anglo-Saxon kings? (Exam style)
    • They had no contact outside Britain
    • They were Christian bishops rather than kings
    • They were unable to make fine objects
    • They were wealthy and connected to trade across Europe and beyond
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    They were wealthy and connected to trade across Europe and beyond. Its gold, silver and imported items show wealth and wide connections. Revise Medieval Britain →

  3. Which judgement about England in 1066 is best supported? (Stretch)
    • It had advanced administration, but powerful earls and a disputed succession made it vulnerable
    • It had no shires, coinage or taxes, so the Normans built government from nothing
    • It was so strong that no invasion could have succeeded
    • It was still divided into seven independent kingdoms in 1066
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It had advanced administration, but powerful earls and a disputed succession made it vulnerable. The evidence shows sophisticated government alongside political divisions. Revise Medieval Britain →
