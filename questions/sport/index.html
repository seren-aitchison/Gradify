Functional Anatomy and the Musculoskeletal System

  1. In the anatomical position, which way do the palms face? (Foundation recall)
    • Backwards
    • Forwards
    • Towards the thighs
    • Upwards
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Forwards. The anatomical position has the palms facing forwards (anteriorly); this places the radius and ulna side by side, which the directional terms for the forearm assume. Revise Functional Anatomy and the Musculoskeletal System →

  2. A goalkeeper dives sideways to save a shot, moving the whole body away from the midline. In which plane is the movement? (Exam style)
    • Frontal
    • Longitudinal
    • Sagittal
    • Transverse
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Frontal. Sideways movement that divides front from back happens in the frontal plane, rotating about the sagittal axis. "Longitudinal" is an axis, not a plane. Revise Functional Anatomy and the Musculoskeletal System →

  3. Why are shoulder dislocations far more common in sport than hip dislocations? (Stretch)
    • Shoulder muscles are always weaker than hip muscles
    • The humerus is weaker than the femur
    • The shoulder girdle's loose attachment and shallow socket favour mobility over stability
    • The shoulder has no ligaments
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The shoulder girdle's loose attachment and shallow socket favour mobility over stability. The pectoral girdle attaches to the trunk only at the sternoclavicular joint and the glenoid is shallow, so the joint is mobile but easily displaced; the deep acetabulum and fused pelvic girdle make the hip very stable. Revise Functional Anatomy and the Musculoskeletal System →

The Cardiorespiratory System in Exercise

  1. Which valve lies between the left atrium and the left ventricle? (Foundation recall)
    • Aortic valve
    • Bicuspid (mitral) valve
    • Pulmonary valve
    • Tricuspid valve
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Bicuspid (mitral) valve. The bicuspid or mitral valve is the left atrioventricular valve; the tricuspid is on the right, and the aortic and pulmonary valves are semilunar valves at the exits of the ventricles. Revise The Cardiorespiratory System in Exercise →

  2. Blood leaves the right ventricle. Where does it go next? (Exam style)
    • Into the right atrium
    • Through the aortic valve into the aorta
    • Through the pulmonary valve into the pulmonary artery to the lungs
    • Through the pulmonary vein to the lungs
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Through the pulmonary valve into the pulmonary artery to the lungs. The right ventricle pumps deoxygenated blood via the pulmonary valve and artery to the lungs; the pulmonary veins return it to the left atrium. Revise The Cardiorespiratory System in Exercise →

  3. What makes the semilunar valves open during ventricular systole? (Stretch)
    • An impulse from the AV node
    • Atrial pressure falling below zero
    • Contraction of the chordae tendineae
    • Ventricular pressure rising above the pressure in the aorta and pulmonary artery
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Ventricular pressure rising above the pressure in the aorta and pulmonary artery. Valves are passive: they open when the pressure behind them exceeds the pressure in front. Revise The Cardiorespiratory System in Exercise →

Energy Systems and Exercise Metabolism

  1. What are the products of ATP breakdown? (Foundation recall)
    • ADP, inorganic phosphate and energy
    • ATP and carbon dioxide
    • Creatine and phosphate
    • Glucose and oxygen
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    ADP, inorganic phosphate and energy. ATPase hydrolyses ATP to ADP and Pi, releasing energy; creatine and phosphate come from phosphocreatine breakdown. Revise Energy Systems and Exercise Metabolism →

  2. During a 30-minute run, what happens to the concentration of ATP in the leg muscles? (Exam style)
    • It falls to zero within a minute
    • It is replaced by glucose
    • It rises steadily
    • It stays nearly constant because resynthesis matches breakdown
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It stays nearly constant because resynthesis matches breakdown. The energy systems resynthesise ATP as fast as it is used, so its concentration is defended while turnover rises enormously. Revise Energy Systems and Exercise Metabolism →

  3. Which is a weakness of the ATP–PC system? (Stretch)
    • It is the slowest system
    • It needs oxygen to work
    • It produces lactate
    • It yields only one ATP per molecule of PC
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It yields only one ATP per molecule of PC. The system's yield and stores are small. It is anaerobic, fast and produces no lactate. Revise Energy Systems and Exercise Metabolism →

Exercise Physiology: Environment, Adaptation and Populations

  1. What is the main way heat is lost during exercise in hot conditions? (Foundation recall)
    • Conduction to the ground
    • Evaporation of sweat
    • Shivering
    • Vasoconstriction
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Evaporation of sweat. Applying the standard result: Evaporation removes most heat. Revise Exercise Physiology: Environment, Adaptation and Populations →

  2. A cyclist feels a cooling breeze. Which mechanism is this? (Exam style)
    • Conduction
    • Convection
    • Evaporation only
    • Radiation
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Convection. Applying the standard result: Moving air carries heat away. Revise Exercise Physiology: Environment, Adaptation and Populations →

  3. Why can conduction, convection and radiation add heat on a very hot day? (Stretch)
    • Muscles stop producing heat
    • Sweat stops working completely
    • The hypothalamus switches off
    • The surroundings are hotter than the skin
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The surroundings are hotter than the skin. Applying the standard result: Heat moves from hot to cool. Revise Exercise Physiology: Environment, Adaptation and Populations →

Biomechanics

  1. Which of these quantities is a vector? (Foundation recall)
    • Displacement
    • Distance
    • Mass
    • Speed
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Displacement. Displacement has both size and a stated direction. Revise Biomechanics →

  2. A swimmer completes 100 m in a 50 m pool in 60 s, finishing at the start wall. What is the average velocity? (Exam style)
    • 0 m/s
    • 0.83 m/s
    • 1.67 m/s
    • 2.0 m/s
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    0 m/s. Displacement is zero because she ends where she began, so velocity is zero. Revise Biomechanics →

  3. A cyclist rides round a circular velodrome at a constant 15 m/s. Which statement is correct? (Stretch)
    • Her displacement increases steadily each lap
    • Her velocity is constant because speed is constant
    • She has zero acceleration because speed is constant
    • She is accelerating because her direction changes
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    She is accelerating because her direction changes. Velocity is a vector, so a change of direction is an acceleration even at constant speed. Revise Biomechanics →

Training Principles and Programme Design

  1. Which principle states that training should match the demands of the sport? (Foundation recall)
    • Overload
    • Reversibility
    • Specificity
    • Variance
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Specificity. Specificity links adaptations to the type of training performed. Revise Training Principles and Programme Design →

  2. Which session is most specific for a 100 m sprinter? (Exam style)
    • 4 × 20 minutes of cycling at moderate pace
    • 6 × 30 m maximal sprints with full recovery
    • A 10 km steady run
    • A 45-minute low-intensity swim
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    6 × 30 m maximal sprints with full recovery. Short maximal sprints with full recovery stress the ATP–PC system. Revise Training Principles and Programme Design →

  3. A swimmer's coach replaces half the pool sessions with running. What is the main concern? (Stretch)
    • Running adaptations transfer poorly to swimming
    • Running gives no fitness gains at all
    • Running only trains the ATP–PC system
    • Running reverses all swimming fitness
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Running adaptations transfer poorly to swimming. Movement patterns and muscles differ, so transfer to swimming is limited. Revise Training Principles and Programme Design →

Fitness Testing and Performance Analysis

  1. What is a baseline measurement? (Foundation recall)
    • A score taken after a competition
    • An initial score used for later comparison
    • The average score of a national squad
    • The best score an athlete has ever achieved
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    An initial score used for later comparison. Baselines are taken before training to measure change. Revise Fitness Testing and Performance Analysis →

  2. A coach retests a squad after eight weeks and sees no improvement in aerobic scores. What should she do? (Exam style)
    • Assume the test must be broken
    • Review and adjust the training programme
    • Select only the fittest players
    • Stop testing aerobic fitness
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Review and adjust the training programme. Lack of change suggests the programme needs changing. Revise Fitness Testing and Performance Analysis →

  3. Why should a coach be cautious about using tests alone for team selection? (Stretch)
    • Tests always lower athlete motivation
    • Tests are never reliable enough to use
    • Tests do not capture skill, tactics and decision-making
    • Tests measure only body composition
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Tests do not capture skill, tactics and decision-making. Selection needs a broader picture of performance. Revise Fitness Testing and Performance Analysis →

Sport and Exercise Nutrition

  1. Which of these counts as a free sugar? (Foundation recall)
    • The lactose in plain milk
    • The starch in a potato
    • The sugar in a whole apple
    • The sugar in unsweetened orange juice
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The sugar in unsweetened orange juice. Juicing releases sugars from the fruit's cells, so they count as free sugars; sugars in whole fruit and plain milk don't. Revise Sport and Exercise Nutrition →

  2. An adult eats about 18 g of fibre a day. Which change would increase it most? (Exam style)
    • Choosing low-fat yoghurt instead of full-fat yoghurt
    • Drinking more fruit juice with every meal of the day
    • Eating more cheese and eggs instead of meat
    • Switching white bread and rice to wholegrain versions and adding a portion of beans
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Switching white bread and rice to wholegrain versions and adding a portion of beans. Wholegrains and pulses are rich in fibre; juice, cheese, eggs and yoghurt contain little or none. Revise Sport and Exercise Nutrition →

  3. A cereal is labelled "no added sugar" but is sweetened with concentrated fruit juice. How should it be judged? (Stretch)
    • It contains no free sugars, because fruit is natural
    • It contains only fibre, because fruit is a plant food
    • It must be healthier than any cereal with added sugar
    • Its sweetness still comes from free sugars, so it may not be a lower-sugar choice
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Its sweetness still comes from free sugars, so it may not be a lower-sugar choice. Concentrated fruit juice counts as free sugars; check the "of which sugars" line on the label. Revise Sport and Exercise Nutrition →

Motivation, Emotion and Wellbeing

  1. Reading a book because you enjoy the story shows… (Foundation recall)
    • Drive reduction
    • Extrinsic motivation
    • Intrinsic motivation
    • Overjustification
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Intrinsic motivation. Enjoyment of the activity itself is intrinsic. Revise Motivation, Emotion and Wellbeing →

  2. Children promised an award for drawing later draw less in free time. This is… (Exam style)
    • Drive reduction
    • Flow
    • Positive reinforcement
    • The overjustification effect
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The overjustification effect. Expecting an award reduced the children's later free-choice interest in drawing. Revise Motivation, Emotion and Wellbeing →

  3. Why might the same reward have different effects? (Stretch)
    • It can feel controlling or informative
    • Only money matters
    • People ignore rewards
    • Rewards are always identical
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It can feel controlling or informative. Framing determines whether a reward undermines or supports motivation. Revise Motivation, Emotion and Wellbeing →

Skill Acquisition and Coaching

  1. Which word best describes a skill? (Foundation recall)
    • Fixed
    • Inherited
    • Innate
    • Learned
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Learned. Applying the standard result: Skills are learned through practice. Revise Skill Acquisition and Coaching →

  2. A netball player reads a defender's position and passes into space. What type of skill is this? (Exam style)
    • A basic ability
    • Perceptual-motor
    • Purely cognitive
    • Purely motor
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Perceptual-motor. It combines perception, decision and movement. Revise Skill Acquisition and Coaching →

  3. Which feature best shows that a gymnast is highly skilled? (Stretch)
    • She attempts every move very quickly
    • She performs the routine fluently and consistently
    • She trains more hours than her teammates
    • She was born with excellent balance
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    She performs the routine fluently and consistently. Consistent, fluent and efficient performance indicates skill. Revise Skill Acquisition and Coaching →

Injury Prevention and Rehabilitation

  1. Which is an overuse injury? (Foundation recall)
    • A broken wrist from a fall
    • A dislocated shoulder from a rugby tackle
    • A ligament tear from an awkward landing
    • A stress fracture of the shin in a runner
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A stress fracture of the shin in a runner. Stress fractures develop from repeated loading over time. Revise Injury Prevention and Rehabilitation →

  2. What is the most common cause of overuse injuries? (Exam style)
    • A single heavy collision during a match
    • Drinking too much water during training
    • Increasing training load faster than tissues can adapt
    • Warming up for too long before exercise
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Increasing training load faster than tissues can adapt. Overuse injuries reflect a mismatch between load and tissue capacity. Revise Injury Prevention and Rehabilitation →

  3. A tennis player's long-standing Achilles pain ends with a sudden rupture. How is this best described? (Stretch)
    • A purely acute injury with no previous cause
    • A purely chronic injury that needs no urgent care
    • An acute-on-chronic injury, where overuse weakened the tendon before it failed
    • An injury caused only by poor footwear on the day
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    An acute-on-chronic injury, where overuse weakened the tendon before it failed. Chronic degeneration can predispose a tissue to sudden failure. Revise Injury Prevention and Rehabilitation →

Physical Activity

  1. Which is an example of physical activity that isn't exercise? (Foundation recall)
    • A planned gym circuit session
    • A structured running plan
    • A weekly swimming training programme
    • Cycling to work
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Cycling to work. Active travel is physical activity, though not planned exercise for fitness. Revise Physical Activity →

  2. About how many METs is a moderate-intensity activity? (Exam style)
    • 3–6 METs
    • Exactly 1 MET
    • Over 10 METs only
    • Under 1.5 METs
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    3–6 METs. Moderate activities use about three to six times resting energy. Revise Physical Activity →

  3. A person can comfortably sing while walking. What does this suggest about the intensity? (Stretch)
    • It is light, so walking faster would bring it into the moderate range
    • It is moderate, because walking always counts as moderate
    • It is vigorous, so they should slow down
    • The talk test can't be used for walking
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It is light, so walking faster would bring it into the moderate range. Being able to sing indicates light intensity; moderate activity makes breathing faster so singing is difficult. Revise Physical Activity →
