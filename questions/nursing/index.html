Nursing practice questions

Foundations of Nursing and Professional Practice

  1. Which is a registered nurse's core professional activity? (Foundation recall)
    • Assessing needs, planning and giving care, and evaluating it
    • Diagnosing and prescribing for every patient
    • Only giving personal care
    • Running the hospital's finances
    • Working only under a doctor's instructions
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Assessing needs, planning and giving care, and evaluating it. Assessment, planning, implementation and evaluation underpin nursing practice in every setting. Revise Foundations of Nursing and Professional Practice →

  2. A student asks why all fields share one set of standards of proficiency. What is the best answer? (Exam style)
    • Because only adult nursing has standards
    • Because the four fields are identical
    • Because the standards are only advisory
    • So every nurse meets the same core standards
    • So that nurses can also practise as midwives
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    So every nurse meets the same core standards. The NMC sets one set of proficiencies for all registered nurses; field-specific knowledge is built on top. Revise Foundations of Nursing and Professional Practice →

  3. A doctor tells a nurse to give a medicine in a way that the nurse believes is unsafe. What is the nurse's position? (Stretch)
    • Ask a student nurse to give it instead
    • Don't proceed; raise and escalate the concern
    • Give half the dose as a compromise
    • Proceed and record that the doctor insisted
    • Proceed, because the doctor is accountable
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Don't proceed; raise and escalate the concern. Nurses remain accountable for their own practice and must not act in a way they believe is unsafe; they must raise concerns. Revise Foundations of Nursing and Professional Practice →

Human Anatomy and Physiology

  1. The wrist is ___ to the elbow. (Foundation recall)
    • Distal
    • Medial
    • Proximal
    • Superior
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Distal. Distal means further from the limb's attachment to the trunk; the wrist is further from the shoulder than the elbow. Revise Human Anatomy and Physiology →

  2. A patient has pain just below the right ribs, lateral to the midline. Which quadrant is this? (Exam style)
    • Epigastric region
    • Left upper quadrant
    • Right lower quadrant
    • Right upper quadrant
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Right upper quadrant. Below the right costal margin and lateral to the midline is the right upper quadrant, which contains the liver and gallbladder. Revise Human Anatomy and Physiology →

  3. Red blood cells are placed in a solution with a much lower solute concentration than their cytoplasm. What happens? (Stretch)
    • Nothing, because the membrane blocks water
    • Solutes leave the cells by active transport
    • Water enters by osmosis and the cells swell and may burst
    • Water leaves and the cells shrink
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Water enters by osmosis and the cells swell and may burst. Water moves towards the higher solute concentration, which is inside the cells, so they swell and can lyse. Revise Human Anatomy and Physiology →

Health Assessment and Clinical Reasoning

  1. Which comes first in the nursing process? (Foundation recall)
    • Assessment
    • Discharge
    • Evaluation
    • Implementation
    • Planning
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Assessment. Care must be based on an assessment of needs. Revise Health Assessment and Clinical Reasoning →

  2. A patient's NEWS2 score is 0, but the nurse feels something is wrong because the patient is unusually quiet. What should the nurse do? (Exam style)
    • Ask the patient to call if they feel worse
    • Do nothing, because the score is normal
    • Escalate per local policy and reassess sooner
    • Record "patient quiet" and simply continue
    • Wait until the next scheduled observations
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Escalate per local policy and reassess sooner. Clinical concern is a valid reason to escalate regardless of the score. Revise Health Assessment and Clinical Reasoning →

  3. A patient's temperature is 35.4°C, pulse 118 and respiratory rate 24. They seem confused. What should the nurse consider? (Stretch)
    • Give paracetamol and observe for change
    • Possible sepsis: escalate urgently
    • Recheck the temperature in four hours
    • The patient is cold: offer a blanket only
    • The temperature is normal for older people
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Possible sepsis: escalate urgently. A low temperature with tachycardia, tachypnoea and new confusion is a red flag. Revise Health Assessment and Clinical Reasoning →

Pharmacology and Medicines Management

  1. Which reference gives UK doses, cautions and interactions? (Foundation recall)
    • NEWS2 chart
    • The British National Formulary (BNF)
    • The Caldicott Principles
    • The Mental Capacity Act Code
    • The NMC Code
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The British National Formulary (BNF). The BNF is the standard UK prescribing reference. Revise Pharmacology and Medicines Management →

  2. A nurse is asked to give a medicine they've never heard of. What should they do first? (Exam style)
    • Ask a student nurse to give it instead
    • Ask the patient what it's for, then give it
    • Give it, since it has been prescribed
    • Look it up or ask a pharmacist first
    • Skip it and document "not given"
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Look it up or ask a pharmacist first. Nurses must understand what they administer. Revise Pharmacology and Medicines Management →

  3. A patient on ramipril, furosemide and ibuprofen develops vomiting and reduced urine output. What should the nurse do? (Stretch)
    • Escalate: high risk of AKI; doses may need pausing
    • Give extra ibuprofen for the discomfort
    • Give the next doses as usual and monitor
    • Increase oral fluids only and continue all
    • Wait for the routine bloods next week
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Escalate: high risk of AKI; doses may need pausing. Dehydration with the triple whammy is a recognised cause of AKI. Revise Pharmacology and Medicines Management →

Pathophysiology and Clinical Conditions

  1. Which term describes the cause of a disease? (Foundation recall)
    • Aetiology
    • Morbidity
    • Pathogenesis
    • Prevalence
    • Prognosis
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Aetiology. Aetiology means cause; pathogenesis is how the disease develops. Revise Pathophysiology and Clinical Conditions →

  2. Why should nurses understand pathophysiology? (Exam style)
    • Only to pass written exams
    • To link signs to causes and spot deterioration
    • To make medical diagnoses independently
    • To prescribe all treatments themselves
    • To replace the need for investigations
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    To link signs to causes and spot deterioration. Knowing the mechanism helps nurses assess and act safely. Revise Pathophysiology and Clinical Conditions →

  3. A patient who had chemotherapy 8 days ago phones the ward feeling shivery with a temperature of 38.2°C. What should the nurse advise? (Stretch)
    • Attend urgently: possible neutropenic sepsis
    • Book a routine GP appointment this week
    • Drink more fluids and stay at home
    • Take paracetamol and rest at home
    • Wait 24 hours to see if it settles
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Attend urgently: possible neutropenic sepsis. Neutropenic sepsis can deteriorate rapidly. Revise Pathophysiology and Clinical Conditions →

Core Nursing Procedures and Clinical Skills

  1. You have just helped a patient with C. difficile to use the commode. How should you clean your hands? (Foundation recall)
    • Alcohol hand rub
    • An antiseptic wipe
    • Gloves alone
    • Soap and water
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Soap and water. Alcohol does not reliably kill C. difficile spores; mechanical washing with soap and water removes them. Revise Core Nursing Procedures and Clinical Skills →

  2. Which moment applies immediately before you access a patient's peripheral cannula to give a flush? (Exam style)
    • After body fluid exposure
    • After touching surroundings
    • Before a clean or aseptic procedure
    • Before touching a patient
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Before a clean or aseptic procedure. Accessing a vascular device is an aseptic procedure, so hands are cleaned immediately before it, even if they were cleaned on entering the bay. Revise Core Nursing Procedures and Clinical Skills →

  3. A port is wiped with an antiseptic and accessed immediately while still wet. Why is this a problem? (Stretch)
    • It isn't a problem as long as gloves are worn
    • The antiseptic may damage the syringe tip
    • The antiseptic needs to dry to work, and wet ports can carry organisms in
    • Wet ports are easier to access, so it's best practice
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The antiseptic needs to dry to work, and wet ports can carry organisms in. Antiseptics need contact and drying time to decontaminate effectively. Revise Core Nursing Procedures and Clinical Skills →

Clinical Practice and Care Planning

  1. Which stage of the nursing process judges whether goals have been achieved? (Foundation recall)
    • Assessment
    • Evaluation
    • Implementation
    • Problem identification
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Evaluation. Evaluation compares outcomes with the goals set in the plan and leads to revising it. Revise Clinical Practice and Care Planning →

  2. A patient's mobility improves faster than expected after surgery. What should happen to the care plan? (Exam style)
    • It is changed only if the patient asks
    • It is completed and filed as finished
    • It is evaluated and updated to reflect new goals
    • It stays the same until discharge
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It is evaluated and updated to reflect new goals. The nursing process is cyclical: new information leads to reassessment and a revised plan. Revise Clinical Practice and Care Planning →

  3. A patient's MUST score is 0, but the nurse notices they have eaten almost nothing for three days. What should the nurse do? (Stretch)
    • Accept the score of 0 and continue the current plan without change
    • Act on the observation: record intake, reassess and refer according to local policy
    • Ask the family to bring snacks and record nothing
    • Wait until the next weekly MUST screen and reassess then
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Act on the observation: record intake, reassess and refer according to local policy. Tools support judgement; recent poor intake is a concern even with a low score. Revise Clinical Practice and Care Planning →

Infection Prevention and Control

  1. Which microorganisms can reproduce only inside host cells? (Foundation recall)
    • Bacteria
    • Fungi
    • Protozoa
    • Viruses
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Viruses. Viruses lack the machinery to reproduce independently and must use host cells. Revise Infection Prevention and Control →

  2. A patient's nasal swab grows MRSA, but they have no signs of infection. How is this best described? (Exam style)
    • Active infection needing urgent treatment
    • Colonisation
    • Contamination of the swab only
    • Disease
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Colonisation. Organisms present without harm is colonisation, though precautions and decolonisation may follow local policy. Revise Infection Prevention and Control →

  3. A ward has several patients with sudden vomiting and diarrhoea. Which hand hygiene method should staff use? (Stretch)
    • Alcohol rub only, because it is faster
    • Antiseptic wipes only
    • Gloves instead of hand hygiene
    • Soap and water, because norovirus resists alcohol
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Soap and water, because norovirus resists alcohol. Suspected norovirus needs soap and water; alcohol rub isn't reliably effective against non-enveloped viruses. Revise Infection Prevention and Control →

Acute, Emergency and Critical Care Nursing

  1. Which is a principle of acute nursing? (Foundation recall)
    • Escalating early, before the patient is in extremis
    • Focusing on one task until it is finished
    • Taking observations only once a day
    • Waiting for a doctor to notice changes
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Escalating early, before the patient is in extremis. Early escalation is central to preventing harm. Revise Acute, Emergency and Critical Care Nursing →

  2. What is situational awareness? (Exam style)
    • Being aware of the time of each break
    • Knowing where every item of equipment is stored
    • Perceiving, understanding and anticipating what is happening
    • Remembering every patient's date of birth
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Perceiving, understanding and anticipating what is happening. It describes understanding a situation and predicting what comes next. Revise Acute, Emergency and Critical Care Nursing →

  3. A patient has a snoring noise when breathing and is drowsy. What should the nurse do first? (Stretch)
    • Check the wound dressing
    • Measure the blood pressure
    • Open the airway and call for help
    • Take a full drug history
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Open the airway and call for help. Snoring suggests partial airway obstruction, the most urgent problem. Revise Acute, Emergency and Critical Care Nursing →

Adult Nursing

  1. What does a NEWS2 score of 3 in a single parameter usually trigger? (Foundation recall)
    • A routine review the next day
    • An urgent clinical review
    • Discharge planning
    • No action
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    An urgent clinical review. A single red score of 3 needs an urgent response under the RCP guidance. Revise Adult Nursing →

  2. A patient's NEWS2 total is 7. What does this require? (Exam style)
    • A routine ward round review
    • An emergency response, usually from a team with critical care skills
    • Record the score and inform the night team at handover
    • Repeat the observations in 12 hours and review then
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    An emergency response, usually from a team with critical care skills. A total of 7 or more triggers an emergency assessment. Revise Adult Nursing →

  3. An adult with a learning disability is more agitated than usual and holding his abdomen. Staff say "that's just his behaviour." What should the nurse do? (Stretch)
    • Accept that it is his usual behaviour
    • Assess for a physical cause with his carers and escalate
    • Give prescribed sedation to settle his agitation
    • Wait for him to say what is wrong before acting
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Assess for a physical cause with his carers and escalate. Behaviour change can be a sign of pain or illness; avoiding diagnostic overshadowing saves lives. Revise Adult Nursing →

Children's and Young People's Nursing

  1. Why do children lose heat and fluid faster than adults? (Foundation recall)
    • Their metabolism is slower than adults'
    • They have a larger surface area relative to their body size
    • They have thicker skin than adults
    • They sweat less than adults at all ages
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    They have a larger surface area relative to their body size. A large surface area for size increases heat and fluid loss. Revise Children's and Young People's Nursing →

  2. Which sign usually appears late when a child is becoming seriously unwell? (Exam style)
    • A prolonged capillary refill time
    • A rising heart rate
    • A rising respiratory rate
    • Low blood pressure
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Low blood pressure. Children compensate, so hypotension is a late and worrying sign. Revise Children's and Young People's Nursing →

  3. A baby born at 32 weeks is now 12 weeks old. At what age should her growth be plotted? (Stretch)
    • 12 weeks actual age
    • 4 weeks corrected age
    • At birth, as she was premature
    • Her mother's due date
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    4 weeks corrected age. Corrected age is actual age minus the weeks born early (8 weeks here). Revise Children's and Young People's Nursing →

Mental Health Nursing

  1. What is the biopsychosocial model? (Foundation recall)
    • A legal framework for detaining people
    • A model used only for physical illness in hospital
    • Health explained by interacting biological, psychological and social factors
    • Health explained only by brain chemistry and genetics
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Health explained by interacting biological, psychological and social factors. It integrates biological, psychological and social influences on health. Revise Mental Health Nursing →

  2. Why do people with serious mental illness die earlier than average? (Exam style)
    • Because of old age at diagnosis
    • Mostly from preventable physical illnesses
    • Mostly from rare genetic disorders
    • Only from suicide in every case
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Mostly from preventable physical illnesses. Physical illnesses such as heart disease are the main cause, which is why physical health checks matter. Revise Mental Health Nursing →

  3. A person says, "Part of me wants to stop drinking, but it's the only thing that helps me sleep." What motivational interviewing approach fits best? (Stretch)
    • Ignore the comment and move on
    • Lecture them on the harms of alcohol
    • Reflect the ambivalence and explore their own reasons for change
    • Tell them firmly to stop drinking now
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Reflect the ambivalence and explore their own reasons for change. Reflecting ambivalence and eliciting change talk strengthens their motivation. Revise Mental Health Nursing →

    • The Care Act
    • The Mental Capacity Act
    • The NHS Long Term Plan
    • Valuing People
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

  2. What training became mandatory for health and social care staff in England under the Health and Care Act 2022? (Exam style)
    • Advanced life support training
    • Dementia awareness training only
    • Forklift safety training
    • The Oliver McGowan training
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The Oliver McGowan training. The Oliver McGowan Mandatory Training is required for staff in regulated services. Revise Learning Disability Nursing →

  3. A woman with a learning disability needs a dental check, a blood test and an ear examination, all of which distress her. What adjustment could help? (Stretch)
    • Cancelling all of them
    • Combining them under one anaesthetic
    • Doing them on three separate busy days
    • Doing them without explanation to save time
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Combining them under one anaesthetic. Combining procedures can reduce distress and improve access. Revise Learning Disability Nursing →

Community, Public Health and Health Promotion

    • Clinical audit only
    • Health improvement
    • Health protection
    • Healthcare public health
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Health protection. Health protection deals with infections and environmental hazards. Revise Community, Public Health and Health Promotion →

  2. Which body leads public health in Scotland? (Exam style)
    • NHS England
    • Public Health Scotland
    • The Public Health Agency
    • The UK Health Security Agency only
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Public Health Scotland. Each nation has its own public health body. Revise Community, Public Health and Health Promotion →

  3. A patient with COPD is frequently readmitted and says he can't afford to heat his flat. What should the nurse do? (Stretch)
    • Focus only on his inhaler technique
    • Note it and take no action
    • Refer to welfare, energy and housing support
    • Tell him to wear more jumpers
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

Older Adult, Palliative and End-of-Life Nursing

  1. What does "start low, go slow" mean in prescribing for older people? (Foundation recall)
    • Avoid all medicines in older age
    • Begin with low doses and increase gradually
    • Give the maximum dose immediately
    • Stop medicines when symptoms ease
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Begin with low doses and increase gradually. Altered pharmacokinetics make older people more sensitive to medicines. Revise Older Adult, Palliative and End-of-Life Nursing →

  2. Why do some fat-soluble sedatives last longer in older people? (Exam style)
    • Kidney function improves with age
    • Older people have more body water
    • The proportion of body fat increases with age
    • They absorb medicines faster
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The proportion of body fat increases with age. More fat increases the distribution and duration of fat-soluble drugs. Revise Older Adult, Palliative and End-of-Life Nursing →

  3. A previously independent 88-year-old becomes confused and falls after starting a new sleeping tablet. Which concept best explains this? (Stretch)
    • A sudden psychiatric illness
    • An unrelated coincidence
    • Homeostenosis
    • Normal ageing that needs no action
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Homeostenosis. Reduced reserve means a small change causes major decline. Revise Older Adult, Palliative and End-of-Life Nursing →

Research, Evidence-Based Practice and Nursing Leadership

  1. Which database focuses on nursing and allied health literature? (Foundation recall)
    • CINAHL
    • NEWS2
    • The BNF
    • The Green Book
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    CINAHL. CINAHL indexes nursing and allied health research. Revise Research, Evidence-Based Practice and Nursing Leadership →

  2. In PICO, what is "C"? (Exam style)
    • The comparison
    • The conclusion
    • The cost
    • The country
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The comparison. C is the comparison intervention or control. Revise Research, Evidence-Based Practice and Nursing Leadership →

  3. A very large trial finds a statistically significant reduction in blood pressure of 1 mmHg. How should a nurse interpret this? (Stretch)
    • A major clinical breakthrough
    • A Type II error
    • Proof that the study is flawed
    • Statistically significant but probably not clinically important
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Statistically significant but probably not clinically important. Large samples can detect tiny differences; clinical significance matters. Revise Research, Evidence-Based Practice and Nursing Leadership →

Nursing Law, Ethics and Safeguarding

  1. Which legislation established the Nursing and Midwifery Council? (Foundation recall)
    • The Care Act 2014
    • The Health and Social Care Act 2012
    • The Mental Capacity Act 2005
    • The Nursing and Midwifery Order 2001
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The Nursing and Midwifery Order 2001. The NMC was established by the Nursing and Midwifery Order 2001. Revise Nursing Law, Ethics and Safeguarding →

  2. How many written reflective accounts are required for NMC revalidation? (Exam style)
    • Five
    • None
    • Ten
    • Two
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Five. Revalidation requires five written reflective accounts, discussed with another registrant. Revise Nursing Law, Ethics and Safeguarding →

  3. A newly qualified nurse is unsure how to manage a deteriorating patient but doesn't ask for help, and the patient comes to harm. What standard will their care be judged against? (Stretch)
    • No standard, because they were newly qualified
    • That of a member of the public
    • That of a reasonably competent registered nurse in that post
    • That of a student nurse
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    That of a reasonably competent registered nurse in that post. The standard relates to the post; asking for help would have been the reasonable action. Revise Nursing Law, Ethics and Safeguarding →
