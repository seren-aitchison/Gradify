Professional Skills practice questions

30 questions from Gradify's free Professional Skills course, with the answer and why behind each reveal. Answer before you open it: that's what makes it stick.

Professional Communication

  1. In the transmission model, what does "noise" refer to? (Foundation recall)
    • Anything that distorts or blocks the message on its way to the receiver
    • The channel chosen to carry the message, such as email or a meeting
    • The reply the receiver sends to show they understood the message
    • The sender's process of turning an idea into words or images
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Anything that distorts or blocks the message on its way to the receiver. Noise is interference of any kind: physical, semantic, psychological or cultural. Feedback is the reply; encoding is the sender's process; the channel is the medium. Revise Professional Communication →

  2. You must tell a busy operations director that a supplier will deliver late. What should come first in your email? (Exam style)
    • A full history of the supplier relationship and earlier problems
    • An apology for writing, followed by a summary of your own workload
    • The detailed explanation of what went wrong in the supplier's factory
    • The new delivery date, its impact and any decision you need from them
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The new delivery date, its impact and any decision you need from them. A senior reader needs the news and its consequence first so they can act; background can follow or be attached for those who want it. Revise Professional Communication →

  3. A project update is accurate, complete and grammatically perfect, yet the steering group takes no decision. What is the most likely reason? (Stretch)
    • It contained too much information about the project budget
    • It never made clear what decision was needed from them, or by when
    • It used too few technical terms to sound credible to senior readers
    • It was sent by email rather than printed and circulated on paper
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It never made clear what decision was needed from them, or by when. Accuracy isn't enough: a message without a clear purpose and call to action leaves the reader unsure what to do. Channel and jargon are secondary here. Revise Professional Communication →

Social Psychology

  1. Overestimating how many people share your opinions is the… (Foundation recall)
    • False consensus effect
    • Halo effect
    • Primacy effect
    • Spotlight effect
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    False consensus effect. We assume others think as we do more often than they do. Revise Social Psychology →

  2. A new employee assumes meetings will start with small talk, then an agenda. Which concept is this? (Exam style)
    • A script
    • A self-schema
    • Controlled thinking
    • The spotlight effect
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A script. Expectations about event sequences are scripts. Revise Social Psychology →

  3. Why is the "motivated tactician" view more complete than the "cognitive miser" view? (Stretch)
    • People never use shortcuts
    • People switch strategy depending on goals
    • Shortcuts are always wrong
    • Thinking is always controlled
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    People switch strategy depending on goals. People choose effortful or quick thinking as needed. Revise Social Psychology →

Management and Organisational Behaviour

  1. Which management function involves comparing actual results with targets and correcting deviations? (Foundation recall)
    • Controlling
    • Leading
    • Organising
    • Planning
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Controlling. Controlling closes the cycle: it measures performance against the plan and takes corrective action, which then feeds into new plans. Revise Management and Organisational Behaviour →

  2. A team leader on a factory line spends most of the day solving machine problems and showing staff how to set up jobs. Which skill is this manager mainly using? (Exam style)
    • Conceptual skill
    • Political skill
    • Strategic skill
    • Technical skill
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Technical skill. Knowing how the work itself is done is technical skill, which Katz found matters most at first-line level. Revise Management and Organisational Behaviour →

  3. A chief executive is praised for detailed budgets and tight cost control but staff say the firm has no sense of direction. Using Kotter's distinction, what is missing? (Stretch)
    • Administration: more record-keeping is needed
    • Leadership: setting a direction and aligning people behind it
    • Management: the firm needs more planning and control
    • Nothing: control is enough for a large firm
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Leadership: setting a direction and aligning people behind it. Kotter separates management (coping with complexity through plans and control) from leadership (coping with change by setting direction). The CEO shows strong management but weak leadership. Revise Management and Organisational Behaviour →

Project Management

  1. Which is an example of business as usual? (Foundation recall)
    • Building a new hospital wing
    • Launching a new product
    • Moving to a new office
    • Processing monthly payroll
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Processing monthly payroll. Applying the standard result: Payroll repeats each month. Revise Project Management →

  2. A client adds features to a project but keeps the deadline fixed. What is most likely? (Exam style)
    • Costs will fall
    • Costs will rise
    • Nothing will change
    • Scope will shrink
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Costs will rise. Applying the standard result: The triple constraint links them. Revise Project Management →

  3. Why should a project agree which constraint is most fixed? (Stretch)
    • It guides trade-off decisions when problems arise
    • It means scope never changes
    • It removes all risk
    • It replaces planning
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It guides trade-off decisions when problems arise. Applying the standard result: Priorities guide trade-offs. Revise Project Management →

Productivity and Personal Effectiveness

  1. In goal-setting theory, which kind of goal usually leads to the best performance? (Foundation recall)
    • A goal set without any feedback
    • A specific and challenging goal
    • A vague instruction to do your best
    • An easy goal that guarantees success
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A specific and challenging goal. Locke and Latham found specific, difficult goals outperform "do your best" goals. Revise Productivity and Personal Effectiveness →

  2. Which of these is the best-written key result for the objective "Make onboarding painless for new starters"? (Exam style)
    • 90% of new starters rate onboarding 4 out of 5 or higher
    • Ask managers to be friendlier to their new starters
    • Hold a meeting with HR about onboarding problems
    • Write a new induction pack for the HR intranet
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    90% of new starters rate onboarding 4 out of 5 or higher. A key result is a measurable outcome; the other options are tasks or vague intentions. Revise Productivity and Personal Effectiveness →

  3. A new analyst faces a complex task she has never done before. What does goal-setting research suggest? (Stretch)
    • Avoid setting any goals until she is fully trained
    • Set a learning goal to find effective strategies first
    • Set the hardest possible output target immediately
    • Tie the output target directly to her bonus straight away
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Set a learning goal to find effective strategies first. For complex, unfamiliar tasks, learning goals often work better than tough performance targets. Revise Productivity and Personal Effectiveness →

Strategy

  1. Which is a strategic decision? (Foundation recall)
    • Acquiring a rival company in another country
    • Changing the weekly staff rota
    • Reordering stock for one store
    • Running a two-week discount promotion
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Acquiring a rival company in another country. Acquisitions commit major resources over the long term and affect the whole organisation. Revise Strategy →

  2. A car maker decides how its marketing department will support a move into electric vehicles. Which level of strategy is this? (Exam style)
    • Business
    • Corporate
    • Functional
    • Operational
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Functional. Supporting the business strategy within one department is functional-level strategy. Revise Strategy →

  3. Two airlines both cut costs by copying each other's check-in technology. Using Porter, what is the best analysis? (Stretch)
    • Copying rivals' technology is the definition of a good strategy
    • Each airline has created a unique and defensible strategic position
    • Strategy plays no real part in the competitive airline industry
    • They are improving operational effectiveness, which rivals can copy, rather than building a distinct strategy
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    They are improving operational effectiveness, which rivals can copy, rather than building a distinct strategy. Porter argues imitable efficiency gains don't give lasting advantage; strategy needs a different set of activities and trade-offs. Revise Strategy →

Data and Digital Skills at Work

  1. Which of these is an example of ordinal data? (Foundation recall)
    • Satisfaction ratings from poor to excellent
    • The department each employee works in
    • The exact time taken to answer a call
    • The names of suppliers on a list
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Satisfaction ratings from poor to excellent. Applying the standard result: Ordinal categories have a natural order. Revise Data and Digital Skills at Work →

  2. A manager's dashboard shows different staff numbers from the HR system. Which quality dimension is most clearly at issue? (Exam style)
    • Consistency
    • Timeliness
    • Uniqueness
    • Validity
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Consistency. Consistency asks whether data matches across systems. Revise Data and Digital Skills at Work →

  3. You are asked to base a staffing decision on a survey that only 12 of 200 staff answered. What is the best response? (Stretch)
    • Ignore all data and decide on instinct alone
    • Increase the results to estimate all 200 replies
    • Treat it as fully representative of all staff views
    • Use it cautiously and say that the low response limits it
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Use it cautiously and say that the low response limits it. Low completeness means results may not reflect the whole group. Revise Data and Digital Skills at Work →

The Business Environment

  1. Which of these is a service rather than a good? (Foundation recall)
    • A car insurance policy
    • A loaf of bread
    • A pair of trainers
    • A smartphone
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A car insurance policy. A service is intangible: insurance provides cover and peace of mind rather than a physical object, whereas bread, phones and trainers are tangible goods. Revise The Business Environment →

  2. A florist buys flowers and wrapping for £8 and sells a bouquet for £30. Which statement is correct? (Exam style)
    • The florist adds £22 of value per bouquet, from which wages, rent and profit must be paid
    • The florist adds £30 of value per bouquet
    • The florist adds no value because the flowers were grown by someone else
    • The florist makes £22 profit on each bouquet
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The florist adds £22 of value per bouquet, from which wages, rent and profit must be paid. Added value is £30 − £8 = £22. It is not profit, because the florist's labour, rent and other overheads have not yet been paid; skill and presentation are how the value is added. Revise The Business Environment →

  3. Which is the strongest reason for defining a business without reference to profit? (Stretch)
    • Charities, social enterprises and public bodies also combine resources to produce valued goods and services
    • Large companies prefer to talk about purpose rather than profit
    • Most businesses make a loss in their first year
    • Profit is hard to measure accurately
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Charities, social enterprises and public bodies also combine resources to produce valued goods and services. The defining activity, producing goods or services for customers using combined resources, is shared by organisations with very different goals. Losses or measurement problems don't change what a business is. Revise The Business Environment →

Ethics, Professionalism and Employment Rights

  1. Which behaviour best shows accountability? (Foundation recall)
    • Admitting your own error and fixing it
    • Asking a colleague to take the blame
    • Blaming a system for every problem
    • Waiting to see if anyone notices
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Admitting your own error and fixing it. Accountability means owning decisions and outcomes. Revise Ethics, Professionalism and Employment Rights →

  2. Which is a feature of a legally regulated profession? (Exam style)
    • Anyone may use the professional title freely
    • Members never need to update their skills
    • No qualifications are needed to practise
    • Practising without registration can be unlawful
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Practising without registration can be unlawful. Regulators such as the GMC control who may practise. Revise Ethics, Professionalism and Employment Rights →

  3. A consultant is asked to advise on a technical area she has never worked in. What is the most professional response? (Stretch)
    • Accept and learn quietly as she goes along
    • Accept but charge a lower fee for the work
    • Explain her limits and suggest someone with that expertise
    • Refuse to talk to the client again
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Explain her limits and suggest someone with that expertise. Competence includes knowing and being honest about the limits of your expertise. Revise Ethics, Professionalism and Employment Rights →

Motivation, Emotion and Wellbeing

  1. Reading a book because you enjoy the story shows… (Foundation recall)
    • Drive reduction
    • Extrinsic motivation
    • Intrinsic motivation
    • Overjustification
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Intrinsic motivation. Enjoyment of the activity itself is intrinsic. Revise Motivation, Emotion and Wellbeing →

  2. Children promised an award for drawing later draw less in free time. This is… (Exam style)
    • Drive reduction
    • Flow
    • Positive reinforcement
    • The overjustification effect
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The overjustification effect. Expecting an award reduced the children's later free-choice interest in drawing. Revise Motivation, Emotion and Wellbeing →

  3. Why might the same reward have different effects? (Stretch)
    • It can feel controlling or informative
    • Only money matters
    • People ignore rewards
    • Rewards are always identical
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It can feel controlling or informative. Framing determines whether a reward undermines or supports motivation. Revise Motivation, Emotion and Wellbeing →
