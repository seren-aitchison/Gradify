Foundations of Health and Wellbeing

  1. Which statement is a negative definition of health? (Foundation recall)
    • Health is a reserve of strength to draw on
    • Health is complete physical, mental and social wellbeing
    • Health is the ability to adapt and self-manage
    • Health is the absence of disease or illness
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Health is the absence of disease or illness. A negative definition says what health is not (disease); the others describe something present, so they are positive or functional definitions. Revise Foundations of Health and Wellbeing →

  2. A man with well-controlled epilepsy works full time, plays football and says he feels well. Which definition is least likely to class him as healthy? (Exam style)
    • A functional lay definition
    • A psychosocial wellbeing definition
    • Huber's ability-to-adapt definition
    • The negative (absence of disease) definition
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The negative (absence of disease) definition. He has a diagnosed condition, so a strictly negative definition excludes him, while adaptive, functional and wellbeing definitions focus on how he copes and lives. Revise Foundations of Health and Wellbeing →

  3. What is the strongest criticism of the word "complete" in the WHO definition? (Stretch)
    • Almost nobody meets it for long, so it risks medicalising ordinary life and excludes people living well with chronic conditions
    • It defines health only as the absence of disease, which ignores wellbeing entirely
    • It leaves out physical health altogether and focuses only on mental and social wellbeing
    • It was written by doctors rather than by politicians, so governments have never accepted it
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Almost nobody meets it for long, so it risks medicalising ordinary life and excludes people living well with chronic conditions. "Complete" wellbeing is an ideal few reach; with an ageing population and common long-term conditions, it would label most people unhealthy. Revise Foundations of Health and Wellbeing →

The Human Body

  1. Which cell structure controls what enters and leaves the cell? (Foundation recall)
    • The cell membrane
    • The Golgi apparatus
    • The nucleus
    • The ribosome
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The cell membrane. The phospholipid membrane is selectively permeable; the nucleus holds DNA and ribosomes make proteins. Revise The Human Body →

  2. Blood is classed as which type of tissue? (Exam style)
    • Connective
    • Epithelial
    • Muscle
    • Nervous
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Connective. Blood is a connective tissue: cells in a fluid matrix (plasma), like bone and cartilage, which have cells in a solid matrix. Revise The Human Body →

  3. Why does damage to the small blood vessels in diabetes cause problems in so many different organs? (Stretch)
    • Diabetes is an infection that spreads from organ to organ through the bloodstream over many years
    • Every organ depends on small vessels for its supply, so damage at the tissue level affects eyes, kidneys and nerves alike
    • Insulin is made in every organ, so each one fails separately once insulin production stops
    • Small vessels are found only in the eyes and kidneys, so the other organs are affected by chance
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Every organ depends on small vessels for its supply, so damage at the tissue level affects eyes, kidneys and nerves alike. The endothelium lines vessels throughout the body; when it is damaged, organs with dense small-vessel networks show the effects first. Revise The Human Body →

Nutrition

  1. Which of these counts as a free sugar? (Foundation recall)
    • The lactose in plain milk
    • The starch in a potato
    • The sugar in a whole apple
    • The sugar in unsweetened orange juice
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The sugar in unsweetened orange juice. Juicing releases sugars from the fruit's cells, so they count as free sugars; sugars in whole fruit and plain milk don't. Revise Nutrition →

  2. An adult eats about 18 g of fibre a day. Which change would increase it most? (Exam style)
    • Choosing low-fat yoghurt instead of full-fat yoghurt
    • Drinking more fruit juice with every meal of the day
    • Eating more cheese and eggs instead of meat
    • Switching white bread and rice to wholegrain versions and adding a portion of beans
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Switching white bread and rice to wholegrain versions and adding a portion of beans. Wholegrains and pulses are rich in fibre; juice, cheese, eggs and yoghurt contain little or none. Revise Nutrition →

  3. A cereal is labelled "no added sugar" but is sweetened with concentrated fruit juice. How should it be judged? (Stretch)
    • It contains no free sugars, because fruit is natural
    • It contains only fibre, because fruit is a plant food
    • It must be healthier than any cereal with added sugar
    • Its sweetness still comes from free sugars, so it may not be a lower-sugar choice
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Its sweetness still comes from free sugars, so it may not be a lower-sugar choice. Concentrated fruit juice counts as free sugars; check the "of which sugars" line on the label. Revise Nutrition →

Physical Activity

  1. Which is an example of physical activity that isn't exercise? (Foundation recall)
    • A planned gym circuit session
    • A structured running plan
    • A weekly swimming training programme
    • Cycling to work
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Cycling to work. Active travel is physical activity, though not planned exercise for fitness. Revise Physical Activity →

  2. About how many METs is a moderate-intensity activity? (Exam style)
    • 3–6 METs
    • Exactly 1 MET
    • Over 10 METs only
    • Under 1.5 METs
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    3–6 METs. Moderate activities use about three to six times resting energy. Revise Physical Activity →

  3. A person can comfortably sing while walking. What does this suggest about the intensity? (Stretch)
    • It is light, so walking faster would bring it into the moderate range
    • It is moderate, because walking always counts as moderate
    • It is vigorous, so they should slow down
    • The talk test can't be used for walking
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It is light, so walking faster would bring it into the moderate range. Being able to sing indicates light intensity; moderate activity makes breathing faster so singing is difficult. Revise Physical Activity →

Sleep, Stress and Rest

  1. Which hormone is released mainly during deep sleep and supports growth and repair? (Foundation recall)
    • Adrenaline
    • Glucagon
    • Growth hormone
    • Insulin
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Growth hormone. Growth hormone secretion peaks during slow-wave (deep) sleep. Revise Sleep, Stress and Rest →

  2. A driver has slept 5 hours a night all week and feels "fine". What does research suggest? (Exam style)
    • Feeling fine always means performance is normal
    • He has fully adapted and is at no extra risk
    • His reaction time and attention are likely impaired, increasing crash risk
    • Short sleep improves alertness after a week
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    His reaction time and attention are likely impaired, increasing crash risk. Chronic restriction impairs performance despite little feeling of sleepiness. Revise Sleep, Stress and Rest →

  3. Which is the best evaluation of the glymphatic theory of sleep? (Stretch)
    • It explains every known function of sleep on its own
    • It has been disproved by recent research and abandoned
    • It is proven beyond doubt by large trials in human volunteers
    • It is supported by animal studies, but evidence in humans is still developing
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It is supported by animal studies, but evidence in humans is still developing. Brain clearance during sleep has been shown in mice; human evidence is promising but limited. Revise Sleep, Stress and Rest →

Mental Health and Wellbeing

  1. In the dual continua model, what is "languishing"? (Foundation recall)
    • A diagnosed illness with good wellbeing
    • A severe mental illness
    • High wellbeing with no illness
    • Low wellbeing without a diagnosable illness
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Low wellbeing without a diagnosable illness. Languishing describes low wellbeing in the absence of illness. Revise Mental Health and Wellbeing →

  2. A person with well-managed depression reports enjoying life and work. Where are they on the dual continua model? (Exam style)
    • Coping: illness present with good wellbeing
    • Flourishing: no illness and high wellbeing
    • Languishing: no illness and low wellbeing
    • Struggling: illness with low wellbeing
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Coping: illness present with good wellbeing. The model allows good wellbeing alongside a diagnosis. Revise Mental Health and Wellbeing →

  3. Why might critics argue against relying only on diagnostic categories? (Stretch)
    • Categories always capture every experience of distress perfectly
    • Diagnoses are never useful in planning any treatment
    • Distress varies along a spectrum, and categories may medicalise ordinary difficulties
    • Mental health cannot be studied scientifically at all
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Distress varies along a spectrum, and categories may medicalise ordinary difficulties. Dimensional views see distress as continuous rather than all-or-nothing. Revise Mental Health and Wellbeing →

Health Behaviour and Behaviour Change

  1. Which is a major behavioural risk factor? (Foundation recall)
    • Blood group
    • Eye colour
    • Height
    • Smoking
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Smoking. Applying the standard result: Smoking is a leading modifiable risk. Revise Health Behaviour and Behaviour Change →

  2. What does clustering of health behaviours mean? (Exam style)
    • Behaviours are spread randomly across the population
    • Healthy behaviours never combine
    • Only one behaviour affects each person
    • Unhealthy behaviours tend to occur together in the same people
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Unhealthy behaviours tend to occur together in the same people. Smoking, drinking and inactivity often co-occur. Revise Health Behaviour and Behaviour Change →

  3. Why are population-wide policies such as tobacco taxes important alongside individual support? (Stretch)
    • Individual support is always enough on its own
    • Taxes have been shown to have no effect on smoking
    • They change the environment for everyone and can reduce inequalities
    • They only change the behaviour of the wealthiest people
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    They change the environment for everyone and can reduce inequalities. Price and availability shape behaviour across groups. Revise Health Behaviour and Behaviour Change →

Disease and Long-term Conditions

  1. Which type of pathogen causes measles? (Foundation recall)
    • A bacterium
    • A fungus
    • A parasitic worm
    • A virus
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A virus. Applying the standard result: Measles is a viral infection. Revise Disease and Long-term Conditions →

  2. Malaria is spread by mosquitoes. What route of transmission is this? (Exam style)
    • Blood transfusion only
    • Direct contact
    • Faecal–oral
    • Vector-borne
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Vector-borne. Applying the standard result: Mosquitoes act as vectors. Revise Disease and Long-term Conditions →

  3. Which person is likely to be most vulnerable to a serious infection? (Stretch)
    • A healthy 25-year-old athlete
    • A teenager with a cold
    • An adult who exercises daily
    • Someone receiving chemotherapy
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Someone receiving chemotherapy. Applying the standard result: Chemotherapy weakens immunity. Revise Disease and Long-term Conditions →

Public Health and Epidemiology

  1. What is the main focus of public health? (Foundation recall)
    • Hospital management only
    • Private health insurance
    • The health of populations
    • Treatment of individual patients
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The health of populations. Public health works at the level of whole populations, mainly through prevention. Revise Public Health and Epidemiology →

  2. Since 2013, who has led most public health work in England at a local level? (Exam style)
    • GP surgeries
    • Hospital trusts
    • Local authorities
    • The World Health Organization
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Local authorities. The Health and Social Care Act 2012 moved public health responsibilities to local authorities. Revise Public Health and Epidemiology →

  3. Which question is most typical of a public health approach? (Stretch)
    • How long will this patient stay in hospital?
    • Should this patient have surgery?
    • Which inhaler suits this patient best?
    • Why does this town have high rates of lung disease?
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Why does this town have high rates of lung disease?. Public health asks why disease is common in a population, not just why one person is ill. Revise Public Health and Epidemiology →

Health and Social Care Systems

  1. Which minister led the creation of the NHS? (Foundation recall)
    • Aneurin Bevan
    • Florence Nightingale
    • William Beveridge
    • Winston Churchill
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Aneurin Bevan. Aneurin Bevan was Minister of Health and steered the 1946 Act through Parliament. Revise Health and Social Care Systems →

  2. Which principle means patients do not pay when they receive treatment? (Exam style)
    • Based on ability to pay
    • Free at the point of use
    • Means-tested
    • Private insurance
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Free at the point of use. NHS care is funded mainly by taxation, not charges at the time of care. Revise Health and Social Care Systems →

  3. Why did Aneurin Bevan resign in 1951? (Stretch)
    • Charges were introduced for dentures and glasses
    • Doctors refused to join the NHS
    • Prescription charges were scrapped
    • The NHS was abolished
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Charges were introduced for dentures and glasses. Bevan resigned in protest at charges that breached the free-at-the-point-of-use principle. Revise Health and Social Care Systems →

Health Across the Life Course

  1. When is the anomaly scan usually offered? (Foundation recall)
    • At about 18 to 21 weeks of pregnancy
    • At about 38 weeks only
    • At about 4 weeks of pregnancy
    • Just after the baby is born
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    At about 18 to 21 weeks of pregnancy. The mid-pregnancy scan checks the baby's development. Revise Health Across the Life Course →

  2. Which risk is increased by smoking in pregnancy? (Exam style)
    • A shorter labour
    • Higher intelligence in the baby
    • Low birth weight
    • Lower blood pressure in the mother
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Low birth weight. Smoking restricts the baby's oxygen and growth. Revise Health Across the Life Course →

  3. Why are outcomes of pregnancy unequal in the UK? (Stretch)
    • All women receive exactly the same results
    • Antenatal care is unavailable in poorer areas
    • Deprivation and ethnicity are linked to worse access, conditions and outcomes
    • Inequalities only affect older mothers
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Deprivation and ethnicity are linked to worse access, conditions and outcomes. Maternal outcomes reflect wider health inequalities. Revise Health Across the Life Course →
