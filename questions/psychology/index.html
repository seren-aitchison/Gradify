Psychology practice questions

48 questions from Gradify's free Psychology course, with the answer and why behind each reveal. Answer before you open it: that's what makes it stick.

Foundations of Psychology

  1. Which is the best definition of psychology? (Foundation recall)
    • The interpretation of dreams and personality
    • The scientific study of mind and behaviour
    • The study of mental illness and its treatment
    • The study of the brain's anatomy
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The scientific study of mind and behaviour. Psychology covers all mental processes and behaviour, studied scientifically; therapy, anatomy and dreams are narrower topics within or beside it. Revise Foundations of Psychology →

  2. A newspaper claims that "everyone knows" stress causes stomach ulcers. How would a psychologist respond? (Exam style)
    • Accept it because it is common knowledge
    • Ask a few friends whether they agree
    • Reject it because newspapers are unreliable
    • Treat it as a hypothesis and look at controlled evidence
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Treat it as a hypothesis and look at controlled evidence. Psychology treats widely held beliefs as claims to test. (In fact most ulcers are caused by Helicobacter pylori infection, though stress may play a role: a good example of why testing matters.) Revise Foundations of Psychology →

  3. Which is the strongest reply to the claim that psychology is "just common sense"? (Stretch)
    • Common sense contains contradictory sayings and is often wrong when tested, as myths show
    • Common sense is always wrong, so psychology is always right
    • Psychologists have degrees and other people don't
    • Psychology uses technical words that common sense lacks
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Common sense contains contradictory sayings and is often wrong when tested, as myths show. The key point is that intuition must be tested; it is sometimes right and sometimes wrong, and only evidence tells us which. Jargon or credentials don't make a claim true. Revise Foundations of Psychology →

Biological Psychology and Neuroscience

  1. The spinal cord is part of the… (Foundation recall)
    • Autonomic nervous system
    • Central nervous system
    • Endocrine system
    • Somatic nervous system
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Central nervous system. The spinal cord and brain together make up the central nervous system. Revise Biological Psychology and Neuroscience →

  2. You pull your hand away from a pin before you feel pain. Why? (Exam style)
    • A spinal reflex arc acts first
    • Motor neurones are absent
    • Pain signals travel nowhere
    • The brain reacts faster than the spine
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A spinal reflex arc acts first. The reflex passes through the spinal cord before the brain registers pain. Revise Biological Psychology and Neuroscience →

  3. Why is damage to the medulla often life-threatening? (Stretch)
    • It controls breathing and heart rate
    • It controls vision only
    • It produces cerebrospinal fluid
    • It stores long-term memories
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It controls breathing and heart rate. Vital autonomic functions depend on the medulla. Revise Biological Psychology and Neuroscience →

Cognitive Psychology

  1. Which measure is most used to infer the duration of mental processes? (Foundation recall)
    • Body temperature
    • Heart rate
    • Reaction time
    • Self-report
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Reaction time. Differences in reaction time indicate extra processing. Revise Cognitive Psychology →

  2. Responding takes 50 ms longer when participants must also decide which hand to use. What does the subtractive method suggest? (Exam style)
    • Decisions take no time
    • Hands are slower than eyes
    • The decision stage takes about 50 ms
    • The task is unreliable
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The decision stage takes about 50 ms. The extra time is attributed to the added stage. Revise Cognitive Psychology →

  3. Which is the strongest criticism of the computer analogy in information processing? (Stretch)
    • It denies mental processes exist
    • It is too biological
    • It neglects emotion, context and the body
    • It uses too many reaction times
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It neglects emotion, context and the body. Embodied and emotional factors shape cognition in ways the analogy misses. Revise Cognitive Psychology →

Learning and Behaviour

  1. In Pavlov's experiment, salivation to the bell after conditioning is the… (Foundation recall)
    • Conditioned response
    • Neutral stimulus
    • Unconditioned response
    • Unconditioned stimulus
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Conditioned response. It is learned, and triggered by the bell, so it is the conditioned response. Revise Learning and Behaviour →

  2. A child is stung by a wasp in a garden and afterwards feels anxious in that garden. What is the conditioned stimulus? (Exam style)
    • The anxiety
    • The garden
    • The pain of the sting
    • The sting
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The garden. The garden was neutral until paired with the sting (UCS); now it triggers anxiety (CR). Revise Learning and Behaviour →

  3. Which is the strongest methodological criticism of the Little Albert study? (Stretch)
    • It studied adults rather than infants
    • It took place outside a laboratory
    • It used too many participants
    • It was a single, poorly measured case
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It was a single, poorly measured case. One infant, informal observations and no follow-up limit what can be concluded. Revise Learning and Behaviour →

Developmental Psychology

  1. During which stage do major organs first form? (Foundation recall)
    • Embryonic
    • Fetal
    • Germinal
    • Neonatal
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Embryonic. Organs form in weeks 3 to 8, the embryonic stage. Revise Developmental Psychology →

    • Genetic memory
    • Learning before birth
    • Object permanence
    • Teratogen effects

  3. Why is it hard to show that prenatal stress causes later child problems? (Stretch)
    • Children are never studied
    • Shared genes and settings
    • Stress can never be measured
    • Stress has no biological effects

    Shared genes and settings. Shared genes and postnatal settings confound the link. Revise Developmental Psychology →

Social Psychology

  1. Overestimating how many people share your opinions is the… (Foundation recall)
    • False consensus effect
    • Halo effect
    • Primacy effect
    • Spotlight effect
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    False consensus effect. We assume others think as we do more often than they do. Revise Social Psychology →

  2. A new employee assumes meetings will start with small talk, then an agenda. Which concept is this? (Exam style)
    • A script
    • A self-schema
    • Controlled thinking
    • The spotlight effect
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A script. Expectations about event sequences are scripts. Revise Social Psychology →

  3. Why is the "motivated tactician" view more complete than the "cognitive miser" view? (Stretch)
    • People never use shortcuts
    • People switch strategy depending on goals
    • Shortcuts are always wrong
    • Thinking is always controlled
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    People switch strategy depending on goals. People choose effortful or quick thinking as needed. Revise Social Psychology →

Personality and Individual Differences

  1. Which statistical method did Cattell use to reduce trait words to 16 factors? (Foundation recall)
    • Chi-square
    • Factor analysis
    • Meta-analysis
    • Regression
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Factor analysis. Factor analysis groups correlated items into factors. Revise Personality and Individual Differences →

  2. A person is outgoing at parties but quiet in lectures. Which view best explains this? (Exam style)
    • Interactionism
    • Psychoanalysis
    • Pure situationism
    • Pure trait theory
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Interactionism. Traits and situations together shape behaviour. Revise Personality and Individual Differences →

  3. Why does aggregating behaviour strengthen trait correlations? (Stretch)
    • Random situational noise averages out
    • Single acts are always typical
    • Situations disappear
    • Traits change with aggregation
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Random situational noise averages out. Averaging many occasions reveals stable tendencies. Revise Personality and Individual Differences →

Psychopathology

  1. Defining abnormality as behaviour that is rare is… (Foundation recall)
    • Deviation from social norms
    • Failure to function
    • Ideal mental health
    • Statistical infrequency
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Statistical infrequency. Defining abnormality by how rare a behaviour or score is, is statistical infrequency. Revise Psychopathology →

  2. A person stops washing, misses work and can't keep relationships going. Which definition best applies? (Exam style)
    • Deviation from ideal mental health
    • Failure to function adequately
    • Harmful dysfunction only
    • Statistical infrequency
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Failure to function adequately. Being unable to cope with daily life fits this definition. Revise Psychopathology →

  3. Why is deviation from social norms a risky definition? (Stretch)
    • It applies equally in every culture
    • It ignores behaviour completely
    • Norms vary and have been used to control people
    • Social norms never change over time
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Norms vary and have been used to control people. Same-sex relationships were once wrongly labelled disorder. Revise Psychopathology →

Clinical Psychology and Interventions

  1. A plan agreed with someone for staying safe in a crisis is a… (Foundation recall)
    • Diagnosis
    • Formulation
    • Questionnaire
    • Safety plan
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Safety plan. Safety plans are developed collaboratively. Revise Clinical Psychology and Interventions →

  2. A psychologist uses an interview, the PHQ-9 and a conversation with a GP (with consent). This shows… (Exam style)
    • Combining assessment methods
    • Diagnostic overshadowing
    • Exposure therapy
    • Relying on one source
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Combining assessment methods. Combining several sources of information gives a fuller and more reliable picture. Revise Clinical Psychology and Interventions →

  3. Why is cultural humility important in assessment? (Stretch)
    • Culture never affects any assessment
    • It removes the need for interviews
    • It replaces the need for questionnaires
    • Misreading culture can bias how difficulties are interpreted
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Misreading culture can bias how difficulties are interpreted. Assessment must understand the person's context. Revise Clinical Psychology and Interventions →

Research Methods

  1. Deriving a specific prediction from a general theory is… (Foundation recall)
    • Deduction
    • Induction
    • Operationalisation
    • Replication
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Deduction. Deduction moves from the general to the specific. Revise Research Methods →

  2. A researcher only reports the three participants whose results fit her theory. Which error is this? (Exam style)
    • Confirmation bias
    • Counterbalancing
    • Induction
    • Random allocation
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Confirmation bias. Selecting supportive evidence is confirmation bias, here as selective reporting. Revise Research Methods →

  3. Which design feature best protects against confirmation bias in analysis? (Stretch)
    • Analysing data before deciding the hypothesis
    • Preregistering predictions and analyses
    • Testing only people who agree
    • Using a bigger font in questionnaires
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Preregistering predictions and analyses. Committing to predictions and analyses in advance limits choosing results that fit. Revise Research Methods →

Statistics

  1. Reaction time in milliseconds is… (Foundation recall)
    • Interval data only
    • Nominal data
    • Ordinal data
    • Ratio data
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Ratio data. It has equal intervals and a true zero, so ratios (twice as slow) make sense. Revise Statistics →

  2. A researcher records which of four therapy types each client received. Which average is appropriate? (Exam style)
    • The mean
    • The median
    • The mode
    • The standard deviation
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The mode. Therapy type is nominal, so only the most frequent category (mode) makes sense. Revise Statistics →

  3. Why is the level of measurement relevant to choosing between a t-test and a Mann–Whitney test? (Stretch)
    • Non-parametric tests need ratio data
    • Parametric tests assume at least interval data
    • The level makes no difference to test choice
    • The t-test is only for nominal data
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Parametric tests assume at least interval data. Ordinal data suit non-parametric tests; interval or ratio data allow parametric tests if other assumptions hold. Revise Statistics →

Evolutionary Psychology and Behavioural Ecology

  1. The whiteness of bones is an example of a… (Foundation recall)
    • Adaptation
    • By-product
    • Exaptation
    • Mismatch
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    By-product. Colour is a side effect of calcium's use. Revise Evolutionary Psychology and Behavioural Ecology →

  2. A preference for sugary food now contributes to obesity. This illustrates… (Exam style)
    • A just-so story
    • Evolutionary mismatch
    • Exaptation
    • Noise
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Evolutionary mismatch. An ancestral preference misfits a modern environment. Revise Evolutionary Psychology and Behavioural Ecology →

  3. What counts as evidence of special design? (Stretch)
    • A trait being common
    • A trait being recent
    • Any plausible story at all
    • Efficient, reliable problem-solving
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Efficient, reliable problem-solving. Williams set criteria of efficient, economical, reliable design. Revise Evolutionary Psychology and Behavioural Ecology →

Motivation, Emotion and Wellbeing

  1. Reading a book because you enjoy the story shows… (Foundation recall)
    • Drive reduction
    • Extrinsic motivation
    • Intrinsic motivation
    • Overjustification
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Intrinsic motivation. Enjoyment of the activity itself is intrinsic. Revise Motivation, Emotion and Wellbeing →

  2. Children promised an award for drawing later draw less in free time. This is… (Exam style)
    • Drive reduction
    • Flow
    • Positive reinforcement
    • The overjustification effect
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The overjustification effect. Expecting an award reduced the children's later free-choice interest in drawing. Revise Motivation, Emotion and Wellbeing →

  3. Why might the same reward have different effects? (Stretch)
    • It can feel controlling or informative
    • Only money matters
    • People ignore rewards
    • Rewards are always identical
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It can feel controlling or informative. Framing determines whether a reward undermines or supports motivation. Revise Motivation, Emotion and Wellbeing →

    • Ofsted
    • The EEF
    • The National Curriculum

  2. A teacher breaks a complex task into smaller steps with worked examples. This applies… (Exam style)
    • Cognitive load theory
    • Drive theory
    • Learning styles
    • Psychoanalysis
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Cognitive load theory. Reducing working memory load supports learning. Revise Applied Psychology →

  3. Why does the EEF stress implementation? (Stretch)
    • Approaches never work at all
    • Context changes the effect

    • Research is irrelevant to schools

Cultural and Cross-Cultural Psychology

  1. Focusing on objects, categories and rules rather than context is… (Foundation recall)
    • Acculturation
    • Analytic thinking
    • Collectivism
    • Holistic thinking
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Analytic thinking. Analytic thinking isolates objects from their context. Revise Cultural and Cross-Cultural Psychology →

  2. Shown an aquarium scene, a participant describes the plants, rocks and how fish interact rather than the biggest fish. This fits… (Exam style)
    • Analytic attention
    • Cultural relativism
    • Ethnocentrism
    • Holistic attention
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Holistic attention. Attending to context and relationships is holistic. Revise Cultural and Cross-Cultural Psychology →

  3. Why must cross-cultural comparisons of thinking style be interpreted cautiously? (Stretch)
    • All samples are fully representative
    • Culture never affects cognition at all
    • Effects are always extremely large
    • They can stereotype nations and hide variation
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    They can stereotype nations and hide variation. National averages hide regional, class and generational variation. Revise Cultural and Cross-Cultural Psychology →

Contemporary Psychology

  1. The probability that a study detects a real effect is its… (Foundation recall)
    • Effect size
    • Reliability coefficient
    • Reverse inference
    • Statistical power
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Statistical power. Statistical power is the chance of detecting a real effect and depends heavily on sample size. Revise Contemporary Psychology →

  2. A news story claims "scientists found the love centre" because one region lit up when people saw partners. This relies on… (Exam style)
    • A large sample
    • Brain stimulation
    • Lesion evidence
    • Reverse inference
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Reverse inference. Inferring love from activity is reverse inference. Revise Contemporary Psychology →

  3. Why has research shifted from brain "centres" to networks? (Stretch)
    • Brain networks don't exist at all
    • Each function has exactly one region
    • Functions depend on distributed regions working together
    • Imaging can't detect any regions
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Functions depend on distributed regions working together. Distributed activity better explains cognition. Revise Contemporary Psychology →
