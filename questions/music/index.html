Notation and Rhythm

  1. What are the notes on the lines of the treble clef, from the bottom? (Foundation recall)
    • A C E G
    • E G B D F
    • F A C E
    • G B D F A
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    E G B D F. Treble lines spell E G B D F from the bottom; F A C E fills the spaces between them. Revise Notation and Rhythm →

  2. A note sits in the second space from the bottom of the bass clef. What is it? (Exam style)
    • A (A2)
    • B (B2)
    • C (C3)
    • E (E3)
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    C (C3). Bass clef spaces from the bottom are A C E G, so the second space is C, an octave below middle C. Revise Notation and Rhythm →

  3. A pianist reads a chord with one note in each stave. Why is the grand stave clearer than writing both notes on one stave? (Stretch)
    • It forces both hands to play exactly the same notes an octave apart
    • It keeps notes near the five lines and makes middle C a shared reference, so both hands read with few ledger lines
    • It makes the notes in each hand sound louder and clearer when the chord is played
    • It means the pianist can ignore the bass clef entirely
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It keeps notes near the five lines and makes middle C a shared reference, so both hands read with few ledger lines. The grand stave divides the piano's range at middle C so each hand reads close to its own stave instead of across many ledger lines. Revise Notation and Rhythm →

Scales, Keys and Intervals

  1. What is the pattern of a major scale? (Foundation recall)
    • S T T T S T T
    • T S T T S T T
    • T T S T T T S
    • T T T S T T S
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    T T S T T T S. The major scale has semitones between 3–4 and 7–8, giving T T S T T T S. Revise Scales, Keys and Intervals →

  2. Which note is in E♭ major? (Exam style)
    • A
    • A♭
    • D♭
    • G♭
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A♭. E♭ major is E♭ F G A♭ B♭ C D E♭: the fourth is A♭, making G–A♭ a semitone. Revise Scales, Keys and Intervals →

  3. A student writes A major as A B C♯ D E F♯ G A. What is the error? (Stretch)
    • C♯ should be C natural
    • D should be D♯, to make the fourth a tone above the third
    • F♯ should be F natural, because A major has two sharps
    • The seventh should be G♯, so that the scale ends with a semitone G♯–A
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The seventh should be G♯, so that the scale ends with a semitone G♯–A. A major has three sharps: F♯, C♯ and G♯. Without G♯ the step 7–8 is a tone. Revise Scales, Keys and Intervals →

Chords and Harmony

  1. What interval separates the root and fifth of a triad? (Foundation recall)
    • A fifth
    • A sixth
    • A third
    • An octave
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A fifth. Two stacked thirds make a fifth from root to top note. Revise Chords and Harmony →

  2. A chord has the notes E, C and G spread across two octaves. What is its root? (Exam style)
    • C
    • E
    • G
    • It has no root
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    C. Rearranged into thirds, the notes form C–E–G, so C is the root. Revise Chords and Harmony →

  3. A student says the chord with G in the bass and C and E above has root G. What is the error? (Stretch)
    • Nothing: the lowest note is always the root
    • The bass note isn't always the root; arranged in thirds the notes are C–E–G, so the root is C
    • The chord has two roots, C and G
    • Triads have no root when spread out
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The bass note isn't always the root; arranged in thirds the notes are C–E–G, so the root is C. Inversions put the third or fifth in the bass; the root comes from the stack of thirds. Revise Chords and Harmony →

Voice Leading and Cadences

  1. In SATB short score, which way do the alto's stems point? (Foundation recall)
    • Alternately up and down
    • Down
    • It depends on the pitch
    • Up
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Down. The alto shares the treble staff with the soprano and always has stems down. Revise Voice Leading and Cadences →

  2. Which note is outside the usual tenor range of C3 to G4? (Exam style)
    • B4
    • D4
    • E3
    • G3
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    B4. B4 is above the tenor's usual top note of G4. Revise Voice Leading and Cadences →

  3. A student writes a tenor part that sits on F4 and G4 for most of an exercise. What is the problem? (Stretch)
    • Nothing: any note in range is equally comfortable
    • The tenor is too low and will be inaudible
    • The tenor should be written on the treble staff
    • The tenor stays at the top of its range, which is tiring and strained
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The tenor stays at the top of its range, which is tiring and strained. A part should mostly use the middle of its range; long stretches at the top strain the voice. Revise Voice Leading and Cadences →

Melody and Form

  1. A melody rises to a high point and then falls. What contour is this? (Foundation recall)
    • A descending line
    • A wave
    • An arch
    • An inverted arch
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    An arch. Applying the standard result: An arch rises then falls. Revise Melody and Form →

  2. A melody moves mostly by large leaps. How would you describe its motion? (Exam style)
    • Chromatic
    • Conjunct
    • Disjunct
    • Static
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Disjunct. Applying the standard result: Movement by leap is disjunct. Revise Melody and Form →

  3. A student's melody reaches its top note five times. Why might this weaken it? (Stretch)
    • High notes are never allowed in melodies
    • It means the melody has no range at all
    • It turns the melody into a bass line
    • Repeating the climax reduces its sense of arrival and direction
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Repeating the climax reduces its sense of arrival and direction. A climax is most effective when reached once. Revise Melody and Form →

Ear Training and Musicianship

  1. A waltz is felt as strong–weak–weak. What is its metre? (Foundation recall)
    • Compound duple
    • Duple
    • Quadruple
    • Triple
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Triple. Applying the standard result: Three beats per group is triple metre. Revise Ear Training and Musicianship →

  2. Each beat in a piece divides into three equal parts. What kind of time is it? (Exam style)
    • Compound time
    • Free time
    • Irregular time
    • Simple time
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Compound time. Division into three defines compound time. Revise Ear Training and Musicianship →

  3. A piece has six quavers per bar grouped 3 + 3. Which time signature fits best? (Stretch)
    • 2/4
    • 3/4
    • 4/4
    • 6/8
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    6/8. Two groups of three quavers make compound duple. Revise Ear Training and Musicianship →

Counterpoint and Texture

  1. Which repertoire is the classic example of Western monophony? (Foundation recall)
    • A Bach fugue
    • A Mozart sonata
    • A Palestrina mass
    • Gregorian chant
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Gregorian chant. Applying the standard result: Chant is a single unaccompanied line. Revise Counterpoint and Texture →

  2. A melody is accompanied by broken-chord figures in the left hand. What texture is this? (Exam style)
    • Heterophony
    • Homorhythm
    • Imitative polyphony
    • Melody-dominated homophony
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Melody-dominated homophony. Applying the standard result: A tune with distinct accompaniment. Revise Counterpoint and Texture →

  3. In which period did homophony become the dominant texture? (Stretch)
    • The Classical period
    • The early Baroque only
    • The medieval period
    • The Renaissance
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The Classical period. Clarity of melody and accompaniment defined Classical style. Revise Counterpoint and Texture →

Sound, Instruments and Orchestration

  1. What is the unit of frequency? (Foundation recall)
    • Decibel
    • Hertz
    • Metre
    • Newton
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Hertz. Frequency is measured in hertz, vibrations per second. Revise Sound, Instruments and Orchestration →

  2. Which physical property mainly determines how loud a sound seems? (Exam style)
    • Amplitude
    • Frequency
    • Speed
    • Wavelength alone
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Amplitude. Larger pressure variations carry more energy and are heard as louder. Revise Sound, Instruments and Orchestration →

  3. Why are bass loudspeakers and instruments usually large? (Stretch)
    • Large objects vibrate faster
    • Low notes have higher energy per wave
    • Low notes have long wavelengths that need large radiating surfaces or air columns
    • Low notes travel more slowly
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Low notes have long wavelengths that need large radiating surfaces or air columns. A 55 Hz note has a wavelength over 6 m, so efficient low-frequency sound needs size. Revise Sound, Instruments and Orchestration →

Music History: Medieval to Classical

  1. What is the texture of plainchant? (Foundation recall)
    • Heterophonic with instruments
    • Homophonic
    • Monophonic
    • Polyphonic
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Monophonic. Chant is a single melodic line sung without harmony. Revise Music History: Medieval to Classical →

  2. Which part of the Mass changes according to the day or feast? (Exam style)
    • The Credo
    • The Kyrie
    • The Ordinary
    • The Proper
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The Proper. Proper texts vary; Ordinary texts stay the same. Revise Music History: Medieval to Classical →

  3. What was Guido of Arezzo's main contribution? (Stretch)
    • A staff that fixed pitch and solmisation syllables for teaching
    • Equal temperament tuning
    • The first polyphonic Mass by a known composer
    • The invention of the organ
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A staff that fixed pitch and solmisation syllables for teaching. His methods made it possible to learn new chants from notation. Revise Music History: Medieval to Classical →

Music History: Romantic to the Present

  1. Which feature is typical of Romantic music? (Foundation recall)
    • Avoidance of all expression marks
    • Chromatic harmony and a wide dynamic range
    • Music for small courts only
    • Strict Baroque basso continuo throughout
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Chromatic harmony and a wide dynamic range. Romantic composers used richer harmony and greater contrasts. Revise Music History: Romantic to the Present →

  2. Why did public concerts grow in the nineteenth century? (Exam style)
    • Aristocrats banned private music
    • Churches stopped all music
    • Pianos became illegal at home
    • The growing middle class could pay to attend
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The growing middle class could pay to attend. Middle-class audiences supported public concert life. Revise Music History: Romantic to the Present →

  3. Which critic defended absolute music against programme music? (Stretch)
    • Eduard Hanslick
    • Franz Liszt
    • Hector Berlioz
    • Richard Wagner
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Eduard Hanslick. Hanslick argued music's beauty lies in its forms rather than stories. Revise Music History: Romantic to the Present →

Popular Music, Jazz and World Music

  1. In a standard twelve-bar blues, how many bars of the tonic chord open the progression? (Foundation recall)
    • Eight
    • Four
    • Six
    • Two
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Four. The first line of the blues sits on four bars of chord I before the move to IV in bar 5. Revise Popular Music, Jazz and World Music →

  2. Which notes are usually treated as blue notes? (Exam style)
    • Any note that is played louder than the beat
    • The flattened third, fifth and seventh
    • The raised fourth, sixth and second degrees
    • The tonic, the dominant and the leading note
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The flattened third, fifth and seventh. Blue notes are the flattened or bent 3rd, 5th and 7th, often pitched between major and minor. Revise Popular Music, Jazz and World Music →

  3. An early 1950s Chicago recording features amplified guitar, harmonica, bass and drums. Which style is this most likely to be? (Stretch)
    • Classic 1920s blues with a woman singer
    • New Orleans brass band music in the street
    • Solo Delta blues with an acoustic guitar
    • Urban electric blues
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Urban electric blues. Amplified small bands in Chicago after the Great Migration define urban electric blues. Revise Popular Music, Jazz and World Music →

Composition and Analysis

  1. Which analytical approach is designed mainly for atonal music? (Foundation recall)
    • Analysis of cadences
    • Pitch-class set analysis
    • Roman numeral analysis
    • Schenkerian reduction
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Pitch-class set analysis. Set theory was developed by Allen Forte and others to analyse post-tonal music. Revise Composition and Analysis →

  2. What does motivic analysis mainly investigate? (Exam style)
    • How many bars each large section contains
    • How small ideas generate a whole work
    • How the fundamental line descends to the tonic
    • Which recordings use the fastest tempo
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    How small ideas generate a whole work. Motivic analysis traces how short ideas are varied and developed across a piece. Revise Composition and Analysis →

  3. A scholar compares eight recordings of a Chopin nocturne, measuring tempo changes with software. What kind of analysis is this? (Stretch)
    • Harmonic analysis
    • Paradigmatic analysis
    • Performance analysis
    • Schenkerian analysis
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Performance analysis. Measuring and comparing recorded performances is performance analysis. Revise Composition and Analysis →
