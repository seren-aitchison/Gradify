The Business Environment

  1. Which of these is a service rather than a good? (Foundation recall)
    • A car insurance policy
    • A loaf of bread
    • A pair of trainers
    • A smartphone
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A car insurance policy. A service is intangible: insurance provides cover and peace of mind rather than a physical object, whereas bread, phones and trainers are tangible goods. Revise The Business Environment →

  2. A florist buys flowers and wrapping for £8 and sells a bouquet for £30. Which statement is correct? (Exam style)
    • The florist adds £22 of value per bouquet, from which wages, rent and profit must be paid
    • The florist adds £30 of value per bouquet
    • The florist adds no value because the flowers were grown by someone else
    • The florist makes £22 profit on each bouquet
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The florist adds £22 of value per bouquet, from which wages, rent and profit must be paid. Added value is £30 − £8 = £22. It is not profit, because the florist's labour, rent and other overheads have not yet been paid; skill and presentation are how the value is added. Revise The Business Environment →

  3. Which is the strongest reason for defining a business without reference to profit? (Stretch)
    • Charities, social enterprises and public bodies also combine resources to produce valued goods and services
    • Large companies prefer to talk about purpose rather than profit in their reports
    • Most businesses make a loss in their first year, so profit cannot define them
    • Profit is hard to measure accurately, so it is a poor basis for a definition
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Charities, social enterprises and public bodies also combine resources to produce valued goods and services. The defining activity, producing goods or services for customers using combined resources, is shared by organisations with very different goals. Losses or measurement problems don't change what a business is. Revise The Business Environment →

Management and Organisational Behaviour

  1. Which management function involves comparing actual results with targets and correcting deviations? (Foundation recall)
    • Controlling
    • Leading
    • Organising
    • Planning
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Controlling. Controlling closes the cycle: it measures performance against the plan and takes corrective action, which then feeds into new plans. Revise Management and Organisational Behaviour →

  2. A team leader on a factory line spends most of the day solving machine problems and showing staff how to set up jobs. Which skill is this manager mainly using? (Exam style)
    • Conceptual skill
    • Political skill
    • Strategic skill
    • Technical skill
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Technical skill. Knowing how the work itself is done is technical skill, which Katz found matters most at first-line level. Revise Management and Organisational Behaviour →

  3. A chief executive is praised for detailed budgets and tight cost control but staff say the firm has no sense of direction. Using Kotter's distinction, what is missing? (Stretch)
    • Administration: more record-keeping is needed
    • Leadership: setting a direction and aligning people behind it
    • Management: the firm needs more planning and control
    • Nothing: control is enough for a large firm
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Leadership: setting a direction and aligning people behind it. Kotter separates management (coping with complexity through plans and control) from leadership (coping with change by setting direction). The CEO shows strong management but weak leadership. Revise Management and Organisational Behaviour →

Strategy

  1. Which is a strategic decision? (Foundation recall)
    • Acquiring a rival company in another country
    • Changing the weekly staff rota
    • Reordering stock for one store
    • Running a two-week discount promotion
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Acquiring a rival company in another country. Acquisitions commit major resources over the long term and affect the whole organisation. Revise Strategy →

  2. A car maker decides how its marketing department will support a move into electric vehicles. Which level of strategy is this? (Exam style)
    • Business
    • Corporate
    • Functional
    • Operational
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Functional. Supporting the business strategy within one department is functional-level strategy. Revise Strategy →

  3. Two airlines both cut costs by copying each other's check-in technology. Using Porter, what is the best analysis? (Stretch)
    • Copying rivals' technology is the definition of a good strategy
    • Each airline has created a unique and defensible strategic position
    • Strategy plays no real part in the competitive airline industry
    • They are improving operational effectiveness, which rivals can copy, rather than building a distinct strategy
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    They are improving operational effectiveness, which rivals can copy, rather than building a distinct strategy. Porter argues imitable efficiency gains don't give lasting advantage; strategy needs a different set of activities and trade-offs. Revise Strategy →

Marketing

  1. Which orientation starts by identifying what customers need? (Foundation recall)
    • Marketing orientation
    • Product orientation
    • Production orientation
    • Sales orientation
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Marketing orientation. A marketing orientation puts customer needs first. Revise Marketing →

  2. A firm makes the best-engineered typewriters but sales are falling as customers move to computers. Which mistake has it made? (Exam style)
    • Excessive focus on reducing its prices
    • Marketing myopia, defining itself by the product, not the need
    • Over-reliance on customer research and feedback
    • Too broad a view of its market and customers
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Marketing myopia, defining itself by the product, not the need. The customer need is document production, which computers now meet better. Revise Marketing →

  3. A fast-food chain adds healthier options and reduces packaging waste while remaining profitable. Which approach does this reflect? (Stretch)
    • Product orientation, focusing on technical features
    • Production orientation, focusing on cheap output
    • Sales orientation, pushing existing products harder
    • Societal marketing, balancing customers, profit and society
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Societal marketing, balancing customers, profit and society. Societal marketing considers long-term wellbeing alongside needs and profit. Revise Marketing →

Operations and Supply Chain Management

  1. Which performance objective means delivering on time as promised? (Foundation recall)
    • Dependability
    • Flexibility
    • Quality
    • Speed
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Dependability. Dependability is about keeping delivery promises. Revise Operations and Supply Chain Management →

  2. A budget airline's main operations priority is most likely to be which? (Exam style)
    • Bespoke service to each passenger
    • Cost
    • Flexibility of route at short notice
    • Luxury quality of cabins
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Cost. Low fares depend on tightly controlled costs. Revise Operations and Supply Chain Management →

  3. How can improving quality reduce an operation's costs? (Stretch)
    • Better quality increases inventory
    • Fewer errors mean less rework, waste and complaint handling
    • Higher quality always means higher costs
    • Quality and cost are unrelated
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Fewer errors mean less rework, waste and complaint handling. Internal quality improvements cut failure costs. Revise Operations and Supply Chain Management →

People Management

  1. Which of Ulrich's roles focuses on representing employees' concerns? (Foundation recall)
    • Administrative expert
    • Change agent
    • Employee champion
    • Strategic partner
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Employee champion. The employee champion builds commitment and voice. Revise People Management →

  2. In the Harvard model, which outcome refers to employees' skills? (Exam style)
    • Commitment
    • Competence
    • Congruence
    • Cost-effectiveness
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Competence. Applying the standard result: Competence is one of the four Cs. Revise People Management →

  3. An HR department spends all its time on payroll and contracts. What does Ulrich's model suggest is missing? (Stretch)
    • Fewer employee surveys
    • More administrative tasks
    • Removal of the HR function altogether
    • Strategic and change roles linking HR to business goals
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Strategic and change roles linking HR to business goals. Ulrich argued HR should add strategic value beyond administration. Revise People Management →

Finance for Managers

  1. Which user group is most interested in whether loans will be repaid? (Foundation recall)
    • Competitors
    • Customers
    • Employees
    • Lenders
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Lenders. Applying the standard result: Lenders assess repayment ability. Revise Finance for Managers →

  2. How does management accounting differ from financial accounting? (Exam style)
    • It is always audited by external auditors
    • It is internal and forward-looking with no set format
    • It is published for shareholders in a legal format
    • It reports only on past tax payments
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It is internal and forward-looking with no set format. Management accounting serves internal decisions. Revise Finance for Managers →

  3. A fast-growing firm reports record profits but cannot pay its suppliers. What is the most likely cause? (Stretch)
    • Cash tied up in stock and slow-paying customers
    • It has too much cash invested in a bank
    • Profits are always equal to the cash received
    • The accounts must contain a deliberate error
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Cash tied up in stock and slow-paying customers. Applying the standard result: Growth can absorb cash despite profit. Revise Finance for Managers →

Economics for Business

  1. What happens to quantity demanded when price rises, other things equal? (Foundation recall)
    • It doubles
    • It falls
    • It rises
    • It stays the same
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It falls. Applying the standard result: This is the law of demand. Revise Economics for Business →

  2. Incomes rise and demand for a good falls. What kind of good is it? (Exam style)
    • A complementary good
    • A luxury good
    • A normal good
    • An inferior good
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    An inferior good. Applying the standard result: Demand falls as income rises. Revise Economics for Business →

  3. The price of coffee rises sharply. What happens to the demand for tea? (Stretch)
    • It shifts left, as tea is a complement
    • It shifts right, as tea is a substitute
    • Nothing, as the goods are unrelated
    • There is a movement along the tea curve
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It shifts right, as tea is a substitute. Applying the standard result: Buyers switch to the substitute. Revise Economics for Business →

Entrepreneurship and Innovation

  1. What is intrapreneurship? (Foundation recall)
    • Buying an existing franchise
    • Entrepreneurial behaviour inside an established organisation
    • Managing a charity shop
    • Running a business from home
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Entrepreneurial behaviour inside an established organisation. Intrapreneurs innovate within large firms rather than starting new ones. Revise Entrepreneurship and Innovation →

  2. Which example best fits Schumpeter's idea of the entrepreneur? (Exam style)
    • A family running a long-established bakery
    • A firm introducing streaming that displaces DVD rental
    • A manager cutting costs by 2%
    • A shop matching a rival's prices
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A firm introducing streaming that displaces DVD rental. Schumpeter's entrepreneur introduces new combinations that destroy old industries. Revise Entrepreneurship and Innovation →

  3. Which statement about new firms is best supported by evidence? (Stretch)
    • A small share of fast-growing firms creates many new jobs
    • All new firms are innovative
    • Most new firms grow into large companies
    • New firms rarely create any jobs
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A small share of fast-growing firms creates many new jobs. Job creation is concentrated in a minority of high-growth firms. Revise Entrepreneurship and Innovation →

Digital Business and Information Systems

  1. Which system records day-to-day sales and payroll? (Foundation recall)
    • A decision support system
    • A transaction processing system
    • An executive information system
    • An expert recommendation system
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A transaction processing system. Transaction processing systems capture routine transactions as they happen. Revise Digital Business and Information Systems →

  2. A manager tests how a 5% price rise would affect profit. Which system suits this best? (Exam style)
    • A decision support system
    • A payroll processing system
    • A transaction processing system
    • An office email system
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A decision support system. Decision support systems allow what-if modelling of semi-structured problems. Revise Digital Business and Information Systems →

  3. Why do some expensive information systems fail to add value? (Stretch)
    • Data are always too accurate to be useful
    • Hardware is the only part that matters
    • Processes and staff do not change to use them well
    • Software can never improve decision-making
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Processes and staff do not change to use them well. Systems create value only when combined with redesigned processes and trained people. Revise Digital Business and Information Systems →

Ethics, ESG and Corporate Governance

  1. Which ethical theory judges an action mainly by its consequences? (Foundation recall)
    • Deontology
    • Rights theory
    • Utilitarianism
    • Virtue ethics
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Utilitarianism. Utilitarianism is a form of consequentialism that weighs outcomes for everyone affected. Revise Ethics, ESG and Corporate Governance →

  2. A manager refuses to mislead a customer even though a small lie would close a profitable sale. Which theory best explains this? (Exam style)
    • Deontology, because lying breaks a duty
    • Egoism, because it protects the manager
    • Shareholder theory, because it maximises value
    • Utilitarianism, because profit helps many
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Deontology, because lying breaks a duty. Deontology holds that some acts, such as lying, are wrong regardless of their outcome. Revise Ethics, ESG and Corporate Governance →

  3. What is the most common criticism of using utilitarianism alone to make business decisions? (Stretch)
    • It can justify harming a minority if the majority gains
    • It cannot be applied to decisions involving money
    • It ignores outcomes and looks only at rules
    • It requires managers to have perfect characters
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It can justify harming a minority if the majority gains. Adding up total benefit can override the rights of the few who bear the costs. Revise Ethics, ESG and Corporate Governance →
