Research and the Dissertation practice questions

The Research Landscape and Research Degrees

  1. Which element is part of the REF definition of research? (Foundation recall)
    • The new insights must be effectively shared
    • The work must be carried out in a university
    • The work must be funded by a research council
    • The work must use statistics
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The new insights must be effectively shared. The REF definition has three parts: investigation, new insights and effective sharing. Funding source, method type and setting are not part of it. Revise The Research Landscape and Research Degrees →

  2. A team studies how a protein folds, hoping the knowledge will eventually help design drugs. Which category fits best? (Exam style)
    • Consultancy
    • Purely applied research
    • Scholarship of teaching
    • Use-inspired basic research (Pasteur's quadrant)
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Use-inspired basic research (Pasteur's quadrant). The work seeks fundamental understanding but is motivated by a possible use, which is exactly Stokes's Pasteur's quadrant. Revise The Research Landscape and Research Degrees →

  3. A Master's student compiles every published study on remote working into an annotated list, without a stated search method or synthesis. A supervisor says it is "not yet research". Which reason is strongest? (Stretch)
    • It contains no primary data, and only primary data count as research
    • It gathers existing knowledge without a systematic, reproducible method or a new synthesis
    • It has not yet been examined, and only examined work is research
    • It is about a business topic rather than a science
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It gathers existing knowledge without a systematic, reproducible method or a new synthesis. Secondary work can be research (systematic reviews are), but only when a stated method produces a new insight. Primary data are not required, and topic or examination status is irrelevant. Research Questions and the Literature Review

  1. Which is the best source for spotting open questions in a field? (Foundation recall)
    • Newspaper editorials only
    • Personal opinion alone
    • Recent reviews and "future research" sections
    • Textbooks from twenty years ago
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Recent reviews and "future research" sections. Applying the standard result: Reviews map what is unresolved. Revise Research Questions and the Literature Review →

  2. A student proposes "education in the UK" as a PhD topic. What is the main problem? (Exam style)
    • It cannot be ethically approved
    • It is far too broad to investigate in depth
    • It is too original to be accepted
    • It lacks any significance
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It is far too broad to investigate in depth. Applying the standard result: Narrowing is needed. Revise Research Questions and the Literature Review →

  3. A topic is interesting and significant but needs archives the student cannot access. Which criterion fails? (Stretch)
    • Feasibility
    • Interest
    • Originality
    • Significance
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Feasibility. Applying the standard result: Access is a feasibility issue. Revise Research Questions and the Literature Review →

Philosophy of Research

    • What is the nature of social reality?
    • When is the deadline?
    • Which software should I use?

  2. A researcher believes organisations exist independently of the people in them. Which position is this? (Exam style)
    • Constructionism
    • Interpretivism
    • Objectivism
    • Relativism

    • It makes their research more transparent and coherent
    • It removes the need for methods

Research Design

  1. Which of these is a research design rather than a research method? (Foundation recall)
    • A focus group topic guide
    • A longitudinal panel
    • A semi-structured interview
    • An online questionnaire item
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A longitudinal panel. Longitudinal is a structure for timing data collection; the other options are data collection tools. Revise Research Design →

  2. A researcher wants to compare how two hospitals implemented the same policy. Which design family fits best? (Exam style)
    • Comparative
    • Cross-sectional survey of patients nationwide
    • Experimental
    • Single ethnography
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Comparative. Two contrasting cases studied with similar methods is the defining feature of a comparative design. Revise Research Design →

  3. A student plans a study but cannot say what comparison their data will allow. What is the main weakness? (Stretch)
    • The design cannot support the claims intended
    • The ethics form will be rejected automatically
    • The method is too old-fashioned for a thesis
    • The sample is necessarily too small to publish
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The design cannot support the claims intended. Without a planned comparison, evidence cannot discriminate between rival answers to the question. Revise Research Design →

Quantitative Methods and Statistics

  1. Which level of measurement has ordered categories but unknown gaps between them? (Foundation recall)
    • Interval
    • Nominal
    • Ordinal
    • Ratio
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Ordinal. Ordinal variables can be ranked, but the distances between ranks are not known to be equal. Revise Quantitative Methods and Statistics →

  2. A study links coffee drinking with heart disease, but smokers drink more coffee. What is smoking here? (Exam style)
    • A confounding variable
    • A dependent variable
    • A mediating variable
    • A nominal outcome
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A confounding variable. Smoking relates to both coffee and heart disease and may create a misleading association. Revise Quantitative Methods and Statistics →

  3. Which summary is most appropriate for degree classification (First, 2:1, 2:2, Third)? (Stretch)
    • The coefficient of variation
    • The mean classification code
    • The median classification
    • The standard deviation of codes
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The median classification. Degree classes are ordered but not equally spaced, so the median suits this ordinal variable. Revise Quantitative Methods and Statistics →

Qualitative Methods

  1. Which aim is most typical of qualitative research? (Foundation recall)
    • Estimating the prevalence of a condition
    • Measuring change with standardised scales
    • Testing a causal hypothesis by random allocation
    • Understanding how people make sense of experiences
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Understanding how people make sense of experiences. Qualitative research focuses on meanings and interpretation in context. Revise Qualitative Methods →

  2. Which research question is best suited to a qualitative approach? (Exam style)
    • Does a new drug reduce blood pressure more than placebo?
    • How do carers experience hospital discharge planning?
    • Is age correlated with length of hospital stay?
    • What percentage of patients are readmitted in 30 days?
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    How do carers experience hospital discharge planning?. A question about experience calls for rich accounts rather than measurement. Revise Qualitative Methods →

  3. A critic says a 15-interview study "cannot be generalised, so it is worthless". What is the best response? (Stretch)
    • Interviews are always more valid than surveys
    • It is generalisable because 15 is a large number
    • It offers conceptual insight and transferable understanding
    • Qualitative research never needs justification
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It offers conceptual insight and transferable understanding. Qualitative studies generalise to concepts and theory, and thick description lets readers judge transfer. Revise Qualitative Methods →

Mixed Methods and Evidence Synthesis

  1. Which purpose for mixing seeks convergence of results from different methods? (Foundation recall)
    • Development
    • Expansion
    • Initiation
    • Triangulation
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Triangulation. Triangulation compares results from different methods to see whether they agree. Revise Mixed Methods and Evidence Synthesis →

  2. Survey and interview findings unexpectedly contradict each other, leading to a new research question. Which of Greene's purposes does this reflect? (Exam style)
    • Complementarity
    • Development
    • Initiation
    • Triangulation
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Initiation. Initiation uses paradox and contradiction between methods to open new lines of inquiry. Revise Mixed Methods and Evidence Synthesis →

  3. A study uses interviews and participant observation and calls itself mixed methods. What is the most accurate judgement? (Stretch)
    • It is a convergent mixed methods design
    • It is a quantitative experimental design
    • It is mixed methods because two methods are used
    • It is multimethod qualitative research
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It is multimethod qualitative research. Both methods are qualitative, so it is multimethod rather than mixed methods. Revise Mixed Methods and Evidence Synthesis →

Research Ethics and Integrity

  1. Which principle is mainly protected by informed consent? (Foundation recall)
    • Justice in sampling
    • Research impact
    • Respect for persons
    • Statistical power
    • Value for money
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Respect for persons. Consent respects people's autonomy to decide whether to take part. Revise Research Ethics and Integrity →

  2. Which framework sets ethics principles for UKRI-funded social science research? (Exam style)
    • The ESRC Framework for Research Ethics
    • The FAIR principles
    • The Research Excellence Framework
    • The UK Professional Standards Framework
    • The Vitae Researcher Development Framework
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The ESRC Framework for Research Ethics. The ESRC framework sets expectations for ethical social science research. Revise Research Ethics and Integrity →

  3. A study's benefits are high, but it carries some risk of distress. What is the best approach? (Stretch)
    • Abandon all research that has any risk
    • Ask participants not to report distress
    • Hide the risk from the ethics committee
    • Ignore the risk because benefits are high
    • Reduce the risk with safeguards and justify the balance
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Reduce the risk with safeguards and justify the balance. Researchers minimise risks and justify the balance of risk and benefit. Revise Research Ethics and Integrity →

    • A library opening times notice
    • A student union newsletter

    • Interview transcripts from a study

    • Reusing others' data

Academic Writing and the Thesis

  1. Which sentence shows appropriate hedging? (Foundation recall)
    • Everyone knows sleep matters
    • It is obvious that sleep is everything
    • The data show something about sleep
    • The findings prove beyond doubt that sleep causes memory
    • The findings suggest a link between sleep and memory
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The findings suggest a link between sleep and memory. Hedging reflects the real strength of the evidence. Revise Academic Writing and the Thesis →

  2. Which change most improves clarity? (Exam style)
    • Adding more technical jargon
    • Adding vague words such as "things"
    • Removing all evidence
    • Splitting a long sentence into two shorter ones
    • Using more passive constructions
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Splitting a long sentence into two shorter ones. Shorter sentences with one idea each are easier to follow. Revise Academic Writing and the Thesis →

  3. A draft says "This proves the theory is correct in all cases". What is the main problem? (Stretch)
    • It is too cautious
    • It is too short to be useful
    • It is written in the first person
    • It overclaims beyond what evidence can show
    • It uses too many citations
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It overclaims beyond what evidence can show. Single studies rarely prove theories in all cases. Revise Academic Writing and the Thesis →

Publishing, Presenting and Impact

  1. Which output is shared before peer review? (Foundation recall)
    • A peer-reviewed article
    • A preprint
    • A published book
    • A thesis after the viva
    • An accepted conference paper
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A preprint. Preprints are posted before formal review. Revise Publishing, Presenting and Impact →

  2. What usually happens after a paper is submitted to a journal? (Exam style)
    • It is automatically rejected
    • It is printed in a newspaper
    • It is published immediately
    • It is sent to the author's supervisor
    • The editor screens it for scope and quality
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The editor screens it for scope and quality. Editors decide whether to send papers for review. Revise Publishing, Presenting and Impact →

  3. Why has the academic publishing system been criticised? (Stretch)
    • High costs, slow timelines and incentives favouring positive results
    • It is far too fast and costs authors nothing
    • It is run entirely by governments
    • It never involves experts in the field at all
    • It publishes every paper without any review

    High costs, slow timelines and incentives favouring positive results. Critics highlight cost, speed and publication bias. Revise Publishing, Presenting and Impact →

The Viva and Beyond

  1. What should students agree with supervisors before submitting? (Foundation recall)
    • A realistic submission date with time for feedback
    • The examiners' questions
    • The external examiner's fee
    • The final mark
    • The viva outcome
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A realistic submission date with time for feedback. Agreeing a plan allows full-draft feedback and revisions. Revise The Viva and Beyond →

  2. Which is a final check before submission? (Exam style)
    • Figures need not be numbered
    • Formatting follows university regulations
    • References are optional
    • The abstract can be left out
    • The thesis is as long as possible
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Formatting follows university regulations. Regulations set formatting requirements that must be met. Revise The Viva and Beyond →

  3. Why might a student request an embargo on her deposited thesis? (Stretch)
    • To allow publication of papers first or protect confidential material
    • To avoid the viva
    • To change her results later

    To allow publication of papers first or protect confidential material. Embargoes delay public access for valid reasons. Revise The Viva and Beyond →
