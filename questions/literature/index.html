English Literature practice questions

Foundations of Literary Study

  1. Which critic argued that art "defamiliarises" familiar things so that we perceive them afresh? (Foundation recall)
    • Matthew Arnold
    • Raymond Williams
    • Samuel Johnson
    • Viktor Shklovsky
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Viktor Shklovsky. Shklovsky's essay "Art as Technique" (1917) is the classic statement of defamiliarisation, a key idea in Russian Formalism. Revise Foundations of Literary Study →

  2. A student says a cereal advert that uses rhyme and alliteration must be literature. Which reply is best? (Exam style)
    • Adverts cannot use literary techniques, so the claim is wrongly described
    • Only texts written before 1900 can be literature
    • Patterned language is one feature of literature but not enough on its own to make a text literary
    • The advert is literature because any rhymed text counts as poetry
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Patterned language is one feature of literature but not enough on its own to make a text literary. The Formalist test identifies literariness, but literature is also defined by purpose and value; many patterned texts are not literature. Revise Foundations of Literary Study →

  3. Which is the strongest objection to defining literature purely as "writing a culture values"? (Stretch)
    • It cannot explain why some texts reward close reading more than others, so it may make value seem arbitrary
    • It excludes all poetry from the definition, since poems are rarely valued by a culture
    • It is wrong because cultures never change their minds about which texts they value
    • It was invented by the Russian Formalists, who rejected it in favour of defamiliarisation
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It cannot explain why some texts reward close reading more than others, so it may make value seem arbitrary. A purely institutional definition captures how canons are formed, but critics argue it leaves out the qualities of texts that make readers return to them. Revise Foundations of Literary Study →

Poetry and Poetics

  1. Which poet described poetry as "emotion recollected in tranquillity"? (Foundation recall)
    • Blake
    • Coleridge
    • Sidney
    • Wordsworth
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Wordsworth. The phrase comes from Wordsworth's Preface to Lyrical Ballads, in the 1802 version. Revise Poetry and Poetics →

  2. In "London", what does repeating "charter'd" across two lines most clearly suggest? (Exam style)
    • That Blake made an error in revision
    • That even the river is controlled by commerce and law
    • That the speaker likes old maps of London
    • That the Thames flows freely past the city
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    That even the river is controlled by commerce and law. Chartered means legally granted or owned; applying it to the river as well as the streets shows control reaching into nature. Revise Poetry and Poetics →

  3. A student says a prose poem proves line breaks don't matter in poetry. What is the best response? (Stretch)
    • All poems should really be read as if they were prose paragraphs
    • Line breaks matter only in poems that also rhyme regularly
    • Prose poems are simply prose and can safely be ignored by critics
    • Prose poems show the line isn't essential, but in lineated poems the line remains a key source of meaning
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Prose poems show the line isn't essential, but in lineated poems the line remains a key source of meaning. The prose poem complicates any single definition, but that doesn't make the line irrelevant where poets use it. Revise Poetry and Poetics →

Drama and Shakespeare

  1. Which feature most distinguishes drama from prose fiction? (Foundation recall)
    • It always includes a narrator who explains the action
    • It is always shorter than a novel or a poem
    • It is always written in rhyming verse throughout
    • It is written to be performed before an audience
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It is written to be performed before an audience. Drama's meaning is completed in performance, usually without a narrator. Revise Drama and Shakespeare →

  2. Two productions of the same play present one character as comic and as tragic, without changing the text. What does this show? (Exam style)
    • One production must be wrong
    • Performance choices shape meaning beyond the words
    • Plays have no fixed words
    • The text must have been altered secretly
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Performance choices shape meaning beyond the words. Delivery, casting and staging can produce different interpretations of the same lines. Revise Drama and Shakespeare →

  3. A student writes, "The reader feels tension as Macbeth looks at his hands." What is the best improvement? (Stretch)
    • Discuss the audience and how staging, pauses and sound effects create tension
    • Remove any reference to tension, since plays can't create it
    • Replace the word "reader" with "narrator" throughout the essay
    • Summarise the plot of the whole play instead of one moment
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Discuss the audience and how staging, pauses and sound effects create tension. Drama analysis should consider the audience and theatrical effects, not just the reader. Revise Drama and Shakespeare →

Prose Fiction and the Novel

  1. Which term describes the order in which a text presents its events? (Foundation recall)
    • Fabula
    • Genre
    • Syuzhet
    • Theme
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Syuzhet. Syuzhet is the formalist term for the presented arrangement of events. Revise Prose Fiction and the Novel →

  2. In Forster's distinction, what turns a story into a plot? (Exam style)
    • A first-person narrator
    • A happy ending
    • A larger number of events
    • Causal connections between events
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Causal connections between events. Plot adds "why" to the sequence of "and then". Revise Prose Fiction and the Novel →

  3. A novel opens with a murder and then moves back to show the events leading to it. What effect does this arrangement most likely create? (Stretch)
    • A plot in which no event is causally linked to any other
    • A strictly chronological story told without any rearrangement
    • Complete loss of interest, because the ending is already known
    • Suspense about how and why, rather than what, happened
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Suspense about how and why, rather than what, happened. Revealing the outcome first shifts interest to causes and motives. Revise Prose Fiction and the Novel →

Medieval to Eighteenth-Century Literature

  1. What links the two halves of an Old English verse line? (Foundation recall)
    • A fixed number of syllables
    • A refrain
    • Alliteration on stressed syllables
    • End rhyme
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Alliteration on stressed syllables. Old English verse uses alliteration rather than rhyme. Revise Medieval to Eighteenth-Century Literature →

  2. What does the kenning "whale-road" refer to? (Exam style)
    • A battlefield
    • A river
    • A ship
    • The sea
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The sea. The compound describes the sea as the road of whales. Revise Medieval to Eighteenth-Century Literature →

  3. How does The Dream of the Rood combine heroic and Christian values? (Stretch)
    • It describes a pagan god fighting a dragon
    • It ignores the Crucifixion entirely
    • It presents Christ as a young warrior who eagerly mounts the cross
    • It rejects all heroic imagery in favour of humility
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It presents Christ as a young warrior who eagerly mounts the cross. The poem uses the language of heroic loyalty to describe Christ and the cross. Revise Medieval to Eighteenth-Century Literature →

Romantic to Contemporary Literature

  1. Which idea is central to Romanticism? (Foundation recall)
    • Satire of fashionable society
    • Scientific detachment from feeling
    • Strict obedience to classical rules
    • The power of imagination
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The power of imagination. Applying the standard result: Romantics prized creative imagination. Revise Romantic to Contemporary Literature →

  2. What does Blake suggest with "mind-forg'd manacles"? (Exam style)
    • Factories in London made iron manacles for prisons
    • Londoners were forced to wear iron chains by law
    • People are imprisoned by ways of thinking imposed by institutions
    • The speaker works as a blacksmith in the city
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    People are imprisoned by ways of thinking imposed by institutions. The image turns physical chains into mental oppression. Revise Romantic to Contemporary Literature →

  3. Why did many Romantics respond to the Industrial Revolution with images of nature? (Stretch)
    • Industry did not change the landscape in any way
    • Nature was irrelevant to Romantic poets' concerns
    • They admired factories above all other subjects
    • They saw nature as a source of renewal threatened by industry and cities
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    They saw nature as a source of renewal threatened by industry and cities. Nature offered a counterpoint to industrial change. Revise Romantic to Contemporary Literature →

Literary Theory and Criticism

  1. What does literary theory mainly examine? (Foundation recall)
    • The assumptions behind ways of reading
    • The biographies of famous authors
    • The rules of English grammar
    • The sales figures of best-selling novels
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The assumptions behind ways of reading. Applying the standard result: Theory reflects on how we read. Revise Literary Theory and Criticism →

  2. Which approach focuses mainly on the reader's role in making meaning? (Exam style)
    • Marxist criticism
    • Narratology
    • New Criticism
    • Reader-response theory
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Reader-response theory. Applying the standard result: Reader-response centres the reader. Revise Literary Theory and Criticism →

  3. A student says, "I just read the text; I don't use theory." What would Eagleton reply? (Stretch)
    • Every reading uses theory, even if unexamined
    • Only professors are allowed to use theory
    • Reading without theory is always more accurate
    • Theory is only needed for poetry
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Every reading uses theory, even if unexamined. Applying the standard result: Assumptions are always present. Revise Literary Theory and Criticism →

Set Texts: Drama and Nineteenth-Century Prose

  1. Which king was Shakespeare's patron when Macbeth was written? (Foundation recall)
    • Charles I
    • Elizabeth I
    • Henry VIII
    • James I
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    James I. James became patron of the King's Men after succeeding to the English throne in 1603. Revise Set Texts: Drama and Nineteenth-Century Prose →

  2. How does the play show disorder in nature after Duncan's murder? (Exam style)
    • Darkness covers the day and Duncan's horses eat each other
    • Macbeth becomes a better ruler
    • Scotland enjoys a long peace
    • The witches disappear for ever
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Darkness covers the day and Duncan's horses eat each other. Unnatural events show the Great Chain of Being disrupted by regicide. Revise Set Texts: Drama and Nineteenth-Century Prose →

  3. Which change to Holinshed best reflects Shakespeare's audience? (Stretch)
    • Giving Macbeth a wife
    • Including witches at the start
    • Making Banquo innocent of Duncan's murder
    • Setting the play in Scotland
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Making Banquo innocent of Duncan's murder. In Holinshed Banquo is an accomplice; James I's ancestor had to be innocent. Revise Set Texts: Drama and Nineteenth-Century Prose →

Set Texts: Romantic, Victorian and War Poetry

  1. When were Songs of Innocence and of Experience first published together? (Foundation recall)
    • 1794
    • 1798
    • 1819
    • 1850
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    1794. Innocence appeared in 1789, and the combined collection in 1794. Revise Set Texts: Romantic, Victorian and War Poetry →

  2. What does the repetition of "charter'd" in "London" suggest? (Exam style)
    • London is a peaceful rural place
    • The city and even the river are owned and controlled
    • The speaker loves the city's freedom
    • The Thames flows freely into the sea
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The city and even the river are owned and controlled. Charters granted ownership and rights, implying control by commerce. Revise Set Texts: Romantic, Victorian and War Poetry →

  3. Why are Blake's Songs written in simple, childlike forms? (Stretch)
    • Blake could not write in more complex forms
    • Simple forms were required by his publisher
    • The simplicity heightens the shock of their darker social and spiritual meanings
    • They were intended only for very young children
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The simplicity heightens the shock of their darker social and spiritual meanings. The contrast between form and content sharpens the critique. Revise Set Texts: Romantic, Victorian and War Poetry →

Unseen, Comparative and Essay Writing

  1. What is the first step in reading an unseen poem? (Foundation recall)
    • Count the syllables in each line
    • Look up the poet's biography
    • Read it through for overall sense
    • Write the conclusion first
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Read it through for overall sense. An initial reading gives the overall meaning. Revise Unseen, Comparative and Essay Writing →

  2. Why should an unseen answer be organised by ideas rather than line by line? (Exam style)
    • It avoids using quotations
    • It builds an argument that answers the question
    • It makes the answer shorter
    • It means the ending can be ignored
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It builds an argument that answers the question. Thematic organisation shows analysis rather than paraphrase. Revise Unseen, Comparative and Essay Writing →

  3. A poem's last line is much shorter than the rest. What might a strong response say? (Stretch)
    • Line length is never worth discussing
    • The broken pattern emphasises a final change or loss
    • The poet ran out of ideas
    • The printer made an error
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The broken pattern emphasises a final change or loss. Departures from a pattern create emphasis and meaning. Revise Unseen, Comparative and Essay Writing →
