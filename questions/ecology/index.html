Ecology and Environmental Science practice questions

33 questions from Gradify's free Ecology and Environmental Science course, with the answer and why behind each reveal. Answer before you open it: that's what makes it stick.

Foundations of Ecology

  1. Which of these is a biotic factor affecting a population of rabbits? (Foundation recall)
    • The average winter temperature
    • The number of foxes in the area
    • The pH of the soil
    • The slope of the hillside
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The number of foxes in the area. Foxes are living organisms (predators), so they are a biotic factor; the others are physical or chemical conditions. Revise Foundations of Ecology →

  2. A campaign leaflet says "ecology tells us we must ban all new building on farmland". What is the best response? (Exam style)
    • Ecology can predict effects on wildlife, but whether to ban building is a value judgement
    • Ecology has nothing to say about land use
    • Ecology proves that all new building harms the environment
    • The leaflet is right, because ecology is the same as environmentalism
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Ecology can predict effects on wildlife, but whether to ban building is a value judgement. Ecology provides evidence about consequences; deciding what to do also involves values, costs and other interests. Revise Foundations of Ecology →

  3. Why is the red squirrel's decline in Britain a good example of a typical ecological explanation? (Stretch)
    • A single abiotic factor, climate, explains it fully
    • It proves that introduced species always replace native ones
    • It shows that biotic factors never matter when habitat is suitable
    • Several interacting causes, competition, disease and history, combine to explain it
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Several interacting causes, competition, disease and history, combine to explain it. Ecological patterns usually arise from several interacting biotic and abiotic causes; single-factor explanations are rarely enough. Revise Foundations of Ecology →

Population Ecology

  1. Which is a population? (Foundation recall)
    • All the animals in a woodland
    • All the birds of every species in a city park
    • All the organisms in a pond
    • All the red squirrels on the Isle of Wight
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    All the red squirrels on the Isle of Wight. A population is a single species in a defined area; the others include several species. Revise Population Ecology →

  2. Salmon from one river return to breed there and rarely stray to other rivers. How should they be treated? (Exam style)
    • As a community
    • As a separate population from salmon in other rivers
    • As a separate species
    • As part of one global salmon population with no structure
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    As a separate population from salmon in other rivers. Fidelity to the home river limits gene flow, so each river's salmon form a distinct population. Revise Population Ecology →

  3. An ecologist counts a grass that spreads by underground runners. What is the main difficulty in measuring population size? (Stretch)
    • Deciding what counts as an individual, since many shoots may be one genetic clone
    • Grass can't be counted because it moves
    • Grasses don't belong to species
    • Grasses have no birth or death rates
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Deciding what counts as an individual, since many shoots may be one genetic clone. In clonal plants, many shoots (ramets) can belong to one genetic individual (genet), so population size depends on the unit chosen. Revise Population Ecology →

Communities and Species Interactions

  1. Which is an example of exploitation competition? (Foundation recall)
    • A bird defending its territory from a rival
    • A plant releasing chemicals that stop seeds germinating nearby
    • Two deer fighting over a mate
    • Two plant species taking up the same limited soil nitrogen
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Two plant species taking up the same limited soil nitrogen. Exploitation is indirect, through using a shared resource; the others involve direct interference. Revise Communities and Species Interactions →

  2. Why is intraspecific competition often more intense than interspecific competition? (Exam style)
    • Individuals of different species never share resources
    • Individuals of the same species need exactly the same resources
    • Intraspecific competition only affects animals, not plants
    • It occurs only at very low population densities
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Individuals of the same species need exactly the same resources. Members of the same species have identical requirements, so overlap is complete. Revise Communities and Species Interactions →

  3. Two species of beetle feed on the same leaves, but the leaves are always abundant. Are they competing? (Stretch)
    • No, because insects never compete with each other
    • Not necessarily: competition needs the resource to be limiting
    • Yes, because beetles always compete for food
    • Yes: any overlap in diet proves competition
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Not necessarily: competition needs the resource to be limiting. Overlap without a limiting resource doesn't reduce either species' fitness. Revise Communities and Species Interactions →

Ecosystems and Energy Flow

  1. Which is an abiotic component of an ecosystem? (Foundation recall)
    • A population of rabbits
    • Algae on rocks
    • Decomposing fungi
    • Soil temperature
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Soil temperature. Abiotic factors are non-living, physical or chemical conditions. Revise Ecosystems and Energy Flow →

  2. What ultimately happens to the energy that enters an ecosystem as sunlight? (Exam style)
    • It is lost as heat after passing through organisms
    • It is recycled endlessly between organisms
    • It is stored permanently in the soil
    • It returns to the Sun as reflected light
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It is lost as heat after passing through organisms. Energy flows one way; each transfer releases heat. Revise Ecosystems and Energy Flow →

  3. An ecologist studying a lake includes its whole catchment. Why might this be sensible? (Stretch)
    • Catchments contain no living organisms
    • It removes all exchange with the surroundings
    • Lakes are closed systems with no exchanges
    • Water and nutrients from the catchment strongly affect the lake
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Water and nutrients from the catchment strongly affect the lake. Boundaries are often drawn where flows are weakest, such as a watershed. Revise Ecosystems and Energy Flow →

Biogeochemical Cycles

  1. Which of these is cycled rather than flowing one way through ecosystems? (Foundation recall)
    • Heat energy
    • Kinetic energy of wind
    • Light energy
    • Nitrogen atoms
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Nitrogen atoms. Matter such as nitrogen is reused; energy is lost as heat. Revise Biogeochemical Cycles →

  2. A reservoir gains more of an element each year than it loses. What is it called? (Exam style)
    • A flux
    • A residence time
    • A sink
    • A source
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A sink. Applying the standard result: A sink accumulates material over time. Revise Biogeochemical Cycles →

  3. Why will carbon dioxide from fossil fuels affect climate for centuries? (Stretch)
    • Carbon dioxide breaks down within days in the atmosphere
    • Fossil carbon is destroyed when it is burnt
    • Parts of the carbon cycle that remove it have long residence times and slow fluxes
    • Plants absorb all extra carbon dioxide within a year
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Parts of the carbon cycle that remove it have long residence times and slow fluxes. Removal into the deep ocean and rocks is slow, so excess persists. Revise Biogeochemical Cycles →

Biodiversity and Evolution

  1. Who popularised the term biodiversity? (Foundation recall)
    • Arthur Tansley
    • Carl Linnaeus
    • Charles Darwin
    • E. O. Wilson
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    E. O. Wilson. Wilson edited the 1988 book BioDiversity. Revise Biodiversity and Evolution →

  2. Roughly how many species have been formally described? (Exam style)
    • About 100 million
    • About 2 million
    • About 20,000
    • About 200
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    About 2 million. Applying the standard result: Around 2 million species are described. Revise Biodiversity and Evolution →

  3. Why are estimates of total species numbers uncertain? (Stretch)
    • All species on Earth have already been described and named
    • Many groups, especially insects, microbes and deep-sea life, are poorly sampled
    • Scientists agree on an exact and fixed total figure
    • Species cannot be counted by any scientific method
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Many groups, especially insects, microbes and deep-sea life, are poorly sampled. Undersampled groups make extrapolation difficult. Revise Biodiversity and Evolution →

Conservation Biology

  1. When did conservation biology emerge as a distinct field? (Foundation recall)
    • In the 1880s
    • In the 1930s
    • In the 1980s
    • In the 2010s
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    In the 1980s. The Society for Conservation Biology was founded in 1985. Revise Conservation Biology →

  2. Why must conservation biology draw on the social sciences? (Exam style)
    • Biology alone already explains all extinctions
    • Laws are irrelevant to biodiversity
    • Most threats are driven by human behaviour and institutions
    • Social scientists are needed to count species
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Most threats are driven by human behaviour and institutions. Applying the standard result: Human activity drives most threats. Revise Conservation Biology →

  3. A conservation team must decide whether to protect a site before research on a rare species is complete. What principle supports acting now? (Stretch)
    • Action should always wait for full certainty
    • Delay risks irreversible loss in a crisis discipline
    • Rare species are never worth protecting
    • Research must be stopped once action starts
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Delay risks irreversible loss in a crisis discipline. Applying the standard result: Extinction cannot be reversed. Revise Conservation Biology →

Climate Change

  1. Which describes climate rather than weather? (Foundation recall)
    • A thunderstorm in Bristol last night
    • The average July temperature in Cardiff over 30 years
    • This morning's wind speed at Heathrow
    • Tomorrow's chance of rain in Leeds
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The average July temperature in Cardiff over 30 years. Climate is the long-term statistical pattern, not a single day's conditions. Revise Climate Change →

  2. Which component of the climate system includes permafrost and sea ice? (Exam style)
    • The biosphere
    • The cryosphere
    • The hydrosphere
    • The lithosphere
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The cryosphere. The cryosphere covers all frozen water on Earth, including permafrost. Revise Climate Change →

  3. A record cold day occurs in a region that is warming. What is the best interpretation? (Stretch)
    • Climate change has reversed
    • Natural variability still produces cold extremes, but less often
    • The region is no longer warming
    • Thermometers must be faulty
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Natural variability still produces cold extremes, but less often. Warming shifts the distribution, making cold records rarer but not impossible. Revise Climate Change →

Field Methods and Statistics

  1. Which is a testable hypothesis? (Foundation recall)
    • Bluebells are more abundant where the woodland canopy is more open
    • Bluebells are the most beautiful woodland flower
    • Bluebells will always grow in Britain
    • Woodlands should be protected
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Bluebells are more abundant where the woodland canopy is more open. A testable hypothesis makes a prediction that data can check. Revise Field Methods and Statistics →

  2. Why is peer review important in science? (Exam style)
    • It guarantees that every published result is completely true
    • It replaces the need for any statistical analysis of data
    • It stops scientists from sharing their raw data with others
    • Other experts check the methods and conclusions before publication
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Other experts check the methods and conclusions before publication. Peer review helps catch errors and weak reasoning. Revise Field Methods and Statistics →

  3. Why are ecological conclusions often treated as provisional? (Stretch)
    • Complex interacting factors and limited data mean new evidence may change them
    • Ecological data are usually invented to fit the hypothesis
    • Ecologists never use statistics to analyse their data
    • Nature never changes, so conclusions can never be wrong
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Complex interacting factors and limited data mean new evidence may change them. Complexity and incomplete data make revision likely. Revise Field Methods and Statistics →

Resources and Pollution

  1. Which is a secondary energy source? (Foundation recall)
    • Coal
    • Crude oil
    • Electricity
    • Sunlight
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Electricity. Electricity is generated by converting primary sources. Revise Resources and Pollution →

  2. What did the closure of Ratcliffe-on-Soar in 2024 mark? (Exam style)
    • The end of coal-fired electricity generation in the UK
    • The end of gas use for heating in UK homes
    • The opening of the UK's first wind farm
    • The start of nuclear power in the UK
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The end of coal-fired electricity generation in the UK. Ratcliffe-on-Soar was the UK's last operating coal power station. Revise Resources and Pollution →

  3. Why does the 2022 gas price crisis illustrate the energy trilemma? (Stretch)
    • Dependence on imported gas threatened both security and affordability
    • It had no effect on household bills
    • It proved renewables are always more expensive
    • It showed energy prices never change
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Dependence on imported gas threatened both security and affordability. Supply shocks show how security and affordability are linked. Revise Resources and Pollution →

Sustainability and Environmental Policy

  1. In which year was Our Common Future, the Brundtland Report, published? (Foundation recall)
    • 1972
    • 1987
    • 1992
    • 2015
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    1987. The World Commission on Environment and Development reported in 1987. Revise Sustainability and Environmental Policy →

  2. What is the main criticism of the three pillars model? (Exam style)
    • It gives too much weight to the environment over the economy
    • It ignores the economy entirely
    • It implies environmental losses can be offset by economic gains
    • It was never used by governments or businesses
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It implies environmental losses can be offset by economic gains. Equal pillars suggest trade-offs are always acceptable. Revise Sustainability and Environmental Policy →

  3. A government argues that selling North Sea oil is sustainable because the revenue is invested in schools. Which view does this reflect? (Stretch)
    • Deep ecology
    • Strong sustainability
    • The precautionary principle in its strongest form
    • Weak sustainability
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Weak sustainability. Converting natural capital into human capital assumes substitution is possible. Revise Sustainability and Environmental Policy →
