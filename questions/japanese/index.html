First Steps: Sounds and Hiragana

  1. How many vowel sounds does standard Japanese have? (Foundation recall)
    • About twenty, as in English
    • Five: a, i, u, e and o
    • Seven, as in Italian
    • Three: a, i and u only
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Five: a, i, u, e and o. Japanese has five vowels, a, i, u, e and o, each short and steady, which makes the vowel system far simpler than English. Revise First Steps: Sounds and Hiragana →

  2. How many morae are in とうきょう (Tōkyō)? (Exam style)
    • Five
    • Four
    • Three
    • Two
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Four. と, the lengthening う, きょ (one mora written with two kana) and the final う make four beats, though English hears two syllables. Revise First Steps: Sounds and Hiragana →

  3. A learner pronounces おばあさん (obāsan, "grandmother") with a short a. What has gone wrong? (Stretch)
    • A mora has been dropped, giving おばさん, "aunt"
    • Nothing, because vowel length is optional
    • The final ん has been pronounced too strongly
    • The pitch accent has moved to the first syllable
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A mora has been dropped, giving おばさん, "aunt". Long vowels count as two morae. Dropping one turns obāsan (grandmother, five morae) into obasan (aunt, four morae): a different word. Revise First Steps: Sounds and Hiragana →

Katakana, First Kanji and the Basic Sentence

  1. Which katakana spells the sound ka? (Foundation recall)
    • カ
    • ク
    • ケ
    • タ
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    カ. カ is ka; it grew from the left part of the kanji 加. ク is ku, タ is ta and ケ is ke. Revise Katakana, First Kanji and the Basic Sentence →

  2. What does the katakana word カメラ mean? (Exam style)
    • Camera
    • Cola
    • Comedy
    • Lemon
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Camera. カ ka, メ me and ラ ra spell kamera, the English word camera reshaped into Japanese morae. Revise Katakana, First Kanji and the Basic Sentence →

  3. A learner writes the name "Sue" as スウ instead of スー. Why is this a problem? (Stretch)
    • It should be written in hiragana, not katakana
    • ウ is not part of the katakana chart
    • ウ suggests a separate u vowel instead of a length mark
    • ス cannot be followed by any vowel letter
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    ウ suggests a separate u vowel instead of a length mark. Loanwords and foreign names mark long vowels with the bar ー, so a standard spelling is スー; スウ would usually be read as two separate vowels. Revise Katakana, First Kanji and the Basic Sentence →

Verbs, Particles and Daily Routine

  1. Which verb is a ru-verb? (Foundation recall)
    • かく (kaku)
    • たべる (taberu)
    • のむ (nomu)
    • はなす (hanasu)
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    たべる (taberu). たべる ends in -eru and is a regular ru-verb, so it drops る to conjugate. Revise Verbs, Particles and Daily Routine →

  2. Which verb ending in -iru or -eru is actually a u-verb? (Exam style)
    • おきる (okiru, get up)
    • ねる (neru, sleep)
    • はいる (hairu, enter)
    • みる (miru, see)
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    はいる (hairu, enter). はいる is a known exception: it ends in -iru but conjugates as a u-verb. Revise Verbs, Particles and Daily Routine →

  3. Why must learners know a verb's group? (Stretch)
    • Each group forms the masu, negative and other forms differently
    • Groups only matter in written Japanese
    • The group decides the verb's person and number
    • The group shows whether the verb is past or present
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Each group forms the masu, negative and other forms differently. The group tells you which rule to use to change the ending for every other form. Revise Verbs, Particles and Daily Routine →

Adjectives, Counters and Description

  1. What is the present negative of やすい (yasui, cheap)? (Foundation recall)
    • やすいじゃないです
    • やすいないです
    • やすくです
    • やすくないです
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    やすくないです. Replace the final い with くない and add です: やすくないです. Revise Adjectives, Counters and Description →

  2. Which word is not an い-adjective despite ending in い? (Exam style)
    • あつい (atsui)
    • きれい (kirei)
    • ながい (nagai)
    • はやい (hayai)
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    きれい (kirei). きれい is a な-adjective, so it does not take くない. Revise Adjectives, Counters and Description →

  3. Which sentence says "This film isn't interesting"? (Stretch)
    • この えいがは おもしろいくないです
    • この えいがは おもしろいじゃないです
    • この えいがは おもしろくありますです
    • この えいがは おもしろくないです
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    この えいがは おもしろくないです. おもしろい is an い-adjective, so い becomes くない. Revise Adjectives, Counters and Description →

The Te-form and Plain Forms

  1. What is the te-form of たべる (taberu, eat)? (Foundation recall)
    • たべいて
    • たべって
    • たべて
    • たべんで
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    たべて. たべる is a ru-verb, so drop る and add て to make たべて. Revise The Te-form and Plain Forms →

  2. What is the te-form of かく (kaku, write)? (Exam style)
    • かいて
    • かきて
    • かって
    • かんで
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    かいて. Verbs ending in く change to いて, so かく becomes かいて. Revise The Te-form and Plain Forms →

  3. What is the te-form of いく (iku), and why is it unusual? (Stretch)
    • いいて, because it follows the く rule
    • いきて, because it is a ru-verb
    • いって, because く verbs normally give いて
    • いんで, because it ends in く
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    いって, because く verbs normally give いて. いく is the one く-verb that takes って instead of いて. Revise The Te-form and Plain Forms →

Everyday Life: GCSE Themes

  1. Which word means "my father" when speaking about your own family (chichi)? (Foundation recall)
    • あに
    • おとうさん
    • そふ
    • ちち
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    ちち. ちち is the humble term for your own father. Revise Everyday Life: GCSE Themes →

  2. Which word refers to someone else's younger sister (imōtosan)? (Exam style)
    • あね
    • いもうと
    • いもうとさん
    • おねえさん
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    いもうとさん. いもうとさん is the respectful term for another person's younger sister. Revise Everyday Life: GCSE Themes →

  3. How would you say "I get on well with my older brother" (ani to naka ga ii desu)? (Stretch)
    • あにが なかです
    • あにと けんかを します
    • あにと なかが いいです
    • あにを なかが いいです
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    あにと なかが いいです. なかが いい means to get on well with someone. Revise Everyday Life: GCSE Themes →

N4 Grammar: Ability, Intention and Conditions

  1. What is the potential form of のむ (nomu, drink)? (Foundation recall)
    • のまれる
    • のみられる
    • のむれる
    • のめる
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    のめる. U-verbs change the final u sound to e and add る, so のむ becomes のめる. Revise N4 Grammar: Ability, Intention and Conditions →

  2. Which particle normally marks the object of a potential verb, as in にほんご__ はなせます (nihongo __ hanasemasu)? (Exam style)
    • が
    • で
    • に
    • へ
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    が. Potential verbs usually take が for their object in standard Japanese. Revise N4 Grammar: Ability, Intention and Conditions →

  3. What is たべれる (tabereru)? (Stretch)
    • A colloquial ra-less potential of たべる
    • A spelling mistake for たべる
    • The causative of たべる
    • The passive of たべる
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A colloquial ra-less potential of たべる. Dropping ら from たべられる is common in casual speech but non-standard. Revise N4 Grammar: Ability, Intention and Conditions →

Keigo and Social Register

  1. Which speech level would you use with a close friend of the same age? (Foundation recall)
    • Formal written style only
    • Humble kenjōgo for everything
    • Plain speech with dictionary forms
    • Respectful sonkeigo verbs
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Plain speech with dictionary forms. Friends normally use plain speech with each other. Revise Keigo and Social Register →

  2. Which type of keigo is used for a teacher's actions, as in せんせいが いらっしゃいます (sensei ga irasshaimasu)? (Exam style)
    • Casual speech
    • Humble language, けんじょうご
    • Plain speech
    • Respectful language, そんけいご
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Respectful language, そんけいご. そんけいご raises the actions of the person you respect. Revise Keigo and Social Register →

  3. Why does a young shop assistant use keigo with an older customer and a younger customer alike? (Stretch)
    • Keigo follows the role of customer, not only age
    • Keigo is only for older people
    • Shop assistants must speak casually
    • Younger customers must be addressed in plain speech
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Keigo follows the role of customer, not only age. Service roles call for keigo with all customers. Revise Keigo and Social Register →

N3 Grammar: Nuance and Viewpoint

  1. What does さいふを なくして しまいました (saifu o nakushite shimaimashita) express? (Foundation recall)
    • A plan to buy a wallet
    • A wish for a new wallet
    • Permission to lose a wallet
    • Regret at losing a wallet
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Regret at losing a wallet. ～て しまう with なくす expresses an unfortunate result the speaker regrets. Revise N3 Grammar: Nuance and Viewpoint →

  2. What is the casual form of たべて しまった (tabete shimatta)? (Exam style)
    • たべしまった
    • たべじゃった
    • たべちゃう
    • たべちゃった
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    たべちゃった. ～て しまった shortens to ～ちゃった in casual speech. Revise N3 Grammar: Nuance and Viewpoint →

  3. How does でんしゃに わすれものを して しまいました (wasuremono o shite shimaimashita) differ from ～を しました? (Stretch)
    • It describes a future plan
    • It is more casual than the plain past
    • It means the speaker found something
    • It shows the speaker did not want the result
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It shows the speaker did not want the result. ～て しまう adds a feeling of regret about the result. Revise N3 Grammar: Nuance and Viewpoint →

Kanji and Reading to N3

  1. What is the Jōyō kanji list? (Foundation recall)
    • A list of kanji banned in newspapers
    • A list of kanji used only in names
    • A list of the oldest Chinese characters
    • The official list of kanji for everyday use
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The official list of kanji for everyday use. The Jōyō list sets the kanji used in everyday and official writing. Revise Kanji and Reading to N3 →

  2. Roughly how many kanji are commonly estimated for JLPT N3? (Exam style)
    • About 10,000
    • About 3,000
    • About 50
    • About 650
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    About 650. Common estimates put N3 at around 650 kanji, though no official list exists. Revise Kanji and Reading to N3 →

  3. What is the most effective way to learn kanji for reading? (Stretch)
    • Avoiding kana completely
    • Learning only the stroke order
    • Learning them through common vocabulary in context
    • Memorising all readings of each kanji separately
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Learning them through common vocabulary in context. Words such as 学校 and 学生 give kanji meaning and useful readings. Revise Kanji and Reading to N3 →

Japanese Culture and Society

  1. Which period lasted from 1603 to 1868 under the Tokugawa shoguns? (Foundation recall)
    • The Edo period
    • The Heian period
    • The Jōmon period
    • The Meiji period
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The Edo period. The Edo period was a long era of peace under the Tokugawa shogunate. Revise Japanese Culture and Society →

  2. What was a major effect of the Meiji Restoration? (Exam style)
    • Rapid modernisation and adoption of Western institutions
    • The end of rice farming across the country
    • The first arrival of Buddhism from Korea
    • The writing of The Tale of Genji at court
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Rapid modernisation and adoption of Western institutions. From 1868, Japan modernised quickly and moved its capital to Tokyo. Revise Japanese Culture and Society →

  3. Why is knowing era names such as 令和 (Reiwa) useful today? (Stretch)
    • They are names of famous temples
    • They are only used in history books
    • They are still used for dates on official documents
    • They replace all Western dates
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    They are still used for dates on official documents. Japanese dates often combine era names with year numbers. Revise Japanese Culture and Society →

Exam Skills: GCSE and JLPT

  1. Which is the hardest JLPT level? (Foundation recall)
    • N1
    • N3
    • N4
    • N5
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    N1. N1 is the most advanced level of the JLPT. Revise Exam Skills: GCSE and JLPT →

  2. What is needed to pass the JLPT? (Exam style)
    • A speaking interview
    • Only a perfect score in reading
    • Passing a written essay
    • The overall pass mark and a minimum score in each section
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The overall pass mark and a minimum score in each section. Candidates must meet both overall and sectional requirements. Revise Exam Skills: GCSE and JLPT →

  3. Why should JLPT candidates also practise speaking separately? (Stretch)
    • Speaking is half of the JLPT score
    • Speaking replaces the reading section
    • The JLPT has no speaking section
    • The JLPT is entirely spoken
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The JLPT has no speaking section. The JLPT measures only receptive skills and language knowledge. Revise Exam Skills: GCSE and JLPT →
