Design Thinking and Creativity practice questions

36 questions from Gradify's free Design Thinking and Creativity course, with the answer and why behind each reveal. Answer before you open it: that's what makes it stick.

Foundations of Design Thinking

  1. Which statement best describes design? (Foundation recall)
    • Copying an existing product and lowering its price
    • Decorating an object so that it looks more attractive
    • Expressing a personal idea with no intended user
    • Planning something new to change a situation into a preferred one
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Planning something new to change a situation into a preferred one. Design is purposeful problem solving (Simon, 1969); decoration and self-expression are narrower activities. Revise Foundations of Design Thinking →

  2. A bank's new app is easy to use and customers love it, but each account costs more to run than it earns. Which lens does it fail? (Exam style)
    • Desirability
    • Feasibility
    • Usability
    • Viability
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Viability. It works and people want it, so desirability and feasibility are met; it fails viability because it cannot be sustained financially. Revise Foundations of Design Thinking →

  3. A critic says "design is just making things pretty". Which reply is strongest? (Stretch)
    • Appearance is one requirement, but a design is judged on function, use, cost and impact too
    • Design has nothing to do with appearance, only engineering
    • Designers rarely think about appearance, which is the job of marketing teams
    • Pretty things always sell, so appearance is the only test of a design
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Appearance is one requirement, but a design is judged on function, use, cost and impact too. The strongest reply accepts appearance matters and shows it is one of several requirements; the other options are exaggerations. Revise Foundations of Design Thinking →

The Psychology of Creativity

  1. According to the standard definition, which idea is creative? (Foundation recall)
    • A random design that nobody can use
    • An exact copy of a successful product
    • An unusual design that also solves the user's problem
    • Any idea that took a long time
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    An unusual design that also solves the user's problem. Creativity needs originality and effectiveness together; random or copied ideas lack one of them. Revise The Psychology of Creativity →

  2. A Year 10 student invents a folding mechanism, unaware that an identical one was patented in 1950. How would Boden describe it? (Exam style)
    • Big-C creativity
    • H-creative but not P-creative
    • Neither P-creative nor H-creative
    • P-creative but not H-creative
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    P-creative but not H-creative. It is new to the student (P-creative) but not new in history (not H-creative). Revise The Psychology of Creativity →

  3. A critic argues a wildly unusual but unusable chair is "the most creative chair ever made". Which reply is strongest? (Stretch)
    • Only professional designers can make creative chairs
    • Originality alone is not enough; a creative design must also be effective for its purpose
    • Unusual objects are never creative because they break the rules
    • Usability is irrelevant to any judgement about creativity
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Originality alone is not enough; a creative design must also be effective for its purpose. Under the standard definition, novelty without effectiveness is not creativity, though as an art object its purpose might differ. Revise The Psychology of Creativity →

Ideation Methods

  1. Which is one of Osborn's rules of brainstorming? (Foundation recall)
    • Allow only realistic ideas to be shared
    • Evaluate each idea as it is suggested
    • Let the most senior person speak first
    • Withhold criticism until later
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Withhold criticism until later. Deferring judgement is a core rule; the others contradict the method. Revise Ideation Methods →

  2. In a team brainstorm, several people say they had ideas but forgot them while waiting to speak. What is this? (Exam style)
    • Design fixation
    • Evaluation apprehension
    • Production blocking
    • Social loafing
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Production blocking. Waiting for a turn causes ideas to be lost: production blocking (Diehl and Stroebe, 1987). Revise Ideation Methods →

  3. Which change would most improve the idea output of a group brainstorm? (Stretch)
    • Let the manager choose which ideas are recorded
    • Make the session longer and louder with no structure
    • Remove the rule against criticism so ideas are tested early
    • Start with silent individual idea writing, then share and build in the group
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Start with silent individual idea writing, then share and build in the group. Individual generation removes blocking and apprehension; the group phase keeps combination. Revise Ideation Methods →

User Research and Empathy

  1. Which is a main purpose of user research? (Foundation recall)
    • Discovering needs and checking assumptions before building
    • Making the portfolio look more thorough
    • Proving the designer's first idea was right
    • Replacing the need for any testing later
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Discovering needs and checking assumptions before building. Research discovers needs and reduces risk; it should test, not confirm, assumptions. Revise User Research and Empathy →

  2. Shoppers say they always read nutrition labels, but in-store observation shows most glance for under two seconds. What explains the gap? (Exam style)
    • Production blocking in the interviews
    • Shoppers were deliberately lying to researchers
    • Social desirability bias and the gap between saying and doing
    • The observation was a usability test
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Social desirability bias and the gap between saying and doing. People report what sounds responsible; observation reveals actual behaviour. Revise User Research and Empathy →

  3. A start-up says user research is too slow and costly. Which is the strongest reply? (Stretch)
    • Finding a wrong assumption early costs far less than discovering it after launch
    • Research guarantees that a product will succeed
    • Research is always free, so cost is never relevant
    • Users always know exactly what they want
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Finding a wrong assumption early costs far less than discovering it after launch. Early research reduces the much larger cost of building the wrong thing, though it guarantees nothing. Revise User Research and Empathy →

Defining Problems

  1. What did Schön call the activity of defining what a problem is before solving it? (Foundation recall)
    • Problem blocking
    • Problem scoring
    • Problem setting
    • Problem solving
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Problem setting. Schön distinguished problem setting (framing) from problem solving. Revise Defining Problems →

  2. A school says "we need a bigger car park" because the road is jammed at drop-off. Which reframe opens the most options? (Exam style)
    • How might we build the biggest car park possible?
    • How might we fine parents who park badly?
    • How might we make it easy for families to get pupils to school without jams?
    • How might we paint new lines in the car park?
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    How might we make it easy for families to get pupils to school without jams?. Reframing to the goal (getting pupils to school) opens walking buses, staggered times and other options. Revise Defining Problems →

  3. When is reframing least useful? (Stretch)
    • After a frame has been tested and works, when the team needs to deliver
    • At the start of a complex, poorly understood problem
    • When early solutions keep failing
    • When stakeholders disagree about what the problem is
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    After a frame has been tested and works, when the team needs to deliver. Reframing is valuable early or when solutions fail; once a tested frame works, delivery matters. Revise Defining Problems →

Prototyping, Testing and Iteration

  1. Which is a main purpose of prototyping? (Foundation recall)
    • Finding problems while changes are still cheap
    • Making the portfolio look finished
    • Producing the final product for sale
    • Replacing the need for a design brief
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Finding problems while changes are still cheap. Prototypes help learn and reduce risk early, before costly commitments. Revise Prototyping, Testing and Iteration →

  2. A team wants to know if a new wearable fits comfortably on the wrist. Which prototype dimension is it testing? (Exam style)
    • Implementation
    • Integration
    • Look and feel
    • Role
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Look and feel. Comfort and fit concern what it is like to wear and use: look and feel. Revise Prototyping, Testing and Iteration →

  3. A team has two weeks and is unsure which of three concepts to pursue. What does the research suggest? (Stretch)
    • Build the most expensive one first to impress the client
    • Make rough prototypes of all three, get feedback, then choose
    • Pick one and refine it in detail for the whole two weeks
    • Wait until the concept is certain before prototyping
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Make rough prototypes of all three, get feedback, then choose. Parallel prototyping improves outcomes and avoids early lock-in (Dow et al., 2010). Revise Prototyping, Testing and Iteration →

The Visual Language of Art and Design

  1. Which of these is best described as design rather than fine art? (Foundation recall)
    • A marble figure carved for a sculpture park
    • A typeface commissioned for a city's road signs
    • A video installation in a museum
    • An oil painting shown in a gallery
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A typeface commissioned for a city's road signs. A typeface answers a brief for users (drivers reading signs), which is the defining purpose of design; the others are made chiefly to be contemplated. Revise The Visual Language of Art and Design →

  2. Why is Duchamp's Fountain (1917) often used to discuss the definition of art? (Exam style)
    • It was presented as art although the artist did not make it by hand
    • It was rejected because of poor craftsmanship
    • It was the first sculpture made of porcelain
    • It was the first work sold at auction in New York
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It was presented as art although the artist did not make it by hand. The work's status rested on choice, signature and context, not handmade skill, which challenged skill-based and formalist definitions. Revise The Visual Language of Art and Design →

  3. A critic argues that a pile of bricks in a gallery is art only because the gallery shows it. Which theory is the critic using? (Stretch)
    • Expression theory
    • Formalism
    • The imitation (mimesis) theory
    • The institutional theory of art
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The institutional theory of art. The institutional theory (Dickie) defines art by its acceptance within the art world; formalism looks at visual form, mimesis at likeness, expression at feeling conveyed. Revise The Visual Language of Art and Design →

Accessibility and Inclusive Design

  1. About what proportion of the world's population does the WHO estimate experience significant disability? (Foundation recall)
    • About 1%
    • About 16%
    • About 50%
    • About 75%
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    About 16%. The WHO (2022) estimated around 1.3 billion people, roughly 16%. Revise Accessibility and Inclusive Design →

  2. A designer adds large touch targets for users with tremors. Who else is most likely to benefit? (Exam style)
    • Nobody else at all
    • Only users of desktop computers
    • Only users with tremors
    • People using a phone on a bumpy bus
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    People using a phone on a bumpy bus. Larger targets help anyone with reduced precision, including situational limits. Revise Accessibility and Inclusive Design →

  3. Which is the strongest argument for designing accessibility in from the start? (Stretch)
    • Accessibility laws only apply after a product is launched
    • Accessible designs always cost more than other designs
    • Disabled people are a small group, so a quick check is enough
    • Retrofitting is costly, and early inclusion improves the design for everyone
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Retrofitting is costly, and early inclusion improves the design for everyone. Designing in early avoids expensive rework and brings wider usability benefits. Revise Accessibility and Inclusive Design →

Mechanics of Materials

  1. A 2,000 N force acts on an area of 40 mm2. What is the stress? (Foundation recall)
    • 0.02 MPa
    • 2,040 MPa
    • 50 MPa
    • 80,000 MPa
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    50 MPa. Stress = F/A = 2,000 ÷ 40 = 50 N/mm2, which is 50 MPa. Revise Mechanics of Materials →

  2. A 2 m rod extends by 1.2 mm under load. What is the strain? (Exam style)
    • 0.0006
    • 0.0024
    • 0.6
    • 1.67
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    0.0006. Strain = δ/L = 1.2 ÷ 2,000 = 0.0006, or 600 microstrain. Revise Mechanics of Materials →

  3. A tensile test specimen necks before fracture. Why does engineering stress fall while true stress keeps rising? (Stretch)
    • Engineering stress uses the original area, but the actual area shrinks rapidly at the neck
    • The load increases sharply during necking
    • The material becomes stronger as it necks
    • True stress ignores the cross-sectional area
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Engineering stress uses the original area, but the actual area shrinks rapidly at the neck. Engineering stress divides load by the original area; true stress uses the smaller, actual area. Revise Mechanics of Materials →

Service and Systems Design

  1. Which characteristic means an unsold cinema seat cannot be stored and sold tomorrow? (Foundation recall)
    • Heterogeneity
    • Inseparability
    • Intangibility
    • Perishability
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Perishability. Services are perishable: unused capacity is lost. Revise Service and Systems Design →

  2. A bank's app is easy to use but transfers take days because two internal systems do not talk to each other. Where is the problem? (Exam style)
    • Backstage
    • Frontstage
    • In the evidence
    • In the line of sight
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Backstage. The failure lies in the hidden systems that support the visible service. Revise Service and Systems Design →

  3. A hospital asks a designer to improve its appointment letter, but patients miss appointments because the booking phone line is always busy. What should the designer do? (Stretch)
    • Add the hospital logo to the letter
    • Look at the whole booking service, not just the letter
    • Redesign the letter with brighter colours
    • Shorten the letter to one paragraph
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Look at the whole booking service, not just the letter. Service design is holistic, so the designer should address the real cause across channels. Revise Service and Systems Design →

Sustainable and Ethical Design

  1. Which UK body published the Green Claims Code? (Foundation recall)
    • The Advertising Standards Agency of Europe
    • The Competition and Markets Authority
    • The Design Council
    • The Environment Agency board
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The Competition and Markets Authority. The CMA published the Green Claims Code in 2021 to help businesses avoid misleading claims. Revise Sustainable and Ethical Design →

  2. A product is labelled "eco-friendly" with no evidence or explanation. Which Green Claims Code principle does this most clearly fail? (Exam style)
    • Claims must be clear and substantiated
    • Claims must be printed in green
    • Claims must make fair comparisons
    • Claims must use the company logo
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Claims must be clear and substantiated. Vague claims without evidence can mislead consumers. Revise Sustainable and Ethical Design →

  3. A designer can reduce a kettle's environmental impact most at which point? (Stretch)
    • When choosing its materials, energy use and lifespan
    • When choosing the delivery van colour
    • When designing the shop display
    • When writing the warranty card
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    When choosing its materials, energy use and lifespan. Early design choices determine most of a product's footprint. Revise Sustainable and Ethical Design →

Collaboration, Communication and Intellectual Property

  1. Which researcher is most associated with the concept of team psychological safety? (Foundation recall)
    • Amy Edmondson
    • Bruce Tuckman
    • Meredith Belbin
    • Tim Brown
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Amy Edmondson. Edmondson's 1999 study linked psychological safety to team learning. Revise Collaboration, Communication and Intellectual Property →

  2. A team member says nothing about a usability problem because the lead dislikes bad news. What is lacking? (Exam style)
    • A larger budget
    • A T-shaped skill set
    • More detailed personas
    • Psychological safety
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Psychological safety. Without safety, people hide problems and the team cannot learn. Revise Collaboration, Communication and Intellectual Property →

  3. A design team spends every meeting brainstorming aloud and the quietest members rarely contribute. What is the best change? (Stretch)
    • Add solo idea time before group discussion
    • Ban disagreement in meetings
    • Let only the lead suggest ideas
    • Make the meetings longer
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Add solo idea time before group discussion. Individual thinking first reduces production blocking and includes quieter voices. Revise Collaboration, Communication and Intellectual Property →
