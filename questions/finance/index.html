Accounting and Finance practice questions

Accounting and the Double-Entry System

  1. Which activity is bookkeeping rather than the wider work of accounting? (Foundation recall)
    • Advising whether to lease or buy a van
    • Entering each day's sales invoices in the sales day book
    • Explaining to the owner why profit has fallen
    • Preparing the year's financial statements
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Entering each day's sales invoices in the sales day book. Bookkeeping is the recording stage: entering transactions accurately. Interpreting, reporting and advising are the accountant's wider work. Revise Accounting and the Double-Entry System →

  2. A shop owner signs an agreement on 30 June to buy a new till, which will be delivered and invoiced on 5 July. When is the purchase first recorded? (Exam style)
    • 30 June, when the agreement is signed
    • 5 July, when the till is delivered and invoiced
    • It is never recorded because it is a capital item
    • Only when the invoice is eventually paid
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    5 July, when the till is delivered and invoiced. An agreement to buy in the future isn't yet a transaction: nothing has been exchanged. The purchase is recorded when the till is received and the obligation to pay arises. Revise Accounting and the Double-Entry System →

  3. Why do accounting failures such as Carillion's collapse matter beyond the company's own shareholders? (Stretch)
    • Accounting failures matter only if they involve tax evasion
    • Lenders, suppliers, employees and pension schemes all rely on the accounts when dealing with the company
    • Only shareholders read company accounts, so the harm is limited to them
    • They matter only because the company stops paying its auditors
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Lenders, suppliers, employees and pension schemes all rely on the accounts when dealing with the company. Many stakeholders make decisions using published accounts; when the accounts mislead, losses spread to creditors, staff, pensioners and the public. Revise Accounting and the Double-Entry System →

Preparing Financial Statements

  1. Which is revenue expenditure for a haulage firm? (Foundation recall)
    • A new lorry
    • Fitting a refrigerated unit that lets a lorry carry chilled goods
    • Fuel for its lorries
    • Legal fees for buying a depot
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Fuel for its lorries. Fuel is consumed in day-to-day operations; the others create or improve non-current assets. Revise Preparing Financial Statements →

  2. A café replaces its worn floor tiles with identical new tiles for £4,000. How should this be treated? (Exam style)
    • A capital receipt
    • Capital expenditure, because it is a large amount
    • Capital expenditure, because the tiles last several years
    • Revenue expenditure: it restores the original condition
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Revenue expenditure: it restores the original condition. Like-for-like replacement maintains the asset's condition; it doesn't improve it, so it is a repair. Revise Preparing Financial Statements →

  3. A company capitalises £50,000 of routine repairs as an improvement to its building. What is the effect this year? (Stretch)
    • Liabilities are overstated by £50,000
    • Only the cash balance is affected, by the full £50,000
    • Profit and non-current assets are both overstated, by up to £50,000 less any depreciation charged
    • Profit is understated by £50,000, and non-current assets are unaffected this year
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Profit and non-current assets are both overstated, by up to £50,000 less any depreciation charged. Moving an expense into assets raises profit and assets this year; the cost then leaks into later years through depreciation. Revise Preparing Financial Statements →

Financial Reporting for Companies

  1. Which companies must use UK-adopted IFRS for their consolidated accounts? (Foundation recall)
    • All UK private companies
    • Groups with securities traded on a UK regulated market
    • Micro-entities
    • Sole traders
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Groups with securities traded on a UK regulated market. Listed groups must use adopted international standards; most other companies may choose FRS 102. Revise Financial Reporting for Companies →

  2. A private company with no listed securities wants simpler reporting than full IFRS. Which standard is it most likely to use? (Exam style)
    • FRS 102
    • IAS 1 only
    • IFRS 18
    • IFRS 9
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    FRS 102. FRS 102 is the main UK GAAP standard for unlisted companies. Revise Financial Reporting for Companies →

  3. An investor compares a listed company using IFRS with a private rival using FRS 102. What is the best advice? (Stretch)
    • Check accounting differences, such as goodwill amortisation, before comparing figures
    • FRS 102 accounts are always unreliable
    • Only cash figures differ between the frameworks
    • The figures are identical because both standards are based on IFRS
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Check accounting differences, such as goodwill amortisation, before comparing figures. FRS 102 differs from IFRS in several areas, so adjusted comparisons are needed. Revise Financial Reporting for Companies →

Interpreting Financial Statements and Group Accounts

  1. Which user is most interested in a company's ability to repay a loan? (Foundation recall)
    • A competitor's employees
    • A customer buying a single product
    • A lender
    • A local resident
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A lender. Lenders focus on liquidity, cash flow, gearing and interest cover. Revise Interpreting Financial Statements and Group Accounts →

  2. Profit rose from £400,000 to £460,000. What is the percentage increase? (Exam style)
    • 13%
    • 15%
    • 6%
    • 60%
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    15%. Percentage change = change ÷ original: (460,000 − 400,000) ÷ 400,000 = 15%. Revise Interpreting Financial Statements and Group Accounts →

  3. A company's gross margin is 25%. An analyst calls this poor. What is missing from the analysis? (Stretch)
    • Comparison with the company's previous years and with the industry
    • Nothing, because 25% is always a poor margin
    • The company's registered office address
    • The name of the company's auditor
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Comparison with the company's previous years and with the industry. Whether 25% is good depends on industry norms and the trend; supermarkets have low margins, software firms high ones. Revise Interpreting Financial Statements and Group Accounts →

Management Accounting: Costing

  1. Which feature distinguishes management accounting from financial accounting? (Foundation recall)
    • It has no legally prescribed format
    • It is published for shareholders
    • It must follow IFRS Accounting Standards
    • It reports only past results
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It has no legally prescribed format. Management accounts are internal and unregulated, so their format suits the business. Revise Management Accounting: Costing →

  2. A manager compares this month's actual costs with the budget and investigates overspends. Which role is this? (Exam style)
    • Control
    • Financial reporting
    • Planning
    • Tax compliance
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Control. Comparing actual with plan and acting on differences is control. Revise Management Accounting: Costing →

  3. A report costing £20,000 a year to produce saves managers about £5,000 a year. Which quality of good information does it fail? (Stretch)
    • Completeness, because it omits tax
    • Cost-effectiveness, because its cost exceeds its benefit
    • Relevance, because it concerns past costs
    • Timeliness, because it is produced annually
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Cost-effectiveness, because its cost exceeds its benefit. Information should only be produced when its benefits exceed its costs. Revise Management Accounting: Costing →

Management Accounting: Planning, Control and Decisions

  1. Which cost is relevant to a decision? (Foundation recall)
    • A future cost that changes because of the decision
    • A lease payment that must be paid regardless
    • Depreciation on existing machinery
    • Research spending incurred last year
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A future cost that changes because of the decision. Only future incremental cash flows are relevant. Revise Management Accounting: Planning, Control and Decisions →

  2. Material in stock cost £5 a kg; it is regularly used and now costs £7 to replace. What is its relevant cost? (Exam style)
    • £0
    • £12 per kg
    • £5 per kg
    • £7 per kg
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    £7 per kg. Using it means buying more at the replacement cost. Revise Management Accounting: Planning, Control and Decisions →

  3. A special order's price exceeds relevant cost, but accepting it would cause regular customers to demand the same low price. What should managers do? (Stretch)
    • Accept, as only the order's contribution matters
    • Ignore customers' reactions
    • Reject all special orders permanently
    • Weigh the long-term impact on regular prices alongside the short-term contribution
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Weigh the long-term impact on regular prices alongside the short-term contribution. Qualitative and longer-term effects can outweigh one-off gains. Revise Management Accounting: Planning, Control and Decisions →

Financial Management

  1. Which decision concerns how much profit to pay to shareholders? (Foundation recall)
    • The dividend decision
    • The financing decision
    • The investment decision
    • The pricing decision
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The dividend decision. Dividend policy decides distribution versus retention. Revise Financial Management →

  2. How is financial management different from financial accounting? (Exam style)
    • It ignores cash flows entirely
    • It looks forward at cash flows, risk and value
    • It must follow IFRS for external users
    • It only reports historical results
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It looks forward at cash flows, risk and value. Financial management supports forward-looking decisions. Revise Financial Management →

  3. A firm plans major investment but also wants to raise its dividend sharply. Why might this be a problem? (Stretch)
    • Dividends have no link with investment
    • Higher dividends always lower borrowing costs
    • Higher dividends reduce retained earnings available for investment, increasing the need for external finance
    • Investment never needs funding
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Higher dividends reduce retained earnings available for investment, increasing the need for external finance. Applying the standard result: The three decisions are interdependent. Revise Financial Management →

Audit, Assurance and Ethics

  1. To whom does the statutory auditor of a UK company report? (Foundation recall)
    • The company's bank
    • The directors
    • The shareholders
    • The tax authority
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The shareholders. Applying the standard result: The auditor reports to the members. Revise Audit, Assurance and Ethics →

  2. What level of assurance does an external audit provide? (Exam style)
    • Absolute assurance
    • Limited assurance
    • No assurance at all
    • Reasonable assurance
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Reasonable assurance. Audits give high but not absolute assurance. Revise Audit, Assurance and Ethics →

  3. Why does separating ownership from management increase demand for audit? (Stretch)
    • Audit replaces the need for directors
    • Managers are always dishonest about results
    • Owners cannot easily verify the managers' reports
    • Shareholders prepare the accounts themselves
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Owners cannot easily verify the managers' reports. Applying the standard result: Audit reduces the information gap. Revise Audit, Assurance and Ethics →

Taxation

  1. Which of these is an indirect tax? (Foundation recall)
    • Corporation tax
    • Income tax
    • Inheritance tax
    • Value added tax
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Value added tax. VAT is charged by businesses and paid over to HMRC, so the person who bears it (the customer) does not pay it directly. Revise Taxation →

  2. A taxpayer relies on an HMRC manual that conflicts with the wording of the statute. What will a tribunal follow? (Exam style)
    • The HMRC manual, because HMRC wrote it
    • The most recent HMRC press release
    • The statute as interpreted by the courts
    • Whichever gives the lower tax bill
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The statute as interpreted by the courts. HMRC guidance is not law. The tribunal applies the legislation, though the guidance may matter to other remedies. Revise Taxation →

  3. A government raises tobacco duty sharply. Which description fits best? (Stretch)
    • A direct tax designed mainly to redistribute income
    • A progressive tax intended to fund health services
    • A proportional tax set to be economical to collect
    • An indirect tax used partly to discourage harmful activity
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    An indirect tax used partly to discourage harmful activity. Excise duty is collected from producers and passed on, and its high rate aims to reduce smoking as well as raise revenue. Revise Taxation →

Business and Company Law

  1. Which is a civil matter? (Foundation recall)
    • A company fined for a health and safety offence
    • A director prosecuted for fraudulent trading
    • A supplier suing a customer for an unpaid invoice
    • A trader prosecuted for insider dealing
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A supplier suing a customer for an unpaid invoice. Contract disputes between parties are civil; the others are criminal prosecutions. Revise Business and Company Law →

  2. What part of an earlier judgment binds a lower court? (Exam style)
    • The dissenting judgment
    • The judge's summary of the facts
    • The obiter dicta
    • The ratio decidendi
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The ratio decidendi. Only the legal reasoning necessary for the decision is binding. Revise Business and Company Law →

  3. A High Court judge finds that an earlier Court of Appeal case had materially different facts. What may the judge do? (Stretch)
    • Distinguish the earlier case
    • Ignore all precedent
    • Overrule the Court of Appeal
    • Refer it to Parliament
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Distinguish the earlier case. Distinguishing avoids following a precedent when facts differ materially. Revise Business and Company Law →

Finance and Financial Markets

  1. A company sells new shares to investors for the first time. This takes place in (Foundation recall)
    • The foreign exchange market
    • The money market only
    • The primary market
    • The secondary market
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The primary market. Primary markets issue new securities and raise funds. Revise Finance and Financial Markets →

  2. Why do financial intermediaries exist? (Exam style)
    • They are required to lend only to government
    • They guarantee every investment will make a profit
    • They prevent companies from issuing shares
    • They reduce transaction costs, pool risk and overcome information asymmetry
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    They reduce transaction costs, pool risk and overcome information asymmetry. Intermediaries make it cheaper and safer to match savers and borrowers. Revise Finance and Financial Markets →

  3. Why does an active secondary market help companies raise funds in the primary market? (Stretch)
    • Investors pay more for securities they know they can sell easily
    • It allows companies to avoid paying dividends
    • It lets companies receive money each time shares are traded
    • It removes all risk from holding shares
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Investors pay more for securities they know they can sell easily. Liquidity makes securities more attractive, lowering the cost of capital. Revise Finance and Financial Markets →

Accounting, Technology and Society

  1. Which transaction cycle includes invoicing customers and collecting cash from them? (Foundation recall)
    • The expenditure cycle
    • The financing cycle
    • The production cycle
    • The revenue cycle
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The revenue cycle. Applying the standard result: The revenue cycle runs from customer order to cash receipt. Revise Accounting, Technology and Society →

  2. An invoice from a supplier does not agree with the goods received note. What should a well-controlled system do? (Exam style)
    • Hold the invoice for review before payment
    • Pay the invoice and correct the ledger later
    • Post the invoice to the suspense account and pay it
    • Reduce the purchase order to match the invoice automatically
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Hold the invoice for review before payment. Three-way matching exists to stop payment for goods not received or not ordered. Revise Accounting, Technology and Society →

  3. After an ERP implementation, one clerk can create suppliers, approve invoices and release payments. What is the main risk? (Stretch)
    • Higher depreciation on the new software licence
    • Inability to produce a trial balance from the ledger
    • Lost segregation of duties allowing fictitious supplier fraud
    • Slower month-end close because of duplicated data entry
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Lost segregation of duties allowing fictitious supplier fraud. One person controlling the whole payment process could set up and pay a false supplier. Revise Accounting, Technology and Society →
