Dentistry practice questions

36 questions from Gradify's free Dentistry course, with the answer and why behind each reveal. Answer before you open it: that's what makes it stick.

Oral and Head and Neck Anatomy

  1. Which of these bones is part of the viscerocranium rather than the neurocranium? (Foundation recall)
    • Ethmoid bone
    • Palatine bone
    • Parietal bone
    • Sphenoid bone
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Palatine bone. The palatine bones form the posterior hard palate and are facial bones; the sphenoid, ethmoid and parietal bones contribute to the braincase. Revise Oral and Head and Neck Anatomy →

  2. A patient has numbness of the lower lip and chin after a lesion at the skull base. Through which opening does the affected nerve trunk leave the skull? (Exam style)
    • Foramen ovale
    • Foramen rotundum
    • Stylomastoid foramen
    • Superior orbital fissure
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Foramen ovale. The mental nerve is a branch of the inferior alveolar nerve from V3, which leaves through foramen ovale; rotundum carries V2 and the stylomastoid foramen carries the motor facial nerve. Revise Oral and Head and Neck Anatomy →

  3. After a fall onto the cheek, a patient has a flattened cheekbone and numbness of the upper lip and side of the nose. What best explains the numbness? (Stretch)
    • The facial nerve has been torn at the stylomastoid foramen
    • The fracture runs through the infraorbital foramen and injures the infraorbital nerve
    • The mandibular nerve has been compressed at foramen ovale
    • The ophthalmic nerve has been cut in the superior orbital fissure
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The fracture runs through the infraorbital foramen and injures the infraorbital nerve. Zygomatic complex fractures commonly cross the orbital floor and infraorbital foramen, so the infraorbital branch of V2, which supplies the upper lip, cheek and side of nose, is injured. The facial nerve is motor to the face, not sensory. Revise Oral and Head and Neck Anatomy →

Teeth: Morphology, Development and Oral Histology

  1. In FDI notation, which tooth is 47? (Foundation recall)
    • Lower left second permanent molar
    • Lower right primary second molar
    • Lower right second permanent molar
    • Upper right second permanent molar
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Lower right second permanent molar. Quadrant 4 is lower right permanent; tooth 7 is the second molar. Primary lower right teeth are 81 to 85. Revise Teeth: Morphology, Development and Oral Histology →

  2. A referral asks for extraction of Universal tooth 30. What is it in FDI? (Exam style)
    • 16
    • 36
    • 46
    • 47
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    46. Universal 17 to 32 runs from the lower left third molar to the lower right third molar; 30 is the lower right first molar, FDI 46. Revise Teeth: Morphology, Development and Oral Histology →

  3. A record reads "extract 11" with no system stated, and a mirrored radiograph is attached. What should the dentist do before treatment? (Stretch)
    • Confirm which tooth is meant from the referrer, the clinical findings and correctly oriented radiographs
    • Extract the upper left canine, because Universal numbers are more common on radiographs
    • Extract the upper right central incisor, because FDI is the UK standard
    • Extract whichever tooth looks most damaged
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Confirm which tooth is meant from the referrer, the clinical findings and correctly oriented radiographs. "11" is the upper right central incisor in FDI but the upper left canine in Universal, and mirrored images reverse sides; the tooth must be confirmed before an irreversible procedure. Revise Teeth: Morphology, Development and Oral Histology →

Foundations of Medical Science

  1. Which organelle is the main site of ATP production by oxidative phosphorylation? (Foundation recall)
    • Golgi apparatus
    • Mitochondrion
    • Peroxisome
    • Rough endoplasmic reticulum
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Mitochondrion. Oxidative phosphorylation takes place on the inner mitochondrial membrane; the Golgi sorts proteins, the RER makes them and peroxisomes oxidise very-long-chain fatty acids. Revise Foundations of Medical Science →

  2. A drug used for gout and the vinca alkaloids used in chemotherapy share a target. What is it? (Exam style)
    • DNA topoisomerase
    • The bacterial-type ribosome
    • The Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase pump
    • Tubulin, the protein of microtubules
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Tubulin, the protein of microtubules. Colchicine and vincristine both stop microtubule polymerisation: one reduces neutrophil migration, the other arrests dividing cells in mitosis. Revise Foundations of Medical Science →

  3. A child inherits a mitochondrial DNA mutation. Which statement about the family is most accurate? (Stretch)
    • Each child of an affected parent has a 50% risk regardless of the parent's sex
    • It cannot be inherited because mitochondria are made fresh in each cell
    • It passes from father to son only, like a Y-linked gene
    • The mother passes it to all her children, but severity varies with the proportion of mutant mitochondria
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The mother passes it to all her children, but severity varies with the proportion of mutant mitochondria. Mitochondria come from the egg, so transmission is maternal; heteroplasmy (a mix of normal and mutant copies) explains the variable severity. Revise Foundations of Medical Science →

Dental Caries, Prevention and Dental Public Health

  1. Which statement best defines dental caries? (Foundation recall)
    • A bacterial infection that always leads to a cavity
    • A biofilm-mediated, sugar-driven disease of mineral loss and gain in the teeth
    • Inflammation of the gingiva caused by plaque
    • Wear of the teeth caused by acidic drinks
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A biofilm-mediated, sugar-driven disease of mineral loss and gain in the teeth. Caries is a dynamic process of demineralisation and remineralisation driven by biofilm acids from sugars; erosion is acid wear without bacteria, and gingivitis is a gum disease. Revise Dental Caries, Prevention and Dental Public Health →

  2. A white spot lesion on a dried smooth surface is chalky, rough and covered by plaque. How should it be classified? (Exam style)
    • Active, non-cavitated lesion
    • Arrested, non-cavitated lesion
    • Developmental enamel defect
    • Extensive cavitated lesion
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Active, non-cavitated lesion. Chalky, rough enamel under plaque indicates an active initial lesion; arrested lesions are shiny and hard. Revise Dental Caries, Prevention and Dental Public Health →

  3. A dentist restores every cavity in a young adult but gives no dietary or fluoride advice; two years later several new lesions have appeared. What best explains this? (Stretch)
    • Caries is genetically determined and cannot be prevented
    • Restorations treat lesions, not the disease process, which continued unchanged
    • The new lesions are developmental defects
    • The restorations caused the new lesions
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Restorations treat lesions, not the disease process, which continued unchanged. Without changing the biofilm, sugar exposure and fluoride, the imbalance driving caries persists, so new lesions form; restoration alone doesn't control the disease. Revise Dental Caries, Prevention and Dental Public Health →

Periodontology

  1. Which of these is part of the periodontium? (Foundation recall)
    • Cementum
    • Enamel
    • The dental pulp
    • The oral vestibule
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Cementum. The periodontium is gingiva, periodontal ligament, cementum and alveolar bone; enamel and pulp belong to the tooth itself. Revise Periodontology →

  2. A treated periodontitis patient has residual attachment loss but no bleeding and probing depths of 4 mm or less. How is the periodontium described? (Exam style)
    • Gingivitis on an intact periodontium
    • Healthy on a reduced periodontium
    • Healthy on an intact periodontium
    • Untreated stage IV periodontitis
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Healthy on a reduced periodontium. Past attachment loss means the periodontium is reduced, but without inflammation it can be classed as health on a reduced periodontium. Revise Periodontology →

  3. Why does a crown margin placed deep below the gingiva often lead to persistent inflammation? (Stretch)
    • It always causes immediate pulp death in the prepared tooth
    • It invades the supracrestal tissue attachment, so the tissues remain inflamed and bone may be lost to re-establish it
    • It makes the crown too short to be retained by the cement
    • It prevents saliva reaching the crown surface so it cannot be cleaned
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It invades the supracrestal tissue attachment, so the tissues remain inflamed and bone may be lost to re-establish it. Violating the zone of junctional epithelium and connective tissue attachment provokes chronic inflammation; bone may resorb until the attachment is re-formed. Revise Periodontology →

Clinical Method and Reasoning

  1. Which part of the history records the reaction a patient had to a drug? (Foundation recall)
    • Drug history, as the allergy and its reaction
    • Family history
    • Past surgical history
    • Systems review
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Drug history, as the allergy and its reaction. Allergies are documented with the drug history, always with the nature of the reaction so allergy can be distinguished from intolerance. Revise Clinical Method and Reasoning →

  2. A patient says she is "allergic to amoxicillin" because it gave her loose stools. How should this be recorded? (Exam style)
    • As a penicillin allergy, so all penicillins are avoided
    • As an intolerance (diarrhoea), not an allergy, with the reaction described
    • As anaphylaxis to be safe
    • It needn't be recorded at all
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    As an intolerance (diarrhoea), not an allergy, with the reaction described. Gastrointestinal upset is a predictable side effect, not an immune reaction. Labelling it as allergy would needlessly exclude penicillins; recording the reaction lets prescribers decide. Revise Clinical Method and Reasoning →

  3. Which question adds the most diagnostic value in a patient with a headache? (Stretch)
    • Did it reach its maximum intensity within a minute or so?
    • Do you drink coffee?
    • Have you ever had a headache before?
    • Is there anyone else at home?
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Did it reach its maximum intensity within a minute or so?. A thunderclap onset is a red flag for subarachnoid haemorrhage and changes management immediately. Discriminating questions shift probability; generic ones rarely do. Revise Clinical Method and Reasoning →

Dental Materials

  1. Which term describes the energy absorbed by a material before it fractures? (Foundation recall)
    • Creep
    • Hardness
    • Stiffness
    • Toughness
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Toughness. Toughness is the area under the stress–strain curve up to fracture. Revise Dental Materials →

  2. Why can a mismatch in thermal expansion cause leakage at a restoration margin? (Exam style)
    • Saliva becomes more acidic when cold
    • The gap opens and closes with temperature
    • The material dissolves faster in hot drinks
    • The pulp swells when heated
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The gap opens and closes with temperature. Repeated expansion and contraction pumps fluid in and out of the margin, called percolation. Revise Dental Materials →

  3. A clinician wants a posterior crown material that resists crack growth in thin sections. Which property matters most? (Stretch)
    • Fluorescence
    • Fracture toughness
    • Thermal conductivity
    • Water sorption
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Fracture toughness. Thin ceramics fail by crack propagation, so fracture toughness is the key property. Revise Dental Materials →

Dental Radiography and Radiation Protection

  1. Which process releases electrons from the filament of an X-ray tube? (Foundation recall)
    • Compton scattering
    • Photoelectric absorption
    • Radioactive decay
    • Thermionic emission
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Thermionic emission. Heating the tungsten filament releases electrons. Revise Dental Radiography and Radiation Protection →

  2. Which exposure factor mainly controls the energy of the X-ray photons? (Exam style)
    • Exposure time
    • Film speed
    • Kilovoltage
    • Tube current
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Kilovoltage. Higher kV accelerates electrons more, giving more energetic photons. Revise Dental Radiography and Radiation Protection →

  3. Why is a copper block used to hold the tungsten target? (Stretch)
    • It conducts heat away from the target
    • It filters low-energy photons
    • It focuses the electron beam
    • It produces most of the X-rays
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It conducts heat away from the target. Most electron energy becomes heat, which copper removes. Revise Dental Radiography and Radiation Protection →

Restorative Dentistry: Operative, Endodontics and Prosthodontics

  1. Which special test assesses the response of pulpal nerves? (Foundation recall)
    • Percussion
    • Periodontal probing
    • Sensibility testing
    • Tooth mobility grading
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Sensibility testing. Cold and electric tests assess pulp sensibility. Revise Restorative Dentistry: Operative, Endodontics and Prosthodontics →

  2. Which phase should come before definitive crown and bridge work? (Exam style)
    • Implant placement
    • Maintenance recall
    • Stabilisation and reassessment
    • Tooth whitening
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Stabilisation and reassessment. Disease must be controlled before definitive work. Revise Restorative Dentistry: Operative, Endodontics and Prosthodontics →

  3. A patient with uncontrolled periodontitis asks for a bridge to replace a missing incisor. What is the best initial plan? (Stretch)
    • Make the bridge now and treat gums later
    • Place an implant immediately
    • Refuse treatment entirely
    • Treat the periodontitis and reassess first
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Treat the periodontitis and reassess first. Abutment teeth need healthy support, so stabilise disease first. Revise Restorative Dentistry: Operative, Endodontics and Prosthodontics →

Paediatric Dentistry and Orthodontics

  1. Which scale is used to record a child's cooperation? (Foundation recall)
    • The BEWE
    • The Frankl scale
    • The Glasgow Coma Scale
    • The IOTN
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The Frankl scale. The Frankl scale rates behaviour from definitely negative to definitely positive. Revise Paediatric Dentistry and Orthodontics →

  2. Which technique gradually exposes a child to feared steps? (Exam style)
    • General anaesthesia
    • Modelling
    • Physical restraint
    • Systematic desensitisation
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Systematic desensitisation. Desensitisation reduces fear step by step. Revise Paediatric Dentistry and Orthodontics →

  3. A nine-year-old needs several fillings but is too anxious to accept local anaesthetic despite repeated tell-show-do. What is the best next step? (Stretch)
    • Consider inhalation sedation
    • Extract all affected teeth at once
    • Restrain the child and proceed
    • Stop all treatment permanently
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Consider inhalation sedation. Inhalation sedation can help anxious children who cannot cope otherwise. Revise Paediatric Dentistry and Orthodontics →

Oral Surgery, Pain and Anxiety Control

  1. Which local anaesthetic is most widely used in UK dental cartridges? (Foundation recall)
    • Benzocaine 20% gel for injection
    • Bupivacaine 0.5% for every procedure
    • Cocaine solution for infiltration
    • Lidocaine 2% with 1:80,000 adrenaline
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Lidocaine 2% with 1:80,000 adrenaline. Lidocaine with adrenaline is the standard UK dental cartridge. Revise Oral Surgery, Pain and Anxiety Control →

  2. Why is a vasoconstrictor added to local anaesthetic? (Exam style)
    • It makes the injection itself completely painless for the patient
    • It prolongs anaesthesia, reduces bleeding and lowers systemic toxicity
    • It speeds up liver metabolism of the drug
    • It turns an amide into an ester
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It prolongs anaesthesia, reduces bleeding and lowers systemic toxicity. Reduced blood flow keeps the drug at the site longer. Revise Oral Surgery, Pain and Anxiety Control →

  3. Which agent is most associated with methaemoglobinaemia at high doses? (Stretch)
    • Articaine
    • Lidocaine
    • Mepivacaine
    • Prilocaine
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Prilocaine. A metabolite of prilocaine can oxidise haemoglobin. Revise Oral Surgery, Pain and Anxiety Control →

Professionalism, Ethics and Law

  1. Which law established the GDC's current framework? (Foundation recall)
    • The Children Act 1989
    • The Data Protection Act 2018
    • The Dentists Act 1984
    • The Equality Act 2010
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The Dentists Act 1984. The Dentists Act 1984 sets out the GDC's role and powers. Revise Professionalism, Ethics and Law →

  2. Who must follow the Standards for the Dental Team? (Exam style)
    • All GDC registrants, including dental care professionals
    • Only dental students before qualifying
    • Only dentists who own a practice
    • Only hospital consultants
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    All GDC registrants, including dental care professionals. The standards apply to the whole registered team. Revise Professionalism, Ethics and Law →

  3. Which principle applies when a dental nurse sees a colleague working while impaired by alcohol? (Stretch)
    • Communicate effectively with patients
    • Have a clear complaints procedure
    • Maintain and protect patients' information
    • Raise concerns if patients are at risk
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Raise concerns if patients are at risk. Principle 8 requires action when patients are at risk. Revise Professionalism, Ethics and Law →
