Engineering Mathematics

  1. Which is the pascal written in SI base units? (Foundation recall)
    • kg m s-2
    • kg m-1 s-2
    • kg m2 s-2
    • kg m2 s-3
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    kg m-1 s-2. A pascal is a newton (kg m s-2) per square metre, giving kg m-1 s-2. The distractors are the newton, joule and watt. Revise Engineering Mathematics →

  2. Which of these equations of motion is dimensionally consistent (s distance, u and v speeds, a acceleration, t time)? (Exam style)
    • s = ut + ½at3
    • v = u + as
    • v2 = u + 2at
    • v2 = u2 + 2as
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    v2 = u2 + 2as. Each term of v2 = u2 + 2as has dimension L2 T-2. In s = ut + ½at3 the last term is L T, and in v = u + as the last term is L2 T-2, so neither balances. Revise Engineering Mathematics →

  3. A student derives a formula for the deflection of a beam and finds it is dimensionally homogeneous. What can she conclude? (Stretch)
    • Only that it may be correct: its numerical constant and any missing dimensionless group are untested
    • That it is correct in SI units but may fail in other unit systems
    • That it is correct, because every term has the same dimensions
    • That the exponents are right but the constant may be wrong, since dimensions fix all exponents
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Only that it may be correct: its numerical constant and any missing dimensionless group are untested. Dimensional homogeneity is necessary, not sufficient. When more variables are involved than dimensions, exponents are not fully fixed either, and a homogeneous equation holds in any consistent unit system. Revise Engineering Mathematics →

Statics

  1. A 100 N force acts at 90° to the x-axis. What is its x-component? (Foundation recall)
    • 0 N
    • 100 N
    • 50 N
    • 70.7 N
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    0 N. cos 90° = 0, so a force along the y-axis has no x-component. Revise Statics →

  2. Forces of 6 kN along +x and 8 kN along −y act at a point. What is the resultant? (Exam style)
    • 10 kN at 36.9° above the x-axis
    • 10 kN at 53.1° below the x-axis
    • 14 kN along the x-axis
    • 2 kN at 45° below the x-axis
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    10 kN at 53.1° below the x-axis. R = √(62 + 82) = 10 kN; tan-1(8/6) = 53.1°, below the axis because the y-component is negative. Revise Statics →

  3. A cable runs from A(0, 0, 0) to B(2, 3, 6) m and carries 700 N. What is the force on A? (Stretch)
    • 100i + 150j + 300k N
    • 200i + 300j + 600k N
    • 2i + 3j + 6k N
    • 700i + 700j + 700k N
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    200i + 300j + 600k N. Length AB = √(4 + 9 + 36) = 7 m; unit vector (2/7, 3/7, 6/7); F = 700 × that = (200, 300, 600) N. Revise Statics →

Dynamics

  1. A body starts from rest with a = 2 m/s2. What is its speed after 5 s? (Foundation recall)
    • 10 m/s
    • 2.5 m/s
    • 25 m/s
    • 7 m/s
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    10 m/s. With constant acceleration, v = u + at = 0 + 2 × 5 = 10 m/s. Revise Dynamics →

  2. A train accelerates uniformly from 10 m/s to 30 m/s over 400 m. What is its acceleration? (Exam style)
    • 0.05 m/s2
    • 0.5 m/s2
    • 1.0 m/s2
    • 2.0 m/s2
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    1.0 m/s2. v2 = u2 + 2as: 900 = 100 + 800a, so a = 1.0 m/s2. Revise Dynamics →

  3. A particle's acceleration is a = 6t m/s2, starting from rest at the origin. What is its displacement at t = 2 s? (Stretch)
    • 12 m
    • 24 m
    • 6 m
    • 8 m
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    8 m. Integrate: v = 3t2, s = t3; at t = 2, s = 8 m. The constant-acceleration equations don't apply. Revise Dynamics →

Mechanics of Materials

  1. A 2,000 N force acts on an area of 40 mm2. What is the stress? (Foundation recall)
    • 0.02 MPa
    • 2,040 MPa
    • 50 MPa
    • 80,000 MPa
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    50 MPa. Stress = F/A = 2,000 ÷ 40 = 50 N/mm2, which is 50 MPa. Revise Mechanics of Materials →

  2. A 2 m rod extends by 1.2 mm under load. What is the strain? (Exam style)
    • 0.0006
    • 0.0024
    • 0.6
    • 1.67
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    0.0006. Strain = δ/L = 1.2 ÷ 2,000 = 0.0006, or 600 microstrain. Revise Mechanics of Materials →

  3. A tensile test specimen necks before fracture. Why does engineering stress fall while true stress keeps rising? (Stretch)
    • Engineering stress uses the original area, but the actual area shrinks rapidly at the neck
    • The load increases sharply during necking
    • The material becomes stronger as it necks
    • True stress ignores the cross-sectional area
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Engineering stress uses the original area, but the actual area shrinks rapidly at the neck. Engineering stress divides load by the original area; true stress uses the smaller, actual area. Revise Mechanics of Materials →

Materials Science

  1. Which bond type involves a "sea" of shared electrons? (Foundation recall)
    • Covalent bonding
    • Hydrogen bonding
    • Ionic bonding
    • Metallic bonding
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Metallic bonding. Metals share valence electrons among all ion cores. Revise Materials Science →

  2. Why are most ceramics electrical insulators? (Exam style)
    • Their atoms are held only by van der Waals forces
    • Their ionic and covalent bonds hold electrons in place
    • They contain a sea of free electrons
    • They have very low melting points
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Their ionic and covalent bonds hold electrons in place. Without free electrons, charge cannot flow easily. Revise Materials Science →

  3. A designer needs a material with high stiffness and a very high melting point for a furnace lining. Which bond type suggests a suitable class? (Stretch)
    • Hydrogen bonding, as in ice
    • Metallic bonding in low-melting alloys
    • Strong ionic and covalent bonding, as in ceramics
    • Weak van der Waals bonding, as in thermoplastics
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Strong ionic and covalent bonding, as in ceramics. Ceramics combine stiffness with high melting points. Revise Materials Science →

Thermodynamics

  1. Which is an intensive property? (Foundation recall)
    • Temperature
    • Total internal energy
    • Total mass
    • Total volume
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Temperature. Temperature does not depend on the amount of matter. Revise Thermodynamics →

  2. A gauge reads 300 kPa where atmospheric pressure is 100 kPa. Which pressure goes into thermodynamic equations? (Exam style)
    • 100 kPa atmospheric
    • 200 kPa difference
    • 300 kPa gauge
    • 400 kPa absolute
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    400 kPa absolute. Absolute pressure is gauge plus atmospheric pressure. Revise Thermodynamics →

  3. Why can a quasi-equilibrium process be drawn as a line on a p–V diagram? (Stretch)
    • Each point along it is an equilibrium state with defined properties
    • Only cycles can be plotted on such diagrams
    • Pressure stays exactly constant in every such process
    • The process happens too fast to have any states
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Each point along it is an equilibrium state with defined properties. Non-equilibrium states have no single values of p and V. Revise Thermodynamics →

Fluid Mechanics

  1. What is the specific gravity of mercury? (Foundation recall)
    • About 0.85
    • About 1.0
    • About 13.6
    • About 136
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    About 13.6. Applying the standard result: Mercury is 13.6 times denser than water. Revise Fluid Mechanics →

  2. Which fluid is non-Newtonian? (Exam style)
    • Air
    • Petrol
    • Toothpaste
    • Water
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Toothpaste. Applying the standard result: Its viscosity depends on shear rate. Revise Fluid Mechanics →

  3. The gap in an oil film halves while the plate speed is unchanged. What happens to the shear stress? (Stretch)
    • It doubles
    • It halves
    • It quadruples
    • It stays the same
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It doubles. Applying the standard result: du/dy doubles, so τ doubles. Revise Fluid Mechanics →

Electrical Circuits and Electronics

  1. What is the SI unit of electric charge? (Foundation recall)
    • The ampere
    • The coulomb
    • The volt
    • The watt
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The coulomb. Charge is measured in coulombs; the ampere is one coulomb per second. Revise Electrical Circuits and Electronics →

  2. A current of 2 A flows for 30 s. How much charge passes? (Exam style)
    • 0.067 C
    • 15 C
    • 32 C
    • 60 C
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    60 C. Charge is current multiplied by time: Q = It = 2 × 30 = 60 C. Revise Electrical Circuits and Electronics →

  3. Current of 2 A leaves the positive terminal of a 12 V element. What is happening? (Stretch)
    • It absorbs 24 W
    • It absorbs 6 W
    • It delivers 24 W
    • It delivers 6 W
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It delivers 24 W. Current leaving the positive terminal means the element is a source delivering P = VI = 24 W. Revise Electrical Circuits and Electronics →

Control Systems and Signals

  1. Which is an example of closed-loop control? (Foundation recall)
    • A tap opened to a fixed position
    • A thermostat controlling a heater
    • A toaster set on a timer
    • A washing machine on a fixed programme
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A thermostat controlling a heater. The thermostat measures temperature and adjusts the heater. Revise Control Systems and Signals →

  2. In a feedback loop, what does the sensor do? (Exam style)
    • Applies power to the plant
    • Generates the reference signal
    • Measures the output and feeds it back
    • Removes all disturbances
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Measures the output and feeds it back. The sensor provides the measured output for comparison. Revise Control Systems and Signals →

  3. Why might making a controller more aggressive be undesirable? (Stretch)
    • It always makes the system slower
    • It can increase overshoot and reduce stability margins
    • It reduces the accuracy to zero
    • It removes the need for a sensor
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It can increase overshoot and reduce stability margins. Faster response often trades off against overshoot and robustness. Revise Control Systems and Signals →

Design and Manufacturing

  1. Which stage produces full drawings with tolerances? (Foundation recall)
    • Conceptual design
    • Detail design
    • Market research
    • Problem definition
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Detail design. Detail design produces the information needed to manufacture each part. Revise Design and Manufacturing →

  2. A prototype fails a test and the team changes the concept. This illustrates (Exam style)
    • Detail design only
    • Final production
    • Iteration in design
    • Validation of marketing
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Iteration in design. Design loops back to earlier stages when problems are found. Revise Design and Manufacturing →

  3. Why should engineers spend effort on early design decisions? (Stretch)
    • Early decisions commit most of the life-cycle cost, and changes later are expensive
    • Early design stages are where most of the money is actually spent
    • Late changes in production cost nothing to make
    • Manufacturing choices have no effect on product cost
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Early decisions commit most of the life-cycle cost, and changes later are expensive. Concept and material choices lock in most of the cost. Revise Design and Manufacturing →

Engineering in Society

  1. Which of these is one of the four principles in the Statement of Ethical Principles? (Foundation recall)
    • Accuracy and rigour
    • Loyalty to the employer above all
    • Profitability and growth
    • Speed and efficiency in delivery
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Accuracy and rigour. Accuracy and rigour means working within competence and using reliable evidence. Revise Engineering in Society →

  2. How do the professional engineering institutions use the Statement of Ethical Principles? (Exam style)
    • Their codes of conduct are built on it
    • They apply it only to chartered engineers working abroad
    • They replace it with legally binding contracts
    • They treat it as optional advice for students only
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Their codes of conduct are built on it. Each institution's code of conduct draws on the joint statement and is binding on members. Revise Engineering in Society →

  3. An engineer is offered an expensive gift by a supplier bidding for a contract she will assess. Which response best follows the principles? (Stretch)
    • Accept it as long as the value is kept secret
    • Accept it but score the bid fairly anyway
    • Decline the gift and declare the approach
    • Pass the gift to a colleague on the panel instead
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Decline the gift and declare the approach. Honesty and integrity require rejecting inducements and declaring possible conflicts of interest. Revise Engineering in Society →

Projects and Professional Practice

  1. Which of these best describes a project? (Foundation recall)
    • A permanent department within a company
    • A routine process repeated every day without end
    • A single meeting with a client to agree a price
    • A temporary undertaking to deliver a unique result
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A temporary undertaking to deliver a unique result. Projects are temporary and unique, unlike ongoing operations. Revise Projects and Professional Practice →

  2. A client adds three features without extending the deadline or budget. What is the most likely result? (Exam style)
    • No effect, because scope does not affect cost
    • Pressure on quality, or overruns in time or cost
    • The project automatically finishes early
    • The work breakdown structure becomes unnecessary
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Pressure on quality, or overruns in time or cost. Scope, time and cost are linked, so extra scope must be paid for somewhere. Revise Projects and Professional Practice →

  3. Which is the best example of a SMART objective for a design project? (Stretch)
    • Do the best possible job on the bracket design
    • Improve everything about the bracket eventually
    • Make the bracket much better than before
    • Reduce bracket mass by 15% by the end of March
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Reduce bracket mass by 15% by the end of March. It is specific, measurable, achievable, relevant and time-bound. Revise Projects and Professional Practice →
