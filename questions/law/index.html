Legal Foundations and the English Legal System

  1. Which statement best describes legal positivism? (Foundation recall)
    • A rule that is unjust cannot be law
    • Law and custom are the same thing
    • Law depends on public opinion polls
    • Law is identified by its source, whatever its moral content
    • Law is only what judges do in practice
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Law is identified by its source, whatever its moral content. Positivists separate the question "is it law?" from "is it good law?"; natural lawyers connect them. Revise Legal Foundations and the English Legal System →

  2. A client says, "Breaking my promise to help a friend move house must be illegal because it's wrong." What is the best response? (Exam style)
    • A social promise is usually unenforceable because there is no intention to create legal relations
    • Any promise is enforceable as a contract if the other person relied on it at all
    • Breaking a promise is a moral wrong, so it is also a civil wrong in tort
    • The promise is binding because consideration is never needed between friends
    • The promise is enforceable because it was made to a friend, not a stranger
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A social promise is usually unenforceable because there is no intention to create legal relations. Contract law requires, among other things, an intention to create legal relations, which is presumed absent in social and domestic arrangements (Balfour v Balfour [1919]). Revise Legal Foundations and the English Legal System →

  3. Delegated legislation conflicts with the Act under which it was made. What follows? (Stretch)
    • Both stand until Parliament repeals one
    • Only the minister can resolve the conflict
    • The courts must apply both equally
    • The delegated legislation can be declared ultra vires and invalid
    • The delegated legislation prevails because it is more recent
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The delegated legislation can be declared ultra vires and invalid. Delegated legislation is valid only within the powers granted by its parent Act; courts can quash it on judicial review. Revise Legal Foundations and the English Legal System →

Constitutional and Administrative Law

  1. Which is an authoritative work on parliamentary procedure? (Foundation recall)
    • Blackstone's Statutes
    • Erskine May
    • Hansard Society report
    • The Cabinet Office manual on catering
    • The Highway Code
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Erskine May. Erskine May's Parliamentary Practice is the leading work on procedure. Revise Constitutional and Administrative Law →

  2. Why is the UK constitution described as flexible? (Exam style)
    • It can be changed by ordinary legislation
    • It can only be changed by the courts
    • It changes with each monarch
    • It has no rules at all
    • It needs a referendum to change
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    It can be changed by ordinary legislation. No special amendment procedure is required. Revise Constitutional and Administrative Law →

  3. An Act states that decisions of a tribunal "shall not be called in question in any court". A claimant says the tribunal made an error of law. What approach might the courts take? (Stretch)
    • Always give full effect
    • Ignore the error
    • Read the ouster narrowly
    • Refer it to Parliament
    • Strike down the Act
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Read the ouster narrowly. Anisminic and Privacy International show courts read ouster clauses narrowly. Revise Constitutional and Administrative Law →

Contract Law

  1. Which contract must be in writing and signed by both parties? (Foundation recall)
    • A contract for the sale of an interest in land
    • A contract of employment
    • A contract to buy a car
    • A contract to buy groceries
    • A contract to hire a builder
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A contract for the sale of an interest in land. LP(MP)A 1989, s 2 requires land contracts to be in writing, incorporating all terms, signed by both parties. Revise Contract Law →

  2. A client says, "I didn't really mean to agree, so there's no contract." What principle answers this? (Exam style)
    • Any party may withdraw if they didn't mean it
    • Contracts bind only once they are notarised
    • Only signed written contracts are binding
    • The objective approach of the reasonable person
    • The parties' secret, subjective intentions
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The objective approach of the reasonable person. English law judges agreement objectively (Smith v Hughes). Revise Contract Law →

  3. A promises £500 to anyone who completes a marathon in a set week. B has run 20 miles when A announces the offer is withdrawn. What is the likely position? (Stretch)
    • A can revoke at any time before completion
    • A cannot revoke once B has started performance of a unilateral offer
    • B can claim £500 immediately
    • B must have written to A to accept
    • There was never an offer because it was made to the world
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A cannot revoke once B has started performance of a unilateral offer. In unilateral contracts, the offeror cannot revoke once the offeree has begun performance (Errington; Daulia v Four Millbank). Revise Contract Law →

Tort Law

  1. Which tort is actionable without proof of damage? (Foundation recall)
    • Negligence
    • Occupiers' liability
    • Private nuisance
    • Product liability
    • Trespass to the person
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Trespass to the person. Trespass torts are actionable per se, vindicating rights even without loss. Revise Tort Law →

  2. A client wants to sue a surveyor they hired for a negligent report. What is the best analysis? (Exam style)
    • They can only sue the surveyor in contract
    • They can only sue the surveyor in tort
    • They can't sue a professional at all
    • They may sue in contract and in tort
    • They must bring a private prosecution
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    They may sue in contract and in tort. Concurrent liability lets a claimant choose the more advantageous route. Revise Tort Law →

  3. A hospital negligently delays treatment; even with prompt treatment the patient had a 25% chance of a full recovery. What is the likely position in English law? (Stretch)
    • Full damages for the lost outcome
    • No recovery: causation isn't proved on balance
    • Recovery in contract instead of tort
    • Recovery of 25% of the full damages
    • Recovery under the Fairchild exception
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    No recovery: causation isn't proved on balance. Loss of a chance under 50% is not recoverable in clinical negligence. Revise Tort Law →

Criminal Law

  1. Who bears the legal burden of proving the defendant's guilt? (Foundation recall)
    • The defendant
    • The judge
    • The jury foreman
    • The prosecution
    • The victim
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The prosecution. Woolmington v DPP: the prosecution must prove guilt beyond reasonable doubt, subject to limited exceptions. Revise Criminal Law →

  2. A defendant raises self-defence and adduces some evidence of it. What must happen next? (Exam style)
    • The defendant must prove it fully
    • The defendant must prove it on balance
    • The judge must withdraw the charge
    • The jury must assume it succeeds
    • The prosecution must disprove it
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The prosecution must disprove it. Once the evidential burden is met, the prosecution must disprove the defence beyond reasonable doubt. Revise Criminal Law →

  3. D throws a stone at V, misses and breaks a window behind V. Can D's intention to injure V be used for criminal damage to the window? (Stretch)
    • No, because no one was hurt
    • No: malice transfers only within the same offence
    • Yes, because D acted unlawfully
    • Yes, if the window was V's
    • Yes: transferred malice always applies
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    No: malice transfers only within the same offence. R v Pembliton (1874): intent to injure a person doesn't transfer to property damage. Revise Criminal Law →

    • The Crown Prosecution Service

    • The police alone
    • The victim

    • Full silence with no record of reasons

Land Law

  1. Which statute reduced the number of legal estates to two? (Foundation recall)
    • Land Charges Act 1972
    • Land Registration Act 2002
    • Law of Property Act 1925
    • Settled Land Act 1925
    • TOLATA 1996
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Law of Property Act 1925. Section 1 of the LPA 1925 limits legal estates to the fee simple and the term of years. Revise Land Law →

  2. A neighbour lets you cross her field as a favour. She sells the field. Is the buyer bound? (Exam style)
    • Only if the buyer is a relative
    • Probably not: a bare licence is personal
    • Yes, because it's in writing
    • Yes, if you used it for a year
    • Yes: all rights over land bind buyers
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Probably not: a bare licence is personal. A bare licence is a personal permission that doesn't bind a successor. Revise Land Law →

  3. H, the sole registered owner, mortgages the house to a bank. W contributed to the price and lives there. Is W's interest overreached? (Stretch)
    • No, because W is married to H
    • No: only one trustee received the money
    • Only if W signed
    • Yes: overreaching always applies
    • Yes: the bank paid the owner
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    No: only one trustee received the money. Boland: with a sole trustee, there was no overreaching, so W's interest bound the bank. Revise Land Law →

    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

  2. A sale is in a chain of four. The seller's onward purchase is delayed. What is the practical effect? (Exam style)
    • Exchange may need to wait for the whole chain
    • Only the estate agent is affected
    • The buyer must complete first
    • The chain is broken automatically
    • The sale proceeds regardless
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Exchange may need to wait for the whole chain. Chains need synchronised exchange, so delay affects everyone. Revise Property Law and Practice →

  3. A seller of an empty, mortgage-free flat insists on completing within a week and will only communicate by email. What should the seller's solicitor do? (Stretch)
    • Ask the buyer's solicitor to check
    • Nothing: email instructions are normal
    • Proceed quickly to keep the client happy
    • Rely on the estate agent's checks
    • Treat it as high risk and verify identity rigorously
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Treat it as high risk and verify identity rigorously. These are classic warning signs of identity fraud. Revise Property Law and Practice →

Equity and Trusts

  1. Which statute now provides that equity prevails over the common law where they conflict? (Foundation recall)
    • Charities Act 2011, s 3
    • Law of Property Act 1925, s 1
    • Senior Courts Act 1981, s 49
    • Supreme Court of Judicature Act 1873, s 25 (repealed)
    • Trustee Act 2000, s 1
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Senior Courts Act 1981, s 49. Section 49 continues the Judicature Acts rule. Revise Equity and Trusts →

  2. What is the "two streams" view of fusion? (Exam style)
    • Equity applies only in Chancery
    • Equity was abolished
    • Law and equity became one body of rules
    • Law and equity remain distinct, administered together
    • The common law was abolished
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Law and equity remain distinct, administered together. The two streams view says only administration was fused. Revise Equity and Trusts →

  3. A company borrows money "solely to pay employees' wages" and goes into liquidation before paying them. Who is entitled to the money? (Stretch)
    • The company's shareholders
    • The employees automatically
    • The general creditors
    • The lender, under a Quistclose trust
    • The liquidator personally
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The lender, under a Quistclose trust. The specific purpose failed, so the money is held on trust for the lender. Revise Equity and Trusts →

Wills, Probate and Administration of Estates

  1. How does a beneficial joint tenant's share pass on death? (Foundation recall)
    • By survivorship to the other joint tenants
    • To the Crown
    • To their personal representatives
    • Under the intestacy rules
    • Under their will
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    By survivorship to the other joint tenants. Survivorship takes priority over the will. Revise Wills, Probate and Administration of Estates →

  2. A deceased left a will, a home owned jointly with her husband as joint tenants, and a life policy written in trust for her children. Which pass under the will? (Exam style)
    • Both
    • Everything, because there's a will
    • Neither the home nor the policy
    • Only the home
    • Only the policy
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Neither the home nor the policy. The home passes by survivorship and the policy to the trustees. Revise Wills, Probate and Administration of Estates →

  3. An intestate leaves a wife, two adult children and an estate of £600,000 (excluding chattels). Roughly how is it divided? (Stretch)
    • Children take everything
    • Equal thirds
    • Wife takes everything
    • Wife takes half; children take half
    • Wife: £322,000 plus half the rest; children share half the rest
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Wife: £322,000 plus half the rest; children share half the rest. With issue, the spouse takes the statutory legacy and half the residue. Revise Wills, Probate and Administration of Estates →

Business Law and Practice

  1. Which structure gives separate legal personality but is taxed like a partnership? (Foundation recall)
    • A general partnership
    • A limited liability partnership
    • A limited partnership
    • A private limited company
    • A sole trader
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A limited liability partnership. An LLP is a body corporate but tax-transparent. Revise Business Law and Practice →

  2. A client wants to start a small business with a friend, protect personal assets and keep things simple. Which structure is usually most suitable? (Exam style)
    • A general partnership
    • A private limited company
    • A public limited company
    • An unincorporated association
    • Two sole traders
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    A private limited company. A private company gives limited liability with modest formality. Revise Business Law and Practice →

  3. A former employee bound by a non-compete covenant sets up a company owned by his wife to compete. What may the court do? (Stretch)
    • Fine the wife personally
    • Nothing: the company is separate
    • Only award damages against the company
    • Pierce the veil and restrain both
    • Wind up the company automatically
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Pierce the veil and restrain both. Gilford Motor v Horne: the company was a device to evade his existing obligation. Revise Business Law and Practice →

    • Small claims track
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

  2. A party ignores court directions, causing delay and expense. Which part of the overriding objective is most relevant? (Exam style)
    • Allotting resources to other cases only
    • Enforcing compliance with rules and orders
    • Ensuring equal footing
    • None: compliance is optional
    • Promoting ADR only
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Enforcing compliance with rules and orders. CPR 1.1(2)(f) includes enforcing compliance with rules, practice directions and orders. Revise Dispute Resolution and Civil Litigation →

  3. Particulars are served on 1 March. The defendant files an acknowledgment of service. When is the defence due, ignoring extensions? (Stretch)

    • 14 days after service
    • 21 days after service

    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    28 days after service, around 29 March. CPR 15.4: 28 days after service of the particulars where an acknowledgment is filed. Revise Dispute Resolution and Civil Litigation →

European Union and International Law

  1. When did the UK leave the EU? (Foundation recall)
    • 1 January 2021
    • 23 June 2016
    • 29 March 2019
    • 31 December 2020
    • 31 January 2020
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    31 January 2020. The UK left on 31 January 2020; the transition period ended on 31 December 2020. Revise European Union and International Law →

  2. The EU wants to legislate on an issue in an area of shared competence that member states can handle well alone. Which principle is engaged? (Exam style)
    • Direct effect
    • Mutual recognition
    • State liability
    • Subsidiarity
    • Supremacy
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Subsidiarity. Subsidiarity limits EU action where member states can achieve the objective. Revise European Union and International Law →

  3. The deadline for implementing a directive passes and a member state hasn't transposed it. What can individuals do? (Stretch)

    • Rely on its clear provisions against the state
    • Sue private companies directly
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Rely on its clear provisions against the state. Vertical direct effect allows reliance against the state after the deadline. Revise European Union and International Law →

Legal Ethics and Professional Conduct

  1. Why do solicitors owe duties beyond those to their clients? (Foundation recall)
    • Clients pay less than the public does
    • The SRA directly employs all solicitors
    • Their client contracts require it
    • They are officers of the court
    • They don't: their duties are only to clients
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    They are officers of the court. Solicitors' role in the justice system brings wider duties. Revise Legal Ethics and Professional Conduct →

  2. A client instructs a solicitor to present evidence the solicitor knows is false. Which duty prevails? (Exam style)
    • The duty not to mislead the court
    • The duty of confidentiality
    • The duty to act in the client's best interests
    • The duty to follow instructions
    • The duty to maximise fees
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    The duty not to mislead the court. Duties to the court take precedence over client instructions. Revise Legal Ethics and Professional Conduct →

  3. A solicitor learns confidential financial information while acting for Company A. Company B then asks the firm to act against A in a related dispute. What is the position? (Stretch)
    • Act freely, because A is only a former client
    • Act only after asking the SRA for approval
    • Act, and tell B the confidential information
    • Act, as long as it charges B a higher fee
    • Not act unless the risk is removed or A consents
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Not act unless the risk is removed or A consents. Para 6.5: the firm holds A's confidential information material to the dispute, so it can act only with effective information barriers (the Bolkiah standard) or A's informed consent. Revise Legal Ethics and Professional Conduct →

  2. A client in Glasgow asks whether an English Court of Appeal decision on tenancy law binds their case. What is the best answer? (Exam style)

  3. What did the UK Supreme Court decide in the 2022 independence referendum reference? (Stretch)

    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

  2. A friend who has finished an LLB offers to represent you in court for a fee, calling herself a "legal adviser". What is the main concern? (Exam style)
    • Only the fee is a problem
    • Rights of audience are a reserved legal activity she isn't authorised to carry on
    • She can do so if she calls herself a solicitor
    • She must simply wear a gown
    • There is no concern: an LLB gives rights of audience
    Reveal the answer and mark-scheme reasoning

    Rights of audience are a reserved legal activity she isn't authorised to carry on. Exercising rights of audience requires authorisation under the Legal Services Act 2007. Revise Routes to Qualification →

  3. In an SQE2 interview, a candidate gives perfect legal advice but doesn't notice that the client is acting under pressure from a relative. What is the main weakness? (Stretch)

    Missing a professional conduct issue. Ethics is assessed pervasively; identifying whose instructions you take is essential. Revise Routes to Qualification →
