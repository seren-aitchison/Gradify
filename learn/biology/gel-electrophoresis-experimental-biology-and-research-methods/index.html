Gel Electrophoresis

Agarose gels are poured with wells at the negative end, loaded with samples in loading dye, run and stained; sizes are estimated from a calibration curve of log size against distance, and smears or missing bands indicate problems.

Biology · Experimental Biology and Research Methods · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Prepare and run an agarose gel safely
  • Estimate fragment sizes using a calibration curve
  • Identify common problems in gel results

Running a gel

  • Agarose is dissolved in buffer by heating, cooled and poured with a comb to form wells.
  • Samples are mixed with loading dye and pipetted into wells.
  • Wells are placed at the negative end so DNA runs towards the positive electrode.
  • Gels are stained with a DNA dye; safer alternatives to ethidium bromide are now common.

Analysis and problems

  • A calibration curve plots log(fragment size) against distance travelled for the ladder.
  • Unknown sizes are read from the curve.
  • Smearing suggests degraded DNA or overloading.
  • No bands suggest failed loading, staining or reaction.

Distance travelled is roughly proportional to the logarithm of fragment size, so plotting log size gives a straight line. Running the gel too fast generates heat, distorting bands. UV light used to view some stains requires eye and skin protection.

Worked example

Using a calibration curve.

  1. Ladder bands are plotted as log(size) against distance.
  2. The points form a straight line.
  3. An unknown band travelled 32 mm.
  4. Reading from the line gives a size of about 600 bp.
Common mistakePlacing wells at the positive electrode. DNA would run off the gel in the wrong direction.

In 3 lines: Pour, load, run, stain. Log size calibration. Recognise problems.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. At which electrode end should the wells be placed?
    • The positive electrode
    • The negative electrode
    • Either electrode end
    • In the centre only
    Show the answer

    Answer: The negative electrode. Negatively charged DNA moves away from the negative end.

  2. Why is log(size) plotted against distance for a calibration curve?
    • Size and distance are always equal
    • Logarithms make bands brighter
    • Migration is roughly linear with the logarithm of size
    • It removes the need for a ladder
    Show the answer

    Answer: Migration is roughly linear with the logarithm of size. Log transformation gives a straight-line relationship.

  3. Why should a gel not be run at very high voltage?
    • DNA stops moving at high voltage
    • High voltage reverses DNA direction
    • It makes the ladder invisible
    • Excess heat distorts bands and can melt the gel
    Show the answer

    Answer: Excess heat distorts bands and can melt the gel. Excess heat distorts bands and can even melt the agarose gel.

Key terms

Loading dye
A dense, coloured solution helping load and track samples
Calibration curve
A graph used to estimate unknown values from standards
Smearing
A blurred trail indicating degraded or overloaded DNA
