Bacterial Growth

Bacteria multiply by binary fission with generation times as short as about 20 minutes; growth curves have lag, log, stationary and death phases, numbers can be calculated as N × 2ⁿ and conditions such as temperature affect growth.

Biology · Microbiology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe binary fission
  • Describe the phases of a bacterial growth curve
  • Calculate bacterial population growth

Binary fission

  • A bacterium copies its chromosome and divides into two identical cells.
  • Generation time: the time for a population to double.
  • E. coli can divide about every 20 minutes in ideal conditions.
  • Population after n generations = initial number × 2ⁿ.

Growth curve

  • Lag phase: cells adjust and make enzymes; little division.
  • Log (exponential) phase: rapid doubling.
  • Stationary phase: growth equals death as nutrients run out and wastes build up.
  • Death phase: more cells die than divide.

Growth is affected by temperature, pH, nutrients, oxygen and water availability. Because growth is exponential, numbers are often plotted on a logarithmic scale. Food is refrigerated because low temperatures slow growth, and the "danger zone" for food is often given as 8–63 °C in the UK.

Worked example

Calculating growth.

  1. A culture starts with 100 bacteria.
  2. Generation time is 20 minutes.
  3. After 2 hours there have been 6 generations.
  4. Population = 100 × 2⁶ = 6,400 bacteria.
Common mistakeThinking bacteria keep growing exponentially forever. Nutrients run out and wastes accumulate, causing stationary and death phases.

In 3 lines: Binary fission. Four growth phases. N × 2ⁿ.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. In which phase do bacteria divide most rapidly?
    • Lag phase
    • Stationary phase
    • Log phase
    • Death phase
    Show the answer

    Answer: Log phase. Exponential growth occurs in the log phase.

  2. Why does growth enter the stationary phase?
    • Bacteria stop needing nutrients
    • All bacteria die at once
    • Temperature always drops
    • Nutrients run out and toxic wastes build up
    Show the answer

    Answer: Nutrients run out and toxic wastes build up. Limiting resources balance growth with death.

  3. Why is food stored in the fridge?
    • Low temperatures slow bacterial growth
    • Cold kills all bacteria instantly
    • Bacteria grow faster in the cold
    • Refrigeration removes nutrients
    Show the answer

    Answer: Low temperatures slow bacterial growth. Enzymes in bacteria work slowly at low temperatures, so growth slows.
