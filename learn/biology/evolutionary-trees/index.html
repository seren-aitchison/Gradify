Evolutionary Trees

Evolutionary trees have branches, nodes as common ancestors, tips as taxa and a root; relatedness depends on the most recent common ancestor, branches can rotate without changing meaning, living species are not each other's ancestors, and clades include an ancestor and all descendants.

Biology · Evolution and Natural Selection · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Identify the parts of an evolutionary tree
  • Interpret relationships from branching patterns
  • Avoid common misreadings of evolutionary trees

Parts

  • Branches represent lineages.
  • Nodes represent common ancestors where lineages split.
  • Tips represent living or extinct taxa.
  • The root represents the common ancestor of all taxa on the tree.
  • A clade is an ancestor and all its descendants.

Reading trees

  • Relatedness is shown by how recently two taxa share a common ancestor, not by how close their tips are.
  • Rotating branches at a node does not change relationships.
  • Living species at the tips are not ancestors of one another.
  • Branch lengths may represent time or amount of change in some trees.

A common misconception is reading a tree as a ladder of progress, with humans at the top. In fact all living species are equally evolved from the root. Clades are also called monophyletic groups; a group that excludes some descendants, such as traditional "reptiles" without birds, is paraphyletic. Cladograms and phylogenetic trees are the basis of modern classification.

Worked example

Who is closer?

  1. A tree shows humans, chimpanzees and gorillas.
  2. Humans and chimpanzees share a more recent common ancestor than either does with gorillas.
  3. So humans are more closely related to chimpanzees.
  4. Swapping the positions of humans and chimps at their node does not change this.
Common mistakeReading a tree as a ladder of progress. All living tips are equally descended from the root.

In 3 lines: Branches, nodes, tips, root. Most recent common ancestor shows relatedness. Not a ladder.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. On an evolutionary tree, what determines how closely two taxa are related?
    • How close their tips are on the page
    • How recently they share a common ancestor
    • Which one is drawn at the top
    • How many letters are in their names
    Show the answer

    Answer: How recently they share a common ancestor. Relatedness depends on the most recent common ancestor.

  2. What happens if branches are rotated around a node?
    • All relationships are reversed
    • The tree becomes invalid
    • The relationships stay the same
    • New species are created
    Show the answer

    Answer: The relationships stay the same. Rotation changes only the drawing, not the relationships.

  3. Why is the traditional group "reptiles" excluding birds called paraphyletic?
    • It includes all descendants
    • It contains only extinct species
    • It has no common ancestor
    • It excludes some descendants of its common ancestor
    Show the answer

    Answer: It excludes some descendants of its common ancestor. Birds descend from reptile ancestors but are left out.

Key terms

Node
A point on a tree representing a common ancestor
Clade
An ancestor and all its descendants
Paraphyletic group
A group that excludes some descendants of its common ancestor
