Incomplete Dominance

In incomplete dominance, heterozygotes have an intermediate phenotype, such as pink snapdragons, giving a 1:2:1 phenotype ratio equal to the genotype ratio, often because one allele produces half the product, and the alleles do not blend permanently.

Biology · Genetics and Molecular Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define incomplete dominance
  • Predict ratios from crosses with incomplete dominance
  • Explain incomplete dominance at the molecular level

What it is

  • Neither allele is completely dominant, so heterozygotes have an intermediate phenotype.
  • Example: snapdragons: red (CᴿCᴿ) × white (CᵂCᵂ) gives pink (CᴿCᵂ).
  • Symbols use a base letter with superscripts, not capitals and lower case.

Ratios

  • Pink × pink (CᴿCᵂ × CᴿCᵂ) gives 1 red : 2 pink : 1 white.
  • Genotype and phenotype ratios are the same, because each genotype has a distinct phenotype.
  • This differs from the 3:1 ratio of complete dominance.

At the molecular level, incomplete dominance often reflects dosage: one functional allele makes half as much pigment, giving an intermediate colour. Some human traits show partial dominance, such as familial hypercholesterolaemia, where heterozygotes have raised cholesterol and homozygotes much higher levels. Incomplete dominance does not mean the alleles blend permanently: red and white reappear in the F₂.

Worked example

A snapdragon cross.

  1. Two pink snapdragons (CᴿCᵂ) are crossed.
  2. Gametes: Cᴿ and Cᵂ from each parent.
  3. Offspring: 1 CᴿCᴿ (red) : 2 CᴿCᵂ (pink) : 1 CᵂCᵂ (white).
  4. The reappearance of red and white shows alleles remain separate.
Common mistakeThinking incomplete dominance means alleles mix permanently. Pure parental types reappear in the next generation.

In 3 lines: Heterozygote is intermediate. 1:2:1 ratio. Alleles do not blend.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is the phenotype of a CᴿCᵂ snapdragon?
    • Red flowers
    • White flowers
    • Pink flowers
    • Red and white patches
    Show the answer

    Answer: Pink flowers. Incomplete dominance gives an intermediate pink phenotype.

  2. Why does a heterozygote show an intermediate colour in many cases of incomplete dominance?
    • Both alleles are completely silenced
    • The heterozygote has no pigment gene
    • The recessive allele makes double pigment
    • One functional allele makes about half the pigment
    Show the answer

    Answer: One functional allele makes about half the pigment. The dosage of functional product sets the phenotype.

  3. What evidence shows that alleles do not blend in incomplete dominance?
    • Red and white offspring reappear from pink × pink crosses
    • All later offspring are pink forever
    • Pink plants cannot reproduce at all
    • White flowers turn pink with age
    Show the answer

    Answer: Red and white offspring reappear from pink × pink crosses. Unchanged parental phenotypes return in the F₂.

Key terms

Incomplete dominance
A heterozygote showing an intermediate phenotype
Intermediate phenotype
A phenotype between the two homozygous forms
Dosage effect
A phenotype depending on how much product is made
