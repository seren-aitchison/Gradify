Hydrogen Bonding

Hydrogen bonds form between δ+ hydrogen on O or N and lone pairs on other electronegative atoms; they are weak individually but stabilise water, protein secondary structure, DNA base pairing and cellulose, and break with heat.

Biology · Biochemistry and Biomolecules · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe how hydrogen bonds form
  • Compare hydrogen bonds with covalent and ionic bonds
  • Explain the roles of hydrogen bonds in biological molecules

What a hydrogen bond is

  • A hydrogen bond is an attraction between a δ+ hydrogen atom covalently bonded to an electronegative atom (O or N) and a lone pair on another electronegative atom.
  • Typical strength is roughly 5–30 kJ mol⁻¹, far weaker than a C–C covalent bond (about 350 kJ mol⁻¹).
  • They form and break easily at body temperature.

Roles in biology

  • Between water molecules: cohesion and high boiling point.
  • In proteins: stabilise α-helices and β-sheets.
  • In DNA: hold complementary base pairs together: A–T (two hydrogen bonds), G–C (three).
  • In cellulose: link neighbouring chains into strong microfibrils.

Because each hydrogen bond is weak, many are needed to stabilise a structure, but this also allows structures to be opened when needed. DNA strands can be separated by helicase during replication, and enzymes can change shape. Heat breaks hydrogen bonds, which is why high temperatures denature proteins and separate DNA strands in PCR. The GC content of DNA affects how much energy is needed to separate the strands.

Worked example

DNA melting.

  1. A DNA sample rich in G–C pairs needs a higher temperature to separate than one rich in A–T pairs.
  2. Each G–C pair has three hydrogen bonds; each A–T pair has two.
  3. More hydrogen bonds require more energy to break.
  4. This is used in PCR, where heating to about 95 °C separates strands.
Common mistakeCalling a hydrogen bond a covalent bond to hydrogen. It is a weaker attraction between molecules or parts of molecules.

In 3 lines: Weak but numerous. Stabilise water, proteins and DNA. Broken by heat.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Between which atoms do hydrogen bonds typically form?
    • Two carbon atoms
    • A δ+ hydrogen bonded to O or N and a lone pair on another O or N
    • Two metal ions
    • Hydrogen atoms in a hydrocarbon
    Show the answer

    Answer: A δ+ hydrogen bonded to O or N and a lone pair on another O or N. Hydrogen bonds need hydrogen attached to an electronegative atom.

  2. Why does DNA with more G–C pairs separate at a higher temperature?
    • G–C pairs are joined by covalent bonds
    • A–T pairs are not hydrogen bonded
    • G–C pairs have three hydrogen bonds rather than two
    • G–C pairs contain more phosphate
    Show the answer

    Answer: G–C pairs have three hydrogen bonds rather than two. More hydrogen bonds need more energy to break.

  3. Why is the weakness of individual hydrogen bonds an advantage in DNA?
    • It makes DNA permanently single stranded
    • It prevents mutations completely
    • It makes DNA insoluble in water
    • Strands can be separated for replication and transcription
    Show the answer

    Answer: Strands can be separated for replication and transcription. Weak bonds let enzymes open DNA when needed.

Key terms

Hydrogen bond
A weak attraction between δ+ hydrogen and a lone pair on O or N
Electronegativity
The tendency of an atom to attract shared electrons
Complementary base pairing
A–T and G–C pairing held by hydrogen bonds
