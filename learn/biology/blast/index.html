BLAST

BLAST quickly finds similar sequences using word seeds extended into local alignments; programs include blastn, blastp and blastx, and hits are judged by E-value, percentage identity, coverage and bit score.

Biology · Bioinformatics and Computational Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the purpose of BLAST
  • Distinguish the main BLAST programs
  • Interpret E-values and percentage identity

What BLAST does

  • BLAST (Basic Local Alignment Search Tool) finds similar sequences in large databases quickly.
  • It uses short word matches as seeds, then extends them into local alignments.
  • It is faster than full Smith-Waterman but may miss some matches.
  • Programs: blastn (DNA vs DNA), blastp (protein vs protein), blastx (translated DNA vs protein).

Interpreting results

  • E-value: the number of hits expected by chance; smaller is more significant.
  • E-values below about 10⁻⁵ usually indicate meaningful similarity.
  • Percentage identity and query coverage show how well sequences match.
  • Bit score measures alignment quality independent of database size.

BLAST, published in 1990, is one of the most cited scientific papers. It is used to identify unknown sequences, find homologues in other species and check primers. Very large databases increase the chance of random matches, which E-values account for.

Worked example

Identifying an unknown gene.

  1. A researcher sequences an unknown DNA fragment.
  2. blastn against the nucleotide database returns a top hit with 99% identity and E-value 0.
  3. The hit is a known bacterial gene.
  4. The fragment almost certainly comes from that gene.
Common mistakeThinking a large E-value means a strong match. Smaller E-values indicate more significant matches.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which E-value indicates the most significant match?
    • 1 × 10⁻²
    • 1 × 10⁻⁵⁰
    • 0.5
    • 10
    Show the answer

    Answer: 1 × 10⁻⁵⁰. Smaller E-values mean a match is less likely by chance.

  2. Why is BLAST faster than full Smith-Waterman alignment?
    • It ignores most sequences entirely
    • It uses no scoring
    • It starts from short word matches and extends them
    • It only compares two letters
    Show the answer

    Answer: It starts from short word matches and extends them. Seeding avoids comparing every position fully.

  3. Why do E-values depend on database size?
    • Smaller databases are always wrong
    • Database size has no effect
    • E-values measure database speed
    • Larger databases offer more chances of random matches
    Show the answer

    Answer: Larger databases offer more chances of random matches. More sequences in a database give more chances of random hits.

Key terms

BLAST
A tool searching databases for similar sequences
E-value
Expected number of chance hits with a given score
Query coverage
Proportion of the query included in an alignment
