Gastrulation

Gastrulation moves cells inward, through the primitive streak in mammals, to form three germ layers: ectoderm forming skin and nerves, mesoderm forming muscle, bone, blood and heart, and endoderm forming gut and respiratory linings and associated organs, establishing the body plan.

Biology · Cell Division and Development · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the process of gastrulation
  • Name the three germ layers and the tissues they form
  • Explain the significance of gastrulation for the body plan

Process

  • Cells move inwards from the surface of the embryo through a primitive streak in mammals and birds.
  • Cell movements include folding, migration and spreading.
  • The result is a gastrula with three germ layers.
  • Body axes become established.

Germ layers

  • Ectoderm (outer): skin epidermis and the nervous system.
  • Mesoderm (middle): muscles, bones, blood, heart, kidneys and gonads.
  • Endoderm (inner): lining of the gut and respiratory tract, liver and pancreas.
  • Animals with three germ layers are triploblastic.

The developmental biologist Lewis Wolpert remarked that "it is not birth, marriage or death, but gastrulation which is truly the most important time in your life", because it establishes the basic body plan. In humans, gastrulation occurs in the third week after fertilisation. Errors during gastrulation can cause severe defects.

Worked example

Tracing origins.

  1. The brain develops from ectoderm.
  2. The heart develops from mesoderm.
  3. The liver develops from endoderm.
  4. Gastrulation determines which layer each cell joins.
Common mistakeSaying the nervous system comes from mesoderm. It develops from ectoderm.

In 3 lines: Cells move inward. Ectoderm, mesoderm, endoderm. Sets up the body plan.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which germ layer forms the lining of the gut?
    • Ectoderm
    • Mesoderm
    • Trophoblast
    • Endoderm
    Show the answer

    Answer: Endoderm. The inner germ layer lines the gut and respiratory tract.

  2. What structure do cells move through during gastrulation in mammals?
    • The primitive streak
    • The zona pellucida
    • The neural tube
    • The placenta
    Show the answer

    Answer: The primitive streak. Cells ingress through the primitive streak.

  3. Why is gastrulation so important for development?
    • It is the stage when fertilisation happens
    • It establishes germ layers and the basic body plan
    • It forms the inner cell mass
    • It ends all cell division
    Show the answer

    Answer: It establishes germ layers and the basic body plan. Gastrulation forms the germ layers, which give rise to every tissue in the body.

Key terms

Gastrulation
The formation of germ layers in the embryo
Ectoderm
The outer germ layer, forming skin and nervous system
Endoderm
The inner germ layer, forming gut lining and associated organs
