Succession

Primary succession starts on bare ground with pioneers such as lichens and secondary succession where soil remains; communities change as conditions improve until a climax community forms, unless human activity deflects succession.

Biology · Ecology and Environmental Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Distinguish primary and secondary succession
  • Describe the stages of succession
  • Explain how human activity can halt succession

Types and stages

  • Primary succession: begins on bare ground with no soil, such as rock or sand dunes.
  • Secondary succession: begins where soil remains, such as after fire or farming.
  • Pioneer species, such as lichens and mosses, colonise first and begin forming soil.
  • Each stage changes conditions, allowing new species to establish.
  • The final stable community is the climax community, often woodland in the UK.

Changes and management

  • Soil depth, nutrients and water retention increase.
  • Biodiversity usually increases, then may fall slightly at climax.
  • Deflected succession: human activity, such as grazing or burning, prevents the climax.
  • Heathland and chalk grassland are maintained by management.

Sand dune systems show primary succession from bare sand near the sea to woodland inland, as at Studland in Dorset. Marram grass stabilises dunes and tolerates burial and drought. Conservationists sometimes deliberately halt succession to protect valuable habitats such as heathland.

Worked example

Sand dune succession.

  1. Near the shore, bare sand is colonised by sea rocket and sand couch.
  2. Marram grass stabilises the sand and dunes grow.
  3. Further inland, organic matter builds up and other grasses and shrubs establish.
  4. Eventually woodland can develop as the climax community.
Common mistakeThinking succession stops once plants arrive. It continues as each stage changes conditions for the next.

In 3 lines: Primary and secondary. Pioneers to climax. Deflected succession.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which type of succession starts on bare rock?
    • Secondary succession
    • Deflected succession
    • Climax succession
    • Primary succession
    Show the answer

    Answer: Primary succession. Primary succession begins where there is no soil.

  2. How do pioneer species allow later species to colonise?
    • They help form soil and improve conditions
    • They prevent soil forming
    • They poison the ground
    • They remove all nutrients
    Show the answer

    Answer: They help form soil and improve conditions. Weathering and decay begin building soil.

  3. Why is heathland called a deflected succession?
    • It is the natural climax everywhere
    • Grazing or burning prevents it reaching woodland
    • It has no plants at all
    • It never changes over time
    Show the answer

    Answer: Grazing or burning prevents it reaching woodland. Grazing or burning holds it at an earlier stage than woodland.

Key terms

Succession
Gradual change in a community over time
Pioneer species
The first species to colonise bare ground
Climax community
The stable final community of succession
