Types of Mutation

Substitutions can be silent, missense or nonsense; insertions and deletions not in multiples of three cause frameshifts, while repeat expansions, duplications and inversions also occur, and effects depend on type and position.

Biology · Genetics and Molecular Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe substitution, insertion and deletion mutations
  • Distinguish silent, missense and nonsense mutations
  • Explain the effects of frameshift mutations

Point mutations

  • Substitution: one base replaced by another.
  • Silent: the codon changes but codes for the same amino acid, because the code is degenerate.
  • Missense: a different amino acid is coded, which may or may not affect function, as in sickle cell anaemia.
  • Nonsense: a stop codon is created, producing a shortened protein.

Insertions, deletions and others

  • Insertion or deletion of bases not in multiples of three causes a frameshift, changing all later codons.
  • Insertions or deletions of three bases add or remove one amino acid without a frameshift.
  • Expansion of repeats: such as CAG repeats in Huntington's disease.
  • Duplication and inversion of sections also occur.

The effect of a mutation depends on its position and type. Changes in the active site of an enzyme are more likely to harm function than changes on the surface. Cystic fibrosis is most commonly caused by ΔF508, a deletion of three bases removing one phenylalanine, which causes the CFTR protein to misfold. Mutations in regulatory regions can change how much protein is made.

Worked example

Classifying substitutions.

  1. Original codon: GAG (glutamate).
  2. GAA: still glutamate, so silent.
  3. GTG: valine, so missense (the sickle cell mutation, in DNA notation).
  4. TAG: stop, so nonsense, producing a shortened protein.
Common mistakeAssuming every substitution changes the protein. Silent mutations leave the amino acid unchanged.

In 3 lines: Silent, missense, nonsense. Frameshifts change all later codons. Position matters.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which mutation creates a premature stop codon?
    • Silent
    • Missense
    • Nonsense
    • Inversion only
    Show the answer

    Answer: Nonsense. Nonsense mutations end translation early.

  2. Why does deleting one base usually have more effect than substituting one base?
    • Deletions never affect proteins
    • Substitutions always cause frameshifts
    • Deletions only affect introns
    • The deletion causes a frameshift affecting all later codons
    Show the answer

    Answer: The deletion causes a frameshift affecting all later codons. A frameshift changes many amino acids downstream.

  3. Why might a missense mutation have little effect on an enzyme?
    • It may occur away from the active site or swap a similar amino acid
    • Missense mutations never change amino acids
    • It always creates a stop codon
    • It prevents transcription
    Show the answer

    Answer: It may occur away from the active site or swap a similar amino acid. Position and chemical similarity determine impact.

Key terms

Missense mutation
A substitution changing one amino acid
Nonsense mutation
A substitution creating a stop codon
Frameshift
A shift in the reading frame from an insertion or deletion
