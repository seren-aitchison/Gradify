Antibiotics

Antibiotics are selectively toxic, targeting cell walls, ribosomes, DNA replication or metabolism; they can be bactericidal or bacteriostatic and broad- or narrow-spectrum, and sensitivity is tested by disc diffusion and MIC.

Biology · Microbiology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain the modes of action of antibiotics
  • Distinguish bactericidal and bacteriostatic antibiotics
  • Explain how antibiotic sensitivity is tested

Targets

  • Cell wall synthesis: penicillins and cephalosporins block peptidoglycan cross-linking.
  • Protein synthesis: tetracyclines and aminoglycosides target 70S ribosomes.
  • Nucleic acid synthesis: quinolones block DNA gyrase.
  • Metabolic pathways: trimethoprim blocks folate synthesis.
  • Antibiotics are selectively toxic, targeting bacterial features not found in human cells.

Types and testing

  • Bactericidal antibiotics kill bacteria; bacteriostatic ones stop them multiplying.
  • Broad-spectrum antibiotics act on many bacteria; narrow-spectrum on few.
  • Disc diffusion: discs of antibiotics on an agar lawn; larger clear zones show greater sensitivity.
  • Minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC): the lowest concentration preventing visible growth.

Many antibiotics come from microbes, such as streptomycin from Streptomyces soil bacteria. Few new classes of antibiotics have been discovered since the 1980s. Narrow-spectrum antibiotics are preferred where possible, because they cause less damage to the microbiome and less selection for resistance.

Worked example

A disc diffusion test.

  1. Bacteria from a patient are spread over an agar plate.
  2. Paper discs containing different antibiotics are placed on it.
  3. After incubation, clear zones appear around some discs.
  4. The antibiotic with the largest zone is most effective.
Common mistakeThinking antibiotics work against colds and flu. They act on bacteria, not viruses.

In 3 lines: Walls, ribosomes, DNA, metabolism. Bactericidal or bacteriostatic. Disc diffusion and MIC.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which antibiotic target is not found in human cells?
    • Phospholipid membranes
    • DNA in the nucleus
    • 80S ribosomes
    • Peptidoglycan cell walls
    Show the answer

    Answer: Peptidoglycan cell walls. Human cells have no peptidoglycan, so wall-targeting drugs are selective.

  2. In a disc diffusion test, what does a large clear zone indicate?
    • The bacteria are sensitive to that antibiotic
    • The bacteria are resistant
    • The antibiotic did not diffuse
    • The plate is contaminated
    Show the answer

    Answer: The bacteria are sensitive to that antibiotic. A larger inhibition zone means more effective killing.

  3. Why are narrow-spectrum antibiotics preferred when possible?
    • They kill more types of bacteria
    • They harm fewer beneficial bacteria and select less resistance
    • They work against viruses
    • They are always cheaper
    Show the answer

    Answer: They harm fewer beneficial bacteria and select less resistance. Targeted treatment protects the microbiome.

Key terms

Selective toxicity
Harming pathogens without harming host cells
Bactericidal
Killing bacteria
Minimum inhibitory concentration
The lowest concentration that stops visible growth
