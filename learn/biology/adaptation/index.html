Adaptation

Adaptations are inherited features improving survival and reproduction in an environment, classed as anatomical, physiological or behavioural; they arise by natural selection, convergent evolution produces similar adaptations in unrelated species, and not every feature is adaptive.

Biology · Evolution and Natural Selection · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define adaptation
  • Distinguish anatomical, physiological and behavioural adaptations
  • Explain how adaptations arise by natural selection

Types

  • Anatomical: body structures, such as thick fur in polar bears or sunken stomata in desert plants.
  • Physiological: internal processes, such as concentrated urine in desert rodents.
  • Behavioural: actions, such as migration or nocturnal activity.
  • Adaptations suit organisms to their niche.

How adaptations arise

  • Variation exists in a population.
  • Individuals with features that better suit the environment survive and reproduce more.
  • Over many generations, the feature becomes common.
  • Convergent evolution: unrelated species evolve similar adaptations to similar environments, such as streamlined shapes in sharks and dolphins.

Not every feature is an adaptation: some are by-products, results of ancestry or neutral changes. Adaptations are relative to a particular environment, so a feature useful in one setting may be harmful in another. Testing whether a trait is adaptive requires evidence, such as experiments or comparison across species.

Worked example

Desert adaptations in the fennec fox.

  1. Large ears radiate heat and help detect prey: anatomical.
  2. Kidneys produce concentrated urine to save water: physiological.
  3. Activity at night avoids daytime heat: behavioural.
  4. Together these suit it to the Sahara.
Common mistakeAssuming every feature is an adaptation. Some traits are by-products, inherited from ancestors or neutral.

In 3 lines: Anatomical, physiological, behavioural. Arise by selection. Convergence and limits.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which is a physiological adaptation?
    • Having thick white fur for camouflage
    • Migrating to warmer areas
    • Being active only at night
    • Producing very concentrated urine
    Show the answer

    Answer: Producing very concentrated urine. Internal processes such as kidney function are physiological.

  2. Why do sharks and dolphins have similar streamlined shapes?
    • Convergent evolution in similar aquatic environments
    • They are closely related fish
    • Dolphins evolved from sharks
    • Both inherited it from a shark-like ancestor
    Show the answer

    Answer: Convergent evolution in similar aquatic environments. Similar selection pressures produced similar shapes.

  3. Why might a trait not be an adaptation?
    • Every trait is always an adaptation
    • It could be a by-product, ancestral or neutral
    • Adaptations never involve genes
    • Traits cannot be inherited
    Show the answer

    Answer: It could be a by-product, ancestral or neutral. Not all features have been shaped by selection for a function.

Key terms

Adaptation
An inherited feature that increases survival and reproduction
Convergent evolution
Evolution of similar features in unrelated groups
Niche
The role and conditions of a species in its ecosystem
