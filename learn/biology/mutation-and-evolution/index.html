Mutation and Evolution

Mutation creates new alleles randomly with respect to need; most are neutral or harmful and a few beneficial, large populations and short generations speed adaptation, and gene duplication creates new functions, as shown by experiments such as Luria and Delbrück's.

Biology · Evolution and Natural Selection · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain why mutation is essential for evolution
  • Describe the range of fitness effects of mutations
  • Explain how mutation rate and population size affect evolution

Mutation as raw material

  • Mutation creates new alleles; without it, variation would eventually be lost.
  • Mutations arise randomly with respect to need: they are not caused by the environment to help organisms.
  • Most new mutations are neutral or slightly harmful; a few are beneficial.
  • Beneficial mutations can be favoured by natural selection.

Rates and populations

  • Mutation rates per base are low, but genomes and populations are large.
  • In large populations, beneficial mutations arise more often.
  • Organisms with short generation times, such as bacteria and viruses, can evolve quickly.
  • Gene duplication creates extra copies that can evolve new functions.

The Luria-Delbrück experiment of 1943 showed that bacterial mutations for phage resistance arise randomly before exposure, not in response to it. Gene duplication followed by divergence explains gene families, such as the globin genes. Mutation, selection, drift and gene flow together determine how populations evolve.

Worked example

The Luria-Delbrück experiment.

  1. Many separate bacterial cultures were grown, then exposed to a virus.
  2. If mutations were induced by the virus, each culture would have similar numbers of resistant cells.
  3. Instead, numbers varied hugely, with some cultures having many resistant cells.
  4. This showed resistance mutations arose randomly before exposure.
Common mistakeSaying the environment causes the mutations an organism needs. Mutations arise randomly; selection then favours useful ones.

In 3 lines: Mutation is the raw material. Random with respect to need. Duplication creates new genes.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Why is mutation essential for evolution?
    • It always improves an organism's fitness
    • It creates new alleles for selection to act on
    • It happens only when organisms need it
    • It removes variation from populations
    Show the answer

    Answer: It creates new alleles for selection to act on. Without mutation, variation would eventually be used up.

  2. Why do bacteria evolve resistance quickly?
    • Bacteria cannot mutate
    • Bacteria have very long lives
    • Short generation times and huge populations
    • Bacteria reproduce only sexually
    Show the answer

    Answer: Short generation times and huge populations. Many generations and individuals give many chances for mutations.

  3. In the Luria-Delbrück experiment, what result showed mutations arose before exposure?
    • Exactly equal numbers of resistant cells in each culture
    • No resistant cells in any culture
    • All cells becoming resistant at once
    • Large variation in resistant cells between cultures
    Show the answer

    Answer: Large variation in resistant cells between cultures. Random early mutations produce some cultures with many resistant cells.

Key terms

Beneficial mutation
A mutation that increases fitness
Neutral mutation
A mutation with no effect on fitness
Gene duplication
The copying of a gene, creating an extra copy
