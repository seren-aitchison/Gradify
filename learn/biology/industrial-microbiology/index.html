Industrial Microbiology

Industrial fermenters control temperature, pH, oxygen, mixing and nutrients under sterile conditions; batch and continuous cultures produce antibiotics, enzymes, insulin, citric acid and mycoprotein, with secondary metabolites made in the stationary phase.

Biology · Microbiology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the structure and control of an industrial fermenter
  • Distinguish batch and continuous culture
  • Describe products made by industrial microbiology

Fermenters

  • Large sterile vessels in which microbes grow under controlled conditions.
  • Controls: temperature (water jacket), pH (probes and additions), oxygen (sterile air), mixing (paddles) and nutrients.
  • Everything is sterilised to avoid contamination.
  • Downstream processing extracts and purifies the product.

Culture types and products

  • Batch culture: nutrients added at the start; product harvested at the end.
  • Continuous culture: nutrients added and products removed continuously.
  • Products: antibiotics (penicillin), enzymes (for detergents), insulin, citric acid, mycoprotein and ethanol.
  • Primary metabolites are made during growth; secondary metabolites, such as penicillin, during the stationary phase.

Penicillin production increased dramatically during the Second World War after a high-yield strain of Penicillium was found on a mouldy melon in Illinois. Mycoprotein in Quorn is produced by continuous culture of Fusarium venenatum. Many biological medicines, such as monoclonal antibodies, are now made in cultured mammalian cells in bioreactors.

Worked example

Making penicillin.

  1. Penicillium is grown in a batch fermenter with sugars and nutrients.
  2. It grows rapidly at first, then enters the stationary phase.
  3. Penicillin, a secondary metabolite, is produced as growth slows.
  4. The penicillin is extracted, purified and formulated.
Common mistakeThinking all fermentation is anaerobic. Many industrial fermentations are aerobic and supplied with sterile air.

In 3 lines: Controlled sterile fermenters. Batch versus continuous. Many products.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Why is sterile air pumped into many fermenters?
    • To cool the fermenter down
    • To remove nutrients
    • To kill the microbes
    • To supply oxygen for aerobic microbes
    Show the answer

    Answer: To supply oxygen for aerobic microbes. Many industrial microbes respire aerobically.

  2. When is penicillin mainly produced in a batch culture?
    • During the stationary phase
    • During the lag phase
    • Before any growth
    • Only after all cells have died
    Show the answer

    Answer: During the stationary phase. As a secondary metabolite, penicillin forms as growth slows.

  3. What is an advantage of continuous culture?
    • It is easier to keep sterile than batch culture
    • Product is made continuously without stopping to restart
    • It produces only secondary metabolites
    • It needs no nutrients
    Show the answer

    Answer: Product is made continuously without stopping to restart. Continuous operation gives steady output.

Key terms

Fermenter
A vessel for growing microbes under controlled conditions
Secondary metabolite
A product not needed for growth, made in the stationary phase
Downstream processing
Extraction and purification of a product
