Sex-linked Inheritance

Females are XX and males XY; males are hemizygous for X-linked genes, so X-linked recessive conditions such as haemophilia are more common in males, carrier mothers pass them to half their sons and there is no father-to-son transmission.

Biology · Genetics and Molecular Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain how sex is determined in humans
  • Describe X-linked recessive inheritance
  • Predict outcomes of crosses involving X-linked genes

Sex determination and X-linked genes

  • Females are XX and males XY; the SRY gene on the Y chromosome triggers male development.
  • The X chromosome carries many genes not found on the Y.
  • Males have only one X, so they are hemizygous for X-linked genes.
  • Genotypes are written with superscripts: Xᴴ, Xʰ.

X-linked recessive conditions

  • Examples: haemophilia A, Duchenne muscular dystrophy and red-green colour blindness.
  • Much more common in males, who need only one recessive allele.
  • Females are usually carriers (XᴴXʰ).
  • Affected males inherit the allele from their mothers; fathers pass X-linked alleles only to daughters.

Because a father passes his Y to sons, there is no father-to-son transmission of X-linked conditions. A carrier mother and an unaffected father have a 1 in 2 chance of an affected son and a 1 in 2 chance of a carrier daughter. Queen Victoria was a carrier of haemophilia, which spread through European royal families.

Worked example

A carrier mother.

  1. Mother: XᴴXʰ (carrier). Father: XᴴY (unaffected).
  2. Daughters: XᴴXᴴ or XᴴXʰ: unaffected, half are carriers.
  3. Sons: XᴴY or XʰY: half are affected.
  4. Overall, 1 in 4 children is an affected son.
  • In humans, sex is determined at fertilisation by the sperm: every egg carries an X chromosome, and a sperm carries either an X (giving an XX, female, embryo) or a Y (giving an XY, male, embryo), so the two are about equally likely.
  • In an XY embryo, the SRY gene switches on testis development at about seven weeks; hormones from the testes, testosterone and anti-Müllerian hormone, then direct male development. Without SRY, the gonads develop as ovaries.
Common mistakeSaying an affected father can pass an X-linked condition to his son. Fathers pass their Y, not their X, to sons.

In 3 lines: XX females, XY males. X-linked recessive more common in males. No father-to-son transmission.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which is an X-linked recessive condition?
    • Haemophilia A
    • Cystic fibrosis
    • Huntington's disease
    • Sickle cell anaemia
    Show the answer

    Answer: Haemophilia A. Haemophilia A is caused by an X-linked recessive allele.

  2. A carrier mother and unaffected father have a son. What is the chance he is affected?
    • 1 in 4
    • 1 in 2
    • None at all
    • Every son is affected
    Show the answer

    Answer: 1 in 2. Each son receives one of his mother's two X chromosomes.

  3. Why can't an affected father pass an X-linked condition to his son?
    • Sons never inherit any genes from fathers
    • The allele is destroyed in sperm
    • He passes his Y chromosome, not his X, to sons
    • X-linked genes only affect daughters
    Show the answer

    Answer: He passes his Y chromosome, not his X, to sons. Sons receive their X chromosome from their mother and the Y from their father.

Key terms

Sex-linked gene
A gene located on a sex chromosome
Hemizygous
Having only one copy of a gene, as males do for X-linked genes
Carrier
A female with one recessive X-linked allele
