Controls and Reproducibility

Controls rule out alternative explanations; reproducibility by others is what makes a result reliable knowledge.

Biology · Foundations of Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Distinguish positive, negative and placebo controls
  • Explain the difference between repeatability and reproducibility
  • Describe why the "reproducibility crisis" matters and how it is being addressed

Kinds of control

  • Negative control: a treatment expected to give no effect (enzyme boiled to denature it, or no enzyme), showing that any effect needs the active factor.
  • Positive control: a treatment known to give an effect, showing that the method can detect one (a known antibiotic in an antibiotic test).
  • Placebo control: in drug trials, an inactive treatment that looks identical, so that expectations don't bias results; with blinding, neither patients nor researchers know who gets what.

Repeatability and reproducibility

Repeatable results are similar when the same person repeats the experiment with the same method and equipment. Reproducible results are similar when someone else repeats it, perhaps with different equipment or methods. Reproducibility is the stronger test.

The reproducibility problem

Surveys and large replication projects in the 2010s found that many published results, in fields including cancer biology and psychology, could not be reproduced. Causes include small sample sizes, selective reporting of positive results, and flexible analysis. Remedies include pre-registering study plans, sharing data and code, larger samples and publishing negative results.

Worked example

Design the controls for a test of whether a plant extract kills bacteria on an agar plate.

  1. Negative control: a disc soaked in the solvent used to make the extract, which should give no clear zone.
  2. Positive control: a disc with a known antibiotic, which should give a clear zone and shows the bacteria are sensitive and the method works.
  3. Test: discs with the extract, repeated on several plates, with the zones measured blind.
Common mistakeThinking a control group is "the group where nothing happens". A control is chosen to answer a specific question; positive controls are supposed to show an effect.

In 2 lines: Negative controls show necessity, positive controls show sensitivity, placebos remove expectation bias. Reproducibility by independent groups is stronger evidence than repeatability.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. In an experiment on the effect of amylase on starch, which is the best negative control?
    • Starch solution with active amylase
    • Starch solution at a higher temperature
    • Starch solution with boiled amylase
    • A different enzyme
    Show the answer

    Answer: Starch solution with boiled amylase. Boiled (denatured) amylase shows that starch breakdown needs active enzyme, with all other conditions the same.

  2. A drug trial gives one group the drug and another group an identical-looking sugar pill, and neither patients nor doctors know who gets which. This design is called…
    • an observational study
    • a positive control
    • a pilot study
    • a double-blind, placebo-controlled trial
    Show the answer

    Answer: a double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. A placebo removes expectation effects, and double-blinding stops bias from both patients and researchers.

  3. A laboratory reports that a gene knock-out doubles lifespan in mice, but three other laboratories find no effect. What is the most appropriate conclusion?
    • The original result is doubtful until it is reproduced; possible causes include chance, differences in conditions or bias
    • The original result is correct because it was published first
    • The other laboratories must have made mistakes
    • The gene has no function
    Show the answer

    Answer: The original result is doubtful until it is reproduced; possible causes include chance, differences in conditions or bias. Failure to reproduce weakens confidence in a finding; investigating the differences and running larger studies is the way forward.

Key terms

Negative control
A treatment expected to produce no effect, used to show that an effect depends on the factor tested
Positive control
A treatment known to produce an effect, used to show the method works
Reproducibility
Getting consistent results when an experiment is repeated independently by others
