Nervous System

The nervous system provides sensory input, integration and motor output through the CNS (brain and spinal cord) and the PNS; reflex arcs from receptor to effector via sensory, relay and motor neurones produce rapid protective responses.

Biology · Human Anatomy and Physiology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the main functions of the nervous system
  • Distinguish the central and peripheral nervous systems
  • Describe a reflex arc

Functions and divisions

  • Functions: sensory input, integration and motor output.
  • Central nervous system (CNS): brain and spinal cord.
  • Peripheral nervous system (PNS): nerves connecting the CNS to the body.
  • The nervous system works with the endocrine system to coordinate the body.

Reflexes

  • A reflex is a rapid, automatic response to a stimulus.
  • A reflex arc: receptor, sensory neurone, relay neurone (in the spinal cord), motor neurone, effector.
  • Reflexes protect the body, such as withdrawing a hand from a hot object.
  • Many reflexes do not require conscious thought in the brain.

Nervous communication is fast and short-lived, whereas hormonal communication is slower but longer-lasting. The nervous system contains around 86 billion neurones in the brain alone, supported by glial cells. Damage to the spinal cord can disrupt communication between the brain and body below the injury.

Worked example

The withdrawal reflex.

  1. A finger touches a hot pan, and pain receptors are stimulated.
  2. A sensory neurone carries impulses to the spinal cord.
  3. A relay neurone passes the signal to a motor neurone.
  4. The biceps contracts and the hand is pulled away before pain is felt.
Common mistakeThinking every response goes through conscious thought in the brain. Many reflexes are processed in the spinal cord.

In 3 lines: Input, integration, output. CNS and PNS. Reflex arcs are fast.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which neurone carries impulses from a receptor to the spinal cord?
    • Motor neurone
    • Sensory neurone
    • Relay neurone
    • Glial cell
    Show the answer

    Answer: Sensory neurone. Sensory neurones carry information towards the CNS.

  2. How does nervous communication differ from hormonal communication?
    • It is slower and longer-lasting
    • It uses the blood to carry signals
    • It is faster and shorter-lived
    • It cannot reach muscles
    Show the answer

    Answer: It is faster and shorter-lived. Electrical impulses act quickly and briefly.

  3. Why can a hand be withdrawn from heat before pain is felt?
    • The brain processes the reflex first
    • Pain receptors are absent from the hand
    • Motor neurones are slower than sensory ones
    • The reflex is processed in the spinal cord before signals reach the brain
    Show the answer

    Answer: The reflex is processed in the spinal cord before signals reach the brain. The short spinal pathway is faster than conscious processing.

Key terms

Central nervous system
The brain and spinal cord
Reflex arc
The pathway of a reflex from receptor to effector
Relay neurone
A neurone connecting sensory and motor neurones in the CNS
