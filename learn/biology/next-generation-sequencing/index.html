Next-generation Sequencing

Next-generation sequencing reads millions of fragments in parallel; short-read sequencing by synthesis is accurate and cheap, long-read methods such as nanopore span repeats, and reads are aligned or assembled, with coverage indicating reliability.

Biology · Biotechnology and Genetic Engineering · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the principle of massively parallel sequencing
  • Compare short-read and long-read sequencing
  • Describe applications of next-generation sequencing

Short-read sequencing

  • DNA is broken into short fragments and adapters are attached.
  • Fragments are attached to a surface and amplified into clusters.
  • Bases are added one at a time with fluorescent labels; a camera records each cycle (sequencing by synthesis).
  • Millions to billions of reads, typically 100–300 bases long, are produced in parallel.

Long reads and analysis

  • Long-read methods, such as nanopore and PacBio, read thousands to millions of bases.
  • Nanopore: DNA passes through a protein pore and changes in current identify bases.
  • Reads are aligned to a reference genome or assembled de novo.
  • Coverage (depth) is the number of reads covering each base.

Short reads are accurate and cheap but struggle with repetitive regions; long reads span repeats and structural variants. Portable nanopore devices were used to track Ebola in West Africa and SARS-CoV-2 variants. Applications include whole-genome sequencing, RNA-seq to measure gene expression and metagenomics.

Worked example

Calculating coverage.

  1. A genome is 3 billion bases long.
  2. Sequencing produces 900 million reads of 100 bases.
  3. Total bases = 90 billion.
  4. Coverage = 90 ÷ 3 = 30×, so each base is read about 30 times.
Common mistakeThinking one read covers the whole genome. Millions of overlapping reads are assembled computationally.

In 3 lines: Massively parallel. Short and long reads. Coverage and assembly.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which method produces long reads spanning repeats?
    • Short-read Illumina sequencing
    • Gel electrophoresis
    • Nanopore sequencing
    • Restriction mapping
    Show the answer

    Answer: Nanopore sequencing. Nanopore reads can be thousands of bases long.

  2. Why do short reads struggle with repetitive regions?
    • Short reads are always inaccurate
    • Repeats cannot be sequenced at all
    • Short reads only read RNA
    • Short repeated sequences cannot be placed uniquely
    Show the answer

    Answer: Short repeated sequences cannot be placed uniquely. Identical short reads could come from many locations.

  3. Why is high coverage important?
    • It increases confidence that each base is correct
    • It makes sequencing cheaper
    • It reduces the number of reads
    • It removes the need for alignment
    Show the answer

    Answer: It increases confidence that each base is correct. Many reads of each base reduce random errors.

Key terms

Sequencing by synthesis
Reading bases as they are added to growing strands
Coverage
Number of reads covering each base
Nanopore sequencing
Reading DNA as it passes through a protein pore
