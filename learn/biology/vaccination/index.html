Vaccination

Vaccines stimulate memory without disease, using live attenuated, inactivated, subunit, toxoid, mRNA or viral vector platforms; herd immunity protects the unvaccinated when coverage exceeds a threshold of roughly 1 − 1/R₀.

Biology · Immunology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain how vaccines produce immunity
  • Describe the main types of vaccine
  • Explain herd immunity

How vaccines work

  • Vaccines contain antigens, or instructions to make antigens, from a pathogen.
  • They trigger a primary response and create memory cells without causing disease.
  • On real infection, a fast secondary response prevents or reduces illness.
  • Adjuvants boost responses.

Types

  • Live attenuated: weakened pathogens, such as MMR.
  • Inactivated: killed pathogens, such as some polio and influenza vaccines.
  • Subunit and conjugate: purified proteins or polysaccharides, such as HPV and pneumococcal vaccines.
  • Toxoid: inactivated toxins, such as tetanus.
  • mRNA and viral vector: instructions for making an antigen, such as COVID-19 vaccines.

Herd immunity occurs when enough people are immune that a pathogen cannot spread easily, protecting those who cannot be vaccinated, such as babies and immunosuppressed people. The threshold depends on R₀: roughly 1 − 1/R₀. Smallpox was declared eradicated in 1980 after a global vaccination campaign. Falling vaccine uptake has led to measles outbreaks in the UK.

Worked example

Calculating a herd immunity threshold.

  1. A disease has an R₀ of 5.
  2. Threshold = 1 − 1/5 = 0.8.
  3. About 80% of the population must be immune.
  4. For measles, with R₀ around 15, the threshold is above 90%.
Common mistakeBelieving vaccines cause the disease they prevent. Non-live vaccines cannot cause the disease, and live vaccines are attenuated.

In 3 lines: Memory without disease. Many vaccine types. Herd immunity threshold.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What do vaccines create to provide long-term protection?
    • Red blood cells
    • Antibiotics
    • Memory cells
    • Platelets
    Show the answer

    Answer: Memory cells. Memory cells allow a fast response to later infection.

  2. How do mRNA vaccines produce immunity?
    • The mRNA kills the virus directly
    • They contain live weakened virus
    • They change the person's DNA
    • Cells use the mRNA to make an antigen that triggers a response
    Show the answer

    Answer: Cells use the mRNA to make an antigen that triggers a response. Cells briefly make the antigen, training the immune system.

  3. Why does herd immunity protect babies too young to be vaccinated?
    • Low spread means they are unlikely to meet the pathogen
    • Babies make antibodies without exposure
    • Vaccines pass through the air
    • Babies cannot be infected
    Show the answer

    Answer: Low spread means they are unlikely to meet the pathogen. High coverage breaks chains of transmission.

Key terms

Vaccine
A preparation that stimulates protective immunity
Herd immunity
Indirect protection when most people are immune
Attenuated
Weakened so as not to cause disease
