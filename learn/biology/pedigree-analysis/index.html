Pedigree Analysis

Pedigrees use squares for males, circles for females and shading for affected individuals; autosomal recessive traits can skip generations, autosomal dominant traits appear each generation and X-linked recessive traits affect more males without father-to-son transmission, and clues allow genotypes and probabilities to be deduced.

Biology · Genetics and Molecular Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Interpret standard pedigree symbols
  • Identify autosomal dominant, autosomal recessive and X-linked patterns
  • Deduce genotypes and probabilities from pedigrees

Symbols

  • Squares are males; circles are females.
  • Shaded symbols show affected individuals.
  • Horizontal lines join partners; vertical lines lead to children.
  • Generations are numbered with Roman numerals.

Recognising patterns

  • Autosomal recessive: can skip generations; two unaffected parents can have an affected child.
  • Autosomal dominant: appears in every generation; an affected child usually has an affected parent; two affected parents can have an unaffected child.
  • X-linked recessive: more affected males; no father-to-son transmission; affected males often have carrier mothers.
  • X-linked dominant: affected fathers pass the trait to all daughters.

To analyse a pedigree, look for key clues: unaffected parents with an affected child rule out dominant inheritance; an affected father with an affected son rules out X-linked inheritance. Then assign genotypes, starting with affected individuals in recessive patterns. Probabilities are calculated using known genotypes, such as 2/3 for an unaffected child of two carriers being a carrier.

Worked example

Analysing a family.

  1. Two unaffected parents have an affected daughter.
  2. The trait cannot be dominant, because neither parent is affected.
  3. It cannot be X-linked recessive, because the father would then be affected.
  4. So it is autosomal recessive, and both parents are carriers.
Common mistakeConcluding a trait is dominant because it appears often. Use key clues, such as unaffected parents with affected children.

In 3 lines: Squares, circles, shading. Patterns have key clues. Deduce genotypes and probabilities.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What does a shaded circle represent in a pedigree?
    • An unaffected male
    • An affected male
    • A carrier male
    • An affected female
    Show the answer

    Answer: An affected female. Circles are females and shading means affected.

  2. Two unaffected parents have an affected son and an affected daughter. Which pattern is most likely?
    • Autosomal recessive
    • Autosomal dominant
    • X-linked dominant
    • Y-linked inheritance
    Show the answer

    Answer: Autosomal recessive. Unaffected parents with affected children of both sexes suggests autosomal recessive.

  3. An affected father has an affected son. Which pattern does this rule out?
    • Autosomal dominant inheritance
    • X-linked recessive inheritance
    • Autosomal recessive inheritance
    • Any genetic inheritance at all
    Show the answer

    Answer: X-linked recessive inheritance. Fathers pass a Y, not an X, to sons, so X-linked recessive inheritance is ruled out.

Key terms

Pedigree
A diagram showing inheritance of a trait in a family
Proband
The first affected person identified in a family
Skipping a generation
A trait absent in one generation reappearing later
