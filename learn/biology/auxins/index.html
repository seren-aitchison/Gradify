Auxins

Auxin (IAA) is made in shoot tips, moves by polar transport and causes cell elongation by acidifying and loosening cell walls; it causes apical dominance, affects roots and shoots differently and is used in rooting powders, weedkillers and seedless fruit.

Biology · Plant Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain how auxin causes cell elongation
  • Explain apical dominance
  • Describe commercial uses of auxins

How auxin works

  • The main natural auxin is indole-3-acetic acid (IAA).
  • It is made in shoot tips and young leaves.
  • It moves by polar transport from cell to cell, using carrier proteins.
  • Acid growth hypothesis: auxin causes cells to pump H⁺ into walls, loosening them so the cell can elongate.

Effects and uses

  • Apical dominance: auxin from the shoot tip inhibits side buds.
  • Removing the tip allows side shoots to grow, making plants bushier.
  • High concentrations inhibit growth in roots, while low ones promote it.
  • Uses: rooting powder for cuttings, selective weedkillers that kill broad-leaved weeds, and seedless fruit.

Synthetic auxins such as 2,4-D kill broad-leaved plants by causing uncontrolled growth but leave grasses, such as cereals, largely unharmed. Gardeners pinch out tips to make plants bushier. Shoots and roots respond differently to the same auxin concentration, which explains their opposite responses to gravity.

Worked example

Pinching out a basil plant.

  1. A basil plant grows tall with few side shoots.
  2. The gardener removes the shoot tip.
  3. Auxin from the tip no longer inhibits side buds.
  4. Side shoots grow, producing a bushier plant.
Common mistakeThinking auxin always promotes growth. High concentrations can inhibit growth, especially in roots.

In 3 lines: IAA from shoot tips. Acid growth. Apical dominance and uses.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Where is auxin mainly produced?
    • In root hairs only
    • In mature woody stems
    • In flowers only
    • In shoot tips and young leaves
    Show the answer

    Answer: In shoot tips and young leaves. Auxin is made in actively growing shoot tissues.

  2. How does auxin cause cells to elongate?
    • It acidifies cell walls, loosening them
    • It thickens cell walls with lignin
    • It stops water entering cells
    • It breaks down the cell membrane
    Show the answer

    Answer: It acidifies cell walls, loosening them. Lower pH activates proteins that loosen walls.

  3. Why do selective weedkillers kill weeds but not cereal crops?
    • Cereals cannot absorb any chemicals
    • Broad-leaved plants are more sensitive to synthetic auxins
    • Weeds produce no natural auxin
    • Cereals break down all chemicals instantly
    Show the answer

    Answer: Broad-leaved plants are more sensitive to synthetic auxins. Differences in sensitivity let the herbicide target broad-leaved weeds.

Key terms

IAA
Indole-3-acetic acid, the main natural auxin
Polar transport
Directional movement of auxin between cells
Acid growth hypothesis
Auxin loosens cell walls by acidifying them
