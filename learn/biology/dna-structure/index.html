DNA Structure

X-ray diffraction and Chargaff's rules supported the double helix; DNA stores information in its base sequence, is stabilised by covalent backbones and hydrogen bonds, and can be copied accurately because each strand is a template for complementary pairing.

Biology · Genetics and Molecular Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the evidence used to determine DNA structure
  • Explain how DNA's structure allows information storage
  • Explain how DNA's structure allows accurate copying

Evidence for the double helix

  • Rosalind Franklin's X-ray diffraction images, especially Photo 51, showed a helical structure.
  • Chargaff's rules: A = T and G = C in DNA.
  • Watson and Crick built a model consistent with these data in 1953.
  • The helix has about 10 base pairs per turn and a diameter of about 2 nm.

Structure and function

  • Information storage: the sequence of bases encodes genetic information.
  • Stability: covalent sugar-phosphate backbones and many hydrogen bonds protect the sequence.
  • Copying: complementary base pairing lets each strand act as a template.
  • Compactness: long molecules can be coiled and packaged.

The base sequence is inside the helix, protected from chemical attack, while the charged backbone faces outward. Major and minor grooves allow proteins such as transcription factors to read the sequence without unwinding the helix. Hydrogen bonds between bases are weak enough to allow strands to separate for replication and transcription.

Worked example

Structure explains copying.

  1. Watson and Crick noted that base pairing "immediately suggests a possible copying mechanism".
  2. If the strands separate, each can pair with new nucleotides.
  3. A pairs with T and G with C, so new strands are complementary.
  4. Two identical DNA molecules result.
Common mistakeOverlooking Rosalind Franklin's role. Her X-ray data were essential evidence for the double helix.

In 3 lines: Franklin's X-rays and Chargaff's rules. Sequence stores information. Pairing allows copying.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which evidence showed that amounts of A equal T and G equal C?
    • Mendel's ratios
    • Darwin's observations
    • The Human Genome Project
    • Chargaff's rules
    Show the answer

    Answer: Chargaff's rules. Chargaff measured base ratios in DNA from many species.

  2. Why can proteins read DNA without separating the strands?
    • Grooves in the helix expose edges of the bases
    • DNA has no hydrogen bonds
    • Bases face outward on the backbone
    • Proteins only bind RNA
    Show the answer

    Answer: Grooves in the helix expose edges of the bases. Major and minor grooves allow sequence recognition.

  3. Why are the bases inside the double helix an advantage?
    • They can easily leave the molecule
    • They are protected from chemical damage
    • They make DNA single stranded
    • They prevent replication
    Show the answer

    Answer: They are protected from chemical damage. The backbone shields the information-carrying bases.

Key terms

X-ray diffraction
A technique using X-ray patterns to reveal molecular structure
Template strand
A strand used as a pattern to make a complementary strand
Major groove
The wider groove in the DNA helix where proteins can read bases
