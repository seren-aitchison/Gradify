Enzymes

Enzymes are mostly globular proteins that act as biological catalysts with specific active sites, lowering activation energy without being used up or changing equilibrium; they work inside or outside cells and often need cofactors or coenzymes.

Biology · Biochemistry and Biomolecules · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define enzymes as biological catalysts
  • Explain how enzymes lower activation energy
  • Distinguish intracellular and extracellular enzymes

What enzymes are

  • Mostly globular proteins; some RNA molecules (ribozymes) also catalyse reactions.
  • They speed up reactions without being used up.
  • Each enzyme has an active site that binds a specific substrate.
  • Enzymes are named by function, often ending in -ase: amylase, lipase, catalase.

Activation energy and location

  • Activation energy is the energy needed to start a reaction.
  • Enzymes lower it by stabilising the transition state, so reactions proceed faster at body temperature.
  • Intracellular enzymes work inside cells, such as catalase breaking down hydrogen peroxide.
  • Extracellular enzymes work outside cells, such as amylase in saliva.

Enzymes do not change the equilibrium of a reaction or its overall energy change; they only speed up reaching equilibrium. Many need cofactors: inorganic ions such as Cl⁻ for amylase, or organic coenzymes such as NAD derived from vitamins. Enzymes control metabolism by catalysing specific steps in pathways, and they are widely used in industry, such as in washing powders and food processing.

Worked example

Catalase in action.

  1. Hydrogen peroxide is a toxic by-product of metabolism.
  2. Catalase breaks it down: 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂.
  3. One catalase molecule can break down millions of hydrogen peroxide molecules per second.
  4. Adding liver to hydrogen peroxide produces rapid bubbling of oxygen.
Common mistakeSaying enzymes provide energy for reactions. They lower the activation energy needed; they do not supply energy.

In 3 lines: Biological catalysts. Lower activation energy. Intracellular or extracellular.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. How do enzymes speed up reactions?
    • By increasing the temperature of the cell
    • By supplying ATP to the reaction
    • By changing the equilibrium position
    • By lowering the activation energy
    Show the answer

    Answer: By lowering the activation energy. Enzymes stabilise the transition state, reducing activation energy.

  2. Which is an extracellular enzyme?
    • Salivary amylase
    • Catalase in liver cells
    • DNA polymerase in the nucleus
    • ATP synthase in mitochondria
    Show the answer

    Answer: Salivary amylase. Amylase is secreted and works in the mouth.

  3. Why don't enzymes change the equilibrium of a reaction?
    • They are consumed in the reaction
    • They speed up forward and reverse reactions equally
    • They supply extra energy
    • They only work on irreversible reactions
    Show the answer

    Answer: They speed up forward and reverse reactions equally. Catalysts affect rate, not the final equilibrium.

Key terms

Enzyme
A biological catalyst, usually a protein
Activation energy
The energy needed to start a reaction
Cofactor
A non-protein substance needed for enzyme activity
