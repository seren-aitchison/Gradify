Mutations

Mutations are changes in DNA sequence, occurring spontaneously or caused by mutagens such as UV and chemicals; somatic mutations affect body cells and germline mutations can be inherited, with effects ranging from harmful to neutral to beneficial.

Biology · Genetics and Molecular Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define mutation and distinguish somatic and germline mutations
  • Describe causes of mutation
  • Explain the possible effects of mutations

What mutations are

  • A mutation is a change in the base sequence of DNA.
  • Gene mutations affect one gene; chromosome mutations affect structure or number of chromosomes.
  • Somatic mutations occur in body cells and are not inherited by offspring.
  • Germline mutations occur in gametes or their precursors and can be inherited.

Causes and effects

  • Spontaneous errors in replication.
  • Mutagens: UV light, ionising radiation, some chemicals such as those in tobacco smoke and some viruses.
  • Effects can be harmful (disease), neutral (no effect) or beneficial (an advantage).
  • Mutations are the ultimate source of genetic variation.

Mutation rates are low because of proofreading and repair, but across large genomes and populations, many new mutations arise each generation: each human carries roughly 50 to 100 new mutations not found in either parent. Most fall in non-coding DNA and have no effect. Somatic mutations accumulate with age and can lead to cancer, while germline mutations provide the raw material for evolution.

Worked example

Somatic versus germline.

  1. A mutation in a skin cell caused by sunburn may lead to skin cancer.
  2. It is not passed to the person's children.
  3. A mutation in a sperm cell may be present in every cell of a child.
  4. Only germline mutations are inherited.
Common mistakeAssuming all mutations are harmful. Many are neutral, and some are beneficial.

In 3 lines: Changes in DNA sequence. Somatic or germline. Harmful, neutral or beneficial.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which is an example of a mutagen?
    • Glucose
    • Water
    • Oxygen at normal levels
    • Ultraviolet light
    Show the answer

    Answer: Ultraviolet light. UV light damages DNA, increasing mutations.

  2. Why is a mutation in a skin cell not inherited by a person's children?
    • It is somatic and not present in gametes
    • Skin cells make gametes
    • Somatic mutations always reverse
    • Children inherit only maternal DNA
    Show the answer

    Answer: It is somatic and not present in gametes. Only germline mutations reach the next generation.

  3. Why are mutations important for evolution?
    • They always improve survival
    • They are the ultimate source of new genetic variation
    • They prevent natural selection
    • They only occur in plants
    Show the answer

    Answer: They are the ultimate source of new genetic variation. Selection acts on variation produced by mutation.

Key terms

Mutation
A change in the base sequence of DNA
Mutagen
An agent that increases the mutation rate
Germline mutation
A mutation in cells that give rise to gametes
