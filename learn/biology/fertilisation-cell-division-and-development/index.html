Fertilisation

In mammalian fertilisation, capacitated sperm undergo the acrosome reaction to penetrate the zona pellucida, fuse with the egg and deliver their nucleus; the cortical reaction hardens the zona to prevent polyspermy, restoring diploidy and activating development.

Biology · Cell Division and Development · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the acrosome reaction and sperm-egg fusion
  • Explain how polyspermy is prevented
  • Explain the significance of fertilisation for chromosome number and development

Steps in mammals

  • Sperm are capacitated in the female reproductive tract, becoming able to fertilise.
  • At the egg, the acrosome reaction releases enzymes that digest the zona pellucida.
  • The sperm membrane fuses with the egg membrane, and the sperm nucleus enters.
  • The egg completes meiosis II, and the two haploid nuclei combine.

Blocking polyspermy

  • The cortical reaction: cortical granules release enzymes that harden the zona pellucida.
  • This prevents more sperm from binding or entering.
  • Polyspermy would produce a zygote with too many chromosomes, which does not develop normally.

Fertilisation restores the diploid number, combines genetic material from two parents and activates the egg to begin development. In humans, fertilisation usually occurs in the oviduct (fallopian tube). In vitro fertilisation (IVF) brings sperm and eggs together in a laboratory, and intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI) injects a single sperm directly into an egg.

Worked example

One sperm only.

  1. Millions of sperm are released, but only a few hundred reach the egg.
  2. One sperm's acrosome digests a path through the zona pellucida.
  3. Its membrane fuses with the egg membrane.
  4. The cortical reaction then hardens the zona, blocking other sperm.
Common mistakeThinking many sperm usually enter the egg. The cortical reaction normally allows only one.

In 3 lines: Acrosome reaction penetrates the zona. Cortical reaction blocks polyspermy. Diploid zygote forms.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What does the acrosome reaction release?
    • Cortical granules that block sperm
    • Enzymes that digest the zona pellucida
    • Hormones that start menstruation
    • Mitochondria for the zygote
    Show the answer

    Answer: Enzymes that digest the zona pellucida. Acrosomal enzymes let the sperm penetrate the egg coat.

  2. How does the cortical reaction prevent polyspermy?
    • It kills all sperm in the reproductive tract
    • It removes the egg's nucleus
    • It hardens the zona pellucida so no more sperm can enter
    • It speeds up the acrosome reaction
    Show the answer

    Answer: It hardens the zona pellucida so no more sperm can enter. Released enzymes alter the zona to block further sperm.

  3. Why is preventing polyspermy important?
    • Polyspermy would make the zygote haploid
    • Polyspermy speeds up healthy development
    • Extra sperm provide extra energy
    • Extra sperm would give too many chromosomes and abnormal development
    Show the answer

    Answer: Extra sperm would give too many chromosomes and abnormal development. A triploid or worse zygote does not develop normally.

Key terms

Acrosome reaction
Release of enzymes from the sperm head to penetrate the egg's coat
Zona pellucida
The glycoprotein layer around the mammalian egg
Cortical reaction
Release of cortical granules that block polyspermy
