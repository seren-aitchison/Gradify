DNA Repair

DNA is protected by proofreading, mismatch repair, base and nucleotide excision repair and double-strand break repair by homologous recombination or end joining; defects, as in xeroderma pigmentosum and BRCA mutations, increase mutation and cancer risk.

Biology · Genetics and Molecular Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe proofreading and mismatch repair
  • Describe excision repair and double-strand break repair
  • Explain the consequences of defective DNA repair

Repairing errors and damage

  • Proofreading: DNA polymerase removes incorrect nucleotides as it builds.
  • Mismatch repair: enzymes find and correct mismatched bases missed by proofreading.
  • Base excision repair: removes single damaged bases, such as oxidised bases.
  • Nucleotide excision repair: removes bulky damage, such as thymine dimers caused by UV light.

Double-strand breaks and failures

  • Double-strand breaks are repaired by homologous recombination, using a sister chromatid as a template, or by non-homologous end joining, which can lose bases.
  • Defects in repair increase mutation rates and cancer risk.
  • Xeroderma pigmentosum: defective nucleotide excision repair, causing extreme UV sensitivity.
  • BRCA1 and BRCA2 mutations reduce homologous recombination repair, increasing breast and ovarian cancer risk.

Repair systems use the complementary strand as a template, which is why double-stranded DNA is an advantage. The 2015 Nobel Prize in Chemistry recognised work on DNA repair mechanisms. Some cancer drugs, such as PARP inhibitors, exploit repair defects in BRCA-mutated tumours, killing cancer cells that rely on alternative repair routes.

Worked example

Repairing UV damage.

  1. UV light causes adjacent thymines to bond, forming a thymine dimer.
  2. Nucleotide excision repair enzymes detect the distortion.
  3. A section of the damaged strand is cut out.
  4. DNA polymerase fills the gap using the other strand, and ligase seals it.
Common mistakeAssuming all DNA damage becomes a mutation. Most damage is repaired before it can be passed on.

In 3 lines: Proofreading and mismatch repair. Excision repair removes damage. Repair defects cause disease.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which repair system corrects errors as DNA polymerase builds a strand?
    • Nucleotide excision repair
    • Proofreading
    • Non-homologous end joining
    • Homologous recombination
    Show the answer

    Answer: Proofreading. DNA polymerase checks and removes incorrect nucleotides as it works.

  2. Which condition results from defective nucleotide excision repair?
    • Sickle cell anaemia
    • Cystic fibrosis
    • Xeroderma pigmentosum
    • Huntington's disease
    Show the answer

    Answer: Xeroderma pigmentosum. Patients cannot repair UV damage and are extremely sensitive to sunlight.

  3. Why is double-stranded DNA an advantage for repair?
    • Double-stranded DNA can never be chemically damaged
    • Only one of the two strands carries any information
    • Two strands stop the DNA from being replicated
    • The undamaged strand provides a template for correct repair
    Show the answer

    Answer: The undamaged strand provides a template for correct repair. Repair enzymes copy the complementary strand to restore the sequence.

Key terms

Mismatch repair
Correction of mismatched base pairs after replication
Thymine dimer
A UV-induced bond between adjacent thymines
Homologous recombination
Repair of breaks using a matching DNA template
