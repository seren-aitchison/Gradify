Stem-cell Biology

Stem cells self-renew and differentiate; they range from totipotent and pluripotent to multipotent and unipotent, are used in bone marrow transplants and research, and iPSCs reprogrammed from adult cells avoid embryo use, while embryonic research is regulated.

Biology · Cell Division and Development · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define stem cells and their key properties
  • Distinguish totipotent, pluripotent, multipotent and unipotent stem cells
  • Describe uses and ethical issues of stem cells

Properties and types

  • Stem cells can self-renew and differentiate.
  • Totipotent: can form all cell types, including placenta; the zygote and early embryo cells.
  • Pluripotent: can form all body cell types; embryonic stem cells.
  • Multipotent: can form a limited range, such as haematopoietic stem cells forming blood cells.
  • Unipotent: form one cell type, such as some skin stem cells.

Uses

  • Bone marrow transplants use haematopoietic stem cells to treat leukaemia.
  • Induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) are made by reprogramming adult cells, as shown by Yamanaka in 2006.
  • Research uses stem cells to model diseases and test drugs.
  • Possible future therapies for conditions such as Parkinson's disease and macular degeneration.

Embryonic stem cell research raises ethical issues because embryos are destroyed, and it is regulated in the UK by the Human Fertilisation and Embryology Authority. iPSCs avoid embryo use and can be matched to a patient, reducing rejection, but they carry risks such as tumour formation. Plants have meristems, regions of stem cells that allow growth throughout life.

Worked example

Treating leukaemia.

  1. A patient's diseased bone marrow is destroyed by chemotherapy.
  2. Haematopoietic stem cells from a matched donor are transplanted.
  3. The stem cells settle in the marrow and divide.
  4. They differentiate into all types of blood cell, restoring blood production.
Common mistakeSaying adult stem cells can form any cell type. Most adult stem cells are multipotent or unipotent.

In 3 lines: Self-renew and differentiate. Totipotent to unipotent. Transplants, iPSCs, ethics.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which type of stem cell can form any cell, including placenta?
    • Pluripotent
    • Multipotent
    • Unipotent
    • Totipotent
    Show the answer

    Answer: Totipotent. Totipotent cells, such as the zygote, can form all cell types.

  2. Which stem cells are used in bone marrow transplants?
    • Haematopoietic stem cells
    • Embryonic stem cells only
    • Neural stem cells
    • Muscle satellite cells
    Show the answer

    Answer: Haematopoietic stem cells. Haematopoietic stem cells form all blood cell types.

  3. Why are iPSCs seen as an alternative to embryonic stem cells?
    • They are always free of any risks
    • They avoid destroying embryos and can match the patient
    • They are totipotent and form placenta
    • They cannot differentiate
    Show the answer

    Answer: They avoid destroying embryos and can match the patient. Reprogrammed adult cells avoid key ethical issues.

Key terms

Pluripotent
Able to form all body cell types
Multipotent
Able to form a limited range of cell types
Induced pluripotent stem cell
An adult cell reprogrammed to pluripotency
