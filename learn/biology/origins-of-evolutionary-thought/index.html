Origins of Evolutionary Thought

Early naturalists saw species as fixed, geologists showed an ancient Earth, Lamarck proposed inheritance of acquired traits, Darwin and Wallace proposed natural selection in 1858–1859, and the modern synthesis combined selection with genetics.

Biology · Evolution and Natural Selection · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe early ideas about the diversity of life
  • Compare Lamarck's ideas with Darwin and Wallace's theory
  • Explain how the modern synthesis combined evolution and genetics

Before Darwin

  • Many early naturalists, such as Linnaeus, classified species as fixed and unchanging.
  • Geologists such as Hutton and Lyell argued that the Earth was very old and shaped by slow processes.
  • Lamarck (early 1800s) proposed that species change over time, through the inheritance of acquired characteristics.
  • Fossils of extinct species challenged the idea that species never change.

Darwin, Wallace and after

  • Charles Darwin and Alfred Russel Wallace independently proposed evolution by natural selection, presented together in 1858.
  • Darwin published On the Origin of Species in 1859.
  • Darwin lacked a mechanism of inheritance; Mendel's work was rediscovered in 1900.
  • The modern synthesis (1930s–1940s) combined natural selection with Mendelian and population genetics.

Lamarck's mechanism, such as giraffes stretching their necks and passing longer necks to offspring, is not supported, because acquired characteristics are not generally inherited through DNA. Darwin's theory explained adaptation and diversity through variation, overproduction, competition and inheritance. Today evolutionary biology also includes molecular evidence, developmental biology and genomics.

Worked example

Lamarck versus Darwin on giraffes.

  1. Lamarck: giraffes stretched to reach leaves, and their longer necks were inherited.
  2. Darwin: giraffes varied in neck length; those with longer necks fed better and left more offspring.
  3. Over generations, the proportion of long-necked giraffes increased.
  4. Only Darwin's explanation fits what we know about inheritance.
Common mistakeDescribing Darwin's theory as "animals adapt because they need to". Selection acts on existing variation; individuals do not change their genes by need.

In 3 lines: Species once seen as fixed. Darwin and Wallace: natural selection. The modern synthesis added genetics.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. When was On the Origin of Species published?
    • 1758
    • 1900
    • 1859
    • 1953
    Show the answer

    Answer: 1859. Darwin published his book in 1859, a year after the joint presentation with Wallace.

  2. Why is Lamarck's mechanism of evolution not supported?
    • Species never change over time
    • Fossils show no extinct species
    • Natural selection has never been observed
    • Characteristics acquired during life are not generally inherited
    Show the answer

    Answer: Characteristics acquired during life are not generally inherited. Changes from use or disuse are not passed on through DNA.

  3. What did Darwin's theory lack that the modern synthesis supplied?
    • A mechanism of inheritance from genetics
    • Any evidence from fossils
    • The idea of variation
    • The concept of competition
    Show the answer

    Answer: A mechanism of inheritance from genetics. Mendelian genetics explained how variation is inherited.

Key terms

Natural selection
Differential survival and reproduction of individuals due to heritable variation
Inheritance of acquired characteristics
Lamarck's unsupported idea that traits gained in life are passed on
Modern synthesis
The integration of natural selection with genetics
