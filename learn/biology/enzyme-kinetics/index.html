Enzyme Kinetics

Reaction rate rises with substrate concentration until active sites are saturated at Vmax; Km is the substrate concentration giving half Vmax, a low Km means high affinity, and v = Vmax[S] ÷ (Km + [S]).

Biology · Biochemistry and Biomolecules · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe how reaction rate changes with substrate concentration
  • Define Vmax and Km
  • Interpret enzyme kinetics graphs

Rate and substrate concentration

  • At low substrate concentration, rate rises steeply as more substrate molecules meet active sites.
  • At high concentration, all active sites are occupied (saturation), so rate levels off.
  • The maximum rate is Vmax.
  • The rate is measured as the initial rate, before substrate is used up.

Vmax and Km

  • Km (the Michaelis constant) is the substrate concentration at which the rate is half of Vmax.
  • A low Km means high affinity: the enzyme reaches half-maximum rate at low substrate concentration.
  • Michaelis-Menten equation: v = Vmax[S] ÷ (Km + [S]).
  • Vmax depends on enzyme concentration; Km is a property of the enzyme and substrate.

Initial rates are found by drawing a tangent to the start of a product-time graph. Plotting initial rate against substrate concentration gives a hyperbolic curve. Comparing Km values tells us which substrate an enzyme prefers or how isoenzymes differ: hexokinase has a low Km for glucose, while glucokinase in the liver has a higher Km, suiting its role in removing excess glucose after meals.

Worked example

Using the Michaelis-Menten equation.

  1. An enzyme has Vmax = 100 µmol min⁻¹ and Km = 2 mM.
  2. At [S] = 2 mM, v = 100 × 2 ÷ (2 + 2) = 50 µmol min⁻¹.
  3. So at [S] = Km, the rate is half of Vmax.
  4. At [S] = 18 mM, v = 100 × 18 ÷ 20 = 90 µmol min⁻¹, approaching Vmax.
Common mistakeThinking a high Km means high affinity. A low Km means the enzyme reaches half-maximal rate at low substrate concentration, so higher affinity.

In 3 lines: Rate rises, then plateaus. Km at half Vmax. Low Km, high affinity.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is Vmax?
    • The minimum substrate concentration
    • The maximum rate when all active sites are saturated
    • The rate at zero substrate
    • The temperature at which the enzyme denatures
    Show the answer

    Answer: The maximum rate when all active sites are saturated. Vmax is the plateau rate at saturating substrate.

  2. Enzyme A has a Km of 0.1 mM and enzyme B a Km of 10 mM for the same substrate. Which has higher affinity?
    • Enzyme B
    • Both are equal
    • Enzyme A
    • Neither binds the substrate
    Show the answer

    Answer: Enzyme A. A lower Km means higher affinity for the substrate.

  3. Why is the initial rate used in enzyme kinetics experiments?
    • The rate is always zero at the start
    • Products have accumulated to their maximum
    • The enzyme has not yet folded
    • Substrate concentration is known and has not yet fallen
    Show the answer

    Answer: Substrate concentration is known and has not yet fallen. Early in the reaction, [S] is effectively constant and product inhibition is minimal.

Key terms

Vmax
The maximum rate of an enzyme-catalysed reaction
Km
The substrate concentration at which the rate is half of Vmax
Saturation
The state in which all active sites are occupied
