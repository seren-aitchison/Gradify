Mitosis

Mitosis proceeds through prophase, metaphase, anaphase and telophase as spindle fibres attached at centromeres separate sister chromatids; cytokinesis divides the cytoplasm by a cleavage furrow in animals and a cell plate in plants, producing identical cells.

Biology · Cell Division and Development · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the stages of mitosis
  • Explain the role of spindle fibres and centromeres
  • Describe cytokinesis in animal and plant cells

Stages

  • Prophase: chromosomes condense; the nuclear envelope breaks down; the spindle forms.
  • Metaphase: chromosomes line up at the equator of the spindle, attached by their centromeres.
  • Anaphase: centromeres divide and sister chromatids are pulled to opposite poles.
  • Telophase: chromatids reach the poles, decondense and nuclear envelopes reform.

Spindle and cytokinesis

  • Spindle fibres are microtubules attached to kinetochores at centromeres.
  • Shortening of fibres pulls chromatids apart.
  • Cytokinesis in animal cells: a cleavage furrow pinches the cell in two.
  • In plant cells: a cell plate forms between the nuclei, becoming a new cell wall.

Mitosis produces two nuclei with identical chromosome sets, so it is used for growth, repair and asexual reproduction. The mitotic index, the proportion of cells in mitosis, can be measured in root tip squashes stained to show chromosomes. Errors in chromatid separation lead to daughter cells with the wrong chromosome number.

Worked example

Calculating mitotic index.

  1. In a root tip squash, a student counts 200 cells.
  2. 30 cells are in prophase, metaphase, anaphase or telophase.
  3. Mitotic index = 30 ÷ 200 = 0.15.
  4. So 15% of cells are dividing, typical of a growing region.
Common mistakeSaying chromosomes pair up in mitosis. Homologous pairing happens in meiosis, not mitosis.

In 3 lines: Prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase. Spindle separates chromatids. Cleavage furrow or cell plate.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. In which stage do chromosomes line up at the equator?
    • Prophase
    • Anaphase
    • Telophase
    • Metaphase
    Show the answer

    Answer: Metaphase. Chromosomes align at the spindle equator in metaphase.

  2. What attaches spindle fibres to chromosomes?
    • Kinetochores at the centromeres
    • Telomeres at the chromosome ends
    • Ribosomes along the arms
    • Histones in the nucleosomes
    Show the answer

    Answer: Kinetochores at the centromeres. Spindle microtubules bind kinetochore proteins.

  3. Why do plant cells form a cell plate rather than a cleavage furrow?
    • They have no cytoplasm to divide
    • Their rigid cell wall prevents the membrane pinching in
    • They lack microtubules entirely
    • They divide only by meiosis
    Show the answer

    Answer: Their rigid cell wall prevents the membrane pinching in. A new wall must be built from the middle outward.

Key terms

Spindle
A structure of microtubules that separates chromosomes
Kinetochore
A protein structure at the centromere where spindle fibres attach
Mitotic index
The proportion of cells undergoing mitosis
