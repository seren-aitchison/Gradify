Recombinant Proteins

Recombinant proteins are produced by expressing cloned genes in hosts grown in bioreactors, then purified by centrifugation and chromatography; examples include insulin, growth hormone, EPO, factor VIII, vaccines and industrial enzymes.

Biology · Biotechnology and Genetic Engineering · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the stages of recombinant protein production
  • Describe methods of protein purification
  • Give examples of recombinant proteins and their uses

Production

  • The gene is cloned into an expression vector and introduced into host cells.
  • Cells are grown in bioreactors under controlled conditions.
  • Protein is either secreted into the medium or extracted by breaking cells.
  • Downstream processing purifies the protein.

Purification and uses

  • Centrifugation removes cells and debris.
  • Chromatography separates proteins, such as affinity chromatography using tags.
  • Quality testing checks purity, activity and safety.
  • Examples: insulin, growth hormone, erythropoietin, clotting factor VIII, hepatitis B vaccine and enzymes for detergents.

Before recombinant insulin, diabetes was treated with insulin from pigs and cows, which caused allergic reactions in some people. Recombinant factor VIII avoids the risk of infections from donated blood, which affected many people with haemophilia in the 1970s and 1980s. Recombinant chymosin is used in most cheese production.

Worked example

Safer clotting factor.

  1. People with haemophilia A lack clotting factor VIII.
  2. Factor VIII from donated blood once transmitted HIV and hepatitis C.
  3. The factor VIII gene was cloned and expressed in mammalian cells.
  4. Recombinant factor VIII is free of blood-borne viruses.
Common mistakeThinking recombinant proteins are less safe than natural ones. They often avoid contamination risks of animal or human sources.

In 3 lines: Clone, express, grow. Purify by chromatography. Medicines and enzymes.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which method separates proteins using a specific binding tag?
    • Gel electrophoresis of DNA
    • Affinity chromatography
    • Centrifugation alone
    • Polymerase chain reaction
    Show the answer

    Answer: Affinity chromatography. Tagged proteins bind a column and others wash through.

  2. Why is recombinant factor VIII safer than factor VIII from blood?
    • It is made from pig blood
    • It needs no purification
    • It cannot carry blood-borne viruses
    • It works without being injected
    Show the answer

    Answer: It cannot carry blood-borne viruses. Production in cells avoids donor infections.

  3. Why did recombinant insulin replace animal insulin?
    • Animal insulin no longer works
    • It is made without any genes
    • It lasts forever in the body
    • It is identical to human insulin and causes fewer reactions
    Show the answer

    Answer: It is identical to human insulin and causes fewer reactions. Human sequence reduced allergic responses.

Key terms

Recombinant protein
A protein made from a cloned gene in a host cell
Affinity chromatography
Purification using specific binding
Bioreactor
A vessel for growing cells under controlled conditions
