Monohybrid Crosses

Monohybrid crosses use symbols, gametes and Punnett squares; Aa × Aa gives a 3:1 phenotype ratio and Aa × aa gives 1:1; a test cross with a homozygous recessive reveals an unknown genotype, and ratios are probabilities.

Biology · Genetics and Molecular Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Construct genetic diagrams and Punnett squares for monohybrid crosses
  • Predict genotype and phenotype ratios
  • Use a test cross to determine an unknown genotype

Setting up a cross

  • Choose symbols: a capital letter for the dominant allele, the same lower-case letter for the recessive.
  • Write parental genotypes and phenotypes.
  • Show the gametes each parent can produce.
  • Combine gametes in a Punnett square to find offspring genotypes.

Expected ratios

  • Heterozygous × heterozygous (Aa × Aa): genotypes 1 AA : 2 Aa : 1 aa; phenotypes 3:1.
  • Heterozygous × homozygous recessive (Aa × aa): 1:1.
  • Homozygous dominant × any: all offspring show the dominant phenotype.
  • Ratios are probabilities; small families often differ from them.

A test cross determines whether an organism showing a dominant phenotype is homozygous or heterozygous: cross it with a homozygous recessive. If any offspring show the recessive phenotype, the parent is heterozygous. Observed results can be compared with expected ratios using the chi-squared test to see whether differences are due to chance.

Worked example

A test cross.

  1. A purple-flowered plant could be PP or Pp.
  2. It is crossed with a white-flowered plant (pp).
  3. If all offspring are purple, the parent was probably PP.
  4. If about half are white, the parent was Pp.
Common mistakeExpecting exactly 3 of every 4 offspring to show the dominant phenotype in a small family. Ratios are probabilities, not guarantees.

In 3 lines: Symbols, gametes, Punnett square. 3:1 and 1:1 ratios. Test cross reveals genotype.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What ratio of phenotypes results from Aa × aa?
    • 1:1
    • 3:1
    • 9:3:3:1
    • All dominant
    Show the answer

    Answer: 1:1. Half the offspring receive A and half receive only a.

  2. A plant with the dominant phenotype is crossed with a homozygous recessive and some offspring show the recessive phenotype. What is its genotype?
    • Homozygous dominant
    • Heterozygous
    • Homozygous recessive
    • It cannot be known
    Show the answer

    Answer: Heterozygous. Recessive offspring must have received a recessive allele from the tested parent.

  3. Why might a family with four children from Aa × Aa parents have no recessive children?
    • The 3:1 ratio is always exact in every family
    • Recessive alleles cannot be inherited
    • Each child's genotype is independent, so ratios are only probabilities
    • Aa parents cannot produce aa offspring
    Show the answer

    Answer: Each child's genotype is independent, so ratios are only probabilities. Chance means small samples often deviate from expected ratios.

Key terms

Monohybrid cross
A cross following one gene
Punnett square
A grid showing possible offspring genotypes
Test cross
A cross with a homozygous recessive to reveal an unknown genotype
