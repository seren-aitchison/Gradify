Introduction to Genetics

Genetics studies heredity and variation; genes are DNA sequences coding for products, the central dogma runs from DNA to RNA to protein, and milestones include Mendel, Avery, the double helix and the Human Genome Project.

Biology · Genetics and Molecular Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define genetics, heredity and variation
  • Describe the relationship between DNA, genes and proteins
  • Outline key milestones in the history of genetics

Core ideas

  • Heredity: the passing of characteristics from parents to offspring.
  • Variation: differences between individuals, caused by genes, environment or both.
  • A gene is a sequence of DNA that codes for a functional product, usually a polypeptide.
  • The central dogma: DNA is transcribed into RNA, which is translated into protein.

Milestones

  • 1866: Gregor Mendel published his work on inheritance in pea plants.
  • 1944: Avery, MacLeod and McCarty showed DNA is the genetic material.
  • 1953: Watson and Crick proposed the double helix, using Franklin's data.
  • 2003: the Human Genome Project completed a reference human genome sequence.

Genetics links molecular biology, cell biology and evolution. Differences in DNA sequence between individuals produce differences in proteins and so in characteristics, while environment affects how genes are expressed. Modern genetics uses sequencing, gene editing and large population studies to understand disease, develop treatments and trace ancestry.

Worked example

From gene to trait.

  1. The gene for an enzyme in melanin production is transcribed into mRNA.
  2. The mRNA is translated into the enzyme.
  3. The enzyme helps produce melanin, the pigment in skin and hair.
  4. A change in the gene can reduce melanin, as in some forms of albinism.
Common mistakeThinking all variation is genetic. Many characteristics are affected by both genes and environment.

In 3 lines: Heredity and variation. DNA to RNA to protein. Key discoveries shaped genetics.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What does the central dogma describe?
    • The flow of information from DNA to RNA to protein
    • The movement of proteins into the nucleus
    • The division of cells by mitosis
    • The structure of the cell membrane
    Show the answer

    Answer: The flow of information from DNA to RNA to protein. DNA is transcribed into RNA, which is translated into protein.

  2. Which experiment showed that DNA is the genetic material?
    • Mendel's crosses of tall and short pea plants
    • Avery, MacLeod and McCarty's transformation experiments
    • The sequencing work of the Human Genome Project
    • Darwin's observations of finches in the Galápagos
    Show the answer

    Answer: Avery, MacLeod and McCarty's transformation experiments. They showed DNA could transform bacteria, carrying genetic information.

  3. Why can two people with the same gene variant show different characteristics?
    • Genes never affect characteristics
    • DNA changes daily
    • Environment also affects how genes are expressed
    • Proteins are not made from genes
    Show the answer

    Answer: Environment also affects how genes are expressed. Many traits depend on gene-environment interaction.

Key terms

Gene
A sequence of DNA coding for a functional product
Heredity
The passing of characteristics from parents to offspring
Central dogma
The flow of genetic information from DNA to RNA to protein
