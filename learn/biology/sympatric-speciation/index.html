Sympatric Speciation

Sympatric speciation occurs without geographic separation, often through polyploidy in plants, where auto- or allopolyploidy creates instant reproductive isolation, or through ecological and behavioural divergence, as in apple maggot flies and cichlids.

Biology · Evolution and Natural Selection · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define sympatric speciation
  • Explain speciation by polyploidy
  • Explain speciation by ecological and behavioural divergence

Polyploidy

  • Polyploidy: having more than two sets of chromosomes, common in plants.
  • Autopolyploidy: duplication within one species, for example after failed meiosis.
  • Allopolyploidy: hybridisation between species followed by chromosome doubling.
  • Polyploids often cannot breed with the parent species, so speciation can be instant.
  • Bread wheat is a hexaploid formed by hybridisations.

Ecological and behavioural divergence

  • Groups specialise on different resources or habitats within one area.
  • Apple maggot flies in North America shifted from hawthorn to apple trees and now mate on their host fruit.
  • Cichlid fish in African lakes diverged through mate choice based on colour.
  • Divergence reduces gene flow even without a barrier.

Sympatric speciation was once considered rare, but polyploidy shows it is common in plants: a large proportion of flowering plant species have polyploidy in their history. In animals, it requires strong disruptive selection or assortative mating to overcome gene flow. Genomic studies help identify cases where it has occurred.

Worked example

Allopolyploid speciation.

  1. Species A (2n = 14) hybridises with species B (2n = 14).
  2. The hybrid (14 chromosomes) is sterile because chromosomes cannot pair.
  3. Chromosome doubling gives 28 chromosomes, so each has a partner.
  4. The new fertile polyploid cannot breed with either parent: a new species.
Common mistakeThinking speciation always needs a physical barrier. Sympatric speciation occurs within one area.

In 3 lines: Same area, new species. Polyploidy in plants. Ecological and behavioural divergence.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which process can produce a new plant species in a single generation?
    • Geographic separation by mountains
    • Gradual drift over millions of years
    • Gene flow between populations
    • Polyploidy
    Show the answer

    Answer: Polyploidy. Polyploids can be instantly isolated from parents.

  2. Why is a hybrid of two species with 14 chromosomes often sterile until doubling occurs?
    • Its chromosomes lack partners to pair with in meiosis
    • It has too many chromosome pairs
    • It cannot carry out mitosis
    • It has no chromosomes
    Show the answer

    Answer: Its chromosomes lack partners to pair with in meiosis. Doubling gives each chromosome a homologue.

  3. Why is sympatric speciation in animals thought to be less common than allopatric speciation?
    • Animals cannot become polyploid at all
    • Gene flow within one area must be overcome by strong selection or assortative mating
    • Animals never live in the same area
    • Sympatric speciation only happens in bacteria
    Show the answer

    Answer: Gene flow within one area must be overcome by strong selection or assortative mating. Ongoing gene flow tends to prevent divergence.

Key terms

Sympatric speciation
Speciation without geographic separation
Polyploidy
Having more than two chromosome sets
Allopolyploidy
Polyploidy from hybridisation between species
