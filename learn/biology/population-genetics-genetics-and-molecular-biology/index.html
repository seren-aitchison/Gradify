Population Genetics

A gene pool contains all alleles in a population; p + q = 1 and p² + 2pq + q² = 1 under Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium, which requires a large, randomly mating population without mutation, migration or selection, and the equation estimates carrier frequencies.

Biology · Genetics and Molecular Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Calculate allele and genotype frequencies
  • State and apply the Hardy-Weinberg equation
  • Explain the conditions required for Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium

Frequencies and Hardy-Weinberg

  • A gene pool is all the alleles in a population.
  • Allele frequency: the proportion of all copies of a gene that are a particular allele.
  • For two alleles with frequencies p and q: p + q = 1.
  • Hardy-Weinberg equation: p² + 2pq + q² = 1, giving frequencies of AA, Aa and aa.

Conditions and uses

  • Equilibrium requires: large population, random mating, no mutation, no migration and no natural selection.
  • Real populations rarely meet these, so changes in frequency indicate evolution.
  • The equation estimates carrier frequency from the frequency of a recessive condition.

For example, if cystic fibrosis affects about 1 in 2,500 people in a population, q² = 0.0004, so q = 0.02 and p = 0.98. The carrier frequency 2pq is about 0.039, roughly 1 in 25. This shows why recessive alleles persist: most copies are hidden in carriers, where selection cannot act on them.

Worked example

Estimating carriers.

  1. A recessive condition affects 1 in 10,000 people: q² = 0.0001.
  2. q = 0.01 and p = 0.99.
  3. 2pq = 2 × 0.99 × 0.01 ≈ 0.0198.
  4. About 1 in 50 people are carriers.
Common mistakeConfusing q² with q. q² is the frequency of the homozygous recessive genotype; q is the allele frequency.

In 3 lines: p + q = 1. p² + 2pq + q² = 1. Conditions rarely met.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. If p = 0.7, what is q?
    • 0.7
    • 0.49
    • 0.3
    • 1.7
    Show the answer

    Answer: 0.3. Allele frequencies add up to 1, so q = 1 − 0.7.

  2. Which situation would disturb Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium?
    • A very large population size
    • Random mating between individuals
    • Absence of any migration
    • Natural selection against one genotype
    Show the answer

    Answer: Natural selection against one genotype. Selection changes allele frequencies over generations.

  3. Why do harmful recessive alleles persist in populations?
    • Most copies are hidden in heterozygous carriers
    • They are always selected for in homozygotes
    • Mutations never create them
    • They only occur in small populations
    Show the answer

    Answer: Most copies are hidden in heterozygous carriers. Selection mainly acts on homozygotes, so carrier alleles persist.
