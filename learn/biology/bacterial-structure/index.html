Bacterial Structure

Bacteria have a peptidoglycan cell wall, membrane, 70S ribosomes, a nucleoid and plasmids, sometimes with capsules, flagella and pili; Gram-positive bacteria have thick walls and stain purple, Gram-negative have thin walls with an outer membrane and stain pink.

Biology · Microbiology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the structures of a bacterial cell
  • Compare Gram-positive and Gram-negative cell walls
  • Describe bacterial shapes

Structures

  • Cell wall made of peptidoglycan.
  • Cell membrane, cytoplasm and 70S ribosomes.
  • Nucleoid: a single circular chromosome, with no nucleus.
  • Plasmids: small circles of extra DNA.
  • Some have a capsule, flagella for movement and pili for attachment.

Gram stain and shapes

  • Gram-positive: thick peptidoglycan wall, stains purple.
  • Gram-negative: thin peptidoglycan and an outer membrane, stains pink.
  • Shapes: cocci (spheres), bacilli (rods) and spirilla (spirals).
  • Cocci may form chains (streptococci) or clusters (staphylococci).

The Gram stain, developed by Hans Christian Gram in 1884, is still one of the first tests used to identify bacteria. The outer membrane of Gram-negative bacteria contains lipopolysaccharide, which can trigger strong immune responses, and it blocks some antibiotics, making these bacteria harder to treat. Some bacteria form endospores, highly resistant dormant structures.

Worked example

Identifying a sample.

  1. A sample from a wound is Gram stained.
  2. Under the microscope, purple spheres in clusters are seen.
  3. They are Gram-positive cocci in clusters.
  4. This suggests Staphylococcus, guiding the choice of antibiotic.
Common mistakeThinking bacteria have no organised structures. They have walls, ribosomes, flagella and other specialised structures.

In 3 lines: Wall, membrane, nucleoid, plasmids. Gram-positive and negative. Cocci, bacilli, spirilla.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is a bacillus?
    • A rod-shaped bacterium
    • A spherical bacterium
    • A spiral bacterium
    • A bacterial virus
    Show the answer

    Answer: A rod-shaped bacterium. Bacilli are rod-shaped, like Bacillus and E. coli.

  2. Why are Gram-negative bacteria often harder to treat?
    • They have thicker peptidoglycan walls
    • Their outer membrane blocks some antibiotics
    • They lack ribosomes
    • They cannot form plasmids
    Show the answer

    Answer: Their outer membrane blocks some antibiotics. The extra membrane is a barrier to many drugs.

  3. Why are endospores a problem in hospitals?
    • They are easily killed by washing
    • They are a form of virus
    • They resist heat, drying and disinfectants
    • They only occur in plants
    Show the answer

    Answer: They resist heat, drying and disinfectants. Endospores survive harsh treatments and can later germinate.

Key terms

Peptidoglycan
The polymer forming bacterial cell walls
Plasmid
A small circular DNA molecule separate from the chromosome
Endospore
A resistant dormant structure formed by some bacteria
