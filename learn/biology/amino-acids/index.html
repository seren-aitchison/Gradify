Amino Acids

Amino acids have an α-carbon with amino, carboxyl, hydrogen and R groups; 20 R groups are classed as non-polar, polar, acidic or basic, amino acids exist as zwitterions at physiological pH, and nine are essential in humans.

Biology · Biochemistry and Biomolecules · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the general structure of an amino acid
  • Classify amino acids by their R groups
  • Explain how amino acids behave in solution

General structure

  • A central α-carbon bonded to an amino group (–NH₂), a carboxyl group (–COOH), a hydrogen atom and an R group.
  • There are 20 amino acids commonly used in proteins, differing only in their R groups.
  • Glycine has the simplest R group: a hydrogen atom.
  • Most amino acids are chiral, and proteins use the L form.

R group classes

  • Non-polar (hydrophobic): such as alanine, valine and leucine.
  • Polar uncharged: such as serine and threonine.
  • Acidic (negatively charged): aspartate and glutamate.
  • Basic (positively charged): lysine, arginine and histidine.
  • Cysteine has a thiol (–SH) group that can form disulfide bridges.

In solution at physiological pH, amino acids exist mainly as zwitterions: the amino group is protonated (–NH₃⁺) and the carboxyl group is deprotonated (–COO⁻). This allows them to act as buffers. Humans cannot synthesise nine amino acids, called essential amino acids, which must come from the diet. R group properties determine how a protein folds, as hydrophobic groups tend to cluster inside globular proteins.

Worked example

Comparing R groups.

  1. Leucine has a non-polar R group and is often found inside a folded protein, away from water.
  2. Lysine has a positively charged R group and is often on the surface.
  3. Cysteines can link through disulfide bridges, stabilising shape.
  4. R group chemistry guides folding.
Common mistakeThinking amino acids differ in their amino or carboxyl groups. They differ only in their R groups.

In 3 lines: Amino, carboxyl, H and R. 20 amino acids, different R groups. Zwitterions in solution.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which group varies between different amino acids?
    • The R group
    • The amino group
    • The carboxyl group
    • The α-hydrogen
    Show the answer

    Answer: The R group. Amino acids share the same backbone and differ in their R groups.

  2. Which amino acid can form disulfide bridges?
    • Glycine
    • Cysteine
    • Leucine
    • Lysine
    Show the answer

    Answer: Cysteine. Cysteine's thiol group can bond with another cysteine.

  3. Where in a globular protein are non-polar R groups usually found?
    • On the surface facing water
    • Only at the ends of the chain
    • Buried in the interior, away from water
    • Outside the cell
    Show the answer

    Answer: Buried in the interior, away from water. Hydrophobic R groups cluster inside the folded protein.

Key terms

R group
The variable side chain that distinguishes amino acids
Zwitterion
A molecule with both positive and negative charges but no net charge
Essential amino acid
An amino acid that must come from the diet
