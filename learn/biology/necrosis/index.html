Necrosis

Necrosis is uncontrolled cell death from severe damage, with swelling, rupture and leakage causing inflammation, unlike controlled apoptosis; it occurs in heart attacks and gangrene, and released proteins such as troponin aid diagnosis.

Biology · Cell Division and Development · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the features of necrosis
  • Compare necrosis with apoptosis
  • Explain the causes and consequences of necrosis

Features and causes

  • Necrosis results from severe damage, such as lack of oxygen, toxins, infection or physical trauma.
  • The cell swells, organelles are damaged and the membrane ruptures.
  • Cell contents leak out, triggering inflammation.
  • It typically affects groups of cells, not single cells.

Comparison with apoptosis

  • Apoptosis: controlled, needs ATP, cell shrinks, no inflammation, often single cells.
  • Necrosis: uncontrolled, cell swells and bursts, causes inflammation, often many cells.
  • Some forms, such as necroptosis, are regulated forms of necrotic death.

In a heart attack, blocked blood flow deprives heart muscle of oxygen, and the affected cells die by necrosis, releasing proteins such as troponin that can be measured in blood for diagnosis. Gangrene is extensive necrosis of tissue, often from poor blood supply or infection. Inflammation from necrosis can damage surrounding healthy tissue.

Worked example

A heart attack.

  1. A coronary artery is blocked by a clot.
  2. Heart muscle cells beyond the block lack oxygen and ATP.
  3. Cells swell and burst, releasing troponin into the blood.
  4. A blood test detects raised troponin, confirming heart muscle damage.
Common mistakeThinking necrosis is a normal part of development. Development uses apoptosis; necrosis follows injury.

In 3 lines: Uncontrolled death. Swelling, rupture, inflammation. Heart attack and gangrene.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which feature describes necrosis?
    • The cell shrinks into tidy apoptotic bodies
    • The cell divides into two identical cells
    • The cell enters the G₀ resting state
    • The cell swells and bursts, releasing its contents
    Show the answer

    Answer: The cell swells and bursts, releasing its contents. In necrosis, cells swell and their membranes rupture, releasing contents.

  2. What usually causes necrosis?
    • Severe injury such as lack of oxygen
    • Normal developmental signals in the embryo
    • Successful DNA repair after damage
    • Activation of the cyclin-CDK complexes
    Show the answer

    Answer: Severe injury such as lack of oxygen. Necrosis follows damage rather than programmed signals.

  3. Why is necrosis more damaging to surrounding tissue than apoptosis?
    • Necrosis affects only one cell at a time
    • Leaked contents trigger inflammation that harms nearby cells
    • Necrosis is controlled by caspases
    • Necrosis does not release anything
    Show the answer

    Answer: Leaked contents trigger inflammation that harms nearby cells. Inflammation from necrosis spreads damage.

Key terms

Necrosis
Uncontrolled cell death from injury
Inflammation
A tissue response to damage involving swelling and immune cells
Troponin
A heart muscle protein released when cells die
