Renal System

The renal system of kidneys, ureters, bladder and urethra excretes urea, controls water, salt and pH, and makes EPO; kidney failure is treated by dialysis or transplant.

Biology · Human Anatomy and Physiology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the organs of the renal system
  • Describe the main functions of the kidneys
  • Describe kidney failure and its treatment

Organs

  • Two kidneys filter blood.
  • Ureters carry urine to the bladder.
  • The bladder stores urine; the urethra carries it out of the body.
  • Each kidney contains about a million nephrons.
  • The kidney has an outer cortex, inner medulla and pelvis.

Functions

  • Excretion of urea and other wastes.
  • Osmoregulation: control of water balance.
  • Control of salt (ion) levels and blood pH.
  • Endocrine roles: making erythropoietin (EPO) and activating vitamin D.

Kidney failure can result from diabetes, high blood pressure or infection. It is treated by dialysis, where blood passes across a partially permeable membrane against dialysis fluid, or by kidney transplant. In the UK, most people on the transplant waiting list are waiting for a kidney. EPO stimulates red blood cell production, so kidney failure often causes anaemia.

Worked example

How haemodialysis works.

  1. Blood is pumped from the patient's arm into a dialysis machine.
  2. It flows past a partially permeable membrane.
  3. Dialysis fluid has normal levels of glucose and salts but no urea.
  4. Urea and excess salts diffuse out, and the cleaned blood is returned.
Common mistakeThinking dialysis fluid contains no glucose or salts. It contains normal levels so that only excess diffuses out.

In 3 lines: Kidneys, ureters, bladder, urethra. Excretion and balance. Dialysis and transplant.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is the functional unit of the kidney?
    • The ureter
    • The bladder
    • The nephron
    • The urethra
    Show the answer

    Answer: The nephron. Each kidney contains about a million nephrons.

  2. Why does dialysis fluid contain normal levels of glucose?
    • So glucose diffuses into the blood
    • To remove urea faster
    • To kill bacteria in the blood
    • So glucose does not diffuse out of the blood
    Show the answer

    Answer: So glucose does not diffuse out of the blood. Equal concentrations prevent loss of useful glucose.

  3. Why does kidney failure often cause anaemia?
    • Less erythropoietin is produced, so fewer red cells are made
    • The kidneys destroy red blood cells
    • Dialysis adds too much iron
    • Urea increases red cell production
    Show the answer

    Answer: Less erythropoietin is produced, so fewer red cells are made. EPO from the kidneys stimulates bone marrow.
