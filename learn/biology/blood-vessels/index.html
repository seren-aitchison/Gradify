Blood Vessels

Arteries have thick elastic walls for high pressure, capillaries have thin walls for exchange and veins have wide lumens and valves for low-pressure return; tissue fluid forms by hydrostatic pressure and returns by osmosis, with excess drained by lymph.

Biology · Human Anatomy and Physiology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the structure of arteries, capillaries and veins
  • Relate the structure of each vessel to its function
  • Explain how tissue fluid forms at capillaries

Structure and function

  • Arteries: thick, elastic, muscular walls and a narrow lumen; carry blood at high pressure away from the heart.
  • Capillaries: walls one cell thick, very narrow; allow exchange of substances with tissues.
  • Veins: thinner walls, wide lumen and valves; carry blood at low pressure back to the heart.
  • Arterioles control blood flow by constricting or dilating.

Tissue fluid

  • At the arterial end of capillaries, hydrostatic pressure forces fluid out into tissues.
  • At the venous end, water returns by osmosis due to plasma proteins.
  • Excess fluid is drained by the lymphatic system.
  • Blockage or protein loss can cause swelling (oedema).

Elastic fibres in arteries stretch and recoil to smooth the pulse of blood from the heart. Veins rely on valves and squeezing by skeletal muscles to return blood; when valves fail, varicose veins can form. Capillary networks are dense in active tissues such as muscle, providing a large surface area for exchange.

Worked example

Why veins have valves.

  1. In the legs, blood must return upwards against gravity.
  2. Pressure in veins is low.
  3. Leg muscles squeeze veins when they contract.
  4. Valves stop blood flowing backwards, so it moves towards the heart.
Common mistakeSaying arteries carry oxygenated blood and veins deoxygenated blood by definition. Arteries carry blood away from the heart; veins carry it back.

In 3 lines: Arteries: thick and elastic. Capillaries: thin for exchange. Veins: valves and wide lumen.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which vessel has valves to prevent backflow?
    • Arteries
    • Veins
    • Capillaries
    • Arterioles
    Show the answer

    Answer: Veins. Valves help low-pressure blood return to the heart.

  2. Why do arteries have elastic walls?
    • To allow exchange of gases
    • To store blood for later use
    • To stretch and recoil, smoothing high-pressure blood flow
    • To stop blood flowing at all
    Show the answer

    Answer: To stretch and recoil, smoothing high-pressure blood flow. Elastic recoil maintains pressure between beats.

  3. Why does fluid return to capillaries at the venous end?
    • Hydrostatic pressure is highest there
    • Valves pump the fluid back
    • Red blood cells absorb the fluid
    • Plasma proteins draw water back by osmosis
    Show the answer

    Answer: Plasma proteins draw water back by osmosis. Lower hydrostatic pressure lets osmosis dominate.

Key terms

Lumen
The central space inside a vessel
Capillary
A microscopic vessel for exchange with tissues
Tissue fluid
Fluid surrounding cells, formed from plasma
