Microscopy

Magnification is image size divided by actual size and resolution is the ability to separate close points; light microscopes view living, stained cells at about 200 nm resolution, while TEM and SEM give much higher resolution of dead samples in a vacuum.

Biology · Microbiology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Distinguish magnification and resolution
  • Compare light and electron microscopes
  • Calculate magnification and actual size

Key ideas

  • Magnification: how many times larger an image is than the object.
  • Resolution: the ability to distinguish two close points as separate.
  • Magnification = image size ÷ actual size.
  • Light microscopes: up to about ×1,500, resolution about 200 nm; can view living cells and use stains.

Electron microscopes

  • Transmission electron microscope (TEM): beam passes through thin sections; very high resolution, around 0.1–1 nm in practice for biological samples.
  • Scanning electron microscope (SEM): beam scans the surface, giving 3D images.
  • Samples must be dead and in a vacuum.
  • Images are black and white, though they may be coloured afterwards.

Fluorescence microscopy uses fluorescent dyes or proteins to label specific structures, and confocal microscopy produces sharp optical sections. Cryo-electron microscopy, which won the 2017 Nobel Prize in Chemistry, reveals the structures of proteins and viruses in near-atomic detail. Electron microscopy first allowed viruses to be seen.

Worked example

Calculating actual size.

  1. An image of a bacterium is 15 mm long.
  2. The magnification is ×5,000.
  3. Actual size = 15 mm ÷ 5,000 = 0.003 mm.
  4. This equals 3 µm.
Common mistakeThinking more magnification always shows more detail. Detail depends on resolution, not just magnification.

In 3 lines: Magnification versus resolution. Light versus electron. Image ÷ actual size.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which microscope gives 3D images of surfaces?
    • Transmission electron microscope
    • Light microscope
    • Scanning electron microscope
    • Hand lens
    Show the answer

    Answer: Scanning electron microscope. SEM scans the surface to produce 3D images.

  2. Why can't electron microscopes view living cells?
    • Electrons are too large
    • They use visible light
    • They magnify too little
    • Samples must be in a vacuum and specially prepared
    Show the answer

    Answer: Samples must be in a vacuum and specially prepared. The vacuum and sample preparation kill the specimen before viewing.

  3. Why could viruses not be seen with light microscopes?
    • Most are smaller than the light microscope's resolution limit
    • They are invisible to electrons
    • They move too fast
    • They dissolve in stains
    Show the answer

    Answer: Most are smaller than the light microscope's resolution limit. Most viruses are under 200 nm across, below the light resolution limit.
