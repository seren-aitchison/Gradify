Accuracy and Precision

Random error affects precision and is reduced by repeats; systematic error affects accuracy and is reduced by calibration.

Biology · Foundations of Biology · about 3 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Distinguish accuracy, precision and resolution
  • Relate random error to precision and systematic error to accuracy
  • Suggest how to improve the accuracy and precision of a measurement

Definitions

  • Accuracy: closeness of a measurement (or mean) to the true value.
  • Precision: closeness of repeated measurements to each other (low spread).
  • Resolution: the smallest change an instrument can show (a ruler marked in mm has a resolution of 1 mm).

Picture darts: a tight cluster away from the bullseye is precise but not accurate; darts spread evenly around the bullseye are accurate on average but imprecise.

Errors

  • Random errors vary unpredictably from one measurement to the next (reaction time with a stopwatch, small fluctuations in temperature). They reduce precision and are reduced by taking more repeats and calculating a mean.
  • Systematic errors shift every measurement in the same direction (a balance that reads 0.5 g too high, parallax from always reading a scale from above). They reduce accuracy and are not reduced by repeating; they must be found and corrected, for example by calibration.

Uncertainty

Every measurement has an uncertainty. A common estimate is half the range of the repeats, or half the resolution for a single reading. Percentage uncertainty = (uncertainty ÷ measured value) × 100.

percentage uncertainty = (uncertainty / value) × 100
Worked example

A student times the same reaction five times: 41, 43, 42, 44 and 40 s. Find the mean and its uncertainty.

  1. Mean = (41 + 43 + 42 + 44 + 40) / 5 = 42 s.
  2. Range = 44 − 40 = 4 s, so uncertainty ≈ ± half the range = ± 2 s.
  3. Percentage uncertainty = 2 / 42 × 100 ≈ 4.8%.
Common mistakeThinking that repeating measurements improves accuracy. Repeats reduce the effect of random error; a systematic error stays in every reading.

In 2 lines: Accurate means close to the truth; precise means consistent. Calibrate to remove systematic error; repeat to reduce random error.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Repeated measurements that are very close together but far from the true value are…
    • accurate but not precise
    • precise but not accurate
    • accurate and precise
    • neither accurate nor precise
    Show the answer

    Answer: precise but not accurate. Low spread means precise; being far from the true value means inaccurate, usually because of a systematic error.

  2. Students always read a measuring cylinder from above the meniscus. What effect does this have?
    • A random error that reduces precision
    • No effect because the error averages out
    • A systematic error that makes all volume readings inaccurate in the same direction
    • It improves the resolution
    Show the answer

    Answer: A systematic error that makes all volume readings inaccurate in the same direction. Reading from the same wrong angle every time (parallax) shifts every reading the same way.

  3. Two methods measure glucose in the same sample. Method A gives 5.1, 5.0, 5.2 mmol/L; method B gives 4.4, 5.8, 5.0 mmol/L. The true value is 5.0 mmol/L. Which statement is correct?
    • B is more precise
    • A is inaccurate
    • B is more accurate because it has a larger range
    • A is more precise, and both have means close to the true value
    Show the answer

    Answer: A is more precise, and both have means close to the true value. A's readings vary much less (higher precision); both means are about 5.1 and 5.07, close to the true value.

Key terms

Accuracy
How close a measured value is to the true value
Precision
How close repeated measurements are to each other
Systematic error
An error that shifts all measurements in the same direction
