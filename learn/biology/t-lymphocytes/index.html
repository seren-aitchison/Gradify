T Lymphocytes

T cells mature in the thymus through positive and negative selection; helper T cells coordinate responses, cytotoxic T cells kill infected cells, regulatory T cells maintain tolerance and memory T cells provide faster responses, all recognising peptides on MHC.

Biology · Immunology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe T cell development in the thymus
  • Distinguish helper, cytotoxic and regulatory T cells
  • Explain how T cells recognise antigen

Development

  • T cell precursors move from bone marrow to the thymus.
  • Each develops a unique T cell receptor (TCR).
  • Positive selection: cells must recognise self-MHC.
  • Negative selection: cells reacting strongly to self are removed.
  • Most developing T cells die in the thymus.

Types

  • Helper T cells (CD4⁺): release cytokines that activate B cells, macrophages and cytotoxic T cells.
  • Cytotoxic T cells (CD8⁺): kill infected and cancer cells.
  • Regulatory T cells: suppress responses and maintain tolerance.
  • Memory T cells: provide faster responses on re-exposure.

T cells recognise antigen only as peptides presented on MHC molecules, not free antigen: CD4 cells recognise MHC class II, and CD8 cells recognise MHC class I. Helper T cells come in subsets, such as Th1, Th2 and Th17, that direct different kinds of response. The thymus shrinks with age, so fewer new T cells are produced in older people.

Worked example

Thymic selection.

  1. A developing T cell has a receptor that cannot bind any self-MHC.
  2. It fails positive selection and dies.
  3. Another binds self-MHC loaded with a self-peptide very strongly.
  4. It fails negative selection and is removed, preventing autoimmunity.
Common mistakeThinking T cells recognise whole pathogens directly. They recognise peptide fragments presented on MHC molecules.

In 3 lines: Selected in the thymus. Helper, cytotoxic, regulatory. Recognise peptide on MHC.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which T cells carry the CD4 marker?
    • Helper T cells
    • Cytotoxic T cells
    • Natural killer cells
    • Plasma cells
    Show the answer

    Answer: Helper T cells. CD4 marks helper T cells, which recognise MHC class II.

  2. What happens during negative selection in the thymus?
    • T cells learn to make antibodies
    • Self-reactive T cells are removed
    • T cells become B cells
    • All T cells survive
    Show the answer

    Answer: Self-reactive T cells are removed. Removing self-reactive cells prevents autoimmunity.

  3. Why do older people produce fewer new T cells?
    • Bone marrow stops working entirely
    • MHC molecules disappear
    • The thymus shrinks with age
    • T cells become B cells
    Show the answer

    Answer: The thymus shrinks with age. The thymus shrinks with age, so it releases fewer new T cells.

Key terms

T cell receptor
The antigen receptor of a T cell
Negative selection
Removal of self-reactive T cells in the thymus
Regulatory T cell
A T cell that suppresses immune responses
