Peripheral Nervous System

The PNS has sensory and motor divisions; the motor division includes the somatic system for voluntary movement and the autonomic system, whose sympathetic and parasympathetic branches have opposing fight-or-flight and rest-and-digest effects on most organs.

Biology · Human Anatomy and Physiology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the divisions of the peripheral nervous system
  • Compare the sympathetic and parasympathetic nervous systems
  • Explain how the autonomic system controls organs

Divisions

  • Sensory (afferent) division carries information to the CNS.
  • Motor (efferent) division carries commands from the CNS.
  • The motor division includes the somatic system (voluntary control of skeletal muscle) and the autonomic system (involuntary control of organs).
  • There are 12 pairs of cranial nerves and 31 pairs of spinal nerves.

Autonomic nervous system

  • Sympathetic: "fight or flight"; increases heart rate, dilates pupils and airways, and slows digestion.
  • Parasympathetic: "rest and digest"; slows heart rate and stimulates digestion.
  • Most organs receive both, with opposing effects.
  • Sympathetic neurones mainly release noradrenaline; parasympathetic neurones release acetylcholine at target organs.

The balance between sympathetic and parasympathetic activity adjusts organ function moment to moment. Beta blockers, used for high blood pressure and anxiety, block some sympathetic effects on the heart. The vagus nerve carries much of the parasympathetic output to the heart, lungs and gut.

Worked example

Fight or flight.

  1. A person sees a dog running towards them.
  2. Sympathetic activity increases.
  3. Heart rate rises, pupils dilate and airways widen.
  4. Digestion slows, and the adrenal glands release adrenaline.
Common mistakeThinking the sympathetic system is always "on" and the parasympathetic "off". Both are active and balance each other.

In 3 lines: Sensory and motor. Somatic and autonomic. Sympathetic versus parasympathetic.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which effect is caused by the sympathetic nervous system?
    • Increased heart rate
    • Increased digestion
    • Constriction of the pupils
    • Slower breathing rate
    Show the answer

    Answer: Increased heart rate. Fight or flight speeds up the heart to deliver more oxygen.

  2. How do beta blockers lower heart rate?
    • They stimulate the sympathetic system
    • They block some sympathetic effects on the heart
    • They increase adrenaline release
    • They block the vagus nerve
    Show the answer

    Answer: They block some sympathetic effects on the heart. Blocking sympathetic receptors reduces heart stimulation.

  3. Why do most organs receive both sympathetic and parasympathetic nerves?
    • Each nerve does the same job twice
    • Only one nerve is ever active
    • Opposing inputs allow fine adjustment of activity
    • Organs need both to survive injury
    Show the answer

    Answer: Opposing inputs allow fine adjustment of activity. Antagonistic control gives precise regulation.

Key terms

Somatic nervous system
The part controlling voluntary skeletal muscles
Sympathetic nervous system
The autonomic branch for fight or flight
Parasympathetic nervous system
The autonomic branch for rest and digest
