Chemistry of Life

Living things are mainly carbon, hydrogen, oxygen and nitrogen, with phosphorus, sulfur and ions; covalent bonds build molecules, weaker bonds shape them, and polymers are made by condensation and broken by hydrolysis.

Biology · Biochemistry and Biomolecules · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Identify the main elements found in living organisms
  • Describe the types of chemical bond important in biology
  • Recognise key functional groups in biological molecules

Elements of life

  • About 96% of the mass of living organisms is carbon, hydrogen, oxygen and nitrogen (CHON).
  • Phosphorus and sulfur are also important, in nucleic acids, ATP and some amino acids.
  • Ions such as Na⁺, K⁺, Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺ and Cl⁻ are needed in small amounts.
  • Carbon forms four covalent bonds, allowing chains, rings and branched skeletons.

Bonds and groups

  • Covalent bonds hold atoms within molecules.
  • Ionic bonds, hydrogen bonds and hydrophobic interactions are weaker and shape large molecules.
  • Functional groups include hydroxyl (–OH), carboxyl (–COOH), amino (–NH₂), phosphate (–PO₄) and methyl (–CH₃).
  • Large biological molecules are often polymers made from monomers joined by condensation reactions and broken by hydrolysis.

Four classes of biological molecule dominate living cells: carbohydrates, lipids, proteins and nucleic acids. Most are polymers built by condensation, which removes a water molecule as each bond forms, and broken down by hydrolysis, which adds water. Weak interactions such as hydrogen bonds are individually easy to break but, together, give large molecules stable shapes that can still change, which is essential for function.

Worked example

Condensation and hydrolysis.

  1. Two glucose molecules join by a condensation reaction to form maltose and water.
  2. In digestion, amylase and maltase catalyse hydrolysis of starch and maltose back into glucose.
  3. The same type of bond is made by removing water and broken by adding it.
  4. This pattern repeats for proteins, nucleic acids and triglycerides.
Common mistakeThinking hydrolysis means "breaking with heat". Hydrolysis breaks bonds by adding water, usually catalysed by enzymes.

In 3 lines: CHON make up most living mass. Weak bonds shape big molecules. Condensation builds; hydrolysis breaks.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which element can form four covalent bonds and so build the skeletons of biological molecules?
    • Sodium
    • Chlorine
    • Helium
    • Carbon
    Show the answer

    Answer: Carbon. Carbon's four bonds allow chains, rings and branched structures.

  2. What happens in a hydrolysis reaction?
    • A bond is broken by adding a water molecule
    • Two molecules join and release water
    • A molecule is heated until it melts
    • An electron is removed from an atom
    Show the answer

    Answer: A bond is broken by adding a water molecule. Hydrolysis uses water to split bonds, often catalysed by enzymes.

  3. Why are weak interactions such as hydrogen bonds important in large biological molecules?
    • They are stronger than covalent bonds
    • Many of them together stabilise shape while still allowing changes needed for function
    • They hold atoms together within each monomer
    • They prevent any change in molecular shape
    Show the answer

    Answer: Many of them together stabilise shape while still allowing changes needed for function. Collectively stable but individually breakable bonds allow both stability and flexibility.

Key terms

Monomer
A small molecule that joins with others to form a polymer
Condensation reaction
A reaction joining molecules with the release of water
Hydrolysis
Breaking a bond by adding water
