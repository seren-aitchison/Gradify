Transmission

Pathogens spread by direct contact, droplets and aerosols, the faecal-oral route, vectors and fomites; reservoirs maintain them, R₀ measures transmissibility, and control measures aim to reduce R below 1.

Biology · Microbiology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the main routes of transmission
  • Explain the role of vectors and reservoirs
  • Describe ways of reducing transmission

Routes

  • Direct contact: touching, sexual contact, such as HIV and gonorrhoea.
  • Droplets and aerosols: coughing and sneezing, such as influenza, TB and COVID-19.
  • Faecal-oral: contaminated food or water, such as cholera and norovirus.
  • Vectors: organisms that carry pathogens, such as mosquitoes and ticks.
  • Fomites: contaminated objects such as door handles.

Reservoirs and control

  • A reservoir is where a pathogen normally lives, such as animals for rabies.
  • R₀: the average number of new infections from one case in a fully susceptible population.
  • Control: hand washing, masks, ventilation, clean water, safe sex, vector control and vaccination.
  • If R falls below 1, an outbreak declines.

John Snow traced a cholera outbreak in London in 1854 to the Broad Street water pump, an early example of epidemiology. Measles has an R₀ of about 12–18, making it one of the most transmissible diseases, so very high vaccine coverage is needed. Different routes require different control measures.

Worked example

John Snow and the Broad Street pump.

  1. In 1854, cholera killed hundreds in Soho, London.
  2. Snow mapped deaths and found they clustered around one water pump.
  3. He persuaded officials to remove the pump handle.
  4. This supported the idea that cholera spreads through contaminated water.
Common mistakeThinking all infections spread the same way. Each route needs specific control measures.

In 3 lines: Contact, droplets, faecal-oral, vectors. Reservoirs and R₀. Control measures.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which disease is spread mainly by the faecal-oral route?
    • Influenza
    • Malaria
    • Cholera
    • HIV
    Show the answer

    Answer: Cholera. Cholera spreads through contaminated water and food.

  2. What happens to an outbreak when R falls below 1?
    • It grows exponentially
    • It stays exactly constant
    • It becomes a pandemic
    • It declines because each case infects fewer than one other
    Show the answer

    Answer: It declines because each case infects fewer than one other. Fewer new cases than old ones shrinks the outbreak.

  3. Why does measles need very high vaccine coverage?
    • Its high R₀ means most people must be immune to stop spread
    • Vaccines do not work against the measles virus at all
    • Vaccines do not work against measles
    • Measles spreads only by vectors
    Show the answer

    Answer: Its high R₀ means most people must be immune to stop spread. Higher R₀ raises the herd immunity threshold.

Key terms

Vector
An organism that transmits a pathogen
Reservoir
Where a pathogen normally lives and multiplies
R₀
The basic reproduction number of an infection
