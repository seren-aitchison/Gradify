Fitness

Fitness is reproductive contribution to the next generation, measured as absolute or relative fitness; inclusive fitness includes helping relatives, and kin selection favours altruism when rB > C, with fitness always relative to environment.

Biology · Evolution and Natural Selection · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define biological fitness
  • Distinguish absolute and relative fitness
  • Explain inclusive fitness and kin selection

Fitness

  • Fitness is an organism's contribution of offspring, and so genes, to the next generation.
  • It depends on survival and reproduction.
  • Absolute fitness: the number of surviving offspring.
  • Relative fitness: fitness compared with the best genotype, often scaled so the fittest is 1.

Inclusive fitness

  • Individuals can pass on genes by helping relatives who share them.
  • Inclusive fitness includes effects on relatives' reproduction.
  • Kin selection favours helping relatives when rB > C (Hamilton's rule), where r is relatedness, B the benefit to the relative and C the cost to the helper.
  • This explains altruism, such as sterile worker bees.

Fitness is always relative to an environment: an allele that increases fitness in one setting may lower it in another. The phrase "survival of the fittest" can mislead, because fitness means reproductive success, not strength. A long-lived individual that leaves no offspring has a fitness of zero in evolutionary terms.

Worked example

Hamilton's rule.

  1. A worker bee helps raise her sisters instead of reproducing.
  2. Because of the haplodiploid system, sisters share about 0.75 of their genes (r = 0.75).
  3. If helping produces several extra sisters at little cost, rB exceeds C.
  4. Kin selection can favour this altruism.
Common mistakeEquating fitness with strength or health. Fitness means reproductive success.

In 3 lines: Fitness = reproductive success. Absolute and relative. Inclusive fitness and kin selection.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which individual has the highest fitness?
    • One that is the strongest but childless
    • One that lives the longest without breeding
    • One with the largest body size only
    • One leaving the most surviving offspring
    Show the answer

    Answer: One leaving the most surviving offspring. Fitness depends on reproductive success, not strength or lifespan.

  2. What does relative fitness compare?
    • A genotype's fitness with the fittest genotype
    • Fitness in different years
    • The strength of individuals
    • Brain size between species
    Show the answer

    Answer: A genotype's fitness with the fittest genotype. Relative fitness scales genotypes against the best.

  3. Why does a long-lived but childless animal have a fitness of zero?
    • Long life always lowers fitness
    • It contributes no offspring to the next generation
    • Fitness only measures speed
    • Childless animals have no genes
    Show the answer

    Answer: It contributes no offspring to the next generation. Without reproduction, it passes on no genes directly.

Key terms

Fitness
Contribution of offspring and genes to the next generation
Relative fitness
Fitness compared with the best genotype
Kin selection
Selection favouring behaviour that helps relatives
