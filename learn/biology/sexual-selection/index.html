Sexual Selection

Sexual selection results from differences in mating success, through competition within one sex or mate choice by the other, producing sexual dimorphism and costly traits that can spread if mating gains outweigh survival costs, often acting as honest signals.

Biology · Evolution and Natural Selection · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define sexual selection
  • Distinguish intrasexual and intersexual selection
  • Explain why sexual selection can favour costly traits

Types

  • Sexual selection arises from differences in mating success.
  • Intrasexual selection: competition within one sex, often males, for mates, such as antlers in deer.
  • Intersexual selection (mate choice): one sex, often females, chooses mates based on traits, such as peacock tails.
  • It often produces sexual dimorphism: differences between males and females.

Costly traits

  • Traits such as large tails or bright colours may reduce survival by attracting predators.
  • They spread if increased mating success outweighs the survival cost.
  • Honest signals: costly traits may show genetic quality, because only healthy individuals can afford them.
  • Females often invest more in offspring, so they are often more choosy.

Darwin introduced sexual selection to explain traits that seemed to reduce survival. Experiments with widowbirds showed that males with artificially lengthened tails attracted more females. Sexual selection can also act on behaviour, such as courtship songs and displays, and can contribute to speciation when mate preferences diverge.

Worked example

Widowbird tails.

  1. Researchers shortened some male widowbird tails and lengthened others.
  2. Males with lengthened tails attracted more females.
  3. Males with shortened tails attracted fewer.
  4. Female choice favours long tails despite their cost.
Common mistakeThinking sexual selection always benefits survival. It can favour traits that reduce survival but increase mating success.

In 3 lines: Competition and choice. Dimorphism and costly traits. Honest signals.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which is an example of intersexual selection?
    • Stags fighting with antlers
    • Bacteria becoming resistant
    • Peahens choosing males with impressive tails
    • Moths changing colour with soot
    Show the answer

    Answer: Peahens choosing males with impressive tails. Mate choice by one sex is intersexual selection.

  2. Why can a costly trait such as a long tail spread?
    • Costly traits always improve survival
    • The trait has no effect on mating
    • Predators prefer short tails
    • Extra mating success outweighs the survival cost
    Show the answer

    Answer: Extra mating success outweighs the survival cost. Reproductive gains from attracting mates can exceed the survival costs.

  3. Why are females often the choosier sex?
    • They often invest more in each offspring
    • They always produce more gametes
    • Males never compete
    • Females do not reproduce
    Show the answer

    Answer: They often invest more in each offspring. Higher investment makes mate choice more important.

Key terms

Sexual selection
Selection based on mating success
Sexual dimorphism
Differences between the sexes of a species
Honest signal
A costly trait that reliably indicates quality
