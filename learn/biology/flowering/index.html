Flowering

Flowers have sepals, petals, stamens and carpels, specified by genes in the ABC model; flowering is triggered by photoperiod, temperature and age, with leaves producing the florigen FT protein that travels to the shoot apex, and some plants need vernalisation.

Biology · Plant Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the structure and parts of a flower
  • Describe how environmental signals trigger flowering
  • Explain the role of florigen and vernalisation

Flower structure

  • Sepals: protect the flower bud.
  • Petals: often coloured to attract pollinators.
  • Stamens (male): anther produces pollen on a filament.
  • Carpels (female): stigma, style and ovary containing ovules.
  • The ABC model explains how genes control which organ forms in each whorl.

Triggering flowering

  • Signals include photoperiod, temperature, age and hormones.
  • Leaves detect day length and produce florigen, identified as the FT protein.
  • FT travels in the phloem to the shoot apex, triggering flowering.
  • Vernalisation: some plants need a period of cold, such as winter wheat.

Grafting experiments showed that a signal from induced leaves could make another plant flower, long before FT was identified in 2007. Flowering at the right time matters: flowering too early risks frost, too late risks missing pollinators. Climate change is shifting flowering times, with some UK plants now flowering weeks earlier than in past decades.

Worked example

Vernalisation in winter wheat.

  1. Winter wheat is sown in autumn.
  2. It grows leaves through winter cold.
  3. The cold period allows flowering genes to be expressed in spring.
  4. If sown in spring without cold, it does not flower properly.
Common mistakeThinking flowers are only for decoration. Flowers are the reproductive organs of flowering plants.

In 3 lines: Sepals, petals, stamens, carpels. Florigen from leaves. Vernalisation needs cold.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which part of the flower produces pollen?
    • The anther
    • The stigma
    • The ovary
    • The sepal
    Show the answer

    Answer: The anther. Anthers at the top of stamens release pollen.

  2. How does the flowering signal travel from leaves to the shoot apex?
    • As a gas through the air
    • As FT protein in the phloem
    • As water in the xylem
    • Through root hairs
    Show the answer

    Answer: As FT protein in the phloem. FT moves through phloem to the growing tip.

  3. Why is the timing of flowering important for a plant?
    • Timing has no effect on reproduction
    • Plants flower only once in history
    • Flowering must match pollinators and avoid frost
    • Flowers are produced at random
    Show the answer

    Answer: Flowering must match pollinators and avoid frost. Mistimed flowering reduces seed production.

Key terms

Florigen
The mobile signal that triggers flowering, the FT protein
Vernalisation
A cold period needed for some plants to flower
Carpel
The female reproductive organ of a flower
