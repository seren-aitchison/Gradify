Neurones

Neurones have a cell body, dendrites, an axon and terminals; sensory, relay and motor neurones carry signals in defined directions, and myelin made by glial cells allows fast saltatory conduction, which is lost in multiple sclerosis.

Biology · Human Anatomy and Physiology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the structure of a neurone
  • Distinguish sensory, relay and motor neurones
  • Explain the role of myelination and glial cells

Structure

  • Cell body: contains the nucleus and most organelles.
  • Dendrites: branched extensions that receive signals.
  • Axon: a long fibre carrying impulses away from the cell body.
  • Axon terminals: release neurotransmitters at synapses.
  • Many axons are covered by a myelin sheath with gaps called nodes of Ranvier.

Types and support

  • Sensory neurones carry impulses from receptors to the CNS.
  • Relay (inter) neurones connect neurones within the CNS.
  • Motor neurones carry impulses from the CNS to effectors.
  • Glial cells support neurones: Schwann cells and oligodendrocytes make myelin.

Myelin insulates the axon, so impulses jump between nodes of Ranvier in saltatory conduction, which is much faster than in unmyelinated fibres. Myelinated axons can conduct at up to about 120 m/s. In multiple sclerosis, the immune system damages myelin in the CNS, slowing or blocking impulses.

Worked example

Myelin and speed.

  1. An unmyelinated axon conducts impulses at about 1 m/s.
  2. A large myelinated motor axon conducts at up to about 120 m/s.
  3. Impulses jump between nodes of Ranvier.
  4. This allows rapid signalling over long distances, such as from spinal cord to foot.
Common mistakeThinking myelin conducts the impulse. Myelin insulates; the impulse jumps between gaps in it.

In 3 lines: Cell body, dendrites, axon. Sensory, relay, motor. Myelin speeds conduction.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which part of a neurone carries impulses away from the cell body?
    • The axon
    • The dendrites
    • The nucleus
    • The myelin sheath
    Show the answer

    Answer: The axon. Axons conduct impulses towards the axon terminals.

  2. What is saltatory conduction?
    • Impulses travelling along dendrites
    • Impulses jumping between nodes of Ranvier
    • Neurotransmitter diffusing across a synapse
    • Myelin carrying the electrical charge
    Show the answer

    Answer: Impulses jumping between nodes of Ranvier. Myelin makes the impulse leap from node to node.

  3. Why does multiple sclerosis slow nerve impulses?
    • It increases the number of nodes of Ranvier
    • It makes axons longer
    • Damage to myelin prevents fast saltatory conduction
    • It speeds up neurotransmitter release
    Show the answer

    Answer: Damage to myelin prevents fast saltatory conduction. Loss of insulation slows or blocks impulses.

Key terms

Axon
A fibre carrying impulses away from the cell body
Myelin sheath
An insulating layer around many axons
Saltatory conduction
Impulses jumping between nodes of Ranvier
