Water and Biological Systems

Water is polar and forms hydrogen bonds, making it an excellent solvent with high specific heat capacity, high latent heat of vaporisation, strong cohesion and less dense ice, all of which support life.

Biology · Biochemistry and Biomolecules · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain why water is a polar molecule
  • Describe the properties of water that are important for life
  • Relate water's properties to examples in organisms and habitats

Structure and polarity

  • Oxygen is more electronegative than hydrogen, so it pulls shared electrons towards itself.
  • The oxygen end is slightly negative (δ−) and the hydrogen ends slightly positive (δ+).
  • The bent shape (about 104.5°) makes the whole molecule polar.
  • Water molecules form hydrogen bonds with each other.

Key properties

  • Solvent: dissolves ions and polar molecules, so reactions occur in solution and substances are transported.
  • High specific heat capacity: temperature changes slowly, stabilising cells and aquatic habitats.
  • High latent heat of vaporisation: evaporation cools organisms, as in sweating and transpiration.
  • Cohesion and surface tension: water columns in xylem and pond-skater habitats.
  • Ice is less dense than liquid water, so ice floats and insulates water below.

Many of water's properties come from hydrogen bonding between molecules, which requires energy to break. This explains its high boiling point for a small molecule, its high heat capacity and its cohesion. Water is also a reactant, in hydrolysis and photosynthesis, and a product, in condensation and respiration. Non-polar molecules, such as lipids, do not dissolve in water, which drives membrane formation.

Worked example

Water in a plant.

  1. Water evaporates from leaves, cooling them (latent heat of vaporisation).
  2. Cohesion between water molecules pulls a continuous column up the xylem.
  3. Adhesion to xylem walls helps the column move.
  4. Minerals travel dissolved in the water (solvent property).
Common mistakeSaying water molecules are held together by hydrogen bonds within the molecule. Covalent bonds join O and H; hydrogen bonds form between molecules.

In 3 lines: Water is polar. Hydrogen bonds explain many properties. Solvent, heat buffer, coolant, cohesive.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Why is water described as polar?
    • Water has no charge at all
    • Water contains ions only
    • Oxygen pulls electrons more strongly, giving partial charges
    • Hydrogen is more electronegative than oxygen
    Show the answer

    Answer: Oxygen pulls electrons more strongly, giving partial charges. Uneven electron sharing gives δ− oxygen and δ+ hydrogens.

  2. Which property helps keep lake temperatures stable through the day?
    • Low boiling point
    • Low surface tension
    • Ice sinking below liquid water
    • High specific heat capacity
    Show the answer

    Answer: High specific heat capacity. A high specific heat capacity means water temperature changes slowly.

  3. Why does ice floating benefit aquatic life in winter?
    • It forms an insulating layer so water below stays liquid
    • It makes the water warmer than air
    • It increases oxygen in the water
    • It stops light reaching the water entirely
    Show the answer

    Answer: It forms an insulating layer so water below stays liquid. Ice on the surface insulates the liquid water where organisms live.

Key terms

Polar molecule
A molecule with an uneven distribution of charge
Specific heat capacity
Energy needed to raise the temperature of 1 kg of a substance by 1 °C
Cohesion
Attraction between molecules of the same substance
