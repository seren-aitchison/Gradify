DNA Sequence Analysis

DNA sequences are stored in FASTA format and analysed by reverse complementing, translating in six frames to find ORFs and calculating GC content, which affects stability and varies between genomes.

Biology · Bioinformatics and Computational Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe common file formats for sequences
  • Explain how open reading frames are found
  • Calculate GC content and describe its significance

Basic analysis

  • Sequences are often stored in FASTA format: a header line starting with ">" and the sequence.
  • Reverse complement: the sequence of the opposite strand, read 5′ to 3′.
  • Translation in six reading frames (three on each strand).
  • Open reading frames (ORFs): stretches from a start codon (ATG) to a stop codon.

GC content and motifs

  • GC content: the percentage of bases that are G or C.
  • GC pairs have three hydrogen bonds, so high-GC DNA is more stable.
  • GC content varies between species and genome regions.
  • Motifs, such as promoter sequences and restriction sites, can be searched for.

Long ORFs suggest protein-coding genes, especially in bacteria, where genes lack introns. In eukaryotes, gene finding is harder because of introns and alternative splicing. Primer design for PCR considers GC content so primers bind at suitable temperatures.

Worked example

Calculating GC content.

  1. A sequence has 100 bases.
  2. It contains 22 G and 26 C.
  3. GC content = (22 + 26) ÷ 100 × 100 = 48%.
  4. The remaining 52% are A and T.
Common mistakeThinking every ORF is a real gene. Many short ORFs occur by chance and need further evidence.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which character begins the header line in a FASTA file?
    • >
    • #
    • @
    • %
    Show the answer

    Answer: >. The ">" symbol marks the description line.

  2. Why is high-GC DNA more stable?
    • G-C pairs form one hydrogen bond
    • G-C pairs form three hydrogen bonds
    • G and C are heavier bases
    • A-T pairs are covalent
    Show the answer

    Answer: G-C pairs form three hydrogen bonds. More hydrogen bonds hold strands together more strongly.

  3. Why is gene finding harder in eukaryotes than bacteria?
    • Eukaryotes have no start codons
    • Bacteria have no genes
    • Introns interrupt coding sequences
    • Eukaryotic DNA cannot be sequenced
    Show the answer

    Answer: Introns interrupt coding sequences. Split genes do not form single long ORFs.

Key terms

FASTA
A simple text format for sequences
Open reading frame
A sequence from a start codon to a stop codon
GC content
The percentage of G and C bases in DNA
