Water Cycle

Water cycles by evaporation, transpiration, condensation, precipitation, infiltration and runoff; plants return water to the air and forests regulate rainfall and runoff, while deforestation and urbanisation disrupt the cycle.

Biology · Ecology and Environmental Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the processes of the water cycle
  • Explain the role of plants in the water cycle
  • Describe human impacts on the water cycle

Processes

  • Evaporation from oceans, lakes and soil.
  • Transpiration from plants.
  • Condensation forms clouds.
  • Precipitation: rain, snow and hail.
  • Infiltration into soil and runoff into rivers.
  • Water is stored in oceans, ice, groundwater, lakes and the atmosphere.

Living things and humans

  • Plants return large amounts of water to the air by transpiration.
  • Forests influence local rainfall and reduce runoff.
  • Deforestation reduces transpiration and rainfall and increases flooding and erosion.
  • Urban areas with hard surfaces increase runoff.

The Amazon rainforest generates much of its own rainfall through transpiration, and large-scale deforestation could push parts of it towards savanna. About 97% of Earth's water is salty; most fresh water is locked in ice and groundwater. Climate change is intensifying the water cycle, causing more extreme rainfall and droughts.

Worked example

Deforestation and flooding.

  1. A hillside forest is cut down.
  2. Rain hits bare soil instead of leaves.
  3. Less water infiltrates and more runs off quickly.
  4. Rivers downstream flood more often, carrying eroded soil.
Common mistakeThinking plants play little role in the water cycle. Transpiration returns large amounts of water to the atmosphere.

In 3 lines: Evaporation and precipitation. Plants and transpiration. Human impacts.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which process forms clouds?
    • Evaporation
    • Infiltration
    • Condensation
    • Runoff
    Show the answer

    Answer: Condensation. Water vapour cools and condenses into droplets.

  2. How does deforestation increase flooding?
    • Trees cause more rain to fall
    • It increases transpiration
    • It stores more water in soil
    • Less water infiltrates and more runs off quickly
    Show the answer

    Answer: Less water infiltrates and more runs off quickly. Without trees, rain reaches rivers faster.

  3. Why could Amazon deforestation reduce rainfall in the region?
    • Less transpiration means less water vapour for rain
    • More trees grow in cleared areas
    • Deforestation increases evaporation from oceans
    • Rain only comes from the sea
    Show the answer

    Answer: Less transpiration means less water vapour for rain. The forest recycles much of its own rainfall.

Key terms

Transpiration
Water loss from plants to the atmosphere
Infiltration
Water soaking into the ground
Runoff
Water flowing over land into rivers
