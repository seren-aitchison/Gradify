Cell Theory

Every organism is made of cells, and every cell comes from another cell: life is continuous at the cellular level.

Biology · Cell Biology · about 3 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • State the three principles of cell theory
  • Describe the observations that led to it, and who made them
  • Calculate magnification and actual size from a microscope image

The three principles

  • All living organisms are made of one or more cells.
  • The cell is the basic unit of structure and function in living things.
  • All cells arise from pre-existing cells, by division.

Modern versions add that cells contain hereditary information (DNA) that is passed on when they divide, and that the basic chemistry of life happens inside cells.

How the idea was built

Robert Hooke described the "cells" of cork in Micrographia (1665): he saw the empty walls of dead plant cells. Antonie van Leeuwenhoek, with much better single-lens microscopes, saw living bacteria and protists in the 1670s. In 1838–39 Matthias Schleiden (plants) and Theodor Schwann (animals) proposed that all organisms are made of cells. Robert Remak and then Rudolf Virchow (1855, "omnis cellula e cellula") established that cells come only from other cells, ending the idea that cells form spontaneously.

Seeing cells: magnification and resolution

Most cells are 1–100 µm across, too small to see unaided. Magnification is how much bigger the image is than the object; resolution is the smallest separation at which two points can still be told apart. A light microscope resolves about 0.2 µm, enough for nuclei and chloroplasts; electron microscopes resolve below 1 nm, enough for ribosomes and membranes.

magnification = image size / actual size
Worked example

A cell is 25 mm across in a photograph taken at ×500. What is its actual size?

  1. Actual size = image size ÷ magnification = 25 mm ÷ 500 = 0.05 mm.
  2. 0.05 mm × 1000 = 50 µm.
  • The theory rests on observations made with better and better microscopes: Hooke saw the walls of dead cork cells (1665), van Leeuwenhoek saw living single-celled organisms (1670s), Schleiden and Schwann found cells in every plant and animal they examined (1838–39), and observations of cells dividing led Remak and Virchow to conclude that cells come only from cells (1850s).
Common mistakeConfusing magnification with resolution. Enlarging a blurry image more doesn't reveal more detail; only better resolution does.

In 3 lines: Cell theory rests on three principles: cells make up all organisms, are their basic unit, and come from existing cells. It was built from microscope evidence between 1665 and the 1850s. Magnification = image size ÷ actual size; resolution limits the detail you can see.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Who stated that all cells arise from pre-existing cells?
    • Robert Hooke
    • Gregor Mendel
    • Rudolf Virchow
    • Charles Darwin
    Show the answer

    Answer: Rudolf Virchow. Virchow popularised "omnis cellula e cellula" in 1855, building on Remak's work; Hooke named cells, Mendel studied inheritance and Darwin evolution.

  2. A student says a light microscope at ×2000 should show ribosomes clearly. Why is this wrong?
    • Ribosomes are transparent to light
    • Ribosomes are only found in bacteria
    • Magnification above ×1000 is impossible
    • Ribosomes are about 25 nm across, far below the light microscope's resolution of about 0.2 µm
    Show the answer

    Answer: Ribosomes are about 25 nm across, far below the light microscope's resolution of about 0.2 µm. Resolution, not magnification, decides what can be seen; ribosomes need an electron microscope.

  3. Which observation would most directly challenge the third principle of cell theory?
    • A new cell forming from non-living chemicals in a sealed sterile flask
    • A cell with no nucleus
    • A very large single-celled organism
    • Cells dividing faster in warm conditions
    Show the answer

    Answer: A new cell forming from non-living chemicals in a sealed sterile flask. Only the formation of a cell without a parent cell contradicts "all cells arise from pre-existing cells"; the others are consistent with cell theory.

Key terms

Cell theory
All organisms are made of cells, the cell is the basic unit of life, and cells arise only from existing cells
Magnification
How many times larger an image is than the real object
Resolution
The smallest distance between two points that can still be distinguished as separate
