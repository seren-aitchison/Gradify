Statistical Foundations

Summarise data with an average and a measure of spread, and use a statistical test to judge whether a difference could be due to chance.

Biology · Foundations of Biology · about 3 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Calculate mean, median, mode, range and standard deviation
  • Describe the normal distribution and the meaning of standard deviation
  • Choose an appropriate statistical test and interpret a p-value

Averages and spread

  • Mean: the sum divided by the number of values. Median: the middle value when ordered. Mode: the most frequent value.
  • Range: largest minus smallest; sensitive to outliers.
  • Standard deviation (s): the typical distance of values from the mean.
s = √( Σ(x − x̄)² / (n − 1) )sample standard deviation

The normal distribution

Many continuous biological variables (heights, leaf lengths) follow a roughly bell-shaped normal distribution. About 68% of values lie within one standard deviation of the mean and about 95% within two.

Choosing a test

  • Student's t-test: compare the means of two groups of normally distributed data.
  • Chi-squared test: compare observed counts in categories with expected counts (for example genetic ratios).
  • Spearman's rank or Pearson's correlation: test the strength of a correlation between two variables.

Each test gives a statistic and a p-value: the probability of results at least as extreme if the null hypothesis were true. Biologists usually use p < 0.05 as the threshold for statistical significance.

Worked example

Find the mean and standard deviation of 4, 6, 7, 7 and 6.

  1. Mean = 30 ÷ 5 = 6.
  2. Squared deviations: 4, 0, 1, 1, 0; sum = 6.
  3. s = √(6 ÷ 4) = √1.5 ≈ 1.22.
Common mistakeThinking "statistically significant" means "large" or "important". It means the result would be unlikely if there were no real effect; a tiny effect can be significant in a large sample.

In 3 lines: Mean, median and mode summarise the centre; range and standard deviation the spread. About 95% of normally distributed values lie within two standard deviations of the mean. Choose t-tests for means, chi-squared for counts, correlation tests for relationships.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which measure of spread is most affected by a single extreme value?
    • The standard deviation
    • The median
    • The interquartile range
    • The range
    Show the answer

    Answer: The range. The range uses only the largest and smallest values, so one outlier changes it completely.

  2. Which test should be used to compare mean leaf length in two populations of normally distributed data?
    • A t-test
    • A chi-squared test
    • Spearman's rank correlation
    • No test is needed
    Show the answer

    Answer: A t-test. A t-test compares two means; chi-squared is for counts and correlation tests for relationships.

  3. Group A has mean 50 and standard deviation 2; group B has mean 52 and standard deviation 10. Both groups have 10 measurements. Which statement is best?
    • B is clearly larger because its mean is higher
    • B's large spread means the 2-unit difference in means may well be due to chance; a statistical test is needed
    • A is more variable than B
    • The difference must be significant because the means differ
    Show the answer

    Answer: B's large spread means the 2-unit difference in means may well be due to chance; a statistical test is needed. Overlap between groups depends on spread as well as means; with B so variable, the difference could easily arise by chance.

Key terms

Standard deviation
A measure of how spread out values are around the mean
Normal distribution
A symmetrical, bell-shaped distribution of a continuous variable
p-value
The probability of obtaining results at least as extreme as those observed if the null hypothesis were true
