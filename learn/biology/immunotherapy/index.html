Immunotherapy

Immunotherapy includes monoclonal antibodies, checkpoint inhibitors that release T cell brakes, CAR-T cells engineered to target cancer and cancer vaccines; it can give lasting responses but not all patients respond and side effects include immune-related damage and cytokine release syndrome.

Biology · Immunology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe checkpoint inhibitors
  • Describe CAR-T cell therapy
  • Evaluate the benefits and risks of immunotherapy

Approaches

  • Monoclonal antibodies target specific molecules, such as trastuzumab against HER2 in breast cancer.
  • Checkpoint inhibitors block PD-1, PD-L1 or CTLA-4, releasing the brakes on T cells.
  • CAR-T cell therapy: a patient's T cells are engineered with a chimeric antigen receptor, expanded and returned.
  • Cancer vaccines stimulate responses to tumour antigens.

Benefits and risks

  • Some patients with advanced cancers have long-lasting responses.
  • Not all patients respond, and biomarkers help predict who will.
  • Side effects include immune-related adverse events, as T cells attack normal tissues.
  • CAR-T can cause cytokine release syndrome and is expensive.

James Allison and Tasuku Honjo received the 2018 Nobel Prize for discovering checkpoint inhibition through CTLA-4 and PD-1. CAR-T therapies targeting CD19 have produced remissions in some children and adults with leukaemia and lymphoma resistant to other treatments. Personalised mRNA cancer vaccines are being tested in trials, including in the NHS.

Worked example

Making CAR-T cells.

  1. T cells are collected from a patient's blood.
  2. They are engineered with a gene for a receptor targeting CD19 on leukaemia cells.
  3. The cells are grown into millions and infused back.
  4. The CAR-T cells recognise and kill the cancer cells.
Common mistakeThinking immunotherapy works for every cancer patient. Many patients do not respond, and biomarkers guide use.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which molecule do many checkpoint inhibitors block?
    • Insulin
    • Haemoglobin
    • Histamine
    • PD-1
    Show the answer

    Answer: PD-1. Blocking PD-1 removes a brake on T cells.

  2. Why can checkpoint inhibitors cause side effects in normal tissues?
    • Released T cells can attack healthy tissues
    • They kill all white blood cells
    • They stop T cells working
    • They only affect bacteria
    Show the answer

    Answer: Released T cells can attack healthy tissues. Removing brakes can trigger autoimmune-like damage.

  3. What is a major risk of CAR-T therapy?
    • Complete loss of red blood cells
    • Cytokine release syndrome from massive T cell activation
    • Instant cure of all cancers
    • Inability to make any antibodies ever
    Show the answer

    Answer: Cytokine release syndrome from massive T cell activation. Strong activation can release dangerous cytokine levels.

Key terms

Checkpoint inhibitor
A drug blocking signals that switch off T cells
CAR-T cell
A T cell engineered with a chimeric antigen receptor
Immune-related adverse event
A side effect from immune attack on normal tissue
