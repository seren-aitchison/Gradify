Fungi

Fungi are eukaryotes with chitin walls, existing as yeasts or as hyphae forming mycelia; they feed saprotrophically by external digestion, act as decomposers and mycorrhizal partners, are used in food and medicine and can cause disease.

Biology · Microbiology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the structure of fungi
  • Explain how fungi obtain nutrients
  • Describe the ecological and economic importance of fungi

Structure

  • Fungi are eukaryotes with cell walls made of chitin.
  • Yeasts are single-celled; moulds grow as hyphae, forming a mycelium.
  • Mushrooms are fruiting bodies that produce spores.
  • Fungi reproduce by spores, sexually or asexually; yeasts also bud.

Nutrition and importance

  • Fungi are heterotrophs that digest food externally by secreting enzymes and absorbing the products (saprotrophic nutrition).
  • They are major decomposers, recycling nutrients.
  • Mycorrhizae help plant roots absorb nutrients.
  • Uses: bread, beer, antibiotics (penicillin), Quorn (mycoprotein).
  • Some cause disease, such as athlete's foot, thrush and ash dieback.

Fungi are more closely related to animals than to plants. Fungal infections in humans can be hard to treat because fungal cells are similar to human cells, limiting drug targets. Ash dieback, caused by Hymenoscyphus fraxineus, is expected to kill most ash trees in the UK. The fungal kingdom may contain millions of species, most undescribed.

Worked example

How bread rises.

  1. Yeast is mixed into dough with sugar and water.
  2. Yeast respires anaerobically, producing ethanol and carbon dioxide.
  3. CO₂ bubbles make the dough rise.
  4. Baking kills the yeast and evaporates the ethanol.
Common mistakeThinking fungi are plants. Fungi are a separate kingdom, more closely related to animals.

In 3 lines: Yeasts and moulds. External digestion. Decomposers, uses and disease.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. How do fungi obtain nutrients?
    • By photosynthesis using chloroplasts in their hyphae
    • By engulfing food particles
    • By secreting enzymes and absorbing digested food
    • By fixing nitrogen gas
    Show the answer

    Answer: By secreting enzymes and absorbing digested food. Saprotrophic fungi digest food outside their cells.

  2. Why are fungal infections hard to treat?
    • Fungi have no cell walls for any drug to attack
    • Fungi have no cell walls
    • All antibiotics kill fungi easily
    • Fungal cells are similar to human cells, limiting drug targets
    Show the answer

    Answer: Fungal cells are similar to human cells, limiting drug targets. Shared eukaryotic features make selective drugs harder to find.

  3. Why are fungi important in ecosystems?
    • They decompose dead material and recycle nutrients
    • They produce most oxygen
    • They are the main herbivores
    • They fix carbon by photosynthesis
    Show the answer

    Answer: They decompose dead material and recycle nutrients. Fungal decomposition returns nutrients to the soil.

Key terms

Hypha
A thread-like filament of a fungus
Mycelium
A network of hyphae
Saprotrophic nutrition
Feeding by external digestion of dead matter
