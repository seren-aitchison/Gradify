Classification and Taxonomy

Classification uses nested ranks and binomial names, and aims to group organisms by common ancestry.

Biology · Foundations of Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Put the taxonomic ranks in order
  • Write a species name correctly using binomial nomenclature
  • Explain why modern classification is based on evolutionary relationships

The hierarchy of ranks

The ranks, from broadest to narrowest, are domain, kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus and species. Humans are domain Eukarya, kingdom Animalia, phylum Chordata, class Mammalia, order Primates, family Hominidae, genus Homo, species Homo sapiens.

Binomial nomenclature

Carl Linnaeus (1707–1778) introduced two-part Latin names: the genus (capitalised) and the specific epithet (lower case), written in italics, or underlined when handwritten. Names are governed by international codes so that every species has one valid scientific name, avoiding confusion between common names (a "robin" is a different bird in Britain and North America).

Classification and evolution

Linnaeus grouped organisms by visible similarities. Today, phylogenetic classification groups organisms by shared ancestry, using DNA sequences as well as anatomy. Groups should be monophyletic: an ancestor and all its descendants. Some traditional groups failed this test: "reptiles" in the old sense excluded birds, though birds evolved from reptile ancestors.

What is a species?

The biological species concept defines a species as a group of organisms that can interbreed to produce fertile offspring. It doesn't work for asexual organisms or fossils, so biologists also use genetic and morphological species concepts.

Common mistakeWriting "homo Sapiens" or "Homo Sapiens". The genus is capitalised and the species epithet is not: Homo sapiens.

In 3 lines: Domain, kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus, species. Species names are binomials, written in italics with a capital only on the genus. Modern classification follows evolutionary relationships.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which is the correct way to write the scientific name of the lion?
    • Panthera Leo
    • Panthera leo
    • panthera leo
    • Panthera-Leo
    Show the answer

    Answer: Panthera leo. The genus is capitalised, the specific epithet lower case, and both are italicised.

  2. Two populations of frogs look identical but don't interbreed in the wild and their hybrids in the laboratory are sterile. Under the biological species concept they are…
    • the same species
    • the same subspecies
    • separate species
    • different genera
    Show the answer

    Answer: separate species. Reproductive isolation with sterile hybrids means they are separate biological species despite looking alike (cryptic species).

  3. Birds are now classified within the reptile clade. What is the main reason?
    • Birds lay eggs
    • Birds have scales on their legs
    • Linnaeus classified them as reptiles
    • DNA and fossil evidence show birds descend from reptilian (dinosaur) ancestors, so a monophyletic reptile group must include them
    Show the answer

    Answer: DNA and fossil evidence show birds descend from reptilian (dinosaur) ancestors, so a monophyletic reptile group must include them. A monophyletic group includes all descendants of a common ancestor; excluding birds from "reptiles" would leave a group that isn't monophyletic.

Key terms

Taxonomy
The science of naming, describing and classifying organisms
Binomial nomenclature
The two-part scientific naming system of genus and specific epithet
Monophyletic group
A group containing an ancestor and all of its descendants
