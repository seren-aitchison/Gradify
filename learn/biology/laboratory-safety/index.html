Laboratory Safety

A hazard can cause harm and risk is its likelihood and severity; risk assessments under COSHH identify controls following the hierarchy of controls, and safe practice uses hazard pictograms, safety data sheets, PPE, emergency equipment and incident reporting.

Biology · Experimental Biology and Research Methods · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Distinguish hazard and risk
  • Interpret hazard symbols and safety data
  • Describe safe practice and emergency procedures

Hazards and risk

  • A hazard is something that could cause harm.
  • Risk is the likelihood and severity of harm.
  • Risk assessment identifies hazards, who could be harmed and control measures.
  • COSHH regulations cover hazardous substances in the UK.

Safe practice

  • GHS hazard pictograms show dangers such as flammable, corrosive and toxic.
  • Safety data sheets give handling, storage and first aid information.
  • Wear appropriate PPE: lab coat, eye protection and gloves.
  • Know the location of eyewash stations, fire extinguishers and exits.
  • Report accidents and near misses.

The hierarchy of controls prefers eliminating or substituting hazards before relying on PPE. Sharps go in dedicated containers, and broken glass is cleared with a dustpan, not hands. In schools, CLEAPSS provides model risk assessments.

Worked example

A risk assessment for a practical.

  1. A practical uses 1 mol per dm³ hydrochloric acid.
  2. Hazard: the acid is an irritant to skin and eyes.
  3. Control measures: eye protection, small volumes and rinsing spills with water.
  4. With these controls, the risk is low.
Common mistakeThinking PPE is the first line of defence. Eliminating or reducing hazards comes first; PPE is the last resort.

In 3 lines: Hazard versus risk. Pictograms and data sheets. Hierarchy of controls.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What does COSHH regulate in the UK?
    • Animal research licences
    • Medicines advertising
    • Hazardous substances at work
    • Food labelling
    Show the answer

    Answer: Hazardous substances at work. COSHH covers control of hazardous substances.

  2. Why is PPE the last resort in the hierarchy of controls?
    • PPE is always very uncomfortable
    • PPE never works
    • Hazards cannot be removed
    • Removing or reducing hazards protects more reliably
    Show the answer

    Answer: Removing or reducing hazards protects more reliably. Engineering out hazards is more effective than relying on PPE.

  3. Why should near misses be reported?
    • They reveal hazards before someone is harmed
    • They are always someone's fault
    • Reporting replaces risk assessment
    • Near misses never matter
    Show the answer

    Answer: They reveal hazards before someone is harmed. Learning from near misses prevents future accidents.

Key terms

Risk
