Muscle Contraction

Muscle fibres contain myofibrils divided into sarcomeres with actin and myosin; nerve impulses release Ca²⁺, which binds troponin to expose actin, myosin forms cross-bridges and pulls actin, and ATP is needed to detach and re-cock myosin.

Biology · Human Anatomy and Physiology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the structure of a skeletal muscle fibre and sarcomere
  • Explain the sliding filament theory
  • Explain the roles of calcium and ATP in contraction

Structure

  • Muscles contain bundles of muscle fibres, which contain myofibrils.
  • Myofibrils are divided into sarcomeres, the units of contraction.
  • Actin (thin) and myosin (thick) filaments overlap, giving striations.
  • The sarcoplasmic reticulum stores calcium ions.

Sliding filaments

  • A nerve impulse causes Ca²⁺ release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum.
  • Ca²⁺ binds troponin, moving tropomyosin to expose binding sites on actin.
  • Myosin heads bind actin, forming cross-bridges, then pivot (the power stroke), pulling actin.
  • ATP binds myosin to release it, and its hydrolysis re-cocks the head.
  • Sarcomeres shorten, but filaments stay the same length.

In death, ATP runs out, so myosin cannot detach from actin, causing rigor mortis. Muscles contain slow-twitch fibres, suited to endurance, and fast-twitch fibres, suited to rapid powerful contractions. Phosphocreatine provides a quick reserve to regenerate ATP during intense activity.

Worked example

Rigor mortis.

  1. After death, cells stop making ATP.
  2. Myosin heads remain bound to actin.
  3. Without ATP, cross-bridges cannot be broken.
  4. Muscles become stiff until proteins break down.
Common mistakeThinking the filaments themselves shorten. Filaments slide past each other; the sarcomere shortens.

In 3 lines: Sarcomeres of actin and myosin. Ca²⁺ exposes binding sites. ATP detaches myosin.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which protein forms the thick filaments?
    • Actin
    • Myosin
    • Troponin
    • Tropomyosin
    Show the answer

    Answer: Myosin. Thick filaments are made of myosin, whose heads form cross-bridges.

  2. What does calcium do in muscle contraction?
    • Breaks down ATP directly
    • Shortens the actin filaments
    • Binds troponin, moving tropomyosin to expose actin sites
    • Stores energy in the sarcomere
    Show the answer

    Answer: Binds troponin, moving tropomyosin to expose actin sites. Calcium binding to troponin uncovers the myosin binding sites on actin.

  3. Why does rigor mortis occur after death?
    • Calcium is removed from the muscle
    • Actin filaments dissolve immediately
    • Myosin heads cannot bind actin
    • No ATP is available to detach myosin from actin
    Show the answer

    Answer: No ATP is available to detach myosin from actin. Without ATP, cross-bridges remain locked.

Key terms

Sarcomere
The contractile unit of a myofibril
Cross-bridge
A connection between a myosin head and actin
Troponin
A protein that binds calcium and moves tropomyosin
