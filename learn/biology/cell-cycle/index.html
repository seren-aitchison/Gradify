Cell Cycle

The cell cycle has interphase (G₁, S and G₂) and M phase (mitosis and cytokinesis), with G₀ as a non-dividing state; DNA doubles in S phase and halves in mitosis, producing identical cells for growth, replacement and repair.

Biology · Cell Division and Development · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the phases of the eukaryotic cell cycle
  • Explain what happens to DNA content during the cycle
  • Explain the importance of the cell cycle for growth and repair

Phases

  • Interphase: G₁ (growth), S (DNA synthesis) and G₂ (preparation for division).
  • M phase: mitosis (nuclear division) and cytokinesis (division of the cytoplasm).
  • Some cells leave the cycle and enter G₀, a resting or non-dividing state, such as most neurones.
  • A typical cycle in cultured human cells takes about 24 hours, mostly in interphase.

DNA content

  • After S phase, each chromosome has two sister chromatids.
  • DNA content doubles from 2C to 4C in S phase.
  • Mitosis returns each daughter cell to 2C.
  • Daughter cells are genetically identical to the parent.

The cell cycle allows growth of multicellular organisms, replacement of worn-out cells, such as skin and gut lining, and repair of wounds. Single-celled eukaryotes use it for asexual reproduction. Because errors can cause cancer, the cycle is tightly controlled by checkpoints and regulatory proteins.

Worked example

Tracking DNA content.

  1. A cell in G₁ has 46 chromosomes, each one chromatid: DNA content 2C.
  2. After S phase, it still has 46 chromosomes, but each has two chromatids: 4C.
  3. In mitosis, sister chromatids separate into two nuclei.
  4. Each daughter cell has 46 single-chromatid chromosomes: 2C.
Common mistakeThinking interphase is a resting phase. Cells are very active in interphase, growing and copying DNA.

In 3 lines: G₁, S, G₂, M. DNA doubles in S. Growth, replacement, repair.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which phase includes mitosis and cytokinesis?
    • G₁ phase
    • M phase
    • S phase
    • G₂ phase
    Show the answer

    Answer: M phase. M phase is when the nucleus and cytoplasm divide.

  2. What happens to DNA content during S phase?
    • It halves from 2C to 1C
    • It stays exactly the same
    • It doubles from 2C to 4C
    • It is destroyed and rebuilt
    Show the answer

    Answer: It doubles from 2C to 4C. DNA replication produces two sister chromatids per chromosome.

  3. Why is interphase described as an active phase rather than a resting phase?
    • Cells do nothing during interphase
    • Cells divide repeatedly during it
    • DNA is destroyed during interphase
    • Cells grow, make proteins and replicate DNA during it
    Show the answer

    Answer: Cells grow, make proteins and replicate DNA during it. Interphase involves growth and preparation for division.

Key terms

Interphase
The phase between divisions, including G₁, S and G₂
S phase
The phase in which DNA is replicated
G₀
A non-dividing state outside the cycle
