DNA Replication

DNA replication is semi-conservative, as Meselson and Stahl showed; helicase unwinds DNA, primase adds primers, DNA polymerase adds nucleotides 5′ to 3′ with proofreading, the lagging strand forms Okazaki fragments and ligase joins them.

Biology · Genetics and Molecular Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe semi-conservative replication and its evidence
  • Explain the roles of key enzymes in replication
  • Explain leading and lagging strand synthesis

Semi-conservative replication

  • Each new DNA molecule contains one original strand and one new strand.
  • Shown by Meselson and Stahl (1958) using bacteria grown in heavy (¹⁵N) and light (¹⁴N) nitrogen.
  • After one generation in ¹⁴N, all DNA was hybrid density; after two, half hybrid and half light.

Enzymes and steps

  • Helicase unwinds the helix, breaking hydrogen bonds between bases.
  • Primase adds short RNA primers.
  • DNA polymerase adds complementary nucleotides to the 3′ end, building strands 5′ to 3′.
  • The leading strand is made continuously; the lagging strand in short Okazaki fragments.
  • DNA ligase joins Okazaki fragments.

Because DNA polymerase only adds nucleotides to a 3′ end, the two antiparallel strands are copied differently at the replication fork. DNA polymerase also proofreads, removing mismatched nucleotides, giving an error rate of roughly one in a billion bases after repair. Eukaryotic chromosomes have many origins of replication, allowing large genomes to be copied quickly.

Worked example

Interpreting Meselson and Stahl.

  1. Bacteria grown in ¹⁵N have heavy DNA.
  2. After one division in ¹⁴N, all DNA is intermediate density.
  3. This rules out conservative replication, which would give one heavy and one light band.
  4. After two divisions, there are intermediate and light bands, as semi-conservative replication predicts.
Common mistakeSaying DNA polymerase unwinds DNA. Helicase unwinds the helix; DNA polymerase builds new strands.

In 3 lines: One old, one new strand. Helicase, polymerase, ligase. Leading and lagging strands.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which enzyme unwinds the DNA double helix?
    • DNA polymerase
    • DNA ligase
    • Helicase
    • Primase
    Show the answer

    Answer: Helicase. Helicase breaks hydrogen bonds between bases to separate strands.

  2. Why is the lagging strand made in fragments?
    • The lagging strand has no bases
    • Helicase works backwards
    • Ligase prevents continuous synthesis
    • DNA polymerase can only add nucleotides to a 3′ end
    Show the answer

    Answer: DNA polymerase can only add nucleotides to a 3′ end. Antiparallel strands mean one strand must be built discontinuously.

  3. In Meselson and Stahl's experiment, what result after one generation ruled out conservative replication?
    • All DNA had intermediate density
    • Half the DNA was heavy and half light
    • All DNA was heavy
    • No DNA was detected
    Show the answer

    Answer: All DNA had intermediate density. Conservative replication would have given separate heavy and light bands.

Key terms

Semi-conservative replication
Copying in which each new molecule keeps one original strand
Okazaki fragment
A short DNA section made on the lagging strand
DNA ligase
An enzyme that joins DNA fragments
