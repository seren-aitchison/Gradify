Factors Affecting Enzyme Activity

Enzyme activity rises with temperature to an optimum, then falls as the enzyme denatures; each enzyme has an optimum pH; rate rises with substrate concentration to saturation and with enzyme concentration when substrate is in excess, and investigations need controlled variables and initial rates.

Biology · Biochemistry and Biomolecules · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain the effects of temperature and pH on enzyme activity
  • Explain the effects of substrate and enzyme concentration
  • Describe how to investigate enzyme activity in the laboratory

Temperature and pH

  • Rising temperature increases kinetic energy and collisions, so rate rises up to the optimum.
  • Above the optimum, bonds break, the active site changes and the enzyme denatures.
  • Q₁₀: the rate often roughly doubles for each 10 °C rise below the optimum.
  • Each enzyme has an optimum pH: pepsin about pH 2, salivary amylase about pH 7, trypsin about pH 8.
  • pH changes alter R group charges, affecting substrate binding and shape.

Concentrations

  • More substrate raises rate until active sites are saturated.
  • More enzyme raises rate as long as substrate is in excess.
  • Inhibitors and cofactors also affect activity.

In investigations, control variables carefully: use a water bath for temperature, buffers for pH and the same volumes and concentrations. Measure rate by product formed or substrate used over time, for example oxygen volume from catalase or the time for starch to disappear using iodine. Calculate initial rates and repeat trials to assess reliability, plotting rate against the variable to find optimum conditions.

Worked example

Investigating temperature.

  1. A student mixes amylase and starch at 20, 30, 40, 50 and 60 °C.
  2. She samples the mixture every 30 seconds into iodine until it no longer turns blue-black.
  3. The shortest time, and so highest rate, is at about 40 °C.
  4. At 60 °C, the starch is not broken down because amylase is denatured.
Common mistakeSaying enzymes are "killed" by high temperatures. Enzymes are molecules, not living things; they are denatured.

In 3 lines: Temperature: optimum, then denaturation. Each enzyme has an optimum pH. Concentration effects and saturation.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Why does enzyme activity fall above the optimum temperature?
    • Substrate molecules stop moving
    • The enzyme multiplies
    • pH always becomes neutral
    • The enzyme denatures and the active site changes shape
    Show the answer

    Answer: The enzyme denatures and the active site changes shape. High temperatures break bonds holding the active site.

  2. Why does adding more enzyme increase rate only when substrate is in excess?
    • Extra active sites need available substrate to act on
    • Enzymes produce substrate
    • More enzyme always doubles rate
    • Substrate inhibits enzymes
    Show the answer

    Answer: Extra active sites need available substrate to act on. Rate depends on enzyme-substrate collisions.

  3. Why are buffers used in enzyme temperature experiments?
    • To denature the enzyme
    • To keep pH constant so only temperature varies
    • To add substrate
    • To speed up the reaction
    Show the answer

    Answer: To keep pH constant so only temperature varies. Controlling pH ensures a fair test of temperature.

Key terms

Optimum temperature
The temperature at which an enzyme's rate is highest
Q₁₀
The factor by which rate increases for a 10 °C rise
Buffer
A solution that keeps pH constant
