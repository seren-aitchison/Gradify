Triglycerides

Triglycerides are glycerol joined to three fatty acids by ester bonds formed in condensation reactions, hydrolysed by lipase; they store about twice the energy per gram of carbohydrates, are insoluble and compact and also insulate, protect and aid buoyancy.

Biology · Biochemistry and Biomolecules · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the structure and formation of a triglyceride
  • Explain why triglycerides are good energy stores
  • Describe other functions of triglycerides

Structure and formation

  • A triglyceride is made of glycerol and three fatty acids.
  • Each fatty acid joins glycerol by a condensation reaction, forming an ester bond and releasing water.
  • Three ester bonds form, releasing three water molecules.
  • Hydrolysis, catalysed by lipase, breaks triglycerides into glycerol and fatty acids.

Why they store energy well

  • High ratio of C–H bonds releases much energy when oxidised: about 37 kJ g⁻¹, versus about 17 kJ g⁻¹ for carbohydrates.
  • Insoluble, so they do not affect water potential.
  • Stored without water, so they are compact and light.
  • Low density helps buoyancy in aquatic animals.

Triglycerides are stored in adipose tissue in animals and in seeds such as sunflower and rapeseed in plants. Besides energy storage, adipose tissue insulates against heat loss, protects organs and, in brown fat, generates heat. Because they are non-polar, triglycerides are transported in blood within lipoproteins. Their fatty acids can be saturated or unsaturated, affecting whether the fat is solid or liquid.

Worked example

Forming a triglyceride.

  1. Glycerol has three –OH groups.
  2. Each reacts with the –COOH of a fatty acid in a condensation reaction.
  3. Three ester bonds form, and three molecules of water are released.
  4. The resulting triglyceride is non-polar and insoluble.
Common mistakeCalling the bond in a triglyceride a glycosidic bond. Triglycerides contain ester bonds.

In 3 lines: Glycerol + three fatty acids. Ester bonds by condensation. High energy, insoluble, compact.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which bond joins fatty acids to glycerol in a triglyceride?
    • An ester bond
    • A glycosidic bond
    • A peptide bond
    • A hydrogen bond
    Show the answer

    Answer: An ester bond. Ester bonds form between the carboxyl groups of fatty acids and hydroxyl groups of glycerol.

  2. Why do triglycerides store more energy per gram than carbohydrates?
    • They contain more oxygen atoms
    • They contain a higher proportion of C–H bonds
    • They are polymers of glucose
    • They are soluble in water
    Show the answer

    Answer: They contain a higher proportion of C–H bonds. Oxidising C–H bonds releases a lot of energy.

  3. Why are triglycerides transported in blood inside lipoproteins?
    • They are too small to be carried freely
    • Lipoproteins break them into glucose
    • They are non-polar and would not dissolve in plasma
    • Blood contains no water
    Show the answer

    Answer: They are non-polar and would not dissolve in plasma. Lipoproteins package insoluble lipids for transport.

Key terms

Triglyceride
A lipid made of glycerol and three fatty acids
Ester bond
A bond formed between a carboxyl group and a hydroxyl group
Lipase
An enzyme that hydrolyses triglycerides
