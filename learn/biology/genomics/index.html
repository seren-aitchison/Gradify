Genomics

Genomes are sequenced by Sanger, next-generation and long-read methods, now quickly and cheaply; genomics is used in diagnosis, pharmacogenomics, pathogen tracking and evolution, with ethical issues of privacy, consent and discrimination.

Biology · Genetics and Molecular Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe methods of DNA sequencing
  • Explain the main applications of genomics
  • Discuss ethical issues in genomics

Sequencing

  • Sanger sequencing (1977) uses chain-terminating nucleotides; accurate but slow.
  • Next-generation sequencing reads millions of short fragments in parallel.
  • Long-read sequencing reads much longer fragments, helping assemble repetitive regions.
  • Sequencing a human genome now costs a few hundred pounds and takes days, compared with about 13 years for the original project.

Applications

  • Medicine: diagnosing rare diseases and guiding cancer treatment.
  • Pharmacogenomics: choosing drugs and doses based on genotype.
  • Public health: tracking pathogens, as in COVID-19 variant surveillance.
  • Evolution and conservation: comparing species and monitoring populations.

In England, the NHS Genomic Medicine Service offers whole genome sequencing for some rare diseases and cancers, building on the 100,000 Genomes Project. Ethical issues include privacy of genetic data, consent, incidental findings and the risk of discrimination, for example by insurers. UK insurers follow a voluntary code limiting the use of predictive genetic test results.

Worked example

Diagnosing a rare disease.

  1. A child has an undiagnosed developmental condition.
  2. The genomes of the child and parents are sequenced.
  3. Comparison finds a new mutation in the child, absent in both parents.
  4. The mutation in a known gene explains the condition, guiding care.
Common mistakeAssuming genome sequencing always gives clear answers. Many variants are of uncertain significance.

In 3 lines: Sequencing is fast and cheap. Diagnosis, drugs, tracking. Ethical issues matter.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which sequencing method uses chain-terminating nucleotides?
    • Long-read nanopore sequencing
    • Sanger sequencing
    • Polymerase chain reaction
    • Gel electrophoresis alone
    Show the answer

    Answer: Sanger sequencing. Sanger's 1977 method stops synthesis at labelled nucleotides.

  2. How was genomics used during the COVID-19 pandemic?
    • To vaccinate people directly
    • To cure infected patients overnight
    • To track new variants of the virus
    • To prevent all mutations
    Show the answer

    Answer: To track new variants of the virus. Sequencing virus samples identified and tracked variants.

  3. Why is genetic privacy an ethical concern?
    • Genetic data never reveal anything personal
    • Sequencing destroys DNA samples
    • Privacy only matters for bacteria
    • Genetic data could be misused, for example by insurers
    Show the answer

    Answer: Genetic data could be misused, for example by insurers. Genomes reveal sensitive information about individuals and relatives.

Key terms

Genomics
The study of whole genomes
Next-generation sequencing
High-throughput methods that read many DNA fragments in parallel
Pharmacogenomics
Using genetic information to guide drug treatment
