Energy Flow

Energy enters ecosystems as sunlight captured by producers, flows along food chains and leaves as heat; at each level energy is lost through respiration, uneaten material, faeces and excretion, so transfer is typically about 10% efficient.

Biology · Ecology and Environmental Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe how energy enters and leaves ecosystems
  • Explain why energy is lost between trophic levels
  • Calculate the efficiency of energy transfer

Energy in and out

  • Energy enters as sunlight, captured by producers.
  • Only a small fraction, often around 1–2%, of sunlight is converted to chemical energy.
  • Energy passes along food chains as food.
  • Energy eventually leaves as heat; it is not recycled.

Losses

  • Respiration releases energy as heat.
  • Some material is not eaten, such as roots and bones.
  • Some is not digested and is lost in faeces.
  • Some is lost in excretion, such as urea.
  • Typically about 10% passes to the next level, though it varies.

Efficiency = (energy transferred to next level ÷ energy available at previous level) × 100. Endotherms such as mammals lose more energy as heat than ectotherms such as insects, so transfer efficiency to the next level is lower. Livestock farming reduces losses by limiting movement and keeping animals warm, raising ethical questions.

Worked example

Calculating efficiency.

  1. Grass contains 20,000 kJ per m² per year.
  2. Rabbits eating it gain 2,000 kJ per m² per year.
  3. Efficiency = 2,000 ÷ 20,000 × 100 = 10%.
  4. The other 90% is lost in respiration, uneaten parts and waste.
Common mistakeThinking energy is recycled in ecosystems like nutrients. Energy flows through and is lost as heat; nutrients are recycled.

In 3 lines: Sunlight in, heat out. Losses at each level. About 10% transferred.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is the original source of energy for most ecosystems?
    • Soil minerals
    • Sunlight
    • Decomposers
    • Carbon dioxide
    Show the answer

    Answer: Sunlight. Producers capture light energy by photosynthesis.

  2. Why is energy transfer less efficient to mammals than to insects?
    • Mammals eat less food
    • Insects respire more
    • Mammals lose more energy as heat to keep warm
    • Mammals do not produce faeces
    Show the answer

    Answer: Mammals lose more energy as heat to keep warm. Endotherms use much energy maintaining body temperature.

  3. Why are energy and nutrients treated differently in ecosystems?
    • Energy is recycled; nutrients are lost
    • Both are recycled equally
    • Neither moves between organisms
    • Energy flows through and is lost; nutrients are recycled
    Show the answer

    Answer: Energy flows through and is lost; nutrients are recycled. Heat cannot be reused, but atoms are reused.

Key terms

Energy flow
Movement of energy through an ecosystem
Transfer efficiency
Percentage of energy passed to the next trophic level
Endotherm
An animal that generates its own body heat
