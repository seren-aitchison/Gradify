Virulence

Pathogenicity is the ability to cause disease and virulence its severity; virulence factors include adhesins, capsules, exotoxins, endotoxins and invasive enzymes, and virulence evolves through trade-offs between transmission and host survival.

Biology · Microbiology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define virulence and pathogenicity
  • Describe common virulence factors
  • Explain how virulence can evolve

Definitions and factors

  • Pathogenicity: the ability to cause disease.
  • Virulence: how severe the disease is.
  • Adhesins and pili attach to host cells.
  • Capsules resist phagocytosis.
  • Toxins: exotoxins are secreted proteins; endotoxins are lipopolysaccharides in Gram-negative walls.
  • Invasive enzymes break down tissues.

Evolution of virulence

  • Virulence genes are often carried on plasmids or pathogenicity islands.
  • Pathogens spread by close contact may evolve lower virulence, as hosts must stay mobile.
  • Pathogens spread by vectors or water may remain highly virulent.
  • Trade-off hypothesis: virulence balances transmission against killing the host too quickly.

The myxoma virus, released in Australia in 1950 to control rabbits, initially killed over 99% of infected rabbits, but less virulent strains soon became common as they allowed rabbits to live longer and spread the virus. Meanwhile, rabbits evolved resistance. E. coli O157 is highly virulent because of Shiga toxin genes acquired from a phage.

Worked example

Myxomatosis in Australia.

  1. Myxoma virus was released to control rabbits in 1950.
  2. At first, almost all infected rabbits died within days.
  3. Less virulent strains kept rabbits alive longer, so mosquitoes spread them more.
  4. Over time, moderate virulence became most common.
Common mistakeAssuming pathogens always evolve to become harmless. Virulence evolves to balance transmission and may stay high.

In 3 lines: Pathogenicity and virulence. Adhesins, capsules, toxins. Virulence evolves.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which virulence factor helps bacteria resist phagocytosis?
    • A ribosome
    • A plasmid origin
    • A nucleoid
    • A capsule
    Show the answer

    Answer: A capsule. Slippery capsules make bacteria harder to engulf.

  2. Why did myxoma virus become less virulent in Australia?
    • Milder strains kept hosts alive longer and spread more
    • Rabbits stopped carrying the virus
    • Mosquitoes disappeared
    • The virus stopped mutating
    Show the answer

    Answer: Milder strains kept hosts alive longer and spread more. Longer-lived hosts allowed more transmission.

  3. According to the trade-off hypothesis, why might a pathogen not evolve maximum virulence?
    • Virulence always increases transmission
    • Killing hosts too fast reduces transmission
    • Pathogens cannot evolve
    • Hosts never die
    Show the answer

    Answer: Killing hosts too fast reduces transmission. Pathogens need hosts alive long enough to spread.

Key terms

Virulence
The severity of disease caused by a pathogen
Exotoxin
A toxin secreted by a bacterium
Endotoxin
Lipopolysaccharide from Gram-negative bacterial walls
