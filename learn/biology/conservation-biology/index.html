Conservation Biology

Conservation biology protects biodiversity at all levels, recognising the risks facing small populations; the IUCN Red List assesses extinction risk, and frameworks such as CITES, the CBD, the Wildlife and Countryside Act and the Environment Act protect species and habitats.

Biology · Ecology and Environmental Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain the aims of conservation biology
  • Describe how species are assessed for extinction risk
  • Describe international and UK conservation frameworks

Principles

  • Conservation protects genetic, species and ecosystem diversity.
  • Small populations face inbreeding, drift and chance events, increasing extinction risk.
  • Minimum viable population: the smallest population likely to persist.
  • Protecting habitats usually protects many species at once.

Assessment and law

  • The IUCN Red List classifies species from Least Concern to Extinct.
  • Categories include Vulnerable, Endangered and Critically Endangered.
  • CITES regulates international trade in endangered species.
  • The Convention on Biological Diversity set a goal to protect 30% of land and sea by 2030.
  • In the UK, the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981 protects species and Sites of Special Scientific Interest.

Conservation must balance human needs, such as farming and housing, with biodiversity. Flagship species, such as pandas and tigers, attract public support. The Environment Act 2021 introduced biodiversity net gain for new developments in England.

Worked example

Red List assessment.

  1. Scientists collect data on population size, trend and range of a species.
  2. The population has declined by over 50% in ten years.
  3. This meets the criteria for Endangered.
  4. The listing guides funding and protection.
Common mistakeThinking conservation only means protecting famous animals. Protecting habitats and genetic diversity is often more effective.

In 3 lines: Protect all levels. Red List categories. Laws and agreements.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which IUCN category indicates the highest risk short of extinction in the wild?
    • Critically Endangered
    • Vulnerable
    • Near Threatened
    • Least Concern
    Show the answer

    Answer: Critically Endangered. Critically Endangered species face extremely high risk.

  2. Why are small populations at greater risk of extinction?
    • They have more genetic variation
    • Inbreeding, drift and chance events affect them more
    • They never meet predators
    • They reproduce faster
    Show the answer

    Answer: Inbreeding, drift and chance events affect them more. Small numbers make populations fragile to inbreeding and chance events.

  3. Why is protecting habitats often more effective than protecting single species?
    • Habitats contain only one species
    • Single species need no habitat
    • It conserves many species and their interactions at once
    • Habitat protection costs nothing
    Show the answer

    Answer: It conserves many species and their interactions at once. Whole ecosystems support many dependent species.

Key terms

IUCN Red List
A global assessment of species' extinction risk
Minimum viable population
The smallest population likely to survive long-term
Biodiversity net gain
Requirement that development leaves biodiversity better off
