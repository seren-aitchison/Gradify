Cell Junctions

Tight junctions seal, anchoring junctions resist pulling forces, and gap junctions connect cytoplasm; plasmodesmata connect plant cells.

Biology · Cell Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe tight junctions, anchoring junctions and gap junctions
  • Relate each type of junction to the tissues where it is common
  • Describe plasmodesmata in plants

Three kinds of junction in animals

  • Tight junctions: rows of proteins (claudins and occludin) stitch neighbouring membranes together, sealing the gap so substances can't leak between cells. They are vital in the gut lining and the blood–brain barrier, so that material has to pass through the cells and their transporters.
  • Anchoring junctions: adherens junctions and desmosomes link cells through cadherin proteins attached to the cytoskeleton inside each cell, spreading mechanical stress across a tissue. Hemidesmosomes anchor cells to the basement membrane with integrins. They are abundant in skin and heart muscle.
  • Gap junctions: channels made of connexin proteins join the cytoplasm of neighbouring cells, letting ions and small molecules (up to about 1 kDa) pass. In heart muscle they let the wave of electrical excitation spread so the cells contract together.

Plant connections

Plant cells are held together by the middle lamella between their walls, and connected by plasmodesmata, channels through the walls lined with plasma membrane. Like gap junctions, they let small molecules and signals pass, but they can also carry some proteins and RNA.

When junctions fail

Blistering diseases such as pemphigus occur when the immune system attacks desmosome proteins, so skin layers separate. Some bacteria and toxins open tight junctions in the gut, causing fluid loss in diarrhoea.

Cancer cells often lose cadherins, which loosens their junctions and helps them break away from the tissue and spread.

Common mistakeConfusing gap junctions with gaps between cells. Gap junctions are channels that connect cells; it is tight junctions that close the gaps between them.

In 3 lines: Tight junctions seal epithelia such as the gut lining. Desmosomes and adherens junctions hold cells together against stress. Gap junctions and plasmodesmata let small molecules pass between cells.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which junction links the cytoplasm of neighbouring animal cells?
    • Gap junction
    • Tight junction
    • Desmosome
    • Hemidesmosome
    Show the answer

    Answer: Gap junction. Gap junction channels of connexin let small molecules pass directly from cell to cell.

  2. In pemphigus, antibodies attack desmosome proteins in the skin. What would you expect?
    • Faster heart rate
    • Blisters, as layers of skin cells separate
    • Leaky blood–brain barrier only
    • Increased absorption in the gut
    Show the answer

    Answer: Blisters, as layers of skin cells separate. Desmosomes hold skin cells together against friction; losing them lets the layers come apart.

  3. Why does the blood–brain barrier depend especially on tight junctions?
    • They let all molecules into the brain
    • They anchor neurons to bone
    • They stop substances passing between the cells of capillary walls, so entry is controlled by the cells' transporters
    • They pump glucose out of the brain
    Show the answer

    Answer: They stop substances passing between the cells of capillary walls, so entry is controlled by the cells' transporters. Without the seal, substances would leak around the cells; tight junctions force traffic through regulated routes.

Key terms

Tight junction
A junction that seals neighbouring cells together, preventing leakage between them
Desmosome
An anchoring junction that links the intermediate filaments of neighbouring cells through cadherins
Gap junction
A channel of connexin proteins that links the cytoplasm of neighbouring cells
