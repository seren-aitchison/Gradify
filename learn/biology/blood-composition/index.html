Blood Composition

Blood consists of plasma, red cells carrying oxygen with haemoglobin, white cells for defence and platelets for clotting via fibrin; ABO and Rhesus antigens determine blood groups, and mismatched transfusions cause agglutination.

Biology · Human Anatomy and Physiology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the components of blood
  • Explain the functions of red cells, white cells and platelets
  • Describe the ABO blood group system

Components

  • Plasma (about 55%): water carrying glucose, amino acids, hormones, CO₂, urea, proteins and salts.
  • Red blood cells (erythrocytes): carry oxygen using haemoglobin; biconcave, no nucleus.
  • White blood cells (leucocytes): defend against infection.
  • Platelets: cell fragments involved in clotting.

Clotting and blood groups

  • Damaged vessels trigger platelets and a cascade that converts fibrinogen to fibrin, forming a clot.
  • ABO blood groups depend on antigens A and B on red cells.
  • Group O has neither antigen; group AB has both.
  • Mismatched transfusions cause agglutination due to antibodies in plasma.
  • The Rhesus (Rh) factor is another important antigen.

Red blood cells live about 120 days and are made in bone marrow, at a rate of about 2 million per second. Anaemia is a shortage of red cells or haemoglobin, often due to iron deficiency. Group O negative blood can be given to almost anyone in emergencies, so it is called the universal donor type.

Worked example

A blood transfusion.

  1. A patient has group A blood, with anti-B antibodies in their plasma.
  2. If given group B blood, the antibodies bind the B antigens.
  3. The red cells clump together (agglutinate).
  4. This can be fatal, so blood must be matched before transfusion.
Common mistakeThinking group AB is the universal donor. Group O negative is the universal donor; AB positive is the universal recipient.

In 3 lines: Plasma, red cells, white cells, platelets. Clotting via fibrin. ABO and Rhesus groups.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which component of blood carries oxygen?
    • Red blood cells
    • White blood cells
    • Platelets
    • Plasma proteins
    Show the answer

    Answer: Red blood cells. Haemoglobin inside red blood cells binds and carries oxygen.

  2. Why do red blood cells have no nucleus?
    • To help them divide faster
    • To leave more room for haemoglobin
    • So they can make more proteins
    • To fight infections
    Show the answer

    Answer: To leave more room for haemoglobin. More space for haemoglobin increases oxygen capacity.

  3. Why can a group A patient not receive group B blood?
    • Group B blood has no red cells
    • Group A blood lacks plasma
    • Their anti-B antibodies would cause the donor cells to agglutinate
    • Group B cells have no antigens
    Show the answer

    Answer: Their anti-B antibodies would cause the donor cells to agglutinate. Antibodies against B antigens attack the donor cells.

Key terms

Plasma
The liquid part of blood
Haemoglobin
The oxygen-carrying protein in red blood cells
Agglutination
Clumping of red cells due to antibodies
