Natural Killer Cells

NK cells are innate lymphocytes that kill virus-infected and cancer cells without prior exposure, recognising "missing self" when MHC class I is lost and stress signals, and killing with perforin and granzymes or antibody-dependent cytotoxicity.

Biology · Immunology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the features of natural killer cells
  • Explain how NK cells recognise target cells
  • Describe how NK cells kill target cells

Features

  • NK cells are innate lymphocytes from the lymphoid lineage.
  • They do not need prior exposure to kill.
  • They target virus-infected cells and some cancer cells.
  • They make up about 5–15% of blood lymphocytes.

Recognition and killing

  • Healthy cells display MHC class I, which binds inhibitory receptors on NK cells.
  • Many viruses and cancers reduce MHC class I: "missing self".
  • Activating receptors detect stress signals on abnormal cells.
  • NK cells release perforin, forming pores, and granzymes, triggering apoptosis.
  • They also kill antibody-coated cells (antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity).

The missing-self hypothesis explains how NK cells complement cytotoxic T cells: viruses that hide from T cells by lowering MHC class I become visible to NK cells. NK cells also release interferon-gamma, activating macrophages. Researchers are developing NK cell therapies for cancer.

Worked example

Missing self.

  1. A virus infects a cell and reduces MHC class I on its surface.
  2. Cytotoxic T cells can no longer recognise it easily.
  3. An NK cell meets the cell and receives no inhibitory signal.
  4. The NK cell releases perforin and granzymes, killing it.
Common mistakeThinking NK cells need to recognise a specific antigen. They respond to missing MHC and stress signals.

In 3 lines: Innate killers. Missing self. Perforin and granzymes.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What do NK cells release to trigger apoptosis?
    • Antibodies
    • Histamine
    • Insulin
    • Granzymes
    Show the answer

    Answer: Granzymes. Granzymes enter through perforin pores and trigger cell death.

  2. Why do NK cells attack cells with low MHC class I?
    • They lose the inhibitory signal that normally stops NK cells
    • MHC class I activates NK cells
    • Low MHC means the cell is healthy
    • NK cells only attack bacteria
    Show the answer

    Answer: They lose the inhibitory signal that normally stops NK cells. Missing MHC removes the brake on killing.

  3. How do NK cells complement cytotoxic T cells?
    • They kill only bacteria in blood
    • They kill cells that hide by lowering MHC class I
    • They produce antibodies for T cells
    • They prevent all apoptosis
    Show the answer

    Answer: They kill cells that hide by lowering MHC class I. Viruses evading T cells become NK cell targets.

Key terms

Natural killer cell
An innate lymphocyte that kills abnormal cells
Perforin
A protein forming pores in target cells
Missing self
Recognition of cells lacking normal MHC class I
