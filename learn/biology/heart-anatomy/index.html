Heart Anatomy

The heart has four chambers, with the right side pumping to the lungs and the left to the body; atrioventricular and semilunar valves prevent backflow, coronary arteries supply the muscle and the left ventricle is thickest to generate high pressure.

Biology · Human Anatomy and Physiology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the chambers and major vessels of the heart
  • Explain the role of the heart valves
  • Explain why the left ventricle has a thicker wall

Chambers and vessels

  • Four chambers: right atrium, right ventricle, left atrium and left ventricle.
  • The right side pumps deoxygenated blood to the lungs via the pulmonary artery.
  • The left side pumps oxygenated blood to the body via the aorta.
  • Blood returns via the venae cavae (right atrium) and pulmonary veins (left atrium).
  • The septum separates the two sides.

Valves and supply

  • Atrioventricular valves (tricuspid on the right, bicuspid or mitral on the left) prevent backflow into the atria.
  • Semilunar valves (pulmonary and aortic) prevent backflow into the ventricles.
  • Coronary arteries supply heart muscle with oxygen.
  • Cardiac muscle is myogenic: it contracts without nervous stimulation.

The left ventricle wall is much thicker than the right because it must generate higher pressure to push blood around the whole body, whereas the right ventricle pumps only to the nearby lungs. Valve defects can cause heart murmurs, and faulty valves can be replaced with mechanical or biological valves.

Worked example

Following one drop of blood.

  1. Blood from the body enters the right atrium via the vena cava.
  2. It passes through the tricuspid valve to the right ventricle, then to the lungs via the pulmonary artery.
  3. Oxygenated blood returns via the pulmonary veins to the left atrium.
  4. It passes the bicuspid valve to the left ventricle and leaves via the aorta.
Common mistakeThinking all arteries carry oxygenated blood. The pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood to the lungs.

In 3 lines: Four chambers. Valves prevent backflow. Thick left ventricle.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which chamber receives blood from the pulmonary veins?
    • The right atrium
    • The right ventricle
    • The left ventricle
    • The left atrium
    Show the answer

    Answer: The left atrium. Oxygenated blood from the lungs enters the left atrium.

  2. Why does the left ventricle have a thicker wall than the right?
    • It must pump blood at higher pressure around the whole body
    • It pumps blood only to the lungs
    • It holds more blood than the atria
    • It receives blood from the body
    Show the answer

    Answer: It must pump blood at higher pressure around the whole body. Higher pressure to reach the whole body needs more muscle.

  3. What does "myogenic" mean for cardiac muscle?
    • It needs constant nervous input
    • It contracts without nervous stimulation
    • It never tires under any conditions
    • It contracts only when hormones arrive
    Show the answer

    Answer: It contracts without nervous stimulation. The heartbeat starts within the muscle itself.

Key terms

Atrium
An upper heart chamber receiving blood
Ventricle
A lower heart chamber pumping blood out
Semilunar valve
A valve between a ventricle and an artery
