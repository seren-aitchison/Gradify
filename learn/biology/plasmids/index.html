Plasmids

Plasmids are small circular DNA molecules that replicate independently and carry genes such as resistance; engineered vectors have an origin of replication, a selectable marker and a multiple cloning site, and are introduced by heat shock or electroporation.

Biology · Biotechnology and Genetic Engineering · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe natural plasmids and their genes
  • Describe the features of an engineered plasmid vector
  • Explain how plasmids are introduced into cells

Natural plasmids

  • Small, circular, double-stranded DNA separate from the chromosome.
  • Replicate independently using an origin of replication (ori).
  • Carry useful genes, such as antibiotic resistance, toxin production or metabolism of unusual compounds.
  • Can be transferred between bacteria by conjugation.

Engineered vectors

  • Origin of replication for copying in the host.
  • Selectable marker, such as an ampicillin resistance gene.
  • Multiple cloning site (polylinker) with many unique restriction sites.
  • Sometimes a promoter to drive expression of the inserted gene.
  • Introduced by heat shock with calcium chloride or by electroporation.

Plasmids are useful vectors because they are small, easy to isolate and copy to high numbers. However, they can only carry inserts of up to about 10–15 kb, so larger DNA needs other vectors. Ti plasmids from Agrobacterium tumefaciens are used to transfer genes into plants.

Worked example

Heat-shock transformation.

  1. E. coli cells are chilled in calcium chloride, making membranes more permeable.
  2. Recombinant plasmids are added.
  3. The cells are heated briefly to 42 °C, then cooled.
  4. Some cells take up the plasmid and are selected on antibiotic agar.
Common mistakeThinking plasmids are part of the bacterial chromosome. They are separate DNA molecules.

In 3 lines: Natural roles. ori, marker, cloning site. Heat shock and electroporation.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which feature allows a plasmid to be copied in a host cell?
    • Multiple cloning site
    • Origin of replication
    • Reporter gene
    • Sticky end
    Show the answer

    Answer: Origin of replication. Replication begins at the ori sequence, letting the plasmid be copied.

  2. Why are plasmids limited as vectors for very large genes?
    • They cannot enter bacterial cells at all
    • They lack restriction sites
    • They can only carry inserts up to about 10–15 kb
    • They are too large to isolate
    Show the answer

    Answer: They can only carry inserts up to about 10–15 kb. Large inserts make plasmids unstable, so bigger DNA needs other vectors.

  3. Why are cells chilled in calcium chloride before heat shock?
    • It kills unwanted bacteria
    • It cuts the plasmid
    • It removes antibiotic resistance
    • It makes membranes more permeable to DNA
    Show the answer

    Answer: It makes membranes more permeable to DNA. Calcium ions help DNA cross the membrane.

Key terms

Plasmid
A small circular DNA molecule separate from the chromosome
Multiple cloning site
A region with many restriction sites for inserting DNA
Electroporation
Using an electric pulse to introduce DNA into cells
