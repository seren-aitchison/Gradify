Digestive System

The digestive system includes the mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small and large intestines and accessory organs; mechanical and chemical digestion break down food, the stomach uses acid and pepsin and the large intestine absorbs water with help from gut microbiota.

Biology · Human Anatomy and Physiology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the organs of the digestive system
  • Distinguish mechanical and chemical digestion
  • Explain the roles of the stomach and the large intestine

Organs

  • Mouth: teeth chew food; saliva contains amylase.
  • Oesophagus: moves food by peristalsis.
  • Stomach: churns food; secretes hydrochloric acid and pepsin.
  • Small intestine: main site of digestion and absorption.
  • Large intestine: absorbs water and forms faeces; rectum stores them.
  • Accessory organs: liver, gall bladder and pancreas.

Types of digestion

  • Mechanical digestion: physical breakdown by chewing and churning.
  • Chemical digestion: enzymes break large molecules into small soluble ones.
  • Hydrochloric acid in the stomach kills microbes and provides optimum pH for pepsin.
  • The large intestine contains gut microbiota that ferment fibre and produce some vitamins.

Peristalsis is waves of muscle contraction that push food along the gut. The stomach lining is protected from acid by mucus; when this protection fails, ulcers can form, often linked to the bacterium Helicobacter pylori. Dietary fibre adds bulk and supports healthy gut microbes.

Worked example

Discovering H. pylori.

  1. Ulcers were long thought to be caused by stress and acid.
  2. In 1982, Barry Marshall and Robin Warren identified H. pylori in ulcer patients.
  3. Marshall drank a culture of the bacterium and developed gastritis.
  4. Antibiotics are now used to cure many ulcers, and they won the 2005 Nobel Prize.
Common mistakeThinking most absorption happens in the stomach. Most nutrient absorption occurs in the small intestine.

In 3 lines: Mouth to rectum. Mechanical and chemical digestion. Stomach acid and gut microbes.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which enzyme is found in saliva?
    • Pepsin
    • Amylase
    • Lipase
    • Trypsin
    Show the answer

    Answer: Amylase. Salivary amylase begins starch digestion.

  2. Why does the stomach produce hydrochloric acid?
    • To digest starch into glucose
    • To absorb water from food
    • To kill microbes and give the optimum pH for pepsin
    • To neutralise bile
    Show the answer

    Answer: To kill microbes and give the optimum pH for pepsin. Acid provides defence and enzyme conditions.

  3. What did Marshall and Warren's work change about ulcer treatment?
    • Ulcers were shown to be caused only by stress
    • Surgery became the only option
    • Acid was shown to be harmless
    • Many ulcers could be cured with antibiotics
    Show the answer

    Answer: Many ulcers could be cured with antibiotics. Identifying H. pylori led to antibiotic treatment.

Key terms

Peristalsis
Waves of muscle contraction moving food along the gut
Chemical digestion
Enzyme breakdown of large food molecules
Gut microbiota
Microorganisms living in the intestines
