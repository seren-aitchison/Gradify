Environmental Microbiology

Soil and water contain vast microbial communities that decompose, fix nitrogen and produce oxygen; sewage treatment uses aerobic and anaerobic microbes, producing biogas, and bioremediation uses microbes to clean pollutants.

Biology · Microbiology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe microbial communities in soil and water
  • Explain the role of microbes in sewage treatment
  • Describe bioremediation

Soil and water

  • A gram of soil can contain billions of bacteria and thousands of species.
  • Soil microbes decompose organic matter, fix nitrogen and form mycorrhizae.
  • In water, phytoplankton fix carbon and produce oxygen.
  • Water quality is monitored using indicator organisms, such as E. coli, showing faecal contamination.

Sewage and bioremediation

  • Sewage treatment: screening, settling, then aerobic digestion by microbes in activated sludge.
  • Anaerobic digestion of sludge produces biogas (mainly methane).
  • Bioremediation: using microbes to clean pollutants, such as oil spills.
  • Adding nutrients can boost natural oil-degrading bacteria.

After the Exxon Valdez oil spill in 1989, fertilisers were added to beaches to stimulate oil-degrading bacteria. Wastewater testing was used during the COVID-19 pandemic to track community infection levels. Sewage overflows into rivers raise concerns about water quality and bathing safety in the UK.

Worked example

Cleaning up oil.

  1. An oil spill coats a beach.
  2. Natural bacteria can break down hydrocarbons but are limited by nitrogen and phosphorus.
  3. Fertiliser is added to the beach.
  4. Bacterial growth increases and oil is broken down faster.
Common mistakeThinking all bacteria in water are harmful. Most aquatic microbes are harmless and essential; indicator organisms signal contamination.

In 3 lines: Soil and water communities. Sewage treatment. Bioremediation.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which organism is used as an indicator of faecal contamination in water?
    • Escherichia coli
    • Penicillium mould
    • Plasmodium
    • Rhizobium
    Show the answer

    Answer: Escherichia coli. E. coli lives in the gut, so its presence signals faecal pollution.

  2. Why were fertilisers added to oil-covered beaches?
    • To kill all bacteria on the beach
    • To boost growth of oil-degrading bacteria
    • To dissolve the oil chemically
    • To feed seabirds
    Show the answer

    Answer: To boost growth of oil-degrading bacteria. Nitrogen and phosphorus limited the bacteria's growth.

  3. How was wastewater testing useful during COVID-19?
    • It cured infected people
    • It vaccinated the population
    • It tracked infection levels across whole communities
    • It replaced all hospital testing
    Show the answer

    Answer: It tracked infection levels across whole communities. Viral RNA in sewage reflected community spread.

Key terms

Indicator organism
A microbe whose presence signals contamination
Bioremediation
Using organisms to remove pollutants
Biogas
Gas, mainly methane, from anaerobic digestion
