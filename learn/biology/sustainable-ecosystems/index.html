Sustainable Ecosystems

Sustainability meets current needs without harming the future; fisheries, forests and farms can be managed sustainably using quotas, maximum sustainable yield, certification and wildlife-friendly practices, with trade-offs between land sharing, land sparing and human needs.

Biology · Ecology and Environmental Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define sustainability in an ecological context
  • Describe sustainable management of fisheries, forests and farmland
  • Evaluate trade-offs between conservation and human needs

Sustainable management

  • Sustainable fisheries: quotas, minimum mesh sizes, closed seasons and marine protected areas.
  • Maximum sustainable yield: the largest catch that can be taken without reducing future stocks.
  • Sustainable forestry: selective felling, replanting and certification such as FSC.
  • Sustainable farming: crop rotation, integrated pest management, hedgerows and reduced fertiliser.

Trade-offs

  • Land sharing: wildlife-friendly farming across large areas.
  • Land sparing: intensive farming on less land, leaving more for nature.
  • Balancing food security, livelihoods, biodiversity and climate.
  • Local communities often play key roles in success.

The collapse of the Grand Banks cod fishery off Canada in 1992, after decades of overfishing, shows the cost of unsustainable harvesting. In England, Environmental Land Management schemes pay farmers for public goods such as habitat creation. Diets with less meat require less land, reducing pressure on ecosystems.

Worked example

The cod collapse.

  1. Atlantic cod were fished intensively off Newfoundland for decades.
  2. Catches exceeded the population's ability to recover.
  3. In 1992 the population collapsed and a fishing ban began.
  4. Tens of thousands of jobs were lost and recovery has been slow.
Common mistakeThinking sustainable means no use of resources. It means using them at a rate that can continue.

In 3 lines: Sustainable fishing and forestry. Sustainable farming. Trade-offs.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which practice supports sustainable forestry?
    • Replanting after felling
    • Clear-felling without replanting
    • Burning forests for farmland
    • Removing all young trees
    Show the answer

    Answer: Replanting after felling. Replanting allows the forest to regrow and supply timber in future.

  2. Why do minimum mesh sizes help fish stocks?
    • They catch more young fish
    • Young fish escape and can breed
    • They increase bycatch
    • They stop all fishing
    Show the answer

    Answer: Young fish escape and can breed. Letting juveniles survive maintains future populations.

  3. What does the Grand Banks cod collapse show?
    • Fish stocks always recover quickly
    • Fishing has no effect on cod
    • Overharvesting beyond recovery can destroy a resource
    • Quotas cause collapses
    Show the answer

    Answer: Overharvesting beyond recovery can destroy a resource. Unsustainable catches led to lasting collapse.

Key terms

Sustainability
Meeting present needs without harming future generations
Maximum sustainable yield
The largest catch that does not reduce future stocks
Land sparing
Farming intensively on less land to leave more for nature
