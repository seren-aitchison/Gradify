Scientific Notation

Write numbers as A × 10ⁿ with 1 ≤ A < 10; add powers when multiplying and subtract when dividing.

Biology · Foundations of Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Write very large and very small numbers in standard form
  • Multiply and divide numbers in standard form
  • Use significant figures appropriately

Standard form

A number in standard form is written as A × 10ⁿ, where 1 ≤ A < 10 and n is an integer. 3,700,000 = 3.7 × 10⁶; 0.000052 = 5.2 × 10⁻⁵.

Calculating

  • Multiplying: multiply the numbers and add the powers: (3 × 10⁴) × (2 × 10³) = 6 × 10⁷.
  • Dividing: divide the numbers and subtract the powers: (8 × 10⁶) ÷ (4 × 10²) = 2 × 10⁴.
  • Then adjust A to lie between 1 and 10 if needed: 12 × 10⁵ = 1.2 × 10⁶.
Worked example

A bacterium divides every 20 minutes. Starting from one cell, how many cells are there after 10 hours, in standard form?

  1. 10 hours = 600 minutes = 30 generations.
  2. Number of cells = 2³⁰ = 1,073,741,824 ≈ 1.07 × 10⁹.

Significant figures

Give answers to the same number of significant figures as the least precise data used. Zeros between non-zero digits count; leading zeros don't (0.0052 has 2 significant figures).

Common mistakeWriting 25 × 10³ as standard form. A must be between 1 and 10: 2.5 × 10⁴.

In 2 lines: Standard form handles huge and tiny numbers. Round answers to match the precision of the data.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is 6.2 × 10⁻³ written as an ordinary number?
    • 0.062
    • 0.0062
    • 6,200
    • 0.00062
    Show the answer

    Answer: 0.0062. A negative power moves the decimal point left: three places gives 0.0062.

  2. A virus is 1 × 10⁻⁷ m across and a cell 2 × 10⁻⁵ m. How many times wider is the cell?
    • 20
    • 2,000
    • 200
    • 0.005
    Show the answer

    Answer: 200. Divide the numbers and subtract the powers: (2 × 10⁻⁵) ÷ (1 × 10⁻⁷) = 2 × 10² = 200.

  3. A population of bacteria grows from 5 × 10³ to 8 × 10⁶ cells. Approximately how many generations (doublings) is this?
    • about 3
    • about 1,600
    • about 160
    • about 11
    Show the answer

    Answer: about 11. 8 × 10⁶ ÷ 5 × 10³ = 1,600, and 2¹⁰·⁶ ≈ 1,600, so about 11 doublings.

Key terms

Standard form
A way of writing numbers as A × 10ⁿ where 1 ≤ A < 10
Significant figures
The digits in a number that carry meaningful information about its precision
