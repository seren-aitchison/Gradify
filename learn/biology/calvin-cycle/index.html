Calvin Cycle

In the Calvin cycle, RuBisCO fixes CO₂ to RuBP forming GP, GP is reduced to TP using ATP and NADPH, most TP regenerates RuBP and some forms sugars and other molecules; RuBisCO can also cause photorespiration.

Biology · Plant Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe carbon fixation by RuBisCO
  • Explain the reduction and regeneration stages
  • Explain the products of the Calvin cycle

Three stages

  • Carbon fixation: CO₂ combines with RuBP (5-carbon), catalysed by RuBisCO, forming two molecules of GP (3-carbon).
  • Reduction: GP is reduced to triose phosphate (TP) using ATP and NADPH.
  • Regeneration: most TP is used to regenerate RuBP, using ATP.
  • One in six TP molecules leaves the cycle to make sugars.

Products and RuBisCO

  • TP is used to make glucose, sucrose, starch, amino acids and lipids.
  • Six turns of the cycle fix six CO₂ to make one glucose.
  • RuBisCO is probably the most abundant protein on Earth.
  • RuBisCO also reacts with O₂ (photorespiration), wasting energy.

Melvin Calvin and colleagues traced the pathway using radioactive carbon-14 and the alga Chlorella, earning the 1961 Nobel Prize. The cycle is called light-independent but depends on ATP and NADPH from the light reactions, so it stops soon after the light is removed. Photorespiration increases at high temperatures.

Worked example

The lollipop experiment.

  1. Calvin supplied Chlorella with radioactive CO₂ for a few seconds.
  2. The algae were dropped into hot alcohol to stop reactions.
  3. GP was the first radioactive compound detected.
  4. Longer times showed label in TP, sugars and RuBP, revealing the cycle.
Common mistakeThinking the Calvin cycle needs no light at all. It depends on ATP and NADPH made in the light.

In 3 lines: Fixation by RuBisCO. Reduction to TP. Regeneration of RuBP.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What does carbon dioxide combine with in carbon fixation?
    • Glucose
    • Pyruvate
    • Ribulose bisphosphate (RuBP)
    • Oxygen
    Show the answer

    Answer: Ribulose bisphosphate (RuBP). CO₂ joins the 5-carbon RuBP to form two GP.

  2. Why does the Calvin cycle stop soon after the light is removed?
    • It needs light to activate glucose
    • RuBisCO is destroyed in the dark
    • Carbon dioxide disappears in darkness
    • It runs out of ATP and NADPH from the light reactions
    Show the answer

    Answer: It runs out of ATP and NADPH from the light reactions. The cycle depends on products of the light reactions.

  3. Why is photorespiration wasteful?
    • RuBisCO fixes oxygen instead of CO₂, losing energy and carbon
    • It produces extra glucose
    • It saves ATP for the plant
    • It only occurs at night
    Show the answer

    Answer: RuBisCO fixes oxygen instead of CO₂, losing energy and carbon. Fixing O₂ produces compounds that cost energy to recycle.

Key terms

RuBisCO
The enzyme fixing CO₂ to RuBP
Triose phosphate
A 3-carbon sugar produced in the Calvin cycle
Photorespiration
Reaction of RuBisCO with oxygen, wasting energy
