Xylem

Xylem vessels and tracheids are dead, lignified cells forming tubes without cytoplasm or end walls; lignin waterproofs and strengthens them against tension, pits allow sideways flow, and wood is secondary xylem.

Biology · Plant Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the structure of xylem vessels and tracheids
  • Relate xylem structure to its function
  • Explain the role of lignin

Structure

  • Vessel elements: dead cells joined end to end to form continuous tubes with no end walls.
  • Tracheids: long narrow dead cells with tapered ends, found in all vascular plants.
  • Walls are thickened with lignin, often in rings or spirals.
  • Pits in the walls allow water to move sideways between vessels.
  • Xylem also contains parenchyma and fibres.

Structure and function

  • No cytoplasm or end walls means water flows without obstruction.
  • Lignin makes walls waterproof and strong, preventing collapse under tension.
  • Narrow tubes help water rise by capillarity and cohesion.
  • Flowering plants have mainly vessels; conifers mainly tracheids.

Wood is secondary xylem. Lignin is one of the most abundant organic polymers on Earth and makes wood difficult to digest. Air bubbles, called embolisms, can block xylem vessels in drought or freezing, and pits allow water to bypass blocked vessels.

Worked example

Why xylem does not collapse.

  1. Water in xylem is under tension as it is pulled up by transpiration.
  2. This tension would collapse a thin-walled tube.
  3. Lignin rings and spirals reinforce the walls.
  4. The vessels stay open, like a reinforced hose.
Common mistakeThinking xylem cells are alive when functional. Mature vessel elements and tracheids are dead.

In 3 lines: Dead, hollow tubes. Lignin strengthens. Pits and embolisms.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What substance thickens and strengthens xylem walls?
    • Cellulose only
    • Starch
    • Lignin
    • Chlorophyll
    Show the answer

    Answer: Lignin. Lignin reinforces and waterproofs xylem walls.

  2. Why are mature xylem vessels dead?
    • Living cells are needed to pump water
    • Dead cells carry sugars better
    • They died from lack of light
    • Empty tubes let water flow freely without cytoplasm
    Show the answer

    Answer: Empty tubes let water flow freely without cytoplasm. Without contents, xylem forms open pipes.

  3. How do pits help when an embolism blocks a vessel?
    • Water can bypass the blocked vessel sideways
    • They remove the air bubble instantly
    • They stop all water flow
    • They make the vessel wider
    Show the answer

    Answer: Water can bypass the blocked vessel sideways. Pits let water reroute into neighbouring vessels.

Key terms

Vessel element
A dead xylem cell forming part of a continuous tube
Lignin
A strong waterproof polymer in cell walls
Embolism
An air bubble blocking a xylem vessel
