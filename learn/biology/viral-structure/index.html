Viral Structure

Viruses have a DNA or RNA genome in a protein capsid, sometimes with a lipid envelope; they lack cells and metabolism and replicate only inside hosts, and are classified by genome type.

Biology · Microbiology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the structure of a virus
  • Explain why viruses are not usually considered living
  • Classify viruses by genome type

Structure

  • A genome of DNA or RNA, single- or double-stranded.
  • A protein coat called a capsid, made of capsomeres.
  • Some have a lipid envelope from the host membrane, with glycoproteins.
  • Sizes are typically 20–300 nm, much smaller than bacteria.
  • Shapes include helical, icosahedral and complex (such as bacteriophages).

Living or not

  • Viruses have no cells, no ribosomes and no metabolism.
  • They can only replicate inside host cells.
  • They do evolve and carry genetic information.
  • Viruses are classified by genome type, such as DNA viruses (herpes), RNA viruses (influenza) and retroviruses (HIV).

Viruses infect all forms of life, including bacteria (bacteriophages), plants (tobacco mosaic virus) and animals. Enveloped viruses, such as influenza and SARS-CoV-2, are destroyed by soap and alcohol because these disrupt the lipid envelope. Viruses were first identified when Dmitri Ivanovsky showed in 1892 that tobacco mosaic disease passed through filters that held back bacteria.

Worked example

Why soap works on SARS-CoV-2.

  1. SARS-CoV-2 has a lipid envelope with spike glycoproteins.
  2. Soap molecules insert into the lipid envelope.
  3. The envelope breaks apart.
  4. The virus can no longer infect cells.
Common mistakeThinking antibiotics kill viruses. Antibiotics target bacterial structures that viruses do not have.

In 3 lines: Genome in a capsid. Not cells. Classified by genome.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is a virus's genetic material made of?
    • Only protein
    • Only lipid
    • DNA or RNA
    • Peptidoglycan
    Show the answer

    Answer: DNA or RNA. Viruses carry either DNA or RNA genomes, never both in one particle.

  2. Why does soap inactivate enveloped viruses?
    • It dissolves their DNA directly
    • It removes their ribosomes
    • It feeds the virus
    • It disrupts their lipid envelope
    Show the answer

    Answer: It disrupts their lipid envelope. Breaking the envelope prevents infection.

  3. Why are antibiotics ineffective against viruses?
    • Viruses lack the bacterial structures antibiotics target
    • Viruses are far too large for antibiotics to reach them
    • Antibiotics only work on fungi
    • Viruses have cell walls
    Show the answer

    Answer: Viruses lack the bacterial structures antibiotics target. Viruses have no peptidoglycan or ribosomes of their own.

Key terms

Capsid
The protein coat of a virus
Envelope
A lipid membrane surrounding some viruses
Bacteriophage
A virus that infects bacteria
