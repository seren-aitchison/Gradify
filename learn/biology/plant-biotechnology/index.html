Plant Biotechnology

Plant biotechnology includes micropropagation, genetic modification using Agrobacterium, gene editing and marker-assisted selection; examples such as Bt crops and Golden Rice offer benefits but raise concerns about gene flow, resistance and control.

Biology · Plant Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe micropropagation
  • Explain how genetically modified crops are produced
  • Evaluate the benefits and risks of plant biotechnology

Techniques

  • Micropropagation: tissue culture produces many clones from small samples.
  • Genetic modification: genes are inserted, often using Agrobacterium tumefaciens to carry DNA into plant cells.
  • Gene editing, such as CRISPR, makes precise changes without adding foreign DNA.
  • Marker-assisted selection speeds up conventional breeding.

Examples and debate

  • Bt crops produce an insecticidal protein from Bacillus thuringiensis.
  • Golden Rice produces beta-carotene to reduce vitamin A deficiency.
  • Benefits: higher yields, pest resistance, drought tolerance, improved nutrition.
  • Concerns: gene flow to wild plants, resistance in pests, corporate control and public acceptance.

In England, the Genetic Technology (Precision Breeding) Act 2023 created a new regulatory route for gene-edited plants whose changes could have arisen through traditional breeding. Golden Rice was approved for cultivation in the Philippines in 2021. Biotechnology is also used to conserve rare plants and produce disease-free stock.

Worked example

Making a Bt crop.

  1. The Bt toxin gene is inserted into a plasmid in Agrobacterium.
  2. Agrobacterium infects plant cells and transfers the gene.
  3. Transformed cells are grown into whole plants by tissue culture.
  4. The plants produce Bt toxin, killing certain insect pests that eat them.
Common mistakeThinking all gene-edited plants contain foreign DNA. Gene editing can make precise changes without adding foreign genes.

In 3 lines: Micropropagation. GM and gene editing. Benefits and concerns.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which bacterium is often used to insert genes into plants?
    • Agrobacterium tumefaciens
    • Escherichia coli
    • Staphylococcus aureus
    • Streptococcus pneumoniae
    Show the answer

    Answer: Agrobacterium tumefaciens. Agrobacterium naturally transfers DNA into plant cells.

  2. What is a concern about growing Bt crops widely?
    • Bt crops cannot grow in fields
    • Pests may evolve resistance to the Bt toxin
    • Bt toxin harms all mammals
    • They produce no grain
    Show the answer

    Answer: Pests may evolve resistance to the Bt toxin. Strong selection may favour resistant insects.

  3. How does gene editing differ from traditional genetic modification?
    • It always adds genes from other species
    • It uses no enzymes
    • It can make precise changes without adding foreign DNA
    • It only works in animals
    Show the answer

    Answer: It can make precise changes without adding foreign DNA. CRISPR edits existing sequences directly.

Key terms

Micropropagation
Producing many clones using tissue culture
Agrobacterium
A bacterium used to transfer genes into plants
Bt crop
A crop producing an insecticidal protein from Bacillus thuringiensis
