MHC Molecules

MHC (HLA) molecules display peptides in a groove to T cells; class I binds short and class II longer peptides, MHC genes are highly polymorphic and codominant, protecting populations, and HLA matching is crucial in transplantation and some drug safety.

Biology · Immunology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the structure and function of MHC molecules
  • Explain why MHC genes are highly polymorphic
  • Explain the importance of MHC in transplantation

Structure and function

  • Major histocompatibility complex (MHC) molecules are cell-surface proteins.
  • In humans they are called HLA (human leucocyte antigens).
  • Each has a peptide-binding groove that holds a short peptide.
  • Class I binds peptides of about 8–10 amino acids; class II binds longer peptides.

Diversity

  • MHC genes are the most polymorphic genes in humans, with thousands of alleles.
  • Each person inherits a set from each parent and expresses both (codominance).
  • Different alleles bind different peptides.
  • Diversity means a population is unlikely to be wiped out by one pathogen.

Because HLA types differ between people, transplanted organs are recognised as foreign, so donors and recipients are matched as closely as possible. Some HLA alleles are linked to disease: HLA-B27 is associated with ankylosing spondylitis. HLA-B*57:01 predicts a serious reaction to the HIV drug abacavir, so patients are tested before treatment.

Worked example

Testing before abacavir.

  1. A patient with HIV is due to start abacavir.
  2. A genetic test checks for the HLA-B*57:01 allele.
  3. The allele is present.
  4. A different drug is chosen to avoid a dangerous hypersensitivity reaction.
Common mistakeThinking everyone has the same MHC molecules. HLA types vary greatly between people.

In 3 lines: Peptide-binding groove. Highly polymorphic. Matching and drug safety.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Where on an MHC molecule does the peptide bind?
    • In the peptide-binding groove
    • In the hinge region
    • In the constant region
    • In the nucleus
    Show the answer

    Answer: In the peptide-binding groove. The groove holds a short peptide for display.

  2. Why is MHC diversity an advantage to a population?
    • It makes every person identical
    • Different alleles present different peptides, so no pathogen escapes everyone
    • It stops all T cell responses
    • It prevents transplants
    Show the answer

    Answer: Different alleles present different peptides, so no pathogen escapes everyone. Varied presentation protects the population as a whole.

  3. Why are patients tested for HLA-B*57:01 before taking abacavir?
    • The drug only works with this allele
    • The test diagnoses HIV infection
    • The allele predicts a dangerous hypersensitivity reaction
    • It measures the viral load
    Show the answer

    Answer: The allele predicts a dangerous hypersensitivity reaction. Screening avoids a serious immune reaction.

Key terms

MHC
Major histocompatibility complex molecules presenting peptides
HLA
Human leucocyte antigens, the human MHC
Polymorphic
Having many different alleles in a population
