C4 Photosynthesis

C4 plants fix CO₂ with PEP carboxylase in mesophyll cells to form 4-carbon acids, which release CO₂ in bundle sheath cells for RuBisCO; this reduces photorespiration and suits hot, dry conditions at an extra ATP cost.

Biology · Plant Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the C4 pathway and Kranz anatomy
  • Explain how C4 plants reduce photorespiration
  • Compare C3 and C4 plants

The pathway

  • In mesophyll cells, PEP carboxylase fixes CO₂ to PEP, forming 4-carbon oxaloacetate, then malate.
  • PEP carboxylase does not bind O₂.
  • Malate moves to bundle sheath cells, where CO₂ is released.
  • RuBisCO in bundle sheath cells fixes this CO₂ in the Calvin cycle.
  • Kranz anatomy: bundle sheath cells arranged in a ring around veins.

Advantages and costs

  • High CO₂ in bundle sheath cells almost eliminates photorespiration.
  • C4 plants grow well in hot, sunny, dry conditions.
  • They use water and nitrogen more efficiently.
  • The pathway costs extra ATP, so C4 plants have less advantage in cool conditions.

About 3% of plant species are C4, but they include important crops such as maize, sugarcane and sorghum, and they account for around a quarter of land photosynthesis. C4 photosynthesis has evolved independently more than 60 times. Researchers are trying to engineer C4 traits into rice to increase yields.

Worked example

Maize in summer.

  1. Maize grows in hot, sunny fields.
  2. PEP carboxylase fixes CO₂ even when stomata are partly closed.
  3. Malate delivers CO₂ to bundle sheath cells.
  4. RuBisCO works at high CO₂, so maize grows rapidly.
Common mistakeThinking C4 plants do not use the Calvin cycle. They use it in bundle sheath cells.

In 3 lines: PEP carboxylase first. CO₂ concentrated in bundle sheath. Suits hot, dry conditions.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is the first product of CO₂ fixation in C4 plants?
    • A 4-carbon acid such as oxaloacetate
    • The 3-carbon compound GP
    • Glucose directly
    • Ribulose bisphosphate
    Show the answer

    Answer: A 4-carbon acid such as oxaloacetate. PEP carboxylase forms 4-carbon oxaloacetate.

  2. How do C4 plants reduce photorespiration?
    • They remove RuBisCO entirely
    • They concentrate CO₂ around RuBisCO in bundle sheath cells
    • They open stomata only at night
    • They use more oxygen
    Show the answer

    Answer: They concentrate CO₂ around RuBisCO in bundle sheath cells. High CO₂ lets RuBisCO fix carbon rather than oxygen.

  3. Why do C4 plants have less advantage in cool conditions?
    • They cannot photosynthesise in cold
    • RuBisCO works faster in C4 plants
    • The extra ATP cost outweighs the small photorespiration saving
    • Cool conditions increase photorespiration
    Show the answer

    Answer: The extra ATP cost outweighs the small photorespiration saving. Photorespiration is low in cool weather, so C3 plants are competitive.

Key terms

PEP carboxylase
An enzyme fixing CO₂ into 4-carbon acids
Bundle sheath cell
A cell around veins where C4 plants run the Calvin cycle
Kranz anatomy
The ring arrangement of bundle sheath cells
