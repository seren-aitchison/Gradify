Introduction to Microbiology

Microbiology studies bacteria, archaea, fungi, protists and viruses; key figures include Leeuwenhoek, Pasteur, Koch and Fleming, and microbes drive nutrient cycles and are mostly harmless or beneficial.

Biology · Microbiology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Name the main groups of microorganisms
  • Describe key discoveries in the history of microbiology
  • Explain the importance of microbes to life on Earth

Groups of microbes

  • Bacteria and archaea: prokaryotic, single-celled.
  • Fungi: yeasts and moulds, eukaryotic.
  • Protists: single-celled eukaryotes such as amoebae and algae.
  • Viruses: non-cellular particles that replicate only inside host cells.
  • Most microbes are harmless or beneficial; only a small fraction cause disease.

History

  • Antonie van Leeuwenhoek observed "animalcules" with simple microscopes in the 1670s.
  • Louis Pasteur disproved spontaneous generation with swan-necked flasks in the 1860s.
  • Robert Koch linked specific microbes to specific diseases, such as anthrax and tuberculosis.
  • Alexander Fleming discovered penicillin in 1928.

Microbes drive nutrient cycles such as the carbon and nitrogen cycles, produced much of Earth's oxygen through ancient cyanobacteria and live in and on every human. Koch's postulates set out criteria for showing a microbe causes a disease, though not all pathogens fit them, for example those that cannot be grown in culture.

Worked example

Pasteur's swan-necked flask.

  1. Pasteur boiled broth in flasks with long, curved necks.
  2. Air could enter, but dust and microbes were trapped in the bend.
  3. The broth stayed clear for a long time.
  4. When the neck was broken, microbes entered and the broth became cloudy.
Common mistakeThinking most microbes cause disease. Only a small proportion of microbes are pathogens.

In 3 lines: Bacteria, archaea, fungi, protists, viruses. Leeuwenhoek, Pasteur, Koch, Fleming. Microbes are essential.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which group of microbes is non-cellular?
    • Bacteria
    • Viruses
    • Fungi
    • Protists
    Show the answer

    Answer: Viruses. Viruses are particles that replicate only inside host cells.

  2. What did Koch's postulates establish?
    • That all microbes found in sick people are harmful
    • That viruses can always be grown on nutrient agar
    • Criteria for proving a microbe causes a specific disease
    • That bacteria arise spontaneously
    Show the answer

    Answer: Criteria for proving a microbe causes a specific disease. Koch linked specific microbes to specific diseases.

  3. Why do Koch's postulates not apply to all pathogens?
    • All pathogens can be grown easily
    • Postulates only apply to viruses
    • Koch's postulates were disproved entirely
    • Some pathogens cannot be grown in pure culture
    Show the answer

    Answer: Some pathogens cannot be grown in pure culture. Unculturable microbes and carriers break the rules.

Key terms

Microorganism
A living organism too small to see without a microscope
Koch's postulates
Criteria linking a microbe to a disease
Spontaneous generation
The disproved idea that life arises from non-living matter
