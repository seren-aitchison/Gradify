Genomes

A genome is all an organism's DNA; the human genome has about 3.1 billion base pairs and 20,000 protein-coding genes, with only 1–2% coding protein, and eukaryotic genomes are larger and less gene-dense than prokaryotic ones, with size not matching complexity.

Biology · Genetics and Molecular Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define genome and describe genome size
  • Describe the composition of the human genome
  • Compare prokaryotic and eukaryotic genomes

Genome size and content

  • The genome is the complete set of DNA in an organism, including genes and non-coding DNA.
  • The human nuclear genome has about 3.1 billion base pairs and roughly 20,000 protein-coding genes.
  • Protein-coding sequences make up only about 1–2% of the human genome.
  • The rest includes regulatory sequences, introns, repetitive DNA and genes for functional RNAs.

Prokaryotes and eukaryotes

  • Prokaryotic genomes are small and gene-dense, with few introns.
  • Eukaryotic genomes are larger, with introns, many regulatory regions and repeats.
  • Genome size does not reliably reflect complexity: some plants and amphibians have much larger genomes than humans.
  • Mitochondria and chloroplasts carry small separate genomes.

The Human Genome Project, completed in 2003, produced a reference sequence; a complete, gap-free sequence was published by the Telomere-to-Telomere Consortium in 2022. Comparing genomes between species reveals evolutionary relationships and conserved genes. Sequencing is now fast and cheap, enabling personalised medicine and large population studies.

Worked example

The C-value paradox.

  1. Humans have about 3.1 billion base pairs.
  2. Some onion species have genomes several times larger.
  3. The extra DNA is mostly repetitive and non-coding.
  4. So genome size does not simply match organismal complexity.
Common mistakeAssuming most of the human genome codes for proteins. Only about 1–2% does.

In 3 lines: Genome: all the DNA. About 20,000 human genes. Most DNA does not code protein.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is a genome?
    • A single gene
    • A chromosome pair
    • All the DNA of an organism
    • One protein
    Show the answer

    Answer: All the DNA of an organism. The genome includes all genes and non-coding DNA.

  2. How do prokaryotic genomes usually differ from eukaryotic ones?
    • They contain more introns
    • They are always larger
    • They have no genes
    • They are smaller and more gene-dense
    Show the answer

    Answer: They are smaller and more gene-dense. Prokaryotic genomes pack genes closely with little non-coding DNA.

  3. Why does genome size not reliably reflect an organism's complexity?
    • Much DNA is non-coding and repetitive, varying greatly between species
    • Complex organisms have no DNA in their cells at all
    • Every species has exactly the same genome size
    • Genes are never found within genomes of any kind
    Show the answer

    Answer: Much DNA is non-coding and repetitive, varying greatly between species. Large genomes often contain extensive repeats, not more genes.

Key terms

Genome
All the DNA in an organism
Non-coding DNA
DNA that does not code for proteins
Intron
A non-coding region within a gene
