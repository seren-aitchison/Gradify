Viral Replication

Viral replication involves attachment, entry, replication, assembly and release; the lytic cycle bursts cells while the lysogenic cycle integrates viral DNA, and retroviruses use reverse transcriptase to insert DNA into the host genome.

Biology · Microbiology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the stages of viral replication
  • Compare the lytic and lysogenic cycles
  • Explain how retroviruses replicate

Stages

  • Attachment: viral proteins bind specific host cell receptors.
  • Entry: by fusion or endocytosis, or injection of DNA by phages.
  • Replication: the viral genome is copied and viral proteins made using host machinery.
  • Assembly: new viruses are put together.
  • Release: by lysis (bursting) or budding.

Cycles and retroviruses

  • Lytic cycle: the virus replicates immediately and the cell bursts.
  • Lysogenic cycle: viral DNA integrates into the host chromosome as a prophage and is copied with it, later becoming lytic.
  • Retroviruses, such as HIV, use reverse transcriptase to make DNA from RNA.
  • This DNA integrates into the host genome as a provirus.

Host specificity depends on receptors: HIV binds CD4 on helper T cells, and SARS-CoV-2 binds ACE2. Antiviral drugs target viral steps: reverse transcriptase inhibitors for HIV, and neuraminidase inhibitors that block release of influenza. Latent viruses, such as herpes simplex, can hide in cells and reactivate, causing cold sores.

Worked example

HIV replication.

  1. HIV binds CD4 receptors on a helper T cell.
  2. Its RNA enters and reverse transcriptase makes DNA.
  3. The DNA integrates into the host chromosome.
  4. The cell makes new HIV particles, which bud from the membrane.
Common mistakeThinking viruses can infect any cell. They need specific receptors on host cells.

In 3 lines: Attach, enter, replicate, assemble, release. Lytic versus lysogenic. Retroviruses integrate.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is the first stage of viral replication?
    • Release by lysis
    • Attachment to host cell receptors
    • Assembly of new particles
    • Reverse transcription
    Show the answer

    Answer: Attachment to host cell receptors. Viruses must first bind specific receptors.

  2. What does reverse transcriptase do?
    • Makes RNA from DNA
    • Makes proteins from RNA
    • Makes DNA from an RNA template
    • Breaks down viral DNA
    Show the answer

    Answer: Makes DNA from an RNA template. Retroviruses convert their RNA genome into DNA.

  3. How do neuraminidase inhibitors treat influenza?
    • They stop the virus attaching
    • They destroy the host cell
    • They boost reverse transcriptase
    • They stop new viruses being released from cells
    Show the answer

    Answer: They stop new viruses being released from cells. Blocking release limits spread to new cells.

Key terms

Lytic cycle
Viral replication ending in cell lysis
Lysogenic cycle
Integration of viral DNA into the host genome
Reverse transcriptase
An enzyme making DNA from RNA
