Adaptive Immunity

Adaptive immunity is specific, diverse and has memory; humoral immunity uses antibodies from B cells and cell-mediated immunity uses T cells, lymphocytes are selected by clonal selection and secondary responses are faster and stronger.

Biology · Immunology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the features of adaptive immunity
  • Distinguish humoral and cell-mediated immunity
  • Compare primary and secondary immune responses

Features

  • Specific: each lymphocyte recognises one antigen.
  • Memory: faster, stronger responses on re-exposure.
  • Diversity: millions of different receptors.
  • Takes days to develop on first exposure.

Two branches

  • Humoral immunity: B cells make antibodies that act in body fluids.
  • Cell-mediated immunity: T cells act directly, with cytotoxic T cells killing infected cells.
  • Helper T cells support both branches.
  • Clonal selection: lymphocytes that recognise an antigen are selected and multiply.

In the primary response, it takes about 1–2 weeks for antibody levels to rise; in the secondary response, antibodies rise faster, higher and last longer. This is the principle behind vaccines and booster doses. Adaptive immunity evolved in jawed vertebrates, while jawless fish have a different adaptive system.

Worked example

Primary and secondary responses.

  1. A child is exposed to measles virus for the first time.
  2. Antibodies rise slowly over about two weeks, and they become ill.
  3. Years later, they are exposed again.
  4. Memory cells produce antibodies within days, and they do not become ill.
Common mistakeThinking adaptive immunity acts immediately. On first exposure it takes days to develop.

In 3 lines: Specific with memory. Humoral and cell-mediated. Primary and secondary responses.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which feature is unique to adaptive immunity?
    • Physical barriers
    • Immunological memory
    • Phagocytosis
    • Inflammation
    Show the answer

    Answer: Immunological memory. Memory cells allow faster responses on re-exposure.

  2. How does the secondary response differ from the primary response?
    • It is slower and weaker
    • It uses no antibodies
    • It is faster, stronger and longer-lasting
    • It only involves innate cells
    Show the answer

    Answer: It is faster, stronger and longer-lasting. Memory cells produce a rapid, large response.

  3. Why are booster vaccine doses given?
    • To replace innate immunity
    • To cure existing infections
    • To remove memory cells
    • To strengthen and extend immune memory
    Show the answer

    Answer: To strengthen and extend immune memory. Re-exposure increases antibody levels and memory.
