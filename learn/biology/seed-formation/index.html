Seed Formation

Seeds have a testa, embryo with radicle, plumule and cotyledons, and a food store; monocots have one cotyledon and eudicots two, and seeds are dispersed by wind, animals, water and explosion, reducing competition.

Biology · Plant Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the structure of a seed
  • Compare monocot and eudicot seeds
  • Describe methods of seed and fruit dispersal

Seed structure

  • Testa (seed coat): protects the embryo.
  • Embryo: radicle (young root), plumule (young shoot) and cotyledons (seed leaves).
  • Food store: in the endosperm or cotyledons.
  • Seeds dry out and become dormant.

Monocots, eudicots and dispersal

  • Monocots (such as maize) have one cotyledon and food in the endosperm.
  • Eudicots (such as beans) have two cotyledons that often store food.
  • Dispersal methods: wind (sycamore wings, dandelion parachutes), animals (fleshy fruits, hooks), water (coconuts) and explosion (peas, gorse).

Dispersal reduces competition with the parent and allows colonisation of new areas. Seed banks, such as the Millennium Seed Bank at Kew, store seeds of tens of thousands of species at low temperature and humidity to conserve plant diversity. Some seeds can remain viable for very long periods: a date palm was grown from a seed about 2,000 years old.

Worked example

The Millennium Seed Bank.

  1. Seeds are collected from wild plants worldwide.
  2. They are dried and frozen at about −20 °C.
  3. This slows metabolism and keeps them viable for decades or more.
  4. Stored seeds can be used to restore threatened species.
Common mistakeThinking all seeds contain endosperm when mature. In many eudicots, the cotyledons absorb the endosperm.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is the role of the testa?
    • To protect the embryo
    • To store food
    • To absorb sunlight
    • To produce pollen
    Show the answer

    Answer: To protect the embryo. The seed coat shields the embryo from damage.

  2. Why is seed dispersal important?
    • It keeps seeds close to the parent
    • It reduces competition with the parent and spreads the species
    • It prevents germination
    • It increases competition
    Show the answer

    Answer: It reduces competition with the parent and spreads the species. Spreading seeds lets offspring find new space.

  3. Why are seeds stored dry and cold in seed banks?
    • Warmth helps seeds germinate in storage
    • Wet seeds last longer
    • Low temperature and humidity slow metabolism, keeping them viable
    • Seeds die instantly when cold
    Show the answer

    Answer: Low temperature and humidity slow metabolism, keeping them viable. Slowed metabolism preserves stored seeds.
