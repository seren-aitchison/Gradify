Plant Cell Structure

Plant cells have a cellulose wall, a large vacuole maintaining turgor, plastids such as chloroplasts and amyloplasts, and plasmodesmata linking cells into the symplast; turgor supports the plant, and its loss causes wilting.

Biology · Plant Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Identify structures found in plant cells but not animal cells
  • Relate the cell wall, vacuole and plastids to plant function
  • Explain how plant cells communicate through plasmodesmata

Distinctive structures

  • Cellulose cell wall: provides strength and resists bursting.
  • Large central vacuole: filled with cell sap, maintains turgor pressure.
  • Plastids: chloroplasts for photosynthesis, amyloplasts for starch storage and chromoplasts for pigments.
  • Plasmodesmata: channels through cell walls linking neighbouring cells.

Functions in the plant

  • Turgid cells press against their walls, keeping stems and leaves firm.
  • When water is lost, cells become flaccid and the plant wilts.
  • In strong solutions, the membrane pulls away from the wall: plasmolysis.
  • Secondary walls containing lignin strengthen woody tissues.

Plasmodesmata allow water, sugars and signalling molecules to pass between cells, forming a continuous pathway called the symplast. Some viruses spread through plants via plasmodesmata. The cell wall also forms the apoplast, a pathway for water outside the membranes, which is important in roots.

Worked example

Wilting lettuce.

  1. A lettuce leaf is left out on a warm day.
  2. Its cells lose water by evaporation.
  3. The vacuoles shrink and turgor pressure falls.
  4. The leaf becomes limp; placing it in water restores turgor.
Common mistakeThinking all plant cells contain chloroplasts. Root cells and many internal cells lack them.

In 3 lines: Wall, vacuole and plastids. Turgor supports plants. Plasmodesmata link cells.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which plastid stores starch in plant cells?
    • Amyloplast
    • Chromoplast
    • Mitochondrion
    • Ribosome
    Show the answer

    Answer: Amyloplast. Amyloplasts are starch-storing plastids found in roots and tubers.

  2. Why does a plant wilt when it lacks water?
    • Its cell walls dissolve completely
    • Its cells lose turgor pressure and become flaccid
    • Its chloroplasts stop working
    • Its roots grow too quickly
    Show the answer

    Answer: Its cells lose turgor pressure and become flaccid. Without water, vacuoles shrink and cells stop pressing on walls.

  3. What is the symplast pathway?
    • Movement through cell walls only
    • Movement inside xylem vessels only
    • Movement through cytoplasm connected by plasmodesmata
    • Movement through the air spaces of leaves
    Show the answer

    Answer: Movement through cytoplasm connected by plasmodesmata. The symplast links the cytoplasm of many cells into one pathway.

Key terms

Turgor pressure
Pressure of cell contents against the cell wall
Plasmodesmata
Channels linking adjacent plant cells
Plastid
A plant organelle such as a chloroplast or amyloplast
