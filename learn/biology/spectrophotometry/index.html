Spectrophotometry

Spectrophotometry measures absorbance at chosen wavelengths after zeroing with a blank; by the Beer-Lambert law absorbance is proportional to concentration, and it is used for nucleic acids, proteins, cell density and enzyme rates.

Biology · Experimental Biology and Research Methods · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain absorbance and transmission
  • Apply the Beer-Lambert law
  • Describe uses of spectrophotometry in biology

Principles

  • A spectrophotometer passes light of a chosen wavelength through a sample.
  • Absorbance measures how much light is absorbed.
  • A blank containing everything except the substance sets zero.
  • Colorimeters use coloured filters; spectrophotometers select precise wavelengths.

Beer-Lambert law

  • A = εcl: absorbance equals molar absorption coefficient × concentration × path length.
  • Absorbance is proportional to concentration over a working range.
  • Calibration curves of known concentrations give unknown values.
  • At high concentrations the relationship becomes non-linear.

Biological uses include measuring nucleic acids at 260 nm, protein assays such as the Bradford assay, bacterial culture density at 600 nm and enzyme reaction rates by following colour change. Cuvettes must be clean and handled by their frosted sides.

Worked example

Following bacterial growth.

  1. A bacterial culture is measured at 600 nm every 30 minutes.
  2. Absorbance rises as cells multiply and scatter light.
  3. Plotting absorbance against time shows a growth curve.
  4. This is quicker than counting colonies.
Common mistakeForgetting to zero with a blank. Without a blank, absorbance from the solvent or cuvette is included.

In 3 lines: Absorbance at a wavelength. A = εcl. Many biological uses.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Why is a blank used before measuring samples?
    • To increase the sample absorbance
    • To set zero absorbance for the solvent and cuvette
    • To measure the sample twice
    • To clean the cuvette
    Show the answer

    Answer: To set zero absorbance for the solvent and cuvette. The blank removes background absorbance from solvent and cuvette.

  2. Why does the Beer-Lambert relationship fail at high concentrations?
    • Absorbance always drops to zero instead
    • Path length changes
    • Absorbance stops rising in proportion to concentration
    • Light wavelength shifts randomly
    Show the answer

    Answer: Absorbance stops rising in proportion to concentration. At high concentration the response becomes non-linear.

  3. Why are cuvettes handled by their frosted sides?
    • The frosted sides are kept hotter for samples
    • It keeps samples cold
    • The clear sides are fragile
    • Fingerprints on clear sides would absorb or scatter light
    Show the answer

    Answer: Fingerprints on clear sides would absorb or scatter light. Fingerprints on the light path would absorb or scatter light.

Key terms

Absorbance

Blank
A reference sample used to zero the instrument
Beer-Lambert law
