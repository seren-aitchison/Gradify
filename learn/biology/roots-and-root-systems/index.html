Roots and Root Systems

Roots have a cap, growth zones, root hairs and layers including the endodermis with a Casparian strip; root systems are tap or fibrous, and root hairs and mycorrhizal fungi increase absorption of water and minerals.

Biology · Plant Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the structure of a root
  • Compare tap root and fibrous root systems
  • Explain how roots are adapted for absorption

Root structure

  • Root cap: protects the growing tip.
  • Zones: cell division, elongation and maturation.
  • Root hairs in the maturation zone increase surface area.
  • Internal layers: epidermis, cortex, endodermis and the central vascular cylinder.
  • The endodermis has a Casparian strip that controls entry to the xylem.

Root systems

  • Tap root: one main root with smaller branches, as in carrots and dandelions.
  • Fibrous roots: many similar roots, as in grasses.
  • Some roots store food, such as carrots and beetroot.
  • Many roots form partnerships with mycorrhizal fungi.

Root hairs greatly increase surface area for absorbing water by osmosis and minerals by active transport. Mycorrhizal fungi extend the root system and are especially important for phosphate uptake; most land plants form such associations. Fibrous root systems help prevent soil erosion, while tap roots can reach deeper water.

Worked example

Mycorrhizal partnership.

  1. Fungal threads (hyphae) grow around and into root cells.
  2. They extend far into the soil, reaching phosphate the roots cannot.
  3. The fungus passes phosphate and water to the plant.
  4. The plant gives the fungus sugars from photosynthesis.
Common mistakeThinking roots absorb water through the root cap. Most absorption occurs through root hairs in the maturation zone.

In 3 lines: Cap, zones and root hairs. Tap and fibrous systems. Mycorrhizal partnerships.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which structure protects the growing tip of a root?
    • The root hairs
    • The endodermis
    • The root cap
    • The xylem
    Show the answer

    Answer: The root cap. The root cap shields dividing cells as the root pushes through soil.

  2. How do mycorrhizal fungi benefit plants?
    • They carry out photosynthesis for the plant
    • They feed on the plant's roots harmfully
    • They prevent roots from growing
    • They increase uptake of phosphate and water
    Show the answer

    Answer: They increase uptake of phosphate and water. Fungal hyphae extend far into the soil and pass on nutrients.

  3. What is the role of the Casparian strip?
    • It forces water and ions through endodermal cell membranes
    • It speeds water up through cell walls
    • It stores starch in the root
    • It protects the root tip from damage
    Show the answer

    Answer: It forces water and ions through endodermal cell membranes. Blocking the wall pathway lets the plant control what enters the xylem.

Key terms

Root hair
An extension of a root epidermal cell increasing surface area
Casparian strip
A waterproof band in endodermal cell walls
Mycorrhiza
A mutualistic association between fungi and roots
