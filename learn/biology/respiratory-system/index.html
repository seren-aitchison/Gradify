Respiratory System

Air passes through the nose, pharynx, larynx, trachea, bronchi and bronchioles to the alveoli; mucus and cilia protect the airways, and diseases such as asthma, COPD and lung cancer, often linked to smoking, impair breathing.

Biology · Human Anatomy and Physiology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the structures of the respiratory system
  • Explain how the airways are protected
  • Describe common respiratory diseases

Structures

  • Air enters through the nose and mouth, then passes the pharynx and larynx.
  • The trachea is supported by C-shaped rings of cartilage.
  • It divides into two bronchi, then many bronchioles.
  • Bronchioles end in alveoli, where gas exchange happens.
  • The lungs are surrounded by pleural membranes and protected by the ribcage.

Protection and disease

  • Goblet cells produce mucus that traps particles and microbes.
  • Cilia sweep mucus upwards to the throat.
  • Smoking damages cilia, contributing to chronic bronchitis.
  • Asthma: inflammation and narrowing of airways.
  • COPD (chronic obstructive pulmonary disease): long-term airflow obstruction, mainly from smoking.

The nose warms, moistens and filters air. Smooth muscle in bronchiole walls can constrict, which is what happens in an asthma attack; inhalers containing bronchodilators relax this muscle. Lung cancer is the leading cause of cancer death in the UK, and most cases are linked to smoking.

Worked example

An asthma attack.

  1. A trigger, such as pollen, inflames the airways.
  2. Smooth muscle in bronchioles contracts and mucus increases.
  3. The airways narrow, making breathing difficult.
  4. A reliever inhaler relaxes the muscle and opens the airways.
Common mistakeThinking the trachea is held open by muscle. C-shaped cartilage rings keep it open.

In 3 lines: Trachea to alveoli. Mucus and cilia protect. Asthma, COPD and cancer.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Where does gas exchange take place in the lungs?
    • In the trachea
    • In the bronchi
    • In the alveoli
    • In the larynx
    Show the answer

    Answer: In the alveoli. Alveoli have thin walls and a large surface area.

  2. How does smoking contribute to chronic bronchitis?
    • It increases the number of alveoli
    • It strengthens the cartilage rings
    • It reduces mucus production completely
    • It damages cilia so mucus builds up
    Show the answer

    Answer: It damages cilia so mucus builds up. Mucus that is not cleared leads to infection and inflammation.

  3. How do reliever inhalers help during an asthma attack?
    • They relax smooth muscle to widen the bronchioles
    • They kill bacteria in the lungs
    • They increase mucus production
    • They strengthen cartilage rings
    Show the answer

    Answer: They relax smooth muscle to widen the bronchioles. Bronchodilators reverse airway narrowing.

Key terms

Alveolus
A tiny air sac where gas exchange occurs
Cilia
Hair-like structures that move mucus
COPD
Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease
