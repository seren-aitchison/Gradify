Microscopy

Temporary slides use thin specimens, stains and careful coverslip placement; microscopes are focused from low power with coarse then fine focus, and graticules are calibrated with a stage micrometer to measure cells.

Biology · Experimental Biology and Research Methods · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Prepare temporary slides and use stains
  • Use a light microscope correctly
  • Measure cells using an eyepiece graticule and stage micrometer

Slide preparation

  • Thin specimens let light pass through.
  • Temporary mounts: place the specimen in water or stain and lower a coverslip at an angle to avoid air bubbles.
  • Stains improve contrast, such as iodine for starch and methylene blue for animal cells.
  • Permanent slides are fixed, sectioned and stained.

Using and calibrating

  • Start with the lowest-power objective and focus with the coarse focus.
  • Switch to higher power and use only the fine focus.
  • An eyepiece graticule is calibrated against a stage micrometer for each objective.
  • Actual size = number of graticule units × value of one unit.

Calibration is needed because the graticule's divisions represent different real lengths at different magnifications. Biological drawings should use clear lines, no shading, labelled structures and a scale. Fluorescence and confocal microscopy extend light microscopy using fluorescent labels.

Worked example

Calibrating a graticule.

  1. At ×400, 25 graticule divisions line up with 0.1 mm (100 µm) on the stage micrometer.
  2. One division = 100 ÷ 25 = 4 µm.
  3. A cell spans 12 graticule divisions.
  4. Its length is 12 × 4 = 48 µm.
Common mistakeUsing the same graticule value at every magnification. It must be recalibrated for each objective.

In 3 lines: Thin, stained specimens. Low power first. Calibrate to measure.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which focus knob should be used at high power?
    • The coarse focus only
    • Both at full speed
    • Neither knob
    • The fine focus only
    Show the answer

    Answer: The fine focus only. Coarse focus at high power can crash into the slide.

  2. Why must a graticule be calibrated for each objective?
    • Each division represents a different real length at each magnification
    • Graticules change their size between each use
    • Stage micrometers vary randomly
    • It is only needed once ever
    Show the answer

    Answer: Each division represents a different real length at each magnification. Magnification changes what one division measures.

  3. Why should biological drawings avoid shading?
    • Shading makes drawings far too accurate
    • Shading obscures structures and is not scientifically precise
    • Examiners require colour instead
    • Shading shows scale
    Show the answer

    Answer: Shading obscures structures and is not scientifically precise. Clear lines communicate structures unambiguously.

Key terms

Eyepiece graticule
A scale in the eyepiece for measuring
Stage micrometer
A slide with a precise scale for calibration
Temporary mount
A slide prepared for immediate viewing
