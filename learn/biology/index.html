Biology explained

Foundations of Biology

  • Levels of Biological Organisation Living things are organised in a hierarchy, from atoms and molecules through cells, tissues, organs and organisms to populations, communities, ecosystems and the biosphere.
  • Classification and Taxonomy With an estimated several million species alive today, biologists need a system to name and organise them.
  • The Kingdoms of Life Within the domain Eukarya, organisms are traditionally divided into kingdoms: animals, plants, fungi and protists.
  • Scientific Method Science is a way of finding out about the natural world by testing ideas against evidence.
  • Hypotheses and Predictions A good investigation starts with a clear hypothesis and a precise prediction.
  • Variables and Experimental Design A fair experiment changes one factor, measures its effect on another and keeps everything else the same.
  • Controls and Reproducibility Controls show that a result is caused by the factor being tested and not by something else.
  • Accuracy and Precision Accuracy is how close a measurement is to the true value; precision is how close repeated measurements are to each other.
  • Reliability and Validity A result is reliable if it can be obtained consistently, and valid if it actually measures what it claims to and answers the question asked.
  • Biological Measurements Biologists measure things across an enormous range of scales, from nanometre-sized molecules to whole ecosystems.
  • Units and Conversions Science uses the International System of Units (SI) so that measurements can be compared worldwide.
  • Scientific Notation Biology deals with numbers from the 10⁻¹⁰ m of an atom to the 10¹³ cells of a human body.
  • Graphs and Data Presentation Choosing the right table or graph makes patterns in data clear and honest.
  • Statistical Foundations Living things vary, so biologists need statistics to summarise data and decide whether differences are real or due to chance.
  • Sampling Methods Biologists can rarely measure every individual, so they study samples.

Cell Biology

  • Cell Theory Cell theory says that every living thing is made of cells, that the cell is the basic unit of life, and that new cells come only from existing cells.
  • Prokaryotic Cells Prokaryotes, the bacteria and archaea, are cells without a nucleus or membrane-bound organelles.
  • Animal Cells Animal cells are eukaryotic cells without a cell wall or chloroplasts.
  • Plant Cells Plant cells share the eukaryotic plan with animal cells but add three features: a cellulose wall, chloroplasts and a large permanent vacuole.
  • Fungal Cells Fungi, including yeasts, moulds and mushrooms, are eukaryotes with cell walls made of chitin rather than cellulose.
  • Bacterial Cells Bacteria are the most familiar prokaryotes.
  • Cell Organelles Organelles are the specialised compartments and structures inside eukaryotic cells.
  • Endoplasmic Reticulum The endoplasmic reticulum is a network of membranes spreading through the cytoplasm from the nuclear envelope.
  • Golgi Apparatus The Golgi apparatus is the cell's processing and dispatch centre.
  • Membrane Structure Membranes are built from a double layer of phospholipids with proteins floating in it, a structure described by the fluid mosaic model.
  • Membrane Transport Substances cross membranes in several ways, which differ in whether they need energy and whether they need a protein.
  • Diffusion Diffusion is the net movement of particles from where they are more concentrated to where they are less concentrated, driven by their random motion.
  • Osmosis Osmosis is the diffusion of water across a partially permeable membrane.
  • Facilitated Diffusion Facilitated diffusion is passive movement down a concentration gradient through specific membrane proteins.
  • Active Transport Active transport moves substances against their concentration gradient using energy, usually from ATP.
  • Endocytosis and Exocytosis Some cargo is far too large for channels or carriers.
  • Stem Cells Stem cells are unspecialised cells that can divide to renew themselves and can also give rise to specialised cells.
  • Cell Signalling Cells constantly send and receive chemical signals that coordinate growth, metabolism and defence.
  • Cell Junctions In tissues, cells are physically joined to their neighbours.
  • Cellular Organisation Multicellular organisms are organised in levels: specialised cells form tissues, tissues form organs, and organs work together in organ systems.

Biochemistry and Biomolecules

  • Chemistry of Life Living things are built from a small set of elements arranged into large, complex molecules.
  • Water and Biological Systems Water makes up most of the mass of cells, and its unusual properties make life possible.
  • Hydrogen Bonding Hydrogen bonds are weak attractions, yet they shape water, proteins and DNA.
  • PH and Biological Buffers The pH of body fluids and cells must stay within narrow limits.
  • Carbohydrates Carbohydrates provide energy, store energy and build structures.
  • Monosaccharides Monosaccharides are the building blocks of all carbohydrates.
  • Disaccharides Disaccharides form when two monosaccharides join by a glycosidic bond.
  • Polysaccharides Polysaccharides are long chains of sugar units used for storage and structure.
  • Lipids Lipids are a diverse group of molecules that are insoluble in water.
  • Fatty Acids Fatty acids are the main components of triglycerides and phospholipids.
  • Triglycerides Triglycerides are the main form of stored fat in plants and animals.
  • Phospholipids Phospholipids form the basic structure of all cell membranes.
  • Steroids Steroids are lipids with a ring structure rather than fatty acid chains.
  • Proteins Proteins carry out most of the work in cells, from catalysis to transport and structure.
  • Amino Acids Amino acids are the monomers of proteins.
  • Peptide Bonds Amino acids join through peptide bonds to form polypeptides.
  • Protein Primary Structure The primary structure is the sequence of amino acids in a polypeptide.
  • Protein Secondary Structure Secondary structure is the regular folding of the polypeptide backbone into helices and sheets.
  • Protein Tertiary Structure Tertiary structure is the overall 3D shape of a single polypeptide.
  • Protein Quaternary Structure Some proteins are made of more than one polypeptide chain.
  • Protein Folding Newly made polypeptides must fold into precise shapes to function.
  • Protein Denaturation Denaturation is the loss of a protein's 3D shape and function.
  • Enzymes Enzymes are biological catalysts that speed up reactions in living cells.
  • Enzyme-substrate Interactions Enzymes bind their substrates with great specificity.
  • Enzyme Kinetics Enzyme kinetics describes how reaction rate depends on substrate concentration.
  • Enzyme Inhibition Inhibitors reduce enzyme activity and are important in metabolic control and medicine.
  • Factors Affecting Enzyme Activity Enzyme activity depends on temperature, pH, substrate concentration and enzyme concentration.
  • Nucleic Acids Nucleic acids store and transmit genetic information.
  • DNA and RNA DNA and RNA are both nucleic acids but differ in structure and role.
  • ATP and Biological Energy ATP is the main energy currency of cells.

Genetics and Molecular Biology

  • Introduction to Genetics Genetics is the study of genes, heredity and variation.
  • DNA Structure The structure of DNA explains how it stores and copies genetic information.
  • DNA Replication Before a cell divides, its DNA must be copied accurately.
  • DNA Repair DNA is constantly damaged and must be repaired to keep genetic information intact.
  • Chromosomes Chromosomes package DNA so that it fits in cells and can be divided accurately.
  • Genomes A genome is all the genetic material of an organism.
  • RNA Structure RNA molecules fold into shapes that suit their roles in gene expression.
  • Transcription Transcription copies the information in a gene into RNA.
  • RNA Processing In eukaryotes, pre-mRNA must be processed before it can be translated.
  • Translation Translation converts the code in mRNA into a sequence of amino acids.
  • Genetic Code The genetic code is the set of rules linking codons to amino acids.
  • Protein Synthesis Protein synthesis brings together transcription, processing and translation.
  • Gene Regulation Cells control which genes are expressed, when and how much.
  • Operons In bacteria, genes for related functions are often grouped in operons and controlled together.
  • Epigenetics Epigenetic changes alter gene expression without changing the DNA sequence.
  • Mutations Mutations are changes in DNA sequence.
  • Types of Mutation Gene mutations take several forms, with very different consequences.
  • DNA Damage DNA is damaged thousands of times a day in every cell.
  • Mendelian Genetics Gregor Mendel's experiments with pea plants revealed the basic rules of inheritance.
  • Dominant and Recessive Inheritance Some alleles mask others in heterozygotes.
  • Genotype and Phenotype Genotype describes the alleles an organism has, while phenotype describes its characteristics.
  • Monohybrid Crosses A monohybrid cross follows the inheritance of one gene.
  • Dihybrid Crosses A dihybrid cross follows two genes at once.
  • Incomplete Dominance In incomplete dominance, heterozygotes show an intermediate phenotype.
  • Codominance In codominance, both alleles in a heterozygote are fully expressed.
  • Sex-linked Inheritance Genes on the sex chromosomes are inherited differently in males and females.
  • Pedigree Analysis Pedigrees show how a trait is passed through a family.
  • Population Genetics Population genetics studies how allele frequencies vary and change in populations.
  • Genetic Linkage Genes close together on the same chromosome tend to be inherited together.
  • Recombination Recombination creates new combinations of alleles.
  • Chromosomal Abnormalities Changes in chromosome number or structure can have major effects on development.
  • Genomics Genomics studies whole genomes, using sequencing and computing.
  • Human Genetic Variation Humans differ from one another at millions of positions in the genome.
  • Genome-wide Association Studies Genome-wide association studies scan the genome for variants linked to traits and diseases.

Cell Division and Development

  • Cell Cycle The cell cycle is the sequence of events by which a cell grows and divides.
  • Interphase Interphase is the longest part of the cell cycle and prepares the cell for division.
  • Mitosis Mitosis divides the nucleus to produce two genetically identical nuclei.
  • Meiosis Meiosis is the division that produces gametes with half the chromosome number.
  • Comparison of Mitosis and Meiosis Mitosis and meiosis are both forms of nuclear division but have different outcomes and roles.
  • Chromosome Segregation Accurate chromosome segregation ensures each daughter cell receives the correct chromosomes.
  • Cell-cycle Regulation The cell cycle is controlled by proteins that drive cells from one phase to the next.
  • Checkpoints Checkpoints are control points where the cell checks whether conditions are right to continue the cycle.
  • Apoptosis Apoptosis is programmed cell death, a controlled process that removes unwanted cells.
  • Necrosis Necrosis is uncontrolled cell death caused by injury or damage.
  • Cellular Senescence Senescent cells stop dividing permanently but remain alive.
  • Cancer Biology Cancer is uncontrolled cell division caused by changes in genes that regulate the cell cycle.
  • Tumour Development Tumours develop in stages from a single altered cell to an invasive cancer.
  • Stem-cell Biology Stem cells can divide to renew themselves and differentiate into specialised cells.
  • Embryonic Development Embryonic development turns a single cell into a complex organism.
  • Fertilisation Fertilisation is the fusion of a sperm and egg to form a zygote.
  • Cleavage Cleavage is the series of rapid cell divisions that follows fertilisation.
  • Gastrulation Gastrulation reorganises the embryo into germ layers, setting up the body plan.
  • Organogenesis Organogenesis is the formation of organs from the germ layers.
  • Developmental Signalling Cells in an embryo communicate using signalling molecules that control their fates.
  • Cell Fate Determination During development, cells become committed to particular fates.
  • Tissue Formation Tissues form as cells of similar types organise and adhere together.
  • Regeneration Some animals can regrow lost body parts, while others, including humans, have limited regeneration.

Evolution and Natural Selection

  • Origins of Evolutionary Thought Ideas about how species change developed over centuries before and after Darwin.
  • Evidence for Evolution Evolution is supported by many independent lines of evidence.
  • Natural Selection Natural selection is the main mechanism of adaptive evolution.
  • Adaptation Adaptations are inherited features that improve survival and reproduction in a particular environment.
  • Genetic Variation Genetic variation is the raw material for evolution.
  • Mutation and Evolution Mutations provide the new genetic variation on which evolution depends.
  • Genetic Drift Genetic drift is change in allele frequencies due to chance.
  • Gene Flow Gene flow is the movement of alleles between populations.
  • Sexual Selection Sexual selection favours traits that improve mating success.
  • Fitness In evolutionary biology, fitness means reproductive success, not physical strength.
  • Speciation Speciation is the formation of new species.
  • Reproductive Isolation Reproductive isolation keeps species separate.
  • Allopatric Speciation Allopatric speciation occurs when populations are geographically separated.
  • Sympatric Speciation Sympatric speciation occurs without geographic separation.
  • Phylogenetics Phylogenetics reconstructs the evolutionary relationships between organisms.
  • Evolutionary Trees Evolutionary trees show relationships between groups of organisms.
  • Common Ancestry All living organisms share a common ancestor.
  • Fossil Evidence Fossils provide direct evidence of past life and evolutionary change.
  • Molecular Evolution Molecular evolution studies how DNA and protein sequences change over time.
  • Human Evolution Human evolution traces the hominin lineage from our common ancestor with chimpanzees to modern humans.
  • Evolutionary Medicine Evolutionary medicine applies evolutionary thinking to health and disease.
  • Evolution of Resistance Resistance to antibiotics, pesticides and other treatments evolves by natural selection.
  • Population Genetics Population genetics provides the mathematical framework for evolution.

Human Anatomy and Physiology

  • Organisation of the Human Body The human body is organised from cells into tissues, organs and organ systems.
  • Homeostasis Homeostasis keeps the internal environment stable.
  • Nervous System The nervous system provides rapid communication throughout the body.
  • Neurones Neurones are cells specialised to transmit electrical impulses.
  • Action Potentials An action potential is a brief electrical impulse along a neurone.
  • Synaptic Transmission Synapses pass signals between neurones or to effectors.
  • Central Nervous System The central nervous system integrates information and controls the body.
  • Peripheral Nervous System The peripheral nervous system connects the CNS to the rest of the body.
  • Hormones and Signalling Hormones signal through receptors on or inside target cells.
  • Cardiovascular System The cardiovascular system transports substances around the body.
  • Heart Anatomy The heart is a muscular double pump.
  • Cardiac Cycle The cardiac cycle is the sequence of events in one heartbeat.
  • Blood Vessels Arteries, capillaries and veins have structures suited to their functions.
  • Blood Composition Blood is a tissue made of cells suspended in plasma.
  • Circulation Humans have a double circulation with pulmonary and systemic circuits.
  • Respiratory System The respiratory system takes in oxygen and removes carbon dioxide.
  • Ventilation Ventilation moves air into and out of the lungs.
  • Oxygen Transport Haemoglobin carries oxygen from the lungs to the tissues.
  • Digestive System The digestive system breaks down food and absorbs nutrients.
  • Digestion and Absorption Food molecules are digested into small units and absorbed in the small intestine.
  • Liver and Pancreas The liver and pancreas have essential digestive and metabolic roles.
  • Renal System The renal system removes wastes and controls water and salt balance.
  • Kidney Function The nephron filters blood and adjusts the composition of urine.
  • Urine Formation Urine composition reflects how the kidneys regulate the body.
  • Musculoskeletal System The musculoskeletal system supports the body and allows movement.
  • Muscle Contraction Skeletal muscle contracts by the sliding of protein filaments.
  • Reproductive System The reproductive systems produce gametes and allow fertilisation.
  • Human Development Human development continues from fertilisation through birth, childhood and ageing.
  • Skin and Thermoregulation The skin protects the body and helps control temperature.

Plant Biology

  • Plant Cell Structure Plant cells share features with animal cells but have distinctive structures.
  • Plant Tissues Plants are built from a few main tissue types.
  • Roots and Root Systems Roots anchor plants and absorb water and minerals.
  • Stems Stems support plants and transport materials between roots and leaves.
  • Leaves Leaves are the main organs of photosynthesis.
  • Plant Vascular Tissue Vascular tissue transports water, minerals and sugars through plants.
  • Xylem Xylem is specialised for transporting water and providing support.
  • Phloem Phloem transports sugars and other organic substances.
  • Water Transport Water moves from soil to leaves through roots and xylem.
  • Photosynthesis Photosynthesis converts light energy into chemical energy in sugars.
  • Light-dependent Reactions The light-dependent reactions capture light energy to make ATP and NADPH.
  • Calvin Cycle The Calvin cycle uses ATP and NADPH to fix carbon dioxide into sugars.
  • C3 Photosynthesis Most plants use C3 photosynthesis, named after its first product.
  • C4 Photosynthesis C4 plants concentrate CO₂ around RuBisCO to avoid photorespiration.
  • CAM Photosynthesis CAM plants separate CO₂ uptake and the Calvin cycle in time.
  • Cellular Respiration in Plants Plants respire as well as photosynthesise.
  • Auxins Auxins control cell elongation and many growth responses.
  • Abscisic Acid Abscisic acid helps plants respond to stress and controls dormancy.
  • Ethylene Ethylene is a gaseous hormone that controls ripening and ageing.
  • Tropisms Tropisms are growth responses to directional stimuli.
  • Photoperiodism Photoperiodism is the response of plants to day length.
  • Flowering Flowers are the reproductive structures of flowering plants.
  • Pollination Pollination is the transfer of pollen to a stigma.
  • Fertilisation In flowering plants, fertilisation follows pollination.
  • Seed Formation Seeds contain an embryo and food store protected by a coat.
  • Plant Biotechnology Plant biotechnology uses scientific techniques to improve and propagate plants.

Microbiology

  • Introduction to Microbiology Microbiology is the study of microorganisms.
  • Bacterial Structure Bacteria have a simple but highly effective cell structure.
  • Bacterial Growth Bacteria multiply by binary fission and can grow very rapidly.
  • Horizontal Gene Transfer Bacteria can share genes without reproducing.
  • Viral Structure Viruses are tiny infectious particles with a simple structure.
  • Viral Replication Viruses hijack host cells to make copies of themselves.
  • Viral Evolution Viruses evolve rapidly, producing new strains and variants.
  • Fungi Fungi are eukaryotic organisms including yeasts, moulds and mushrooms.
  • Microbiomes A microbiome is the community of microbes living in a particular environment, including the human body.
  • Virulence Virulence is the degree of harm a pathogen causes.
  • Transmission Pathogens spread between hosts by several routes.
  • Antibiotics Antibiotics kill or inhibit bacteria by targeting structures they need.
  • Antimicrobial Resistance Antimicrobial resistance is a growing global health threat.
  • Microbial Culture Microbes are grown in the laboratory to study and identify them.
  • Microscopy Microscopes allow us to see microbes and their structures.
  • Molecular Identification Molecular methods identify microbes quickly and accurately from their genes or proteins.
  • Environmental Microbiology Environmental microbiology studies microbes in soil, water and air.
  • Industrial Microbiology Industrial microbiology uses microbes to make products on a large scale.

Immunology

  • Introduction to Immunology Immunology is the study of how the body defends itself.
  • Adaptive Immunity Adaptive immunity is specific and improves with exposure.
  • Natural Killer Cells Natural killer cells destroy infected and cancerous cells without prior sensitisation.
  • B Lymphocytes B lymphocytes produce antibodies.
  • T Lymphocytes T lymphocytes coordinate immune responses and kill infected cells.
  • Antigen Recognition Lymphocytes recognise a vast range of antigens.
  • Antigen Presentation Antigen presentation displays fragments of proteins to T cells.
  • MHC Molecules MHC molecules display peptides to T cells and vary greatly between people.
  • Complement System The complement system is a group of blood proteins that help destroy pathogens.
  • Inflammation Inflammation is the body's response to infection and injury.
  • Immune Memory Immune memory allows faster and stronger responses to pathogens met before.
  • Vaccination Vaccination trains the immune system safely to prevent disease.
  • Hypersensitivity Hypersensitivity reactions are harmful immune responses.
  • Allergy Allergies are type I hypersensitivity reactions to harmless substances.
  • Cancer Immunology The immune system can recognise and destroy cancer cells, but tumours can evade it.
  • Immunotherapy Immunotherapy uses the immune system to treat disease, especially cancer.

Ecology and Environmental Biology

  • Habitats and Niches Every species has a habitat and a niche.
  • Food Webs Food webs show the many interlinked feeding relationships in an ecosystem.
  • Energy Flow Energy flows through ecosystems in one direction and is lost at each step.
  • Trophic Levels Trophic levels describe an organism's position in a food chain.
  • Nutrient Cycles Nutrients cycle between living organisms and the environment.
  • Carbon Cycle Carbon cycles between the atmosphere, living organisms, oceans and rocks.
  • Nitrogen Cycle Nitrogen cycles between the atmosphere, soil and organisms through processes mainly carried out by bacteria.
  • Water Cycle Water moves continuously between the oceans, atmosphere and land.
  • Population Dynamics Populations change in size over time.
  • Succession Succession is the change in a community over time.
  • Species Richness Species richness and diversity indices measure biodiversity.
  • Conservation Biology Conservation biology aims to protect biodiversity.
  • Habitat Fragmentation Habitat fragmentation breaks habitats into small, isolated patches.
  • Climate Change and Ecosystems Climate change is altering ecosystems worldwide.
  • Conservation Strategies Conservation uses in-situ and ex-situ strategies to protect species.
  • Sustainable Ecosystems Sustainability means meeting present needs without harming future generations.

Biotechnology and Genetic Engineering

  • Recombinant DNA Recombinant DNA combines DNA from different sources.
  • Restriction Enzymes Restriction enzymes cut DNA at specific sequences.
  • DNA Ligase DNA ligase seals breaks in DNA by joining fragments.
  • Plasmids Plasmids are small circular DNA molecules widely used as vectors.
  • Cloning Vectors Different vectors carry different sizes of DNA into different hosts.
  • Polymerase Chain Reaction PCR amplifies a specific DNA sequence into millions of copies.
  • Gel Electrophoresis Gel electrophoresis separates DNA fragments by size.
  • Sanger Sequencing Sanger sequencing uses chain-terminating nucleotides to read DNA.
  • Next-generation Sequencing Next-generation sequencing reads millions of DNA fragments at once.
  • Whole-genome Sequencing Whole-genome sequencing reads an organism's entire DNA.
  • CRISPR-Cas Systems CRISPR-Cas systems are bacterial immune defences that have been adapted as gene-editing tools.
  • Gene Editing Gene editing makes targeted changes to DNA.
  • Recombinant Proteins Recombinant proteins are made from cloned genes in host cells.
  • Genetic Testing Genetic testing identifies changes in genes, chromosomes or proteins.
  • Gene Therapy Gene therapy treats disease by adding, replacing or editing genes.
  • Cell Therapy Cell therapy uses living cells as treatments.
  • Industrial Biotechnology Industrial biotechnology uses enzymes and microbes to make products more sustainably.
  • Synthetic Biology Synthetic biology applies engineering ideas to biology to design useful biological systems.
  • Biosafety Biosafety protects people and the environment when working with living organisms.

Bioinformatics and Computational Biology

  • DNA Sequence Analysis Computers analyse DNA sequences to find genes and features.
  • BLAST BLAST searches databases for sequences similar to a query.
  • Phylogenetic Analysis Phylogenetic analysis builds trees from sequence data.
  • Differential Expression Differential expression analysis finds genes whose expression changes between conditions.

Experimental Biology and Research Methods

  • Experimental Design Good experimental design produces reliable answers to biological questions.
  • Hypothesis Development A hypothesis is a testable explanation that guides research.
  • Controls Controls make sure that results are caused by the variable being tested.
  • Microscopy Microscopy is a core laboratory skill in biology.
  • Cell Culture Cell culture grows cells outside the body under controlled conditions.
  • PCR This lesson focuses on the practical side of PCR: designing primers, setting up reactions and troubleshooting.
  • Gel Electrophoresis This lesson focuses on the practical skills of running and analysing agarose gels.
  • Western Blotting Western blotting detects specific proteins in a mixture.
  • ELISA This lesson focuses on running and analysing ELISAs in the laboratory, including standard curves.
  • Chromatography Chromatography separates mixtures based on how components interact with two phases.
  • Spectrophotometry Spectrophotometry measures how much light a solution absorbs.
  • Flow Cytometry Flow cytometry measures properties of thousands of individual cells per second.
  • Statistical Analysis Statistics help decide whether results reflect real effects or chance.
  • Laboratory Safety Laboratory safety protects people from chemical, biological and physical hazards.
