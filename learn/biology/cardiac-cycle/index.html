Cardiac Cycle

The cardiac cycle includes atrial systole, ventricular systole and diastole, with valves controlled by pressure; the SAN initiates each beat, the AVN delays it and the bundle of His and Purkinje fibres spread it, and cardiac output equals heart rate times stroke volume.

Biology · Human Anatomy and Physiology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the stages of the cardiac cycle
  • Explain how pressure changes open and close valves
  • Describe the electrical control of the heartbeat

Stages

  • Atrial systole: atria contract, pushing blood into the ventricles.
  • Ventricular systole: ventricles contract; AV valves close and semilunar valves open.
  • Diastole: the heart relaxes and fills with blood.
  • Valves open and close due to pressure differences.
  • At 75 beats per minute, one cycle lasts about 0.8 s.

Electrical control

  • The sinoatrial node (SAN), the pacemaker, starts each beat.
  • The impulse spreads across the atria, then reaches the atrioventricular node (AVN), which delays it.
  • It passes down the bundle of His and Purkinje fibres to the ventricle walls.
  • Ventricles contract from the apex upwards, pushing blood into the arteries.

Cardiac output = heart rate × stroke volume. At rest it is about 5 L per minute. An electrocardiogram (ECG) records the heart's electrical activity: the P wave shows atrial depolarisation, the QRS complex ventricular depolarisation and the T wave ventricular repolarisation. Abnormal rhythms, such as atrial fibrillation, can be detected on an ECG.

Worked example

Calculating cardiac output.

  1. A person has a heart rate of 70 beats per minute.
  2. Their stroke volume is 70 mL.
  3. Cardiac output = 70 × 70 = 4,900 mL per minute.
  4. This is about 4.9 L per minute.
Common mistakeThinking valves are opened by muscles. Heart valves open and close passively due to pressure differences.

In 3 lines: Atrial systole, ventricular systole, diastole. SAN, AVN, Purkinje fibres. Cardiac output = HR × SV.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What happens during ventricular systole?
    • The atria contract and fill the ventricles
    • The whole heart relaxes and fills
    • The ventricles contract and pump blood into arteries
    • The valves stay closed throughout
    Show the answer

    Answer: The ventricles contract and pump blood into arteries. Ventricular contraction ejects blood into the aorta and pulmonary artery.

  2. Why does the AVN delay the impulse?
    • To stop the heart beating
    • To make the atria contract twice
    • To speed up ventricular contraction
    • So the atria finish emptying before the ventricles contract
    Show the answer

    Answer: So the atria finish emptying before the ventricles contract. The delay allows ventricles to fill fully.

  3. What causes the atrioventricular valves to close?
    • Ventricular pressure rising above atrial pressure
    • Atrial pressure rising above ventricular pressure
    • Nerves pulling the valves shut
    • The SAN directly closing them
    Show the answer

    Answer: Ventricular pressure rising above atrial pressure. Pressure differences passively shut the valves.
