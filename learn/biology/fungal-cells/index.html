Fungal Cells

Fungal cells are eukaryotic with chitin walls and no chloroplasts; fungi feed by external digestion and absorption.

Biology · Cell Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the structure of fungal cells and hyphae
  • Explain how fungi obtain their nutrients
  • Compare fungal cells with plant and animal cells

Structure

Fungal cells have a nucleus, mitochondria, ER, Golgi and vacuoles, like other eukaryotes. Their walls are made mainly of chitin, the same tough polysaccharide found in insect exoskeletons, with glucans. They have no chloroplasts.

Moulds and mushrooms grow as hyphae, threads a few micrometres wide that branch into a network called a mycelium. In many fungi the hyphae are divided by cross-walls (septa) with pores; in others the hyphae are one long cytoplasm with many nuclei. Yeasts grow as single oval cells that reproduce by budding.

Feeding by absorption

Fungi are heterotrophs that secrete digestive enzymes into their surroundings and absorb the small molecules released. A mycelium has an enormous surface area for absorption. Many fungi are decomposers that recycle carbon and nutrients from dead matter; others are parasites or form partnerships with plant roots (mycorrhizae).

Fungal cells and medicine

Because fungal cells are eukaryotic, like ours, antifungal drugs are harder to design than antibiotics. Useful targets are the features we lack: fungal membranes contain ergosterol instead of cholesterol, which azole drugs stop the fungus making and amphotericin binds to, and echinocandins block the synthesis of the wall's glucans.

Common mistakeGrouping fungi with plants. Fungi don't photosynthesise, have chitin rather than cellulose walls, and are more closely related to animals than to plants.

In 3 lines: Fungi have chitin walls, no chloroplasts, and often grow as hyphae forming a mycelium. They digest food externally and absorb the products. Fungi are more closely related to animals than to plants.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which feature distinguishes fungal cells from plant cells?
    • A nucleus
    • Chitin cell walls
    • Mitochondria
    • A plasma membrane
    Show the answer

    Answer: Chitin cell walls. Both are eukaryotic with nuclei, mitochondria and membranes; fungal walls are chitin, plant walls cellulose.

  2. Bread mould spreads over a slice of bread and the bread softens beneath it. What explains the softening?
    • The mould photosynthesises
    • The mould's chloroplasts break down starch
    • Enzymes secreted by the hyphae digest the bread outside the fungus
    • The bread absorbs water from the mould
    Show the answer

    Answer: Enzymes secreted by the hyphae digest the bread outside the fungus. Fungi secrete digestive enzymes and absorb the products, breaking down their food from the outside.

  3. Why does a mycelium suit a fungus's way of feeding?
    • It lets the fungus move quickly to new food
    • It traps sunlight for photosynthesis
    • It stores food as starch
    • Its branching threads give an enormous surface area for secreting enzymes and absorbing nutrients
    Show the answer

    Answer: Its branching threads give an enormous surface area for secreting enzymes and absorbing nutrients. External digestion and absorption both depend on contact with the food, so a large surface area of thin hyphae makes feeding efficient.

Key terms

Chitin
A tough nitrogen-containing polysaccharide in fungal cell walls and arthropod exoskeletons
Hypha
A thread-like filament that makes up the body of most fungi
Mycelium
The network of hyphae that forms a fungus
