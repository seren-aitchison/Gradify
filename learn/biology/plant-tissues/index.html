Plant Tissues

Plants have dermal, vascular and ground tissue systems; ground tissue includes parenchyma, collenchyma and sclerenchyma, and meristems allow lifelong primary and secondary growth.

Biology · Plant Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the three main tissue systems of plants
  • Describe the cell types of ground tissue
  • Explain the role of meristems in plant growth

Tissue systems

  • Dermal tissue: the epidermis, covered by a waxy cuticle, with stomata and root hairs.
  • Vascular tissue: xylem and phloem for transport.
  • Ground tissue: everything else, used for photosynthesis, storage and support.

Ground tissue and meristems

  • Parenchyma: thin-walled living cells for storage and photosynthesis.
  • Collenchyma: unevenly thickened living cells giving flexible support.
  • Sclerenchyma: thick, lignified, often dead cells giving rigid support.
  • Meristems contain dividing cells: apical meristems at shoot and root tips, and lateral meristems (cambium) for thickening.

Unlike animals, plants grow throughout their lives from meristems. Primary growth from apical meristems increases length, while secondary growth from the vascular cambium produces wood and increases girth. Meristem cells can produce all plant tissues, which allows cuttings and tissue culture to work.

Worked example

How trees thicken.

  1. A young tree stem has a ring of vascular cambium.
  2. The cambium divides to produce new xylem inwards and phloem outwards.
  3. Each year a new layer of xylem forms a growth ring.
  4. Counting rings can estimate the age of the tree.
Common mistakeThinking plants stop growing when they reach adult size. Meristems allow growth throughout life.

In 3 lines: Dermal, vascular, ground. Parenchyma, collenchyma, sclerenchyma. Meristems allow growth.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which ground tissue gives rigid support and is often dead?
    • Parenchyma
    • Collenchyma
    • Epidermis
    • Sclerenchyma
    Show the answer

    Answer: Sclerenchyma. Sclerenchyma cells have thick lignified walls for rigid support.

  2. What does the vascular cambium produce?
    • New xylem and phloem, increasing stem girth
    • New leaves at the shoot tip
    • Root hairs on the epidermis
    • The waxy cuticle on leaves
    Show the answer

    Answer: New xylem and phloem, increasing stem girth. Cambium is a lateral meristem responsible for secondary growth.

  3. Why can plants be grown from cuttings?
    • Cuttings contain no living cells
    • Meristem cells can produce all plant tissues
    • Plants cannot regenerate tissues
    • Only seeds can produce new plants
    Show the answer

    Answer: Meristem cells can produce all plant tissues. Meristematic cells can regenerate roots and shoots from cuttings.

Key terms

Meristem
A region of dividing cells in a plant
Parenchyma
Thin-walled living ground tissue cells
Vascular cambium
A lateral meristem producing secondary xylem and phloem
