Oxygen Transport

Haemoglobin carries four O₂ molecules with cooperative binding, giving a sigmoid curve; it loads oxygen in the lungs and unloads in tissues, with the Bohr effect increasing unloading in high CO₂ and fetal haemoglobin having higher affinity.

Biology · Human Anatomy and Physiology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe how haemoglobin carries oxygen
  • Interpret the oxygen dissociation curve
  • Explain the Bohr effect and fetal haemoglobin

Haemoglobin

  • Each haemoglobin molecule has four subunits, each with a haem group containing iron.
  • One haemoglobin can carry four O₂ molecules, forming oxyhaemoglobin.
  • Cooperative binding: binding one O₂ makes the next bind more easily.
  • This gives an S-shaped (sigmoid) dissociation curve.

Unloading oxygen

  • In the lungs, high partial pressure of O₂ leads to near-full saturation.
  • In tissues, low partial pressure causes oxygen release.
  • Bohr effect: high CO₂ (lower pH) shifts the curve right, so haemoglobin releases more oxygen.
  • Fetal haemoglobin has a higher affinity, curve shifted left, taking oxygen from the mother's blood.

Most CO₂ is carried as hydrogencarbonate ions in plasma, formed in red cells by carbonic anhydrase. Carbon monoxide binds haemoglobin about 200 times more strongly than oxygen, reducing oxygen transport, which is why it is so dangerous. At high altitude, people produce more red cells over time to compensate for lower oxygen.

Worked example

The Bohr effect in exercise.

  1. Active muscle produces more CO₂.
  2. CO₂ forms carbonic acid, lowering pH.
  3. The dissociation curve shifts to the right.
  4. Haemoglobin unloads more oxygen where it is needed most.
Common mistakeThinking a curve shifted right means more oxygen is carried. It means lower affinity, so more oxygen is released.

In 3 lines: Four O₂ per haemoglobin. Sigmoid curve. Bohr effect and fetal haemoglobin.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Why is the oxygen dissociation curve S-shaped?
    • Each subunit binds oxygen independently
    • Oxygen is carried only in plasma
    • Cooperative binding of oxygen to haemoglobin
    • Haemoglobin has one binding site
    Show the answer

    Answer: Cooperative binding of oxygen to haemoglobin. Binding of each O₂ makes the next easier.

  2. What does the Bohr effect cause in actively respiring tissue?
    • Less oxygen is released from haemoglobin
    • Haemoglobin binds more carbon dioxide only
    • Oxygen binding stops in the lungs
    • More oxygen is released from haemoglobin
    Show the answer

    Answer: More oxygen is released from haemoglobin. High CO₂ lowers affinity and increases unloading.

  3. Why does fetal haemoglobin have a higher affinity for oxygen?
    • So it can take oxygen from the mother's haemoglobin
    • So it releases oxygen in the lungs
    • Because the fetus breathes air
    • So it binds carbon monoxide instead
    Show the answer

    Answer: So it can take oxygen from the mother's haemoglobin. Higher affinity allows oxygen transfer across the placenta.

Key terms

Oxyhaemoglobin
Haemoglobin combined with oxygen
Bohr effect
Lower oxygen affinity at higher CO₂
Cooperative binding
Binding of one oxygen increasing affinity for the next
