Hormones and Signalling

Peptide hormones bind surface receptors and act through second messengers such as cAMP with signal amplification, while steroid hormones enter cells and bind intracellular receptors that regulate transcription, producing slower, longer-lasting effects.

Biology · Human Anatomy and Physiology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Distinguish peptide and steroid hormones
  • Explain how peptide hormones act via second messengers
  • Explain how steroid hormones affect gene expression

Types of hormone

  • Peptide hormones (such as insulin and ADH) are water-soluble and cannot cross the membrane.
  • Steroid hormones (such as cortisol, oestrogen and testosterone) are lipid-soluble and enter cells.
  • Amine hormones (such as adrenaline and thyroxine) are derived from amino acids.
  • Thyroxine, though an amine, acts on intracellular receptors like steroids.

Mechanisms

  • Peptide hormones bind cell-surface receptors, triggering second messengers such as cyclic AMP.
  • Second messengers activate enzyme cascades, amplifying the signal.
  • Steroid hormones bind intracellular receptors; the complex acts as a transcription factor.
  • Steroid effects are slower but longer-lasting, as new proteins are made.

Adrenaline binding to liver cells activates adenylyl cyclase, increasing cAMP, which activates protein kinase A and leads to glycogen breakdown. Each step amplifies the signal, so a few hormone molecules produce many glucose molecules. Endocrine signalling is one form of chemical signalling; paracrine signals act on nearby cells and autocrine signals on the cell that released them.

Worked example

Adrenaline and glycogen.

  1. Adrenaline binds a receptor on a liver cell.
  2. Adenylyl cyclase converts ATP to cyclic AMP.
  3. cAMP activates protein kinase A, starting an enzyme cascade.
  4. Glycogen is broken down to glucose, raising blood glucose.
Common mistakeThinking all hormones enter their target cells. Peptide hormones act at the cell surface.

In 3 lines: Peptide versus steroid. Second messengers amplify. Steroids change transcription.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Why can steroid hormones enter cells directly?
    • They are large water-soluble proteins
    • They are lipid-soluble and cross the membrane
    • They use ion channels to enter
    • They are carried in by vesicles
    Show the answer

    Answer: They are lipid-soluble and cross the membrane. Lipid solubility lets them pass through the phospholipid bilayer.

  2. What is the effect of a hormone-receptor complex acting as a transcription factor?
    • It immediately opens ion channels
    • It breaks down glycogen directly
    • It changes which genes are expressed
    • It blocks all protein synthesis
    Show the answer

    Answer: It changes which genes are expressed. Steroid complexes regulate gene transcription.

  3. Why do second messenger cascades amplify signals?
    • Each step uses up the hormone
    • Second messengers block enzymes
    • Cascades reduce the number of molecules
    • Each activated enzyme activates many molecules at the next step
    Show the answer

    Answer: Each activated enzyme activates many molecules at the next step. Multiplication at each stage produces a large response.

Key terms

Second messenger
An intracellular molecule relaying a hormone's signal
Steroid hormone
A lipid-soluble hormone acting on intracellular receptors
Signal amplification
Increase of a signal at each step of a cascade
