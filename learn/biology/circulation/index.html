Circulation

Humans have a double circulation with pulmonary and systemic circuits allowing high systemic pressure; blood pressure is measured as systolic over diastolic, regulated by baroreceptors and adjusted by vasoconstriction and vasodilation, which redistributes blood during exercise.

Biology · Human Anatomy and Physiology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain the double circulatory system
  • Describe blood pressure and how it is measured
  • Explain how blood flow is redistributed during exercise

Double circulation

  • Pulmonary circulation: right heart to lungs and back to left heart.
  • Systemic circulation: left heart to body tissues and back to right heart.
  • Blood passes through the heart twice in each complete circuit.
  • This allows high pressure in the systemic circuit while protecting the delicate lungs.

Pressure and regulation

  • Blood pressure is measured as systolic over diastolic, such as 120/80 mmHg.
  • Hypertension is persistently high blood pressure, often defined as 140/90 mmHg or above in clinic.
  • Vasoconstriction and vasodilation of arterioles redirect blood.
  • During exercise, more blood flows to muscles and less to the gut.

Fish have a single circulation, where blood passes through the heart once, so pressure falls after the gills. The double circulation of mammals and birds supports high metabolic rates. Blood pressure is regulated by baroreceptors in the aorta and carotid arteries, which signal the brainstem to adjust heart rate and vessel diameter.

Worked example

Baroreceptor reflex.

  1. A person stands up quickly, and blood pools in the legs.
  2. Blood pressure falls, detected by baroreceptors in the carotid arteries.
  3. The brainstem increases heart rate and constricts vessels.
  4. Blood pressure rises back towards normal.
Common mistakeThinking a single circuit is as efficient as a double one. Pressure drops after the lungs or gills in a single circuit.

In 3 lines: Pulmonary and systemic circuits. Systolic over diastolic. Redistribution and baroreceptors.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. In a double circulation, how many times does blood pass through the heart in one complete circuit?
    • Once
    • Three times
    • Four times
    • Twice
    Show the answer

    Answer: Twice. Blood passes through the pulmonary and then the systemic circuit.

  2. How is blood redirected to muscles during exercise?
    • Arterioles to muscles dilate and those to the gut constrict
    • All arterioles constrict equally
    • The heart stops pumping to the gut
    • Veins carry blood to muscles first
    Show the answer

    Answer: Arterioles to muscles dilate and those to the gut constrict. Changing arteriole diameter redistributes flow.

  3. Why is a double circulation an advantage for mammals?
    • It means blood passes through the heart once
    • It allows high systemic pressure while protecting the lungs
    • It lowers blood pressure in the body
    • It removes the need for lungs
    Show the answer

    Answer: It allows high systemic pressure while protecting the lungs. Separate circuits support high metabolic demand.

Key terms

Pulmonary circulation
The circuit between heart and lungs
Systemic circulation
The circuit between heart and body tissues
Baroreceptor
A receptor detecting changes in blood pressure
