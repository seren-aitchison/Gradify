Protein Quaternary Structure

Quaternary structure is the arrangement of multiple subunits, as in haemoglobin with four subunits and haem prosthetic groups, collagen's triple helix and antibodies; subunit interactions allow cooperative binding.

Biology · Biochemistry and Biomolecules · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define quaternary structure
  • Describe the quaternary structure of haemoglobin and collagen
  • Explain the role of prosthetic groups in conjugated proteins

Quaternary structure

  • The arrangement of two or more polypeptide subunits in a protein.
  • Held by the same interactions as tertiary structure.
  • Not all proteins have quaternary structure.
  • Subunits can interact so that binding at one affects others (cooperativity).

Examples

  • Haemoglobin: four subunits (two α and two β), each with a haem group containing iron that binds oxygen.
  • Collagen: three polypeptides wound into a triple helix, with glycine every third residue allowing tight packing.
  • Antibodies: two heavy and two light chains joined by disulfide bridges.
  • Insulin has two chains joined by disulfide bridges.

Proteins with non-protein parts are conjugated proteins; the non-protein part is a prosthetic group, such as haem in haemoglobin. Haemoglobin's quaternary structure allows cooperative binding: once one oxygen binds, the shape changes so others bind more easily, giving its S-shaped dissociation curve. Collagen fibres are strengthened further by cross-links between molecules.

Worked example

Haemoglobin's design.

  1. Each of the four subunits holds a haem group.
  2. When one haem binds oxygen, the subunit's shape changes.
  3. This change makes it easier for the other subunits to bind oxygen.
  4. Haemoglobin loads oxygen efficiently in the lungs and releases it in tissues.
Common mistakeSaying every protein has quaternary structure. Only proteins made of more than one polypeptide do.

In 3 lines: More than one chain. Haemoglobin and collagen. Prosthetic groups and cooperativity.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which protein has a triple helix of three polypeptides?
    • Haemoglobin
    • Insulin
    • Collagen
    • Amylase
    Show the answer

    Answer: Collagen. Collagen's three chains wind into a strong triple helix.

  2. What does the iron-containing haem group in haemoglobin do?
    • It digests starch
    • It forms disulfide bridges
    • It stores glucose
    • It binds oxygen
    Show the answer

    Answer: It binds oxygen. Each haem group, with its iron ion, binds one oxygen molecule reversibly.

  3. Why does haemoglobin show cooperative oxygen binding?
    • Binding at one subunit changes shape and increases affinity at the others
    • Its subunits never interact
    • Oxygen binds to its peptide bonds
    • Haem groups repel oxygen
    Show the answer

    Answer: Binding at one subunit changes shape and increases affinity at the others. Interactions between subunits cause cooperativity.

Key terms

Quaternary structure
The arrangement of multiple polypeptide subunits
Prosthetic group
A non-protein part permanently attached to a protein
Cooperative binding
Binding at one site increasing binding at others
