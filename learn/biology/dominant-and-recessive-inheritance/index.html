Dominant and Recessive Inheritance

Dominant alleles are expressed in heterozygotes, recessive alleles only in homozygotes; autosomal recessive conditions such as cystic fibrosis involve unaffected carriers, autosomal dominant ones such as Huntington's disease usually appear in each generation, and dominance does not mean commonness.

Biology · Genetics and Molecular Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define dominant and recessive alleles
  • Describe autosomal dominant and recessive inheritance patterns
  • Explain carriers and the probability of inheriting recessive conditions

Dominance

  • A dominant allele is expressed in the phenotype when only one copy is present.
  • A recessive allele is expressed only when two copies are present (homozygous recessive).
  • Dominant alleles are written with a capital letter (A) and recessive with lower case (a).
  • Dominance describes the relationship between alleles, not how common they are.

Human conditions

  • Autosomal recessive: cystic fibrosis, sickle cell anaemia, phenylketonuria.
  • Heterozygous people are carriers, usually unaffected.
  • Autosomal dominant: Huntington's disease, achondroplasia.
  • An affected person with a dominant condition usually has at least one affected parent.

Recessive alleles often code for non-functional proteins: one working copy can produce enough protein, so heterozygotes are unaffected. Two carrier parents have a 1 in 4 chance of an affected child in each pregnancy. Dominant conditions can result from a toxic protein or from too little product from one working copy. A dominant allele can be rare, as with Huntington's disease.

Worked example

Two carriers of cystic fibrosis.

  1. Both parents are Ff.
  2. Each parent passes F or f with equal probability.
  3. Offspring: 1 FF : 2 Ff : 1 ff.
  4. Each child has a 1 in 4 chance of cystic fibrosis and a 1 in 2 chance of being a carrier.
Common mistakeAssuming dominant alleles are always the most common. Some dominant alleles, such as for Huntington's disease, are rare.

In 3 lines: Dominant expressed with one copy. Recessive needs two copies. Carriers pass on recessive alleles.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. When is a recessive allele expressed in the phenotype?
    • When only one copy is present
    • Only when the dominant allele is common
    • When two copies are present
    • Only in the cells of males
    Show the answer

    Answer: When two copies are present. Recessive alleles show only in homozygous individuals.

  2. Two carriers of an autosomal recessive condition have a child. What is the chance the child is affected?
    • 1 in 2
    • 3 in 4
    • None at all
    • 1 in 4
    Show the answer

    Answer: 1 in 4. Each parent passes the recessive allele with probability ½, so ½ × ½ = ¼.

  3. Why are carriers of many recessive conditions unaffected?
    • One working allele makes enough functional protein
    • Carriers have no copies of the gene at all
    • Recessive alleles are removed in carriers
    • Carriers always have two dominant alleles
    Show the answer

    Answer: One working allele makes enough functional protein. A single functional copy is often sufficient.

Key terms

Dominant allele
An allele expressed when one copy is present
Recessive allele
An allele expressed only in the homozygous state
Carrier
A heterozygous person who carries a recessive allele but is usually unaffected
