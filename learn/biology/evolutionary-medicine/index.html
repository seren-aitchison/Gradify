Evolutionary Medicine

Evolutionary medicine explains disease through mismatch with modern environments, trade-offs such as the sickle cell allele, protective defences such as fever, rapid pathogen evolution and cancer as evolution among cells, informing treatment strategies.

Biology · Evolution and Natural Selection · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain the idea of evolutionary mismatch
  • Explain trade-offs and defences in evolutionary medicine
  • Apply evolutionary thinking to pathogens and cancer

Why we are vulnerable

  • Mismatch: bodies evolved in past environments may be poorly suited to modern ones, such as diets high in sugar contributing to type 2 diabetes.
  • Trade-offs: traits that help in one way may harm in another, such as the sickle cell allele protecting against malaria.
  • Defences: symptoms such as fever and coughing can be protective responses.
  • Evolution favours reproduction, not long-term health in old age.

Pathogens and cancer

  • Pathogens evolve rapidly, so treatments can select for resistance.
  • Virulence evolves depending on how pathogens spread.
  • Cancer is an evolutionary process within the body, with selection among cells.
  • Adaptive therapy aims to control, not eliminate, cancer to limit resistance.

The hygiene hypothesis, now often called the old friends hypothesis, suggests that reduced exposure to certain microbes in modern life may contribute to allergies and autoimmune disease. Evolutionary medicine helps design better use of antibiotics and vaccines, for example by considering how vaccines could affect pathogen evolution.

Worked example

Fever as a defence.

  1. During infection, the body raises its temperature.
  2. Higher temperatures can slow growth of some pathogens and boost immune responses.
  3. Fever is costly but can be beneficial.
  4. Evolutionary medicine asks when lowering a fever helps and when it might hinder recovery.
Common mistakeAssuming natural selection has made humans perfectly healthy. Selection favours reproduction and works with trade-offs.

In 3 lines: Mismatch and trade-offs. Defences such as fever. Pathogens and cancer evolve.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which is an example of evolutionary mismatch?
    • Fever helping fight infection
    • Sickle cell trait protecting against malaria
    • Coughing clearing the airways
    • High-sugar diets contributing to type 2 diabetes
    Show the answer

    Answer: High-sugar diets contributing to type 2 diabetes. Bodies evolved for different diets now face plentiful sugar.

  2. Why might fever be beneficial during infection?
    • Higher temperature can slow some pathogens and boost immunity
    • Fever always damages the body
    • Fever stops the immune system
    • Fever increases pathogen growth
    Show the answer

    Answer: Higher temperature can slow some pathogens and boost immunity. Fever can be a protective defence that hinders pathogens and aids immunity.

  3. What is the aim of adaptive therapy in cancer?
    • To eliminate every cancer cell immediately
    • To control tumours while limiting the rise of resistant cells
    • To increase mutation rates in tumours
    • To stop all immune responses
    Show the answer

    Answer: To control tumours while limiting the rise of resistant cells. Keeping sensitive cells can suppress resistant ones.

Key terms

Evolutionary mismatch
A trait poorly suited to a changed environment
Trade-off
A benefit in one respect with a cost in another
Adaptive therapy
Treatment aiming to control rather than eliminate cancer
