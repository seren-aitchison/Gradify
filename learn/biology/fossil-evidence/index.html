Fossil Evidence

Fossils form mainly by mineral replacement in sedimentary rock, are dated relatively by rock layers and absolutely by radiometric methods, show change, transitional forms and extinctions, and form an incomplete but consistent record.

Biology · Evolution and Natural Selection · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe how fossils form
  • Explain how fossils and rocks are dated
  • Explain the strengths and limitations of the fossil record

Formation and dating

  • Fossils form when remains or traces are preserved, often by mineral replacement in sedimentary rock.
  • Hard parts, such as bones and shells, fossilise more readily than soft tissues.
  • Relative dating: lower rock layers are usually older (law of superposition).
  • Radiometric dating: measures decay of radioactive isotopes, such as uranium-lead or potassium-argon, in volcanic rocks.
  • Carbon-14 dating works for remains up to about 50,000 years old.

What fossils show

  • Sequences of change over time, such as in horse evolution.
  • Transitional forms, such as Archaeopteryx with bird and dinosaur features.
  • Mass extinctions, such as the end-Cretaceous extinction 66 million years ago.
  • The order of appearance of major groups, consistent with phylogeny.

The fossil record is incomplete because fossilisation is rare, needs particular conditions and favours hard parts and certain environments. Erosion and geological activity destroy many fossils. Despite gaps, the order of fossils is consistent: no mammal fossils appear in rocks older than the earliest fish, for example. Exceptional sites, such as the Burgess Shale, preserve soft tissues.

Worked example

Dating a fossil.

  1. A fossil lies between two layers of volcanic ash.
  2. The lower ash layer is dated radiometrically at 72 million years.
  3. The upper ash layer is dated at 68 million years.
  4. The fossil is between 68 and 72 million years old.
Common mistakeUsing carbon-14 to date dinosaur fossils. Carbon-14 only works up to about 50,000 years; older fossils need other isotopes.

In 3 lines: Formation by mineral replacement. Relative and radiometric dating. Incomplete but consistent.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which type of rock most commonly contains fossils?
    • Igneous rock
    • Volcanic lava
    • Molten magma
    • Sedimentary rock
    Show the answer

    Answer: Sedimentary rock. Fossils form as sediments bury and preserve remains.

  2. Which method could date a 70-million-year-old fossil?
    • Radiometric dating of nearby volcanic layers
    • Carbon-14 dating of the bone
    • Counting tree rings only
    • Measuring the fossil's length
    Show the answer

    Answer: Radiometric dating of nearby volcanic layers. Older fossils require isotopes with long half-lives.

  3. Why does the order of fossils in rocks support evolution?
    • All groups appear at the same time
    • Groups appear in an order consistent with their evolutionary relationships
    • Fossils are randomly ordered
    • Younger rocks contain only extinct species
    Show the answer

    Answer: Groups appear in an order consistent with their evolutionary relationships. Consistent sequences match predicted phylogeny.

Key terms

Fossil
Preserved remains or traces of past organisms
Radiometric dating
Dating using the decay of radioactive isotopes
Transitional fossil
A fossil with features of two groups
