Cell-cycle Regulation

Cyclins bind and activate CDKs, which phosphorylate targets to drive the cycle; growth factors increase cyclins, proto-oncogenes promote and tumour suppressors such as p53 and RB restrain division, and mutations in these genes can cause cancer.

Biology · Cell Division and Development · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the roles of cyclins and cyclin-dependent kinases
  • Explain how growth factors stimulate division
  • Explain how proto-oncogenes and tumour suppressor genes regulate the cycle

Cyclins and CDKs

  • Cyclin-dependent kinases (CDKs) are enzymes that phosphorylate target proteins to drive the cycle.
  • CDKs are active only when bound to cyclins, whose levels rise and fall through the cycle.
  • Different cyclin-CDK complexes control G₁/S, S and M phases.
  • Cyclins are destroyed after use, resetting the cycle.

Signals and genes

  • Growth factors bind receptors and trigger signalling that increases cyclin production.
  • Proto-oncogenes promote division, such as genes for growth factor receptors.
  • Tumour suppressor genes restrain division, such as p53 and RB.
  • The RB protein blocks entry to S phase until phosphorylated by cyclin-CDK.

Hartwell, Hunt and Nurse shared the 2001 Nobel Prize for discovering CDKs and cyclins. Regulation ensures cells divide only when needed and when conditions are suitable. Mutations that make proto-oncogenes overactive (oncogenes) or inactivate tumour suppressors can lead to uncontrolled division. CDK4/6 inhibitors are used to treat some breast cancers.

Worked example

Entering S phase.

  1. A growth factor signal increases cyclin D production.
  2. Cyclin D binds CDK4/6, forming an active complex.
  3. The complex phosphorylates RB, which releases transcription factors.
  4. Genes for S phase are transcribed, and DNA replication begins.
Common mistakeSaying CDKs are active throughout the cycle. They are active only when bound to cyclins, whose levels change.

In 3 lines: Cyclins activate CDKs. Growth factors stimulate. Oncogenes and tumour suppressors.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What do cyclin-CDK complexes do?
    • Replicate DNA directly
    • Break down chromosomes
    • Build the cell wall
    • Phosphorylate target proteins to drive the cycle
    Show the answer

    Answer: Phosphorylate target proteins to drive the cycle. Phosphorylation changes target activity to move the cycle on.

  2. What happens to RB when it is phosphorylated by cyclin-CDK?
    • It releases transcription factors for S-phase genes
    • It blocks DNA replication more strongly
    • It destroys the cyclin
    • It causes apoptosis immediately
    Show the answer

    Answer: It releases transcription factors for S-phase genes. Phosphorylated RB lets the cell enter S phase.

  3. How can a mutation in a proto-oncogene lead to cancer?
    • It always stops cell division
    • It can make the gene overactive, stimulating division constantly
    • It repairs DNA too efficiently
    • It makes cells enter G₀ permanently
    Show the answer

    Answer: It can make the gene overactive, stimulating division constantly. Overactive oncogenes drive uncontrolled proliferation.

Key terms

Cyclin
A protein whose levels rise and fall, activating CDKs
CDK
Cyclin-dependent kinase, an enzyme driving the cell cycle
Tumour suppressor gene
A gene that restrains cell division
