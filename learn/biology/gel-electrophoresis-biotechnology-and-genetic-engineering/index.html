Gel Electrophoresis

Gel electrophoresis separates negatively charged DNA fragments by size, with smaller fragments moving further towards the positive electrode; sizes are estimated using a DNA ladder, and the method is used in profiling, PCR checks and diagnosis.

Biology · Biotechnology and Genetic Engineering · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain how gel electrophoresis separates DNA
  • Interpret a gel using a DNA ladder
  • Describe uses of gel electrophoresis

How it works

  • DNA samples are loaded into wells in an agarose gel.
  • An electric current is applied.
  • DNA is negatively charged because of its phosphate groups, so it moves towards the positive electrode (anode).
  • Smaller fragments move faster and further through the gel's pores.
  • DNA is stained, for example with a fluorescent dye, and viewed under UV or blue light.

Interpreting

  • A DNA ladder contains fragments of known size for comparison.
  • Fragment sizes are estimated by comparing band positions to the ladder.
  • Bands that line up are the same size.
  • Proteins can be separated by a similar method using SDS-PAGE.

Gel electrophoresis is used in DNA profiling, checking PCR products, analysing restriction digests and diagnosing genetic conditions. Loading dye helps track samples and makes them sink into wells. Agarose concentration affects separation: higher concentrations separate small fragments better.

Worked example

Checking a PCR product.

  1. A PCR was designed to amplify a 500 bp fragment.
  2. The product is run on a gel beside a DNA ladder.
  3. A single band appears level with the 500 bp ladder band.
  4. The PCR successfully produced the expected fragment.
Common mistakeThinking DNA moves towards the negative electrode. DNA is negative, so it moves towards the positive electrode.

In 3 lines: DNA moves to the anode. Small fragments go further. Compare with a ladder.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which fragments travel furthest through the gel?
    • The largest fragments
    • Fragments with most adenine
    • The smallest fragments
    • Fragments with no charge
    Show the answer

    Answer: The smallest fragments. Small fragments pass through gel pores more easily.

  2. How is the size of an unknown band estimated?
    • By measuring the band's brightness only
    • By counting the wells
    • By weighing the gel
    • By comparing its position with ladder bands
    Show the answer

    Answer: By comparing its position with ladder bands. Ladder bands of known size act as a scale.

  3. Why might a higher agarose concentration be chosen?
    • It separates small fragments more clearly
    • It makes DNA move faster
    • It stops DNA moving
    • It changes DNA's charge
    Show the answer

    Answer: It separates small fragments more clearly. Smaller pores improve resolution of small fragments.

Key terms

Gel electrophoresis
Separation of molecules by size using an electric field
DNA ladder
A mixture of DNA fragments of known sizes
Agarose
A gel-forming polysaccharide used in electrophoresis
