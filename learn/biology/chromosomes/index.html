Chromosomes

Eukaryotic DNA wraps around histones into nucleosomes and chromatin, condensing into chromosomes with sister chromatids and centromeres; prokaryotes have one circular chromosome, and humans have 46 chromosomes, shown in a karyotype.

Biology · Genetics and Molecular Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe how DNA is packaged into chromosomes in eukaryotes
  • Compare prokaryotic and eukaryotic chromosomes
  • Describe the human karyotype

Eukaryotic packaging

  • DNA wraps around histone proteins to form nucleosomes, like beads on a string.
  • Nucleosomes coil into chromatin fibres, which loop and condense further.
  • Euchromatin is loosely packed and active; heterochromatin is tightly packed and mostly inactive.
  • Before division, chromosomes condense and consist of two sister chromatids joined at a centromere.

Chromosome types

  • Prokaryotes: usually one circular chromosome, not associated with histones, plus plasmids.
  • Eukaryotes: several linear chromosomes in the nucleus, with telomeres at the ends.
  • Humans have 46 chromosomes: 22 pairs of autosomes and one pair of sex chromosomes (XX or XY).
  • A karyotype is an image of chromosomes arranged by size and shape.

Each human cell contains about 2 metres of DNA, packed into a nucleus a few micrometres across. Packaging also regulates gene expression, because tightly packed DNA is less accessible to transcription. Mitochondria and chloroplasts have their own small, circular DNA, supporting the endosymbiotic theory.

Worked example

Reading a karyotype.

  1. Chromosomes from a dividing cell are stained and photographed.
  2. They are arranged in pairs by size, centromere position and banding.
  3. A person has 22 pairs of autosomes and XY sex chromosomes.
  4. The karyotype shows a typical male with 46 chromosomes.
Common mistakeConfusing a chromosome with a chromatid. A replicated chromosome has two sister chromatids.

In 3 lines: DNA wraps around histones. Prokaryotes: circular; eukaryotes: linear. Humans have 46 chromosomes.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which proteins does eukaryotic DNA wrap around?
    • Histones
    • Haemoglobin
    • Collagen
    • Actin
    Show the answer

    Answer: Histones. DNA wraps around histone proteins, forming the cores of nucleosomes.

  2. How does a prokaryotic chromosome differ from a eukaryotic one?
    • It is linear and capped at each end by telomeres
    • It is usually circular and not wrapped around histones
    • It is enclosed inside a membrane-bound nucleus
    • It is made of RNA rather than of DNA
    Show the answer

    Answer: It is usually circular and not wrapped around histones. Prokaryotes usually have one circular chromosome.

  3. Why are genes in heterochromatin usually not expressed?
    • Heterochromatin is a region that contains no DNA at all
    • Heterochromatin is only found in prokaryotic cells
    • Tight packing makes the DNA inaccessible to transcription machinery
    • Heterochromatin is made entirely of RNA molecules
    Show the answer

    Answer: Tight packing makes the DNA inaccessible to transcription machinery. Condensed chromatin blocks access to genes.

Key terms

Histone
A protein around which DNA is wound
Nucleosome
DNA wrapped around a core of histones
Karyotype
An organised image of an individual's chromosomes
