Proteins

Proteins act as enzymes, transporters, structural molecules, motors, signals and antibodies; they have primary, secondary, tertiary and quaternary structure, are globular or fibrous and are detected by the biuret test.

Biology · Biochemistry and Biomolecules · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the range of functions carried out by proteins
  • Outline the four levels of protein structure
  • Distinguish globular and fibrous proteins

Functions

  • Enzymes: catalyse reactions, such as amylase.
  • Transport: haemoglobin carries oxygen; channel proteins move ions.
  • Structure: collagen and keratin.
  • Movement: actin and myosin in muscle.
  • Signalling: hormones such as insulin and receptors.
  • Defence: antibodies.

Levels of structure and types

  • Primary: amino acid sequence.
  • Secondary: α-helices and β-sheets held by hydrogen bonds.
  • Tertiary: overall 3D folding of one chain.
  • Quaternary: arrangement of more than one chain.
  • Globular proteins (enzymes, haemoglobin) are compact and soluble; fibrous proteins (collagen, keratin) are long, insoluble and strong.

Protein function depends on shape, and shape depends on the amino acid sequence. A single change in sequence can alter function, as in sickle cell haemoglobin. The biuret test detects proteins: adding biuret reagent (sodium hydroxide and copper(II) sulfate) turns a protein solution from blue to purple, due to peptide bonds.

Worked example

Globular and fibrous proteins.

  1. Haemoglobin is a globular protein: compact, soluble and able to bind oxygen reversibly.
  2. Collagen is a fibrous protein: three long chains wound into a strong triple helix.
  3. Haemoglobin suits transport in blood; collagen suits tendons and skin.
  4. Each shape suits its function.
Common mistakeThinking protein function is independent of shape. Function depends on the precise 3D shape determined by the sequence.

In 3 lines: Many functions. Four levels of structure. Globular or fibrous.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which protein transports oxygen in red blood cells?
    • Collagen
    • Haemoglobin
    • Keratin
    • Amylase
    Show the answer

    Answer: Haemoglobin. Haemoglobin is a globular transport protein that binds oxygen.

  2. What does the primary structure of a protein describe?
    • The 3D folding of the chain
    • The arrangement of several chains
    • The sequence of amino acids
    • The number of hydrogen bonds
    Show the answer

    Answer: The sequence of amino acids. Primary structure is the order of amino acids.

  3. Why is collagen better suited than haemoglobin for strengthening tendons?
    • It is compact and soluble
    • It binds oxygen reversibly
    • It is a steroid
    • It is a long, strong fibrous protein with a triple helix
    Show the answer

    Answer: It is a long, strong fibrous protein with a triple helix. Fibrous proteins have tensile strength suited to structural roles.

Key terms

Globular protein
A compact, usually soluble protein, such as an enzyme
Fibrous protein
A long, insoluble, strong protein, such as collagen
Biuret test
A test for proteins giving a purple colour
