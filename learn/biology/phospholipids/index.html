Phospholipids

Phospholipids have a hydrophilic phosphate head and two hydrophobic fatty acid tails, so in water they form bilayers with heads outward and tails inward, creating a fluid barrier to ions and polar molecules.

Biology · Biochemistry and Biomolecules · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the structure of a phospholipid
  • Explain how phospholipids form bilayers in water
  • Relate phospholipid properties to membrane function

Structure

  • A phospholipid has glycerol, two fatty acids and a phosphate group, often linked to another polar group such as choline.
  • The phosphate head is hydrophilic (water-loving).
  • The fatty acid tails are hydrophobic (water-fearing).
  • So phospholipids are amphipathic, with both parts.

Arrangement in water

  • In water, phospholipids form a bilayer: heads face the water on both sides, tails face inwards.
  • They can also form micelles and closed vesicles.
  • The bilayer's hydrophobic core is a barrier to ions and polar molecules.
  • Small non-polar molecules, such as O₂ and CO₂, pass through easily.

The phospholipid bilayer forms spontaneously because it minimises contact between hydrophobic tails and water. It is fluid, as phospholipids move sideways within each layer, and its fluidity depends on fatty acid saturation, temperature and cholesterol. Proteins embedded in the bilayer carry out transport, signalling and recognition, giving the fluid mosaic model of membrane structure.

Worked example

Why a bilayer forms.

  1. Phospholipids are added to water.
  2. Hydrophobic tails avoid water by clustering together.
  3. Hydrophilic heads remain in contact with water.
  4. The most stable arrangement is a bilayer with heads outwards and tails inwards.
Common mistakeSaying the phosphate head is hydrophobic. The head is hydrophilic; the tails are hydrophobic.

In 3 lines: Hydrophilic head, hydrophobic tails. Bilayers form spontaneously. A barrier to ions and polar molecules.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which part of a phospholipid is hydrophilic?
    • The fatty acid tails
    • The glycerol only, without phosphate
    • None of it
    • The phosphate head
    Show the answer

    Answer: The phosphate head. The charged phosphate head interacts with water.

  2. Why do phospholipids form a bilayer in water?
    • Hydrophobic tails avoid water while hydrophilic heads face it
    • Heads repel each other in water
    • Tails are attracted to water
    • Bilayers form only with added energy from ATP
    Show the answer

    Answer: Hydrophobic tails avoid water while hydrophilic heads face it. Bilayers minimise contact between tails and water.

  3. Why can't sodium ions diffuse freely through a phospholipid bilayer?
    • Sodium ions are too large to move
    • The hydrophobic core repels charged particles
    • The bilayer has no core
    • Sodium ions are non-polar
    Show the answer

    Answer: The hydrophobic core repels charged particles. Charged ions cannot cross the non-polar interior easily.

Key terms

Amphipathic
Having both hydrophilic and hydrophobic parts
Phospholipid bilayer
A double layer of phospholipids forming membranes
Hydrophobic
Repelled by water
