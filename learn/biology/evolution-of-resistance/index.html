Evolution of Resistance

Antibiotic resistance evolves when random mutations allow some bacteria to survive treatment and reproduce, with genes spreading by horizontal transfer on plasmids; misuse speeds it, while stewardship, infection prevention, vaccination, new drugs and combination therapy slow it.

Biology · Evolution and Natural Selection · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain how antibiotic resistance evolves
  • Describe how resistance genes spread between bacteria
  • Describe strategies to reduce the evolution of resistance

How resistance evolves

  • Random mutations give some bacteria resistance before the antibiotic is used.
  • The antibiotic kills susceptible bacteria, leaving resistant ones.
  • Resistant bacteria reproduce, so resistance becomes common: directional selection.
  • Bacteria divide rapidly, so this can happen quickly.

Spread and control

  • Resistance genes spread by horizontal gene transfer, especially on plasmids by conjugation.
  • Misuse includes unnecessary prescriptions, not finishing courses where advised, and overuse in farming.
  • Strategies: antibiotic stewardship, infection prevention, vaccination and developing new drugs.
  • Similar processes produce pesticide and herbicide resistance and drug resistance in cancer and HIV.

MRSA (methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus) is a well-known resistant pathogen. The UK's national action plan aims to reduce antibiotic use and infections. Antimicrobial resistance was associated with an estimated 4.95 million deaths worldwide in 2019, including 1.27 million directly attributable deaths. Combination therapy, as used for HIV and tuberculosis, makes it harder for resistance to evolve because several mutations are needed at once.

Worked example

Evolution of MRSA.

  1. Methicillin was widely used against Staphylococcus aureus from 1959.
  2. Some bacteria carried a gene, mecA, giving resistance.
  3. These survived treatment and reproduced, and the gene spread.
  4. MRSA was reported within a few years and became a major hospital pathogen.
Common mistakeSaying bacteria become resistant because antibiotics cause the mutation. Resistance mutations arise randomly; antibiotics select for them.

In 3 lines: Selection of resistant bacteria. Plasmids spread genes. Stewardship slows resistance.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Why do resistant bacteria increase in number during antibiotic treatment?
    • Antibiotics turn susceptible bacteria into resistant ones
    • Resistant bacteria are fed by the antibiotic
    • Susceptible bacteria are killed and resistant ones reproduce
    • Antibiotics stop all mutation
    Show the answer

    Answer: Susceptible bacteria are killed and resistant ones reproduce. Selection removes susceptible cells, leaving resistant ones.

  2. Why does combination therapy slow the evolution of resistance?
    • It uses a lower total dose of drugs
    • It increases the mutation rate
    • It stops horizontal gene transfer completely
    • Bacteria would need several resistance mutations at once
    Show the answer

    Answer: Bacteria would need several resistance mutations at once. Simultaneous resistance to several drugs is very unlikely.

  3. Why can overuse of antibiotics in farming increase resistance in human pathogens?
    • It selects for resistance genes that can transfer to human bacteria
    • It makes farm animals immune
    • It has no connection to human health
    • It kills all resistant bacteria
    Show the answer

    Answer: It selects for resistance genes that can transfer to human bacteria. Resistance genes selected in farms can spread to pathogens.

Key terms

Antibiotic resistance
The ability of bacteria to survive antibiotics
Horizontal gene transfer
Transfer of genes between organisms other than by reproduction
Antibiotic stewardship
Responsible use of antibiotics to slow resistance
