Antimicrobial Resistance

Bacteria resist antibiotics by enzymatic inactivation, target modification, efflux pumps, reduced permeability and bypass pathways; resistance is detected by testing and sequencing, and responses include stewardship, infection control, new drugs and One Health.

Biology · Microbiology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the biochemical mechanisms of antibiotic resistance
  • Explain how resistance is detected and monitored
  • Describe global responses to antimicrobial resistance

Mechanisms

  • Enzymatic inactivation: beta-lactamases break down penicillins.
  • Target modification: altered penicillin-binding proteins in MRSA.
  • Efflux pumps: proteins pump antibiotics out of the cell.
  • Reduced permeability: changes in outer membrane porins.
  • Bypass pathways: alternative enzymes replace the target.

Detection and response

  • Resistance is detected by sensitivity tests and by finding resistance genes with PCR or sequencing.
  • Surveillance tracks resistance in hospitals and communities.
  • Carbapenem-resistant Enterobacterales are a priority threat.
  • Responses: stewardship, infection control, new drugs, diagnostics and the One Health approach linking human, animal and environmental health.

The World Health Organization publishes a list of priority pathogens to guide research. Clavulanic acid, a beta-lactamase inhibitor, is combined with amoxicillin to overcome some resistance. Bacteriophage therapy, using viruses that kill specific bacteria, is being explored as an alternative treatment.

Worked example

Overcoming beta-lactamase.

  1. A bacterium makes beta-lactamase, which destroys amoxicillin.
  2. Amoxicillin alone fails to treat the infection.
  3. Clavulanic acid is added, blocking beta-lactamase.
  4. Amoxicillin can now reach its target and kill the bacteria.
Common mistakeThinking resistance means a person's body becomes resistant. It is the bacteria that become resistant.

In 3 lines: Five resistance mechanisms. Testing and surveillance. Global responses.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What do efflux pumps do in resistant bacteria?
    • Pump nutrients into the cell
    • Break down cell walls
    • Pump antibiotics out of the cell
    • Make new ribosomes
    Show the answer

    Answer: Pump antibiotics out of the cell. Removing the drug keeps its concentration too low to work.

  2. How does clavulanic acid help amoxicillin work?
    • It kills bacteria directly
    • It increases efflux pumps
    • It thickens the cell wall
    • It blocks beta-lactamase enzymes
    Show the answer

    Answer: It blocks beta-lactamase enzymes. Protecting amoxicillin lets it reach its target.

  3. Why is a One Health approach needed for resistance?
    • Resistance spreads between humans, animals and the environment
    • Resistance only occurs in hospitals
    • Animals cannot carry resistant bacteria
    • The environment has no bacteria
    Show the answer

    Answer: Resistance spreads between humans, animals and the environment. Linked sectors must act together to slow spread.

Key terms

Beta-lactamase
An enzyme breaking down penicillin-type antibiotics
Efflux pump
A protein pumping drugs out of a cell
One Health
An approach linking human, animal and environmental health
