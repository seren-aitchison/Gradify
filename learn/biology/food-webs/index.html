Food Webs

Food webs combine interlinked food chains, showing interdependence; removing species causes direct and indirect effects and trophic cascades, and keystone species such as sea otters have large effects on community structure.

Biology · Ecology and Environmental Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Construct and interpret a food web
  • Predict effects of changes in one population
  • Explain the role of keystone species

Food webs

  • A food web combines many food chains.
  • Most animals eat more than one food and are eaten by more than one predator.
  • Webs show how populations are interdependent.
  • More connections often make a web more stable.

Changes and keystones

  • Removing a species can affect many others, directly and indirectly.
  • Trophic cascade: changes at one level ripple through others.
  • Keystone species have a large effect relative to their numbers.
  • Examples: sea otters in kelp forests and wolves in Yellowstone.

When sea otters declined along the Pacific coast, sea urchins increased and destroyed kelp forests; when otters recovered, kelp returned. Robert Paine coined the term keystone species after removing starfish from rock pools, which allowed mussels to take over and reduced diversity. Food webs help predict the effects of fishing, invasive species and extinction.

Worked example

Paine's starfish experiment.

  1. Paine removed the starfish Pisaster from rocky shore plots.
  2. Without the predator, mussels spread and outcompeted other species.
  3. The number of species in plots fell sharply.
  4. The starfish was a keystone species maintaining diversity.
Common mistakeThinking removing a predator always helps prey species. It can cause cascades that reduce overall diversity.

In 3 lines: Interlinked chains. Indirect effects. Keystone species.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Why are food webs more realistic than food chains?
    • Each animal eats only one food
    • Food webs ignore producers
    • Most animals eat and are eaten by several species
    • Food chains include more species
    Show the answer

    Answer: Most animals eat and are eaten by several species. Real feeding relationships form networks.

  2. What happened to kelp forests when sea otters declined?
    • Kelp grew much faster than before
    • Sea urchins disappeared completely
    • Fish ate all the kelp
    • Sea urchins increased and destroyed the kelp
    Show the answer

    Answer: Sea urchins increased and destroyed the kelp. Fewer otters meant more urchins grazing kelp.

  3. Why did removing starfish reduce species diversity in Paine's plots?
    • Mussels were no longer controlled and outcompeted others
    • Starfish were the main producers
    • Mussels died without starfish
    • Removing predators always increases diversity
    Show the answer

    Answer: Mussels were no longer controlled and outcompeted others. The predator prevented one competitor dominating.

Key terms

Food web
A network of interlinked food chains
Keystone species
A species with a large effect on its community relative to its numbers
Trophic cascade
Effects of a change spreading through trophic levels
