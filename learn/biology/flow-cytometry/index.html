Flow Cytometry

Flow cytometry passes single cells past lasers, measuring size by forward scatter, complexity by side scatter and markers by fluorescent antibodies; gating selects populations, and FACS sorts cells, with uses in HIV monitoring and leukaemia diagnosis.

Biology · Experimental Biology and Research Methods · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe how a flow cytometer measures cells
  • Explain how fluorescent antibodies identify cell types
  • Interpret flow cytometry plots and gating

How it works

  • Cells flow in single file past one or more lasers.
  • Forward scatter indicates cell size.
  • Side scatter indicates internal complexity or granularity.
  • Fluorescent labels, often antibodies, detect specific markers.

Analysis

  • Data are shown as histograms or dot plots.
  • Gating selects populations of interest, such as live lymphocytes.
  • Cells can be counted as percentages of a population.
  • Cell sorting (FACS) physically separates cells.

Flow cytometry is used to count CD4 T cells in people with HIV, diagnose leukaemias and study immune responses. Compensation corrects for overlap between fluorescent dyes. Controls such as unstained cells set the threshold for positive staining.

Worked example

Counting CD4 cells.

  1. A blood sample is labelled with fluorescent anti-CD4 antibodies.
  2. Cells pass through the flow cytometer.
  3. Lymphocytes are gated by scatter.
  4. The proportion of CD4-positive cells is measured, guiding HIV care.
Common mistakeAssuming every fluorescent signal is a true positive. Controls and compensation are needed to set thresholds.

In 3 lines: Single cells past lasers. Scatter and fluorescence. Gating and sorting.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What does side scatter indicate?
    • Cell size only
    • DNA sequence
    • Cell age
    • Internal complexity or granularity
    Show the answer

    Answer: Internal complexity or granularity. Granular cells scatter more light sideways.

  2. Why are unstained cells used as a control?
    • To set the threshold for positive fluorescence
    • To increase the fluorescence of all cells
    • To sort the cells
    • To label all markers
    Show the answer

    Answer: To set the threshold for positive fluorescence. Unstained cells show background fluorescence, setting the threshold.

  3. Why is flow cytometry useful for diagnosing leukaemia?
    • It sequences the whole cancer genome
    • It identifies abnormal cell populations by their markers
    • It measures blood pressure
    • It only counts red cells
    Show the answer

    Answer: It identifies abnormal cell populations by their markers. Marker patterns distinguish leukaemia types.
