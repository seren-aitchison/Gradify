Polymerase Chain Reaction

PCR uses template DNA, primers, Taq polymerase and nucleotides in cycles of denaturation, annealing and extension, roughly doubling the target each cycle so that copies equal the starting number times 2ⁿ.

Biology · Biotechnology and Genetic Engineering · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the components of a PCR reaction
  • Explain the three stages of a PCR cycle
  • Calculate the number of DNA copies after PCR

Components

  • Template DNA containing the target sequence.
  • Primers: short single-stranded DNA sequences complementary to each end of the target.
  • Taq polymerase: heat-stable DNA polymerase from Thermus aquaticus.
  • Free nucleotides (dNTPs) and buffer with magnesium ions.
  • A thermal cycler changes temperature automatically.

The cycle

  • Denaturation at about 95 °C separates DNA strands.
  • Annealing at about 50–65 °C lets primers bind.
  • Extension at about 72 °C: Taq adds nucleotides to the primers.
  • Each cycle roughly doubles the DNA.
  • After n cycles, copies = initial number × 2ⁿ.

Kary Mullis invented PCR in 1983 and shared the 1993 Nobel Prize in Chemistry. Taq polymerase was crucial because it survives the repeated heating that would destroy most enzymes. PCR is used in diagnosis, forensics, cloning and research. Contamination is a major risk because tiny amounts of DNA are amplified.

Worked example

Calculating PCR copies.

  1. A sample contains 10 copies of a target sequence.
  2. PCR runs for 20 cycles.
  3. Copies = 10 × 2²⁰ ≈ 10 × 1,048,576.
  4. About 10 million copies are produced.
Common mistakeThinking primers bind at 95 °C. They anneal at the lower annealing temperature after strands separate.

In 3 lines: Template, primers, Taq. Denature, anneal, extend. 2ⁿ copies.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. At about what temperature are DNA strands separated in PCR?
    • 37 °C
    • 55 °C
    • 72 °C
    • 95 °C
    Show the answer

    Answer: 95 °C. High heat breaks hydrogen bonds between the strands.

  2. What do primers do in PCR?
    • Provide a starting point for Taq polymerase
    • Cut DNA at specific sites
    • Join DNA fragments together
    • Stain DNA for viewing
    Show the answer

    Answer: Provide a starting point for Taq polymerase. Polymerase can only extend from an existing primer.

  3. Why is contamination a serious problem in PCR?
    • PCR cannot amplify small samples
    • Even tiny amounts of unwanted DNA are amplified
    • Contamination stops Taq working
    • Primers bind only to contaminants
    Show the answer

    Answer: Even tiny amounts of unwanted DNA are amplified. Exponential amplification magnifies any contaminating DNA.

Key terms

Primer
A short DNA sequence that starts DNA synthesis
Taq polymerase
A heat-stable DNA polymerase used in PCR
Thermal cycler
A machine controlling PCR temperatures
