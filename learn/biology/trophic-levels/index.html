Trophic Levels

Trophic levels place organisms from producers to top consumers; pyramids of numbers can be inverted, biomass pyramids are usually upright and energy pyramids are always upright, and persistent chemicals biomagnify at higher levels.

Biology · Ecology and Environmental Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define trophic level
  • Compare pyramids of numbers, biomass and energy
  • Explain biomagnification

Trophic levels

  • Level 1: producers.
  • Level 2: primary consumers.
  • Level 3: secondary consumers.
  • Level 4: tertiary consumers.
  • Omnivores can occupy more than one level.

Pyramids

  • Pyramid of numbers: number of organisms at each level; can be inverted, as when one oak tree supports many caterpillars.
  • Pyramid of biomass: dry mass at each level; usually upright.
  • Pyramid of energy: energy at each level over time; always upright.
  • Biomass is measured by drying samples to remove water.

Biomagnification is the increasing concentration of persistent chemicals at higher trophic levels. DDT accumulated in birds of prey, causing thin eggshells and population crashes of peregrine falcons in the UK in the 1950s and 1960s. After DDT was banned, populations recovered. Mercury biomagnifies in large fish such as tuna and swordfish.

Worked example

DDT and peregrines.

  1. DDT was sprayed on crops and entered water.
  2. Small organisms absorbed small amounts, which were stored in fat.
  3. Each predator ate many prey, concentrating the DDT.
  4. Peregrines at the top had high levels, which thinned their eggshells.
Common mistakeThinking pyramids of numbers are always upright. A single tree can support many insects, inverting the pyramid.

In 3 lines: Levels 1 to 4. Numbers, biomass, energy pyramids. Biomagnification.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which trophic level do producers occupy?
    • The first
    • The second
    • The third
    • The top
    Show the answer

    Answer: The first. Producers form the base of food chains at the first trophic level.

  2. Why can a pyramid of numbers be inverted?
    • Predators always outnumber prey
    • One large producer can support many small consumers
    • Energy increases up the chain
    • Producers are always tiny
    Show the answer

    Answer: One large producer can support many small consumers. A single tree can feed thousands of insects.

  3. Why did DDT affect peregrine falcons most strongly?
    • They ate DDT directly from crops
    • DDT was sprayed on their nests
    • They were at the top of the food chain, where DDT was most concentrated
    • Peregrines lack fat
    Show the answer

    Answer: They were at the top of the food chain, where DDT was most concentrated. Each level concentrates persistent chemicals further.

Key terms

Trophic level
An organism's feeding position in a food chain
Biomass
The mass of living material
Biomagnification
Increasing concentration of a chemical up a food chain
