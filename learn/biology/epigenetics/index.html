Epigenetics

Epigenetics involves heritable changes in expression without DNA sequence change, through DNA methylation, which usually silences genes, and histone modifications such as acetylation, which usually activate them; examples include X-inactivation, imprinting and differentiation.

Biology · Genetics and Molecular Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define epigenetics
  • Describe DNA methylation and histone modification
  • Explain examples of epigenetic effects in development and disease

Mechanisms

  • Epigenetics: heritable changes in gene expression not caused by changes in DNA sequence.
  • DNA methylation: methyl groups added to cytosine, often near promoters, usually silence genes.
  • Histone acetylation: loosens chromatin, usually activating genes.
  • Histone deacetylation and some methylation tighten chromatin, silencing genes.

Examples

  • X-inactivation: in female mammals, one X chromosome is silenced in each cell.
  • Genomic imprinting: some genes are expressed only from the maternal or paternal copy.
  • Cell differentiation maintains patterns of active and silent genes through cell division.
  • Environment, such as diet and stress, can affect epigenetic marks.

Epigenetic marks can be reversed, which makes them targets for drugs: some cancer treatments inhibit DNA methyltransferases or histone deacetylases. Tortoiseshell cats show X-inactivation, with patches of different fur colour. Evidence for transgenerational epigenetic inheritance in humans is limited and debated, though some marks can pass through cell divisions within an individual.

Worked example

X-inactivation in tortoiseshell cats.

  1. The gene for orange or black fur is on the X chromosome.
  2. A female cat heterozygous for this gene inactivates one X at random in each early cell.
  3. Patches of skin descended from these cells express different alleles.
  4. The cat has patches of orange and black fur.
Common mistakeClaiming epigenetic changes alter the DNA sequence. They alter how DNA is packaged or read, not its sequence.

In 3 lines: Expression changes without sequence change. Methylation silences; acetylation activates. X-inactivation and imprinting.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is epigenetics?
    • Heritable changes in gene expression without DNA sequence changes
    • Changes in DNA base sequence
    • The study of protein folding
    • The movement of chromosomes in mitosis
    Show the answer

    Answer: Heritable changes in gene expression without DNA sequence changes. Epigenetic marks alter expression, not the code itself.

  2. How does histone acetylation usually affect genes?
    • It tightens chromatin and silences genes
    • It loosens chromatin and activates genes
    • It deletes genes
    • It converts DNA to RNA
    Show the answer

    Answer: It loosens chromatin and activates genes. Acetylation reduces histone-DNA attraction, opening chromatin.

  3. Why do some cancer drugs target histone deacetylases?
    • They change the DNA sequence
    • They destroy all histones
    • Reversing deacetylation can reactivate silenced tumour suppressor genes
    • They block DNA replication completely
    Show the answer

    Answer: Reversing deacetylation can reactivate silenced tumour suppressor genes. Epigenetic marks are reversible and can be therapeutic targets.

Key terms

Epigenetics
Heritable changes in gene expression without sequence change
DNA methylation
Addition of methyl groups to DNA, usually silencing genes
Histone acetylation
Addition of acetyl groups to histones, usually activating genes
