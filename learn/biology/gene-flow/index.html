Gene Flow

Gene flow transfers alleles between populations through migration and gamete dispersal, increasing variation within populations and reducing differences between them; lack of gene flow allows divergence, and restoring it helps conservation.

Biology · Evolution and Natural Selection · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define gene flow
  • Explain how gene flow affects genetic variation within and between populations
  • Describe the role of gene flow in conservation and speciation

What gene flow is

  • Gene flow is the transfer of alleles between populations by movement of individuals or gametes.
  • Examples: animals migrating and breeding, pollen carried by wind or insects.
  • It introduces new alleles into a population.
  • It makes populations more similar to each other.

Effects

  • Increases variation within a population.
  • Reduces differences between populations, opposing divergence.
  • Can introduce alleles that are poorly suited to local conditions.
  • Lack of gene flow allows populations to diverge, which may lead to speciation.

Humans increase gene flow in some species, for example by moving plants and animals, and reduce it in others, by fragmenting habitats with roads and farmland. Conservationists create wildlife corridors to restore gene flow between isolated populations, reducing inbreeding. In Florida panthers, introducing females from Texas in 1995 increased genetic diversity and improved survival.

Worked example

Restoring gene flow.

  1. Florida panthers were isolated and inbred, with heart defects and low fertility.
  2. In 1995, eight female pumas from Texas were released.
  3. Their offspring had higher genetic diversity.
  4. The population grew and health problems decreased.
Common mistakeThinking gene flow always helps local adaptation. It can introduce alleles poorly suited to local conditions.

In 3 lines: Alleles move between populations. Makes populations similar. Corridors and genetic rescue.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which is an example of gene flow?
    • A mutation in one plant's cells
    • A change in allele frequency by chance
    • The death of individuals in a storm
    • Pollen carried by bees to a different population
    Show the answer

    Answer: Pollen carried by bees to a different population. Moving pollen transfers alleles between populations.

  2. How does gene flow affect differences between populations?
    • It reduces differences, making them more similar
    • It increases differences between populations
    • It has no effect on any populations
    • It always causes speciation
    Show the answer

    Answer: It reduces differences, making them more similar. Shared alleles homogenise populations, reducing the differences between them.

  3. Why can a lack of gene flow lead to speciation?
    • Isolated populations stop evolving
    • Isolated populations diverge independently over time
    • Gene flow is required for any mutation
    • Isolation increases gene flow
    Show the answer

    Answer: Isolated populations diverge independently over time. Without exchange, populations accumulate differences.

Key terms

Gene flow
Transfer of alleles between populations
Wildlife corridor
A habitat link allowing movement between populations
Genetic rescue
Increasing diversity by introducing individuals
