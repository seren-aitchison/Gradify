Genetic Drift

Genetic drift is random change in allele frequencies, strongest in small populations, and can lose or fix alleles; bottlenecks and founder effects reduce variation, as in elephant seals and the Amish, and drift is unrelated to fitness.

Biology · Evolution and Natural Selection · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define genetic drift
  • Explain the bottleneck and founder effects
  • Compare drift with natural selection

Drift

  • Genetic drift is random change in allele frequencies from generation to generation.
  • It occurs because chance determines which individuals reproduce and which alleles are passed on.
  • Its effect is strongest in small populations.
  • Drift can lead to alleles being lost or fixed (reaching 100%).

Bottlenecks and founders

  • Bottleneck effect: a population is drastically reduced, and the survivors carry only some alleles.
  • Example: northern elephant seals were hunted to about 20 individuals in the 1890s and now have very low genetic diversity.
  • Founder effect: a small group starts a new population with only part of the original variation.
  • Example: high frequency of Ellis-van Creveld syndrome among the Amish of Pennsylvania.

Unlike selection, drift is not related to how traits affect survival: it can even increase harmful alleles by chance. In large populations, selection generally outweighs drift, but neutral alleles change mainly by drift in any population. Drift is a major concern for endangered species with small populations.

Worked example

Simulating drift.

  1. Ten marbles represent a population: five red and five blue.
  2. Ten marbles are drawn at random, with replacement, to form the next generation.
  3. By chance, seven are red and three are blue.
  4. Repeating this, one colour may eventually disappear, without any selection.
Common mistakeThinking drift only affects harmful alleles. Drift acts randomly on any allele.

In 3 lines: Drift is random. Strongest in small populations. Bottlenecks and founders.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is genetic drift?
    • Random change in allele frequencies from chance
    • Change in alleles caused by selection
    • Movement of alleles between populations
    • The creation of new alleles by mutation
    Show the answer

    Answer: Random change in allele frequencies from chance. Drift results from chance in which alleles are passed on.

  2. Why do northern elephant seals have very low genetic diversity?
    • They have a very high mutation rate
    • They went through a severe population bottleneck
    • They migrate between many populations
    • They reproduce asexually
    Show the answer

    Answer: They went through a severe population bottleneck. Hunting reduced them to a tiny population, losing variation.

  3. How does genetic drift differ from natural selection?
    • Drift always favours beneficial alleles
    • Selection is random and drift is not
    • Drift is random, while selection depends on fitness
    • They are the same process
    Show the answer

    Answer: Drift is random, while selection depends on fitness. Drift is unrelated to how traits affect survival.

Key terms

Genetic drift
Random change in allele frequencies
Bottleneck effect
Loss of variation after a sharp fall in population size
Founder effect
Reduced variation in a population started by few individuals
