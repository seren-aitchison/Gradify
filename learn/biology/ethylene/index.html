Ethylene

Ethylene is a gaseous hormone that triggers fruit ripening, often by positive feedback in climacteric fruits, and promotes abscission; it is used and controlled commercially to manage ripening and storage.

Biology · Plant Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the role of ethylene in fruit ripening
  • Explain the role of ethylene in abscission
  • Describe how ethylene is controlled in food storage

Effects

  • Ethylene (ethene, C₂H₄) is a gas made by most plant tissues.
  • It triggers fruit ripening: softening, colour change and sugar production.
  • Climacteric fruits, such as bananas and tomatoes, show a burst of ethylene and respiration when ripening.
  • It promotes abscission (leaf and fruit fall) and senescence.

Commercial control

  • Ripening is a positive feedback process: ethylene triggers more ethylene.
  • Fruit is picked unripe and ripened later with ethylene gas.
  • Storage at low temperature, low O₂ and high CO₂ slows ripening.
  • Some products absorb ethylene to extend shelf life.

"One bad apple spoils the barrel" reflects ethylene's spread from ripening fruit to neighbours. Bananas are shipped green and ripened in ethylene rooms near shops. During leaf fall, ethylene stimulates enzymes that break down cell walls in the abscission zone, while falling auxin levels make the leaf more sensitive.

Worked example

Ripening a green banana.

  1. A green banana is placed in a paper bag with a ripe apple.
  2. The apple releases ethylene gas.
  3. The banana starts producing its own ethylene.
  4. It ripens faster than a banana left alone.
Common mistakeThinking all fruits ripen after picking. Non-climacteric fruits, such as grapes and strawberries, do not ripen much after harvest.

In 3 lines: A gaseous hormone. Ripening by positive feedback. Controlled storage.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What process does ethylene mainly trigger in fruit?
    • Germination
    • Stem elongation
    • Stomatal opening
    • Ripening
    Show the answer

    Answer: Ripening. Ethylene causes softening, colour change and sweetening.

  2. Why does ripening spread quickly through a box of fruit?
    • Ethylene from ripe fruit triggers ethylene production in others
    • Ripe fruit releases auxin
    • Unripe fruit absorbs all the ethylene
    • Cold air carries ripening genes
    Show the answer

    Answer: Ethylene from ripe fruit triggers ethylene production in others. Positive feedback spreads ripening between fruits.

  3. Why are bananas shipped green?
    • Green bananas cannot ripen
    • Unripe fruit is firmer and lasts longer during transport
    • Ripe bananas weigh more
    • Ethylene is banned in shipping
    Show the answer

    Answer: Unripe fruit is firmer and lasts longer during transport. They are ripened with ethylene near their destination.

Key terms

Ethylene
A gaseous plant hormone controlling ripening
Climacteric fruit
Fruit with a burst of ethylene and respiration during ripening
Abscission
The shedding of leaves, flowers or fruit
