Fatty Acids

Fatty acids have a carboxyl head and a hydrocarbon tail; saturated ones have no C=C bonds and straight chains, unsaturated ones have one or more C=C bonds that kink chains, lowering melting point and increasing membrane fluidity.

Biology · Biochemistry and Biomolecules · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the structure of a fatty acid
  • Distinguish saturated, monounsaturated and polyunsaturated fatty acids
  • Explain how fatty acid structure affects melting point and health

Structure

  • A fatty acid has a carboxyl group (–COOH) and a hydrocarbon tail.
  • Tails are typically 14–22 carbons long, often an even number.
  • The carboxyl group is polar; the tail is non-polar.

Saturated and unsaturated

  • Saturated: no C=C double bonds; the tail holds as many hydrogen atoms as possible; straight chains.
  • Unsaturated: one or more C=C double bonds.
  • Monounsaturated: one double bond; polyunsaturated: two or more.
  • Cis double bonds create kinks in the chain.

Kinked unsaturated chains cannot pack closely, so oils rich in unsaturated fatty acids are liquid at room temperature, while saturated fats pack tightly and are solid. In membranes, unsaturated fatty acids increase fluidity. Trans fats, mostly from industrial partial hydrogenation, have straighter chains and are linked to increased risk of heart disease. Some polyunsaturated fatty acids, such as omega-3 and omega-6, are essential and must come from the diet.

Worked example

Butter and olive oil.

  1. Butter is solid at room temperature because it is rich in saturated fatty acids.
  2. Their straight chains pack closely with strong attractions between them.
  3. Olive oil is liquid because it is rich in monounsaturated fatty acids.
  4. Kinks in the chains prevent close packing.
Common mistakeSaying saturated fats contain more double bonds. Saturated means no C=C double bonds.

In 3 lines: Carboxyl head, hydrocarbon tail. Saturated: no C=C; unsaturated: C=C. Kinks lower melting point.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What functional group is found at one end of every fatty acid?
    • An amino group
    • A carboxyl group
    • A phosphate group
    • A ketone group
    Show the answer

    Answer: A carboxyl group. Fatty acids have a –COOH group and a hydrocarbon tail.

  2. How many C=C double bonds does a polyunsaturated fatty acid have?
    • None
    • Exactly one
    • Two or more
    • Only in the carboxyl group
    Show the answer

    Answer: Two or more. Polyunsaturated fatty acids contain at least two double bonds.

  3. Why do membranes containing more unsaturated fatty acids stay more fluid in the cold?
    • Unsaturated fatty acids freeze at higher temperatures
    • They contain more hydrogen atoms
    • They are not present in membranes
    • Kinks stop phospholipids packing tightly
    Show the answer

    Answer: Kinks stop phospholipids packing tightly. Looser packing keeps the membrane fluid at low temperatures.

Key terms

Saturated fatty acid
A fatty acid with no C=C double bonds
Unsaturated fatty acid
A fatty acid with one or more C=C double bonds
Essential fatty acid
A fatty acid the body cannot make and must obtain from food
