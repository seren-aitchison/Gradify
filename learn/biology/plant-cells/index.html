Plant Cells

The cell wall, chloroplasts and large vacuole let plant cells photosynthesise and stay rigid through turgor pressure.

Biology · Cell Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the features that distinguish plant cells
  • Explain how the wall and vacuole keep a plant cell turgid
  • Explain how plasmodesmata connect plant cells

Three distinctive features

  • Cellulose cell wall: a strong, porous layer outside the membrane that resists stretching.
  • Chloroplasts: carry out photosynthesis (in green tissues).
  • Large permanent vacuole: filled with cell sap and surrounded by a membrane called the tonoplast; it can take up most of the cell's volume.

Plant cells also have a nucleus, mitochondria, ER, Golgi and ribosomes, like animal cells. Root cells have no chloroplasts, because they are in the dark.

Turgor: standing up with water

Water enters the vacuole by osmosis and pushes the cytoplasm against the wall. The wall pushes back, creating turgor pressure that makes the cell firm. Turgid cells pressing against each other hold up soft stems and leaves; when they lose water, the plant wilts.

Connected cells

Neighbouring plant cells are joined by plasmodesmata, channels through the walls lined by plasma membrane, through which water, sugars, signals and even some proteins pass directly from cell to cell.

Investigate with the lab. Switch to the plant cell and tap the cell wall, the large vacuole and a chloroplast. Compare each with the animal cell.

Common mistakeThinking the cell wall controls what enters the cell. The wall is fully permeable; it is the plasma membrane that is selective.

In 3 lines: Plant cells have a cellulose wall, chloroplasts and a large permanent vacuole. Turgor pressure, resisted by the wall, keeps cells and soft tissues firm. Plasmodesmata link the cytoplasm of neighbouring cells.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which structure is found in plant cells but not in animal cells?
    • Mitochondria
    • Ribosomes
    • Cellulose cell wall
    • Nucleus
    Show the answer

    Answer: Cellulose cell wall. Plant and animal cells both have mitochondria, ribosomes and a nucleus; only plants have a cellulose wall.

  2. A cell from a carrot root has a cell wall and a large vacuole but no chloroplasts. What does this show?
    • It is an animal cell
    • It is a bacterial cell
    • It must be dead
    • It is a plant cell from a non-photosynthetic tissue
    Show the answer

    Answer: It is a plant cell from a non-photosynthetic tissue. Chloroplasts are absent from plant tissues that are not exposed to light, such as roots.

  3. Why can a cell with a wall withstand being placed in pure water, when an animal cell cannot?
    • The wall resists expansion, so turgor pressure builds up until water stops entering
    • The wall is impermeable to water
    • Plant cells actively pump water out
    • The vacuole stores all the incoming water without swelling
    Show the answer

    Answer: The wall resists expansion, so turgor pressure builds up until water stops entering. As the cell swells, the wall pushes back; the rising pressure balances the tendency of water to enter, so the cell becomes turgid instead of bursting.

  4. Which three structures do plant cells have that animal cells lack?
    • Nucleus, cell membrane and ribosomes
    • Cell wall, large permanent vacuole and chloroplasts
    • Mitochondria, cytoplasm and lysosomes
    • Centrioles, cilia and a nucleolus
    Show the answer

    Answer: Cell wall, large permanent vacuole and chloroplasts. The cellulose wall, permanent vacuole and chloroplasts are features of plant cells.

Key terms

Cell wall
A rigid layer outside the plasma membrane; made of cellulose in plants
Turgor pressure
The pressure of a cell's contents against its wall, caused by water entering by osmosis
Plasmodesma
A channel through plant cell walls that connects the cytoplasm of neighbouring cells
