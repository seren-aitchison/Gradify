Allopatric Speciation

Allopatric speciation occurs when geographic barriers or colonisation separate populations, stopping gene flow so that selection, mutation and drift cause divergence and eventually reproductive isolation, as in snapping shrimp across the Isthmus of Panama.

Biology · Evolution and Natural Selection · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define allopatric speciation
  • Explain how geographic isolation leads to reproductive isolation
  • Describe examples of allopatric speciation

The process

  • A population is split by a geographic barrier, such as a mountain range, river or sea.
  • Or a small group colonises a new area, such as an island (peripatric speciation).
  • Gene flow stops between the populations.
  • Different selection pressures, mutations and drift cause divergence.
  • Over time, reproductive isolation develops.

Examples

  • Snapping shrimp on either side of the Isthmus of Panama, separated about 3 million years ago, now cannot interbreed.
  • Galápagos finches diverged on different islands.
  • Squirrels on either side of the Grand Canyon differ in appearance.

Allopatric speciation is thought to be the most common form of speciation in animals. Its key requirement is the interruption of gene flow, which lets populations diverge independently. If the barrier is later removed, the populations may no longer interbreed, confirming they are separate species, or they may hybridise if isolation is incomplete.

Worked example

The Isthmus of Panama.

  1. About 3 million years ago, the Isthmus of Panama rose and divided the ocean.
  2. Populations of snapping shrimp were split into Pacific and Caribbean groups.
  3. Each group evolved separately.
  4. When paired in the lab, shrimp from opposite sides rarely mate successfully.
Common mistakeThinking the barrier itself makes populations different. The barrier stops gene flow; divergence comes from evolutionary processes.

In 3 lines: Geographic separation. No gene flow, then divergence. Isthmus of Panama example.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which barrier could cause allopatric speciation?
    • A new mountain range dividing a population
    • Two species breeding at different times
    • Hybrid offspring being infertile
    • Different courtship songs
    Show the answer

    Answer: A new mountain range dividing a population. A physical barrier separates populations geographically.

  2. What happened to snapping shrimp separated by the Isthmus of Panama?
    • They became identical
    • They diverged and now rarely mate successfully
    • They merged into one population
    • They all became extinct
    Show the answer

    Answer: They diverged and now rarely mate successfully. Separation led to reproductive isolation.

  3. Why is allopatric speciation thought to be common in animals?
    • Animals never move between areas
    • Gene flow is impossible without barriers
    • Geographic barriers readily interrupt gene flow
    • It requires no evolution
    Show the answer

    Answer: Geographic barriers readily interrupt gene flow. Physical separation easily stops gene exchange.

Key terms

Allopatric speciation
Speciation after geographic separation
Geographic barrier
A physical feature preventing movement between populations
Peripatric speciation
Speciation from a small peripheral or founding population
