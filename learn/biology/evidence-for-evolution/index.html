Evidence for Evolution

Evidence for evolution includes the fossil record and transitional forms, homologous and vestigial structures, embryology, molecular similarities, biogeography and observed evolution, all consistent with common descent.

Biology · Evolution and Natural Selection · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe fossil and anatomical evidence for evolution
  • Describe molecular and biogeographical evidence
  • Explain how observed evolution supports the theory

Fossils and anatomy

  • The fossil record shows changes over time and transitional forms, such as Tiktaalik between fish and tetrapods.
  • Homologous structures: the pentadactyl limb in humans, bats and whales shares a common plan.
  • Vestigial structures: reduced structures with little function, such as the human tailbone.
  • Embryology: vertebrate embryos share features such as pharyngeal arches.

Molecules, geography and observation

  • Molecular evidence: similarities in DNA and protein sequences match relationships from anatomy.
  • Biogeography: species on islands resemble those on nearby mainlands, as with Galápagos finches.
  • Observed evolution: antibiotic resistance, pesticide resistance and changes in peppered moths.
  • All life shares the same genetic code and many core genes.

The strength of the evidence lies in its consistency: fossils, anatomy, molecules and geography independently support the same pattern of relationships. Predictions from evolution can be tested, such as finding transitional fossils in rocks of the predicted age. Experiments, such as Lenski's long-term evolution experiment with E. coli, show evolution happening in real time.

Worked example

A successful prediction.

  1. Evolutionary theory predicted fish-to-tetrapod transitional fossils in rocks about 375 million years old.
  2. Researchers searched rocks of that age in the Canadian Arctic.
  3. In 2004 they found Tiktaalik, with fish and tetrapod features.
  4. The prediction was confirmed.
Common mistakeSaying there is no evidence because transitional fossils are missing. Many transitional fossils, such as Tiktaalik, have been found.

In 3 lines: Fossils and anatomy. Molecules and geography. Evolution observed today.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which is an example of a homologous structure?
    • The wings of birds and of butterflies
    • The pentadactyl limb of humans, bats and whales
    • The eyes of octopuses and of humans
    • The fins of sharks and of dolphins
    Show the answer

    Answer: The pentadactyl limb of humans, bats and whales. Shared bone plans reflect common ancestry.

  2. How does biogeography support evolution?
    • All islands have identical species
    • Species are spread randomly across the world
    • Island species resemble those on the nearest mainland
    • Islands never have unique species
    Show the answer

    Answer: Island species resemble those on the nearest mainland. Colonisation and divergence explain island species.

  3. Why is the consistency between different lines of evidence important?
    • It shows only one type of evidence is needed
    • Consistency means the evidence is all fossils
    • It proves no further research is needed
    • Independent methods agreeing makes the conclusion very strong
    Show the answer

    Answer: Independent methods agreeing makes the conclusion very strong. Agreement across fossils, anatomy and DNA strengthens common descent.

Key terms

Homologous structure
A structure shared because of common ancestry
Vestigial structure
A reduced structure with little or no function
Transitional fossil
A fossil showing features of ancestral and descendant groups
