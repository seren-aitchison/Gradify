Interphase

In G₁ the cell grows and makes proteins and organelles, in S phase it replicates DNA and makes histones, and in G₂ it grows further, makes division proteins, duplicates centrosomes and checks DNA, preparing for mitosis.

Biology · Cell Division and Development · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe events in G₁, S and G₂
  • Explain why interphase is needed before division
  • Relate interphase activities to successful mitosis

G₁ and S

  • G₁: the cell grows, makes proteins and organelles and carries out normal functions.
  • Cells check conditions before committing to division at the G₁ checkpoint.
  • S phase: DNA is replicated, producing sister chromatids joined at centromeres.
  • Histones are made to package new DNA.

G₂

  • The cell continues to grow and makes proteins needed for division, such as tubulin for spindle fibres.
  • Centrosomes are duplicated in animal cells.
  • DNA is checked for errors at the G₂ checkpoint.
  • Energy stores (ATP) are built up.

Interphase ensures the cell has enough size, organelles and energy to make two functional daughter cells, and an accurate copy of its DNA. Chromosomes are uncondensed during interphase, as chromatin, so genes can be transcribed. The length of G₁ varies most between cell types and largely determines how often a cell divides.

Worked example

Preparing to divide.

  1. A skin cell grows and makes new mitochondria in G₁.
  2. It replicates all 46 chromosomes in S phase.
  3. In G₂, it makes tubulin and duplicates its centrosome.
  4. It then enters mitosis with everything needed for two daughter cells.
Common mistakeSaying chromosomes are visible as condensed structures during interphase. They are uncondensed chromatin until mitosis.

In 3 lines: G₁: growth. S: DNA replication. G₂: preparation.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which event takes place in S phase?
    • DNA replication
    • Cytokinesis
    • Separation of chromatids
    • Formation of the cell plate
    Show the answer

    Answer: DNA replication. S stands for synthesis: DNA is replicated in this phase of interphase.

  2. Why does a cell grow during G₁ and G₂?
    • So it can avoid dividing at all
    • So each daughter cell receives enough organelles and cytoplasm
    • So that DNA is destroyed
    • So the nucleus disappears permanently
    Show the answer

    Answer: So each daughter cell receives enough organelles and cytoplasm. Growth provides materials for two functional cells.

  3. Which part of interphase varies most in length between cell types?
    • S
    • G₂
    • G₁
    • M
    Show the answer

    Answer: G₁. G₁ length largely determines how often cells divide.

Key terms

G₁ phase
The first growth phase of interphase
G₂ phase
The second growth phase, preparing for mitosis
Centrosome
An organelle organising spindle microtubules in animal cells
