Gene Editing

After Cas cuts DNA, NHEJ often knocks out genes and HDR with a template makes precise edits; base and prime editors make changes without double-strand breaks, and gene editing is used in research, crops and therapies such as Casgevy, with risks including off-target edits and ethical concerns about germline editing.

Biology · Biotechnology and Genetic Engineering · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain how non-homologous end joining and homology-directed repair produce edits
  • Describe base editing and prime editing
  • Describe applications and risks of gene editing

Repair after a cut

  • Non-homologous end joining (NHEJ): the cell rejoins ends, often adding or deleting bases, which can knock out a gene.
  • Homology-directed repair (HDR): a supplied DNA template is copied, allowing precise changes or knock-ins.
  • NHEJ is common; HDR is less efficient.

Newer tools and uses

  • Base editors change single bases without cutting both strands.
  • Prime editors write small changes using a reverse transcriptase.
  • Uses: research models, crops, disease treatment.
  • Risks: off-target edits, mosaicism and unintended effects.
  • Germline editing of human embryos for reproduction is prohibited in the UK.

In 2023, the UK became the first country to approve a CRISPR-based therapy, Casgevy, for sickle cell disease and beta thalassaemia. It edits patients' blood stem cells to reactivate fetal haemoglobin. In 2018, He Jiankui edited human embryos, leading to births and worldwide condemnation, and he was imprisoned.

Worked example

Treating sickle cell disease.

  1. Blood stem cells are taken from a patient.
  2. CRISPR disrupts a gene that normally switches off fetal haemoglobin.
  3. The edited cells are returned after chemotherapy.
  4. They produce fetal haemoglobin, preventing sickling.
Common mistakeThinking gene editing is always perfectly precise. Off-target edits and unintended changes can occur.

In 3 lines: NHEJ and HDR. Base and prime editing. Therapies and risks.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which repair pathway often knocks out a gene?
    • Homology-directed repair
    • Base excision repair
    • Mismatch repair
    • Non-homologous end joining
    Show the answer

    Answer: Non-homologous end joining. Imprecise rejoining can add or delete bases.

  2. How does Casgevy treat sickle cell disease?
    • It reactivates fetal haemoglobin in edited stem cells
    • It replaces red blood cells with plastic
    • It removes the spleen
    • It kills all blood stem cells permanently
    Show the answer

    Answer: It reactivates fetal haemoglobin in edited stem cells. Fetal haemoglobin prevents red cells sickling.

  3. Why is germline editing of embryos for reproduction prohibited in the UK?
    • It is technically impossible
    • Changes would pass to future generations with unknown risks
    • It would only affect one cell
    • It cures all diseases
    Show the answer

    Answer: Changes would pass to future generations with unknown risks. Heritable edits raise serious safety and ethical concerns.

Key terms

NHEJ
A repair pathway rejoining broken DNA ends, often imprecisely
HDR
A repair pathway using a template for precise changes
Off-target edit
An unintended change at the wrong site
