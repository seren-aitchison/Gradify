Viral Evolution

RNA viruses evolve rapidly through high mutation rates, antigenic drift gradually changes surface proteins while antigenic shift creates new subtypes, and variants and zoonoses arise through mutation, selection and spillover from animals.

Biology · Microbiology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain why RNA viruses evolve rapidly
  • Distinguish antigenic drift and antigenic shift
  • Explain how variants and zoonoses arise

Mechanisms

  • RNA viruses have high mutation rates because RNA polymerases lack proofreading.
  • Large numbers and short replication times speed evolution.
  • Antigenic drift: gradual mutations change surface proteins, as in seasonal influenza.
  • Antigenic shift: reassortment of genome segments from different strains creates a new subtype, which can cause pandemics.

Variants and zoonoses

  • Variants have mutations that change transmissibility or immune escape, such as SARS-CoV-2 Alpha, Delta and Omicron.
  • Zoonoses are infections that spread from animals to humans.
  • Many emerging viruses, such as HIV, Ebola and SARS-CoV-2, are thought to have animal origins.
  • Selection by immunity and treatment favours escape mutations.

Influenza vaccines are updated each year because of antigenic drift. The 2009 H1N1 pandemic arose from reassortment of pig, bird and human influenza genes. Genomic surveillance, such as by the COVID-19 Genomics UK Consortium, tracks variants in real time. Coronaviruses have a proofreading enzyme, so they mutate more slowly than many RNA viruses.

Worked example

Why the flu vaccine changes.

  1. Each winter, influenza circulates and mutates.
  2. Changes in haemagglutinin let the virus partly evade immunity.
  3. Scientists predict which strains will dominate next season.
  4. The vaccine is updated to match them.
Common mistakeThinking viruses mutate in order to escape vaccines. Mutations are random; immunity selects for those that escape.

In 3 lines: High mutation rates. Drift and shift. Variants and zoonoses.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Why do RNA viruses mutate quickly?
    • They have no genome
    • They replicate very slowly
    • They only infect bacteria
    • Their polymerases lack proofreading
    Show the answer

    Answer: Their polymerases lack proofreading. Copying errors in RNA are not corrected because there is no proofreading.

  2. What causes antigenic shift in influenza?
    • Reassortment of genome segments from different strains
    • A single point mutation in one surface protein gene
    • Vaccination of hosts
    • Loss of the viral envelope
    Show the answer

    Answer: Reassortment of genome segments from different strains. Mixing genome segments from two strains creates a new viral subtype.

  3. Why must influenza vaccines be updated every year?
    • The virus stops mutating completely after each winter
    • Antigenic drift changes surface proteins, reducing immunity
    • Vaccines expire within a week
    • Influenza is caused by bacteria
    Show the answer

    Answer: Antigenic drift changes surface proteins, reducing immunity. Drift lets new strains partly evade existing immunity.

Key terms

Antigenic drift
Gradual change in viral surface proteins
Antigenic shift
Sudden change from reassortment of genome segments
Zoonosis
An infection spreading from animals to humans
