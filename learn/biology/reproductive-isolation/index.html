Reproductive Isolation

Prezygotic barriers (habitat, temporal, behavioural, mechanical and gametic) prevent fertilisation, while postzygotic barriers (hybrid inviability, sterility and breakdown) reduce hybrid success; reinforcement can strengthen prezygotic barriers, and isolation makes divergence permanent.

Biology · Evolution and Natural Selection · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe prezygotic isolating mechanisms
  • Describe postzygotic isolating mechanisms
  • Explain how reproductive isolation completes speciation

Prezygotic barriers

  • Habitat isolation: species live in different habitats.
  • Temporal isolation: breeding at different times, such as different flowering seasons.
  • Behavioural isolation: different courtship rituals or songs.
  • Mechanical isolation: incompatible reproductive structures.
  • Gametic isolation: sperm and eggs cannot fuse.

Postzygotic barriers

  • Hybrid inviability: hybrids do not develop or survive.
  • Hybrid sterility: hybrids survive but are infertile, as with mules.
  • Hybrid breakdown: first-generation hybrids are fertile but later generations are weak or sterile.

Prezygotic barriers prevent fertilisation, while postzygotic barriers act after a zygote forms. If hybrids are less fit, natural selection can strengthen prezygotic barriers, a process called reinforcement. Reproductive isolation is the key step that makes divergence permanent, because populations can no longer exchange genes even if they meet again.

Worked example

Temporal isolation in frogs.

  1. Two closely related frog species live in the same area.
  2. One breeds in early spring, the other in late spring.
  3. They rarely meet when ready to mate.
  4. They remain separate species.
Common mistakeThinking reproductive isolation requires a physical barrier. Behavioural or temporal differences alone can isolate species.

In 3 lines: Prezygotic: before fertilisation. Postzygotic: after fertilisation. Reinforcement strengthens barriers.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which is a prezygotic barrier?
    • Infertile hybrid offspring
    • Different courtship songs
    • Hybrids dying as embryos
    • Weak second-generation hybrids
    Show the answer

    Answer: Different courtship songs. Behavioural differences prevent mating before fertilisation.

  2. What is reinforcement in speciation?
    • Hybrids becoming more fertile over time
    • Two species merging into one
    • Selection strengthening prezygotic barriers when hybrids are unfit
    • Postzygotic barriers disappearing
    Show the answer

    Answer: Selection strengthening prezygotic barriers when hybrids are unfit. Selection against unfit hybrids favours avoiding interbreeding.

  3. Why does reproductive isolation make divergence permanent?
    • It makes populations identical
    • It increases gene flow
    • It stops all mutation
    • Populations can no longer exchange genes even if they meet
    Show the answer

    Answer: Populations can no longer exchange genes even if they meet. Without gene flow, differences are not undone.

Key terms

Prezygotic barrier
A barrier preventing fertilisation
Postzygotic barrier
A barrier reducing hybrid survival or fertility
Reinforcement
Strengthening of prezygotic barriers by selection against hybrids
