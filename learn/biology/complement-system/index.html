Complement System

Complement is a cascade of liver-made blood proteins activated by classical, lectin and alternative pathways that converge on C3, leading to opsonisation, inflammation and lysis by the membrane attack complex, with host cells protected by regulators.

Biology · Immunology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the three pathways of complement activation
  • Describe the effects of complement activation
  • Explain how complement is regulated

Activation

  • Complement consists of over 30 proteins made mainly by the liver.
  • Proteins circulate inactive and activate in a cascade.
  • Classical pathway: triggered by antibodies bound to antigen.
  • Lectin pathway: triggered by lectins binding sugars on microbes.
  • Alternative pathway: triggered directly on microbial surfaces.
  • All pathways converge on cleavage of C3.

Effects and regulation

  • Opsonisation: C3b coats pathogens for phagocytosis.
  • Inflammation: C3a and C5a attract and activate immune cells.
  • Lysis: the membrane attack complex (C5b–C9) forms pores in pathogen membranes.
  • Host cells carry regulatory proteins that block complement on their surfaces.

People lacking certain complement proteins are prone to infections with Neisseria bacteria, which cause meningitis. Over-activation of complement contributes to diseases such as atypical haemolytic uraemic syndrome, treated with eculizumab, a C5-blocking antibody. Complement also clears immune complexes and dead cells.

Worked example

The membrane attack complex.

  1. Complement is activated on a bacterium's surface.
  2. C5b binds C6, C7 and C8.
  3. Many C9 molecules join to form a ring-shaped pore.
  4. Water and ions flood in and the bacterium bursts.
Common mistakeThinking complement is a type of cell. It is a group of proteins in the blood.

In 3 lines: Three activation pathways. Opsonise, inflame, lyse. Regulated on host cells.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which complement pathway is triggered by antibodies?
    • The lectin pathway
    • The alternative pathway
    • The classical pathway
    • The terminal pathway
    Show the answer

    Answer: The classical pathway. Antibody bound to antigen starts the classical pathway.

  2. How does C3b help destroy pathogens?
    • It forms pores in their membranes directly
    • It blocks all inflammation
    • It makes antibodies
    • It coats them so phagocytes engulf them more easily
    Show the answer

    Answer: It coats them so phagocytes engulf them more easily. C3b is a powerful opsonin that phagocytes recognise through receptors.

  3. Why do people lacking terminal complement proteins get Neisseria infections?
    • They cannot form the membrane attack complex that kills these bacteria
    • They have no antibodies at all
    • They lack neutrophils
    • They have too much complement
    Show the answer

    Answer: They cannot form the membrane attack complex that kills these bacteria. Neisseria are especially vulnerable to complement lysis.

Key terms

Complement
A group of blood proteins aiding immune defence
Membrane attack complex
A pore formed by complement that lyses cells
C3b
A complement fragment that opsonises pathogens
