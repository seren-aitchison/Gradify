Cellular Respiration in Plants

Plants respire continuously, using ATP for growth and transport; at the compensation point photosynthesis equals respiration, in darkness only respiration occurs, and waterlogged roots respire anaerobically to ethanol.

Biology · Plant Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain why plants carry out respiration
  • Describe the compensation point
  • Describe anaerobic respiration in plants

Respiration in plants

  • Plants respire all the time, in every living cell, day and night.
  • Aerobic respiration: glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water, releasing ATP.
  • ATP is used for active transport, growth, protein synthesis and loading phloem.
  • Roots need oxygen from soil air spaces.

Balance with photosynthesis

  • In bright light, photosynthesis exceeds respiration: net O₂ release and CO₂ uptake.
  • At the compensation point, photosynthesis and respiration rates are equal.
  • In the dark, only respiration occurs: net CO₂ release.
  • In waterlogged soils, roots respire anaerobically, producing ethanol and CO₂.

Plants growing in shade have lower compensation points, so they can grow in less light. Rice and other wetland plants have air channels called aerenchyma that carry oxygen to submerged roots. Fruit stores reduce temperature and oxygen to slow respiration and prolong storage life.

Worked example

Hydrogencarbonate indicator.

  1. Leaves are placed in tubes with hydrogencarbonate indicator.
  2. In bright light, the indicator turns purple as CO₂ is removed.
  3. In darkness, it turns yellow as CO₂ is added.
  4. In dim light at the compensation point, it stays red.
Common mistakeThinking plants only respire at night. Plants respire all the time; in light, photosynthesis masks it.

In 3 lines: Plants respire constantly. Compensation point. Anaerobic in waterlogged soil.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. When do plants carry out respiration?
    • Only during the night
    • Only when it is sunny
    • All the time, day and night
    • Only in winter
    Show the answer

    Answer: All the time, day and night. Every living plant cell respires continuously.

  2. What happens to hydrogencarbonate indicator around leaves in darkness?
    • It turns purple as CO₂ decreases
    • It stays red
    • It becomes colourless
    • It turns yellow as CO₂ increases
    Show the answer

    Answer: It turns yellow as CO₂ increases. Without light there is no photosynthesis, so respiration adds CO₂.

  3. Why do shade plants have low compensation points?
    • They have low respiration rates and use dim light efficiently
    • They photosynthesise only at night
    • They do not respire
    • They need very bright light
    Show the answer

    Answer: They have low respiration rates and use dim light efficiently. A low compensation point allows growth in less light.

Key terms

Compensation point
Light intensity at which photosynthesis equals respiration
Aerenchyma
Tissue with air channels supplying oxygen to roots
Anaerobic respiration
Respiration without oxygen, producing ethanol in plants
