Gene Regulation

Gene regulation lets cells with identical DNA express different genes; it acts on chromatin, transcription through transcription factors and enhancers, RNA through interference and proteins through modifications such as phosphorylation.

Biology · Genetics and Molecular Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain why gene regulation is necessary
  • Describe regulation at the level of transcription
  • Describe regulation after transcription

Why and where

  • All body cells contain the same genes, but different cells express different sets, producing specialised cells.
  • Regulation also responds to signals, such as hormones and nutrients.
  • Levels include chromatin structure, transcription, RNA processing, translation and protein activity.

Transcriptional and post-transcriptional control

  • Transcription factors bind promoters and enhancers, activating or repressing transcription.
  • Steroid hormones such as oestrogen bind receptors that act as transcription factors.
  • RNA interference: small RNAs, such as siRNA and microRNA, bind mRNA and cause its breakdown or block translation.
  • Proteins can be activated or inactivated by modification, such as phosphorylation.

Gene regulation explains cell differentiation: a liver cell and a neurone have the same DNA but express different transcription factors. Faults in regulation can cause disease, such as cancers from overactive growth-promoting genes. Many genes are regulated by combinations of transcription factors, allowing precise control in different tissues and conditions.

Worked example

Oestrogen regulating genes.

  1. Oestrogen diffuses into a target cell.
  2. It binds the oestrogen receptor, which changes shape.
  3. The receptor-hormone complex binds DNA and acts as a transcription factor.
  4. Target genes are transcribed, changing the cell's activity.
Common mistakeSaying specialised cells contain different genes. They contain the same genes but express different ones.

In 3 lines: Same genes, different expression. Transcription factors control transcription. RNAi and protein modification act later.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Why do a liver cell and a neurone have different functions despite identical DNA?
    • They contain different genes
    • Neurones have no DNA
    • They express different sets of genes
    • Liver cells lack ribosomes
    Show the answer

    Answer: They express different sets of genes. Differential gene expression creates specialised cells.

  2. How do steroid hormones such as oestrogen regulate genes?
    • They destroy mRNA
    • They alter the DNA sequence
    • They block all transcription
    • Their receptors act as transcription factors
    Show the answer

    Answer: Their receptors act as transcription factors. Hormone-receptor complexes bind DNA to change transcription.

  3. Why can faults in gene regulation lead to cancer?
    • Growth-promoting genes may be overexpressed or growth-limiting genes silenced
    • Regulation has no effect on cell division
    • Cancer cells have no genes
    • Gene regulation only affects colour
    Show the answer

    Answer: Growth-promoting genes may be overexpressed or growth-limiting genes silenced. Loss of control over growth genes allows uncontrolled division.

Key terms

Transcription factor
A protein that controls transcription of genes
Enhancer
A DNA sequence that increases transcription when bound by factors
RNA interference
Silencing of genes by small RNAs
