Plant Vascular Tissue

Xylem carries water and minerals upwards and phloem carries sugars in both directions; their arrangement differs in roots, stems and leaves, and vascular tissue allowed plants to grow tall on land.

Biology · Plant Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the functions of xylem and phloem
  • Describe the arrangement of vascular tissue in roots, stems and leaves
  • Explain the evolutionary importance of vascular tissue

Xylem and phloem

  • Xylem carries water and minerals upwards from roots.
  • Phloem carries sugars and other organic substances in both directions.
  • Xylem is mostly dead cells with lignified walls; phloem is living.
  • Together they form vascular bundles.

Arrangement

  • In roots, xylem forms a central star or cross, with phloem between its arms.
  • In stems, bundles form a ring (eudicots) or are scattered (monocots).
  • In leaves, bundles form veins, with xylem on the upper side.
  • In woody plants, the vascular cambium produces new xylem and phloem.

Vascular tissue allowed plants to grow tall on land, because water and sugars could be transported over long distances and lignin provided support. Mosses lack true vascular tissue and stay small and in damp places. Ferns, conifers and flowering plants are all vascular plants.

Worked example

A celery experiment.

  1. A celery stalk is placed in water containing blue dye.
  2. After a few hours, a cross-section is cut.
  3. Blue dots appear in a ring near the edge.
  4. These are the xylem vessels in the vascular bundles.
Common mistakeThinking phloem only carries sugars downwards. Phloem carries sugars both up and down, from sources to sinks.

In 3 lines: Xylem: water up. Phloem: sugars both ways. Allowed plants to grow tall.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which tissue transports sugars in plants?
    • Xylem
    • Epidermis
    • Cuticle
    • Phloem
    Show the answer

    Answer: Phloem. Phloem carries sugars from where they are made to where they are needed.

  2. How is vascular tissue arranged in a root?
    • Xylem forms a central star with phloem between its arms
    • Bundles form a ring near the edge
    • Bundles are scattered throughout
    • Xylem forms the outer layer
    Show the answer

    Answer: Xylem forms a central star with phloem between its arms. Roots have a central vascular cylinder with xylem in a star shape.

  3. Why did vascular tissue allow plants to grow taller?
    • It removed the need for water
    • It transported water and sugars far and lignin gave support
    • It made plants lighter
    • It allowed photosynthesis in roots
    Show the answer

    Answer: It transported water and sugars far and lignin gave support. Long-distance transport and strength enabled height.

Key terms

Xylem
Tissue transporting water and minerals
Phloem
Tissue transporting sugars and organic substances
Vascular plant
A plant with xylem and phloem
