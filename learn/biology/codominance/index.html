Codominance

In codominance both alleles are fully expressed, as in roan cattle and AB blood; the ABO gene has three alleles, with Iᴬ and Iᴮ codominant and both dominant to Iᴼ, so A and B parents can have children of all four groups.

Biology · Genetics and Molecular Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define codominance
  • Explain the ABO blood group system as codominance and multiple alleles
  • Predict offspring blood groups from parental genotypes

What it is

  • Both alleles are fully expressed in the heterozygote, rather than blended.
  • Example: in cattle, red (CᴿCᴿ) and white (CᵂCᵂ) produce roan (CᴿCᵂ), with both red and white hairs.
  • Example: MN blood groups, where heterozygotes have both M and N antigens.

ABO blood groups

  • The ABO gene has three alleles: Iᴬ, Iᴮ and Iᴼ (multiple alleles).
  • Iᴬ and Iᴮ are codominant; both are dominant to Iᴼ.
  • Genotypes: IᴬIᴬ or IᴬIᴼ = A; IᴮIᴮ or IᴮIᴼ = B; IᴬIᴮ = AB; IᴼIᴼ = O.
  • Iᴬ and Iᴮ code for enzymes that add different sugars to red cell surface antigens.

Codominance differs from incomplete dominance: in codominance both products are present separately, while in incomplete dominance a single intermediate product appears. Blood group inheritance was once used in paternity questions, as some combinations are impossible: two group O parents cannot have a group A child. Sickle cell trait shows codominance at the molecular level, as heterozygotes make both normal and sickle haemoglobin.

Worked example

Predicting blood groups.

  1. Parents: IᴬIᴼ (group A) and IᴮIᴼ (group B).
  2. Gametes: Iᴬ or Iᴼ, and Iᴮ or Iᴼ.
  3. Offspring: IᴬIᴮ (AB), IᴬIᴼ (A), IᴮIᴼ (B), IᴼIᴼ (O).
  4. Each blood group has a 1 in 4 chance.
Common mistakeConfusing codominance with incomplete dominance. Codominance shows both phenotypes; incomplete dominance shows an intermediate one.

In 3 lines: Both alleles expressed. ABO: three alleles. Iᴬ and Iᴮ codominant.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which blood group results from the genotype IᴼIᴼ?
    • Group A
    • Group O
    • Group B
    • Group AB
    Show the answer

    Answer: Group O. Two Iᴼ alleles give no A or B antigens, so group O.

  2. Can two group O parents have a group A child?
    • Yes, if both parents carry Iᴬ
    • Yes, always in one child of four
    • No, because neither parent has an Iᴬ allele
    • Only if the child is male
    Show the answer

    Answer: No, because neither parent has an Iᴬ allele. Group O parents are both IᴼIᴼ, so all children are group O.

  3. How does codominance differ from incomplete dominance?
    • Codominance always gives a 3:1 ratio
    • In codominance one allele is silent
    • Incomplete dominance shows both phenotypes side by side
    • Both phenotypes appear separately rather than an intermediate
    Show the answer

    Answer: Both phenotypes appear separately rather than an intermediate. Roan cattle show both colours; pink snapdragons show an intermediate.

Key terms

Codominance
Both alleles fully expressed in a heterozygote
Multiple alleles
More than two alleles of a gene in a population
Antigen
A molecule, such as a cell surface marker, recognised by the immune system
