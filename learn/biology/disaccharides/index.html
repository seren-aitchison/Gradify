Disaccharides

Disaccharides form by condensation with a glycosidic bond and are hydrolysed by enzymes; maltose is glucose + glucose, sucrose glucose + fructose and lactose glucose + galactose, and sucrose is non-reducing, detected after acid hydrolysis.

Biology · Biochemistry and Biomolecules · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the formation and breakdown of glycosidic bonds
  • Name the monomers of maltose, sucrose and lactose
  • Explain how to test for reducing and non-reducing sugars

Formation

  • Two monosaccharides join by a condensation reaction, releasing water and forming a glycosidic bond.
  • An α-1,4 glycosidic bond links carbon 1 of one α-glucose to carbon 4 of another.
  • Disaccharides are broken by hydrolysis, catalysed by enzymes such as maltase, sucrase and lactase.

Examples

  • Maltose: glucose + glucose (α-1,4); from starch digestion.
  • Sucrose: glucose + fructose (α-1,β-2); transported in phloem; table sugar.
  • Lactose: glucose + galactose (β-1,4); found in milk.
  • Maltose and lactose are reducing sugars; sucrose is non-reducing.

Sucrose is non-reducing because the bond involves the carbonyl carbons of both monomers, leaving no free reducing group. To detect it, first boil with dilute hydrochloric acid to hydrolyse it, neutralise with sodium hydrogencarbonate, then perform Benedict's test: a positive result after hydrolysis indicates a non-reducing sugar. Lactose intolerance occurs when adults produce little lactase, so lactose reaches the colon and is fermented by bacteria.

Worked example

Testing a sugar solution.

  1. A sample gives a negative Benedict's test: it stays blue.
  2. After boiling with dilute HCl and neutralising, Benedict's turns brick red.
  3. So the sample contained a non-reducing sugar, such as sucrose.
  4. Acid hydrolysis released glucose and fructose, which are reducing.
Common mistakeConcluding that a negative Benedict's test means no sugar. A non-reducing sugar may be present; test again after acid hydrolysis.

In 3 lines: Condensation forms glycosidic bonds. Maltose, sucrose, lactose. Sucrose is non-reducing.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which disaccharide is made of glucose and galactose?
    • Maltose
    • Lactose
    • Sucrose
    • Cellobiose
    Show the answer

    Answer: Lactose. Lactose, the sugar in milk, is glucose plus galactose.

  2. What is produced when a glycosidic bond forms by condensation?
    • Carbon dioxide
    • Oxygen
    • Water
    • Ammonia
    Show the answer

    Answer: Water. Condensation releases one water molecule for each bond formed.

  3. Why must the solution be neutralised before repeating Benedict's test?
    • Acid makes the sugar disappear
    • Neutralising adds glucose
    • Sodium hydrogencarbonate is a reducing sugar
    • Benedict's reagent works in alkaline conditions
    Show the answer

    Answer: Benedict's reagent works in alkaline conditions. Benedict's test needs alkaline conditions to work.

Key terms

Glycosidic bond
A covalent bond joining two sugar units
Disaccharide
A sugar made of two monosaccharides
Non-reducing sugar
A sugar without a free reducing group, such as sucrose
