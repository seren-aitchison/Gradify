Chromosomal Abnormalities

Non-disjunction in meiosis produces gametes with extra or missing chromosomes, causing aneuploidies such as trisomy 21, Turner and Klinefelter syndromes; polyploidy adds whole sets, and structural changes include deletions, duplications, inversions and translocations.

Biology · Genetics and Molecular Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain how non-disjunction causes changes in chromosome number
  • Describe examples of aneuploidy in humans
  • Describe structural chromosome changes

Number changes

  • Non-disjunction: chromosomes or chromatids fail to separate in meiosis, producing gametes with an extra or missing chromosome.
  • Aneuploidy: an abnormal number of one chromosome.
  • Down syndrome: trisomy 21 (three copies of chromosome 21); risk increases with maternal age.
  • Turner syndrome: 45,X; Klinefelter syndrome: 47,XXY.
  • Polyploidy: whole extra sets, common in plants such as wheat.

Structural changes

  • Deletion: loss of a section.
  • Duplication: repeated section.
  • Inversion: a section reversed.
  • Translocation: a section moves to another chromosome, as in the Philadelphia chromosome in chronic myeloid leukaemia.

Most autosomal aneuploidies are lethal before birth, which is why only trisomies of small chromosomes such as 21, 18 and 13 are seen in live births. Prenatal screening and diagnosis, such as non-invasive prenatal testing and amniocentesis, can detect aneuploidy. Polyploidy in plants can create new species and is used in crop breeding.

Worked example

Non-disjunction in meiosis I.

  1. Chromosome 21 homologues fail to separate in meiosis I.
  2. Two gametes receive two copies; two receive none.
  3. A gamete with two copies is fertilised by a normal gamete.
  4. The zygote has three copies: trisomy 21.
Common mistakeThinking Down syndrome is inherited from an affected parent in most cases. It usually arises from non-disjunction during gamete formation.

In 3 lines: Non-disjunction causes aneuploidy. Trisomy 21, Turner, Klinefelter. Structural changes alter chromosomes.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is non-disjunction?
    • Exchange of sections between chromatids
    • Copying DNA before division begins
    • Failure of chromosomes or chromatids to separate
    • Joining of two gametes at fertilisation
    Show the answer

    Answer: Failure of chromosomes or chromatids to separate. Non-disjunction gives gametes with the wrong chromosome number.

  2. Which chromosomal change is linked to chronic myeloid leukaemia?
    • Trisomy of chromosome 21
    • Loss of the X chromosome
    • Duplication of the Y chromosome
    • A translocation forming the Philadelphia chromosome
    Show the answer

    Answer: A translocation forming the Philadelphia chromosome. The Philadelphia translocation fuses genes on chromosomes 9 and 22.

  3. Why are trisomies of only a few small chromosomes seen in live births?
    • Most other aneuploidies are lethal before birth
    • Large chromosomes never undergo non-disjunction
    • Only small chromosomes carry genes
    • Trisomy cannot occur in large chromosomes
    Show the answer

    Answer: Most other aneuploidies are lethal before birth. Extra copies of large chromosomes disrupt development too severely.

Key terms

Non-disjunction
Failure of chromosomes to separate correctly in division
Aneuploidy
An abnormal number of a particular chromosome
Translocation
Movement of a chromosome section to another chromosome
