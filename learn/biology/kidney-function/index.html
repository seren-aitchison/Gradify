Kidney Function

The nephron filters blood at the glomerulus by ultrafiltration, reabsorbs glucose, water and salts in the PCT, uses the loop of Henle to concentrate urine and adjusts water reabsorption in the collecting duct under ADH control.

Biology · Human Anatomy and Physiology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the structure of the nephron
  • Explain ultrafiltration and selective reabsorption
  • Explain how ADH controls water reabsorption

Structure

  • Bowman's capsule surrounds a knot of capillaries called the glomerulus.
  • Proximal convoluted tubule (PCT).
  • Loop of Henle, dipping into the medulla.
  • Distal convoluted tubule (DCT) and collecting duct.

Processes

  • Ultrafiltration: high pressure in the glomerulus forces water, glucose, salts and urea into Bowman's capsule; cells and proteins stay in the blood.
  • Selective reabsorption: in the PCT, all glucose and most water and salts return to the blood.
  • The loop of Henle creates a salt gradient in the medulla, allowing concentrated urine.
  • ADH (antidiuretic hormone) makes the collecting duct more permeable to water via aquaporins.

The kidneys filter about 180 L of fluid each day, but produce only about 1.5 L of urine, so around 99% is reabsorbed. If blood glucose is very high, as in untreated diabetes, the transporters in the PCT are saturated and glucose appears in urine. Alcohol inhibits ADH release, increasing urine volume and contributing to dehydration.

Worked example

ADH and dehydration.

  1. After heavy sweating, blood water potential falls.
  2. Osmoreceptors in the hypothalamus detect this, and the pituitary releases more ADH.
  3. More aquaporins are inserted in collecting duct walls.
  4. More water is reabsorbed, producing small volumes of concentrated urine.
Common mistakeThinking glucose in urine is normal. All glucose is normally reabsorbed; its presence suggests diabetes.

In 3 lines: Glomerulus filters. PCT reabsorbs. ADH controls water.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which substance is normally completely reabsorbed in the PCT?
    • Urea
    • Glucose
    • Excess water
    • Excess salts
    Show the answer

    Answer: Glucose. Healthy kidneys return all filtered glucose.

  2. Why do proteins not pass into Bowman's capsule?
    • They are actively pumped back
    • They are broken down in the glomerulus
    • They are too large to pass through the filter
    • They are reabsorbed in the collecting duct
    Show the answer

    Answer: They are too large to pass through the filter. The filtration barrier blocks large molecules.

  3. Why does drinking alcohol increase urine volume?
    • It increases ADH release
    • It blocks ultrafiltration
    • It makes urine more concentrated
    • It inhibits ADH release, so less water is reabsorbed
    Show the answer

    Answer: It inhibits ADH release, so less water is reabsorbed. Less ADH means fewer aquaporins and more urine.

Key terms

Ultrafiltration
Filtration of blood under high pressure at the glomerulus
Selective reabsorption
Return of useful substances to the blood
ADH
A hormone increasing water reabsorption in the kidney
