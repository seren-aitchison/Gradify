Chromatography

Chromatography separates components between mobile and stationary phases; paper and TLC give Rf values, while column methods separate by size, charge or binding, and HPLC gives fast precise separation.

Biology · Experimental Biology and Research Methods · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain the principle of chromatography
  • Calculate and interpret Rf values
  • Describe column chromatography methods for proteins

Principle

  • A mobile phase moves through a stationary phase.
  • Components separate by how strongly they interact with each phase.
  • Paper and thin-layer chromatography (TLC) separate small molecules such as pigments and amino acids.
  • Rf value = distance moved by spot ÷ distance moved by solvent front.

Column methods

  • Size-exclusion: separates by size.
  • Ion-exchange: separates by charge.
  • Affinity chromatography: separates by specific binding.
  • HPLC uses high pressure for fast, precise separation.

Chlorophyll and carotenoid pigments from leaves can be separated by TLC, each with a characteristic Rf value. Amino acids are colourless, so they are revealed with ninhydrin spray. Rf values depend on the solvent and stationary phase, so conditions must be stated.

Worked example

Separating leaf pigments.

  1. Leaf extract is spotted on a TLC plate.
  2. The solvent front moves 8.0 cm.
  3. A yellow carotene spot moves 7.6 cm.
  4. Rf = 7.6 ÷ 8.0 = 0.95.
Common mistakeThinking Rf values are fixed for a substance under all conditions. They depend on the solvent and stationary phase.

In 3 lines: Two phases separate. Rf values. Column methods.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which column method separates proteins by charge?
    • Size-exclusion chromatography
    • Paper chromatography
    • Ion-exchange chromatography
    • Thin-layer chromatography
    Show the answer

    Answer: Ion-exchange chromatography. Charged proteins bind oppositely charged resins.

  2. Why must solvent conditions be stated with Rf values?
    • Rf is the same in every solvent
    • Solvents do not affect separation
    • Rf measures temperature
    • Rf depends on the solvent and stationary phase
    Show the answer

    Answer: Rf depends on the solvent and stationary phase. Changing phases changes how far spots move.

  3. Why does affinity chromatography give very pure proteins?
    • Only the target binds specifically to the column
    • It separates by molecular size only
    • It removes all proteins
    • It uses no stationary phase
    Show the answer

    Answer: Only the target binds specifically to the column. Specific binding captures the target selectively.

Key terms

Mobile phase
The moving solvent in chromatography
Stationary phase
The fixed material in chromatography
Rf value
Distance moved by a spot divided by the solvent front distance
