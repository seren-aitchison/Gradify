Translation

Ribosomes have small and large subunits with A, P and E sites; translation starts at AUG with methionine, elongates as tRNAs pair with codons and peptide bonds form, and ends at stop codons UAA, UAG or UGA, with polysomes producing many copies.

Biology · Genetics and Molecular Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the structure of the ribosome and its sites
  • Explain initiation, elongation and termination of translation
  • Explain the roles of mRNA, tRNA and ribosomes

Ribosomes

  • Ribosomes have a small subunit and a large subunit, made of rRNA and proteins.
  • Prokaryotes have 70S ribosomes; eukaryotes have 80S in the cytoplasm.
  • Ribosomes have three tRNA sites: A (aminoacyl), P (peptidyl) and E (exit).

Stages

  • Initiation: the small subunit binds mRNA; the initiator tRNA carrying methionine pairs with the start codon AUG; the large subunit joins.
  • Elongation: a tRNA with a matching anticodon enters the A site; a peptide bond forms; the ribosome moves along by one codon.
  • Termination: a stop codon (UAA, UAG or UGA) enters the A site; a release factor frees the polypeptide.

Several ribosomes can translate one mRNA at the same time, forming a polysome, which produces many copies of a protein quickly. Ribosomes free in the cytoplasm make proteins used in the cell, while those on the rough endoplasmic reticulum make proteins for secretion or membranes. Many antibiotics, such as tetracycline and streptomycin, target bacterial 70S ribosomes.

Worked example

Building a short peptide.

  1. mRNA: AUG GCU UGG UAA.
  2. AUG brings methionine; GCU brings alanine; UGG brings tryptophan.
  3. Peptide bonds form between them in order.
  4. UAA is a stop codon, so translation ends and the tripeptide Met-Ala-Trp is released.
Common mistakeSaying stop codons code for an amino acid. They signal the end of translation and have no matching tRNA.

In 3 lines: A, P and E sites. Start at AUG; stop at stop codons. Polysomes speed production.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which amino acid is carried by the initiator tRNA?
    • Alanine
    • Methionine
    • Glycine
    • Tryptophan
    Show the answer

    Answer: Methionine. AUG codes for methionine, the first amino acid.

  2. What happens when a stop codon enters the A site?
    • A new tRNA brings an amino acid
    • The ribosome moves backwards
    • A release factor frees the polypeptide
    • A peptide bond forms with the stop codon
    Show the answer

    Answer: A release factor frees the polypeptide. Stop codons are recognised by release factors, not tRNAs.

  3. Why can antibiotics such as streptomycin target bacteria without harming human ribosomes?
    • Human cells have no ribosomes for the drug to bind
    • Bacterial ribosomes are made of DNA, not RNA
    • Streptomycin acts only on bacterial cell walls
    • Bacterial 70S ribosomes differ in structure from human 80S ribosomes
    Show the answer

    Answer: Bacterial 70S ribosomes differ in structure from human 80S ribosomes. Structural differences allow selective inhibition.

Key terms

Ribosome
The site of translation, made of rRNA and proteins
Start codon
AUG, which begins translation
Stop codon
UAA, UAG or UGA, which end translation
