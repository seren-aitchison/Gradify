Animal Cells

Animal cells have no wall or chloroplasts; their flexible membranes allow movement and engulfing, but make them vulnerable to bursting in dilute solutions.

Biology · Cell Biology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Identify the main structures of an animal cell and state their functions
  • Explain how the lack of a cell wall affects animal cells
  • Relate cell structure to function in a named animal cell

The structures of an animal cell

  • Nucleus: contains the DNA and controls the cell.
  • Plasma membrane: controls what enters and leaves.
  • Cytoplasm: the cytosol and organelles where most reactions take place.
  • Mitochondria: aerobic respiration, making ATP.
  • Rough and smooth endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi apparatus: make, modify and dispatch proteins and lipids.
  • Lysosomes: digest worn-out parts and engulfed material.
  • Centrosome with a pair of centrioles: organises microtubules, including the spindle in cell division.

Life without a wall

Support comes instead from the extracellular matrix, a network of proteins such as collagen and fibronectin secreted around cells, and from the cytoskeleton inside them. Cells attach to the matrix through membrane proteins called integrins.

Without a rigid wall, animal cells can change shape, crawl (white blood cells), contract (muscle) and engulf particles by phagocytosis. The cost is vulnerability to osmosis: in pure water an animal cell swells and bursts, so animals regulate the water and salt content of the fluid around their cells.

Structure fits function

A pancreatic acinar cell, which secretes digestive enzymes, is packed with rough ER and a large Golgi apparatus. A muscle cell is full of mitochondria and contractile protein filaments. The same basic plan is adjusted to each job.

Investigate with the lab. Tap every organelle in the animal cell and note its job. Then find the three structures a plant cell has that an animal cell lacks.

Common mistakeDrawing a large central vacuole in an animal cell. Animal cells have small vesicles and vacuoles, not one large permanent vacuole.

In 3 lines: Animal cells have a nucleus, mitochondria, ER, Golgi, lysosomes and centrioles, but no wall or chloroplasts. Flexibility allows movement and phagocytosis; osmotic balance must be controlled. The proportions of organelles reflect each cell's job.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which organelle carries out aerobic respiration in an animal cell?
    • Ribosome
    • Golgi apparatus
    • Centriole
    • Mitochondrion
    Show the answer

    Answer: Mitochondrion. Mitochondria carry out the later stages of aerobic respiration and make most of the cell's ATP.

  2. A cell secretes large amounts of protein hormone. Which pair of organelles would you expect to be especially extensive?
    • Rough endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi apparatus
    • Lysosomes and centrioles
    • Smooth ER and peroxisomes
    • Chloroplasts and vacuoles
    Show the answer

    Answer: Rough endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi apparatus. Secreted proteins are made on rough ER and processed and packaged by the Golgi before exocytosis.

  3. White blood cells can engulf bacteria but plant cells cannot. What best explains the difference?
    • Plant cells have no membrane
    • Animal cells lack a rigid cell wall, so their membrane can extend around particles
    • Plant cells have no lysosomes or vesicles at all
    • White blood cells contain chloroplasts
    Show the answer

    Answer: Animal cells lack a rigid cell wall, so their membrane can extend around particles. Phagocytosis needs a flexible membrane that can wrap around a particle; a plant's cellulose wall prevents this.

  4. Which structure is found in animal cells but not in plant cells?
    • A nucleus enclosed in an envelope
    • Mitochondria for respiration
    • Centrioles in a centrosome
    • Ribosomes for protein synthesis
    Show the answer

    Answer: Centrioles in a centrosome. Animal cells have centrioles; plant cells also have nuclei, mitochondria and ribosomes.

Key terms

Centriole
A small cylinder of microtubules, found in pairs in the animal centrosome
Cytosol
The fluid part of the cytoplasm, outside the organelles
