Lipids

Lipids are non-polar, insoluble in water and include triglycerides, phospholipids, steroids and waxes; they store energy, form membranes, insulate, waterproof, act as hormones and release metabolic water, and are detected by the emulsion test.

Biology · Biochemistry and Biomolecules · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the general properties of lipids
  • Identify the main classes of lipid
  • Describe the functions of lipids in organisms

Properties and classes

  • Lipids are non-polar or largely non-polar, so they are insoluble in water but soluble in organic solvents.
  • Contain carbon, hydrogen and oxygen, with less oxygen than carbohydrates.
  • Main classes: triglycerides (fats and oils), phospholipids, steroids and waxes.
  • Not polymers: they are not made of repeating monomers.

Functions

  • Energy storage: triglycerides release about twice as much energy per gram as carbohydrates.
  • Membranes: phospholipids and cholesterol.
  • Insulation and protection: subcutaneous fat and fat around organs.
  • Waterproofing: waxy cuticles on leaves.
  • Hormones: steroid hormones such as testosterone and oestrogen.
  • Metabolic water: oxidation of fats releases water, used by desert animals.

The emulsion test detects lipids: shake the sample with ethanol, then add water; a cloudy white emulsion shows lipid is present. The high energy density of lipids comes from their many C–H bonds and low oxygen content. Lipids also provide buoyancy in aquatic animals and form myelin sheaths around neurones, which speed nerve impulses.

Worked example

The emulsion test.

  1. A sample of crushed nuts is shaken with ethanol.
  2. The ethanol is poured into water.
  3. A milky white emulsion forms.
  4. This shows that lipid is present in the nuts.
Common mistakeCalling lipids polymers. They are not made of repeating monomers like polysaccharides and proteins.

In 3 lines: Non-polar and insoluble. Triglycerides, phospholipids, steroids, waxes. Energy, membranes, insulation, hormones.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Why are lipids insoluble in water?
    • They contain many ions
    • They are very small molecules
    • They are polymers of glucose
    • They are largely non-polar
    Show the answer

    Answer: They are largely non-polar. Non-polar molecules do not interact well with polar water.

  2. Which lipid function is shown by a waxy leaf cuticle?
    • Waterproofing
    • Energy storage
    • Hormone signalling
    • Membrane formation
    Show the answer

    Answer: Waterproofing. Waxes reduce water loss from leaf surfaces.

  3. How do camels benefit from fat stored in their humps?
    • Fat stores water directly
    • Oxidising fat releases energy and metabolic water
    • Fat cools the body by evaporation
    • Fat makes blood less salty
    Show the answer

    Answer: Oxidising fat releases energy and metabolic water. Fat oxidation produces water as well as energy.

Key terms

Lipid
A non-polar biological molecule insoluble in water
Emulsion test
A test for lipids using ethanol and water
Metabolic water
Water released by oxidation of food molecules
