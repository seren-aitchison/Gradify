Differential Calculus

The derivative is the limit of chord gradients: the tangent's slope and the instantaneous rate of change.

Mathematics · Calculus · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Interpret the derivative as a gradient and a rate of change
  • Estimate gradients using chords

Gradients of curves

  • A straight line has the same gradient everywhere; a curve's gradient changes.
  • The gradient at a point is the gradient of the tangent there.
  • A chord from (x, f(x)) to (x + h, f(x + h)) has gradient (f(x + h) − f(x))/h. As h → 0, it approaches the tangent's gradient: the derivative.

Rates of change

  • If s is distance and t is time, ds/dt is velocity; dv/dt is acceleration.
  • In economics, marginal cost is dC/dq; in biology, growth rates are dP/dt.
  • Notation: dy/dx (Leibniz), f′(x) (Lagrange), ẏ for time derivatives (Newton).
  • A positive derivative means increasing; negative means decreasing; zero means a stationary point.
Worked example

Estimate the gradient of y = x2 at x = 3 using chords with h = 0.1 and h = 0.01.

  1. h = 0.1: (3.12 − 9)/0.1 = (9.61 − 9)/0.1 = 6.1
  2. h = 0.01: (3.012 − 9)/0.01 = 0.0601/0.01 = 6.01
  3. The estimates approach 6, the derivative 2x at x = 3

Investigate with the lab. Try three different functions and, for each, find where the secant and tangent gradients agree to two decimal places.

Common mistakeConfusing the value of a function with its rate of change: y can be large while dy/dx is small.

In 2 lines: The derivative measures instantaneous rate of change. Chord gradients approach it as h → 0.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What does a negative derivative tell you about a function?
    • It's negative at that point
    • It has a maximum
    • It's increasing
    • It's decreasing at that point
    • It's zero
    Show the answer

    Answer: It's decreasing at that point. The derivative is the gradient: negative gradient means the function is going down.

  2. A chord of y = f(x) from x = 2 to x = 2 + h has gradient 5 + 3h. What is f′(2)?
    • 8
    • 3
    • 5 + 3h
    • 0
    • 5
    Show the answer

    Answer: 5. The derivative is the limit as h → 0: 5 + 3h → 5.

  3. What does the derivative measure at a point?
    • The instantaneous rate of change: the limit of secant gradients as h → 0
    • The area under the curve up to that point
    • The average value of the function over an interval
    • The value of the function itself at the point
    • The distance from the point to the x-axis
    Show the answer

    Answer: The instantaneous rate of change: the limit of secant gradients as h → 0. The derivative is defined as the limit of the difference quotient.

Key terms

Derivative
The instantaneous rate of change of a function, dy/dx or f′(x)
Chord
A straight line joining two points on a curve; its gradient approximates the tangent's
