Optimisation Algorithms

ML training uses gradient-based optimisers; momentum accelerates; Adam adapts step sizes; learning-rate schedules matter greatly; second-order methods help in some settings.

Mathematics · Mathematics for Machine Learning and AI · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe gradient descent, momentum and adaptive methods
  • Explain learning-rate schedules and convergence behaviour

First-order methods

  • Gradient descent: w ← w − η∇L.
  • Momentum and Nesterov acceleration damp oscillations.
  • AdaGrad, RMSProp and Adam adapt step sizes per parameter.
  • AdamW decouples weight decay.

Learning rates

  • Too large: divergence; too small: slow training.
  • Schedules: step decay, cosine annealing, warm-up.
  • Learning-rate range tests to choose η.

Other methods

  • Coordinate descent for lasso.
  • L-BFGS for smooth, moderate-sized problems.
  • Second-order approximations (K-FAC, Shampoo).
  • Convexity guarantees global optima; neural losses are non-convex.
Worked example

With momentum β = 0.9 and η = 0.1, a constant gradient of 1 is applied for three steps from v = 0. Find v after three steps.

  1. v₁ = 0.9 × 0 + 1 = 1
  2. v₂ = 0.9 × 1 + 1 = 1.9
  3. v₃ = 0.9 × 1.9 + 1 = 2.71: momentum builds up, approaching 1/(1 − 0.9) = 10

Investigate with the lab. Compare several learning rates on the narrow valley: too small is slow, too large zigzags or diverges, and a well-chosen rate converges smoothly.

Common mistakeKeeping a fixed large learning rate throughout training, which prevents fine convergence.

In 2 lines: Optimisers drive training. Learning rates matter most.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which optimiser adapts a separate step size for each parameter?
    • Adam
    • Plain gradient descent
    • Newton's method with fixed steps
    • Bisection
    • Simulated annealing
    Show the answer

    Answer: Adam. Adam scales steps by running estimates of squared gradients.

  2. Why is cosine annealing of the learning rate helpful near the end of training?
    • Larger steps near the end always help the optimiser escape bad regions
    • Smaller steps let the optimiser settle into a minimum instead of bouncing around it
    • It removes the need to compute gradients in the final epochs of training
    • It increases the training loss deliberately to prevent overfitting
    • It isn't helpful; a constant learning rate always converges faster
    Show the answer

    Answer: Smaller steps let the optimiser settle into a minimum instead of bouncing around it. Decaying η reduces noise-driven oscillation as training converges.

  3. What does the narrow valley in the lab show about choosing a learning rate?
    • Any rate works equally well
    • Larger rates are always better
    • One fixed rate must suit both the steep and the shallow directions, which forces a compromise
    • The learning rate doesn't matter
    • Only negative rates converge
    Show the answer

    Answer: One fixed rate must suit both the steep and the shallow directions, which forces a compromise. Ill-conditioning is why adaptive methods like Adam rescale each direction.

Key terms

Momentum
v ← βv + ∇L, w ← w − ηv: accumulating past gradients to speed progress along consistent directions
Adam
An adaptive method combining momentum with per-parameter step sizes from running averages of squared gradients
