Integral Calculus

Integration reverses differentiation and accumulates quantities; the definite integral is a signed area.

Mathematics · Calculus · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe integration as anti-differentiation and as accumulated area
  • Distinguish definite and indefinite integrals

Two views of integration

  • Reverse of differentiation: ∫ 2x dx = x2 + c, because d/dx (x2) = 2x.
  • Accumulation: ∫ab f(x) dx adds up f(x) × dx over [a, b], giving the signed area under the curve.
  • The fundamental theorem of calculus connects them (later in this school).

Applications

  • Distance from velocity: s = ∫ v dt; velocity from acceleration.
  • Areas and volumes of curved shapes; centres of mass; work done by a variable force.
  • Probability: areas under probability density functions (School 12).
  • Total change from a rate: if water flows at r(t) litres per minute, ∫010 r(t) dt is the total in 10 minutes.
Worked example

A car's velocity is v = 3t2 m s−1. How far does it travel from t = 0 to t = 4 s?

  1. Distance = ∫04 3t2 dt
  2. An antiderivative is t3
  3. 43 − 03 = 64 m
  • Not every function has an elementary antiderivative: e−x^{2} doesn't, though its definite integrals can still be computed.

Investigate with the lab. Switch between left, right, midpoint and trapezium rules for the same n and rank them by error.

Common mistakeForgetting that areas below the axis count as negative in a definite integral.

In 2 lines: Integration accumulates. It reverses differentiation.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which quantity is found by integrating acceleration with respect to time?
    • Velocity
    • Displacement
    • Force
    • Jerk
    • Mass
    Show the answer

    Answer: Velocity. Acceleration is the derivative of velocity, so integrating it gives velocity.

  2. What does ∫ab f(x) dx represent if f is positive?
    • The gradient at b
    • The area between the curve and the x-axis from a to b
    • The average of f(a) and f(b)
    • The length of the curve
    • f(b) − f(a)
    Show the answer

    Answer: The area between the curve and the x-axis from a to b. For a positive function, the definite integral is the area under the curve.

  3. Which rule usually gives the smallest error for a smooth function with the same number of strips?
    • The left rectangle rule, using each strip's start
    • The right rectangle rule, using each strip's end
    • The midpoint rule (with the trapezium close behind)
    • All four rules give exactly the same error
    • Whichever rule uses the fewest strips
    Show the answer

    Answer: The midpoint rule (with the trapezium close behind). Midpoint and trapezium rules have O(h2) error, while left and right rules are O(h).

Key terms

Integral
Either an antiderivative (indefinite integral) or an accumulated total over an interval (definite integral)
Integrand
The function being integrated, f(x) in ∫ f(x) dx
