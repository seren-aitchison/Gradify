Mathematics explained

61 mathematics lessons you can read here in full, each with a worked example or a diagram, the mistakes students make and practice questions with explanations. To track progress and revise with flashcards, open the free mathematics course.

Number and Arithmetic

  • Irrational Numbers An irrational number can't be written as a fraction of two integers.
  • Factors and Multiples A factor of a number divides it exactly; a multiple is the result of multiplying it by a whole number.
  • Divisibility Divisibility tests tell you whether one number divides another without doing the division.
  • Composite Numbers A composite number is a natural number greater than 1 that isn't prime: it has at least one factor other than 1 and itself.

Number Theory and Cryptography

  • Modular Arithmetic Modular arithmetic is "clock arithmetic": numbers wrap around after reaching a modulus.
  • Primitive Roots A primitive root modulo p is a number whose powers run through every non-zero residue.
  • Integer Factorisation Integer factorisation is splitting a number into primes.

Algebra

  • Simplifying Expressions Simplifying an expression means writing it in an equivalent, shorter form by collecting like terms and using index laws.
  • Expanding Brackets Expanding brackets means multiplying out so that an expression is written as a sum of terms.
  • Factorisation Factorising writes an expression as a product of factors.
  • Quadratic Equations A quadratic equation has the unknown squared, like x2 − 5x + 6 = 0.
  • Inequalities An inequality compares two quantities using <, >, ≤ or ≥.
  • Surds A surd is a root that can't be simplified to a rational number, such as √2 or √12.
  • Factor Theorem The factor theorem is the remainder theorem with remainder zero: (x − a) is a factor of f(x) exactly when f(a) = 0.
  • Roots of Polynomials The roots of a polynomial are the values that make it zero.
  • Functions A function is a rule that takes each input and gives exactly one output.
  • Transformations of Functions Transformations move, stretch and reflect graphs.
  • Complex Numbers Complex numbers extend the reals with i, where i2 = −1.

Geometry

  • Geometric Transformations Transformations move or change shapes: translations, reflections, rotations and enlargements.

Calculus

  • Limits A limit describes the value a function approaches as its input approaches some value.
  • Differentiability A function is differentiable at a point if it has a well-defined tangent there: a single slope.
  • Differential Calculus Differential calculus studies how quantities change.
  • Chain Rule The chain rule differentiates composite functions, functions of functions like (3x + 1)5 or esin x.
  • Curve Sketching Curve sketching uses calculus and algebra together to draw an accurate picture of a function: its intercepts, asymptotes, stationary points, inflections and end behaviour.
  • Integral Calculus Integral calculus is the mathematics of accumulation: adding up infinitely many small pieces to find areas, volumes, distances and totals.
  • Indefinite Integrals An indefinite integral is the family of all antiderivatives of a function.
  • Riemann Sums A Riemann sum approximates the area under a curve by adding up the areas of thin rectangles.
  • Definite Integrals A definite integral ∫ab f(x) dx gives a number: the net signed area between the curve and the x-axis from a to b.
  • Numerical Integration Many integrals can't be found exactly, and data often comes as a table rather than a formula.

Analysis

  • Complex Analysis Complex analysis studies differentiable functions of a complex variable.

Differential Equations

  • Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs) An ordinary differential equation relates an unknown function of one variable to its derivatives.
  • Autonomous Differential Equations An autonomous equation y′ = f(y) has no explicit dependence on the independent variable.
  • Initial Value Problems An initial value problem gives an ODE together with the value of the solution (and enough derivatives) at a starting point.
  • Numerical Solutions of ODEs Most differential equations can't be solved exactly, so computers step forward in small increments.

Linear Algebra and Matrices

  • Matrices A matrix is a rectangular array of numbers.
  • Matrix Inversion The inverse of a square matrix undoes it: A−1A = I.
  • Linear Transformations A linear transformation is a function between vector spaces that respects addition and scaling.
  • Eigenvalues An eigenvalue λ of a square matrix A is a number for which Av = λv has a non-zero solution v.
  • Eigenvectors An eigenvector is a direction a linear map doesn't turn: it's only stretched by its eigenvalue.

Probability

  • Foundations of Probability Modern probability rests on three axioms set out by Andrey Kolmogorov in 1933.
  • Random Variables A random variable assigns a number to each outcome of an experiment: the number of heads, the waiting time, the profit.
  • Law of Large Numbers The law of large numbers says that the average of many independent repetitions settles down to the expected value.
  • Normal Distribution The normal (Gaussian) distribution is the bell-shaped curve defined by its mean and standard deviation.

Statistics

  • Correlation Correlation measures the strength and direction of a linear relationship between two variables.
  • Regression Analysis Regression analysis models how a response variable depends on one or more predictors.
  • Linear Regression Simple linear regression fits the straight line ŷ = a + bx that minimises the sum of squared residuals.
  • Robust Regression Robust regression fits lines that aren't thrown off by outliers or heavy-tailed errors.

Optimisation and Operations Research

  • Unconstrained Optimisation Unconstrained optimisation finds minima of a function over all of ℝⁿ.
  • Nonconvex Optimisation Nonconvex problems can have many local minima, saddle points and flat regions, so finding the global optimum is generally hard.
  • Gradient Descent Gradient descent minimises a function by repeatedly stepping in the direction of steepest decrease, the negative gradient.

Numerical Analysis and Scientific Computing

  • Root-Finding Algorithms Root-finding solves f(x) = 0, from computing square roots to finding equilibria.
  • Newton–Raphson Method The Newton–Raphson method finds roots by following the tangent line to where it crosses the axis.
  • Secant Method The secant method replaces Newton's derivative with the slope through the last two points.
  • Numerical Differentiation Numerical differentiation estimates derivatives from function values using finite differences.
  • Gradient-Based Methods Gradient-based methods use derivative information to solve numerical problems, from minimising functions to fitting models and computing sensitivities.
  • Numerical Optimisation Numerical optimisation turns the theory of minimisation into working algorithms: line searches, trust regions, and methods from gradient descent to Newton.

Mathematical Modelling and Applied Mathematics

  • Population Dynamics Population dynamics models how the number of individuals changes through births, deaths and migration.

Mathematics for Machine Learning and AI

  • Linear Models Linear models predict outputs as weighted sums of inputs.
  • Optimisation Algorithms Training a machine learning model means minimising a loss function, often with millions of parameters.
  • Stochastic Gradient Descent Stochastic gradient descent updates a model using the gradient on a small random batch of data rather than the whole dataset.

Everyday and Practical Mathematics

  • Risk and Probability Everyday decisions involve risk: insurance, health choices, travel, investments and weather.
