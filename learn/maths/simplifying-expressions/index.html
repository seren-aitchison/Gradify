Simplifying Expressions

Combine only like terms, carrying each term's sign; use index laws for products and quotients.

Mathematics · Algebra · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Collect like terms accurately
  • Simplify products and quotients of algebraic terms

Collecting like terms

  • Group like terms (keeping each term's sign), then combine: 4a + 3b − a + 5b = 3a + 8b.
  • Unlike terms can't be combined: 3x + 2y stays as it is, and x2 + x isn't 2x3.
  • Order: write terms in descending powers by convention: 2x2 − 5x + 3.

Multiplying and dividing terms

  • Multiply the numbers, then the letters, using index laws: 3a2b × 4ab3 = 12a3b4.
  • Divide likewise: 12x5y ÷ 3x2y = 4x3.
  • Fractions of terms simplify by cancelling common factors: 6x2/(9x) = 2x/3.

Checking

  • Substitute a value into the original and simplified forms; they must agree (try x = 2, not 0 or 1, which hide errors).
Worked example

Simplify 5x2 − 3x + 7 − 2x2 + 8x − 10.

  1. x2 terms: 5x2 − 2x2 = 3x2
  2. x terms: −3x + 8x = 5x; constants: 7 − 10 = −3
  3. 3x2 + 5x − 3 (check with x = 2: original 20 − 6 + 7 − 8 + 16 − 10 = 19; simplified 12 + 10 − 3 = 19 ✓)
Common mistakeCombining unlike terms, such as x2 + x2 = x4 (it's 2x2).

In 2 lines: Simplify by collecting like terms. Use index laws to multiply and divide terms.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Simplify 7y − 3 + 2y + 8.
    • 9y + 11
    • 14y
    • 9y + 5
    • 5y + 5
    • 9y − 11
    Show the answer

    Answer: 9y + 5. Collect the y terms (7y + 2y = 9y) and the numbers (−3 + 8 = 5), giving 9y + 5.

  2. Simplify 18a4b2 ÷ 6ab2.
    • 3a4
    • 12a3b
    • 3a3b
    • 3a3
    • 3a5b4
    Show the answer

    Answer: 3a3. 18 ÷ 6 = 3, a4 ÷ a = a3, and b2 ÷ b2 = 1.

  3. Which expression is equivalent to 2x2 + 3x − x2 − 3x?
    • x2 + 6x
    • 3x2
    • x4
    • 0
    • x2
    Show the answer

    Answer: x2. The x2 terms give x2; the x terms cancel to 0.
