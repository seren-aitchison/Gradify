Numerical Differentiation

Finite differences approximate derivatives; central differences are second order; truncation and rounding errors trade off, giving optimal h; automatic and complex-step differentiation avoid the trade-off.

Mathematics · Numerical Analysis and Scientific Computing · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Apply forward, backward and central difference formulas
  • Choose step sizes balancing truncation and rounding error

Formulas

  • Forward: (f(x + h) − f(x))/h, error O(h).
  • Central: (f(x + h) − f(x − h))/2h, error O(h2).
  • Second derivative: (f(x + h) − 2f(x) + f(x − h))/h2, error O(h2).
  • Richardson extrapolation raises the order.

Choosing h

  • Total error ≈ Chp + ε/h for unit roundoff ε.
  • Forward difference: optimal h ≈ √ε ≈ 10−8 in double precision.
  • Central difference: h ≈ ε1/3 ≈ 10−5.
  • Too small an h gives noise-dominated results.

Alternatives

  • Automatic differentiation computes exact derivatives of code (used in machine learning).
  • Complex step: accurate to machine precision with tiny h, for analytic functions.
  • Spectral differentiation for smooth periodic data.
  • Smoothing before differentiating noisy data.
Worked example

Estimate the derivative of eˣ at x = 0 with h = 0.1 using forward and central differences.

  1. Forward: (e0.1 − 1)/0.1 = (1.10517 − 1)/0.1 = 1.0517 (error 0.052)
  2. Central: (e0.1 − e−0.1)/0.2 = (1.10517 − 0.90484)/0.2 = 1.00167 (error 0.0017)
  3. Central is far more accurate, as O(h2) predicts

Investigate with the lab. Use the secant gradient as a forward difference: watch the error fall as h shrinks, and note it's roughly proportional to h.

Common mistakeTaking h as small as possible, such as 10−15, and getting rounding noise.

In 2 lines: Differences approximate derivatives. Balance truncation against rounding.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which finite-difference formula has error O(h2)?
    • (f(x + h) − f(x))/h
    • (f(x) − f(x − h))/h
    • f(x + h)/h
    • (f(x + 2h) − f(x))/h
    • (f(x + h) − f(x − h))/(2h)
    Show the answer

    Answer: (f(x + h) − f(x − h))/(2h). Odd error terms cancel in the symmetric central difference.

  2. Why does a forward difference with h = 10−15 give a poor derivative?
    • Rounding error of size ε/h dominates when h is tiny
    • Truncation error grows as h shrinks
    • Derivatives don't exist at that scale
    • The formula needs h = 1
    • It gives an excellent derivative
    Show the answer

    Answer: Rounding error of size ε/h dominates when h is tiny. f(x + h) and f(x) agree in nearly all digits, so their difference is mostly rounding noise.

  3. As the forward-difference step h is halved, roughly what happens to its truncation error?
    • It quarters, since the error is O(h2)
    • It roughly halves, since the error is O(h)
    • It doubles
    • It stays the same
    • It becomes exactly zero
    Show the answer

    Answer: It roughly halves, since the error is O(h). The forward difference has leading error (h/2)f″(x), proportional to h.

Key terms

Central difference
f′(x) ≈ (f(x + h) − f(x − h))/(2h), with error O(h2)
Complex-step derivative
f′(x) ≈ Im f(x + ih)/h, free of subtractive cancellation
