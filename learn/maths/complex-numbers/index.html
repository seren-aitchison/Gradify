Complex Numbers

Treat i as a variable with i2 = −1; divide using the conjugate; real quadratics have conjugate complex roots.

Mathematics · Algebra · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Calculate with complex numbers in the form a + bi
  • Solve quadratic equations with complex roots and represent numbers on an Argand diagram

Arithmetic

  • Add and subtract real and imaginary parts: (3 + 2i) + (1 − 5i) = 4 − 3i.
  • Multiply like brackets, using i2 = −1: (2 + i)(3 − 4i) = 6 − 8i + 3i − 4i2 = 10 − 5i.
  • zz* = a2 + b2 is real, so divide by multiplying top and bottom by the conjugate: (1 + 2i)/(3 − i) = (1 + 2i)(3 + i)/10 = (1 + 7i)/10.

Roots and the Argand diagram

  • x2 + 4x + 13 = 0: discriminant −36, so x = (−4 ± 6i)/2 = −2 ± 3i (a conjugate pair).
  • On the Argand diagram, a + bi is the point (a, b). The modulus |z| = √(a2 + b2) is its distance from the origin; the argument is its angle from the positive real axis.
  • Modulus-argument form z = r(cos θ + i sin θ) and Euler's formula eiθ = cos θ + i sin θ make multiplication a rotation and scaling (School 6 and Further Maths).
Worked example

Solve z2 − 6z + 34 = 0 and find the modulus of each root.

  1. z = (6 ± √(36 − 136))/2 = (6 ± √−100)/2
  2. z = (6 ± 10i)/2 = 3 ± 5i
  3. |3 ± 5i| = √(9 + 25) = √34

Investigate with the lab. Multiply z by i and watch it turn 90°, then multiply z by its conjugate and see the product land on the real axis.

Investigate with the lab. Show the 6th roots of unity, then solve z⁴ = −16. Notice the roots always form a regular polygon centred on the origin.

Common mistakeWriting i2 = 1, or forgetting to multiply the denominator by the conjugate too.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is (4 + i) − (1 − 3i)?
    • 3 − 2i
    • 3 + 4i
    • 5 − 2i
    • 3 + 2i
    • 5 + 4i
    Show the answer

    Answer: 3 + 4i. Real parts: 4 − 1 = 3; imaginary parts: 1 − (−3) = 4.

  2. What are the roots of z2 + 9 = 0?
    • 3 and −3
    • 9i and −9i
    • 3i and −3i
    • 3 + i and 3 − i
    • There are none
    Show the answer

    Answer: 3i and −3i. z2 = −9, and √−9 = 3i, so the roots are the conjugate pair z = ±3i.

  3. Simplify (5 + 5i)/(2 + i).
    • 5 + 5i/2
    • 2.5 + 5i
    • 1 + 3i
    • 3 + i
    • 3 − i
    Show the answer

    Answer: 3 + i. Multiply by (2 − i)/(2 − i): (10 − 5i + 10i + 5)/5 = (15 + 5i)/5 = 3 + i.

  4. On the Argand diagram, what does multiplying a complex number by i do?
    • Reflects it in the real axis
    • Doubles its modulus
    • Rotates it 90° clockwise
    • Moves it one unit up
    • Rotates it 90° anticlockwise about the origin, keeping its modulus
    Show the answer

    Answer: Rotates it 90° anticlockwise about the origin, keeping its modulus. i has modulus 1 and argument 90°, so multiplication adds 90° to the argument and leaves the modulus unchanged.

Key terms

Imaginary unit
i, with i2 = −1
Complex conjugate
For z = a + bi, the conjugate is z* = a − bi
