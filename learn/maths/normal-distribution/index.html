Normal Distribution

Standardise with z = (x − μ)/σ; reverse with x = μ + zσ; independent normals combine with means and variances adding.

Mathematics · Probability · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Calculate normal probabilities and percentiles by standardising
  • Combine independent normal variables

Shape

  • Symmetric about μ; mean = median = mode.
  • Points of inflection at μ ± σ.
  • About 68% within 1σ, 95% within 2σ (more precisely 1.96σ), 99.7% within 3σ.
  • Tails extend forever but fall off very fast.

Calculating

  • P(X < x) = Φ((x − μ)/σ), using tables or a calculator.
  • Inverse: x = μ + zσ, where z is the required percentile of Z.
  • Normal approximation to the binomial: N(np, np(1 − p)) with a continuity correction.

Combining

  • Linear combinations of independent normals are normal: X + Y ~ N(μX + μY, σX2 + σY2).
  • aX + b ~ N(aμ + b, a2σ2).
  • Gauss used it for errors in astronomy (1809); de Moivre found it as a limit of binomials (1733).
Worked example

Adult heights are N(170, 82) cm. Find P(height > 180) and the 90th percentile.

  1. z = (180 − 170)/8 = 1.25, so P(X > 180) = 1 − Φ(1.25) ≈ 1 − 0.8944 = 0.1056
  2. The 90th percentile of Z is 1.2816
  3. x = 170 + 1.2816 × 8 ≈ 180.3 cm

Investigate with the lab. Increase n and watch the histogram of sample means become symmetric and bell-shaped, even though the population is skewed.

Common mistakeDividing by the variance instead of the standard deviation when standardising.

In 2 lines: The normal is defined by μ and σ. Standardise to calculate.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. For a normal distribution, which statement is true?
    • The mean exceeds the median
    • Mean, median and mode are equal
    • It's skewed to the right
    • It has a maximum value
    • Its variance is always 1
    Show the answer

    Answer: Mean, median and mode are equal. The bell curve is symmetric about μ, so its mean, median and mode coincide.

  2. Bags have mass X ~ N(500, 42) g and boxes hold 10 independent bags. What is the SD of a box's total mass?
    • 40 g
    • 4 g
    • 12.6 g
    • 160 g
    • 1.26 g
    Show the answer

    Answer: 12.6 g. The variance is 10 × 16 = 160, so the SD is √160 ≈ 12.6 g.

  3. About what proportion of a normal distribution lies within one standard deviation of the mean?
    • About 50%
    • About 95%
    • About 99.7%
    • About 68%
    • About 34%
    Show the answer

    Answer: About 68%. The 68–95–99.7 rule gives about 68% within ±1σ.

Key terms

Normal distribution
X ~ N(μ, σ2): f(x) = (1/(σ√(2π))) exp(−(x − μ)2/(2σ2))
Standardising
z = (x − μ)/σ converts X to the standard normal Z
