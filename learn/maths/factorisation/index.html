Factorisation

Common factor first; then two numbers for quadratics; spot differences of two squares; check by expanding.

Mathematics · Algebra · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Factorise by taking out common factors and factorising quadratics
  • Recognise the difference of two squares and perfect squares

Common factors

  • Take out the HCF of all terms, including letters: 12x2y − 18xy2 = 6xy(2x − 3y).
  • Check by expanding back.

Quadratics

  • x2 + bx + c: find two numbers that multiply to c and add to b: x2 − x − 12 = (x − 4)(x + 3).
  • ax2 + bx + c with a ≠ 1: find two numbers that multiply to ac and add to b, split the middle term, then factorise in pairs: 6x2 + x − 2 = 6x2 + 4x − 3x − 2 = 2x(3x + 2) − (3x + 2) = (2x − 1)(3x + 2).

Special forms

  • Difference of two squares: a2 − b2 = (a − b)(a + b): 9x2 − 49 = (3x − 7)(3x + 7).
  • Perfect square: x2 + 10x + 25 = (x + 5)2.
  • Always look for a common factor first, then check whether a bracket factorises further: 2x2 − 18 = 2(x2 − 9) = 2(x − 3)(x + 3).
Worked example

Factorise fully 3x3 − 12x.

  1. Common factor 3x: 3x(x2 − 4)
  2. x2 − 4 is a difference of two squares: (x − 2)(x + 2)
  3. 3x(x − 2)(x + 2)

Investigate with the lab. Build a quadratic with roots −1 and 3 and compare the factorised form shown with the expanded one. Then try to factorise one with no real roots.

Common mistakeStopping before fully factorising, as in 2(x2 − 9).

In 2 lines: Factorising reverses expanding. Fully factorise by checking every bracket.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Factorise x2 − 5x + 6.
    • (x + 2)(x + 3)
    • (x − 1)(x − 6)
    • (x − 2)(x + 3)
    • x(x − 5) + 6
    • (x − 2)(x − 3)
    Show the answer

    Answer: (x − 2)(x − 3). −2 and −3 multiply to 6 and add to −5, so the factors are (x − 2)(x − 3).

  2. Factorise fully 8x2 − 2.
    • 2(2x − 1)(2x + 1)
    • (8x − 2)(x + 1)
    • 2(4x2 − 1)
    • (4x − 1)(2x + 2)
    • 8(x − 1)(x + 1)
    Show the answer

    Answer: 2(2x − 1)(2x + 1). Take out 2 to get 2(4x2 − 1), then 4x2 − 1 is a difference of two squares.

  3. Factorise 3x2 − 10x − 8.
    • (3x − 2)(x + 4)
    • (3x + 2)(x − 4)
    • (3x − 4)(x + 2)
    • (3x + 4)(x − 2)
    • 3(x − 4)(x + 2)
    Show the answer

    Answer: (3x + 2)(x − 4). ac = −24: −12 and 2 add to −10, giving 3x(x − 4) + 2(x − 4) = (3x + 2)(x − 4).

Key terms

Factorise
Write an expression as a product of its factors
Fully factorised
Factorised so that no factor can be factorised further
