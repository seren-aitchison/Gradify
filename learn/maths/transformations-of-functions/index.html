Transformations of Functions

Outside the bracket affects y as written; inside affects x in reverse; track key points through each step.

Mathematics · Algebra · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the effect of translations, stretches and reflections on graphs
  • Combine transformations and find equations of transformed graphs

The four basic transformations

  • y = f(x) + a: translation by (0, a) (up a).
  • y = f(x + a): translation by (−a, 0) (left a: the opposite way to the sign).
  • y = af(x): stretch parallel to the y-axis, scale factor a.
  • y = f(ax): stretch parallel to the x-axis, scale factor 1/a.
  • y = −f(x): reflection in the x-axis; y = f(−x): reflection in the y-axis.

Combining

  • Changes inside the bracket act on x (horizontal, and "backwards"); changes outside act on y (vertical, and as you'd expect).
  • y = 2(x − 3)2 + 1 is y = x2 translated 3 right, stretched vertically by 2, then translated up 1: the vertex moves to (3, 1).
  • Points transform too: if (2, 5) is on y = f(x), then (1, 5) is on y = f(2x) and (2, 10) is on y = 2f(x).
Worked example

The curve y = f(x) has a maximum at (4, 6). Find the maximum of y = 3f(x − 1) and of y = f(2x) − 5.

  1. y = 3f(x − 1): move 1 right, stretch vertically by 3: maximum at (5, 18)
  2. y = f(2x) − 5: halve the x-coordinate, then move down 5
  3. Maximum at (2, 1)

Investigate with the lab. Work through the completed-square and reflected-parabola challenges. For each slider, predict the effect before you move it.

Common mistakeMoving y = f(x + 3) to the right instead of to the left.

In 2 lines: Translations, stretches and reflections transform graphs. Inside changes act on x in reverse.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which transformation maps y = x2 to y = (x + 5)2?
    • Translation 5 to the right
    • Translation 5 up
    • Stretch by 5
    • Translation 5 to the left
    • Reflection
    Show the answer

    Answer: Translation 5 to the left. f(x + 5) moves the graph 5 units in the negative x direction.

  2. (3, −2) lies on y = f(x). Which point lies on y = −f(x) + 4?
    • (3, 2)
    • (−3, 6)
    • (3, −6)
    • (7, 2)
    • (3, 6)
    Show the answer

    Answer: (3, 6). Reflecting gives (3, 2), then up 4 gives (3, 6).

  3. Which describes y = f(2x + 6) compared with y = f(x)?
    • Translate 6 left, then stretch by 1/2 parallel to the x-axis
    • Stretch by 1/2, then translate 6 left
    • Translate 3 right
    • Stretch by 2, then translate 6 right
    • Translate 6 right, then stretch by 1/2
    Show the answer

    Answer: Translate 6 left, then stretch by 1/2 parallel to the x-axis. f(2x + 6) = f(2(x + 3)): it's also "stretch by ½, then translate 3 left"; translating 6 left first, then stretching, gives the same graph.

  4. Compared with y = x2, how is y = −2(x + 1)2 + 4 transformed?
    • Shifted 1 right and 4 up, then reflected
    • Shifted 1 left and 4 up, stretched by 2 vertically and reflected in the x-axis
    • Shifted 1 left and 4 down, stretched horizontally by 2
    • Only reflected in the y-axis
    • Shifted 2 left and 4 up
    Show the answer

    Answer: Shifted 1 left and 4 up, stretched by 2 vertically and reflected in the x-axis. The vertex moves to (−1, 4), and the factor −2 stretches by 2 and flips the curve.

Key terms

Translation
A shift of a graph without changing its shape, described by a vector
Stretch
Scaling a graph parallel to an axis by a scale factor
