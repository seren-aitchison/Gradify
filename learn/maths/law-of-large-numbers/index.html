Law of Large Numbers

Averages converge to the mean; variance σ2/n shrinks; the law dilutes streaks rather than correcting them.

Mathematics · Probability · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • State the weak and strong laws of large numbers
  • Explain what the law does and doesn't say about streaks

The statements

  • X₁, X₂, … independent and identically distributed with mean μ; X̄n is their average.
  • Weak law: the average is unlikely to be far from μ when n is large.
  • Strong law: the sequence of averages converges to μ for almost every sequence of outcomes.
  • Jacob Bernoulli proved the first version (1713); Kolmogorov proved the strong law in general.

Why it works

  • Var(X̄n) = σ2/n → 0, and Chebyshev's inequality gives the weak law directly.
  • Frequencies: the proportion of heads converges to P(heads).
  • Monte Carlo methods estimate integrals and probabilities by averaging simulations.

What it doesn't say

  • It doesn't say outcomes "balance out": after 10 extra heads, the expected excess stays 10; it's diluted by later tosses, not cancelled.
  • It needs a finite mean: averages of Cauchy variables never settle down.
  • It says nothing about how fast; the central limit theorem gives the scale of fluctuations.
Worked example

Using Chebyshev, how many tosses of a fair coin ensure P(|p̂ − 0.5| ≥ 0.05) ≤ 0.05?

  1. Var(p̂) = 0.25/n
  2. Chebyshev: P(|p̂ − 0.5| ≥ 0.05) ≤ (0.25/n)/0.052 = 100/n
  3. 100/n ≤ 0.05 needs n ≥ 2,000 (a conservative bound)

Investigate with the lab. Run 10,000 coin flips and get within 0.01 of the true probability (the lab's challenge). Watch the raw count of heads minus n/2 keep wandering even as the proportion settles.

Common mistakeBelieving the law of large numbers means a run of losses must be followed by wins.

In 2 lines: Averages converge to expected values. Streaks are diluted, not corrected.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What does the law of large numbers guarantee?
    • Each outcome becomes more predictable
    • Streaks are followed by opposite streaks
    • The sample mean equals μ after 30 trials
    • The sample mean approaches the population mean as n grows
    • The distribution becomes normal
    Show the answer

    Answer: The sample mean approaches the population mean as n grows. It's about averages, not about individual outcomes or the shape of the distribution.

  2. After 100 tosses, a fair coin shows 60 heads. What is the expected number of heads after 1,000 tosses in total?
    • 500
    • 490
    • 600
    • 550
    • 510
    Show the answer

    Answer: 510. The next 900 tosses are expected to give 450 heads; the earlier excess is diluted, not reversed.

  3. As the number of fair coin flips grows, what settles down?
    • The proportion of heads, towards 0.5
    • The difference between heads and tails, towards 0
    • The number of heads, towards a fixed value
    • Nothing settles
    • The next flip becomes predictable
    Show the answer

    Answer: The proportion of heads, towards 0.5. The law of large numbers concerns averages; the raw difference typically grows like √n.

Key terms

Weak law of large numbers
For any ε > 0, P(|X̄n − μ| > ε) → 0 as n → ∞
Strong law of large numbers
X̄n → μ with probability 1
