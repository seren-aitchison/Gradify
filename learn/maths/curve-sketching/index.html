Curve Sketching

Work through domain, symmetry, intercepts, asymptotes, stationary points and end behaviour; label key points.

Mathematics · Calculus · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Combine intercepts, asymptotes and derivatives to sketch curves
  • Label key features accurately

A checklist

  • Domain and any symmetry (even: f(−x) = f(x); odd: f(−x) = −f(x)).
  • Intercepts: x = 0 for the y-intercept; solve f(x) = 0 for x-intercepts.
  • Asymptotes: vertical where a denominator is zero (and the numerator isn't); horizontal from limits at ±∞; oblique by division.
  • Stationary points and their nature; inflections.
  • End behaviour, then draw smoothly through the features.

Example: y = x/(x2 + 1)

  • Odd function; passes through the origin; no vertical asymptotes; y → 0 as x → ±∞ (horizontal asymptote y = 0).
  • y′ = (1 − x2)/(x2 + 1)2: maximum (1, ½), minimum (−1, −½).
  • So the curve rises from 0 to a peak at x = 1 and decays back towards the axis.
Worked example

Sketch y = (x + 1)/(x − 2), stating its asymptotes and intercepts.

  1. Vertical asymptote x = 2; as x → ±∞, y → 1, so horizontal asymptote y = 1
  2. Intercepts: (−1, 0) and (0, −1/2)
  3. y′ = −3/(x − 2)2 < 0: decreasing on each branch, a hyperbola in the regions above-right and below-left of the asymptotes' crossing

Investigate with the lab. Do the cubic-roots and shifted-reciprocal challenges. Sketch each curve on paper first, then check your roots and asymptotes against the plot.

Common mistakeDrawing a curve crossing a vertical asymptote, or ignoring end behaviour.

In 2 lines: Sketches show key features. Calculus finds turning points; algebra finds intercepts and asymptotes.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is the vertical asymptote of y = 3/(x + 4)?
    • x = 4
    • y = 0
    • x = 3
    • y = 3
    • x = −4
    Show the answer

    Answer: x = −4. The denominator is zero at x = −4, where y grows without bound.

  2. What is the horizontal asymptote of y = (6x2 + 1)/(3x2 − 2)?
    • y = 2
    • y = 6
    • y = 0
    • y = 1/2
    • None
    Show the answer

    Answer: y = 2. As x → ∞, the leading terms dominate: 6x2/3x2 = 2.

  3. Where are the asymptotes of y = 2/(x − 1) + 1?
    • x = −1 and y = 1
    • x = 1 and y = 1
    • x = 2 and y = 1
    • x = 1 and y = 2
    • There are none
    Show the answer

    Answer: x = 1 and y = 1. The denominator is zero at x = 1, and the curve approaches y = 1 as x grows.

Key terms

Asymptote
A line a curve approaches but doesn't reach (vertical, horizontal or oblique)
End behaviour
What the function does as x → ±∞
