Eigenvalues

Solve det(A − λI) = 0; for 2 × 2 use trace and determinant; eigenvalues sum to the trace and multiply to the determinant; 0 is an eigenvalue iff A is singular.

Mathematics · Linear Algebra and Matrices · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Find eigenvalues by solving the characteristic equation
  • Use the trace and determinant as checks

Finding eigenvalues

  • Solve det(A − λI) = 0.
  • 2 × 2: λ2 − (tr A)λ + det A = 0.
  • Triangular matrices: the diagonal entries.
  • Real matrices may have complex eigenvalues (rotations).

Checks and facts

  • Sum of eigenvalues = trace; product = determinant.
  • A is invertible ⇔ 0 isn't an eigenvalue.
  • Eigenvalues of Ak are λk; of A−1, 1/λ; of A + cI, λ + c.

Uses

  • Stability of differential equations and iterations (School 10).
  • Natural frequencies of structures; principal components (later in this school).
  • Google's PageRank uses the dominant eigenvector of a link matrix.
Worked example

Find the eigenvalues of A = [[4, 1], [2, 3]].

  1. tr A = 7, det A = 12 − 2 = 10
  2. λ2 − 7λ + 10 = 0, so (λ − 2)(λ − 5) = 0
  3. λ = 2 and λ = 5 (check: sum 7 = trace, product 10 = det)
  • Example: the identity matrix has every non-zero vector as an eigenvector, with eigenvalue 1.

Investigate with the lab. Find a matrix with no real eigenvectors (the lab's challenge): a rotation turns every direction, so no line stays fixed.

Common mistakeForgetting to subtract λ from every diagonal entry.

In 2 lines: Eigenvalues are the stretching factors. They sum to the trace.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. A has eigenvalue 3. What is an eigenvalue of A2 + I?
    • 9
    • 4
    • 3
    • 10
    • 7
    Show the answer

    Answer: 10. If Av = 3v, then (A2 + I)v = (9 + 1)v.

  2. Why does a singular matrix have 0 as an eigenvalue?
    • Its trace is zero
    • Its entries are zero
    • It has no eigenvalues
    • It doesn't
    • Some non-zero v has Av = 0 = 0v
    Show the answer

    Answer: Some non-zero v has Av = 0 = 0v. Singular means a non-trivial null space, which is the eigenspace for 0.

  3. Why does the rotation matrix [[0, −1], [1, 0]] have no real eigenvalues?
    • It turns every vector 90°, so none is just stretched (λ = ±i)
    • Its determinant is zero
    • It has one repeated eigenvalue 1
    • All matrices have real eigenvalues
    • Its eigenvalues are 0
    Show the answer

    Answer: It turns every vector 90°, so none is just stretched (λ = ±i). The characteristic equation λ2 + 1 = 0 has no real roots.

Key terms

Eigenvalue
λ with Av = λv for some non-zero v
Characteristic equation
det(A − λI) = 0, whose roots are the eigenvalues
