Composite Numbers

Composites have proper factors; one factor pair is enough to prove a number composite.

Mathematics · Number and Arithmetic · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Distinguish prime, composite and neither
  • Find a factor pair that shows a number is composite

Classifying

  • 1 is neither prime nor composite.
  • Composite numbers include every even number greater than 2, every multiple of a prime greater than that prime, and every square of a whole number greater than 1.
  • To show a number is composite, give one factor pair: 91 = 7 × 13.

Runs of composites

  • There are arbitrarily long runs of composite numbers: n! + 2, n! + 3, …, n! + n are all composite (n! + k is divisible by k).
  • So gaps between consecutive primes can be as large as you like.
Worked example

Find five consecutive composite numbers.

  1. Use 6! = 720
  2. 722, 723, 724, 725, 726 are divisible by 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6 respectively
  3. (There are smaller examples, such as 24, 25, 26, 27, 28.)
  • Every composite number n has a prime factor no bigger than √n, which is why trial division only needs to go up to √n.
  • Composite numbers can be co-prime with each other: 8 and 9 share no factors.

Investigate with the lab. Watch which numbers are crossed out and by which prime first. Every crossed-out number is composite, and the prime that crosses it first is its smallest prime factor.

Common mistakeThinking an odd number must be prime: 91 = 7 × 13 is composite.

In 2 lines: Composites have more than two factors. There are arbitrarily long runs of composite numbers.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which number is composite?
    • 113
    • 109
    • 107
    • 103
    • 119
    Show the answer

    Answer: 119. 119 = 7 × 17, so it has factors other than 1 and itself; the others are prime.

  2. Why is 6! + 4 composite?
    • It's divisible by 4
    • It's even and greater than 2 only by coincidence
    • It's a square number
    • It's divisible by 7
    • 6! is prime
    Show the answer

    Answer: It's divisible by 4. 6! = 720 is divisible by 4, so 720 + 4 is too.

  3. In the sieve, which prime first crosses out 91?
    • 13, since 91 = 7 × 13 and 13 is larger
    • 7, since 91 = 7 × 13 and 7 is its smallest prime factor
    • 3, since 9 + 1 = 10 isn't prime
    • 91 is never crossed out because it's prime
    • 2, since every composite is even
    Show the answer

    Answer: 7, since 91 = 7 × 13 and 7 is its smallest prime factor. A composite number is first crossed out by its smallest prime factor, which for 91 = 7 × 13 is 7.

Key terms

Composite number
A natural number greater than 1 with more than two factors
Proper factor
A factor other than 1 and the number itself
