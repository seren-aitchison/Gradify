Robust Regression

Least squares has zero breakdown; LAD, M-estimators and Theil–Sen resist outliers; compare robust and ordinary fits as a diagnostic.

Mathematics · Statistics · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain why least squares is sensitive to outliers
  • Describe M-estimation, least absolute deviations and the Theil–Sen estimator

Methods

  • Least absolute deviations (LAD): minimise Σ|residual|; it's median regression.
  • M-estimators: Huber or bisquare losses, fitted by iteratively reweighted least squares, down-weighting large residuals.
  • Theil–Sen: the median of the slopes between all pairs of points; breakdown point about 29%.
  • RANSAC (computer vision): fit to random subsets and keep the model with most inliers.

When to use

  • Data with outliers that are errors or from a different process.
  • Heavy-tailed errors (financial data).
  • As a check: if robust and ordinary fits differ greatly, investigate.

Cautions

  • Don't use robust methods to hide genuine but inconvenient observations.
  • Inference is more complex; bootstrap standard errors are common.
Worked example

Points (1, 1), (2, 2), (3, 3), (4, 4), (5, 20). Compare the least squares slope with the Theil–Sen slope.

  1. Least squares: Sxy = 40 and Sxx = 10, so b = 4
  2. Theil–Sen: the six pairs among the first four points have slope 1; pairs with (5, 20) give 4.75, 6, 8.5 and 16
  3. Median of the ten slopes = 1: the robust line follows the main pattern

Investigate with the lab. Add a single outlier and measure how far the least-squares line moves. This sensitivity is what robust methods are designed to reduce.

Common mistakeDeleting outliers without investigating them.

In 2 lines: Robust regression resists outliers. Big differences from least squares are a warning.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Why is least squares sensitive to outliers?
    • It ignores large residuals and fits small ones only
    • It uses medians
    • It only fits horizontal lines
    • It isn't sensitive
    • Squaring residuals gives large errors huge weight
    Show the answer

    Answer: Squaring residuals gives large errors huge weight. A residual of 10 contributes 100 to the sum, dominating smaller ones.

  2. Which estimator minimises the sum of absolute residuals?
    • Least absolute deviations (median regression)
    • Ridge regression
    • Ordinary least squares with weights
    • Logistic regression
    • The method of moments
    Show the answer

    Answer: Least absolute deviations (median regression). LAD replaces squares with absolute values, limiting outliers' pull.

  3. What problem do robust regression methods address?
    • Too few data points
    • Least-squares fits being dragged by a few outliers
    • Perfect multicollinearity only
    • Non-numeric data
    • Negative correlations
    Show the answer

    Answer: Least-squares fits being dragged by a few outliers. Methods like Huber or least absolute deviations down-weight extreme residuals.

Key terms

Breakdown point
The fraction of contaminated data an estimator can handle before giving arbitrary results; 0% for least squares
Huber loss
A loss that's quadratic for small residuals and linear for large ones
