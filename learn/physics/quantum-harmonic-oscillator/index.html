Quantum Harmonic Oscillator

The quantum harmonic oscillator has evenly spaced energy levels and a nonzero ground-state energy. Because small vibrations are harmonic, it describes molecular vibrations, phonons, photons in a cavity and quantum fields.

Physics · Quantum Mechanics · about 2 minutes

Energies and states

En = (n + ½)ħω, n = 0, 1, 2…
ψ0 ∝ e−mωx^{2/(2ħ)}Gaussian ground state
  • Higher states: Hermite polynomials times the Gaussian.
  • Zero-point energy ½ħω: atoms vibrate even at absolute zero (why helium stays liquid at 0 K under normal pressure).

Applications

  • Molecular vibrational spectra (IR spectroscopy, selection rule Δn = ±1).
  • Phonons in solids; photons in each EM mode.
  • Coherent states (laser light), squeezed states (LIGO uses squeezed light to beat shot noise).
Worked example

CO vibrates at 6.4 × 1013 Hz. Zero-point energy and level spacing?

  1. ħω = hf = 0.265 eV
  2. Zero-point 0.13 eV; spacing 0.265 eV (absorbs at 4.7 μm)
Common mistakeThe quantum oscillator's ground state isn't at rest at the bottom of the potential; it's spread out with zero-point motion.

Investigate with the lab. Choose the harmonic potential and see the levels evenly spaced by ħω, starting at ½ħω.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. The energy spacing in the quantum harmonic oscillator is:
    • Increasing with n
    • ħω, constant
    • Decreasing with n
    • Zero
    Show the answer

    Answer: ħω, constant. Evenly spaced.

  2. The ground state wavefunction is:
    • A sine wave
    • A step
    • A Gaussian
    • Zero
    Show the answer

    Answer: A Gaussian. e−αx^{2}.

  3. What is the ground-state energy of a quantum harmonic oscillator?
    • 0
    • ħω
    • 2ħω
    • ½ħω
    Show the answer

    Answer: ½ħω. The zero-point energy is ½ħω, and levels are spaced by ħω.
