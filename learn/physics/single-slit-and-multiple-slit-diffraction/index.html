Single-slit and Multiple-slit Diffraction

A single slit produces a broad central maximum with weaker side fringes. Adding more slits keeps that envelope but sharpens the bright fringes inside it, until many slits give the narrow lines of a diffraction grating.

Physics · Optics · about 2 minutes

Single slit

I = I0(sin β/β)2, β = (πa sin θ)/λ
  • Minima at a sin θ = mλ (m ≠ 0); central maximum twice as wide as the others.
  • Side maxima ~4.7%, 1.6%… of the central intensity.

N slits

I = I0(sin β/β)2(sin Nγ/sin γ)2, γ = (πd sin θ)/λ
  • Principal maxima at d sin θ = nλ, intensity ∝ N2.
  • N − 1 minima and N − 2 subsidiary maxima between principal maxima.
  • Width of principal maxima ∝ 1/N: more slits, sharper lines.
  • Missing orders: when a principal maximum falls on a single-slit minimum (d/a an integer).
Worked example

Double slit with d = 0.3 mm and a = 0.1 mm. Which interference orders are missing?

  1. d/a = 3: orders 3, 6, 9… coincide with single-slit minima
Common mistakeThe single-slit envelope multiplies the interference pattern; the two effects aren't separate patterns side by side.

Investigate with the lab. Set the slit separation to three times the slit width and find the missing bright fringe where it meets a diffraction minimum.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. The central maximum of a single slit is:
    • Twice as wide as the others
    • The same width
    • Half as wide
    • Absent
    Show the answer

    Answer: Twice as wide as the others. Between the ±1 minima.

  2. Principal maxima intensity for N slits scales as:
    • N
    • N2
    • √N
    • 1/N
    Show the answer

    Answer: N2. Amplitudes add.

  3. In a double-slit pattern with d = 3a, which interference order is missing?
    • The first order
    • The second order
    • The third order
    • No order is missing
    Show the answer

    Answer: The third order. The third bright fringe falls exactly on the first diffraction minimum, so it vanishes.
