Lorentz Force

The Lorentz force is the total electromagnetic force on a charged particle: the electric force plus the magnetic force. With Maxwell's equations it completes classical electromagnetism.

Physics · Magnetism and Electromagnetism · about 2 minutes

The law

F = q(E + v × B)
  • Electric part acts on all charges, does work.
  • Magnetic part acts only on moving charges, does no work.

Crossed fields

v = E/Bvelocity selector: undeflected particles
  • Thomson used crossed fields (1897) to find e/m for the electron, discovering it.
  • Mass spectrometers combine a velocity selector with a magnetic field.

Relativity

Electric and magnetic forces are two aspects of one field. A charge at rest beside a current-carrying wire feels no force; in the frame of a charge moving along the wire, length contraction makes the wire's charge densities unequal, and the magnetic force appears as an electric force. The field tensor Fμν combines E and B.

Worked example

A velocity selector has E = 2 × 104 V m-1, B = 0.1 T. Which speed passes?

  1. v = E/B = 2 × 105 m s-1
Common mistakeWhether a force is "electric" or "magnetic" depends on the observer's frame; the total Lorentz force transforms consistently.

Investigate with the lab. Turn on an electric field as well as B and watch the particle drift. Then set E so the particle goes straight through.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Thomson measured the electron's:
    • Charge-to-mass ratio
    • Charge alone
    • Mass alone
    • Spin
    Show the answer

    Answer: Charge-to-mass ratio. e/m using crossed fields.

  2. In a frame moving with a charge, its magnetic force appears as:
    • No force
    • An electric force
    • A gravitational force
    • A stronger magnetic force
    Show the answer

    Answer: An electric force. Fields transform.

  3. In a velocity selector, which particles pass straight through?
    • Those with v = B/E
    • All particles at any speed
    • Those with v = E/B
    • Only uncharged particles
    Show the answer

    Answer: Those with v = E/B. The electric and magnetic forces balance when qE = qvB.
