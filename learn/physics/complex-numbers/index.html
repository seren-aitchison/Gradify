Complex Numbers

A complex number z = x + iy, with i2 = −1, is a point in a plane. Through Euler's formula complex exponentials represent oscillations neatly, which is why they run through AC circuits, waves, optics and quantum mechanics.

Physics · Mathematics for Physics · about 2 minutes

Forms

z = x + iy = r(cos θ + i sin θ) = reiθ
r = |z| = √(x2 + y2); θ = arg z, tan θ = y/x
eiθ = cos θ + i sin θEuler's formula
z* = x − iy; |z|2 = zz*
  • Multiplying multiplies moduli and adds arguments: r1eiθ1 × r2eiθ2 = r1r2ei(θ1+θ2).
  • De Moivre: (cos θ + i sin θ)n = cos nθ + i sin nθ.

Why physics uses them

Write an oscillation as the real part of A eiωt. Then d/dt becomes multiplication by iω, and differential equations become algebra. In AC circuits impedances are ZR = R, ZL = iωL, ZC = 1/(iωC). In quantum mechanics the wavefunction is genuinely complex, and only |ψ|2 is observable.

Worked example

Find |Z| and the phase for R = 30 Ω in series with an inductor of reactance 40 Ω.

  1. Z = 30 + 40i Ω
  2. |Z| = 50 Ω; phase φ = arctan(40/30) = 53°, voltage leading current
Common mistakeWhen you use complex notation for real signals, take the real part only at the end, and never multiply two complex representations to get a physical product (like power) without care.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is |3 − 4i|?
    • 7
    • 5
    • 1
    • 25
    Show the answer

    Answer: 5. √(9 + 16) = 5.

  2. What is eiπ?
    • 1
    • i
    • −1
    • 0
    Show the answer

    Answer: −1. cos π + i sin π = −1.

  3. What is the impedance of a capacitor in complex form?
    • iωC
    • iω/C
    • −iωC
    • 1/(iωC) = −i/(ωC)
    Show the answer

    Answer: 1/(iωC) = −i/(ωC). The capacitor current leads the voltage by 90°, giving a factor −i.
