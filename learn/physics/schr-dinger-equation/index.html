Schrödinger Equation

The Schrödinger equation governs how quantum states evolve in time. It's the quantum analogue of Newton's second law: given the potential and the initial wavefunction, it determines the wavefunction at all later times.

Physics · Quantum Mechanics · about 2 minutes

The equation

iħ ∂ψ/∂t = Ĥψ
Ĥ = −(ħ2/2m)∇2 + V(r)Hamiltonian for one particle
  • Linear: superpositions of solutions are solutions.
  • Deterministic and unitary: probabilities are conserved.

Where it comes from

Schrödinger (1926) sought a wave equation consistent with de Broglie's waves: E = ħω and p = ħk with E = p2/2m + V. Replacing E → iħ∂/∂t and p → −iħ∇ gives the equation. It can't be derived from classical physics; it's justified by its success.

Successes and limits

  • Explains hydrogen's spectrum, chemical bonding, solids, tunnelling.
  • Non-relativistic: replaced by the Dirac and Klein–Gordon equations and quantum field theory at high speeds.
  • Exact solutions are rare; most real problems are solved numerically or approximately.
Worked example

Check that ψ = ei(kx − ωt) solves the free Schrödinger equation.

  1. iħ∂ψ/∂t = ħωψ; −(ħ2/2m)∂2ψ/∂x2 = (ħ2k2/2m)ψ
  2. Solution if ħω = ħ2k2/2m: E = p2/2m
Common mistakeThe Schrödinger equation is first order in time, unlike the classical wave equation, so you need only ψ at t = 0 (not ∂ψ/∂t).

Investigate with the lab. Switch between the four potentials and see how each gives a different set of energy levels from the same Schrödinger equation.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. The momentum operator in position representation is:
    • iħ∂/∂t
    • ħk
    • −iħ∇
    • mv
    Show the answer

    Answer: −iħ∇. Canonical quantisation.

  2. Schrödinger's equation fails for:
    • Electrons in atoms
    • Tunnelling
    • Molecules
    • Particles near the speed of light
    Show the answer

    Answer: Particles near the speed of light. Non-relativistic.

  3. What does solving the time-independent Schrödinger equation give for a bound particle?
    • Its allowed energy levels and wavefunctions
    • Its exact trajectory
    • Its speed at every moment
    • Only its mass
    Show the answer

    Answer: Its allowed energy levels and wavefunctions. Bound states exist only at particular energies, each with its wavefunction.
