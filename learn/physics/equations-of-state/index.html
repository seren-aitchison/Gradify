Equations of State

An equation of state relates pressure, volume and temperature (and amount) for a substance. The ideal gas law works for dilute gases; real substances need corrections for molecular size and attraction.

Physics · Thermodynamics · about 2 minutes

Ideal gas

pV = nRT = NkBT
  • Assumes point particles with no forces between them.
  • Good at low pressure and high temperature.

Real gases

(p + an2/V2)(V − nb) = nRTvan der Waals
  • b: excluded volume (molecular size); a: attraction (reduces wall pressure).
  • Predicts liquid–gas transition and a critical point.
  • Virial expansion: pV/(nRT) = 1 + B(T)n/V + C(T)(n/V)2 + …
  • Compressibility factor Z = pV/(nRT): 1 for ideal; < 1 when attraction dominates, > 1 at high pressure.

Other equations of state

  • Solids: Birch–Murnaghan (high pressure, planetary interiors).
  • Stars: degenerate electron gas p ∝ ρ5/3 (white dwarfs).
  • Cosmology: p = wρc2 (w = 0 matter, 1/3 radiation, −1 dark energy).
Worked example

1 mol of CO2 in 1 L at 300 K: compare ideal and van der Waals pressures (a = 0.364 Pa m6 mol-2, b = 4.27 × 10-5 m3 mol-1).

  1. Ideal: 8.31 × 300/10-3 = 24.9 bar
  2. VdW: 2493/(0.957 × 10-3) − 0.364/10-6 = 26.0 − 3.6 = 22.4 bar
Common mistakeThe ideal gas law fails near condensation and at high pressures; check Z before trusting it.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. An ideal gas obeys:
    • pV = nRT
    • pVγ = constant always
    • p = ρg
    • pV = 0
    Show the answer

    Answer: pV = nRT. Definition.

  2. Real gases deviate most from ideal behaviour at:
    • Low pressure and high temperature
    • High pressure and low temperature
    • Room conditions
    • Vacuum
    Show the answer

    Answer: High pressure and low temperature. Molecules close together and slow.

  3. A fixed mass of ideal gas is heated from 300 K to 600 K at constant volume. What happens to its pressure?
    • It halves
    • It stays the same
    • It doubles
    • It rises by 300 Pa
    Show the answer

    Answer: It doubles. p/T is constant at fixed volume, so doubling T doubles p.
