Double-slit Experiments

Sending particles one at a time through two slits builds up an interference pattern dot by dot. Each particle's quantum amplitude passes through both slits; trying to learn which slit it took destroys the pattern. Feynman called it the central mystery of quantum mechanics.

Physics · Quantum Mechanics · about 2 minutes

Key results

  • Electrons (Jönsson 1961; Tonomura 1989, single electrons), neutrons, atoms, molecules up to ~2000 atoms.
  • Probability P = |ψ1 + ψ2|2 = |ψ1|2 + |ψ2|2 + interference terms.
  • Classical particles would give P1 + P2 (no fringes).

Which-path information

  • Any measurement or environmental record of the path makes the terms incoherent: no fringes.
  • Even unread information counts if it exists in principle.
  • Quantum eraser: erasing which-path information (by a later measurement) recovers fringes in correlated subsets of data.

Delayed choice

Wheeler's delayed-choice experiments decide whether to measure path or interference after the particle has passed the slits; results match quantum predictions, showing no particle "decides" in advance how to behave.

Worked example

Electrons at 50 keV (λ ≈ 5.4 pm) through slits 1 μm apart, screen 1 m away. Fringe spacing?

  1. x = λD/d = 5.4 × 10-12/10-6 = 5.4 μm (needs magnification to see)
Common mistakeThe pattern isn't due to particles interfering with each other; one particle at a time still builds it.

Investigate with the lab. Make a double-slit pattern and change the separation and screen distance. The fringes show light behaving as a wave.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. With single electrons sent one at a time, the screen shows:
    • An interference pattern building up dot by dot
    • Two bands only
    • A uniform blur
    • Nothing
    Show the answer

    Answer: An interference pattern building up dot by dot. Each electron interferes with itself.

  2. A quantum eraser shows that:
    • Particles go backwards in time
    • Removing which-path information can restore interference
    • Measurement is impossible
    • Light has mass
    Show the answer

    Answer: Removing which-path information can restore interference. Coherence depends on available information.

  3. What does the double-slit experiment with light show?
    • Light travels only in straight lines
    • Light is made only of particles
    • Light behaves as a wave and interferes
    • Light has no wavelength
    Show the answer

    Answer: Light behaves as a wave and interferes. Interference fringes are evidence of wave behaviour.
