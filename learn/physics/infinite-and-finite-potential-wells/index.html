Infinite and Finite Potential Wells

A particle confined in a potential well can only have certain energies. The infinite square well is the simplest example; finite wells allow the wavefunction to leak into the walls, lowering energies and permitting tunnelling.

Physics · Quantum Mechanics · about 2 minutes

Infinite square well (width L)

En = n2h2/(8mL2) = n2π2ħ2/(2mL2)
ψn = √(2/L) sin(nπx/L)
  • n = 1, 2, 3…; zero-point energy E1 > 0.
  • Narrower wells give larger energies.

Finite well (depth V0)

  • Inside: oscillating; outside: decaying e−κx, κ = √(2m(V0 − E))/ħ.
  • Finite number of bound states (at least one in 1D).
  • Energies lower than the infinite-well values.

Real examples

Quantum dots are nanocrystals whose size sets the energy gap: smaller dots emit bluer light (QLED displays; Nobel Chemistry 2023). Semiconductor quantum wells make efficient laser diodes. Electrons in conjugated molecules behave like particles in a box, explaining dye colours.

Worked example

Ground-state energy of an electron in a 1 nm infinite well?

  1. E1 = h2/(8mL2) = (6.63 × 10-34)2/(8 × 9.11 × 10-31 × 10-18)
  2. = 6.0 × 10-20 J ≈ 0.38 eV
Common mistakeThe particle can't have zero energy in a well; zero would violate the uncertainty principle.

Investigate with the lab. In the infinite well, halve the width and see every energy level go up four times. Then make a finite well so shallow that only one bound state is left.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. The ratio E3/E1 in an infinite well is:
    • 3
    • 6
    • 27
    • 9
    Show the answer

    Answer: 9. n2.

  2. Compared with an infinite well, a finite well's energies are:
    • Lower
    • Higher
    • The same
    • Zero
    Show the answer

    Answer: Lower. Wavefunction spreads out.

  3. An infinite well's width is halved. What happens to the ground-state energy?
    • It doubles
    • It increases four times
    • It halves
    • It stays the same
    Show the answer

    Answer: It increases four times. E ∝ 1/L², so halving L multiplies every level by four.
