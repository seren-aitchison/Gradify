Diffraction

Diffraction is the spreading and bending of light around obstacles and through apertures, producing patterns of light and dark. It's why light can't be focused to an arbitrarily small spot and why fine detail has a limit.

Physics · Optics · about 2 minutes

Regimes

  • Fraunhofer (far field): source and screen effectively at infinity (or with lenses); pattern is the aperture's Fourier transform.
  • Fresnel (near field): curved wavefronts; patterns change with distance (Fresnel number F = a2/(Lλ)).

Key results

a sin θ = mλsingle slit minima
sin θ = 1.22λ/Dfirst dark ring of a circular aperture (Airy disc)
  • Smaller apertures give wider patterns.

Uses and limits

  • Resolution of eyes, cameras, telescopes and microscopes.
  • Spectrometers (gratings), holograms, X-ray crystallography.
  • Lithography: chip features are limited by the diffraction of the light used, so the industry moved from 248 nm to 193 nm (with immersion) to 13.5 nm EUV.
Worked example

The pupil (3 mm) viewing at 550 nm. Smallest resolvable angle?

  1. θ = 1.22 × 5.5 × 10-7/0.003 = 2.2 × 10-4 rad ≈ 0.8′
  2. About the eye's actual acuity (~1′)
Common mistakeDiffraction happens at every edge and aperture; it's not a defect but a fundamental property of waves.

Investigate with the lab. Switch to single-slit mode and narrow the slit: the central maximum gets wider. Widen it and the light spreads less.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Halving an aperture's diameter makes the Airy disc:
    • Half as wide
    • Twice as wide
    • The same
    • Four times wider
    Show the answer

    Answer: Twice as wide. ∝ λ/D.

  2. Fraunhofer diffraction applies when:
    • The screen is very close
    • Light is incoherent
    • Source and screen are effectively at infinity
    • The aperture is large
    Show the answer

    Answer: Source and screen are effectively at infinity. Far field.

  3. What happens to the diffraction pattern when the slit is made narrower?
    • The central maximum gets narrower
    • The pattern disappears
    • The colours separate
    • The central maximum gets wider
    Show the answer

    Answer: The central maximum gets wider. The spread of the central maximum is roughly λ/a, so a narrower slit spreads the light more.
