Quantum Tunnelling

A quantum particle can pass through a barrier higher than its energy, because its wavefunction decays inside the barrier but doesn't vanish. Tunnelling powers the Sun, alpha decay, flash memory and the scanning tunnelling microscope.

Physics · Quantum Mechanics · about 2 minutes

Transmission

T ≈ e−2κd, κ = √(2m(V0 − E))/ħthick barrier
  • Extremely sensitive to barrier width d and to √(mass × height).
  • Electrons tunnel easily; protons less; macroscopic objects effectively never.

Examples

  • Alpha decay: Gamow (1928) explained half-lives ranging from 10-7 s to 1010 years by tunnelling through the Coulomb barrier.
  • Nuclear fusion in stars: protons tunnel through their Coulomb repulsion at "only" 15 million K.
  • Scanning tunnelling microscope (Binnig and Rohrer, Nobel 1986): tunnelling current changes ~10× per Å of gap: atomic resolution.
  • Flash memory: electrons tunnel onto a floating gate.
  • Tunnel diodes, Josephson junctions, enzyme reactions transferring protons.
Worked example

An electron (E = 1 eV) meets a 5 eV, 0.5 nm barrier. Estimate T.

  1. κ = √(2 × 9.11 × 10-31 × 4 × 1.6 × 10-19)/1.055 × 10-34 = 1.02 × 1010 m-1
  2. T ≈ e−10.2 ≈ 4 × 10-5
Common mistakeTunnelling doesn't violate energy conservation; the particle is never found inside the barrier with negative kinetic energy in any measurement.

Investigate with the lab. Get more than 10% of electrons through a barrier higher than their energy. Then make the barrier wider and watch the fraction fall.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Tunnelling probability increases if the barrier is:
    • Thicker
    • Higher
    • Thinner or lower
    • Heavier particle
    Show the answer

    Answer: Thinner or lower. e−2κd.

  2. Which technology relies on tunnelling?
    • Incandescent lamps
    • Transformers
    • Solar thermal panels
    • Flash memory
    Show the answer

    Answer: Flash memory. Floating gate.

  3. How does tunnelling probability depend on barrier width?
    • It falls roughly exponentially as the barrier widens
    • It rises as the barrier widens
    • It doesn't depend on width
    • It is zero for any finite barrier
    Show the answer

    Answer: It falls roughly exponentially as the barrier widens. The wavefunction decays exponentially inside the barrier.
