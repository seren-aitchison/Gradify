Amplitude, Frequency, Period and Phase

An oscillation x = A cos(ωt + φ) is fully specified by its amplitude A, angular frequency ω and phase constant φ. Phase tells you where in its cycle an oscillator is, and phase differences describe how two oscillations line up.

Physics · Oscillations and Vibrations · about 2 minutes

The parameters

  • Amplitude A: maximum displacement from equilibrium (set by initial conditions).
  • Angular frequency ω: set by the system's physics.
  • Phase ωt + φ: the angle in the cycle; φ is the phase at t = 0.
  • Initial conditions fix A and φ: A = √(x02 + (v0/ω)2), tan φ = −v0/(ωx0).

Phase difference

Δφ = 2πΔt/T
  • In phase: Δφ = 0 (or 2π). Antiphase: π. Quadrature: π/2.
  • On a phasor diagram, oscillations are rotating vectors; phase difference is the angle between them.
Worked example

Two pendulums of period 2.0 s pass equilibrium (moving right) 0.5 s apart. Find their phase difference.

  1. Δφ = 2π × 0.5/2.0 = π/2 (90°)
Common mistakePhase is often quoted in degrees or fractions of a cycle; check which, and remember 2π rad = 360° = 1 cycle.

Investigate with the lab. Change the amplitude and check that the period stays the same. Then read the period and work out the frequency from f = 1/T.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. x = 0.2 cos(5t + π/3). The amplitude and ω are:
    • 0.2 m, 5 rad s-1
    • 5 m, 0.2 rad s-1
    • 0.2 m, π/3
    • 0.4 m, 5
    Show the answer

    Answer: 0.2 m, 5 rad s-1. Read from the standard form.

  2. An oscillator released from rest at x = A has φ = ?
    • π/2
    • 0
    • π
    • −π/2
    Show the answer

    Answer: 0. x = A cos(ωt) at t = 0 gives A.

  3. For an ideal mass-spring oscillator, what happens to the period if you double the amplitude?
    • It doubles
    • It halves
    • It stays the same
    • It increases by √2
    Show the answer

    Answer: It stays the same. The period of SHM is independent of amplitude.
