Friction and Drag

Friction opposes relative sliding between surfaces; drag opposes motion through a fluid. Friction is roughly independent of speed and contact area, while drag grows with speed.

Physics · Classical Mechanics · about 2 minutes

Friction

fs ≤ μsNstatic friction: as large as needed, up to a maximum
fk = μkNkinetic friction, usually μk < μs
  • Microscopically, friction comes from bonds and interlocking at the small true contact area, which is ∝ N.
  • Friction is what lets you walk, drive and grip; on wheels rolling without slipping it's static friction.

Drag

FD ≈ bvlow speed, small objects, viscous (Stokes: b = 6πηr)
FD = ½ρCdAv2high speed, turbulent: most everyday objects
  • The Reynolds number Re = ρvL/η decides which regime applies.
  • Streamlining reduces Cd (car ≈ 0.3, flat plate ≈ 1.2).
Worked example

A 20 kg box on a floor with μs = 0.5, μk = 0.4. What force starts it moving, and what keeps it moving at constant speed? (g = 9.8)

  1. Start: μsmg = 98 N
  2. Keep moving: μkmg = 78 N
Common mistakeStatic friction isn't always μsN; that's only its maximum. On a stationary box pushed with 30 N, static friction is exactly 30 N.

Investigate with the lab. Switch air resistance on and off for the same launch and compare the paths. Then find the best angle with drag, which is below 45°.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. A 10 kg block is pushed with 20 N; μs = 0.5 (g = 10). The friction force is:
    • 50 N
    • 0
    • 20 N
    • 30 N
    Show the answer

    Answer: 20 N. Static friction matches the push until it exceeds 50 N.

  2. Doubling a car's speed multiplies the power needed to overcome drag by:
    • 2
    • 4
    • 16
    • 8
    Show the answer

    Answer: 8. P = Fv ∝ v3.

  3. With air resistance, why is the best launch angle below 45°?
    • Drag shortens long, high flights most, so a flatter path loses less range
    • Drag adds a forward force on the ball at high angles
    • Gravity is weaker at higher angles of launch
    • Drag only acts on the way down, so lower is safer
    Show the answer

    Answer: Drag shortens long, high flights most, so a flatter path loses less range. The longer and faster the flight, the more energy drag removes, favouring flatter trajectories.
