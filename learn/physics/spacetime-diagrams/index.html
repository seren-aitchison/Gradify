Spacetime Diagrams

Spacetime diagrams plot time against position, showing events as points and objects' histories as worldlines. They make relativity visual: light travels on 45° lines, and frames are skewed coordinate grids.

Physics · Special Relativity · about 2 minutes

Elements

  • Axes: ct (vertical) and x (horizontal), same units.
  • Event: a point. Worldline: an object's path.
  • Light rays: lines at 45°.
  • Massive objects: worldlines steeper than 45° (slower than light).

Moving frames

  • The ct′ axis (worldline of x′ = 0) tilts towards the light line by angle arctan(v/c).
  • The x′ axis (line of t′ = 0, simultaneous events) tilts up by the same angle.
  • Lines of simultaneity in S′ are parallel to x′: visibly different from S.
  • Unit lengths on primed axes are stretched (hyperbolic calibration).

Using them

Spacetime diagrams resolve paradoxes (twins, ladder in barn) by showing which events are simultaneous in which frame. Light cones divide spacetime into past, future and elsewhere. In general relativity, curved spacetime diagrams (Penrose diagrams) map black holes and the universe.

Worked example

Draw the worldline of an object at rest at x = 2 m and of a light flash leaving the origin.

  1. Object: vertical line at x = 2 m
  2. Light: 45° line from the origin
Common mistakeSpacetime diagrams use Minkowski geometry; the "length" of a tilted worldline segment isn't its Euclidean length on paper.

Investigate with the lab. Use the spacetime diagram and see how the train's time axis tilts as its speed rises.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. A vertical worldline represents an object:
    • At rest
    • Moving at c
    • Accelerating
    • Moving left
    Show the answer

    Answer: At rest. Δx = 0.

  2. A worldline at less than 45° to the x-axis would represent:
    • Rest
    • Faster-than-light motion
    • Light
    • Normal motion
    Show the answer

    Answer: Faster-than-light motion. Spacelike.

  3. On a spacetime diagram, what is a light ray's worldline?
    • A vertical line
    • A horizontal line
    • A line at 45°
    • A curve
    Show the answer

    Answer: A line at 45°. With ct on one axis and x on the other, light travels one unit of x per unit of ct.
