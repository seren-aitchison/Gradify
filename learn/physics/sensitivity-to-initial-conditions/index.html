Sensitivity to Initial Conditions

In chaotic systems, tiny initial differences grow exponentially; forecasts need ensembles and probabilities.

Physics · Nonlinear Dynamics and Chaos · about 2 minutes

The butterfly effect

  • Lorenz (1961) restarted a weather simulation with numbers rounded to three decimals; within weeks of model time it diverged completely.
  • Title of his 1972 talk: "Does the flap of a butterfly's wings in Brazil set off a tornado in Texas?"
  • Errors grow exponentially, not linearly.

Measuring it

  • Run two simulations from slightly different starts; plot ln|δ(t)| against t.
  • The slope gives the largest Lyapunov exponent.
  • Error growth saturates once δ is as large as the attractor.

Consequences

  • Ensemble forecasting: weather centres run ~50 perturbed forecasts to estimate uncertainty and probabilities.
  • Shadowing: numerical trajectories, though wrong in detail, stay close to some true trajectory.
  • Control: small, well-timed nudges can steer chaotic systems (OGY control), used in lasers and spacecraft trajectories.
  • Interplanetary missions exploit chaotic dynamics to change orbits with little fuel.
N(t) ∝ eλtnumber of distinguishable outcomes grows

Not all sensitivity is chaos

Unstable fixed points also show exponential divergence, but only temporarily and not in a bounded, recurring way. True chaos combines sensitivity with boundedness and aperiodicity, so trajectories keep separating and approaching across a bounded attractor.

Worked example

A double pendulum has λ ≈ 2 s-1. An error of 10-6 rad grows to 1 rad in about how long?

  1. t = ln(106)/2 ≈ 13.8/2 ≈ 7 s

Investigate with the lab. Set the difference between the twins to a millionth of a radian and time how long they stay together. Make the difference smaller and see how much longer they last.

Common mistakeSensitivity limits detailed prediction, not statistical prediction; climate averages remain forecastable even if weather is not.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Error growth in chaotic systems is:
    • Linear forever
    • Zero
    • Exponential until it saturates
    • Quadratic only
    Show the answer

    Answer: Exponential until it saturates. eλt, bounded by attractor size.

  2. OGY control uses:
    • Large forces
    • Random noise
    • Feedback disconnection
    • Small perturbations to stabilise orbits
    Show the answer

    Answer: Small perturbations to stabilise orbits. Chaos is easily nudged.

  3. Two double pendulums start a millionth of a radian apart. What happens?
    • They agree at first, then diverge completely
    • They stay together for ever
    • They diverge immediately and randomly
    • They always end up in the same place
    Show the answer

    Answer: They agree at first, then diverge completely. Sensitivity to initial conditions means small differences grow until the motions are unrelated.
