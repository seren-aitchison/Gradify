RL Circuits

An inductor opposes changes in current by generating a back EMF. In an RL circuit, current rises and falls exponentially with time constant L/R, and switching off can produce dangerous voltage spikes.

Physics · Electric Circuits · about 2 minutes

Inductance

ε = −L dI/dt
L = μ0N2A/lsolenoid
U = ½LI2energy stored in the magnetic field

Transients

I = (ε/R)(1 − e−Rt/L)switch on
I = I0e−Rt/Lswitch off through R
τ = L/R

Switching spikes

Interrupting current in an inductor suddenly makes dI/dt huge, so the back EMF can reach kilovolts: it sparks across switch contacts and can destroy transistors. Flyback diodes across relay coils give the current a path to decay safely. Car ignition coils exploit the effect deliberately to make sparks.

Frequency response

XL = ωL
  • Inductors pass DC and block high frequencies: chokes in power supplies, RL filters.
Worked example

L = 0.5 H, R = 10 Ω, ε = 12 V. Time constant and final current? Current after 0.05 s?

  1. τ = 0.05 s; Imax = 1.2 A
  2. I = 1.2(1 − e−1) = 0.76 A
Common mistakeInductors resist changes in current, not current itself; a steady DC current flows through an ideal inductor with no voltage across it.

Investigate with the lab. Switch to the inductor and watch the current rise to E/R while the inductor's voltage dies away.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. The energy stored in a 2 H inductor carrying 3 A is:
    • 9 J
    • 6 J
    • 18 J
    • 3 J
    Show the answer

    Answer: 9 J. ½LI2.

  2. An inductor's reactance:
    • Decreases with frequency
    • Increases with frequency
    • Is constant
    • Is zero at high f
    Show the answer

    Answer: Increases with frequency. XL = ωL.

  3. When an RL circuit is switched on, how does the current change?
    • It jumps instantly to E/R
    • It rises without limit
    • It rises gradually towards E/R
    • It stays at zero
    Show the answer

    Answer: It rises gradually towards E/R. The inductor's back emf opposes the change, so the current grows exponentially towards E/R.
