SI Base and Derived Units

Since 2019 no SI unit depends on a physical artefact; all are defined by exact constants, so they can be realised anywhere with the right experiment.

Physics · Foundations of Physics · about 2 minutes

The seven base units

  • second (s): fixed by the caesium-133 hyperfine frequency ΔνCs = 9 192 631 770 Hz.
  • metre (m): fixed by the speed of light c = 299 792 458 m s-1.
  • kilogram (kg): fixed by the Planck constant h = 6.626 070 15 × 10-34 J s (realised with a Kibble balance).
  • ampere (A): fixed by the elementary charge e = 1.602 176 634 × 10-19 C.
  • kelvin (K): fixed by the Boltzmann constant kB = 1.380 649 × 10-23 J K-1.
  • mole (mol): fixed by the Avogadro constant NA = 6.022 140 76 × 1023 mol-1.
  • candela (cd): fixed by the luminous efficacy Kcd = 683 lm W-1 at 540 THz.

Derived units

N = kg m s-2newton, force
J = N m = kg m2 s-2joule, energy
W = J s-1watt, power
Pa = N m-2pascal, pressure
C = A s; V = W A-1; Ω = V A-1coulomb, volt, ohm
Hz = s-1; T = kg s-2 A-1hertz, tesla
Worked example

Express the volt in base units.

  1. V = W/A = (J s-1)/A
  2. J = kg m2 s-2, so V = kg m2 s-3 A-1
Common mistakeThe radian and steradian are dimensionless derived units. Leaving "rad" out of ω in s-1 is legal but can hide errors between angular frequency ω and frequency f.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which is NOT an SI base unit?
    • Kelvin
    • Mole
    • Ampere
    • Newton
    Show the answer

    Answer: Newton. The newton is derived: kg m s-2.

  2. What is the tesla in SI base units?
    • kg s-2 A-1
    • kg m s-2 A-1
    • kg A s-2 m-1
    • kg m2 s-3 A-1
    Show the answer

    Answer: kg s-2 A-1. From F = BIL: B = N/(A m) = kg m s-2/(A m) = kg s-2 A-1.

  3. The metre is defined by fixing which quantity?
    • The wavelength of krypton-86 light
    • The speed of light
    • A platinum-iridium bar
    • The second and Avogadro's number
    Show the answer

    Answer: The speed of light. Since 1983 c is exact, so the metre is the distance light travels in 1/299 792 458 s.
