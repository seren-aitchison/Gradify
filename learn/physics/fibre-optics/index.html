Fibre Optics

Optical fibres guide light along thin glass strands by total internal reflection. They carry almost all long-distance internet traffic, and also illuminate, sense and image in medicine and industry.

Physics · Optics · about 2 minutes

Structure and guiding

NA = √(ncore2 − nclad2) = sin θacceptance
  • Single-mode: ~9 μm core, one mode, long distance.
  • Multimode: 50–62.5 μm core; modal dispersion limits bandwidth–distance.
  • Graded-index fibres reduce modal dispersion.

Losses and windows

  • Rayleigh scattering (∝ 1/λ4) and infrared absorption give a minimum ~0.2 dB km-1 at 1550 nm.
  • Windows at 850, 1310 and 1550 nm.
  • Erbium-doped fibre amplifiers boost signals optically.

Capacity

Wavelength-division multiplexing puts ~100 channels on one fibre, each carrying hundreds of Gb s-1; a single fibre pair can carry > 100 Tb s-1. Charles Kao (Nobel 2009) showed in 1966 that purifying glass could make long-distance fibre communication practical.

Other uses

  • Endoscopes (bundles of fibres), fibre lasers (industrial cutting), fibre sensors (strain, temperature, distributed acoustic sensing for earthquakes), gyroscopes.
Worked example

A 100 km link at 0.2 dB km-1. Fraction of power received?

  1. Loss = 20 dB ⇒ 1% received
Common mistakeBending a fibre too tightly lets light escape: TIR fails when the angle drops below critical.

Investigate with the lab. Find the critical angle for light leaving glass and push beyond it to trap the light by total internal reflection.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Optical fibres guide light by:
    • Mirrors
    • Total internal reflection
    • Diffraction
    • Absorption
    Show the answer

    Answer: Total internal reflection. Core n > cladding n.

  2. A 30 dB loss means the power received is:
    • 1%
    • 3%
    • 0.1%
    • 30%
    Show the answer

    Answer: 0.1%. 10-3.

  3. Why does light stay inside an optical fibre?
    • Refraction out of the fibre at each bend
    • Absorption by the cladding
    • The fibre is a mirror
    • Total internal reflection at the core's boundary
    Show the answer

    Answer: Total internal reflection at the core's boundary. Light hits the boundary above the critical angle, so it is totally reflected.
