Physics explained

54 physics lessons you can read here in full, each with a worked example or a diagram, the mistakes students make and practice questions with explanations. To track progress and revise with flashcards, open the free physics course.

Foundations of Physics

  • SI Base and Derived Units The International System of Units (SI) defines seven base units by fixing exact values of seven constants of nature.
  • Dimensional Analysis Every physical quantity has dimensions: powers of the base dimensions mass [M], length [L], time [T], current [I], temperature [Θ], amount [N] and luminous intensity [J].
  • Unit Conversions and Prefixes SI prefixes scale units by powers of ten; conversions multiply by factors equal to one.
  • Scalar and Vector Quantities A scalar has magnitude only; a vector has magnitude and direction and adds by the parallelogram rule.
  • Estimation and Order-of-magnitude Calculations An order-of-magnitude estimate gets the answer to within a factor of about 10 using rough inputs.
  • Conservation Laws A conservation law states that some quantity of an isolated system stays constant however the system changes.
  • Uncertainty and Measurement Limitations No measurement is exact.
  • Scientific Reasoning and Falsifiability Science advances by proposing hypotheses that make risky predictions and testing them against observation.

Mathematics for Physics

  • Complex Numbers A complex number z = x + iy, with i2 = −1, is a point in a plane.
  • Vectors and Vector Decomposition Vectors are added, scaled and multiplied using components.

Classical Mechanics

  • Kinematic Equations For constant acceleration, five quantities (s, u, v, a, t) are linked by the suvat equations.
  • Projectile Motion A projectile moves under gravity alone: constant velocity horizontally and constant acceleration g vertically.
  • Friction and Drag Friction opposes relative sliding between surfaces; drag opposes motion through a fluid.

Oscillations and Vibrations

  • Amplitude, Frequency, Period and Phase An oscillation x = A cos(ωt + φ) is fully specified by its amplitude A, angular frequency ω and phase constant φ.
  • Resonance Resonance is the large response of a system driven at (or near) its natural frequency.

Gravitation and Orbital Mechanics

  • Orbital Energy and Angular Momentum An orbit's total energy fixes its size (semi-major axis); its angular momentum fixes its shape.

Waves and Wave Motion

  • Interference Interference is the pattern of reinforcement and cancellation when coherent waves overlap.
  • Boundary Conditions Boundary conditions specify what a wave must do at the edges of its medium: fixed, free, or matched to another medium.

Thermodynamics

  • Equations of State An equation of state relates pressure, volume and temperature (and amount) for a substance.

Electricity and Electrostatics

  • Electric Fields An electric field is the force per unit charge that a test charge would feel at a point.
  • Electric Field Lines Field lines map an electric field: their direction gives the field's direction, and their density gives its strength.

Electric Circuits

  • Internal Resistance Every real source has internal resistance, so its terminal voltage drops when it supplies current.
  • RL Circuits An inductor opposes changes in current by generating a back EMF.
  • Transient Responses A transient is a circuit's temporary response when something changes suddenly, like a switch closing.

Magnetism and Electromagnetism

  • Lorentz Force The Lorentz force is the total electromagnetic force on a charged particle: the electric force plus the magnetic force.

Optics

  • Snell's Law Snell's law gives the angle of a refracted ray: the product of refractive index and the sine of the angle is the same on both sides of an interface.
  • Total Internal Reflection When light travelling in a dense medium meets a less dense one at an angle above the critical angle, none refracts out: it's totally reflected.
  • Thin Lenses A thin lens refracts light at two curved surfaces to bring rays to a focus (converging) or spread them (diverging).
  • Young's Double-slit Experiment Young's double-slit experiment sends light through two narrow slits and shows bright and dark fringes on a screen: interference of waves from the two slits.
  • Interference and Coherence Interference needs coherence: a stable phase relationship between waves.
  • Diffraction Diffraction is the spreading and bending of light around obstacles and through apertures, producing patterns of light and dark.
  • Single-slit and Multiple-slit Diffraction A single slit produces a broad central maximum with weaker side fringes.
  • Diffraction Gratings A diffraction grating has thousands of closely spaced slits or grooves.
  • Fibre Optics Optical fibres guide light along thin glass strands by total internal reflection.

Quantum Mechanics

  • Photoelectric Effect When light shines on a metal, electrons are emitted only if the light's frequency exceeds a threshold, however intense it is.
  • Wave-particle Duality Light and matter both show wave and particle behaviour.
  • Double-slit Experiments Sending particles one at a time through two slits builds up an interference pattern dot by dot.
  • Schrödinger Equation The Schrödinger equation governs how quantum states evolve in time.
  • Infinite and Finite Potential Wells A particle confined in a potential well can only have certain energies.
  • Quantum Tunnelling A quantum particle can pass through a barrier higher than its energy, because its wavefunction decays inside the barrier but doesn't vanish.
  • Quantum Harmonic Oscillator The quantum harmonic oscillator has evenly spaced energy levels and a nonzero ground-state energy.
  • Quantum Barriers and Scattering Quantum scattering describes how particles bounce off or pass through potentials.

Special Relativity

  • Speed of Light Invariance The speed of light in vacuum is the same for every inertial observer, whatever their motion or the source's motion.
  • Time Dilation A clock moving relative to you ticks more slowly than one at rest beside you, by the factor γ.
  • Length Contraction An object moving relative to you is shorter along its direction of motion than when measured at rest, by the factor γ.
  • Spacetime Diagrams Spacetime diagrams plot time against position, showing events as points and objects' histories as worldlines.
  • Twin Paradox One twin travels to a distant star at high speed and returns younger than the twin who stayed home.

Medical Physics

  • X-ray Production and Imaging X-rays are produced when fast electrons strike a metal target, creating a continuous bremsstrahlung spectrum topped with characteristic lines.
  • Magnetic Resonance Imaging Magnetic resonance imaging uses a strong magnetic field and radiofrequency pulses to make hydrogen nuclei in the body emit signals.
  • Ultrasound Imaging Ultrasound imaging sends short pulses of high-frequency sound into the body and listens for echoes from tissue boundaries.
  • Radiation Protection Radiation protection keeps exposures to patients, staff and the public as low as reasonably achievable while allowing the benefits of radiation.

Nonlinear Dynamics and Chaos

  • Deterministic Chaos Deterministic chaos is complex, unpredictable-looking behaviour arising from simple rules with no randomness.
  • Sensitivity to Initial Conditions Sensitivity to initial conditions, the butterfly effect, means that two almost identical starting states end up completely different.
  • Chaos in Mechanical and Electrical Systems Chaos is not just theory: it appears in pendulums, rotating machines, electronic circuits, lasers and orbits.
