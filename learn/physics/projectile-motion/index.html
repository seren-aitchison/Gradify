Projectile Motion

A projectile moves under gravity alone: constant velocity horizontally and constant acceleration g vertically. The path is a parabola, and range, height and time of flight all follow from separating the components.

Physics · Classical Mechanics · about 2 minutes

Results (launch speed u at angle θ, level ground, no drag)

x = u cos θ t; y = u sin θ t − ½gt2
T = 2u sin θ/gtime of flight
H = u2sin2θ/(2g)maximum height
R = u2sin 2θ/grange: maximum at θ = 45°
y = x tan θ − gx2/(2u2cos2θ)the parabolic path
  • Complementary angles (30° and 60°) give the same range.
  • At the top, v is horizontal: u cos θ.

With air resistance

Drag shortens range and height, makes the descent steeper than the ascent, and lowers the optimum angle below 45° (around 30–40° for a golf ball, but backspin lift changes things).

Worked example

A ball is kicked at 20 m s-1 at 30° (g = 9.8). Find its range and maximum height.

  1. R = 400 × sin 60°/9.8 = 35 m
  2. H = 400 × 0.25/19.6 = 5.1 m
Common mistakeThe horizontal velocity doesn't change during flight (no drag); only the vertical component is affected by g.

Investigate with the lab. Turn air resistance off and launch at several angles at the same speed. Find the angle that gives the greatest range, then check that 30° and 60° land in the same place.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. A ball rolls off a 1.25 m high table at 2.0 m s-1 (g = 10). How far from the table does it land?
    • 0.5 m
    • 2.0 m
    • 1.0 m
    • 2.5 m
    Show the answer

    Answer: 1.0 m. Fall time 0.5 s; 2.0 × 0.5 = 1.0 m.

  2. Two projectiles are launched at the same speed at 20° and 70°. Which lands farther?
    • 20°
    • 70°
    • Depends on mass
    • Same range
    Show the answer

    Answer: Same range. sin 40° = sin 140°.

  3. A monkey drops from a branch at the instant a dart is fired straight at it. The dart:
    • Hits the monkey (both fall the same distance in the same time)
    • Passes above
    • Passes below
    • Hits only if fired at 45°
    Show the answer

    Answer: Hits the monkey (both fall the same distance in the same time). Gravity deflects both equally.

  4. Without air resistance, which pair of launch angles gives the same range at the same speed?
    • 30° and 45°
    • 30° and 60°
    • 20° and 40°
    • 45° and 90°
    Show the answer

    Answer: 30° and 60°. Range depends on sin 2θ, and sin 60° = sin 120°, so complementary angles give equal ranges.
