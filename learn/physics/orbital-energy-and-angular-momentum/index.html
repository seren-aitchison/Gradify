Orbital Energy and Angular Momentum

An orbit's total energy fixes its size (semi-major axis); its angular momentum fixes its shape. In circular orbits, kinetic energy is half the magnitude of potential energy and total energy is negative.

Physics · Gravitation and Orbital Mechanics · about 2 minutes

Circular orbit energies

KE = GMm/(2r); U = −GMm/r; E = −GMm/(2r) = −KEvirial theorem: 2KE = −U

Ellipses

E = −GMm/(2a)
L = m√(GMa(1 − e2))
  • For fixed energy, maximum L gives a circle; L → 0 gives a straight-line plunge.
  • Effective potential Ueff = −GMm/r + L2/(2mr2): the L term acts like a centrifugal barrier; the turning points are periapsis and apoapsis.

Raising an orbit

To raise a circular orbit you add energy (fire forward), but at the new, higher orbit the speed is lower. KE falls by the same amount the total energy rises; potential energy rises by twice that.

Worked example

A 500 kg satellite moves from r = 7000 km to 8000 km (circular). Find ΔE, ΔKE and ΔU.

  1. GMm = 3.99 × 1014 × 500 = 2.0 × 1017
  2. E: −14.25 GJ → −12.47 GJ, ΔE = +1.78 GJ; ΔKE = −1.78 GJ; ΔU = +3.56 GJ
Common mistakeTotal orbital energy is negative for bound orbits; "more energy" means less negative.

Investigate with the lab. On an eccentric orbit, watch the kinetic and potential energy exchange while the total stays constant. Then give the planet escape velocity and note the total energy reaches zero.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. For a circular orbit, if KE = 5 GJ, the total energy is:
    • 5 GJ
    • −5 GJ
    • −10 GJ
    • 10 GJ
    Show the answer

    Answer: −5 GJ. E = −KE.

  2. The L2/(2mr2) term in the effective potential:
    • Represents drag
    • Is the gravitational PE
    • Prevents the orbit reaching r = 0
    • Is always zero
    Show the answer

    Answer: Prevents the orbit reaching r = 0. It's a centrifugal barrier.

  3. What is the sign of the total energy of a bound orbit?
    • Positive
    • Zero
    • It changes sign each orbit
    • Negative
    Show the answer

    Answer: Negative. A bound orbit has total energy below zero; at escape velocity it reaches zero.
