Snell's Law

Snell's law gives the angle of a refracted ray: the product of refractive index and the sine of the angle is the same on both sides of an interface. It follows from the change in wave speed, via Huygens' or Fermat's principle.

Physics · Optics · about 2 minutes

The law

n1 sin θ1 = n2 sin θ2
sin θ1/sin θ2 = v1/v2 = λ1/λ2

Derivations

  • Huygens: wavelets travel slower in medium 2, so the wavefront tilts.
  • Fermat: minimising travel time between two points across the interface gives Snell's law (like a lifeguard running then swimming).
  • Wave matching: the tangential component of k must be continuous: k1 sin θ1 = k2 sin θ2.

Measuring n

Trace rays through a glass block at several angles; plot sin i against sin r; the gradient is n. Or find the critical angle, or use a refractometer. Refractive index identifies gems and measures sugar concentration in fruit juice.

Worked example

A gradient graph of sin i against sin r has gradient 1.48. What is the block likely made of?

  1. n = 1.48: Perspex/acrylic (crown glass ~1.52)
Common mistakeUse sines of angles, not the angles themselves; θ1/θ2 ≠ n2/n1 except at small angles.

Investigate with the lab. Measure the angles of incidence and refraction at three settings and check that sin i / sin r stays constant.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Light goes from water (1.33) to glass (1.5) at 30°. Refraction angle:
    • 30°
    • 34°
    • 26°
    • 45°
    Show the answer

    Answer: 26°. sin θ2 = 1.33 × 0.5/1.5.

  2. A graph of sin i against sin r for light entering a block has gradient:
    • 1/n
    • Critical angle
    • Zero
    • n of the block
    Show the answer

    Answer: n of the block. sin i = n sin r.

  3. Light enters glass (n = 1.5) at 30°. What is sin r?
    • About 0.33
    • 0.75
    • 0.5
    • 1.5
    Show the answer

    Answer: About 0.33. sin r = sin 30°/1.5 = 0.5/1.5 ≈ 0.33.
