Statistical Thermodynamics

Statistical thermodynamics connects microstates to bulk properties; the Boltzmann distribution gives relative populations of energy levels and S = k ln W links entropy to the number of microstates.

Chemistry · Physical Chemistry and Molecular Science · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain how microscopic states relate to macroscopic properties
  • Describe the Boltzmann distribution
  • Relate entropy to the number of microstates

Microstates and macrostates

  • A macrostate is described by bulk properties such as T, P and V.
  • A microstate is one specific arrangement of molecules among energy levels.
  • Many microstates correspond to one macrostate.
  • The most probable macrostate has the most microstates.

Key results

  • Boltzmann distribution: Nᵢ/N₀ = e^(−ΔE/kT).
  • Boltzmann entropy: S = k ln W, where W is the number of microstates.
  • Higher temperature populates higher levels.
  • Thermodynamic quantities follow from energy levels.

Statistical thermodynamics explains why entropy increases: systems move towards macrostates with more microstates simply because they are overwhelmingly more probable. Boltzmann's equation S = k ln W is engraved on his tombstone in Vienna.

Worked example

Population of an excited level.

  1. A level lies 2.0 × 10⁻²¹ J above the ground state.
  2. At 300 K, kT = 4.14 × 10⁻²¹ J.
  3. N₁/N₀ = e^(−2.0/4.14) = e^(−0.48) ≈ 0.62.
  4. About 62% as many molecules occupy the upper level.
Common mistakeThinking entropy is just disorder. It is a measure of the number of accessible microstates.

In 3 lines: Micro to macro. Boltzmann distribution. S = k ln W.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What does W represent in S = k ln W?
    • The work done by the system
    • The weight of the sample
    • The number of microstates
    • The wavelength of light
    Show the answer

    Answer: The number of microstates. More accessible microstates mean higher entropy, as S = k ln W shows.

  2. Why does entropy tend to increase?
    • Energy is always destroyed in natural processes
    • Molecules naturally prefer ordered arrangements
    • Temperature always falls as time passes
    • Macrostates with more microstates are overwhelmingly more probable
    Show the answer

    Answer: Macrostates with more microstates are overwhelmingly more probable. Probability drives systems towards the most numerous arrangements.

  3. Why do few molecules occupy high electronic levels at room temperature?
    • Electronic gaps are much larger than kT
    • kT is larger than electronic gaps
    • Boltzmann's law does not apply
    • All levels are equally occupied
    Show the answer

    Answer: Electronic gaps are much larger than kT. A large ΔE/kT makes the Boltzmann factor e^(−ΔE/kT) extremely small.

Key terms

Microstate
A specific arrangement of particles among energy levels
Boltzmann distribution
Populations of levels as a function of energy and temperature
Boltzmann constant
k = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹
