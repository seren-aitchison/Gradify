Introduction to Organic Chemistry

Organic chemistry studies carbon compounds, usually with hydrogen and often other elements; carbon's bonding allows millions of compounds used as fuels, medicines and materials and found in living things, and Wöhler's urea synthesis began the end of vitalism.

Chemistry · Organic Chemistry Foundations · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define organic chemistry
  • Explain why carbon forms so many compounds
  • Describe the importance of organic chemistry

Scope

  • Organic chemistry studies compounds based on carbon, usually with hydrogen.
  • Many also contain oxygen, nitrogen, sulfur, phosphorus and halogens.
  • Millions of organic compounds are known.
  • Simple carbon compounds such as CO₂ and carbonates are usually classed as inorganic.

Importance

  • Fuels: petrol, diesel and natural gas.
  • Medicines: aspirin, paracetamol and antibiotics.
  • Materials: plastics, fibres and dyes.
  • Life: proteins, carbohydrates, fats and DNA.

In 1828 Friedrich Wöhler made urea, a compound from living things, from inorganic ammonium cyanate. This undermined the idea that organic compounds need a "vital force". Today organic synthesis makes most medicines and many materials.

Worked example

Wöhler's urea synthesis.

  1. Wöhler heated ammonium cyanate, NH₄OCN.
  2. It rearranged to urea, CO(NH₂)₂.
  3. Urea had previously been obtained only from urine.
  4. This showed organic compounds can be made in the laboratory.
Common mistakeThinking "organic" means natural or chemical-free. In chemistry, it simply means compounds based on carbon.

In 3 lines: Chemistry of carbon. Fuels, medicines, materials. End of vitalism.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which of these is an organic compound?
    • Sodium chloride, NaCl
    • Ethanol, C₂H₅OH
    • Water, H₂O
    • Calcium carbonate, CaCO₃
    Show the answer

    Answer: Ethanol, C₂H₅OH. Ethanol is built on a chain of carbon atoms bonded to hydrogen.

  2. Why was Wöhler's urea synthesis important?
    • It proved that urea is a metal compound
    • It showed that carbon cannot form chains
    • It showed organic compounds can be made without living things
    • It was the first synthesis of water
    Show the answer

    Answer: It showed organic compounds can be made without living things. Making urea from an inorganic salt challenged the idea of a vital force.

  3. Why is CO₂ usually treated as inorganic although it contains carbon?
    • It contains no carbon atoms
    • It is only found in living things
    • It is a metal oxide with ionic bonding
    • It is a simple compound with no C–H bonds and behaves like inorganic oxides
    Show the answer

    Answer: It is a simple compound with no C–H bonds and behaves like inorganic oxides. Convention groups simple carbon oxides and carbonates with inorganic chemistry.
