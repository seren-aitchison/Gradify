Reaction Mechanisms

Mechanisms are sequences of elementary steps with defined molecularity that add to the overall equation; the rate-determining step and earlier steps determine the rate equation, so mechanisms are tested against experimental rate equations.

Chemistry · Chemical Kinetics · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define elementary steps and molecularity
  • Relate a mechanism to the rate equation
  • Evaluate whether a proposed mechanism is consistent with data

Mechanisms

  • Most reactions occur in several elementary steps.
  • Molecularity is the number of particles in an elementary step: unimolecular, bimolecular or termolecular.
  • Steps add up to the overall equation.
  • Intermediates are formed in one step and used in another.

Linking to rate

  • For an elementary step, the rate equation follows its molecularity.
  • The rate-determining step controls the overall rate.
  • Species in the rate equation appear in or before the slow step.
  • A mechanism must match the experimental rate equation and the overall equation.

Termolecular steps are rare because three particles rarely collide at once. A proposed mechanism can be shown to be wrong if it conflicts with data, but it can never be proved correct, only supported. Mechanisms explain stereochemistry and product distributions as well as rates.

Worked example

Testing a mechanism.

  1. For 2NO₂ + F₂ → 2NO₂F, rate = k[NO₂][F₂].
  2. Proposed: Step 1 (slow) NO₂ + F₂ → NO₂F + F; Step 2 (fast) NO₂ + F → NO₂F.
  3. The slow step involves one NO₂ and one F₂, matching the rate equation.
  4. The steps add to the overall equation, so the mechanism is consistent.
Common mistakeThinking a mechanism is proved by matching the rate equation. Data can only support or rule out a mechanism.

In 3 lines: Elementary steps. Slow step sets rate. Consistent with data.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What must the steps of a mechanism add up to?
    • The overall balanced equation
    • The rate equation only
    • The activation energy
    • The equilibrium constant
    Show the answer

    Answer: The overall balanced equation. Intermediates cancel when steps are summed.

  2. Which species appear in the rate equation?
    • Only the products
    • Those in or before the rate-determining step
    • Only species after the slow step
    • Every species in the overall equation
    Show the answer

    Answer: Those in or before the rate-determining step. Steps after the slow step do not limit the rate.

  3. Why can a mechanism never be proved correct?
    • Mechanisms are always wrong
    • Rate equations cannot be measured
    • Other mechanisms might also fit the data
    • Intermediates never exist
    Show the answer

    Answer: Other mechanisms might also fit the data. Agreement with data supports but does not prove a mechanism.

Key terms

Elementary step
A single step in a mechanism
Molecularity
The number of particles in an elementary step
Mechanism
The sequence of steps from reactants to products
