Molecular Orbital Theory

Molecular orbital theory combines atomic orbitals into lower-energy bonding and higher-energy antibonding orbitals; bond order is half the difference between bonding and antibonding electrons, and unpaired π* electrons explain oxygen's paramagnetism.

Chemistry · Chemical Bonding and Molecular Structure · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe how atomic orbitals combine to form molecular orbitals
  • Calculate bond order from molecular orbital diagrams
  • Explain the paramagnetism of oxygen

Orbitals

  • Atomic orbitals combine to form bonding and antibonding molecular orbitals.
  • Bonding orbitals are lower in energy; antibonding (*) orbitals are higher.
  • Electrons fill molecular orbitals following the Aufbau principle, Pauli principle and Hund's rule.
  • σ and π molecular orbitals form from different overlaps.

Bond order and magnetism

  • Bond order = ½ × (bonding electrons − antibonding electrons).
  • Bond order 0 means no stable bond, such as He₂.
  • Higher bond order means a stronger, shorter bond.
  • O₂ has two unpaired electrons in π* orbitals, so it is paramagnetic.

Lewis structures predict O₂ has all electrons paired, but liquid oxygen is attracted to a magnet, which molecular orbital theory explains. MO theory also explains delocalisation and colour in conjugated molecules.

Worked example

Bond order of N₂.

  1. N₂ has 10 valence electrons.
  2. Eight are in bonding orbitals and two in antibonding orbitals.
  3. Bond order = ½ × (8 − 2) = 3.
  4. This matches the N≡N triple bond.
Common mistakeThinking antibonding orbitals are empty by definition. They can hold electrons, which weaken the bond.

In 3 lines: Bonding and antibonding orbitals. Bond order. Paramagnetic oxygen.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is the bond order of He₂?
    • 1
    • 2
    • 0
    • 0.5
    Show the answer

    Answer: 0. Equal bonding and antibonding electrons give no net bond.

  2. How does an antibonding orbital affect a bond?
    • It strengthens the bond
    • It has no effect
    • It always breaks the molecule
    • Electrons in it weaken the bond
    Show the answer

    Answer: Electrons in it weaken the bond. Antibonding electrons cancel some bonding effect.

  3. What does MO theory explain about O₂ that Lewis structures do not?
    • Its paramagnetism from two unpaired electrons
    • Its double bond length
    • Its colourless gas state
    • Its molecular mass
    Show the answer

    Answer: Its paramagnetism from two unpaired electrons. Lewis structures wrongly pair all electrons in O₂.

Key terms

Molecular orbital
An orbital spread over a whole molecule
Antibonding orbital
A higher-energy orbital weakening a bond
Bond order
Half the difference between bonding and antibonding electrons
