Industrial Electrochemistry

Aluminium is extracted by electrolysing alumina in molten cryolite, using huge amounts of electricity and consuming carbon anodes; the chlor-alkali process makes chlorine, hydrogen and NaOH, electrorefining purifies copper and recycling and cleaner cells reduce impacts.

Chemistry · Redox and Electrochemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the extraction of aluminium by electrolysis
  • Describe the chlor-alkali process
  • Evaluate the energy and environmental impacts of electrochemical industries

Aluminium extraction

  • Alumina (Al₂O₃) is dissolved in molten cryolite, lowering the melting point.
  • Cathode: Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al.
  • Anode: 2O²⁻ → O₂ + 4e⁻; oxygen burns the carbon anodes, which must be replaced.
  • Requires very large amounts of electricity.

Other processes

  • Chlor-alkali: electrolysis of brine produces chlorine, hydrogen and sodium hydroxide; membrane cells keep products separate.
  • Electrorefining of copper: impure copper anode, pure copper cathode.
  • Electroplating coats objects with metals such as chromium or silver.
  • Plants are often sited near cheap electricity, such as hydroelectric power.

The Hall-Héroult process, invented in 1886, made aluminium affordable. Recycling aluminium saves about 95% of the energy. Modern membrane cells replaced mercury cells in chlor-alkali production, removing mercury pollution.

Worked example

Refining copper.

  1. An impure copper anode and a pure copper cathode sit in copper sulfate.
  2. At the anode, Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻; impurities fall as anode sludge.
  3. At the cathode, Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu deposits pure copper.
  4. Precious metals are recovered from the sludge.
Common mistakeThinking aluminium is extracted by heating with carbon. It is too reactive and must be extracted by electrolysis.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Why must carbon anodes be replaced in aluminium extraction?
    • They dissolve slowly in the molten cryolite
    • They turn into aluminium
    • Oxygen produced at the anode burns them away
    • They melt immediately
    Show the answer

    Answer: Oxygen produced at the anode burns them away. Hot oxygen reacts with carbon to form CO₂.

  2. Why are aluminium smelters often built near hydroelectric power?
    • Aluminium ore is found near dams
    • Water is needed to cool the metal
    • Hydroelectric power makes cryolite
    • The process needs huge amounts of cheap electricity
    Show the answer

    Answer: The process needs huge amounts of cheap electricity. Electricity is the biggest cost of production.

  3. Why did membrane cells replace mercury cells in chlor-alkali production?
    • They avoid mercury pollution of the environment
    • Mercury cells were cheaper
    • Membrane cells use more electricity
    • Mercury produces more chlorine
    Show the answer

    Answer: They avoid mercury pollution of the environment. Removing mercury eliminated a serious toxic hazard.

Key terms

Cryolite
A molten mineral used to dissolve alumina
Chlor-alkali process
Electrolysis of brine to make chlorine and NaOH
Electrorefining
Purifying a metal by electrolysis
