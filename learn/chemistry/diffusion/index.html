Diffusion

Diffusion is net movement from high to low concentration by random motion, faster in gases, at higher temperature and with steeper gradients; Graham's law states effusion rate is inversely proportional to the square root of molar mass.

Chemistry · States of Matter and Physical Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define diffusion and explain it using particle motion
  • Explain factors affecting the rate of diffusion
  • Apply Graham's law of effusion

Diffusion

  • Diffusion: net movement of particles from high to low concentration due to random motion.
  • It continues until concentrations are uniform.
  • Faster in gases than liquids and very slow in solids.
  • Rate increases with temperature and concentration gradient.

Graham's law

  • Effusion is the escape of gas through a tiny hole.
  • Graham's law: rate of effusion ∝ 1/√M.
  • Lighter gases effuse and diffuse faster.
  • This was used to separate uranium isotopes as UF₆.

In the classic experiment, ammonia and hydrogen chloride diffuse from opposite ends of a tube; a white ring of ammonium chloride forms nearer the HCl end because NH₃ (M = 17) moves faster than HCl (M = 36.5). Diffusion in liquids is slower because particles collide far more often.

Worked example

The ammonia and HCl tube.

  1. Cotton wool soaked in ammonia is placed at one end of a glass tube.
  2. Cotton wool soaked in hydrochloric acid is placed at the other.
  3. A white ring of NH₄Cl forms closer to the HCl end.
  4. Lighter NH₃ molecules travelled further in the same time.
Common mistakeThinking diffusion stops at equilibrium because particles stop moving. Particles keep moving; there is just no net change.

In 3 lines: High to low concentration. Faster in gases and when hot. Lighter is faster.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Where does the white ring form in the NH₃ and HCl tube experiment?
    • Nearer the NH₃ end
    • Exactly in the middle
    • Outside the tube
    • Nearer the HCl end
    Show the answer

    Answer: Nearer the HCl end. NH₃ is lighter, so it travels further before meeting HCl.

  2. How does temperature affect the rate of diffusion?
    • Higher temperature increases the rate
    • Higher temperature decreases the rate
    • Temperature has no effect
    • Diffusion stops when warm
    Show the answer

    Answer: Higher temperature increases the rate. Faster-moving particles spread out more quickly when warmer.

  3. Why does diffusion continue at the molecular level after concentrations are uniform?
    • Particles stop moving at equilibrium
    • Particles keep moving randomly, with no net change
    • Diffusion reverses at equilibrium
    • New particles are created
    Show the answer

    Answer: Particles keep moving randomly, with no net change. Equal movement in all directions gives no overall change.

Key terms

Diffusion
Net spreading of particles down a concentration gradient
Effusion
Escape of gas through a tiny hole
Graham's law
Effusion rate is proportional to 1/√M
