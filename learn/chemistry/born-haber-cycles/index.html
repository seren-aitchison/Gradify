Born-Haber Cycles

Born-Haber cycles combine enthalpy of formation, atomisation, ionisation energy, electron affinity and lattice enthalpy using Hess's law, allowing lattice enthalpy to be calculated, and explain why some compounds do not form.

Chemistry · Chemical Bonding and Molecular Structure · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Identify the enthalpy changes in a Born-Haber cycle
  • Construct a Born-Haber cycle for an ionic compound
  • Calculate lattice enthalpy from a cycle

The steps

  • Enthalpy of formation of the compound from elements.
  • Atomisation of the metal and non-metal.
  • Ionisation energy of the metal.
  • Electron affinity of the non-metal.
  • Lattice enthalpy forming the solid from gaseous ions.

Using the cycle

  • By Hess's law, the total enthalpy change is the same by either route.
  • ΔfH = ΔatH(metal) + ΔatH(non-metal) + IE + EA + ΔlattH.
  • Rearranging gives the lattice enthalpy.
  • First electron affinities are usually exothermic; second electron affinities are endothermic.

The second electron affinity of oxygen is endothermic because adding an electron to O⁻ involves repulsion. Born-Haber cycles also show why some compounds, such as MgCl, do not exist: their formation would be less favourable than MgCl₂.

Worked example

Lattice enthalpy of NaCl.

  1. ΔfH(NaCl) = −411; ΔatH(Na) = +107; ΔatH(Cl) = +122.
  2. IE(Na) = +496; EA(Cl) = −349 (all kJ mol⁻¹).
  3. −411 = 107 + 122 + 496 − 349 + ΔlattH.
  4. ΔlattH = −787 kJ mol⁻¹.
Common mistakeForgetting to double atomisation or electron affinity for formulae such as MgCl₂. Multiply by the number of atoms involved.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which step converts gaseous metal atoms into ions?
    • Electron affinity
    • Atomisation
    • Lattice enthalpy
    • Ionisation energy
    Show the answer

    Answer: Ionisation energy. Ionisation energy removes electrons from gaseous atoms.

  2. In a cycle for MgCl₂, why is chlorine's electron affinity doubled?
    • Two chlorine atoms each gain one electron
    • Chlorine gains two electrons each
    • Magnesium gains electrons
    • Only one chlorine is involved
    Show the answer

    Answer: Two chlorine atoms each gain one electron. Each of the two Cl atoms contributes one electron affinity.

  3. Why doesn't MgCl exist as a stable compound?
    • Magnesium atoms cannot lose any electrons at all
    • Forming MgCl₂ releases much more energy due to its larger lattice enthalpy
    • Chlorine cannot gain electrons
    • MgCl is too ionic
    Show the answer

    Answer: Forming MgCl₂ releases much more energy due to its larger lattice enthalpy. The extra lattice energy of Mg²⁺ outweighs the second ionisation energy.

Key terms

Born-Haber cycle
An energy cycle for forming an ionic compound
Enthalpy of atomisation
Energy to form one mole of gaseous atoms
Electron affinity
Enthalpy change when gaseous atoms gain electrons
