Protecting Groups

Protecting groups such as silyl and benzyl ethers, Boc and Fmoc, acetals and esters mask reactive groups; they must add and remove in high yield and survive intermediate steps, and orthogonal groups can be removed selectively.

Chemistry · Synthetic Chemistry and Laboratory Methods · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe common protecting groups for alcohols, amines and carbonyls
  • Explain conditions for adding and removing protecting groups
  • Explain orthogonal protection

Common groups

  • Alcohols: silyl ethers (TBS), removed by fluoride; benzyl ethers, removed by hydrogenation.
  • Amines: Boc, removed by acid; Fmoc, removed by base.
  • Carbonyls: acetals, removed by aqueous acid.
  • Carboxylic acids: esters, removed by hydrolysis.

Good practice

  • A protecting group must go on and come off in high yield.
  • It must survive the conditions of the steps in between.
  • Orthogonal groups are removed by different conditions, so one can be removed while others stay.
  • Every protecting group adds two steps, so use them sparingly.

Peptide synthesis relies on orthogonal protection: Fmoc on the amine is removed by base at each cycle, while side-chain groups removed by acid stay until the end. This allows proteins to be built amino acid by amino acid.

Worked example

Orthogonal protection.

  1. A molecule has an amine protected with Boc and another with Fmoc.
  2. Treatment with piperidine removes Fmoc only.
  3. The free amine reacts in the next step.
  4. Later, acid removes the Boc group.
Common mistakeChoosing a protecting group removed by the same conditions as a later step. It will come off too early.

In 3 lines: Mask, react, unmask. High yield, stable. Orthogonal sets.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which amine protecting group is removed by acid?
    • Fmoc
    • Benzyl ester
    • Boc
    • Acetal
    Show the answer

    Answer: Boc. Acid cleaves the tert-butyl carbamate, releasing the free amine.

  2. Why should protecting groups be used sparingly?
    • They always make reactions faster
    • They never need removing
    • They increase atom economy
    • Each one adds two extra steps and lowers overall yield
    Show the answer

    Answer: Each one adds two extra steps and lowers overall yield. Extra steps cost time, material and yield.

  3. Why is orthogonal protection essential in peptide synthesis?
    • The amine can be freed each cycle while side chains stay protected
    • All groups must be removed at once
    • Peptides have no reactive side chains
    • Orthogonal groups cannot be removed
    Show the answer

    Answer: The amine can be freed each cycle while side chains stay protected. Selective deprotection allows stepwise chain building.

Key terms

Orthogonal protection
Groups removed by different, non-interfering conditions
Boc
tert-Butoxycarbonyl, an acid-labile amine protecting group
Fmoc
Fluorenylmethoxycarbonyl, a base-labile amine protecting group
