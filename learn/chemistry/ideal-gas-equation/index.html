Ideal Gas Equation

The ideal gas equation pV = nRT uses SI units with R = 8.314 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹, allowing calculation of amount, volume, pressure and molar mass, with one mole occupying about 24 dm³ at room conditions.

Chemistry · States of Matter and Physical Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • State the ideal gas equation and its units
  • Calculate moles, volume or pressure using pV = nRT
  • Calculate molar mass from gas data

The equation

  • pV = nRT.
  • p in pascals, V in m³, n in moles, T in kelvin.
  • R = 8.314 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹, the gas constant.
  • 1 dm³ = 1 × 10⁻³ m³; 1 cm³ = 1 × 10⁻⁶ m³.

Uses

  • Find the amount of gas: n = pV ÷ RT.
  • Find molar mass: M = mRT ÷ pV.
  • Molar volume: one mole of gas occupies about 24.0 dm³ at 298 K and 101 kPa.
  • The equation assumes ideal behaviour.

The ideal gas equation combines Boyle's, Charles's and Avogadro's laws. Most errors come from unit conversions, especially volumes. It allows the molar mass of volatile liquids to be found by vaporising a known mass.

Worked example

Finding molar mass.

  1. 0.50 g of a gas occupies 3.0 × 10⁻⁴ m³ at 298 K and 100,000 Pa.
  2. n = pV ÷ RT = (100,000 × 3.0 × 10⁻⁴) ÷ (8.314 × 298) = 0.0121 mol.
  3. M = 0.50 ÷ 0.0121.
  4. M ≈ 41 g mol⁻¹.
Common mistakeUsing volume in dm³ with pressure in pascals. Volume must be in m³ when pressure is in pascals.

In 3 lines: pV = nRT. SI units. Amount and molar mass.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is the approximate molar volume of a gas at room temperature and pressure?
    • 2.4 dm³
    • 240 dm³
    • 0.24 dm³
    • 24 dm³
    Show the answer

    Answer: 24 dm³. One mole occupies about 24 dm³ at 298 K and 101 kPa.

  2. Which rearrangement gives the amount of gas?
    • n = pV ÷ RT
    • n = RT ÷ pV
    • n = pVRT
    • n = p ÷ VRT
    Show the answer

    Answer: n = pV ÷ RT. Dividing both sides of pV = nRT by RT isolates n.

  3. Why must volume be in m³ when using pressure in Pa?
    • Pascals only work with litres
    • SI units must be consistent with R in J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹
    • m³ is always smaller than dm³
    • R changes value with every unit
    Show the answer

    Answer: SI units must be consistent with R in J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. Mixing units gives answers wrong by factors of a thousand.
