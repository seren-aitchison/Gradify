Ionisation Energy Trends

First ionisation energy increases across periods and decreases down groups, with dips at groups 2–13 and 15–16 explained by p-orbital energy and electron pairing; jumps in successive ionisation energies reveal the number of outer electrons.

Chemistry · Periodic Table and Periodicity · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain the general trends in first ionisation energy
  • Explain the dips between groups 2 and 3 and groups 15 and 16
  • Use successive ionisation energies to identify an element's group

General trends

  • First ionisation energy increases across a period, as nuclear charge rises and radius falls.
  • It decreases down a group, as the outer electron is further away and more shielded.
  • Noble gases have the highest values in each period.
  • Group 1 metals have the lowest.

Exceptions and successive values

  • Group 2 → 13 dip: the electron in group 13 is removed from a higher-energy p orbital, as in Mg to Al.
  • Group 15 → 16 dip: in group 16, one p orbital holds a pair, and repulsion makes it easier to remove, as in P to S.
  • Successive ionisation energies jump when an electron is removed from a new, inner shell.
  • The position of the big jump shows the number of outer electrons.

Ionisation energy data were key evidence for the existence of subshells. The dips show that electrons in p orbitals are slightly higher in energy than those in s orbitals of the same shell, and that paired electrons repel. Successive values for one element always increase, because each electron is removed from an increasingly positive ion.

Worked example

Finding the group.

  1. An element's successive ionisation energies (kJ mol⁻¹) are 578, 1,817, 2,745 and 11,577.
  2. The big jump comes after the third electron.
  3. So it has three outer electrons.
  4. It is in group 13: aluminium.
Common mistakeThinking the dips disprove the general trend. They reveal subshells and electron pairing within the overall pattern.

In 3 lines: Up across, down down. Two dips. Jumps show shells.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which element in period 3 has the highest first ionisation energy?
    • Sodium
    • Argon
    • Chlorine
    • Silicon
    Show the answer

    Answer: Argon. Noble gases have the highest values in their period.

  2. Why does sulfur have a lower first ionisation energy than phosphorus?
    • Sulfur has fewer protons
    • Sulfur's electron is in an s orbital
    • Repulsion between paired 3p electrons makes one easier to remove
    • Phosphorus has a paired p electron
    Show the answer

    Answer: Repulsion between paired 3p electrons makes one easier to remove. In sulfur one 3p orbital holds a pair, and the paired electrons repel.

  3. Why do successive ionisation energies always increase?
    • Electrons get further from the nucleus
    • Nuclear charge falls each time
    • The atom gains protons
    • Each electron is removed from a more positive ion
    Show the answer

    Answer: Each electron is removed from a more positive ion. Greater positive charge holds remaining electrons more tightly.

Key terms

First ionisation energy
Energy to remove one electron from each atom in a mole of gaseous atoms
Successive ionisation energies
Energies to remove electrons one after another
Subshell
A set of orbitals of the same type in a shell
