Chemical Symbols

Symbols start with a capital letter and any second letter is lower case; some come from Latin names.

Chemistry · Foundations of Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Write element symbols with correct capitalisation
  • Recall the symbols of common elements, including those from Latin names
  • Read the information in a periodic table entry

Writing symbols

A symbol is one capital letter, or a capital followed by a lower-case letter: C (carbon), Ca (calcium), Cl (chlorine), Co (cobalt). Capitalisation matters: Co is cobalt, but CO is carbon monoxide, a compound of carbon and oxygen.

Symbols from other languages

  • Na sodium (natrium), K potassium (kalium), Fe iron (ferrum), Cu copper (cuprum), Ag silver (argentum), Au gold (aurum), Pb lead (plumbum), Sn tin (stannum), Hg mercury (hydrargyrum), W tungsten (wolfram).

A periodic table entry

Each box gives the atomic (proton) number, the symbol, the name and the relative atomic mass. New elements receive temporary names until IUPAC approves permanent names; the four most recent (nihonium, moscovium, tennessine and oganesson) were named in 2016.

Worked example

Decode the formula CuSO₄ and explain why CO and Co are different.

  1. CuSO₄ contains copper (Cu), sulfur (S) and four oxygen (O) atoms.
  2. CO is one carbon atom and one oxygen atom: a compound.
  3. Co is a single symbol, the element cobalt.

Reading the information in an entry

  • Take chlorine: the atomic number 17 is the number of protons (and of electrons in the atom), Cl is the symbol, and the relative atomic mass 35.5 is the average mass of its atoms, weighted by how common each isotope is, on a scale where carbon-12 is exactly 12.
  • Where the entry sits adds more information: the period (row) is the number of occupied electron shells, and the group (column) gives the outer-shell electrons: the group number for groups 1 and 2, and the group number minus 10 for groups 13 to 18.
  • Some periodic tables also show electronegativity or electron configuration in each entry, so check the key before reading a value.
Common mistakeWriting a two-letter symbol with two capitals, such as CL for chlorine. It must be Cl.

In 2 lines: One capital, then lower case: Co is cobalt; CO is carbon monoxide. Na, K, Fe, Cu, Ag, Au, Pb, Sn and Hg come from older names.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is the symbol for potassium?
    • P
    • Po
    • K
    • Pt
    Show the answer

    Answer: K. K comes from kalium; P is phosphorus, Po polonium and Pt platinum.

  2. How many different elements are in Ca(NO₃)₂?
    • Two
    • Five
    • Nine
    • Three
    Show the answer

    Answer: Three. Calcium, nitrogen and oxygen: each capital letter starts a new element symbol.

  3. A student writes "NACL" for sodium chloride. Why is this ambiguous?
    • Without correct case it could be read as N, A, C and L, or as other combinations; NaCl unambiguously means sodium and chlorine
    • Sodium chloride has no formula
    • Capitals are always used
    • NaCl is a mixture
    Show the answer

    Answer: Without correct case it could be read as N, A, C and L, or as other combinations; NaCl unambiguously means sodium and chlorine. Case shows where each symbol starts; NaCl means Na and Cl.

Key terms

Chemical symbol
The internationally agreed one- or two-letter abbreviation for an element
Atomic number
The number of protons in the nucleus of an atom of an element
