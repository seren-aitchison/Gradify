Catalysis in Industry

Industry relies on heterogeneous, homogeneous and enzyme catalysts, judged by activity, selectivity and lifetime; poisoning and sintering deactivate catalysts, and supports and feed purification extend their life.

Chemistry · Materials, Environmental and Industrial Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the importance of catalysts in industry
  • Compare homogeneous, heterogeneous and biocatalysts in industry
  • Explain catalyst deactivation and design

Types in use

  • Heterogeneous: solid catalysts, easy to separate, such as iron (Haber) and zeolites (cracking).
  • Homogeneous: soluble complexes, highly selective, such as rhodium and iridium in carbonylation.
  • Biocatalysts: enzymes, very selective, mild conditions.
  • Most chemical products involve a catalyst at some stage.

Performance and design

  • Activity, selectivity and lifetime measure performance.
  • Poisoning: impurities such as sulfur block active sites.
  • Sintering: particles merge at high temperature, losing surface area.
  • Catalysts are supported on high-area materials such as alumina.

Catalysts save energy and reduce waste by allowing milder conditions and more selective reactions. Enzymes now make medicines such as sitagliptin, replacing metal catalysts. Zeolite catalysts crack heavy oil fractions into petrol.

Worked example

Catalyst poisoning in the Haber process.

  1. Sulfur compounds in the feed gas adsorb strongly on iron.
  2. Active sites are blocked.
  3. The rate of ammonia formation falls.
  4. The feed is purified to remove sulfur first.
Common mistakeThinking catalysts last forever. They are gradually deactivated by poisoning and sintering.

In 3 lines: Three catalyst types. Activity, selectivity, lifetime. Poisoning and sintering.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which catalyst is used to crack heavy oil fractions?
    • Zeolites
    • Iron
    • Vanadium(V) oxide
    • Platinum gauze
    Show the answer

    Answer: Zeolites. Zeolites' acidic pores break large hydrocarbons.

  2. Why are enzymes increasingly used in medicine manufacture?
    • They work only at very high temperatures
    • They are highly selective and work under mild conditions
    • They produce many by-products
    • They are always cheaper than any metal
    Show the answer

    Answer: They are highly selective and work under mild conditions. Selectivity reduces waste and purification.

  3. Why does sintering reduce catalyst activity?
    • It adds more active sites
    • It poisons the catalyst with sulfur
    • Particles merge, lowering surface area and active sites
    • It makes particles smaller
    Show the answer

    Answer: Particles merge, lowering surface area and active sites. Fewer exposed atoms means lower activity.

Key terms

Selectivity
Fraction of reactant converted to the desired product
Catalyst poisoning
Deactivation by strongly adsorbed impurities
Sintering
Merging of catalyst particles at high temperature
