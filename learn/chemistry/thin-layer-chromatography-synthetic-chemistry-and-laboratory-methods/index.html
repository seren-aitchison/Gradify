Thin-layer Chromatography

In synthesis, TLC chooses column solvents by aiming for Rf about 0.2–0.4, identifies compounds by co-spotting with standards, checks column fractions and uses stains for compounds that are not UV active.

Chemistry · Synthetic Chemistry and Laboratory Methods · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Use TLC to choose a solvent system for a column
  • Use co-spotting to identify compounds
  • Interpret TLC to assess column fractions

Choosing solvents

  • Aim for the target compound to have Rf about 0.2–0.4 for a column.
  • Adjust the ratio of polar to non-polar solvents, such as ethyl ethanoate in hexane.
  • Good separation between spots is needed.
  • Try several mixtures quickly on small plates.

Practical uses

  • Co-spotting: spot sample and standard together to see if they match.
  • Check fractions from a column to decide which to combine.
  • Stains such as potassium manganate(VII) reveal non-UV-active compounds.
  • Record plates with drawings or photos.

Co-spotting is more reliable than comparing Rf values on separate plates, because conditions are identical. A single spot after a column suggests the fractions can be combined. TLC is cheap, fast and used constantly in synthetic labs.

Worked example

Choosing a column solvent.

  1. In 10% ethyl ethanoate/hexane, the product's Rf is 0.05.
  2. In 50%, its Rf is 0.80.
  3. In 25%, its Rf is 0.30 and impurities are well separated.
  4. 25% is chosen for the column.
Common mistakeChoosing a column solvent where the product's Rf is very high. It will elute too fast and separate poorly.

In 3 lines: Rf 0.2–0.4. Co-spot to confirm. Check fractions.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which stain reveals compounds that are not UV active?
    • Distilled water
    • Potassium manganate(VII) solution
    • Sodium chloride solution
    • Hexane
    Show the answer

    Answer: Potassium manganate(VII) solution. Manganate(VII) reacts with oxidisable compounds, giving visible spots.

  2. Why is co-spotting more reliable than comparing separate plates?
    • Separate plates always give identical Rf values
    • Co-spotting changes the solvent
    • Both compounds run under exactly the same conditions
    • It makes spots more colourful
    Show the answer

    Answer: Both compounds run under exactly the same conditions. Identical conditions remove plate-to-plate variation.

  3. Why is an Rf of about 0.3 ideal for a column solvent?
    • It means the compound will not move at all
    • It makes the compound run with the solvent front
    • Rf has no link to column behaviour
    • The compound moves at a useful rate while staying separated from others
    Show the answer

    Answer: The compound moves at a useful rate while staying separated from others. Moderate retention gives good resolution.

Key terms

Co-spotting
Spotting a sample and standard together on one lane
Eluent
The solvent used to move compounds
Stain
A reagent that makes spots visible
