Acid-base Titrations

Titrations use a pipette for an exact volume and a burette to add titrant to an indicator end point, repeating to concordant results; concentration is found from moles = c × V and the mole ratio, while careful rinsing and reading reduce errors.

Chemistry · Acids, Bases and Aqueous Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the procedure for an acid-base titration
  • Calculate concentrations from titration results
  • Identify sources of error in titrations

Procedure

  • A pipette measures an exact volume of one solution into a conical flask.
  • A burette adds the other solution until the end point.
  • An indicator shows the end point by a colour change.
  • Repeat until concordant titres (within 0.10 cm³) are obtained.

Calculations

  • Moles = concentration × volume (in dm³).
  • Use the mole ratio from the balanced equation.
  • Find the unknown concentration.
  • Use the mean of concordant titres.

A rough titration is done first to find the approximate end point. Rinsing the burette with the solution it will hold and the pipette with its solution prevents dilution. Reading the bottom of the meniscus at eye level avoids parallax error.

Worked example

Finding a concentration.

  1. 25.0 cm³ of NaOH is neutralised by a mean titre of 22.40 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ HCl.
  2. Moles HCl = 0.100 × 0.02240 = 2.24 × 10⁻³ mol.
  3. Ratio 1:1, so moles NaOH = 2.24 × 10⁻³.
  4. [NaOH] = 2.24 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0250 = 0.0896 mol dm⁻³.
Common mistakeForgetting to convert cm³ to dm³. Divide volumes in cm³ by 1,000.

In 3 lines: Pipette and burette. Moles and ratios. Reduce errors.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which apparatus delivers an exact fixed volume into the flask?
    • A burette
    • A beaker
    • A measuring cylinder
    • A pipette
    Show the answer

    Answer: A pipette. Volumetric pipettes deliver one precise volume.

  2. Why is the burette rinsed with the titrant before filling?
    • To avoid diluting the titrant with water
    • To clean off the indicator
    • To warm the burette
    • To remove air from the flask
    Show the answer

    Answer: To avoid diluting the titrant with water. Leftover water would lower the titrant concentration.

  3. Why are only concordant titres averaged?
    • All titres must be identical
    • Outliers would make the mean less accurate
    • Concordant titres are always the first ones
    • Averaging is not needed in titrations
    Show the answer

    Answer: Outliers would make the mean less accurate. Including outlying titres would make the mean titre less accurate.

Key terms

Titre
The volume of solution added from a burette
End point
The point where the indicator changes colour
Concordant
Results within 0.10 cm³ of each other
