Chemical Manufacturing

The chemical industry includes bulk, fine and speciality sectors built on a few feedstocks; scale-up from lab to pilot to plant must manage heat transfer and mixing, and hazard analysis and quality control keep manufacturing safe and reliable.

Chemistry · Materials, Environmental and Industrial Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the sectors of the chemical industry
  • Explain the stages of scaling up a process
  • Describe quality control and safety in manufacturing

Sectors

  • Bulk (commodity) chemicals: large volumes, low price, such as sulfuric acid and ethene.
  • Fine chemicals: smaller volumes, high purity, such as drug intermediates.
  • Speciality chemicals: valued for function, such as adhesives and coatings.
  • Feedstocks: crude oil, natural gas, salt, air, water and biomass.

Scale-up

  • Laboratory → pilot plant → full-scale plant.
  • Heat transfer and mixing change with scale.
  • Exothermic reactions can run away if heat is not removed.
  • Hazard analysis (such as HAZOP) identifies risks.

Scale-up is challenging because volume rises faster than surface area, making cooling harder. The 1984 Bhopal disaster showed the consequences of poor safety, leading to stronger regulations. Quality control ensures products meet specifications.

Worked example

Why cooling is harder at scale.

  1. A 1 L flask has a large surface area relative to volume.
  2. A 10,000 L reactor has much less surface per litre.
  3. Heat from an exothermic reaction is harder to remove.
  4. Careful design prevents a thermal runaway.
Common mistakeAssuming a lab reaction behaves the same in a large reactor. Heat and mixing change with scale.

In 3 lines: Bulk, fine, speciality. Lab, pilot, plant. Safety and quality.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which sector produces drug intermediates of high purity?
    • Bulk chemicals
    • Fine chemicals
    • Fertilisers
    • Crude oil refining
    Show the answer

    Answer: Fine chemicals. Fine chemicals are made in smaller, purer batches.

  2. Why is cooling harder in large reactors?
    • Large reactors produce no heat
    • Surface area increases faster than volume
    • Volume grows faster than surface area, so less area per litre removes heat
    • Cooling water does not work at scale
    Show the answer

    Answer: Volume grows faster than surface area, so less area per litre removes heat. The surface-to-volume ratio falls with size.

  3. Why are HAZOP studies carried out?
    • To increase reaction rates
    • To reduce product purity
    • To replace quality control
    • To identify possible deviations and hazards before they cause accidents
    Show the answer

    Answer: To identify possible deviations and hazards before they cause accidents. Systematic review prevents dangerous surprises.

Key terms

Bulk chemical
A chemical made in very large quantities at low price
Pilot plant
A small-scale plant used to test a process
HAZOP
A structured hazard and operability study
