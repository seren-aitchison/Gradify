Chemical Potential

Chemical potential is the molar Gibbs energy of a substance in a mixture; substances move from high to low chemical potential until it is equal everywhere, and it increases with concentration, explaining diffusion, osmosis and equilibrium.

Chemistry · Chemical Energetics and Thermodynamics · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define chemical potential
  • Explain how chemical potential drives movement and reaction
  • Relate chemical potential to concentration

Definition

  • Chemical potential (μ) is the molar Gibbs free energy of a substance in a mixture.
  • Substances move from high to low chemical potential.
  • At equilibrium, each substance has the same chemical potential everywhere.
  • For a pure substance, μ equals its molar Gibbs energy.

Concentration and uses

  • μ = μ⦵ + RT ln(a), where a is activity (approximately concentration or partial pressure).
  • Higher concentration means higher chemical potential.
  • Explains diffusion, osmosis and phase equilibria.
  • In reactions, equilibrium is reached when the chemical potentials of reactants and products balance.

Diffusion from high to low concentration is a move from high to low chemical potential. Osmosis occurs because water has higher chemical potential in dilute solutions. Chemical potential unifies these processes under one principle.

Worked example

Osmosis through chemical potential.

  1. Pure water is on one side of a membrane; salt solution on the other.
  2. Dissolved salt lowers water's chemical potential in the solution.
  3. Water moves from higher μ (pure water) to lower μ (solution).
  4. Movement stops when chemical potentials are equal.
Common mistakeThinking chemical potential is the same as electrical potential. It is a measure of Gibbs energy per mole.

In 3 lines: Molar Gibbs energy. High to low μ. Explains diffusion and osmosis.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is true of chemical potential at equilibrium?
    • It is equal for each substance everywhere
    • It is at a maximum
    • It is zero for every substance
    • It changes continuously
    Show the answer

    Answer: It is equal for each substance everywhere. No net movement occurs once chemical potential is equal everywhere.

  2. How does increasing concentration affect chemical potential?
    • It decreases chemical potential
    • It increases chemical potential
    • It has no effect
    • It makes it zero
    Show the answer

    Answer: It increases chemical potential. Chemical potential rises with RT ln of concentration, so it increases.

  3. Why does water move into a salt solution by osmosis?
    • Salt raises water's chemical potential
    • Water moves to higher chemical potential
    • Dissolved salt lowers water's chemical potential there
    • Salt molecules pull water physically
    Show the answer

    Answer: Dissolved salt lowers water's chemical potential there. Water moves to where its chemical potential is lower, inside the solution.
