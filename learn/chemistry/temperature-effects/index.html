Temperature Effects

Higher temperature speeds reactions mainly by increasing the fraction of collisions above Ea, roughly doubling many rates per 10 °C, with consequences for food storage, biology and industrial safety, including thermal runaway.

Chemistry · Chemical Kinetics · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain the effect of temperature on rate using kinetic theory
  • Apply the rule of thumb that rates roughly double per 10 °C
  • Describe practical consequences in food, biology and industry

Explaining the effect

  • Higher temperature increases particle speed and collision frequency.
  • More importantly, a larger fraction of collisions exceed Ea.
  • Many reactions near room temperature roughly double in rate per 10 °C rise.
  • The exact factor depends on Ea.

Consequences

  • Refrigeration slows food spoilage.
  • Body temperature regulates enzyme reaction rates.
  • Industrial processes balance rate against cost and equilibrium yield.
  • Cooling can control dangerously fast reactions.

Fever slightly increases metabolic rate, while hypothermia slows it. Cooking speeds up chemical changes in food. Industrial reactors often need cooling to remove heat from exothermic reactions and prevent thermal runaway, where a hotter reaction goes faster and releases even more heat.

Worked example

Thermal runaway.

  1. An exothermic reaction warms its container.
  2. The higher temperature speeds up the reaction.
  3. Faster reaction releases heat more quickly.
  4. Without cooling, temperature and rate rise uncontrollably.
Common mistakeThinking temperature affects rate only by increasing collision frequency. The main effect is more collisions exceeding Ea.

In 3 lines: Fraction above Ea rises. Roughly double per 10 °C. Food, biology, safety.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. By roughly how much do many reaction rates increase per 10 °C rise?
    • About ten times
    • About double
    • About half
    • They do not change
    Show the answer

    Answer: About double. A rule of thumb for many reactions near room temperature.

  2. What is the main reason temperature increases rate?
    • Particles become larger
    • Activation energy decreases
    • More collisions have energy above Ea
    • Concentration increases
    Show the answer

    Answer: More collisions have energy above Ea. The energetic fraction grows much faster than collision frequency.

  3. Why must exothermic industrial reactors be cooled?
    • Cooling increases the yield of all reactions
    • Exothermic reactions stop when hot
    • Reactors melt at room temperature
    • Heat would speed the reaction, risking thermal runaway
    Show the answer

    Answer: Heat would speed the reaction, risking thermal runaway. Removing heat prevents an uncontrolled feedback loop.

Key terms

Thermal runaway
Uncontrolled acceleration of an exothermic reaction
Refrigeration
Cooling to slow chemical and biological processes
Collision frequency
Number of collisions per unit time
