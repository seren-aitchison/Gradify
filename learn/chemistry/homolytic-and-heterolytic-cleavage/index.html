Homolytic and Heterolytic Cleavage

Homolytic cleavage gives two radicals and is favoured by light, heat and non-polar bonds, while heterolytic cleavage gives ions and is favoured by polar bonds and solvents, with the more electronegative atom taking both electrons.

Chemistry · Organic Reaction Mechanisms · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Distinguish homolytic and heterolytic cleavage
  • Explain conditions favouring each type
  • Relate bond dissociation energy to cleavage

Two ways to break

  • Homolytic: each atom takes one electron, forming two radicals.
  • Heterolytic: one atom takes both electrons, forming a cation and an anion.
  • Homolysis is shown with fishhook arrows.
  • Heterolysis is shown with a full curly arrow.

Conditions

  • Homolysis is favoured by UV light, heat and non-polar bonds, often in the gas phase.
  • Heterolysis is favoured by polar bonds and polar solvents that stabilise ions.
  • Bond dissociation energy measures the energy for homolysis.
  • Weak bonds such as O–O in peroxides break homolytically easily.

Peroxides are used as radical initiators because their weak O–O bond breaks easily on heating. In water, HCl ionises heterolytically, while in the gas phase very high energy is needed to form ions. Ion formation is costly without solvent stabilisation.

Worked example

Two fates of a C–Br bond.

  1. In UV light: C–Br → C• + Br•.
  2. In a polar solvent with a stable carbocation: C–Br → C⁺ + Br⁻.
  3. Bromine takes both electrons because it is more electronegative.
  4. Conditions decide which pathway occurs.
Common mistakeThinking heterolysis puts the electrons on either atom at random. The more electronegative atom usually takes them.

In 3 lines: Radicals or ions. Light or solvent. Electronegativity decides.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What does heterolytic cleavage produce?
    • A cation and an anion
    • Two neutral radicals
    • Two identical atoms
    • A single molecule
    Show the answer

    Answer: A cation and an anion. One atom takes both bonding electrons, so a cation and an anion form.

  2. Why do polar solvents favour heterolytic cleavage?
    • They supply ultraviolet light
    • They stabilise the ions formed by solvation
    • They remove all electrons from bonds
    • They make bonds non-polar
    Show the answer

    Answer: They stabilise the ions formed by solvation. Solvation lowers the energy of charged products.

  3. Why is ion formation in the gas phase so costly?
    • Gas molecules have no electrons
    • Gas-phase bonds are always non-polar
    • No solvent is present to stabilise the separated charges
    • Ions are always more stable in the gas phase
    Show the answer

    Answer: No solvent is present to stabilise the separated charges. Separating charges without solvation needs much more energy.

Key terms

Homolytic cleavage
Bond breaking giving one electron to each atom
Heterolytic cleavage
Bond breaking giving both electrons to one atom
Bond dissociation energy
Energy needed to break a bond homolytically
