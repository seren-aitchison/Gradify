Colour in Transition Metal Complexes

Colour in complexes arises from d-d transitions, where light of energy equal to Δ is absorbed and the complementary colour is seen; d⁰ and d¹⁰ ions are colourless, while intense colours often come from charge-transfer transitions.

Chemistry · Inorganic Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain colour in terms of d-d electron transitions
  • Explain why some complexes are colourless
  • Describe charge-transfer transitions

d-d transitions

  • Light of energy matching Δ promotes an electron from a lower to a higher d orbital.
  • The complex absorbs that colour; we see the complementary colour.
  • ΔE = hf links splitting to the frequency absorbed.
  • Changing ligand, oxidation state or geometry changes Δ and the colour.

Colourless and intense colours

  • d⁰ ions such as Sc³⁺ and d¹⁰ ions such as Zn²⁺ have no d-d transitions and are colourless.
  • d-d transitions are weak because they are partly forbidden.
  • Charge-transfer transitions move electrons between ligand and metal.
  • Charge transfer gives intense colours, as in purple manganate(VII).

[Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ absorbs orange-red light and looks blue. [Ti(H₂O)₆]³⁺ (d¹) absorbs green-yellow light and looks purple. Potassium manganate(VII) is intensely purple even though manganese is d⁰, because oxygen electrons transfer to the metal.

Worked example

Why the colour changes with ammonia.

  1. [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ is pale blue.
  2. Ammonia is a stronger-field ligand than water.
  3. Δ increases, so higher-energy light is absorbed.
  4. [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ appears a deeper, more violet blue.
Common mistakeThinking a complex's visible colour is the colour it absorbs. We see the complementary colour.

In 3 lines: Absorb Δ, see complement. d⁰ and d¹⁰ colourless. Charge transfer is intense.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which ion forms colourless aqueous complexes?
    • Cu²⁺, which has nine d electrons
    • Fe³⁺, which has five d electrons
    • Ti³⁺, which has one d electron
    • Sc³⁺, which has no d electrons
    Show the answer

    Answer: Sc³⁺, which has no d electrons. Without d electrons, no d-d transitions occur.

  2. Why is manganate(VII) intensely purple although Mn(VII) is d⁰?
    • Charge transfer from oxygen to manganese absorbs light strongly
    • It has many d-d transitions
    • It contains copper impurities
    • It reflects only purple light by chance
    Show the answer

    Answer: Charge transfer from oxygen to manganese absorbs light strongly. Ligand-to-metal charge transfer is allowed and intense.

  3. Why are d-d transitions usually weak?
    • They involve no change in energy
    • They are partly forbidden by selection rules
    • They only happen in gases
    • They need X-rays to occur
    Show the answer

    Answer: They are partly forbidden by selection rules. Laporte and spin selection rules lower their intensity.

Key terms

d-d transition
Promotion of an electron between split d orbitals
Complementary colour
The colour seen when another colour is absorbed
Charge transfer
Electron transfer between ligand and metal on absorbing light
