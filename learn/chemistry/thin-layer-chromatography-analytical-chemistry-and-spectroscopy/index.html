Thin-layer Chromatography

TLC uses a polar silica or alumina plate and a solvent; polar compounds have low Rf, spots are viewed under UV or by staining and TLC is used to check purity, monitor reactions and choose solvents.

Chemistry · Analytical Chemistry and Spectroscopy · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe how to carry out TLC
  • Explain how polarity affects Rf values
  • Describe uses of TLC in the laboratory

Method

  • A plate is coated with silica or alumina (polar stationary phase).
  • Spots are applied on a pencil baseline above the solvent level.
  • The plate develops in a covered tank until solvent nears the top.
  • Spots are seen by UV light, iodine or chemical stains.

Interpreting results

  • Polar compounds stick to silica and have low Rf.
  • Less polar compounds move further: high Rf.
  • A more polar solvent increases all Rf values.
  • One spot suggests purity; several spots show a mixture.

TLC is used to monitor reactions: the starting material spot fades as a new product spot appears. It is cheap, fast and needs tiny samples, and it helps choose solvents for column chromatography.

Worked example

Monitoring a reaction.

  1. Spots of starting material, reaction mixture and product are run side by side.
  2. At first, the mixture shows only the starting material spot.
  3. Over time, a new spot with a different Rf appears.
  4. When the starting material spot disappears, the reaction is complete.
Common mistakeDrawing the baseline in pen. Ink dissolves and runs with the solvent.

In 3 lines: Silica plate. Polar means low Rf. Monitor reactions.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which compound has the lowest Rf on silica?
    • The least polar compound
    • The most polar compound
    • A non-polar hydrocarbon
    • The solvent itself
    Show the answer

    Answer: The most polar compound. Polar compounds bind strongly to polar silica.

  2. Why must the baseline be above the solvent level in the tank?
    • It makes the solvent move faster
    • It increases all Rf values
    • Otherwise the spots dissolve into the solvent pool
    • The solvent cannot rise past the baseline
    Show the answer

    Answer: Otherwise the spots dissolve into the solvent pool. Samples must be carried up the plate, not washed away.

  3. Why does a more polar solvent increase Rf values?
    • It makes the silica plate less polar overall
    • It decreases the solubility of each compound
    • It stops the solvent rising up the plate
    • It competes better for silica sites and carries compounds further
    Show the answer

    Answer: It competes better for silica sites and carries compounds further. Stronger eluent displaces compounds from the stationary phase.

Key terms

TLC
Thin-layer chromatography
Baseline
The pencil line where samples are spotted
Solvent front
The furthest point reached by the solvent
