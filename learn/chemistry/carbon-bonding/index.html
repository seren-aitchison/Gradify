Carbon Bonding

Carbon forms four strong covalent bonds and catenates into chains and rings with single, double and triple bonds; sp³ carbons are tetrahedral, sp² trigonal planar and sp linear, giving organic molecules their shapes.

Chemistry · Organic Chemistry Foundations · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain why carbon forms four covalent bonds
  • Explain catenation in carbon compounds
  • Relate hybridisation to shape in organic molecules

Tetravalency and catenation

  • Carbon has four outer electrons and forms four covalent bonds.
  • C–C bonds are strong, allowing long chains and rings (catenation).
  • Carbon forms single, double and triple bonds.
  • C–H bonds are strong and relatively unreactive.

Hybridisation and shape

  • sp³: four single bonds, tetrahedral, 109.5°, as in methane.
  • sp²: one double bond, trigonal planar, 120°, as in ethene.
  • sp: a triple bond or two double bonds, linear, 180°, as in ethyne.
  • Shape affects reactivity and physical properties.

Silicon is in the same group but forms weaker Si–Si bonds and reacts readily with oxygen, so it cannot match carbon's chain chemistry. The strength and variety of carbon bonds allow everything from methane to proteins.

Worked example

Shapes in propene, CH₂=CH–CH₃.

  1. The two CH₂=CH carbons are sp², with 120° angles.
  2. The CH₃ carbon is sp³, with 109.5° angles.
  3. The C=C double bond prevents rotation.
  4. One molecule contains two different geometries.
Common mistakeThinking all carbon atoms in a molecule have the same shape. Each carbon's geometry depends on its own bonds.

In 3 lines: Four bonds. Chains and rings. sp³, sp², sp.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is the bond angle around an sp² carbon?
    • 120°
    • 109.5°
    • 180°
    • 90°
    Show the answer

    Answer: 120°. Three electron regions in a plane spread out to 120° apart.

  2. Why does carbon form many more chain compounds than silicon?
    • Silicon atoms are far smaller than carbon
    • C–C bonds are strong and carbon compounds resist oxidation
    • Carbon forms only ionic bonds with itself
    • Silicon has more outer electrons than carbon
    Show the answer

    Answer: C–C bonds are strong and carbon compounds resist oxidation. Weak Si–Si bonds and reactive Si–H bonds limit silicon chains.

  3. In propene, why do the carbons have different shapes?
    • All three carbons are sp hybridised
    • Propene contains a triple bond
    • Two carbons share a double bond and are sp², while the third is sp³
    • The molecule is completely linear
    Show the answer

    Answer: Two carbons share a double bond and are sp², while the third is sp³. Each carbon's geometry depends on its own pattern of bonds.

Key terms

Catenation
Forming chains of bonded atoms of the same element
Tetravalent
Forming four bonds
sp² carbon
A trigonal planar carbon with one double bond
