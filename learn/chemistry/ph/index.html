PH

pH = −log[H⁺], so each unit is a tenfold change; pH is measured with universal indicator or calibrated pH meters, neutral is 7 at 25 °C and values can extend beyond 0 to 14.

Chemistry · Acids, Bases and Aqueous Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define pH
  • Convert between pH and [H⁺]
  • Describe methods of measuring pH

Definition

  • pH = −log₁₀[H⁺].
  • [H⁺] = 10⁻ᵖᴴ.
  • Each unit change in pH is a tenfold change in [H⁺].
  • At 25 °C, neutral water has pH 7.

Measuring pH

  • Universal indicator gives an approximate colour-based pH.
  • pH meters use a glass electrode for accurate readings.
  • pH meters must be calibrated with buffer solutions.
  • pH can be below 0 or above 14 for very concentrated solutions.

The pH scale was introduced by Søren Sørensen in 1909 to monitor brewing. Blood pH is tightly controlled at 7.35–7.45. Because pH is logarithmic, a solution at pH 3 has 100 times more H⁺ than one at pH 5.

Worked example

Converting pH.

  1. A solution has pH 4.70.
  2. [H⁺] = 10⁻⁴·⁷⁰.
  3. [H⁺] = 2.0 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³.
  4. A solution with pH 3.70 would have ten times more H⁺.
Common mistakeThinking pH 4 is twice as acidic as pH 8. It has 10,000 times more H⁺.

In 3 lines: pH = −log[H⁺]. Tenfold per unit. Meters and indicators.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. How many times more H⁺ does pH 2 have than pH 4?
    • 2
    • 10
    • 1,000
    • 100
    Show the answer

    Answer: 100. Two pH units means a factor of 10² = 100.

  2. What is [H⁺] in a solution of pH 3?
    • 1 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³
    • 3 mol dm⁻³
    • 1 × 10³ mol dm⁻³
    • 0.3 mol dm⁻³
    Show the answer

    Answer: 1 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³. [H⁺] = 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ from the definition of pH.

  3. Why is pH measured on a logarithmic scale?
    • It makes all values equal
    • [H⁺] varies over many orders of magnitude
    • Logarithms remove units of concentration
    • It doubles at each step
    Show the answer

    Answer: [H⁺] varies over many orders of magnitude. A log scale compresses a huge range into small numbers.

Key terms

pH
−log₁₀ of the hydrogen ion concentration
pH meter
An instrument measuring pH with an electrode
Logarithmic scale
A scale where each step is a tenfold change
