Enthalpy of Combustion

Standard enthalpy of combustion is the enthalpy change when one mole burns completely in oxygen, always negative; it is measured by spirit burner or bomb calorimeter, increases regularly along homologous series and compares fuels per mole or per gram.

Chemistry · Chemical Energetics and Thermodynamics · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define standard enthalpy of combustion
  • Describe how combustion enthalpies are measured
  • Compare fuels using combustion data

Definition

  • ΔcH⦵: enthalpy change when one mole of a substance burns completely in oxygen under standard conditions.
  • Always negative (exothermic).
  • Products are CO₂(g) and H₂O(l) for hydrocarbons.
  • Example: CH₄(g) + 2O₂(g) → CO₂(g) + 2H₂O(l), ΔcH⦵ = −891 kJ mol⁻¹.

Measurement and comparison

  • Measured with a spirit burner heating water, or more accurately with a bomb calorimeter.
  • ΔcH⦵ becomes more negative by about 650 kJ mol⁻¹ per extra CH₂ in alkanes.
  • Energy density (kJ g⁻¹) compares fuels by mass.
  • Hydrogen has a very high energy per gram but low energy per volume.

Simple spirit burner experiments give values much less negative than data books because of heat loss, incomplete combustion and evaporation. Bomb calorimeters burn samples in pure oxygen inside a sealed vessel surrounded by water, reducing losses.

Worked example

Comparing fuels by mass.

  1. Methane: ΔcH⦵ = −891 kJ mol⁻¹, M = 16 g mol⁻¹.
  2. Energy per gram = 891 ÷ 16 = 55.7 kJ g⁻¹.
  3. Octane: −5,470 kJ mol⁻¹, M = 114 g mol⁻¹, giving 48.0 kJ g⁻¹.
  4. Methane releases more energy per gram.
Common mistakeThinking simple calorimetry gives accurate combustion values. Heat losses make results much less negative than true values.

In 3 lines: One mole burned completely. Spirit burner or bomb calorimeter. Compare fuels.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which apparatus gives the most accurate combustion enthalpies?
    • A bomb calorimeter
    • A spirit burner and can
    • A polystyrene cup
    • A Bunsen burner and beaker
    Show the answer

    Answer: A bomb calorimeter. A sealed bomb in water minimises heat loss.

  2. How does ΔcH⦵ change along the alkane series?
    • It becomes less negative with each carbon
    • It becomes more negative by about 650 kJ mol⁻¹ per CH₂
    • It stays exactly the same
    • It becomes positive for large alkanes
    Show the answer

    Answer: It becomes more negative by about 650 kJ mol⁻¹ per CH₂. Each extra CH₂ adds similar bonds to burn.

  3. Why is hydrogen hard to store as a fuel despite high energy per gram?
    • It releases no energy when burned
    • It is very dense
    • It has very low energy per volume as a gas
    • It forms carbon dioxide
    Show the answer

    Answer: It has very low energy per volume as a gas. Large tanks or high pressures are needed to store enough.

Key terms

Enthalpy of combustion
Enthalpy change when one mole burns completely
Bomb calorimeter
A sealed vessel measuring combustion energy
Energy density
Energy released per unit mass or volume
