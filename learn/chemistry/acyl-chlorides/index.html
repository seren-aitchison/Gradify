Acyl Chlorides

Acyl chlorides, RCOCl, are very reactive because the carbonyl carbon is strongly δ+ and Cl⁻ leaves easily; they undergo addition-elimination with water, alcohols, ammonia and amines to give acids, esters and amides, releasing HCl.

Chemistry · Functional Group Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the structure of acyl chlorides
  • Explain why acyl chlorides are so reactive
  • Describe nucleophilic addition-elimination reactions of acyl chlorides

Reactivity

  • Structure RCOCl, named with -oyl chloride: ethanoyl chloride.
  • The carbonyl carbon is strongly δ+ due to both O and Cl.
  • Cl⁻ is a good leaving group.
  • React by nucleophilic addition-elimination.

Reactions

  • With water: carboxylic acid + HCl (vigorous, steamy fumes).
  • With alcohols: esters + HCl.
  • With ammonia: primary amides.
  • With amines: N-substituted amides.

Acyl chlorides react faster and more completely than carboxylic acids, so they are used to make esters and amides in high yield. They must be kept dry because they react with moisture. The reaction produces HCl gas, which must be handled in a fume cupboard.

Worked example

Making an amide.

  1. Ethanoyl chloride reacts with methylamine.
  2. The amine's lone pair adds to the carbonyl carbon.
  3. The C=O reforms and Cl⁻ leaves.
  4. N-methylethanamide and HCl form.
Common mistakeThinking acyl chlorides need a catalyst to make esters. They react directly and irreversibly with alcohols.

In 3 lines: Very reactive. Addition-elimination. Acids, esters, amides.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which product forms from ethanoyl chloride and ethanol?
    • Ethanoic acid and ethane
    • Ethyl ethanoate and HCl
    • Ethanamide and water
    • Ethanal and chlorine
    Show the answer

    Answer: Ethyl ethanoate and HCl. The alcohol replaces chloride to form an ester.

  2. Why are acyl chlorides more reactive than carboxylic acids?
    • OH is a better leaving group than Cl
    • Acyl chlorides have no carbonyl group
    • Cl makes the carbon more δ+ and leaves more easily than OH
    • Carboxylic acids are more electrophilic
    Show the answer

    Answer: Cl makes the carbon more δ+ and leaves more easily than OH. Both electrophilicity and leaving group ability increase.

  3. Why must acyl chlorides be stored dry?
    • Water makes them explode
    • They dissolve only in water
    • Moisture turns them into alkenes
    • They hydrolyse rapidly in moist air, releasing HCl
    Show the answer

    Answer: They hydrolyse rapidly in moist air, releasing HCl. Reaction with water destroys the reagent and forms corrosive fumes.

Key terms

Acyl chloride
A compound RCOCl
Addition-elimination
Nucleophile adds, then a leaving group is eliminated
Acylation
Introducing an acyl group, RCO–
