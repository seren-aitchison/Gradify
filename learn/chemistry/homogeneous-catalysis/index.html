Homogeneous Catalysis

Homogeneous catalysts share the reactants' phase and work by forming intermediates, often changing oxidation state, as Fe²⁺ does with peroxodisulfate and iodide and chlorine radicals do in ozone depletion.

Chemistry · Chemical Kinetics · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define homogeneous catalysis
  • Explain how homogeneous catalysts form intermediates
  • Describe examples including Fe²⁺ with peroxodisulfate and ozone depletion

Homogeneous catalysts

  • The catalyst is in the same phase as the reactants, usually in solution or gas.
  • It forms an intermediate with a reactant.
  • The intermediate reacts to give products and regenerate the catalyst.
  • Often involves changes in oxidation state.

Examples

  • Fe²⁺ catalyses S₂O₈²⁻ + 2I⁻ → 2SO₄²⁻ + I₂.
  • Fe²⁺ reduces S₂O₈²⁻ to SO₄²⁻, becoming Fe³⁺; Fe³⁺ oxidises I⁻ to I₂, returning to Fe²⁺.
  • Chlorine radicals catalyse ozone breakdown in the stratosphere.
  • Acid catalysis in esterification.

The direct reaction between S₂O₈²⁻ and I⁻ is slow because both ions are negative and repel. Going through Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺ avoids this repulsion. One chlorine radical can destroy thousands of ozone molecules because it is regenerated each cycle.

Worked example

Ozone depletion.

  1. Cl· + O₃ → ClO· + O₂.
  2. ClO· + O → Cl· + 2O₂.
  3. Overall: O₃ + O → 2O₂.
  4. The chlorine radical is regenerated and continues destroying ozone.
Common mistakeThinking homogeneous catalysts are unchanged at every step. They change temporarily but are regenerated.

In 3 lines: Same phase. Intermediates and regeneration. Fe²⁺ and Cl·.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which ion catalyses the reaction between peroxodisulfate and iodide?
    • Na⁺
    • Cl⁻
    • Fe²⁺
    • SO₄²⁻
    Show the answer

    Answer: Fe²⁺. Iron switches between Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺ during the cycle.

  2. Why can one chlorine radical destroy thousands of ozone molecules?
    • It is a very large molecule
    • It reacts only once
    • It absorbs all UV light
    • It is regenerated at the end of each cycle
    Show the answer

    Answer: It is regenerated at the end of each cycle. Catalytic cycles reuse the radical many times.

  3. Why does using Fe²⁺ speed up the S₂O₈²⁻ and I⁻ reaction?
    • Each step involves oppositely charged ions that attract
    • Fe²⁺ increases the temperature
    • Fe²⁺ makes iodide positive
    • It removes all activation energy
    Show the answer

    Answer: Each step involves oppositely charged ions that attract. Avoiding repulsion between like charges lowers Ea.

Key terms

Homogeneous catalyst
A catalyst in the same phase as the reactants
Intermediate
A species formed and then used up during a reaction
Radical
A species with an unpaired electron
