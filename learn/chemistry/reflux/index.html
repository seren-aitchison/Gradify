Reflux

Reflux heats a reaction mixture in a flask with a vertical condenser so vapour returns, preventing solvent loss, keeping volatile substances in the flask and holding the temperature near the solvent's boiling point.

Chemistry · Synthetic Chemistry and Laboratory Methods · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the apparatus for heating under reflux
  • Explain why reflux is used
  • Explain how the reflux temperature is set

Apparatus

  • A round-bottomed flask with a vertical condenser on top.
  • Cooling water enters at the bottom of the condenser and leaves at the top.
  • Anti-bumping granules or stirring ensure smooth boiling.
  • The top of the condenser is left open.

Purpose

  • Vapour condenses and drips back, so solvent is not lost.
  • Reactions can be heated for hours at constant temperature.
  • The temperature is fixed near the boiling point of the solvent.
  • Volatile reactants and products stay in the flask.

Oxidising a primary alcohol to a carboxylic acid is done under reflux so the intermediate aldehyde cannot escape and is oxidised further. Choosing a solvent with a suitable boiling point sets the reaction temperature.

Worked example

Reflux versus distillation.

  1. Primary alcohol + acidified dichromate under reflux.
  2. The aldehyde formed condenses and returns.
  3. It is oxidised further.
  4. A carboxylic acid is obtained.
Common mistakeStoppering the top of the reflux condenser. The system must stay open to the air.

In 3 lines: Vertical condenser. Nothing escapes. Solvent sets temperature.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is the main purpose of heating under reflux?
    • To heat for a long time without losing volatile substances
    • To separate a mixture by boiling point
    • To dry a solid product quickly
    • To cool a reaction rapidly
    Show the answer

    Answer: To heat for a long time without losing volatile substances. Condensed vapour drips back into the flask, so nothing volatile is lost.

  2. Why do anti-bumping granules help?
    • They make the solution boil at a higher temperature
    • They give bubbles places to form, so boiling is smooth
    • They react with the solvent
    • They stop the condenser working
    Show the answer

    Answer: They give bubbles places to form, so boiling is smooth. Smooth boiling prevents sudden violent bubbles.

  3. Why does reflux give an acid rather than an aldehyde from a primary alcohol?
    • Reflux removes the aldehyde as it forms
    • Dichromate cannot oxidise aldehydes
    • The aldehyde returns to the flask and is oxidised further
    • Reflux reduces the acid back to aldehyde
    Show the answer

    Answer: The aldehyde returns to the flask and is oxidised further. Keeping the aldehyde in contact with oxidant completes oxidation.

Key terms

Reflux
Continuous boiling with vapour condensed back into the flask
Condenser
Glassware cooled by water to condense vapour
Anti-bumping granules
Granules that promote smooth boiling
