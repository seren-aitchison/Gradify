Strong Acids and Bases

Strong acids and bases ionise completely, so [H⁺] equals concentration for monoprotic strong acids; strength means degree of ionisation, unlike concentration, and pH of strong bases uses Kw.

Chemistry · Acids, Bases and Aqueous Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define strong acids and bases
  • Distinguish strength and concentration
  • Calculate pH of strong acid and strong base solutions

Strong acids and bases

  • Strong acids ionise completely: HCl → H⁺ + Cl⁻.
  • Examples: HCl, HNO₃, H₂SO₄ (first proton).
  • Strong bases dissociate completely, such as NaOH and KOH.
  • [H⁺] equals the acid concentration for monoprotic strong acids.

Strength versus concentration

  • Strength describes the degree of ionisation.
  • Concentration describes the amount dissolved per volume.
  • A dilute strong acid can have a higher pH than a concentrated weak acid.
  • pH = −log[H⁺]; for strong bases use [OH⁻] and Kw.

Strong acids conduct electricity well because they are fully ionised. Diluting a strong acid tenfold raises its pH by 1. For strong bases, [H⁺] = Kw ÷ [OH⁻], where Kw = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ mol² dm⁻⁶ at 25 °C.

Worked example

pH of a strong base.

  1. 0.010 mol dm⁻³ NaOH is fully dissociated: [OH⁻] = 0.010.
  2. [H⁺] = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ ÷ 0.010 = 1.0 × 10⁻¹² mol dm⁻³.
  3. pH = −log(1.0 × 10⁻¹²).
  4. pH = 12.
Common mistakeConfusing strong with concentrated. Strength is degree of ionisation; concentration is amount per volume.

In 3 lines: Complete ionisation. Strength ≠ concentration. pH from [H⁺] and Kw.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is the pH of 0.10 mol dm⁻³ HCl?
    • 0.1
    • 1
    • 7
    • 13
    Show the answer

    Answer: 1. HCl is fully ionised, so [H⁺] = 0.10 and pH = −log 0.10 = 1.

  2. How does diluting a strong acid tenfold change its pH?
    • It decreases by 1
    • It increases by 10
    • It increases by 1
    • It does not change
    Show the answer

    Answer: It increases by 1. [H⁺] falls tenfold, so pH rises by one unit.

  3. Why can a dilute strong acid have a higher pH than a concentrated weak acid?
    • Strong acids always have higher pH
    • Weak acids ionise completely
    • pH depends only on strength
    • pH depends on [H⁺], which reflects both strength and concentration
    Show the answer

    Answer: pH depends on [H⁺], which reflects both strength and concentration. A lot of weak acid may release more H⁺ than a little strong acid.

Key terms

Strong acid
An acid that ionises completely in water
Concentration
Amount of substance per unit volume
Monoprotic
Releasing one H⁺ per molecule
