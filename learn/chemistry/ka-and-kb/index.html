Ka and Kb

For conjugate pairs Ka × Kb = Kw and pKa + pKb = 14 at 25 °C, so stronger acids have weaker conjugate bases; salts of weak acids are basic and salts of weak bases acidic due to hydrolysis.

Chemistry · Acids, Bases and Aqueous Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Derive Ka × Kb = Kw for a conjugate pair
  • Use pKa + pKb = 14 at 25 °C
  • Predict the pH of salt solutions

The relationship

  • For a conjugate acid-base pair, Ka × Kb = Kw.
  • pKa + pKb = 14 at 25 °C.
  • A stronger acid has a weaker conjugate base.
  • Example: NH₄⁺ has Ka = Kw ÷ Kb(NH₃) = 5.6 × 10⁻¹⁰.

Salt solutions

  • Salts of strong acid + strong base are neutral (NaCl).
  • Salts of weak acid + strong base are basic (CH₃COONa).
  • Salts of strong acid + weak base are acidic (NH₄Cl).
  • Ions react with water (hydrolysis) to change pH.

Sodium ethanoate is basic because ethanoate ions accept protons from water, producing OH⁻. Ammonium chloride is acidic because NH₄⁺ donates protons to water. This matters for choosing titration indicators.

Worked example

pH of sodium ethanoate.

  1. Ka(ethanoic acid) = 1.74 × 10⁻⁵, so Kb(ethanoate) = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ ÷ 1.74 × 10⁻⁵ = 5.7 × 10⁻¹⁰.
  2. For 0.10 mol dm⁻³: [OH⁻] = √(5.7 × 10⁻¹¹) = 7.6 × 10⁻⁶.
  3. pOH = 5.12.
  4. pH = 8.88, so the solution is basic.
Common mistakeThinking all salt solutions are neutral. Salts of weak acids or weak bases change pH.

In 3 lines: Ka × Kb = Kw. pKa + pKb = 14. Salt solutions vary.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which salt solution is basic?
    • Sodium chloride
    • Ammonium chloride
    • Sodium ethanoate
    • Potassium nitrate
    Show the answer

    Answer: Sodium ethanoate. Ethanoate ions accept protons from water, forming OH⁻.

  2. Why is ammonium chloride solution acidic?
    • Chloride ions donate protons
    • It contains hydroxide ions
    • It is a strong base
    • NH₄⁺ donates protons to water, forming H₃O⁺
    Show the answer

    Answer: NH₄⁺ donates protons to water, forming H₃O⁺. The conjugate acid of a weak base is weakly acidic.

  3. Why does a strong acid have a negligible conjugate base strength?
    • Ka × Kb = Kw, so a huge Ka gives a tiny Kb
    • Strong acids have no conjugate base at all
    • Kb increases with Ka
    • Kw is zero for strong acids
    Show the answer

    Answer: Ka × Kb = Kw, so a huge Ka gives a tiny Kb. Because Ka × Kb = Kw, a very large Ka forces Kb to be tiny.

Key terms

Conjugate pair
An acid and base differing by one proton
Hydrolysis
Reaction of an ion with water changing pH
pKw
−log Kw, 14 at 25 °C
