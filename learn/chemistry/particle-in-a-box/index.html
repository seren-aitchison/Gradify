Particle in a Box

In the particle-in-a-box model, Eₙ = n²h²/8mL², with nonzero zero-point energy; energy gaps grow as the box shrinks, explaining colour in conjugated dyes and size-dependent emission from quantum dots.

Chemistry · Physical Chemistry and Molecular Science · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the particle-in-a-box model
  • Calculate energy levels using Eₙ = n²h²/8mL²
  • Apply the model to conjugated dyes

The model

  • A particle is confined in a 1D box of length L with infinite walls.
  • ψ must be zero at the walls.
  • Allowed energies: Eₙ = n²h² / (8mL²), n = 1, 2, 3…
  • The lowest energy is not zero: zero-point energy.

Consequences

  • Energy levels are spaced further apart as n increases.
  • A smaller box gives larger energy gaps.
  • A heavier particle gives smaller gaps.
  • Nodes in ψ increase with n: n − 1 nodes.

π electrons in a conjugated chain behave roughly like particles in a box. Longer chains mean larger L and smaller gaps, so absorption shifts to longer wavelengths, explaining why long conjugated dyes are coloured. Quantum dots show size-dependent colours for the same reason.

Worked example

Quantum dots.

  1. Quantum dots are nanocrystals a few nm across.
  2. Electrons are confined in a small "box".
  3. Smaller dots have larger energy gaps and emit blue light.
  4. Larger dots have smaller gaps and emit red light.
Common mistakeThinking the particle can have zero energy. Confinement guarantees a minimum zero-point energy.

In 3 lines: Eₙ ∝ n²/L². Zero-point energy. Dyes and quantum dots.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. How does the energy gap change if the box is made smaller?
    • It decreases
    • It increases
    • It stays the same
    • It becomes zero
    Show the answer

    Answer: It increases. Energy is inversely proportional to L², so a smaller box gives bigger gaps.

  2. Why do longer conjugated chains absorb at longer wavelengths?
    • Longer chains have larger gaps
    • Conjugation does not affect absorption
    • A longer "box" narrows the energy gaps
    • Electrons are heavier in long chains
    Show the answer

    Answer: A longer "box" narrows the energy gaps. Larger L lowers the energy needed for transitions.

  3. Why do smaller quantum dots emit bluer light?
    • Smaller dots contain many more electrons
    • The size of the dot has no effect on emission
    • Larger dots always emit the bluest light
    • Stronger confinement gives larger energy gaps and higher-energy photons
    Show the answer

    Answer: Stronger confinement gives larger energy gaps and higher-energy photons. Energy rises as the box shrinks, so emitted photons have shorter wavelengths.

Key terms

Particle in a box
A quantum model of a confined particle
Zero-point energy
The lowest possible energy, which is not zero
Quantum dot
A nanocrystal with size-dependent optical properties
