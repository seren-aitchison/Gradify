Computational Molecular Modelling

Molecular mechanics uses force fields for fast modelling without electrons, quantum methods such as DFT model electrons and bond breaking and QM/MM combines both; geometry optimisation, molecular dynamics, Monte Carlo and docking predict structures and behaviour, validated by experiment.

Chemistry · Physical Chemistry and Molecular Science · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Compare molecular mechanics and quantum mechanical methods
  • Describe geometry optimisation and energy calculations
  • Describe molecular dynamics simulations

Methods

  • Molecular mechanics (MM): atoms as balls and bonds as springs using a force field; fast but no electrons.
  • Quantum mechanical (QM) methods such as DFT: include electrons; slower but can model bond breaking.
  • QM/MM combines both for large systems such as enzymes.
  • Geometry optimisation finds the lowest-energy structure.

Simulations

  • Molecular dynamics (MD) follows atoms over time using Newton's laws.
  • Monte Carlo methods sample random configurations.
  • Docking predicts how drugs fit protein binding sites.
  • Results must be validated against experiment.

Computational chemistry helps design drugs, catalysts and materials before they are made, saving time and cost. The 2013 Nobel Prize recognised multiscale models of complex chemical systems. AI methods now predict protein structures with high accuracy.

Worked example

Choosing a method.

  1. Conformations of a large protein: molecular mechanics or MD.
  2. Bond breaking in a small molecule: DFT.
  3. Reaction in an enzyme active site: QM/MM.
  4. Drug binding to a target: docking, then MD.
Common mistakeTrusting computational results without checking them. Models must be validated experimentally.

In 3 lines: MM, QM and QM/MM. Optimisation and MD. Validate results.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which method can model bond breaking?
    • Classical molecular mechanics
    • Simple ball-and-stick models
    • Quantum mechanical methods such as DFT
    • Docking alone
    Show the answer

    Answer: Quantum mechanical methods such as DFT. Bond breaking requires treating electrons explicitly.

  2. Why is molecular mechanics used for very large systems?
    • It is more accurate than quantum methods
    • It models bond breaking well
    • It needs no parameters
    • It is fast because it ignores electrons
    Show the answer

    Answer: It is fast because it ignores electrons. Speed allows millions of atoms to be simulated.

  3. Why must computational results be validated experimentally?
    • Approximations in models can give inaccurate predictions
    • Computers never make errors
    • Experiments are less reliable than models
    • Validation is only needed for small molecules
    Show the answer

    Answer: Approximations in models can give inaccurate predictions. Models are only as good as their assumptions.
