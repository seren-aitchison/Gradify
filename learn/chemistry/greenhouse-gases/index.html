Greenhouse Gases

Greenhouse gases absorb and re-emit infrared radiation from Earth's surface because their vibrations change dipole moments; CO₂, CH₄, N₂O and water are important, and GWP compares their warming effects relative to CO₂.

Chemistry · Materials, Environmental and Industrial Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain the greenhouse effect
  • Explain why some gases absorb infrared radiation
  • Compare greenhouse gases using global warming potential

The greenhouse effect

  • Sunlight warms Earth's surface, which emits infrared radiation.
  • Greenhouse gases absorb and re-emit IR, warming the lower atmosphere.
  • Without it, Earth would be about 33 °C colder.
  • Extra greenhouse gases enhance the effect.

Which gases

  • Molecules absorb IR if vibrations change their dipole moment.
  • CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, H₂O and CFCs absorb IR; N₂ and O₂ do not.
  • Global warming potential (GWP) compares gases with CO₂ over a time period.
  • Methane's GWP over 100 years is about 28 times CO₂.

Atmospheric CO₂ has risen from about 280 ppm before industrialisation to over 420 ppm. Methane is more powerful per molecule but shorter-lived. Atmospheric lifetime and concentration both determine overall warming effects.

Worked example

Why CO₂ absorbs IR.

  1. CO₂ is linear and non-polar.
  2. Its asymmetric stretch changes the dipole moment.
  3. Its bending vibrations also change the dipole.
  4. These vibrations absorb Earth's infrared radiation.
Common mistakeThinking non-polar molecules cannot be greenhouse gases. CO₂ is non-polar but absorbs IR through dipole-changing vibrations.

In 3 lines: IR absorbed and re-emitted. Dipole-changing vibrations. GWP comparisons.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which gas is not a greenhouse gas?
    • Methane, CH₄
    • Nitrogen, N₂
    • Carbon dioxide, CO₂
    • Nitrous oxide, N₂O
    Show the answer

    Answer: Nitrogen, N₂. N₂ has no vibrations that change its dipole moment, so it cannot absorb IR.

  2. Why is methane considered a powerful greenhouse gas?
    • It stays in the atmosphere for thousands of years
    • It is the most abundant gas in air
    • Each molecule traps much more heat than a CO₂ molecule
    • It does not absorb infrared
    Show the answer

    Answer: Each molecule traps much more heat than a CO₂ molecule. Its GWP over 100 years is about 28 times that of carbon dioxide.

  3. Why does CO₂ absorb IR even though it is non-polar?
    • It has a permanent dipole moment
    • It absorbs only visible light
    • Non-polar molecules always absorb IR
    • Its asymmetric stretch and bends create temporary dipoles
    Show the answer

    Answer: Its asymmetric stretch and bends create temporary dipoles. IR activity needs a dipole change, not a permanent dipole.

Key terms

Greenhouse effect
Warming of the lower atmosphere by absorbed infrared
Global warming potential
Warming effect of a gas relative to CO₂
Atmospheric lifetime
Average time a gas stays in the atmosphere
