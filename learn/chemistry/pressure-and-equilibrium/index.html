Pressure and Equilibrium

Increasing pressure shifts gaseous equilibria towards fewer gas molecules without changing K, by changing Q; the Haber process uses a compromise pressure of about 200 atm to balance yield, rate, cost and safety.

Chemistry · Chemical Equilibrium · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Predict the effect of pressure on gaseous equilibria
  • Explain the effect using concentrations or Q
  • Evaluate the use of high pressure in industry

Effect of pressure

  • Increasing pressure shifts equilibrium towards the side with fewer gas molecules.
  • Decreasing pressure favours the side with more gas molecules.
  • If both sides have equal gas molecules, pressure has no effect on position.
  • K is unchanged by pressure.

Industry

  • Haber: N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃ (4 → 2 molecules), so high pressure increases yield.
  • High pressure also increases rate.
  • But high pressure is costly and dangerous.
  • A compromise of about 200 atm is used.

Adding an inert gas at constant volume does not change partial pressures, so it does not shift equilibrium. Pressure effects work by changing concentrations of gases, which changes Q relative to K.

Worked example

Compressing the Haber mixture.

  1. At equilibrium, the gas mixture is compressed to half its volume.
  2. All partial pressures double.
  3. Q decreases below Kp because the reactant side has more molecules.
  4. The reaction shifts right, forming more ammonia.
Common mistakeThinking pressure affects every equilibrium. It has no effect when both sides have equal gas molecules.

In 3 lines: Fewer molecules at high pressure. K unchanged. Compromise pressures.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. For H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI, what effect does increasing pressure have on position?
    • None, as both sides have two gas molecules
    • It shifts right towards more HI
    • Shifts left
    • Stops the reaction
    Show the answer

    Answer: None, as both sides have two gas molecules. Equal gas molecules on both sides mean no shift.

  2. Why is high pressure used in the Haber process?
    • It lowers the yield but speeds up the rate
    • Fewer molecules on the product side, so yield increases
    • It changes K
    • It cools the reactor
    Show the answer

    Answer: Fewer molecules on the product side, so yield increases. Four reactant molecules form two product molecules.

  3. Why isn't an even higher pressure used in the Haber process?
    • Ammonia decomposes at high pressure
    • K falls with pressure
    • Equipment cost and safety risks rise sharply
    • The catalyst stops working
    Show the answer

    Answer: Equipment cost and safety risks rise sharply. Very high pressures are expensive and dangerous.

Key terms

Gaseous equilibrium
An equilibrium involving gases
Compromise pressure
A pressure balancing yield, cost and safety
Inert gas
A gas not taking part in the reaction
