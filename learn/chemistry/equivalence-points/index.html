Equivalence Points

The equivalence point is when acid and base have reacted in stoichiometric amounts, at pH 7 for strong–strong, above 7 for weak acid–strong base and below for strong acid–weak base; titration curves show steep sections, buffer regions and pKa at half-equivalence.

Chemistry · Acids, Bases and Aqueous Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Distinguish equivalence point and end point
  • Describe titration curves for different acid-base combinations
  • Use titration curves to find pKa

Equivalence point

  • The equivalence point is when moles of acid and base match the equation.
  • The end point is when the indicator changes; ideally it matches the equivalence point.
  • Strong acid–strong base: equivalence at pH 7.
  • Weak acid–strong base: equivalence above pH 7; strong acid–weak base: below pH 7.

Titration curves

  • Plot pH against volume added.
  • A steep vertical section occurs around the equivalence point.
  • For a weak acid titrated with strong base, at the half-equivalence point pH = pKa.
  • The flatter region before equivalence is a buffer region.

The pH at equivalence depends on the salt formed: ethanoate ions are basic, so a weak acid–strong base titration ends above 7. Reading pH at half-equivalence gives pKa directly from the Henderson-Hasselbalch equation, because [HA] = [A⁻].

Worked example

Finding pKa.

  1. 25.0 cm³ of a weak acid is titrated with NaOH; equivalence is at 20.0 cm³.
  2. At 10.0 cm³ (half-equivalence), the pH is 4.8.
  3. At this point [HA] = [A⁻].
  4. So pKa = 4.8.
Common mistakeAssuming every equivalence point is at pH 7. It depends on the strengths of the acid and base.

In 3 lines: Stoichiometric point. pH depends on strengths. pKa at half-equivalence.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is the pH at equivalence for a strong acid–strong base titration?
    • Below 7
    • 7
    • Above 9
    • Exactly 4
    Show the answer

    Answer: 7. The salt of a strong acid and strong base does not hydrolyse, so pH is 7.

  2. Why is the equivalence pH above 7 for ethanoic acid with NaOH?
    • Excess acid remains
    • Sodium ions are acidic
    • Ethanoate ions are basic and produce OH⁻
    • NaOH is a weak base
    Show the answer

    Answer: Ethanoate ions are basic and produce OH⁻. The conjugate base of a weak acid hydrolyses.

  3. Why does pH equal pKa at half-equivalence?
    • All acid has reacted
    • The solution is neutral
    • Indicators change colour there
    • [HA] equals [A⁻], so the log term is zero
    Show the answer

    Answer: [HA] equals [A⁻], so the log term is zero. Henderson-Hasselbalch gives pH = pKa + log 1.

Key terms

Equivalence point
When reactants have reacted in stoichiometric amounts
Half-equivalence point
When half the acid has been neutralised
Titration curve
A graph of pH against volume added
