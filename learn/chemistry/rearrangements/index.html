Rearrangements

Rearrangements include 1,2-hydride and alkyl shifts that convert carbocations into more stable ones and named reactions such as the pinacol, Beckmann and Claisen rearrangements, driven by more stable intermediates or stronger bonds.

Chemistry · Organic Reaction Mechanisms · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe carbocation rearrangements
  • Describe named rearrangement reactions
  • Explain the driving forces behind rearrangements

Carbocation shifts

  • 1,2-hydride shifts move H⁻ to an adjacent carbocation.
  • 1,2-alkyl shifts move a methyl or other alkyl group.
  • Shifts form a more stable carbocation.
  • They occur in SN1, E1 and electrophilic addition.

Named rearrangements

  • Pinacol rearrangement: a 1,2-diol in acid gives a ketone.
  • Beckmann rearrangement: an oxime gives an amide, used to make nylon-6.
  • Claisen rearrangement: an allyl vinyl ether gives a carbonyl compound on heating.
  • Driving forces: more stable intermediates or stronger bonds such as C=O.

Rearrangements can be a problem, giving unexpected products, or a tool, building complex skeletons. The Beckmann rearrangement of cyclohexanone oxime to caprolactam is used industrially on a scale of millions of tonnes per year.

Worked example

A hydride shift in action.

  1. 3-methylbutan-2-ol is treated with HBr.
  2. Water leaves, forming a secondary carbocation.
  3. A hydride shifts from the neighbouring carbon, forming a tertiary carbocation.
  4. The major product is 2-bromo-2-methylbutane.
Common mistakeAssuming the product always has the same skeleton as the starting material. Carbocation shifts can change it.

In 3 lines: Shifts to stabler cations. Named rearrangements. Problem or tool.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which rearrangement converts a 1,2-diol into a ketone?
    • The Beckmann rearrangement
    • The Claisen rearrangement
    • The pinacol rearrangement
    • The Walden inversion
    Show the answer

    Answer: The pinacol rearrangement. Acid-catalysed loss of water and a 1,2-shift give the ketone.

  2. Why does 3-methylbutan-2-ol give 2-bromo-2-methylbutane with HBr?
    • Bromide attacks before any carbocation forms
    • The alcohol has no stereocentre
    • HBr removes a methyl group
    • A hydride shift converts a secondary carbocation into a tertiary one
    Show the answer

    Answer: A hydride shift converts a secondary carbocation into a tertiary one. The more stable carbocation is captured by bromide.

  3. Why is the Beckmann rearrangement industrially important?
    • It converts cyclohexanone oxime into caprolactam for nylon-6
    • It makes ethanol directly from ethene gas
    • It produces sulfuric acid from sulfur dioxide
    • It purifies drinking water on a large scale
    Show the answer

    Answer: It converts cyclohexanone oxime into caprolactam for nylon-6. Caprolactam from the rearrangement is polymerised into nylon-6.

Key terms

Rearrangement
A reaction that changes atom connectivity within a molecule
1,2-shift
Movement of a group to an adjacent atom
Oxime
A compound with C=N–OH
