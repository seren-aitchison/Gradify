Acid Dissociation Constants

Ka = [H⁺][A⁻] ÷ [HA] measures weak acid strength, with pKa = −log Ka; for weak acids [H⁺] ≈ √(Ka[HA]) using approximations valid for small dissociation, and larger Ka or smaller pKa means a stronger acid.

Chemistry · Acids, Bases and Aqueous Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Write the expression for Ka
  • Convert between Ka and pKa
  • Calculate the pH of a weak acid solution

Ka

  • For HA ⇌ H⁺ + A⁻: Ka = [H⁺][A⁻] ÷ [HA].
  • Larger Ka means a stronger weak acid.
  • pKa = −log Ka; smaller pKa means stronger acid.
  • Units: mol dm⁻³.

pH of weak acids

  • Assume [H⁺] = [A⁻].
  • Assume [HA] at equilibrium ≈ initial concentration (small dissociation).
  • So [H⁺] = √(Ka × [HA]).
  • pH = −log[H⁺].

The approximations are valid when the acid is weak and not too dilute, and they break down if dissociation exceeds about 5%. Ka values allow acids to be ranked: ethanoic acid (pKa 4.76) is weaker than methanoic acid (pKa 3.75).

Worked example

pH of ethanoic acid.

  1. 0.10 mol dm⁻³ ethanoic acid has Ka = 1.74 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³.
  2. [H⁺] = √(1.74 × 10⁻⁵ × 0.10).
  3. [H⁺] = 1.32 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³.
  4. pH = 2.88.
Common mistakeThinking a larger pKa means a stronger acid. Larger pKa means a weaker acid.

In 3 lines: Ka expression. pKa = −log Ka. [H⁺] = √(Ka[HA]).

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which acid is stronger: pKa 3.75 or pKa 4.76?
    • The acid with pKa 4.76
    • The acid with pKa 3.75
    • They are equally strong
    • Neither is an acid
    Show the answer

    Answer: The acid with pKa 3.75. Lower pKa means larger Ka and more dissociation.

  2. What two approximations are used for weak acid pH?
    • [HA] = 0 and [A⁻] = 1
    • [H⁺] equals the acid concentration
    • [H⁺] = [A⁻] and [HA] stays about the same as initially
    • Ka equals pH
    Show the answer

    Answer: [H⁺] = [A⁻] and [HA] stays about the same as initially. Small dissociation keeps [HA] near its starting value.

  3. When do the weak acid approximations break down?
    • When the acid is very weak
    • When Ka is very small
    • When the solution is concentrated
    • When the acid dissociates by more than about 5%
    Show the answer

    Answer: When the acid dissociates by more than about 5%. Large dissociation makes [HA] change significantly.
