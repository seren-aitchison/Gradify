Periodic Trends

Across a period, effective nuclear charge rises, so atoms shrink and hold electrons more tightly; down a group, added shells outweigh added protons.

Chemistry · Atomic Structure and Quantum Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain effective nuclear charge and shielding
  • Describe and explain trends in atomic radius, ionisation energy and electronegativity
  • Predict how a property changes between two named elements

Effective nuclear charge

An outer electron doesn't feel the full nuclear charge, because inner electrons repel it. The effective nuclear charge is roughly the nuclear charge minus the shielding by inner electrons. Across period 3, from sodium to argon, protons are added one at a time while the inner 10 electrons (1s² 2s² 2p⁶) stay the same, so the effective nuclear charge on the outer (n = 3) electrons rises steadily.

Three trends

  • Atomic radius decreases across a period (higher effective nuclear charge pulls the same shell in) and increases down a group (more shells). Sodium's atom (about 186 pm) is far larger than chlorine's (about 100 pm covalent radius).
  • First ionisation energy increases across a period and decreases down a group, for the same reasons, with small dips where a new subshell starts or pairing begins.
  • Electronegativity, an atom's pull on shared electrons in a bond, increases across a period and decreases down a group. On the Pauling scale fluorine is highest (3.98) and caesium among the lowest (0.79).

Down a group

Going down a group, the nuclear charge increases a lot, but so does the number of inner shells shielding the outer electrons. The outer electrons are much further out, so overall they are held less strongly: atoms get bigger and lose electrons more easily, which is why reactivity increases down group 1.

Worked example

Predict which has the larger atomic radius, magnesium or sulfur, and explain.

  1. Both are in period 3, with outer electrons in the third shell.
  2. Sulfur has 4 more protons with similar shielding, so its outer electrons are pulled closer: magnesium is larger.
Common mistakeSaying atoms get bigger across a period "because they have more electrons". They get smaller: the added electrons go into the same shell while the rising nuclear charge pulls it in.

In 3 lines: Effective nuclear charge rises across a period; shells are added down a group. Radius falls across a period and rises down a group. Ionisation energy and electronegativity rise across and fall down.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. How does atomic radius change down group 1?
    • It decreases
    • It stays the same
    • It increases then decreases
    • It increases
    Show the answer

    Answer: It increases. Each element down the group has an extra shell of electrons.

  2. Which atom has the highest first ionisation energy: sodium, aluminium, phosphorus or argon?
    • Argon
    • Sodium
    • Aluminium
    • Phosphorus
    Show the answer

    Answer: Argon. Ionisation energy generally rises across period 3, so the noble gas at the end, argon, is highest.

  3. Caesium's nucleus has 55 protons and sodium's 11, yet caesium loses its outer electron more easily. Why?
    • Caesium has fewer protons
    • Caesium's outer electron is in the sixth shell, much further out and shielded by many more inner electrons, so it is held less strongly
    • Sodium's outer electron is in the sixth shell
    • Caesium has no inner electrons
    Show the answer

    Answer: Caesium's outer electron is in the sixth shell, much further out and shielded by many more inner electrons, so it is held less strongly. Extra shielding and distance outweigh the larger nuclear charge.

Key terms

Effective nuclear charge
The net positive charge felt by an outer electron after shielding by inner electrons
Atomic radius
Half the distance between the nuclei of two bonded atoms of the same element
Electronegativity
The ability of an atom to attract the bonding electrons in a covalent bond
