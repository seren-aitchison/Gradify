UV-visible Spectroscopy

UV-visible absorption promotes electrons in chromophores, shifting to longer wavelengths with more conjugation; the Beer-Lambert law A = εcl relates absorbance to concentration, measured at λmax.

Chemistry · Analytical Chemistry and Spectroscopy · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain what causes UV-visible absorption
  • Apply the Beer-Lambert law
  • Describe uses of UV-visible spectroscopy

Absorption

  • UV-visible light promotes electrons to higher energy levels.
  • Absorbing groups are called chromophores, such as C=C, C=O and conjugated systems.
  • More conjugation shifts absorption to longer wavelengths.
  • Transition metal complexes absorb through d-d transitions.

Beer-Lambert law

  • A = εcl: absorbance = molar absorptivity × concentration × path length.
  • Absorbance has no units; l is usually 1 cm.
  • A plot of A against c is a straight line through the origin.
  • Measure at λmax, the wavelength of maximum absorbance.

UV-visible spectroscopy is widely used to measure concentrations of coloured or UV-absorbing species, such as DNA at 260 nm and proteins at 280 nm. β-carotene is orange because its long conjugated chain absorbs blue light.

Worked example

Finding concentration.

  1. A solution has A = 0.45 at λmax in a 1.00 cm cell.
  2. ε = 15,000 dm³ mol⁻¹ cm⁻¹.
  3. c = A ÷ (εl) = 0.45 ÷ 15,000.
  4. c = 3.0 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³.
Common mistakeMeasuring at any wavelength. Measuring at λmax gives the best sensitivity and least error.

In 3 lines: Electrons promoted. A = εcl. Conjugation shifts λmax.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What does UV-visible light do to molecules?
    • Makes bonds vibrate
    • Promotes electrons to higher energy levels
    • Flips nuclear spins
    • Breaks molecules into ions
    Show the answer

    Answer: Promotes electrons to higher energy levels. UV-visible photons match electronic energy gaps.

  2. Why does β-carotene appear orange?
    • It contains orange metal ions
    • It reflects all wavelengths equally
    • Its long conjugated chain absorbs blue light
    • It has no conjugation
    Show the answer

    Answer: Its long conjugated chain absorbs blue light. More conjugation shifts absorption into the visible.

  3. Why is absorbance measured at λmax?
    • It is the only wavelength absorbed
    • Beer-Lambert only works at λmax
    • Absorbance is zero elsewhere
    • Sensitivity is greatest and small wavelength errors matter least
    Show the answer

    Answer: Sensitivity is greatest and small wavelength errors matter least. The peak top is flat and gives the strongest signal.

Key terms

Chromophore
A group that absorbs UV or visible light
Molar absorptivity
ε, how strongly a species absorbs at a wavelength
λmax
The wavelength of maximum absorbance
