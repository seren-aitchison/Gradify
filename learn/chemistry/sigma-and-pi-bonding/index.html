Sigma and Pi Bonding

σ bonds form by end-on overlap and allow rotation, while π bonds form by side-on overlap, prevent rotation and are weaker and more reactive; double bonds contain one σ and one π, triple bonds one σ and two π.

Chemistry · Chemical Bonding and Molecular Structure · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Distinguish sigma and pi bonds by orbital overlap
  • Count sigma and pi bonds in molecules
  • Explain how pi bonds affect rotation and reactivity

Two types

  • σ bonds form by end-on (head-on) overlap along the line between nuclei.
  • π bonds form by side-on overlap of p orbitals above and below the line.
  • Every single bond is a σ bond.
  • A double bond is one σ + one π; a triple bond is one σ + two π.

Consequences

  • σ bonds allow free rotation; π bonds prevent rotation.
  • This causes cis-trans (E/Z) isomerism in alkenes.
  • π bonds are weaker than σ bonds and have exposed electron density.
  • Alkenes react by electrophilic addition at the π bond.

A C=C double bond (612 kJ mol⁻¹) is less than twice as strong as a C–C single bond (347 kJ mol⁻¹), showing the π bond is weaker than the σ bond. The electron-rich π bond attracts electrophiles such as bromine.

Worked example

Counting bonds in ethene.

  1. Ethene, C₂H₄, has four C–H bonds and one C=C bond.
  2. Each C–H is one σ bond.
  3. The C=C is one σ and one π bond.
  4. Total: five σ bonds and one π bond.
Common mistakeThinking a double bond is two identical bonds. It is one σ and one weaker π bond.

In 3 lines: End-on σ, side-on π. Count them. π bonds stop rotation.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. How many π bonds does ethene contain?
    • One
    • Two
    • Zero
    • Five
    Show the answer

    Answer: One. The C=C double bond contains one σ bond and one π bond.

  2. Why can't atoms rotate around a C=C double bond?
    • The σ bond is far too strong to twist
    • Rotation would break the side-on π overlap
    • Double bonds are ionic
    • The atoms are too heavy
    Show the answer

    Answer: Rotation would break the side-on π overlap. The π bond holds the p orbitals parallel.

  3. Why are alkenes more reactive than alkanes?
    • Alkanes have π bonds that are very stable
    • Alkenes have no σ bonds
    • Their π bond is weaker and exposes electron density
    • Alkanes are ionic
    Show the answer

    Answer: Their π bond is weaker and exposes electron density. Exposed π electrons attract electrophiles.

Key terms

σ bond
A bond from end-on orbital overlap
π bond
A bond from side-on overlap of p orbitals
Restricted rotation
Inability to rotate about a double bond
