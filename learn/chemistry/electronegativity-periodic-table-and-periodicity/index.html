Electronegativity

Electronegativity is the ability of an atom to attract bonding electrons, highest for fluorine, increasing across periods and decreasing down groups; differences predict non-polar, polar covalent or ionic bonding along a continuum.

Chemistry · Periodic Table and Periodicity · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define electronegativity
  • Explain trends in electronegativity
  • Use electronegativity differences to predict bond type

Definition and trends

  • Electronegativity is the ability of an atom to attract the bonding pair of electrons in a covalent bond.
  • Measured on the Pauling scale; fluorine is highest at 4.0.
  • Increases across a period and decreases down a group.
  • Nitrogen, oxygen and fluorine are especially electronegative.

Bond type

  • Equal electronegativity: non-polar covalent bond, such as Cl–Cl.
  • Small difference: polar covalent bond, with partial charges δ+ and δ−.
  • Large difference (above about 1.8): mainly ionic bonding.
  • Bonding is a continuum rather than strict categories.

Electronegativity increases across a period because nuclear charge rises and atoms get smaller, and decreases down a group because bonding electrons are further from the nucleus. Polar bonds affect molecular polarity, intermolecular forces and reactivity.

Worked example

Predicting bond polarity.

  1. In H–Cl, H is 2.2 and Cl is 3.2 on the Pauling scale.
  2. The difference is 1.0.
  3. The bond is polar covalent.
  4. Chlorine carries δ− and hydrogen δ+.
Common mistakeThinking electronegativity applies to isolated atoms. It describes attraction for electrons in a bond.

In 3 lines: Attraction for bonding electrons. Up across, down down. Predicts bond type.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. How does electronegativity change across a period?
    • It increases
    • It decreases
    • It stays the same
    • It falls to zero
    Show the answer

    Answer: It increases. Higher nuclear charge and smaller atoms attract bonding electrons more.

  2. In H–F, which atom carries the partial negative charge?
    • Hydrogen
    • Fluorine
    • Both equally
    • Neither atom
    Show the answer

    Answer: Fluorine. Fluorine is far more electronegative, so it draws the electrons towards it.

  3. Why does electronegativity decrease down a group?
    • Nuclear charge decreases down the group
    • Atoms get smaller down the group
    • Bonding electrons are further from the nucleus and more shielded
    • Atoms lose shells
    Show the answer

    Answer: Bonding electrons are further from the nucleus and more shielded. Increased distance weakens attraction for bonding electrons.

Key terms

Electronegativity
Ability of an atom to attract bonding electrons
Polar bond
A bond with unequal sharing of electrons
Pauling scale
A common scale of electronegativity
