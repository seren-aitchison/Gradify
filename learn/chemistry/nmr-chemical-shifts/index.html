NMR Chemical Shifts

The δ scale measures shift relative to TMS in ppm, independent of field strength; electronegative atoms and π systems deshield protons, moving them downfield, and typical ranges let signals be assigned.

Chemistry · Analytical Chemistry and Spectroscopy · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define chemical shift and the δ scale
  • Explain shielding and deshielding effects
  • Use typical ¹H shift values to assign signals

The δ scale

  • δ (ppm) = (frequency difference from TMS ÷ spectrometer frequency) × 10⁶.
  • Independent of magnet strength.
  • Upfield (low δ): shielded; downfield (high δ): deshielded.
  • Most ¹H signals lie between 0 and 12 ppm.

Effects

  • Electronegative atoms deshield nearby protons: CH₃–O at about 3.3 ppm.
  • π systems deshield via ring currents: aromatic H at 6.5–8.0 ppm.
  • Aldehyde H at 9–10 ppm; carboxylic acid H at 10–12 ppm.
  • Alkyl H at 0.9–1.7 ppm.

Chemical shifts let chemists assign each signal to a part of the molecule. Aromatic protons appear far downfield because circulating π electrons create a field that reinforces the applied field at the protons.

Worked example

Assigning ethyl ethanoate.

  1. CH₃ next to C=O: about 2.0 ppm.
  2. O–CH₂: about 4.1 ppm (deshielded by O).
  3. CH₃ of the ethyl group: about 1.3 ppm.
  4. Shifts show which group is next to oxygen.
Common mistakeThinking high δ means more shielding. High δ means deshielded protons.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Where do aromatic protons usually appear?
    • 0.9–1.7 ppm
    • 3.3–4.0 ppm
    • 10–12 ppm
    • 6.5–8.0 ppm
    Show the answer

    Answer: 6.5–8.0 ppm. Ring currents strongly deshield aromatic protons, moving them downfield.

  2. Why does a CH₂ next to oxygen appear at higher δ than an alkyl CH₂?
    • Electronegative oxygen withdraws electrons and deshields it
    • Oxygen adds electron density to the CH₂
    • The CH₂ is shielded by oxygen
    • Oxygen has no effect on shift
    Show the answer

    Answer: Electronegative oxygen withdraws electrons and deshields it. Less shielding by electrons means the signal appears further downfield.

  3. Why are aromatic protons strongly deshielded?
    • Aromatic rings contain no electrons near the protons
    • Ring currents create a field that adds to the applied field at the protons
    • Aromatic protons are bonded directly to oxygen
    • Ring currents cancel out the applied magnetic field
    Show the answer

    Answer: Ring currents create a field that adds to the applied field at the protons. Induced fields reinforce the field outside the ring.

Key terms

Chemical shift
Position of a signal relative to TMS in ppm
Deshielding
Reduction of electron shielding, raising δ
Ring current
Circulating π electrons in an aromatic ring
