Introduction to Chemical Bonding

Atoms bond because it lowers their energy through electrostatic attraction; ionic bonds form between metals and non-metals, covalent bonds between non-metals and metallic bonds between metal atoms.

Chemistry · Chemical Bonding and Molecular Structure · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain why atoms form chemical bonds
  • Distinguish ionic, covalent and metallic bonding
  • Relate bond type to the elements involved

Why atoms bond

  • Bonding lowers the energy of a system, making it more stable.
  • Bonds form through electrostatic attraction between positive and negative charges.
  • Many atoms achieve a stable arrangement such as a full outer shell.
  • Breaking bonds requires energy; making bonds releases energy.

Types of bond

  • Ionic: between metals and non-metals, by electron transfer.
  • Covalent: between non-metals, by sharing electron pairs.
  • Metallic: between metal atoms, with delocalised electrons.
  • Bond type depends on electronegativity difference and metallic character.

The "octet rule" is a useful guide but has many exceptions, such as BF₃ and SF₆. All bonding is fundamentally electrostatic. Intermolecular forces between molecules are much weaker than the bonds within them.

Worked example

Classifying bonds.

  1. Sodium chloride: a metal with a non-metal, so ionic.
  2. Water: two non-metals, so covalent.
  3. Copper: metal atoms only, so metallic.
  4. Each type gives very different properties.
Common mistakeThinking atoms "want" a full shell. Bonding happens because it lowers energy, not because atoms have desires.

In 3 lines: Bonding lowers energy. Ionic, covalent, metallic. Depends on the elements.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which type of bond forms between a metal and a non-metal?
    • Ionic bonding
    • Covalent bonding
    • Metallic bonding
    • Hydrogen bonding
    Show the answer

    Answer: Ionic bonding. Electrons transfer from the metal to the non-metal, forming ions.

  2. Which molecule breaks the octet rule?
    • Methane, CH₄
    • Boron trifluoride, BF₃
    • Water, H₂O
    • Ammonia, NH₃
    Show the answer

    Answer: Boron trifluoride, BF₃. Boron in BF₃ has only six outer electrons, an exception to the rule.

  3. Why does making bonds release energy?
    • Bonds store extra energy when they are formed
    • Atoms lose mass when bonding
    • Attraction between charges lowers the potential energy of the system
    • Electrons gain kinetic energy
    Show the answer

    Answer: Attraction between charges lowers the potential energy of the system. Moving into an attractive arrangement lowers energy, which is released.

Key terms

Chemical bond
An attraction holding atoms together
Octet rule
The tendency to have eight outer electrons
Electrostatic attraction
Attraction between opposite charges
