Group 17

Halogens are diatomic non-metals with ns²np⁵ configurations, darkening and boiling higher down the group; reactivity decreases down the group, more reactive halogens displace less reactive ones and silver nitrate tests distinguish halide ions.

Chemistry · Periodic Table and Periodicity · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the physical properties of the halogens
  • Explain displacement reactions and reactivity trends
  • Describe tests for halide ions

Properties

  • Fluorine, chlorine, bromine, iodine and astatine; outer configuration ns² np⁵.
  • Exist as diatomic molecules: F₂, Cl₂, Br₂, I₂.
  • At room temperature: F₂ and Cl₂ are gases, Br₂ a liquid and I₂ a solid.
  • Colour darkens and boiling point increases down the group.

Reactivity

  • Halogens gain one electron to form halide ions (X⁻).
  • Reactivity decreases down the group.
  • A more reactive halogen displaces a less reactive one: Cl₂ + 2KBr → 2KCl + Br₂.
  • Halides are tested with acidified silver nitrate: chloride white, bromide cream, iodide yellow precipitate.

Down the group, atoms are larger and the incoming electron is further from the nucleus and more shielded, so it is attracted less strongly. Boiling points rise because larger molecules have stronger London forces. Chlorine is used to disinfect drinking water.

Worked example

A displacement reaction.

  1. Chlorine water is added to potassium iodide solution.
  2. The colourless solution turns brown.
  3. Cl₂ + 2I⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + I₂.
  4. Chlorine is more reactive and oxidises iodide to iodine.
Common mistakeThinking reactivity increases down group 17 as in group 1. Halogens gain electrons, which becomes harder down the group.

In 3 lines: Diatomic and coloured. Less reactive down the group. Silver nitrate tests.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which halogen is a liquid at room temperature?
    • Chlorine
    • Bromine
    • Iodine
    • Fluorine
    Show the answer

    Answer: Bromine. Bromine is a dark red-brown liquid at room temperature.

  2. Why does reactivity decrease down group 17?
    • Atoms lose electrons more easily
    • Nuclear charge decreases down the group
    • The incoming electron is further from the nucleus and more shielded
    • Molecules become lighter
    Show the answer

    Answer: The incoming electron is further from the nucleus and more shielded. Weaker attraction makes gaining an electron harder.

  3. Why do boiling points increase down group 17?
    • Covalent bonds get stronger
    • Molecules become ionic
    • Atoms gain protons only
    • Larger molecules have stronger London forces
    Show the answer

    Answer: Larger molecules have stronger London forces. More electrons give stronger temporary dipoles.

Key terms

Halogen
A group 17 element
Displacement reaction
A more reactive element replacing a less reactive one
Halide
An ion of a halogen with a 1− charge
