Benzene Structure

The Kekulé model of alternating bonds cannot explain benzene's equal bond lengths, resistance to addition or extra stability; the delocalised model, with six overlapping p orbitals forming a π ring, is supported by bond lengths, reactivity and hydrogenation enthalpies.

Chemistry · Organic Chemistry Foundations · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the Kekulé model and its problems
  • Describe the delocalised model of benzene
  • Use evidence to support the delocalised model

Kekulé model

  • Proposed in 1865: a six-membered ring of alternating single and double bonds.
  • Predicts two bond lengths, 0.154 nm and 0.134 nm.
  • Predicts benzene should decolourise bromine water readily.
  • Does not explain benzene's stability.

Delocalised model

  • Each carbon is sp², with one p orbital above and below the ring.
  • The six p orbitals overlap to form a delocalised π system.
  • All C–C bonds are equal, 0.139 nm.
  • The ring is drawn as a hexagon with a circle.

Three pieces of evidence support delocalisation: all bond lengths are equal by X-ray diffraction, benzene resists addition reactions, and the enthalpy of hydrogenation is about 150 kJ mol⁻¹ less exothermic than predicted for three C=C bonds. This difference is the delocalisation energy.

Worked example

Hydrogenation evidence.

  1. Cyclohexene + H₂ → cyclohexane: ΔH = −120 kJ mol⁻¹.
  2. Kekulé benzene with three C=C bonds: predicted −360 kJ mol⁻¹.
  3. Measured value for benzene: about −208 kJ mol⁻¹.
  4. Benzene is about 152 kJ mol⁻¹ more stable than predicted.
Common mistakeThinking benzene flips between two Kekulé structures. The electrons are delocalised all the time.

In 3 lines: Kekulé's problems. Delocalised ring. Three lines of evidence.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which feature does the delocalised model predict for benzene?
    • Three short bonds and three long bonds
    • No π electrons in the ring
    • All six C–C bonds have the same length
    • A non-planar chair shape
    Show the answer

    Answer: All six C–C bonds have the same length. Delocalisation makes every C–C bond identical.

  2. What does the hydrogenation enthalpy of benzene show?
    • Benzene contains exactly three localised C=C bonds
    • Benzene is less stable than cyclohexene
    • Benzene cannot be hydrogenated at all
    • Benzene is about 150 kJ mol⁻¹ more stable than the Kekulé model predicts
    Show the answer

    Answer: Benzene is about 150 kJ mol⁻¹ more stable than the Kekulé model predicts. The measured value is much less exothermic than predicted.

  3. Why did the Kekulé model fail to explain benzene's reactions?
    • It predicted alkene-like addition, but benzene mainly undergoes substitution
    • It predicted no reactions at all
    • It suggested benzene was ionic
    • It ignored carbon entirely
    Show the answer

    Answer: It predicted alkene-like addition, but benzene mainly undergoes substitution. Real benzene does not decolourise bromine water readily.

Key terms

Kekulé structure
A benzene model with alternating single and double bonds
Delocalisation energy
The extra stability from delocalised electrons
Delocalised π system
π electrons spread over several atoms
