Harmonic Oscillator

The harmonic oscillator gives equally spaced vibrational levels Eᵥ = (v + ½)hν with frequency depending on force constant and reduced mass; real bonds are anharmonic, with converging levels, overtones and dissociation described by the Morse potential.

Chemistry · Physical Chemistry and Molecular Science · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the harmonic oscillator model of a vibrating bond
  • Calculate vibrational energy levels
  • Explain anharmonicity and bond dissociation

The model

  • A bond acts like a spring obeying Hooke's law.
  • Energy levels: Eᵥ = (v + ½)hν, v = 0, 1, 2…
  • Levels are equally spaced by hν.
  • Frequency depends on force constant k and reduced mass μ: ν = (1/2π)√(k/μ).

Real bonds

  • Real bonds are anharmonic: levels get closer at high energy.
  • Bonds eventually dissociate, which a spring cannot do.
  • The Morse potential models this better.
  • Overtones (Δv = 2, 3) appear weakly because of anharmonicity.

Stronger bonds and lighter atoms vibrate faster, which explains why C–H stretches appear at higher wavenumbers than C–Cl. Replacing hydrogen with deuterium lowers the frequency by about √2, used to assign IR peaks.

Worked example

Isotope effect on vibration.

  1. C–H stretch: about 3000 cm⁻¹.
  2. Replace H with D: the reduced mass roughly doubles.
  3. Frequency falls by about √2.
  4. C–D stretch appears near 2100 cm⁻¹.
Common mistakeThinking vibrational energy can be zero. The lowest level has zero-point energy ½hν.

In 3 lines: Equal spacing. k and μ set frequency. Real bonds are anharmonic.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. How are harmonic oscillator energy levels spaced?
    • Equally, by hν
    • Further apart at higher v
    • Closer together at higher v
    • Randomly
    Show the answer

    Answer: Equally, by hν. The harmonic model gives constant spacing.

  2. Why does a C–D bond vibrate at lower frequency than C–H?
    • The C–D bond is much stronger
    • Deuterium's greater mass increases the reduced mass
    • Deuterium has fewer electrons
    • Frequency does not depend on mass
    Show the answer

    Answer: Deuterium's greater mass increases the reduced mass. Frequency is proportional to √(k/μ), so a larger reduced mass lowers it.

  3. Why do overtone bands appear weakly in IR spectra?
    • Harmonic oscillators allow all transitions equally
    • Overtones are always the strongest bands
    • Anharmonicity relaxes the Δv = ±1 selection rule
    • Only Raman shows overtones
    Show the answer

    Answer: Anharmonicity relaxes the Δv = ±1 selection rule. Without anharmonicity, overtones would be forbidden.

Key terms

Force constant
A measure of bond stiffness
Reduced mass
μ = m₁m₂/(m₁ + m₂)
Anharmonicity
Deviation from ideal spring behaviour
