Formal Charge

Formal charge equals valence electrons minus lone pair electrons minus half the bonding electrons; the best Lewis structure minimises formal charges and places negative charges on electronegative atoms, and formal charge differs from oxidation state.

Chemistry · Chemical Bonding and Molecular Structure · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Calculate formal charge on atoms in a Lewis structure
  • Use formal charges to choose the best structure
  • Distinguish formal charge from oxidation state

Calculation

  • Formal charge = valence electrons − lone pair electrons − ½ × bonding electrons.
  • The sum of formal charges equals the overall charge of the species.
  • In NH₄⁺, nitrogen has formal charge +1.
  • Formal charges are written next to atoms.

Choosing structures

  • The best structure has formal charges as close to zero as possible.
  • Negative formal charges should be on the more electronegative atoms.
  • Avoid adjacent atoms with the same sign of formal charge.
  • Formal charge assumes electrons are shared equally.

Oxidation state assumes bonding electrons go entirely to the more electronegative atom, while formal charge assumes equal sharing. Neither is the real charge, which lies between them. Formal charge explains why the thiocyanate ion is best drawn with nitrogen carrying the negative charge.

Worked example

Formal charge in CO.

  1. In C≡O, carbon has 4 valence electrons, 2 lone pair electrons and 6 bonding electrons.
  2. Carbon: 4 − 2 − 3 = −1.
  3. Oxygen: 6 − 2 − 3 = +1.
  4. The formal charges sum to zero, the overall charge.
Common mistakeTreating formal charge as the actual charge on an atom. It is a bookkeeping tool assuming equal sharing.

In 3 lines: FC = V − L − ½B. Minimise formal charges. Not the real charge.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is the formal charge on nitrogen in NH₄⁺?
    • 0
    • −1
    • +1
    • +4
    Show the answer

    Answer: +1. 5 − 0 − ½(8) = +1, matching the overall charge of the ion.

  2. Which atoms should carry negative formal charges in the best structure?
    • The least electronegative atoms
    • Hydrogen atoms only
    • The central atom always
    • The more electronegative atoms
    Show the answer

    Answer: The more electronegative atoms. Electronegative atoms are best able to hold extra electron density.

  3. How does formal charge differ from oxidation state?
    • Formal charge assumes equal sharing; oxidation state gives electrons to the more electronegative atom
    • They are always identical
    • Formal charge ignores lone pairs
    • Oxidation state ignores bonding electrons
    Show the answer

    Answer: Formal charge assumes equal sharing; oxidation state gives electrons to the more electronegative atom. They use opposite assumptions about bonding electrons.
