Ligands

Ligands are classified by denticity as monodentate, bidentate, hexadentate or ambidentate; they affect coordination number, colour, magnetism and stability, ranked by the spectrochemical series, and strong ligands such as CO explain toxicity.

Chemistry · Inorganic Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Classify ligands by charge and denticity
  • Describe common monodentate and multidentate ligands
  • Explain how ligands affect complex properties

Types

  • Monodentate: one donor atom, such as H₂O, NH₃, Cl⁻, CN⁻.
  • Bidentate: two donor atoms, such as ethane-1,2-diamine (en) and ethanedioate (oxalate).
  • Hexadentate: six donor atoms, such as EDTA⁴⁻.
  • Ambidentate ligands can bind through different atoms, such as SCN⁻.

Effects

  • Ligand size affects coordination number: Cl⁻ is larger than H₂O.
  • Ligands differ in field strength, affecting colour and magnetism.
  • Chelating ligands form more stable complexes.
  • Some ligands are strong π-acceptors, such as CO and CN⁻.

Carbon monoxide is toxic because it binds iron in haemoglobin much more strongly than oxygen. The spectrochemical series ranks ligands by field strength: I⁻ < Cl⁻ < H₂O < NH₃ < en < CN⁻ < CO. Porphyrin rings in haem and chlorophyll are natural multidentate ligands.

Worked example

Why CO is poisonous.

  1. Haemoglobin's iron normally binds O₂ reversibly.
  2. CO also binds to the iron.
  3. CO binds about 200 times more strongly than O₂.
  4. Haemoglobin cannot carry oxygen, causing poisoning.
Common mistakeThinking all ligands bind equally strongly. Ligands differ greatly in field strength and binding.

In 3 lines: Monodentate to hexadentate. Field strength. CO toxicity.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which ligand is hexadentate?
    • Ammonia
    • Water
    • EDTA⁴⁻
    • Chloride
    Show the answer

    Answer: EDTA⁴⁻. EDTA wraps around metals using six donor atoms.

  2. Why does chloride often give tetrahedral complexes with Cu²⁺?
    • Chloride is a hexadentate ligand
    • Water is larger than chloride
    • Chloride cannot bond to copper
    • Chloride ions are larger, so fewer fit around the metal
    Show the answer

    Answer: Chloride ions are larger, so fewer fit around the metal. Larger ligands lower the coordination number.

  3. Why is carbon monoxide poisonous?
    • It binds haemoglobin iron far more strongly than oxygen
    • It dissolves red blood cells in the bloodstream
    • It is a strong acid that burns lung tissue
    • It removes all iron from the body quickly
    Show the answer

    Answer: It binds haemoglobin iron far more strongly than oxygen. Blocked binding sites cannot carry oxygen.

Key terms

Denticity
Number of donor atoms a ligand uses
Ambidentate
Able to bind through different atoms
Spectrochemical series
A ranking of ligands by field strength
