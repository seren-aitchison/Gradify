Retrosynthetic Analysis

Retrosynthesis disconnects bonds into synthons that are matched with real reagents, prioritising C–heteroatom bonds and C–C bonds next to functional groups and using functional group interconversion to reach simple starting materials.

Chemistry · Synthetic Chemistry and Laboratory Methods · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Identify strategic disconnections in a target
  • Translate synthons into real reagents
  • Use functional group interconversion in retrosynthesis

Disconnections and synthons

  • A disconnection is drawn with a ⇒ arrow.
  • Breaking a bond gives idealised fragments called synthons.
  • Each synthon has a real synthetic equivalent (reagent).
  • Disconnect next to functional groups, where reactions are known.

Strategies

  • Disconnect C–X bonds (X = O, N) first, such as esters and amides.
  • Disconnect C–C bonds next to carbonyls or alcohols.
  • Use functional group interconversion (FGI) to reach a group that disconnects easily.
  • Aim for simple, available starting materials.

For an ester, disconnecting the C–O bond gives an acyl cation synthon and an alkoxide synthon, whose equivalents are an acyl chloride and an alcohol. For a tertiary alcohol, disconnecting a C–C bond suggests a Grignard reagent adding to a ketone.

Worked example

Retrosynthesis of 2-methylbutan-2-ol.

  1. Target: a tertiary alcohol.
  2. Disconnect a C–C bond to the carbinol carbon.
  3. Synthons: an ethyl anion and a cation on propanone's carbon.
  4. Equivalents: ethylmagnesium bromide and propanone.
Common mistakeTreating synthons as real reagents. They are idealised fragments needing real equivalents.

In 3 lines: Disconnect at groups. Synthons to reagents. Use FGI.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What are the synthetic equivalents for disconnecting an ester?
    • An alkane and water
    • Two alkenes
    • A ketone and a nitrile
    • An acyl chloride and an alcohol
    Show the answer

    Answer: An acyl chloride and an alcohol. The acyl cation and alkoxide map onto these reagents.

  2. Why are disconnections made next to functional groups?
    • Known reactions can form bonds at those positions
    • Functional groups never react
    • Bonds far from groups are easier to make
    • It makes the target more complex
    Show the answer

    Answer: Known reactions can form bonds at those positions. Reliable reactions build bonds near reactive groups.

  3. Why is functional group interconversion used in retrosynthesis?
    • It removes all functional groups
    • It turns a hard-to-disconnect group into one with a known disconnection
    • It adds extra carbon atoms only
    • It is used only after synthesis
    Show the answer

    Answer: It turns a hard-to-disconnect group into one with a known disconnection. Changing the group to one with a known disconnection opens easier routes.

Key terms

Synthon
An idealised fragment from a disconnection
Synthetic equivalent
A real reagent that acts as a synthon
Functional group interconversion
Converting one group to another in planning
