Gases

Gases fill their containers, are compressible and mix readily; pressure arises from particle collisions, is measured in pascals and temperatures for gas calculations must be in kelvin.

Chemistry · States of Matter and Physical Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the properties of gases
  • Describe how gas pressure arises and is measured
  • Convert between units of pressure and temperature

Properties

  • Gases have no fixed shape or volume and fill their container.
  • They are highly compressible because particles are far apart.
  • They mix completely and diffuse quickly.
  • Gas densities are low, roughly a thousandth of liquid densities.

Pressure and units

  • Pressure is force per unit area, caused by particles colliding with walls.
  • SI unit: the pascal (Pa) = 1 N m⁻²; 1 atm = 101,325 Pa = 101.3 kPa.
  • Temperature in gas calculations must be in kelvin: K = °C + 273.
  • Standard temperature and pressure values vary; IUPAC uses 273.15 K and 100 kPa.

A barometer measures atmospheric pressure, first demonstrated by Torricelli with mercury in 1643. Gas behaviour depends on the amount of gas, volume, temperature and pressure, which are linked by the gas laws. Using kelvin avoids negative temperatures in proportional relationships.

Worked example

Converting units.

  1. A gas is at 25 °C and 2.0 atm.
  2. Temperature = 25 + 273 = 298 K.
  3. Pressure = 2.0 × 101,325 = 202,650 Pa.
  4. This is about 203 kPa.
Common mistakeUsing degrees Celsius in gas law calculations. Temperature must be converted to kelvin.

In 3 lines: Compressible and mixing. Pressure from collisions. Use kelvin.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is the SI unit of pressure?
    • The atmosphere
    • The pascal
    • The bar
    • The millimetre of mercury
    Show the answer

    Answer: The pascal. One pascal is one newton per square metre of area.

  2. Why are gases easily compressed?
    • Their particles are tightly packed together
    • Their particles cannot move at all
    • Their particles are far apart with much empty space
    • Their particles are much larger than in liquids
    Show the answer

    Answer: Their particles are far apart with much empty space. Large gaps between particles let the volume shrink.

  3. Why must temperature be in kelvin for gas calculations?
    • Kelvin values are always smaller than Celsius
    • Celsius cannot measure gases
    • Kelvin is the only unit for liquids
    • Gas laws are proportional relationships starting from absolute zero
    Show the answer

    Answer: Gas laws are proportional relationships starting from absolute zero. Proportionality only works on an absolute scale without negatives.
