Constitutional Isomers

Constitutional isomers differ in atom connectivity, unlike stereoisomers; degrees of unsaturation count rings and π bonds to narrow possible structures, systematic searches find all isomers and tautomers are rapidly interconverting constitutional isomers.

Chemistry · Organic Chemistry Foundations · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define constitutional isomers and contrast them with stereoisomers
  • Use degrees of unsaturation to predict possible structures
  • Find constitutional isomers systematically

Connectivity

  • Constitutional isomers differ in which atoms bond to which.
  • This term is the IUPAC name for structural isomers.
  • Stereoisomers share connectivity; constitutional isomers do not.
  • Tautomers are constitutional isomers that interconvert rapidly.

Degrees of unsaturation

  • Formula: DoU = (2C + 2 + N − H − X) / 2.
  • Each ring or double bond counts as 1; a triple bond counts as 2.
  • C₆H₆ has DoU = 4: benzene's ring plus three π bonds.
  • Helps narrow possible structures from a formula.

Keto-enol tautomerism is a special case where a hydrogen shifts between carbon and oxygen; propanone exists mostly as the keto form. Searching for isomers systematically, starting with the longest chain and then shortening it, avoids missing or repeating structures.

Worked example

Finding isomers of C₅H₁₂.

  1. Longest chain of 5: pentane.
  2. Chain of 4 with a methyl: 2-methylbutane.
  3. Chain of 3 with two methyls: 2,2-dimethylpropane.
  4. Total: three constitutional isomers.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. How many constitutional isomers does C₅H₁₂ have?
    • 3
    • 2
    • 4
    • 5
    Show the answer

    Answer: 3. Pentane, 2-methylbutane and 2,2-dimethylpropane are the only three.

  2. What does a degree of unsaturation of 1 tell you?
    • The molecule is fully saturated
    • The molecule has one ring or one double bond
    • The molecule has a triple bond
    • The molecule has no carbon
    Show the answer

    Answer: The molecule has one ring or one double bond. Each ring or π bond adds one degree of unsaturation.

  3. Why are keto and enol forms called tautomers rather than resonance forms?
    • Only electrons move between the forms
    • They are mirror images of each other
    • A hydrogen atom actually moves, changing connectivity
    • They have different molecular formulas
    Show the answer

    Answer: A hydrogen atom actually moves, changing connectivity. Resonance forms differ only in electron placement.

Key terms

Constitutional isomers
Isomers with different atom connectivity
Degree of unsaturation
Number of rings plus π bonds in a molecule
Tautomers
Rapidly interconverting constitutional isomers
