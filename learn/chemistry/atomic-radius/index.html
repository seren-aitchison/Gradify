Atomic Radius

Atomic radius is half the distance between bonded identical nuclei; it decreases across a period as effective nuclear charge rises and increases down a group as shells are added, influencing ionisation energy and reactivity.

Chemistry · Periodic Table and Periodicity · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define atomic radius
  • Explain the trend in atomic radius across a period
  • Explain the trend in atomic radius down a group

Definition

  • Atomic radius is half the distance between the nuclei of two bonded identical atoms.
  • Covalent radius applies to covalently bonded atoms; metallic radius to metals.
  • Measured in picometres (pm).
  • Noble gas radii are measured differently and are often left out of comparisons.

Trends

  • Across a period: radius decreases, because nuclear charge increases while shielding stays similar.
  • Down a group: radius increases, because extra shells are added.
  • Effective nuclear charge is the net positive charge felt by outer electrons.
  • Transition metals change little in size across a period.

Atomic size affects ionisation energy, electronegativity and reactivity. Across a period, added electrons go into the same shell, so they shield each other poorly and are pulled closer. Down a group, the extra shells outweigh the higher nuclear charge.

Worked example

Comparing sodium and chlorine.

  1. Sodium (Z = 11) has radius about 186 pm.
  2. Chlorine (Z = 17) has radius about 99 pm.
  3. Both have three occupied shells.
  4. Chlorine's six extra protons pull its electrons much closer.
Common mistakeThinking atoms get bigger across a period because they gain electrons. Increasing nuclear charge pulls the same shell inwards.

In 3 lines: Half the internuclear distance. Smaller across a period. Larger down a group.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which atom is largest?
    • Caesium
    • Lithium
    • Fluorine
    • Neon
    Show the answer

    Answer: Caesium. Caesium is at the bottom of group 1 with six shells.

  2. Why does atomic radius decrease across period 3?
    • Electrons are added to new shells
    • Nuclear charge increases while shielding stays similar
    • Protons are removed
    • Atoms lose electrons across the period
    Show the answer

    Answer: Nuclear charge increases while shielding stays similar. Greater pull on the same shell shrinks the atom.

  3. Why does atomic radius change little across the transition metals?
    • No electrons are added
    • Nuclear charge stays constant
    • Added d electrons shield outer 4s electrons fairly well
    • Transition metals have no outer electrons
    Show the answer

    Answer: Added d electrons shield outer 4s electrons fairly well. Inner d electrons offset the rising nuclear charge.

Key terms

Atomic radius
Half the distance between two bonded identical nuclei
Effective nuclear charge
Net positive charge felt by outer electrons
Shielding
Reduction of nuclear attraction by inner electrons
