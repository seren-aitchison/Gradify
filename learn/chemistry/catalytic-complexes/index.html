Catalytic Complexes

Homogeneous catalytic cycles combine ligand dissociation, oxidative addition, migratory insertion and reductive elimination, regenerating the catalyst; examples include Wilkinson's catalyst, the Cativa process, palladium cross-coupling and Ziegler-Natta polymerisation.

Chemistry · Inorganic Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe elementary steps in organometallic catalysis
  • Describe examples of homogeneous catalysts
  • Explain why catalysts regenerate in a cycle

Elementary steps

  • Ligand dissociation: a ligand leaves, opening a site.
  • Oxidative addition: the metal inserts into a bond, raising its oxidation state by 2.
  • Migratory insertion: a ligand such as an alkene inserts into a metal-ligand bond.
  • Reductive elimination: two ligands join and leave, lowering the oxidation state by 2.

Examples

  • Wilkinson's catalyst, RhCl(PPh₃)₃, hydrogenates alkenes.
  • Monsanto and Cativa processes make ethanoic acid from methanol and CO.
  • Palladium catalysts join carbon fragments in cross-coupling.
  • Ziegler-Natta catalysts polymerise alkenes.

Catalytic cycles return the catalyst to its starting form after each turn, so small amounts convert large quantities. Palladium cross-coupling, recognised by the 2010 Nobel Prize, is widely used to make medicines and electronic materials.

Worked example

Hydrogenation with Wilkinson's catalyst.

  1. A phosphine ligand dissociates.
  2. H₂ adds by oxidative addition: Rh(I) to Rh(III).
  3. The alkene binds and inserts into a Rh–H bond.
  4. Reductive elimination releases the alkane and regenerates Rh(I).
Common mistakeThinking a catalyst is used up in the reaction. It is regenerated at the end of each cycle.

In 3 lines: Four elementary steps. Industrial examples. Catalyst regenerated.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which step releases the product and lowers the oxidation state by two?
    • Reductive elimination
    • Oxidative addition
    • Ligand dissociation
    • Migratory insertion
    Show the answer

    Answer: Reductive elimination. In reductive elimination two ligands couple and leave the metal together.

  2. Why can a small amount of catalyst convert a large amount of reactant?
    • It is consumed in each reaction step
    • It is regenerated at the end of every cycle
    • It changes into the main product
    • It only works once and then decomposes
    Show the answer

    Answer: It is regenerated at the end of every cycle. The cycle returns the catalyst to its original form.

  3. Why is palladium cross-coupling valuable in industry?
    • It breaks down waste plastics into monomers
    • It removes all metal traces from products
    • It joins carbon fragments selectively to build complex molecules
    • It produces methane as its only product
    Show the answer

    Answer: It joins carbon fragments selectively to build complex molecules. Selective C–C bond formation is key to medicine synthesis.

Key terms

Oxidative addition
A metal inserts into a bond, raising its oxidation state by two
Reductive elimination
Two ligands couple and leave, lowering the oxidation state by two
Catalytic cycle
A sequence of steps that regenerates the catalyst
