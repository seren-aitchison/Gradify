Molecular Polarity

Molecular polarity depends on whether bond dipoles cancel; symmetrical molecules such as CO₂ and CCl₄ are non-polar, while bent or pyramidal molecules such as H₂O and NH₃ are polar, affecting solubility and intermolecular forces.

Chemistry · Chemical Bonding and Molecular Structure · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain how bond dipoles combine
  • Predict whether molecules are polar from their shape
  • Describe how polarity affects properties

Combining dipoles

  • Bond dipoles are vectors with size and direction.
  • In symmetrical molecules, dipoles cancel: CO₂, BF₃ and CH₄ are non-polar.
  • In unsymmetrical molecules, they do not cancel: H₂O, NH₃ and CHCl₃ are polar.
  • Lone pairs often make molecules unsymmetrical.

Effects

  • Polar molecules have permanent dipoles.
  • They experience dipole-dipole forces.
  • Polar substances dissolve in polar solvents: "like dissolves like".
  • A charged rod deflects a stream of a polar liquid, such as water.

CCl₄ has polar C–Cl bonds but is non-polar because its tetrahedral shape cancels the dipoles, whereas CHCl₃ is polar. Molecular polarity affects boiling points, solubility and how molecules interact in biology.

Worked example

CO₂ versus H₂O.

  1. Both contain polar bonds to oxygen.
  2. CO₂ is linear, so its two dipoles point in opposite directions and cancel.
  3. H₂O is bent, so its dipoles add to give a net dipole.
  4. Water is polar; carbon dioxide is not.
Common mistakeThinking any molecule with polar bonds is polar. Symmetry can cancel all the bond dipoles.

In 3 lines: Dipoles add as vectors. Symmetry cancels. Polarity affects properties.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which molecule is polar?
    • Ammonia, NH₃
    • Methane, CH₄
    • Carbon dioxide, CO₂
    • Tetrachloromethane, CCl₄
    Show the answer

    Answer: Ammonia, NH₃. The pyramidal shape of NH₃ prevents its bond dipoles cancelling.

  2. Why is CCl₄ non-polar despite having polar bonds?
    • Its C–Cl bonds are non-polar
    • Its tetrahedral symmetry cancels the bond dipoles
    • It contains no lone pairs on chlorine
    • Chlorine is less electronegative than carbon
    Show the answer

    Answer: Its tetrahedral symmetry cancels the bond dipoles. Equal dipoles in a symmetric arrangement sum to zero.

  3. Why is chloroform, CHCl₃, polar while CCl₄ is not?
    • CHCl₃ has more chlorine atoms
    • CCl₄ has lone pairs on carbon
    • Replacing one chlorine with hydrogen breaks the symmetry
    • Hydrogen is very electronegative
    Show the answer

    Answer: Replacing one chlorine with hydrogen breaks the symmetry. Unequal bonds around carbon leave a net dipole.

Key terms

Polar molecule
A molecule with a net dipole
Permanent dipole
A lasting separation of charge in a molecule
Vector
A quantity with size and direction
