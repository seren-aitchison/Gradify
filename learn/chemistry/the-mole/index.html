The Mole

One mole is 6.022 × 10²³ particles; its mass in grams equals the relative formula mass.

Chemistry · Foundations of Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define the mole using the Avogadro constant
  • Convert between mass, amount in moles and number of particles
  • Explain why moles, not masses, are used to compare amounts in reactions

Definition

Since the 2019 redefinition of the SI, one mole contains exactly 6.022 140 76 × 10²³ elementary entities (atoms, molecules, ions or electrons). The symbol is mol and the quantity is amount of substance, n. Before 2019 the mole was defined as the number of atoms in 12 g of carbon-12; the two definitions agree to within measurement precision.

Mass, moles and particles

n = m / Mamount (mol) = mass (g) ÷ molar mass (g/mol)
N = n × NAnumber of particles = amount × Avogadro constant
Worked example

How many moles and molecules are in 9.0 g of water (M = 18.0 g/mol)?

  1. n = 9.0 ÷ 18.0 = 0.50 mol.
  2. N = 0.50 × 6.022 × 10²³ = 3.0 × 10²³ molecules.

Why count in moles?

Equations show reacting particles. In 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O, hydrogen and oxygen react in a 2 : 1 ratio by number of molecules, so also 2 : 1 by moles, but not 2 : 1 by mass (4 g of hydrogen reacts with 32 g of oxygen). Moles let us use the ratios in equations directly.

Common mistakeForgetting to say which particles are counted. One mole of O₂ molecules contains two moles of O atoms.

In 2 lines: n = m/M and N = n × Nₐ. Equations give mole ratios, not mass ratios.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is the mass of 1 mol of carbon-12 atoms?
    • 12 kg
    • 6 g
    • 1 g
    • 12 g
    Show the answer

    Answer: 12 g. The molar mass in grams per mole equals the relative mass, so 1 mol of carbon-12 has a mass of 12 g.

  2. Which contains the most molecules?
    • 2 g of hydrogen, H₂ (M = 2 g/mol)
    • 16 g of oxygen, O₂ (M = 32 g/mol)
    • 14 g of nitrogen, N₂ (M = 28 g/mol)
    • 22 g of CO₂ (M = 44 g/mol)
    Show the answer

    Answer: 2 g of hydrogen, H₂ (M = 2 g/mol). 2 g of H₂ is 1 mol; each of the others is 0.5 mol, so H₂ has twice as many molecules.

  3. How many ions are in 1.0 mol of calcium chloride, CaCl₂?
    • 6.0 × 10²³
    • 1.8 × 10²⁴
    • 1.2 × 10²⁴
    • 3.0 × 10²³
    Show the answer

    Answer: 1.8 × 10²⁴. Each formula unit has one Ca²⁺ and two Cl⁻: 3 mol of ions × 6.022 × 10²³ = 1.8 × 10²⁴.

Key terms

Mole
The SI unit of amount of substance, containing exactly 6.02214076 × 10²³ entities
Amount of substance (n)
A quantity proportional to the number of particles, measured in moles
