Dipole-dipole Interactions

Permanent dipole-dipole forces attract δ+ and δ− ends of polar molecules in addition to London forces, raising boiling points for molecules of similar size, though London forces dominate in larger molecules.

Chemistry · Chemical Bonding and Molecular Structure · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain how permanent dipole-dipole forces arise
  • Compare the strength of dipole-dipole and London forces
  • Use dipole-dipole forces to explain physical properties

Permanent dipoles

  • Polar molecules have a permanent dipole.
  • The δ+ end of one molecule attracts the δ− end of another.
  • Molecules tend to line up to maximise attraction.
  • These forces act in addition to London forces.

Comparison

  • For molecules of similar size, dipole-dipole forces raise boiling points.
  • Propanone (polar, M = 58) boils at 56 °C; butane (non-polar, M = 58) at −1 °C.
  • In large molecules, London forces may still dominate.
  • HCl boils lower than HI because HI's larger London forces outweigh HCl's greater polarity.

Dipole-dipole forces also explain why polar solvents dissolve polar solutes. Ion-dipole forces, between ions and polar molecules, explain why ionic solids dissolve in water. The strength of dipole-dipole forces falls quickly with distance.

Worked example

Propanone versus butane.

  1. Both have relative molecular mass 58 and similar numbers of electrons.
  2. Propanone has a polar C=O bond and a permanent dipole.
  3. Butane is almost non-polar.
  4. Propanone boils 57 °C higher because of dipole-dipole forces.
Common mistakeThinking the most polar molecule always has the highest boiling point. Larger molecules may have stronger London forces.

In 3 lines: δ+ attracts δ−. Adds to London forces. Size can dominate.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which molecule has permanent dipole-dipole forces?
    • Propanone
    • Butane
    • Methane
    • Carbon dioxide
    Show the answer

    Answer: Propanone. Propanone's polar C=O bond gives a permanent dipole.

  2. Why does HI boil higher than HCl even though HCl is more polar?
    • HI forms hydrogen bonds between molecules
    • HI has many more electrons, giving stronger London forces
    • HCl is non-polar
    • HI is an ionic solid
    Show the answer

    Answer: HI has many more electrons, giving stronger London forces. Size outweighs polarity here, so HI's London forces dominate.

  3. Why do molecules of similar size but different polarity have different boiling points?
    • Polarity reduces London forces to zero
    • Non-polar molecules form hydrogen bonds
    • Polar ones have extra dipole-dipole attraction
    • Mass alone decides boiling point
    Show the answer

    Answer: Polar ones have extra dipole-dipole attraction. With London forces similar, dipole attraction makes the difference.

Key terms

Permanent dipole-dipole force
Attraction between polar molecules
Ion-dipole force
Attraction between an ion and a polar molecule
Polar solvent
A solvent with polar molecules
