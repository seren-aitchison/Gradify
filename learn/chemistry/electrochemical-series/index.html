Electrochemical Series

The electrochemical series lists reduction potentials from the strongest reducing agents to the strongest oxidising agents, reflecting metal reactivity; a species can oxidise any species with a more negative E⦵, predicting displacement and reactions with acids under standard conditions.

Chemistry · Redox and Electrochemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the arrangement of the electrochemical series
  • Predict which reactions are feasible using the series
  • Relate the series to metal reactivity

The series

  • Half-equations listed in order of E⦵, as reductions.
  • Most negative at one end: strongest reducing agents (Li, K, Na).
  • Most positive at the other: strongest oxidising agents (F₂, MnO₄⁻).
  • It reflects the reactivity series of metals.

Predicting reactions

  • A species can oxidise any species with a more negative E⦵.
  • The "anticlockwise rule": the reduced form lower in the table reacts with the oxidised form higher up (depending on layout).
  • Example: Cl₂ (+1.36 V) oxidises Br⁻ (+1.09 V).
  • Predictions assume standard conditions and say nothing about rate.

The electrochemical series explains displacement reactions: a more reactive metal displaces a less reactive one from solution. It also predicts which metals react with acids to release hydrogen: those with negative E⦵.

Worked example

Will iron react with copper sulfate?

  1. E⦵(Fe²⁺/Fe) = −0.44 V; E⦵(Cu²⁺/Cu) = +0.34 V.
  2. Cu²⁺ has the more positive potential, so it can oxidise Fe.
  3. Fe + Cu²⁺ → Fe²⁺ + Cu; E⦵cell = +0.78 V.
  4. The reaction is feasible, depositing copper on the iron.
Common mistakeThinking the series predicts how fast a reaction happens. It only predicts feasibility.

In 3 lines: Ordered by E⦵. Predict feasibility. Matches reactivity.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which is the strongest oxidising agent?
    • Li⁺ (E⦵ = −3.04 V)
    • Zn²⁺ (E⦵ = −0.76 V)
    • Fe²⁺ (E⦵ = −0.44 V)
    • F₂ (E⦵ = +2.87 V)
    Show the answer

    Answer: F₂ (E⦵ = +2.87 V). The most positive potential belongs to the strongest oxidising agent.

  2. Can bromine (E⦵ = +1.09 V) oxidise chloride ions (E⦵ = +1.36 V)?
    • No, chlorine's potential is more positive
    • Yes, bromine is stronger
    • Yes, both are halogens
    • It depends only on temperature
    Show the answer

    Answer: No, chlorine's potential is more positive. Only a more positive oxidant can oxidise Cl⁻.

  3. Why might a reaction predicted by the series not happen?
    • The series is always wrong
    • It may be very slow or conditions may not be standard
    • All feasible reactions are instant
    • The series ignores oxidising agents
    Show the answer

    Answer: It may be very slow or conditions may not be standard. Predictions concern thermodynamics, not rate or non-standard conditions.

Key terms

Electrochemical series
Half-cells listed in order of E⦵
Displacement reaction
A more reactive element replacing a less reactive one
Reactivity series
Metals ranked by reactivity
