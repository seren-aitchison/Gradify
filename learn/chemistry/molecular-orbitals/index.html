Molecular Orbitals

Atomic orbitals combine into bonding and antibonding MOs; bond order is half the difference in electrons, O₂'s two unpaired π* electrons make it paramagnetic and Hückel theory describes conjugated π systems, whose HOMO-LUMO gap narrows with conjugation.

Chemistry · Physical Chemistry and Molecular Science · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Construct MO diagrams for second-row diatomic molecules
  • Explain the paramagnetism of oxygen using MO theory
  • Describe Hückel theory for conjugated π systems

Diatomic molecules

  • Atomic orbitals combine to form bonding and antibonding MOs.
  • Bond order = ½(bonding − antibonding electrons).
  • O₂ has two unpaired electrons in π* orbitals: it is paramagnetic.
  • In B₂, C₂ and N₂, s-p mixing raises σ2p above π2p.

Conjugated systems

  • Hückel theory treats π orbitals separately from σ.
  • n p orbitals combine into n π molecular orbitals.
  • The HOMO (highest occupied) and LUMO (lowest unoccupied) control reactivity and colour.
  • Longer conjugation narrows the HOMO-LUMO gap.

Lewis structures predict O₂ has all electrons paired, but liquid oxygen sticks to a magnet. MO theory explains this. Frontier orbital theory, using HOMO and LUMO, explains many reactions, including cycloadditions.

Worked example

MO configuration of O₂.

  1. O₂ has 12 valence electrons.
  2. σ2s² σ2s² σ2p² π2p⁴ π2p².
  3. The two π* electrons occupy separate orbitals.
  4. Bond order = ½(8 − 4) = 2; two unpaired electrons.
Common mistakeUsing Lewis structures to predict magnetism. They fail for O₂, which MO theory explains.

In 3 lines: Bonding and antibonding. O₂ paramagnetic. HOMO and LUMO.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Why is O₂ paramagnetic?
    • It has two unpaired electrons in π* orbitals
    • All its electrons are paired
    • It has an odd number of electrons
    • It contains metal atoms
    Show the answer

    Answer: It has two unpaired electrons in π* orbitals. Hund's rule places one electron in each π* orbital.

  2. What happens to the HOMO-LUMO gap as conjugation increases?
    • It increases steadily with each bond
    • It decreases, shifting absorption to longer wavelengths
    • It stays exactly the same
    • It becomes zero immediately
    Show the answer

    Answer: It decreases, shifting absorption to longer wavelengths. More combined p orbitals bring levels closer.

  3. Why does s-p mixing change the MO order in N₂ but not in O₂?
    • O₂ has no p orbitals available for bonding
    • N₂ has no s orbitals in its valence shell
    • The 2s and 2p energies are closer in lighter atoms, allowing mixing
    • s-p mixing only ever happens in charged ions
    Show the answer

    Answer: The 2s and 2p energies are closer in lighter atoms, allowing mixing. Larger s-p gaps in O and F suppress mixing.
