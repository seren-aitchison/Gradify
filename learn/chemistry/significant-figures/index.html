Significant Figures

Round the final answer to match the least precise measurement (multiplication and division) or the fewest decimal places (addition and subtraction).

Chemistry · Foundations of Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Count the significant figures in a measured value
  • Round answers to an appropriate number of significant figures
  • Apply the rules for multiplication, division, addition and subtraction

Counting

  • All non-zero digits are significant (3.42 has 3).
  • Zeros between non-zero digits are significant (2.05 has 3).
  • Leading zeros are not significant (0.0052 has 2).
  • Trailing zeros after a decimal point are significant (25.0 has 3; 0.200 has 3).
  • Trailing zeros in a whole number are ambiguous (1,500 could be 2, 3 or 4); standard form removes the doubt (1.50 × 10³ has 3).

Calculations

  • Multiplying and dividing: give the answer to the same number of significant figures as the least precise value used.
  • Adding and subtracting: give the answer to the same number of decimal places as the value with the fewest decimal places.
  • Keep extra figures in intermediate steps and round only at the end, to avoid rounding errors.
  • Exact numbers (coefficients, counted items, the defined Avogadro constant) don't limit precision.
Worked example

Calculate the concentration when 0.53 g of NaCl (M 58.44) is dissolved in 250.0 cm³.

  1. n = 0.53 ÷ 58.44 = 0.009 069 mol (keep extra figures).
  2. c = 0.009 069 ÷ 0.2500 = 0.036 28 mol/dm³.
  3. The mass has 2 significant figures, so c = 0.036 mol/dm³.
Worked example

Give the mean of the titres 24.15, 24.20 and 24.10 cm³ to an appropriate precision.

  1. Sum = 72.45 cm³; mean = 24.15 cm³.
  2. Each titre has two decimal places, read to ±0.05 cm³, so the mean is given to two decimal places: 24.15 cm³.
Common mistakeCopying every digit from the calculator. An answer of 0.036 275 mol/dm³ claims a precision the data don't have.

In 2 lines: Leading zeros don't count; trailing zeros after a decimal point do. Round only at the end, to the precision of the data.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. How many significant figures does 2.0060 have?
    • 3
    • 5
    • 4
    • 2
    Show the answer

    Answer: 5. All digits count: zeros between non-zero digits and trailing zeros after the decimal point are significant.

  2. 12.5 cm³ is added to 3.25 cm³. How should the total be given?
    • 15.75 cm³
    • 16 cm³
    • 15.8 cm³
    • 15.750 cm³
    Show the answer

    Answer: 15.8 cm³. For addition, use the fewest decimal places (one, from 12.5): 15.75 rounds to 15.8.

  3. A titre of 23.45 cm³ of 0.1000 mol/dm³ acid reacts with 25.0 cm³ of alkali. To how many significant figures should the alkali's concentration be quoted?
    • 4, from the acid concentration
    • 5
    • 2
    • 3, limited by the 25.0 cm³ pipette volume
    Show the answer

    Answer: 3, limited by the 25.0 cm³ pipette volume. For multiplication and division the least precise value (25.0, three significant figures) limits the answer.

Key terms

Significant figures
The digits in a value that are known reliably, plus one estimated digit
Rounding error
An error introduced by rounding intermediate values too early
