Nernst Equation

The Nernst equation E = E⦵ − (RT/nF) ln Q gives electrode potentials under non-standard conditions; lower ion concentrations make metal electrode potentials more negative, explaining concentration cells, pH meters and falling battery voltages.

Chemistry · Redox and Electrochemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • State the Nernst equation
  • Calculate electrode potentials under non-standard conditions
  • Explain concentration cells

The equation

  • E = E⦵ − (RT/nF) ln Q.
  • At 298 K: E = E⦵ − (0.0257/n) ln Q, or (0.0592/n) log Q.
  • For a metal ion half-cell Mⁿ⁺ + ne⁻ ⇌ M: E = E⦵ + (0.0592/n) log[Mⁿ⁺].
  • Decreasing ion concentration makes E more negative.

Uses

  • Concentration cells: identical electrodes in different concentrations produce a voltage.
  • pH meters use the Nernst equation: potential changes by 0.0592 V per pH unit.
  • At equilibrium, E = 0 and Q = K: E⦵ = (RT/nF) ln K.
  • Nerve cells use ion concentration differences to create membrane potentials.

The Nernst equation explains why batteries lose voltage as reactants are used up. It links electrochemistry to equilibrium constants. Walther Nernst developed it in 1889.

Worked example

Copper at low concentration.

  1. E⦵(Cu²⁺/Cu) = +0.34 V; [Cu²⁺] = 0.010 mol dm⁻³; n = 2.
  2. E = 0.34 + (0.0592/2) log 0.010.
  3. E = 0.34 + 0.0296 × (−2).
  4. E = 0.28 V.
Common mistakeAssuming electrode potentials never change. They depend on concentration and temperature.

In 3 lines: Potential depends on concentration. 0.0592/n log term. pH meters and nerves.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. How does lowering [Cu²⁺] affect E for Cu²⁺/Cu?
    • It makes E more positive
    • It has no effect
    • It makes E less positive
    • It makes E exactly zero
    Show the answer

    Answer: It makes E less positive. Lower concentration favours oxidation, lowering the potential.

  2. How much does a pH electrode potential change per pH unit at 298 K?
    • About 1.0 V
    • About 0.34 V
    • About 8.314 V
    • About 0.0592 V
    Show the answer

    Answer: About 0.0592 V. The Nernst slope is 0.0592 V per decade change in [H⁺].

  3. Why does a battery's voltage fall as it is used?
    • Reactant concentrations fall, changing Q and E
    • The electrodes grow larger as it discharges
    • E⦵ increases with use
    • Electrons are destroyed
    Show the answer

    Answer: Reactant concentrations fall, changing Q and E. The Nernst equation shows potential depends on Q.
