Colloids

Colloids contain dispersed particles of 1 nm to 1 μm as sols, emulsions, foams, aerosols and gels; they are stabilised by charge or steric barriers, coagulated by electrolytes and scatter light in the Tyndall effect.

Chemistry · Physical Chemistry and Molecular Science · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Classify colloids by their phases
  • Explain the stability of colloids
  • Describe properties and uses of colloids

Types

  • Sol: solid in liquid (paint, ink).
  • Emulsion: liquid in liquid (milk, mayonnaise).
  • Foam: gas in liquid (whipped cream).
  • Aerosol: liquid or solid in gas (fog, smoke).
  • Gel: liquid trapped in a solid network (jelly).

Stability and properties

  • Particles are kept apart by charge repulsion or steric barriers.
  • Electrolytes can cause coagulation by screening charges.
  • Tyndall effect: colloids scatter light, making a beam visible.
  • Emulsifiers such as lecithin stabilise emulsions.

Colloids are everywhere in food, medicines, cosmetics and the environment. Rivers deposit clay colloids when they meet salty seawater, forming deltas, because salt causes the particles to coagulate. Car headlights show the Tyndall effect in fog.

Worked example

Making mayonnaise.

  1. Oil and vinegar do not mix on their own.
  2. Egg yolk contains lecithin, an emulsifier.
  3. Lecithin's polar head sits in water and its tail in oil.
  4. Tiny oil droplets stay dispersed: a stable emulsion.
Common mistakeThinking colloids are true solutions. Their larger particles scatter light, unlike dissolved molecules.

In 3 lines: Five types. Charge and steric stability. Tyndall effect.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which everyday substance is a foam?
    • Whipped cream, gas in liquid
    • Milk, liquid in liquid
    • Fog, liquid in gas
    • Ink, solid in liquid
    Show the answer

    Answer: Whipped cream, gas in liquid. A foam has gas bubbles dispersed in a liquid.

  2. Why do river deltas form where rivers meet the sea?
    • Seawater dissolves all clay
    • Salt in seawater coagulates charged clay colloids
    • Rivers flow faster at the coast
    • Clay particles grow larger in fresh water
    Show the answer

    Answer: Salt in seawater coagulates charged clay colloids. Electrolytes in seawater screen the repulsive charges, so clay settles.

  3. How does lecithin stabilise mayonnaise?
    • It dissolves all the oil
    • It makes oil denser than water
    • Its polar head sits in water and non-polar tail in oil, coating droplets
    • It reacts with vinegar
    Show the answer

    Answer: Its polar head sits in water and non-polar tail in oil, coating droplets. Emulsifiers sit at the oil-water interface.

Key terms

Colloid
A dispersion with particles 1 nm to 1 μm in size
Tyndall effect
Scattering of light by colloidal particles
Coagulation
Clumping of colloidal particles
