Lattice Enthalpy

Lattice enthalpy is the enthalpy change for forming one mole of ionic solid from gaseous ions; it is larger for higher charges and smaller ions, and differences from theoretical values reveal covalent character.

Chemistry · Chemical Bonding and Molecular Structure · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define lattice enthalpy
  • Explain how ionic charge and size affect lattice enthalpy
  • Compare theoretical and experimental lattice enthalpies

Definition

  • Lattice enthalpy of formation: enthalpy change when one mole of an ionic solid forms from gaseous ions (exothermic).
  • Lattice dissociation enthalpy: the reverse (endothermic).
  • It cannot be measured directly.
  • Larger magnitude means stronger ionic bonding.

Factors

  • Higher charges give larger lattice enthalpies.
  • Smaller ions give larger lattice enthalpies.
  • MgO (−3,791 kJ mol⁻¹) is much larger than NaCl (−787 kJ mol⁻¹).
  • Lattice enthalpy affects melting point and solubility.

Theoretical values calculated from a perfect ionic model agree well with experimental Born-Haber values for most alkali halides. Large differences, as in silver halides, indicate covalent character caused by polarisation of anions by cations.

Worked example

Comparing NaCl and MgO.

  1. Na⁺ and Cl⁻ have charges of 1+ and 1−.
  2. Mg²⁺ and O²⁻ have charges of 2+ and 2−, and are smaller.
  3. Attraction depends on the product of charges divided by distance.
  4. MgO's lattice enthalpy is nearly five times larger.
Common mistakeConfusing lattice formation and dissociation enthalpies. Formation is exothermic; dissociation is endothermic.

In 3 lines: Gaseous ions to solid. Charge and size. Covalent character from mismatch.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which compound has the largest lattice enthalpy?
    • Sodium chloride
    • Magnesium oxide
    • Potassium iodide
    • Caesium bromide
    Show the answer

    Answer: Magnesium oxide. High charges and small ions give the strongest lattice.

  2. What does a large difference between theoretical and experimental lattice enthalpy indicate?
    • Perfectly ionic bonding
    • A measurement error always
    • Significant covalent character
    • No bonding at all
    Show the answer

    Answer: Significant covalent character. Polarisation adds covalent bonding beyond the ionic model.

  3. Why is the lattice enthalpy of MgO about five times that of NaCl?
    • MgO has larger ions
    • NaCl has higher charges
    • Oxygen is less electronegative than chlorine
    • Doubly charged, smaller ions attract far more strongly
    Show the answer

    Answer: Doubly charged, smaller ions attract far more strongly. Attraction scales with charge product over distance.
