Dynamic Equilibrium

At dynamic equilibrium, forward and reverse rates are equal and concentrations are constant but not necessarily equal; reactions continue at molecular level in a closed system and the same equilibrium can be reached from either direction.

Chemistry · Chemical Equilibrium · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the features of dynamic equilibrium
  • Explain why concentrations stay constant at equilibrium
  • Explain the need for a closed system

Features

  • Forward and reverse reactions occur at equal rates.
  • Concentrations of reactants and products stay constant (not equal).
  • Reactions continue at the molecular level: it is dynamic.
  • Equilibrium requires a closed system.

Reaching equilibrium

  • Initially the forward rate is high and the reverse rate is zero.
  • As products build up, the reverse rate increases.
  • Eventually both rates are equal.
  • Equilibrium can be approached from either direction.

Isotope labelling demonstrates the dynamic nature: adding deuterium to an equilibrium mixture of N₂, H₂ and NH₃ leads to deuterium appearing in ammonia, even though concentrations do not change. Macroscopic properties such as colour and pressure stay constant.

Worked example

Approaching equilibrium.

  1. Hydrogen and iodine are heated in a sealed flask.
  2. The purple colour of iodine fades as HI forms.
  3. The colour stops changing, though reaction continues both ways.
  4. Starting with pure HI gives the same equilibrium mixture.
Common mistakeThinking concentrations are equal at equilibrium. They are constant, not necessarily equal.

In 3 lines: Equal rates. Constant concentrations. Closed system.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is true of concentrations at equilibrium?
    • They are always equal
    • They keep changing
    • They are all zero
    • They remain constant
    Show the answer

    Answer: They remain constant. Equal rates mean no net change in concentration.

  2. How does isotope labelling show equilibrium is dynamic?
    • Labelled atoms spread into all species though concentrations stay constant
    • Labelled atoms stay only in the original reactant molecules
    • It stops the reaction
    • It changes the equilibrium position
    Show the answer

    Answer: Labelled atoms spread into all species though concentrations stay constant. Exchange of labelled atoms shows reactions keep happening both ways.

  3. Why does starting with pure HI give the same equilibrium as starting with H₂ and I₂?
    • HI does not decompose
    • Equilibrium can be approached from either direction
    • Different starts give different mixtures
    • Only forward reactions reach equilibrium
    Show the answer

    Answer: Equilibrium can be approached from either direction. The final position depends on conditions, not the starting side.

Key terms

Dynamic equilibrium
Forward and reverse rates equal with constant concentrations
Closed system
A system where no substances enter or leave
Macroscopic property
A bulk property such as colour or pressure
