Reducing Agents

Reducing agents donate electrons and are oxidised; examples include reactive metals, hydrogen, carbon, CO, iodide and thiosulfate, used in metal extraction, titrations, organic reductions and thermite welding.

Chemistry · Redox and Electrochemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define a reducing agent
  • Describe common reducing agents
  • Explain the use of reducing agents in industry and analysis

Definition

  • A reducing agent donates electrons, so it is itself oxidised.
  • Strong reducing agents have very negative electrode potentials.
  • Examples: reactive metals, H₂, C, CO, I⁻ and S₂O₃²⁻.
  • Group 1 metals are strong reducing agents.

Uses

  • Carbon and CO reduce metal oxides in extraction, as in the blast furnace.
  • Thiosulfate reduces iodine in iodine-thiosulfate titrations.
  • Sodium borohydride reduces carbonyl compounds to alcohols.
  • Iron(II) is used in redox titrations with manganate(VII).

Reactive metals such as aluminium act as reducing agents in the thermite reaction, which produces molten iron for welding railway tracks. Halide ions increase in reducing power down group 17, so iodide reduces sulfuric acid further than chloride does.

Worked example

The thermite reaction.

  1. Aluminium powder is mixed with iron(III) oxide and ignited.
  2. 2Al + Fe₂O₃ → Al₂O₃ + 2Fe.
  3. Aluminium is oxidised and acts as the reducing agent.
  4. Molten iron forms, used to weld rails.
Common mistakeThinking a reducing agent is reduced. It is oxidised while reducing something else.

In 3 lines: Donates electrons, is oxidised. Metals, carbon, thiosulfate. Extraction and analysis.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which acts as a reducing agent in the blast furnace?
    • Calcium carbonate
    • Silicon dioxide
    • Carbon monoxide
    • Nitrogen gas
    Show the answer

    Answer: Carbon monoxide. Carbon monoxide removes oxygen from iron oxide, reducing it to iron.

  2. Why does iodide reduce sulfuric acid further than chloride does?
    • Chloride is a stronger reducing agent
    • Iodide is an oxidising agent
    • Both are equally strong
    • Iodide is a stronger reducing agent
    Show the answer

    Answer: Iodide is a stronger reducing agent. Reducing power of halides increases down the group.

  3. Why is aluminium the reducing agent in thermite?
    • It loses electrons to become Al³⁺ in Al₂O₃
    • It gains electrons from iron
    • It is reduced to aluminium metal
    • It adds oxygen to iron
    Show the answer

    Answer: It loses electrons to become Al³⁺ in Al₂O₃. Aluminium is oxidised while iron ions are reduced.

Key terms

Reducing agent
A species that donates electrons
Thermite reaction
Aluminium reducing iron oxide to molten iron
Iodometry
Titration involving iodine and thiosulfate
