Industrial Equilibrium

The Haber process uses about 450 °C, 200 atm and iron with recycling, and the Contact process about 450 °C, low pressure and V₂O₅; conditions are compromises between yield, rate, cost and safety, and recycling raises overall conversion.

Chemistry · Chemical Equilibrium · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe conditions for the Haber process
  • Describe conditions for the Contact process
  • Explain compromises between yield, rate, cost and safety

Haber process

  • N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃, ΔH = −92 kJ mol⁻¹.
  • Conditions: about 400–450 °C, 200 atm, iron catalyst.
  • Ammonia is removed by cooling and liquefying; unreacted gases are recycled.
  • Single-pass conversion is about 15%, but recycling gives overall about 98%.

Contact process

  • 2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃, ΔH = −196 kJ mol⁻¹.
  • Conditions: about 450 °C, 1–2 atm, V₂O₅ catalyst.
  • High conversion (over 99%) is achieved even at low pressure.
  • Low pressure is used because high pressure would add little and cost more.

The choices balance equilibrium yield, reaction rate, energy costs, equipment costs and safety. Recycling unreacted materials makes even low single-pass yields efficient. Atom economy and energy efficiency are increasingly important.

Worked example

Why recycling matters.

  1. Each pass converts only about 15% of N₂ and H₂ to ammonia.
  2. Ammonia is condensed and removed.
  3. Unreacted gases go back through the reactor.
  4. Over many passes, nearly all reactants are converted.
Common mistakeThinking industry always uses conditions for maximum yield. Conditions are compromises with rate and cost.

In 3 lines: Haber conditions. Contact conditions. Compromise and recycling.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What pressure is typically used in the Haber process?
    • About 1 atm
    • About 2,000 atm
    • About 20 atm
    • About 200 atm
    Show the answer

    Answer: About 200 atm. About 200 atm balances higher yield against equipment cost and safety.

  2. Why is low pressure used in the Contact process?
    • Conversion is already very high, so high pressure adds little
    • High pressure would sharply lower the SO₃ yield
    • SO₃ decomposes under pressure
    • The catalyst needs low pressure
    Show the answer

    Answer: Conversion is already very high, so high pressure adds little. Conversion is already over 99%, so extra pressure would cost more for little gain.

  3. Why is recycling unreacted gases important in the Haber process?
    • It changes the equilibrium constant
    • It raises overall conversion despite low single-pass yield
    • It cools the reactor
    • It removes the catalyst
    Show the answer

    Answer: It raises overall conversion despite low single-pass yield. Repeated passes convert nearly all reactants.

Key terms

Recycling
