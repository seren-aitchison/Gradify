Pauli Exclusion Principle

No two electrons in an atom share all four quantum numbers, so each orbital holds at most two electrons with opposite spins.

Chemistry · Atomic Structure and Quantum Chemistry · about 3 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • State the Pauli exclusion principle
  • Use it to explain why an orbital holds at most two electrons with opposite spins
  • Draw orbital box diagrams that obey it

The principle

Wolfgang Pauli proposed in 1925 that no two electrons in an atom can have the same set of four quantum numbers. Two electrons in the same orbital share n, l and mₗ, so they must differ in spin: one +½ and one −½. A third electron would have to repeat a set, so it can't enter that orbital.

Orbital box diagrams

In box diagrams each orbital is a box and each electron an arrow: up for +½, down for −½. Two electrons in a box must point opposite ways (paired). Nitrogen, 1s² 2s² 2p³, is drawn as a pair in the 1s box, a pair in the 2s box, and one arrow in each of the three 2p boxes.

Why it matters

Without the Pauli principle, every electron would fall into the 1s orbital and all atoms would have similar, tiny sizes and no shell structure, so there would be no periodic table and no chemistry as we know it. The principle applies to all particles of its kind (fermions), and the resistance of electrons to being squeezed into the same state is a large part of why solid matter can't be compressed.

Electrons with opposite spins in an orbital are called paired. Atoms or ions with unpaired electrons are attracted into a magnetic field (paramagnetic), which is how unpaired electrons are detected; oxygen gas, with two unpaired electrons, is drawn into the gap of a strong magnet.

Investigate with the lab. Try putting two electrons with the same spin into one orbital box and see the checker reject it.

Common mistakeDrawing two electrons in the same orbital with the same spin. That would give them identical quantum numbers, which the principle forbids.

In 3 lines: No two electrons in an atom have the same four quantum numbers. An orbital holds at most two electrons, with opposite spins. The principle gives atoms their shell structure; unpaired electrons make substances paramagnetic.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Why can an orbital hold only two electrons?
    • Electrons in one orbital can differ only in spin, which has just two values
    • The orbital is full of protons
    • Electrons repel too strongly for three
    • Three electrons would make the atom an ion
    Show the answer

    Answer: Electrons in one orbital can differ only in spin, which has just two values. With n, l and mₗ shared, only the two spin values distinguish electrons in an orbital.

  2. Which orbital box diagram for the 2p electrons of oxygen breaks the Pauli principle?
    • One box with a pair and two boxes with one electron each
    • Three electrons in one 2p box
    • Arrows in opposite directions in a paired box
    • Four electrons spread over three boxes, two paired
    Show the answer

    Answer: Three electrons in one 2p box. Three electrons in one orbital would force two of them to share all four quantum numbers.

  3. Liquid oxygen is attracted to a magnet but liquid nitrogen isn't. What does this suggest about their electrons?
    • Nitrogen has no electrons
    • Oxygen is a metal
    • Oxygen molecules have unpaired electrons, while all electrons in nitrogen molecules are paired
    • Oxygen atoms break the Pauli principle
    Show the answer

    Answer: Oxygen molecules have unpaired electrons, while all electrons in nitrogen molecules are paired. Unpaired electrons make a substance paramagnetic; O₂ has two unpaired electrons.

  4. What does the Pauli exclusion principle state?
    • Electrons fill the lowest energy level first
    • Electrons fill orbitals singly before pairing
    • Electrons orbit the nucleus in fixed circles
    • No two electrons in an atom have the same four quantum numbers
    Show the answer

    Answer: No two electrons in an atom have the same four quantum numbers. So an orbital holds at most two electrons, with opposite spins.

Key terms

Pauli exclusion principle
No two electrons in an atom can have the same four quantum numbers
Paired electrons
Two electrons in the same orbital with opposite spins
Paramagnetic
Attracted into a magnetic field because of unpaired electrons
