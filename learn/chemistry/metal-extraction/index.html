Metal Extraction

Extraction depends on reactivity: electrolysis for very reactive metals, carbon reduction for moderate ones and metal reduction for titanium; Ellingham diagrams show which reductions are feasible, with carbon becoming more effective at high temperatures.

Chemistry · Inorganic Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Relate extraction method to metal reactivity
  • Describe extraction by carbon reduction and electrolysis
  • Use Ellingham diagrams to explain reduction

Methods

  • Very reactive metals (Na, Al) are extracted by electrolysis of molten compounds.
  • Moderately reactive metals (Fe, Zn) are reduced by carbon or CO.
  • Titanium is reduced from TiCl₄ by magnesium or sodium (Kroll process).
  • Unreactive metals (Au) occur native.

Ellingham diagrams

  • Plot ΔG° of oxide formation against temperature.
  • A metal can reduce the oxide of any metal above it on the diagram.
  • Carbon's line slopes downwards (C → CO increases entropy), so it reduces more oxides at high temperatures.
  • Explains why iron oxide is reduced by carbon in a blast furnace.

Aluminium is extracted by electrolysis of alumina dissolved in molten cryolite, which uses large amounts of electricity. Newer methods such as hydrogen-based reduction of iron ore aim to cut carbon emissions. Bioleaching uses bacteria to extract copper from low-grade ores.

Worked example

The blast furnace.

  1. Coke burns to CO₂, then forms CO at high temperature.
  2. CO reduces iron(III) oxide: Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂.
  3. Limestone removes silica impurities as slag.
  4. Molten iron collects at the bottom.
Common mistakeThinking carbon can reduce any metal oxide. Very reactive metals form oxides too stable for carbon to reduce economically.

In 3 lines: Reactivity decides method. Electrolysis or carbon. Ellingham diagrams.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which metal is extracted by electrolysis?
    • Iron, from haematite
    • Zinc, from zinc oxide
    • Gold, found native
    • Aluminium, from molten alumina
    Show the answer

    Answer: Aluminium, from molten alumina. Aluminium oxide is too stable for carbon to reduce economically.

  2. Why does carbon reduce more oxides at high temperature?
    • Forming CO increases entropy, so its ΔG° falls with temperature
    • Carbon melts at low temperature
    • Carbon becomes a metal when hot
    • All oxides decompose at room temperature
    Show the answer

    Answer: Forming CO increases entropy, so its ΔG° falls with temperature. The downward slope crosses other oxide lines at high temperature.

  3. Why is hydrogen being developed for iron ore reduction?
    • It is cheaper than all other fuels
    • It produces water instead of carbon dioxide
    • It makes iron more reactive
    • It needs no energy at all
    Show the answer

    Answer: It produces water instead of carbon dioxide. Hydrogen reduction could cut steelmaking emissions.

Key terms

Ore
A rock from which a metal can be extracted economically
Ellingham diagram
A plot of ΔG° for oxide formation against temperature
Slag
Molten waste formed from impurities
