Ethers

Ethers, R–O–R′, cannot hydrogen bond to each other, so they are volatile and fairly unreactive solvents; they are made by Williamson synthesis from alkoxides and primary haloalkanes, form peroxides on standing and strained epoxides are reactive.

Chemistry · Functional Group Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the structure and naming of ethers
  • Explain the physical properties of ethers
  • Describe the Williamson ether synthesis

Structure and properties

  • General form R–O–R′, named as alkoxyalkanes, such as methoxyethane.
  • Ethers cannot hydrogen bond to each other, so they boil lower than alcohols.
  • They can accept hydrogen bonds from water, so small ethers are slightly soluble.
  • Generally unreactive, making good solvents.

Synthesis and hazards

  • Williamson synthesis: alkoxide + haloalkane → ether by SN2.
  • Works best with methyl or primary haloalkanes.
  • Ethers form explosive peroxides on standing in air.
  • Epoxides are three-membered cyclic ethers, reactive because of ring strain.

Diethyl ether was once used as a general anaesthetic. Today ethers such as tetrahydrofuran (THF) are common solvents, especially for Grignard reactions. Epoxides are important intermediates, opening easily with nucleophiles.

Worked example

Making methoxyethane.

  1. Sodium reacts with ethanol to form sodium ethoxide.
  2. Ethoxide attacks iodomethane by SN2.
  3. Iodide leaves.
  4. Methoxyethane, CH₃OCH₂CH₃, forms.
Common mistakeUsing a tertiary haloalkane in a Williamson synthesis. Elimination wins instead of substitution.

In 3 lines: R–O–R′. Williamson synthesis. Solvents and epoxides.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which reagents make an ether in the Williamson synthesis?
    • An alcohol and an alkene
    • An alkoxide and a primary haloalkane
    • A ketone and an alcohol
    • Two carboxylic acids
    Show the answer

    Answer: An alkoxide and a primary haloalkane. The alkoxide ion displaces halide from the haloalkane by an SN2 reaction.

  2. Why do ethers boil lower than isomeric alcohols?
    • Ethers have higher molecular masses
    • Ethers are ionic
    • Ether molecules cannot hydrogen bond to each other
    • Alcohols have no hydrogen bonding
    Show the answer

    Answer: Ether molecules cannot hydrogen bond to each other. Without O–H bonds, only weaker forces act between ether molecules.

  3. Why are epoxides much more reactive than other ethers?
    • Epoxides contain no oxygen atom at all
    • Epoxides are aromatic and highly stabilised
    • Epoxides form very strong hydrogen bonds
    • Ring strain in the three-membered ring is released on opening
    Show the answer

    Answer: Ring strain in the three-membered ring is released on opening. Bond angles near 60° make the ring easy to open.

Key terms

Ether
A compound with an R–O–R′ linkage
Williamson synthesis
Making ethers from alkoxides and haloalkanes
Epoxide
A three-membered cyclic ether
