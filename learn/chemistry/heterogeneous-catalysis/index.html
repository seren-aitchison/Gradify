Heterogeneous Catalysis

Heterogeneous catalysts are in a different phase, working by adsorption at active sites, bond weakening, reaction and desorption; adsorption strength must be moderate, and high surface area supports reduce costs, as in the Haber process and catalytic converters.

Chemistry · Chemical Kinetics · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define heterogeneous catalysis
  • Describe the adsorption mechanism on catalyst surfaces
  • Describe industrial examples and how catalysts are made efficient

Mechanism

  • The catalyst is in a different phase, usually a solid with gaseous or liquid reactants.
  • Adsorption: reactants bond to active sites on the surface.
  • Bonds in reactants weaken, and new bonds form.
  • Desorption: products leave the surface.

Industrial use

  • Iron in the Haber process.
  • Vanadium(V) oxide in the Contact process.
  • Platinum, palladium and rhodium in catalytic converters.
  • Catalysts are spread on supports with high surface area, such as honeycombs.

Adsorption must be strong enough to weaken bonds but weak enough for products to leave: tungsten adsorbs too strongly and silver too weakly, while nickel and platinum are just right. Using thin coatings on ceramic supports reduces the amount of expensive metal needed.

Worked example

A catalytic converter.

  1. Exhaust gases pass over a honeycomb coated with platinum and rhodium.
  2. CO and NO adsorb onto the metal surface.
  3. They react: 2CO + 2NO → 2CO₂ + N₂.
  4. The products desorb and leave in the exhaust.
Common mistakeThinking stronger adsorption always makes a better catalyst. Too strong adsorption stops products leaving.

In 3 lines: Different phase. Adsorb, react, desorb. Supports and industry.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which metals are used in catalytic converters?
    • Sodium, potassium and lithium
    • Platinum, palladium and rhodium
    • Iron, copper and zinc only
    • Lead, tin and mercury
    Show the answer

    Answer: Platinum, palladium and rhodium. Precious metals catalyse removal of CO, NO and hydrocarbons.

  2. Why are catalysts spread on high surface area supports?
    • Supports increase activation energy
    • Supports react with the gases
    • More active sites are available while using less metal
    • It makes catalysts heavier
    Show the answer

    Answer: More active sites are available while using less metal. Thin coatings on supports maximise the number of exposed metal atoms.

  3. Why is silver a poor catalyst for many reactions?
    • It adsorbs too strongly
    • It is too reactive
    • It melts easily
    • It adsorbs reactants too weakly to weaken their bonds
    Show the answer

    Answer: It adsorbs reactants too weakly to weaken their bonds. Weak adsorption fails to activate the reactants.

Key terms

Heterogeneous catalyst
A catalyst in a different phase from reactants
Adsorption
Bonding of molecules to a surface
Active site
A surface location where reaction occurs
