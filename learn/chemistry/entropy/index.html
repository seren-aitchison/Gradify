Entropy

Entropy measures energy and particle dispersal in J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹, increasing from solids to liquids to gases and with temperature; ΔS⦵ = ΣS⦵(products) − ΣS⦵(reactants), and is positive when gas molecules increase.

Chemistry · Chemical Energetics and Thermodynamics · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define entropy in terms of disorder and energy dispersal
  • Predict the sign of entropy changes
  • Calculate entropy changes from standard entropies

Entropy

  • Entropy (S) measures how energy and particles are spread out or dispersed.
  • Units: J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹.
  • Gases have higher entropy than liquids, which have higher entropy than solids.
  • Entropy increases with temperature.

Entropy changes

  • ΔS is positive when disorder increases: melting, boiling, dissolving, more gas molecules.
  • ΔS⦵ = ΣS⦵(products) − ΣS⦵(reactants).
  • Standard entropies are always positive (except at absolute zero for perfect crystals).
  • Unlike ΔfH⦵, elements have non-zero standard entropies.

The number of ways particles and energy can be arranged increases as substances become more disordered, which is the statistical meaning of entropy described by Boltzmann. Reactions producing more moles of gas usually have positive entropy changes.

Worked example

Decomposing calcium carbonate.

  1. CaCO₃(s) → CaO(s) + CO₂(g).
  2. S⦵: CaCO₃ 93, CaO 40, CO₂ 214 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹.
  3. ΔS⦵ = (40 + 214) − 93 = +161 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹.
  4. A gas forms, so entropy increases.
Common mistakeThinking elements have zero standard entropy. Only enthalpy of formation is zero for elements.

In 3 lines: Dispersal of energy. Gas > liquid > solid. Products minus reactants.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which process has a positive entropy change?
    • Water freezing
    • Steam condensing
    • Ice melting
    • A gas dissolving in cold water
    Show the answer

    Answer: Ice melting. Melting increases the disorder of particles.

  2. Why does a reaction producing more gas molecules usually have positive ΔS?
    • Gases have lower entropy than solids
    • Fewer molecules always increase entropy
    • Entropy ignores gas molecules
    • Gases are far more disordered than solids or liquids
    Show the answer

    Answer: Gases are far more disordered than solids or liquids. More gas means more ways to arrange energy and particles.

  3. Why do elements have non-zero standard entropies?
    • Their particles still have energy spread among many arrangements
    • Entropy is defined relative to elements
    • Elements are always gases
    • Only compounds have entropy
    Show the answer

    Answer: Their particles still have energy spread among many arrangements. Entropy is absolute, measured from zero at 0 K.

Key terms

Entropy
A measure of energy and particle dispersal
Standard entropy
Entropy of one mole under standard conditions
Disorder
A qualitative idea of how spread out particles are
