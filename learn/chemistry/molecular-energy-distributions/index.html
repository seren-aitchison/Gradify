Molecular Energy Distributions

The Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution describes the range of molecular speeds and energies; higher temperature flattens and shifts it, heavier molecules move slower and the fraction above the activation energy rises steeply with temperature, explaining rate changes.

Chemistry · Physical Chemistry and Molecular Science · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution of molecular speeds
  • Explain how temperature and mass affect the distribution
  • Relate the distribution to reaction rates

The distribution

  • Molecules have a range of speeds, not one speed.
  • The curve starts at zero, rises to a peak and has a long tail.
  • Most probable speed < mean speed < root-mean-square speed.
  • Area under the curve equals the total number of molecules.

Effects

  • Higher temperature: curve flattens and shifts to higher speeds.
  • Heavier molecules: lower speeds at the same temperature.
  • The fraction with energy above Eₐ rises sharply with temperature.
  • This explains the strong temperature dependence of rates.

The mean speed of N₂ molecules at room temperature is about 470 m s⁻¹. Light gases such as hydrogen and helium escape from Earth's atmosphere because a significant fraction of molecules exceed escape velocity.

Worked example

Effect of a 10 °C rise.

  1. At 300 K, a small fraction of molecules exceed Eₐ.
  2. At 310 K, the curve shifts slightly right.
  3. The area beyond Eₐ can roughly double.
  4. The reaction rate roughly doubles.
Common mistakeThinking all molecules in a gas move at the same speed. There is a broad distribution.

In 3 lines: Range of speeds. Temperature and mass. Links to rate.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. How does molecular mass affect speed at the same temperature?
    • Heavier molecules move more slowly on average
    • Heavier molecules move faster
    • Mass has no effect on speed
    • All gases have identical speed distributions
    Show the answer

    Answer: Heavier molecules move more slowly on average. Equal average kinetic energy means lower speed for larger mass.

  2. Why does a small temperature rise greatly increase reaction rate?
    • All molecules gain exactly Eₐ
    • The fraction of molecules above Eₐ increases sharply
    • The activation energy falls to zero
    • The number of molecules doubles
    Show the answer

    Answer: The fraction of molecules above Eₐ increases sharply. The high-energy tail grows rapidly with temperature.

  3. Why does hydrogen slowly escape from Earth's atmosphere?
    • Hydrogen molecules are heavier than nitrogen
    • Gravity does not act on hydrogen molecules
    • Its light molecules have a tail of speeds exceeding escape velocity
    • Hydrogen molecules do not move in the air
    Show the answer

    Answer: Its light molecules have a tail of speeds exceeding escape velocity. Light molecules move fastest at a given temperature.

Key terms

Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution
Distribution of molecular speeds or energies in a gas
Most probable speed
The speed at the peak of the distribution
Root-mean-square speed
Square root of the mean of squared speeds
