Second Law of Thermodynamics

The second law states that total entropy increases in spontaneous processes; ΔS(surroundings) = −ΔH ÷ T, and ΔG < 0 is equivalent to ΔS(total) > 0, so local order can increase only if the surroundings gain more entropy.

Chemistry · Chemical Energetics and Thermodynamics · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • State the second law of thermodynamics
  • Calculate total entropy change including surroundings
  • Explain the link between the second law and ΔG

The second law

  • In a spontaneous process, the total entropy of the universe increases: ΔS(total) > 0.
  • ΔS(total) = ΔS(system) + ΔS(surroundings).
  • ΔS(surroundings) = −ΔH ÷ T.
  • Heat flows spontaneously from hot to cold.

Link to ΔG

  • ΔS(total) = ΔS(system) − ΔH ÷ T.
  • Multiplying by −T gives −TΔS(total) = ΔH − TΔS = ΔG.
  • So ΔG < 0 is equivalent to ΔS(total) > 0.
  • Exothermic reactions increase the entropy of the surroundings.

Order can increase locally, as in living cells or freezing water, but only if the surroundings' entropy increases more. No heat engine can convert all heat into work, which limits the efficiency of power stations.

Worked example

Water freezing at −10 °C.

  1. ΔS(system) = −22 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹; ΔH = −6,010 J mol⁻¹; T = 263 K.
  2. ΔS(surroundings) = 6,010 ÷ 263 = +22.9 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹.
  3. ΔS(total) = −22 + 22.9 = +0.9 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹.
  4. Total entropy increases, so freezing is spontaneous at −10 °C.
Common mistakeThinking entropy can never decrease anywhere. It can decrease in a system if the surroundings gain more.

In 3 lines: Total entropy rises. ΔS(surr) = −ΔH ÷ T. Equivalent to ΔG < 0.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. In which direction does heat flow spontaneously?
    • From cold to hot
    • From hot to cold
    • Equally both ways
    • It never flows
    Show the answer

    Answer: From hot to cold. Heat flowing from hot to cold increases the total entropy of the universe.

  2. How can living cells become more ordered without breaking the second law?
    • They destroy entropy
    • The second law does not apply to living things
    • They release heat, increasing the entropy of their surroundings
    • They absorb entropy from food
    Show the answer

    Answer: They release heat, increasing the entropy of their surroundings. Total entropy still rises when surroundings gain more.

  3. Why is ΔG < 0 equivalent to ΔS(total) > 0?
    • ΔG ignores entropy entirely
    • They measure unrelated quantities
    • ΔS(total) is always negative
    • ΔG equals −TΔS(total) at constant T and p
    Show the answer

    Answer: ΔG equals −TΔS(total) at constant T and p. Rearranging the entropy terms gives ΔG directly.
