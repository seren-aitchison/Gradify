Brønsted-Lowry Theory

Brønsted-Lowry acids are proton donors and bases proton acceptors; conjugate pairs differ by one proton, stronger acids have weaker conjugate bases and water is amphoteric, acting as acid or base.

Chemistry · Acids, Bases and Aqueous Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • State the Brønsted-Lowry definitions
  • Identify conjugate acid-base pairs
  • Explain why water is amphoteric

Definitions

  • A Brønsted-Lowry acid is a proton donor.
  • A Brønsted-Lowry base is a proton acceptor.
  • Acid-base reactions involve proton transfer.
  • Works in any solvent, not just water.

Conjugate pairs

  • When an acid donates a proton, it becomes its conjugate base.
  • When a base accepts a proton, it becomes its conjugate acid.
  • Example: HCl + H₂O → Cl⁻ + H₃O⁺ (HCl/Cl⁻ and H₃O⁺/H₂O are conjugate pairs).
  • Water is amphoteric: it can act as an acid or a base.

Ammonia is a base because it accepts a proton from water: NH₃ + H₂O ⇌ NH₄⁺ + OH⁻. The stronger an acid, the weaker its conjugate base. Proposed independently by Brønsted and Lowry in 1923.

Worked example

Identifying pairs.

  1. CH₃COOH + H₂O ⇌ CH₃COO⁻ + H₃O⁺.
  2. CH₃COOH donates a proton: acid; CH₃COO⁻ is its conjugate base.
  3. H₂O accepts a proton: base; H₃O⁺ is its conjugate acid.
  4. Each pair differs by one H⁺.
Common mistakeThinking a conjugate base must contain OH⁻. It is simply the species left after the acid loses a proton.

In 3 lines: Proton donors and acceptors. Conjugate pairs. Water is amphoteric.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is the conjugate acid of NH₃?
    • NH₂⁻
    • N₂
    • H₂O
    • NH₄⁺
    Show the answer

    Answer: NH₄⁺. Adding one proton to ammonia gives the ammonium ion.

  2. Why is water described as amphoteric?
    • It can donate or accept a proton
    • It never reacts with acids
    • It only accepts protons
    • It only donates electrons
    Show the answer

    Answer: It can donate or accept a proton. Water acts as base with HCl and acid with NH₃.

  3. Why does a strong acid have a weak conjugate base?
    • Strong acids have no conjugate base
    • The conjugate base has little tendency to regain a proton
    • The conjugate base is always strong
    • Acid strength and conjugate base are unrelated
    Show the answer

    Answer: The conjugate base has little tendency to regain a proton. Readily giving up a proton means weak attraction to regain it.

Key terms

Proton donor
A Brønsted-Lowry acid
Conjugate base
The species formed when an acid loses a proton
Amphoteric
Able to act as acid or base
