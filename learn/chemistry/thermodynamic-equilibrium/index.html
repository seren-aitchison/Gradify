Thermodynamic Equilibrium

At equilibrium ΔG = 0 and Gibbs energy is minimised; ΔG⦵ = −RT ln K links standard free energy to the equilibrium constant, so negative ΔG⦵ gives K > 1 and positive ΔG⦵ gives K < 1.

Chemistry · Chemical Energetics and Thermodynamics · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain why ΔG = 0 at equilibrium
  • Use ΔG⦵ = −RT ln K
  • Relate the size of K to the sign of ΔG⦵

Equilibrium

  • At equilibrium, forward and reverse rates are equal and ΔG = 0.
  • Gibbs free energy is at a minimum at equilibrium.
  • Systems move towards the composition with lowest G.
  • Phase changes at their transition temperatures are equilibria, such as ice and water at 0 °C.

ΔG⦵ and K

  • ΔG⦵ = −RT ln K.
  • ΔG⦵ < 0 gives K > 1: products favoured.
  • ΔG⦵ > 0 gives K < 1: reactants favoured.
  • ΔG⦵ = 0 gives K = 1.

ΔG⦵ refers to standard conditions, while ΔG for a real mixture depends on its composition. Even reactions with positive ΔG⦵ form some products, because K is never exactly zero. This relationship allows K to be predicted from thermodynamic tables.

Worked example

Calculating K.

  1. A reaction has ΔG⦵ = −10.0 kJ mol⁻¹ at 298 K.
  2. ln K = −ΔG⦵ ÷ RT = 10,000 ÷ (8.314 × 298) = 4.04.
  3. K = e⁴·⁰⁴ ≈ 57.
  4. Products are favoured.
Common mistakeThinking a positive ΔG⦵ means no products form. It means K is less than 1, so some products still form.

In 3 lines: ΔG = 0 at equilibrium. ΔG⦵ = −RT ln K. Sign tells K size.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. If ΔG⦵ is negative, what can be said about K?
    • K is less than 1
    • K equals zero
    • K is negative
    • K is greater than 1
    Show the answer

    Answer: K is greater than 1. Negative ΔG⦵ makes ln K positive, so K must be greater than 1.

  2. Why do reactions with positive ΔG⦵ still produce some products?
    • K is small but never exactly zero
    • They produce no products at all
    • ΔG⦵ is always negative
    • Products form only at 0 K
    Show the answer

    Answer: K is small but never exactly zero. Some products exist at equilibrium even when K < 1.

  3. Why is Gibbs free energy at a minimum at equilibrium?
    • G is at a maximum at equilibrium
    • Any change from equilibrium would increase G
    • Equilibrium has no energy
    • Reactions stop completely
    Show the answer

    Answer: Any change from equilibrium would increase G. Systems move downhill in G until no further decrease is possible.

Key terms

Equilibrium constant
A ratio of product to reactant concentrations at equilibrium
ΔG⦵
Standard Gibbs free energy change
Minimum free energy
The composition at which G is lowest
