Galvanic Cells

Galvanic cells combine half-cells, using platinum for gas or ion/ion systems, measured with high-resistance voltmeters; cell notation lists anode on the left and cathode on the right separated by ‖, and electrons flow from the more reactive metal.

Chemistry · Redox and Electrochemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the construction of a galvanic cell
  • Write conventional cell notation
  • Predict the direction of electron flow

Construction

  • Each half-cell has an electrode in a solution of its ions, such as Zn in Zn²⁺.
  • Gas half-cells use an inert platinum electrode, such as H₂/H⁺.
  • Ion/ion half-cells also use platinum, such as Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺.
  • A high-resistance voltmeter measures the cell potential.

Notation and flow

  • Cell notation: anode ∣ anode solution ‖ cathode solution ∣ cathode.
  • Example: Zn(s) ∣ Zn²⁺(aq) ‖ Cu²⁺(aq) ∣ Cu(s).
  • ‖ represents the salt bridge; ∣ separates phases.
  • Electrons flow from the more reactive metal (anode) to the less reactive (cathode).

The Daniell cell, invented in 1836, uses zinc and copper half-cells and produces about 1.10 V. A high-resistance voltmeter is used so that almost no current flows, giving the maximum potential difference. Platinum is chosen because it is unreactive and conducts.

Worked example

Writing notation.

  1. A cell has a magnesium half-cell and a silver half-cell.
  2. Magnesium is more reactive, so it is the anode.
  3. Notation: Mg(s) ∣ Mg²⁺(aq) ‖ Ag⁺(aq) ∣ Ag(s).
  4. Electrons flow from Mg to Ag through the wire.
Common mistakeWriting the cathode on the left in cell notation. Convention places the anode (oxidation) on the left.

In 3 lines: Two half-cells. Anode ‖ cathode. Electrons from reactive metal.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. In Zn ∣ Zn²⁺ ‖ Cu²⁺ ∣ Cu, which electrode is the anode?
    • Zinc
    • Copper
    • The salt bridge
    • Both electrodes
    Show the answer

    Answer: Zinc. The anode is written on the left of the notation.

  2. Why is a high-resistance voltmeter used?
    • To increase the current
    • So almost no current flows, giving the maximum potential
    • To speed up the reaction
    • To stop electrons flowing completely
    Show the answer

    Answer: So almost no current flows, giving the maximum potential. Drawing current would lower the measured voltage.

  3. Which direction do electrons flow in a Mg-Ag cell?
    • From silver to magnesium
    • Through the salt bridge
    • From magnesium to silver through the wire
    • They do not flow
    Show the answer

    Answer: From magnesium to silver through the wire. The more reactive metal, magnesium, loses electrons, which flow to silver.

Key terms

Half-cell

Cell notation
A shorthand representation of a cell
Inert electrode
An unreactive electrode such as platinum
