Ionic Bonding

Ionic bonds form by electron transfer from metals to non-metals, giving ions held in giant lattices by strong attraction, so ionic compounds have high melting points, conduct when molten or dissolved and are brittle.

Chemistry · Chemical Bonding and Molecular Structure · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the formation of ions by electron transfer
  • Describe the structure of ionic lattices
  • Explain the properties of ionic compounds

Formation

  • Metals lose electrons to form cations; non-metals gain electrons to form anions.
  • Oppositely charged ions attract strongly in all directions.
  • Dot-and-cross diagrams show electron transfer.
  • Formulae balance charges, such as MgCl₂ and Al₂O₃.

Lattices and properties

  • Ions form a giant ionic lattice, such as NaCl with each ion surrounded by six of the opposite charge.
  • High melting points: many strong bonds must be broken.
  • Conduct electricity when molten or dissolved, as ions can move, but not when solid.
  • Often soluble in water and brittle.

Ionic compounds are brittle because shifting layers brings like charges together, which then repel. Stronger ionic bonding occurs with smaller ions and higher charges, so MgO melts at about 2,850 °C while NaCl melts at 801 °C.

Worked example

Forming magnesium oxide.

  1. Magnesium (2,8,2) loses two electrons to form Mg²⁺ (2,8).
  2. Oxygen (2,6) gains two electrons to form O²⁻ (2,8).
  3. The ions attract to form MgO.
  4. The 2+ and 2− charges give very strong ionic bonding.
Common mistakeThinking ionic compounds conduct when solid. Ions are fixed in the solid lattice and cannot move.

In 3 lines: Electron transfer forms ions. Giant lattices. High melting, conducts when molten.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is the formula of aluminium oxide?
    • AlO
    • Al₃O₂
    • AlO₃
    • Al₂O₃
    Show the answer

    Answer: Al₂O₃. Two Al³⁺ (+6) balance three O²⁻ (−6) to give a neutral compound.

  2. Why is magnesium oxide's melting point much higher than sodium chloride's?
    • Its ions have higher charges, giving stronger attraction
    • It contains more atoms in each formula unit
    • It is a covalent compound
    • It contains more atoms per formula
    Show the answer

    Answer: Its ions have higher charges, giving stronger attraction. Doubly charged ions attract each other far more strongly than singly charged ions.

  3. Why are ionic crystals brittle?
    • Ions are only held together by weak forces
    • Shifting layers brings like charges together, which repel
    • Ionic bonds are directional
    • Electrons flow between layers
    Show the answer

    Answer: Shifting layers brings like charges together, which repel. A small displacement aligns like charges and splits the crystal.

Key terms

Cation
A positively charged ion
Anion
A negatively charged ion
Ionic lattice
A regular 3D arrangement of ions
