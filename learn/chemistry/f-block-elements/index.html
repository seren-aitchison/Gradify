F-block Elements

The f-block fills 4f and 5f orbitals; lanthanides mostly form +3 ions, have very similar chemistry and show the lanthanide contraction, while all actinides are radioactive; uses include magnets, phosphors and nuclear fuel.

Chemistry · Periodic Table and Periodicity · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the position and configurations of the f-block
  • Explain the lanthanide contraction
  • Describe uses and radioactivity of f-block elements

Structure

  • f-block elements fill 4f (lanthanides) or 5f (actinides) orbitals.
  • They are usually shown below the main table to keep it compact.
  • Lanthanides commonly form +3 ions.
  • Their chemical properties are very similar, making them hard to separate.

Properties and uses

  • Lanthanide contraction: atomic and ionic radii decrease across the lanthanides because 4f electrons shield poorly.
  • Lanthanides are used in strong magnets (neodymium), phosphors and catalysts.
  • All actinides are radioactive.
  • Uranium and plutonium are used in nuclear power.

Many elements beyond uranium are made artificially in nuclear reactors or accelerators. Rare earth elements are vital for electric motors and wind turbines, raising concerns about supply. The lanthanide contraction makes period 6 transition metals similar in size to period 5 ones.

Worked example

Neodymium magnets.

  1. Neodymium, iron and boron form powerful permanent magnets.
  2. They are used in wind turbines, headphones and electric car motors.
  3. Neodymium is a lanthanide.
  4. Demand for these magnets has grown with green technology.
Common mistakeThinking "rare earth" elements are very scarce. Many are fairly abundant but hard to extract and separate.

In 3 lines: 4f and 5f filling. Similar chemistry and contraction. Magnets and nuclear fuel.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which orbitals do lanthanides fill?
    • 3d orbitals
    • 4f orbitals
    • 5f orbitals
    • 2p orbitals
    Show the answer

    Answer: 4f orbitals. Lanthanides are the series that fills the 4f subshell.

  2. Why are lanthanides hard to separate?
    • They are all gases at room temperature
    • They are very radioactive
    • Their chemical properties are very similar
    • They do not form ions
    Show the answer

    Answer: Their chemical properties are very similar. Similar +3 ions and sizes make separation difficult.

  3. What causes the lanthanide contraction?
    • Lanthanides lose protons
    • 4f electrons are very far from the nucleus
    • Atoms gain extra shells
    • 4f electrons shield the nuclear charge poorly
    Show the answer

    Answer: 4f electrons shield the nuclear charge poorly. Rising nuclear charge pulls electrons closer when shielding is weak.

Key terms

Lanthanide
An element filling 4f orbitals
Actinide
An element filling 5f orbitals
Lanthanide contraction
Decrease in radius across the lanthanides
