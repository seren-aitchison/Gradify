Radical Intermediates

Carbon radicals have an unpaired electron, are roughly planar and are stabilised by alkyl groups and resonance; they react in chain reactions, bromination is more selective than chlorination, and radicals matter in polymerisation, ozone depletion and biology.

Chemistry · Organic Reaction Mechanisms · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the structure and stability of carbon radicals
  • Explain radical chain reactions
  • Describe the importance of radicals in chemistry and biology

Structure and stability

  • A carbon radical has three bonds and one unpaired electron.
  • Roughly planar, with the electron in a p orbital.
  • Stability: tertiary > secondary > primary > methyl.
  • Resonance stabilises allylic and benzylic radicals.

Reactions

  • Chain reactions: initiation, propagation, termination.
  • Selectivity: bromine radicals are more selective than chlorine radicals.
  • Radical polymerisation makes poly(ethene) and poly(styrene).
  • Radical inhibitors such as antioxidants stop chains.

Radicals are highly reactive and usually short-lived. Bromination of alkanes favours tertiary positions much more than chlorination, because the bromine step is slower and more selective. Ozone depletion by chlorine radicals and damage to cells by reactive oxygen species are radical processes.

Worked example

Selectivity of bromination.

  1. 2-methylpropane has primary and tertiary hydrogens.
  2. Chlorine radicals attack both fairly indiscriminately.
  3. Bromine radicals strongly prefer the tertiary hydrogen.
  4. Bromination gives mainly 2-bromo-2-methylpropane.
Common mistakeThinking all radicals react equally unselectively. Bromine radicals are much more selective than chlorine radicals.

In 3 lines: Unpaired electron. Tertiary most stable. Chains and selectivity.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which radical is the most stable?
    • A primary radical
    • A tertiary radical
    • A methyl radical
    • A secondary radical
    Show the answer

    Answer: A tertiary radical. Alkyl groups stabilise the unpaired electron.

  2. Why do antioxidants protect materials and cells?
    • They produce more radicals to speed reactions
    • They absorb all oxygen in the air
    • They react with radicals to stop chain reactions
    • They remove all electrons from molecules
    Show the answer

    Answer: They react with radicals to stop chain reactions. Removing radicals ends the propagation steps.

  3. Why is bromination more selective than chlorination?
    • Bromine radicals are far more reactive
    • Chlorine cannot form radicals
    • Bromine reacts only with methane
    • The hydrogen-abstraction step by bromine is slower and more sensitive to radical stability
    Show the answer

    Answer: The hydrogen-abstraction step by bromine is slower and more sensitive to radical stability. A later transition state reflects product radical stability more.

Key terms

Radical
A species with an unpaired electron
Chain reaction
A reaction in which radicals are regenerated
Antioxidant
A substance that stops radical chain reactions
