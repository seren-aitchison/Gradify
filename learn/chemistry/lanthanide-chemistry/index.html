Lanthanide Chemistry

Lanthanides fill the buried 4f subshell, mostly form +3 ions with ionic, high-coordination complexes and show the lanthanide contraction, which makes them hard to separate, and they are used in magnets, phosphors and MRI agents.

Chemistry · Inorganic Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the electronic structure and oxidation states of lanthanides
  • Explain the lanthanide contraction
  • Describe uses of lanthanides

Properties

  • Lanthanum to lutetium fill the 4f subshell.
  • The common oxidation state is +3; Ce⁴⁺ and Eu²⁺ also occur.
  • 4f orbitals are buried inside, so ligand effects are small.
  • Complexes are mainly ionic with high coordination numbers (8–12).

Lanthanide contraction

  • Ionic radii decrease steadily from La³⁺ to Lu³⁺.
  • 4f electrons shield the rising nuclear charge poorly.
  • Makes lanthanides hard to separate because they are so similar.
  • Makes 5d metals similar in size to 4d metals, as with Zr and Hf.

Lanthanides show sharp, characteristic emission lines because 4f orbitals are shielded. Europium gives red light in screens and euro banknote security features, while neodymium makes strong permanent magnets for motors and wind turbines. Gadolinium complexes enhance MRI images.

Worked example

Why Zr and Hf are so alike.

  1. Hafnium follows the lanthanides in period 6.
  2. The lanthanide contraction offsets the extra shell.
  3. Zr⁴⁺ and Hf⁴⁺ have almost identical radii.
  4. Their chemistry is so similar that they are hard to separate.
Common mistakeThinking lanthanides behave like d-block metals. Their buried 4f orbitals interact little with ligands.

In 3 lines: Fill 4f; mostly +3. Lanthanide contraction. Magnets and phosphors.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which subshell is filled across the lanthanides?
    • 3d
    • 5d
    • 4f
    • 6p
    Show the answer

    Answer: 4f. The lanthanides fill the seven 4f orbitals.

  2. Why are lanthanides hard to separate from each other?
    • They are all gases
    • They never form compounds
    • They have very different chemistry
    • Their ions are very similar in size and charge
    Show the answer

    Answer: Their ions are very similar in size and charge. The contraction is gradual, so neighbours are nearly identical.

  3. Why do lanthanide emission lines appear sharp?
    • The 4f orbitals are shielded, so ligands barely affect their energies
    • Lanthanide ions have no electrons in their f orbitals
    • The lanthanide ions are always present as gases
    • Ligands split the 4f orbitals very strongly indeed
    Show the answer

    Answer: The 4f orbitals are shielded, so ligands barely affect their energies. Transitions within shielded 4f orbitals give narrow emission lines.

Key terms

Rare earth elements
