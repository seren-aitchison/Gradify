Enolates and Enols

α-hydrogens next to carbonyls are acidic, giving enols by tautomerism and enolates with base; enolates are nucleophiles at the α-carbon that undergo aldol reactions, alkylation and halogenation, forming C–C bonds.

Chemistry · Functional Group Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe keto-enol tautomerism
  • Explain how enolates form and react
  • Describe the aldol reaction

Enols and enolates

  • The carbon next to C=O is the α-carbon; its hydrogens are α-hydrogens.
  • Keto-enol tautomerism: the keto form usually dominates.
  • A base removes an α-hydrogen, forming a resonance-stabilised enolate.
  • Enolates are nucleophilic at the α-carbon.

Reactions

  • Aldol reaction: an enolate adds to another carbonyl, giving a β-hydroxy carbonyl.
  • Heating gives the aldol condensation product, an α,β-unsaturated carbonyl.
  • Alkylation: enolates react with haloalkanes.
  • Halogenation at the α-carbon, as in the iodoform reaction.

The aldol reaction is one of the most important ways to form C–C bonds, used in industry and in living cells, for example in glycolysis by the enzyme aldolase. β-diketones have unusually high enol content because the enol is stabilised by hydrogen bonding and conjugation.

Worked example

Aldol reaction of ethanal.

  1. Hydroxide removes an α-hydrogen from ethanal.
  2. The enolate attacks another ethanal's carbonyl carbon.
  3. Protonation gives 3-hydroxybutanal.
  4. Heating eliminates water, giving but-2-enal.
Common mistakeThinking the enol form usually dominates. The keto form is normally far more stable.

In 3 lines: α-hydrogens acidic. Enolate nucleophiles. Aldol C–C bonds.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which form of propanone dominates at equilibrium?
    • The keto form
    • The enol form
    • Equal amounts of both
    • The enolate ion
    Show the answer

    Answer: The keto form. The C=O bond is stronger than the C=C of the enol.

  2. Why are α-hydrogens more acidic than ordinary C–H hydrogens?
    • α-carbons carry a positive charge
    • The enolate formed is stabilised by resonance onto oxygen
    • α-hydrogens are bonded to oxygen
    • Carbonyl groups donate electrons to carbon
    Show the answer

    Answer: The enolate formed is stabilised by resonance onto oxygen. Delocalisation stabilises the conjugate base.

  3. Why do β-diketones have high enol content?
    • β-diketones have no α-hydrogens
    • The keto form cannot exist
    • The enol is stabilised by intramolecular hydrogen bonding and conjugation
    • Enols are always more stable
    Show the answer

    Answer: The enol is stabilised by intramolecular hydrogen bonding and conjugation. Extra stabilisation shifts the tautomer equilibrium.

Key terms

α-carbon
The carbon next to a carbonyl group
Enol
A tautomer with C=C–OH
Aldol reaction
Enolate addition to a carbonyl forming a β-hydroxy carbonyl
