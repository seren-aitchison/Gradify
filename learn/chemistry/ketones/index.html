Ketones

Ketones have C=O between two carbons, are named with -one, are made by oxidising secondary alcohols, resist mild oxidation, are reduced to secondary alcohols, add nucleophiles more slowly than aldehydes and methyl ketones give the iodoform test.

Chemistry · Functional Group Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the structure and naming of ketones
  • Describe the preparation of ketones
  • Compare the reactions of ketones and aldehydes

Structure and preparation

  • The C=O is bonded to two carbon groups.
  • Named with -one: propanone, butanone, pentan-3-one.
  • Made by oxidising secondary alcohols.
  • Also made by Friedel-Crafts acylation of arenes.

Reactions

  • Not oxidised by mild oxidants: no Tollens' or Fehling's reaction.
  • Reduced by NaBH₄ to secondary alcohols.
  • Undergo nucleophilic addition more slowly than aldehydes.
  • Give an orange precipitate with 2,4-DNPH.

Propanone (acetone) is a common solvent used in nail varnish remover and in industry. Methyl ketones give a yellow precipitate of triiodomethane (iodoform) with iodine in alkali. Ketone bodies form in the body during fasting or uncontrolled diabetes.

Worked example

The iodoform test.

  1. Butanone is warmed with iodine in sodium hydroxide.
  2. A pale yellow precipitate of CHI₃ forms, with an antiseptic smell.
  3. This shows a CH₃CO– group.
  4. Pentan-3-one gives no precipitate.
Common mistakeThinking all ketones give the iodoform test. Only those with a CH₃CO– group do.

In 3 lines: C=O in the chain. Resist mild oxidation. Iodoform for methyl ketones.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which alcohol is oxidised to a ketone?
    • A primary alcohol such as propan-1-ol
    • A tertiary alcohol such as 2-methylpropan-2-ol
    • Methanol, the simplest alcohol
    • A secondary alcohol such as propan-2-ol
    Show the answer

    Answer: A secondary alcohol such as propan-2-ol. Secondary carbinol carbons have one H to lose.

  2. Why do ketones not give a silver mirror with Tollens' reagent?
    • They have no hydrogen on the carbonyl carbon to oxidise
    • They are too reactive with silver ions
    • They reduce silver to a black solid
    • They contain no carbonyl group
    Show the answer

    Answer: They have no hydrogen on the carbonyl carbon to oxidise. Mild oxidants need the aldehyde C–H bond, which ketones do not have.

  3. Why does pentan-3-one give a negative iodoform test?
    • It contains no carbonyl group at all
    • It has ethyl groups on both sides of C=O, with no CH₃CO– group
    • It is actually an aldehyde, not a ketone
    • It is far too volatile to react with iodine
    Show the answer

    Answer: It has ethyl groups on both sides of C=O, with no CH₃CO– group. The test needs a methyl directly attached to C=O.

Key terms

Ketone
A compound with C=O between two carbon groups
Iodoform test
Yellow CHI₃ precipitate showing a CH₃CO– group
Ketone bodies
Ketones formed in metabolism during fasting
