Fluorescence Spectroscopy

Fluorescence occurs when molecules absorb light, lose some energy as heat and emit longer-wavelength light; it is highly sensitive, measured at 90° to the beam, can be quenched and is used in biology, diagnostics and trace analysis.

Chemistry · Analytical Chemistry and Spectroscopy · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain absorption and emission in fluorescence
  • Explain why emitted light has a longer wavelength
  • Describe applications of fluorescence

Fluorescence

  • A molecule absorbs light and is excited to a higher electronic state.
  • Some energy is lost as heat through vibrations.
  • It returns to the ground state by emitting light.
  • Emission is at longer wavelength than absorption (Stokes shift).

Analytical use

  • Emission intensity is proportional to concentration at low levels.
  • Fluorescence is very sensitive, detecting tiny amounts.
  • Measured at 90° to the excitation beam to avoid transmitted light.
  • Quenching by other species reduces emission.

Fluorescent tags such as green fluorescent protein (GFP) let scientists track molecules in living cells, earning the 2008 Nobel Prize. Fluorescence is used in DNA sequencing, medical diagnostics and detecting trace pollutants. Phosphorescence is a slower emission that continues after the light source is removed.

Worked example

Measuring quinine in tonic water.

  1. Quinine absorbs UV light at 350 nm.
  2. It emits blue light at about 450 nm.
  3. Emission intensity is measured at 90°.
  4. A calibration curve gives quinine concentration.
Common mistakeThinking emitted light has the same wavelength as absorbed light. Energy lost first makes it longer.

In 3 lines: Absorb, relax, emit. Longer wavelength out. Very sensitive.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Why is emitted fluorescent light at a longer wavelength?
    • The molecule gains energy before emitting
    • Some energy is lost as heat before emission
    • Light always speeds up on emission
    • Emission involves nuclear spin flips
    Show the answer

    Answer: Some energy is lost as heat before emission. Losing some energy first means lower-energy, longer-wavelength emission.

  2. Why is fluorescence more sensitive than absorbance methods?
    • It measures much larger concentrations
    • It needs no light source
    • Emitted light is measured against a dark background
    • Absorbance cannot detect any compounds
    Show the answer

    Answer: Emitted light is measured against a dark background. A small signal against darkness is easier to detect.

  3. Why did GFP transform cell biology?
    • It kills cells for easier study
    • It replaces all microscopes
    • It only works in dead tissue
    • It lets proteins be tracked in living cells by their glow
    Show the answer

    Answer: It lets proteins be tracked in living cells by their glow. Genetically fused tags show where proteins go.

Key terms

Fluorescence
Emission of light shortly after absorbing light
Stokes shift
The difference between absorption and emission wavelengths
Quenching
Reduction of fluorescence by other species
