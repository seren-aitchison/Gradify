Group 18

Noble gases have full outer shells, are monatomic and very unreactive, with uses as inert atmospheres, in lighting and cooling; heavier noble gases such as xenon form compounds with fluorine, first shown by Bartlett in 1962.

Chemistry · Periodic Table and Periodicity · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain the low reactivity of the noble gases
  • Describe uses of the noble gases
  • Describe the discovery of noble gas compounds

Properties

  • Helium, neon, argon, krypton, xenon and radon.
  • Full outer shells: helium 1s², others ns² np⁶.
  • Exist as single atoms (monatomic).
  • Very high ionisation energies and low boiling points.

Uses and compounds

  • Helium: balloons and cooling MRI magnets.
  • Neon: advertising signs; argon: inert atmosphere for welding and in light bulbs.
  • In 1962 Neil Bartlett made the first noble gas compound, XePtF₆.
  • Xenon forms fluorides such as XeF₂ and XeF₄.

Larger noble gases form compounds because their outer electrons are further from the nucleus and easier to remove. Radon is radioactive and can build up in homes in some areas of the UK, increasing lung cancer risk. Helium is a finite resource extracted from natural gas.

Worked example

Argon in welding.

  1. Molten metal reacts with oxygen and nitrogen in air.
  2. Welders surround the weld with argon gas.
  3. Argon does not react with the hot metal.
  4. This produces a stronger, cleaner weld.
Common mistakeThinking noble gases can never form compounds. Xenon and krypton form compounds with very electronegative elements.

In 3 lines: Full outer shells. Inert uses. Xenon compounds exist.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which noble gas is used to cool MRI magnets?
    • Helium
    • Neon
    • Argon
    • Radon
    Show the answer

    Answer: Helium. Liquid helium is cold enough to keep MRI magnets superconducting.

  2. Why can xenon form compounds while helium cannot?
    • Helium has an incomplete shell
    • Xenon's outer electrons are further from the nucleus and easier to remove
    • Xenon is a metal
    • Helium is radioactive
    Show the answer

    Answer: Xenon's outer electrons are further from the nucleus and easier to remove. Larger atoms have lower ionisation energies.

  3. Why is radon a health concern in some UK homes?
    • It is highly flammable
    • It reacts with oxygen to make poisons
    • It is radioactive and can accumulate indoors
    • It removes oxygen from air entirely
    Show the answer

    Answer: It is radioactive and can accumulate indoors. Breathing radioactive radon increases lung cancer risk.

Key terms

Noble gas
A group 18 element with a full outer shell
Monatomic
Existing as single atoms
Inert atmosphere
A surrounding gas that does not react
