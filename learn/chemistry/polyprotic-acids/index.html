Polyprotic Acids

Polyprotic acids donate protons stepwise with decreasing Ka values because each proton leaves a more negative ion; their titration curves show several equivalence points and buffer regions, and phosphate buffers near pH 7.2 are important in biology.

Chemistry · Acids, Bases and Aqueous Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define polyprotic acids
  • Explain why successive Ka values decrease
  • Interpret titration curves of polyprotic acids

Stepwise ionisation

  • Diprotic acids donate two protons, such as H₂SO₄ and H₂CO₃.
  • Triprotic acids donate three, such as H₃PO₄.
  • Each step has its own Ka: Ka1 > Ka2 > Ka3.
  • Each successive proton is harder to remove from a more negative ion.

Titration curves

  • Polyprotic acids show more than one equivalence point.
  • Each step can act as a buffer region.
  • pH is often determined mainly by the first ionisation.
  • Phosphate buffers use H₂PO₄⁻/HPO₄²⁻ near pH 7.2.

For phosphoric acid, Ka1 ≈ 7.5 × 10⁻³, Ka2 ≈ 6.2 × 10⁻⁸ and Ka3 ≈ 4.8 × 10⁻¹³. Sulfuric acid's first ionisation is strong but its second is weak. Phosphate buffers are common in biology because pKa2 is close to physiological pH.

Worked example

Phosphoric acid steps.

  1. H₃PO₄ ⇌ H⁺ + H₂PO₄⁻ (pKa1 ≈ 2.1).
  2. H₂PO₄⁻ ⇌ H⁺ + HPO₄²⁻ (pKa2 ≈ 7.2).
  3. HPO₄²⁻ ⇌ H⁺ + PO₄³⁻ (pKa3 ≈ 12.3).
  4. Each step is much weaker than the last.
Common mistakeThinking all protons of a polyprotic acid ionise equally. Each successive ionisation is much weaker.

In 3 lines: Stepwise ionisation. Ka1 > Ka2 > Ka3. Several equivalence points.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which acid is triprotic?
    • Hydrochloric acid, HCl
    • Nitric acid, HNO₃
    • Phosphoric acid, H₃PO₄
    • Ethanoic acid
    Show the answer

    Answer: Phosphoric acid, H₃PO₄. H₃PO₄ can lose three protons, one in each successive step.

  2. Why is Ka2 smaller than Ka1?
    • The second proton is closer to oxygen
    • Ka values always increase
    • The first proton is held more tightly
    • Removing a proton from a negative ion is harder
    Show the answer

    Answer: Removing a proton from a negative ion is harder. Increasing negative charge attracts protons more strongly.

  3. Why are phosphate buffers widely used in biology?
    • pKa2 is close to physiological pH
    • They are strong acids
    • They have no pKa values
    • They only work at pH 2
    Show the answer

    Answer: pKa2 is close to physiological pH. Buffers work best near their pKa, and pKa2 of phosphate is about 7.2.

Key terms

Polyprotic acid
An acid that can donate more than one proton
Diprotic
Able to donate two protons
Successive Ka
Separate dissociation constants for each step
