Elements and Compounds

A compound has a fixed composition and new properties because its elements are chemically bonded.

Chemistry · Foundations of Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain why a compound's properties differ from its elements'
  • State the law of definite proportions
  • Describe how compounds can be broken down

Elements

An element is a substance made of atoms with the same number of protons (the same atomic number). Elements can't be broken down into simpler substances by chemical means. About 90 occur naturally; the rest have been made in laboratories.

Compounds

In a compound, atoms of different elements are held together by chemical bonds in a fixed ratio. Sodium is a reactive metal that fizzes in water and chlorine is a toxic green gas, but sodium chloride is a stable white solid that we eat. New bonds give new properties.

Fixed composition

The law of definite proportions (Proust, 1797) states that a compound always contains the same elements in the same proportions by mass, however it is made. Water is always 11.2% hydrogen and 88.8% oxygen by mass. This is evidence that compounds are made of atoms combined in fixed whole-number ratios.

Worked example

Calculate the percentage by mass of oxygen in water, H₂O (H = 1.008, O = 15.999).

  1. Mass of one mole of water = 2 × 1.008 + 15.999 = 18.015 g.
  2. % oxygen = 15.999 ÷ 18.015 × 100 = 88.8%.

Breaking compounds down

Compounds can be decomposed by chemical reactions, for example by heating (thermal decomposition of calcium carbonate to calcium oxide and carbon dioxide) or by electrolysis (water to hydrogen and oxygen).

Common mistakeThinking a compound keeps the properties of its elements, like a mixture does. Sodium chloride isn't reactive or toxic like its elements.

In 2 lines: Elements can't be split chemically; compounds can. Compounds have fixed compositions and new properties.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which can't be broken down into simpler substances by chemical reactions?
    • A compound
    • A mixture
    • A solution
    • An element
    Show the answer

    Answer: An element. Elements are the simplest substances chemically; compounds can be decomposed into elements.

  2. Two samples of copper(II) oxide, made in different ways, both contain 79.9% copper by mass. This supports…
    • the law of definite proportions
    • the idea that compounds are mixtures
    • the existence of isotopes
    • the law of conservation of energy
    Show the answer

    Answer: the law of definite proportions. A fixed mass composition regardless of preparation is exactly what the law predicts.

  3. Why is sodium chloride safe to eat when sodium and chlorine are both dangerous?
    • Sodium and chlorine stay as free atoms in the crystal, but in such small amounts that they are too dilute to be dangerous
    • In the compound, sodium and chloride are ions held in a lattice and no longer have the reactivity of the free elements
    • Cooking and dissolving in water turn the sodium and chlorine into harmless forms before the salt is eaten by anyone
    • Sodium chloride is a mixture of the two elements, and mixing the two dangerous elements cancels out their reactivity
    Show the answer

    Answer: In the compound, sodium and chloride are ions held in a lattice and no longer have the reactivity of the free elements. Chemical bonding creates a new substance with new properties; Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions don't behave like sodium metal or chlorine gas.

Key terms

Element
A substance consisting of atoms that all have the same atomic number
Compound
A substance of two or more elements chemically bonded in a fixed ratio
Law of definite proportions
A compound always contains its elements in the same proportions by mass
