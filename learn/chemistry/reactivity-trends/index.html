Reactivity Trends

Metal reactivity increases down groups and decreases across periods, while non-metal reactivity shows the opposite pattern; period 3 oxides change from basic ionic to amphoteric to acidic covalent.

Chemistry · Periodic Table and Periodicity · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain the trend in reactivity of metals
  • Explain the trend in reactivity of non-metals
  • Describe the trend in reactions of period 3 elements with oxygen and water

Metals and non-metals

  • Metals react by losing electrons; reactivity increases down groups and decreases across periods.
  • Non-metals react by gaining electrons; reactivity decreases down groups and increases across periods.
  • The most reactive metals are bottom left; the most reactive non-metals top right (excluding noble gases).
  • Reactivity is linked to ionisation energy and electron affinity.

Period 3

  • Sodium reacts vigorously with water; magnesium slowly; aluminium is protected by oxide.
  • Oxides change from basic (Na₂O, MgO) through amphoteric (Al₂O₃) to acidic (SiO₂, P₄O₁₀, SO₂).
  • Bonding in oxides changes from ionic to covalent across the period.
  • Chlorides change from ionic solids to covalent liquids or gases.

The reactivity series of metals ranks them by how easily they lose electrons, and predicts displacement reactions. Chemical behaviour across period 3 shows how bonding type shifts with electronegativity.

Worked example

Period 3 oxides with water.

  1. Na₂O dissolves to give NaOH, pH about 14.
  2. MgO gives a slightly alkaline solution, pH about 9.
  3. SO₂ dissolves to give an acidic solution, pH about 3.
  4. The change follows the shift from ionic to covalent oxides.
Common mistakeThinking all elements become more reactive down a group. Non-metals become less reactive down a group.

In 3 lines: Metals: down and left. Non-metals: up and right. Period 3 oxides change.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which period 3 oxide is acidic?
    • Sodium oxide
    • Magnesium oxide
    • Aluminium oxide
    • Sulfur dioxide
    Show the answer

    Answer: Sulfur dioxide. Non-metal oxides such as SO₂ dissolve to form acidic solutions.

  2. Why do non-metals become less reactive down a group?
    • Atoms gain electrons less easily as they get larger
    • They lose electrons more easily
    • Nuclear charge decreases
    • They become noble gases
    Show the answer

    Answer: Atoms gain electrons less easily as they get larger. Weaker attraction for incoming electrons lowers reactivity.

  3. Why does oxide character change across period 3?
    • All period 3 oxides are ionic
    • Bonding shifts from ionic to covalent as electronegativity rises
    • Nuclear charge falls across the period
    • Oxygen changes across the period
    Show the answer

    Answer: Bonding shifts from ionic to covalent as electronegativity rises. Covalent non-metal oxides form acids with water.

Key terms

Basic oxide
An oxide that reacts with acids
Acidic oxide
An oxide that reacts with bases
Reactivity series
A ranking of metals by reactivity
