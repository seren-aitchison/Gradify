Modern Periodic Law

Moseley's X-ray measurements showed elements have whole-number atomic numbers, and the modern periodic law orders elements by atomic number, which resolves mass-order anomalies because atomic number determines electron structure.

Chemistry · Periodic Table and Periodicity · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • State the modern periodic law
  • Explain how Moseley determined atomic numbers
  • Explain why atomic number resolves anomalies in mass order

Moseley's work

  • In 1913 Henry Moseley measured X-ray frequencies emitted by elements.
  • The square root of the frequency increased steadily with position in the table.
  • This showed each element has a whole-number atomic number, equal to its number of protons.
  • It revealed gaps for undiscovered elements, such as 43 and 61.

The periodic law

  • Modern periodic law: properties of elements are periodic functions of their atomic number.
  • Ordering by atomic number puts tellurium (52) before iodine (53) naturally.
  • Similarly, argon (18) comes before potassium (19), despite argon's greater mass.
  • Atomic number determines electron arrangement, which determines chemistry.

Moseley's discovery gave a physical basis for the periodic table. Mass order fails in a few places because isotope proportions affect average atomic mass. Moseley was killed at Gallipoli in 1915, aged 27.

Worked example

Resolving argon and potassium.

  1. Argon has relative atomic mass 39.9 and potassium 39.1.
  2. By mass, potassium would come before argon.
  3. But argon is a noble gas and potassium a reactive metal.
  4. Atomic numbers 18 and 19 place them correctly.
Common mistakeThinking the table is ordered by mass. It is ordered by atomic number, the number of protons.

In 3 lines: Moseley measured X-rays. Ordered by atomic number. Anomalies resolved.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. How is the modern periodic table ordered?
    • By atomic mass
    • By date of discovery
    • By atomic number
    • By density
    Show the answer

    Answer: By atomic number. Atomic number counts protons and defines each element.

  2. Why does argon come before potassium in the table?
    • Argon has a smaller atomic mass
    • Potassium is a noble gas
    • Argon was discovered first
    • Argon has atomic number 18 and potassium 19
    Show the answer

    Answer: Argon has atomic number 18 and potassium 19. Atomic number, not mass, sets the order in the modern table.

  3. Why is mass order occasionally wrong?
    • Average atomic mass depends on isotope proportions
    • Atomic mass is always identical to atomic number
    • Protons have no mass
    • Elements change mass over time
    Show the answer

    Answer: Average atomic mass depends on isotope proportions. Isotope mixtures can make a lighter element follow a heavier one.

Key terms

Atomic number
The number of protons in an atom's nucleus
Periodic law
Properties repeat periodically with atomic number
X-ray spectrum
The X-ray frequencies emitted by an element
