Rate Equations

Rate equations have the form rate = k[A]ᵐ[B]ⁿ, where orders are found experimentally and overall order is their sum; k is the rate constant, and the equation reflects the mechanism rather than the balanced equation.

Chemistry · Chemical Kinetics · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Write a rate equation
  • Explain the meaning of orders and the rate constant
  • Explain why orders must be found experimentally

The rate equation

  • Rate = k[A]ᵐ[B]ⁿ.
  • k is the rate constant.
  • m and n are the orders with respect to A and B.
  • The overall order is m + n.

Key points

  • Orders are usually 0, 1 or 2.
  • Orders are found by experiment, not from the balanced equation.
  • Species not in the rate equation do not affect the rate (zero order).
  • The rate equation reflects the mechanism, especially the slow step.

For 2NO + O₂ → 2NO₂, the rate equation is rate = k[NO]²[O₂], which happens to match the coefficients, but this is not true in general. For example, the reaction of propanone with iodine is zero order in iodine.

Worked example

Using a rate equation.

  1. Rate = k[A]²[B].
  2. If [A] doubles, rate increases by 2² = 4 times.
  3. If [B] doubles, rate doubles.
  4. If both double, rate increases 8 times.
Common mistakeTaking orders from the balanced equation coefficients. Orders must be determined by experiment.

In 3 lines: Rate = k[A]ᵐ[B]ⁿ. Orders by experiment. Linked to mechanism.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What does k represent in a rate equation?
    • The concentration of a catalyst
    • The equilibrium constant
    • The activation energy
    • The rate constant
    Show the answer

    Answer: The rate constant. k links rate to concentrations at a given temperature.

  2. Why can't orders be taken from the balanced equation?
    • The rate depends on the mechanism, not the overall stoichiometry
    • Balanced equations are always wrong
    • Orders are always equal to one
    • Coefficients only apply to products
    Show the answer

    Answer: The rate depends on the mechanism, not the overall stoichiometry. Only steps up to the slow step affect rate.

  3. What does a zero-order dependence on a reactant tell you?
    • It is a catalyst that is used up
    • It is not involved in the rate-determining step or earlier
    • It is the only reactant in the slow step
    • Its concentration controls the rate
    Show the answer

    Answer: It is not involved in the rate-determining step or earlier. Changing its concentration does not change the rate.

Key terms

Rate constant

Order of reaction
The power of a concentration in the rate equation
