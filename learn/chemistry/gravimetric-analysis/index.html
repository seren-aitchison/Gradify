Gravimetric Analysis

Gravimetric analysis precipitates the analyte as a pure compound that is filtered, washed, dried to constant mass and weighed, or measures mass loss on heating; it is accurate and needs no calibration but is slow.

Chemistry · Analytical Chemistry and Spectroscopy · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe precipitation gravimetry
  • Describe volatilisation gravimetry
  • Calculate amounts from gravimetric data

Precipitation gravimetry

  • The analyte is precipitated as a pure, insoluble compound.
  • The precipitate is filtered, washed, dried and weighed.
  • Example: sulfate as BaSO₄; chloride as AgCl.
  • Heating to constant mass ensures all water is removed.

Volatilisation gravimetry

  • The sample is heated and the mass loss is measured.
  • Example: water of crystallisation in hydrated salts.
  • Or the released gas is absorbed and weighed.
  • Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) records mass against temperature.

Gravimetric analysis is one of the most accurate classical methods and needs no calibration, because mass is measured directly. It was used to determine many atomic masses. It is slow, so it is used mainly for reference measurements.

Worked example

Finding sulfate in a fertiliser.

  1. 0.500 g of fertiliser is dissolved and excess BaCl₂ added.
  2. BaSO₄ precipitate is filtered, washed and dried: 0.583 g.
  3. Moles BaSO₄ = 0.583 ÷ 233.4 = 2.50 × 10⁻³ mol.
  4. Sulfate mass = 2.50 × 10⁻³ × 96.1 = 0.240 g, which is 48.0% of the sample.
Common mistakeWeighing the precipitate before it is fully dry. Heating to constant mass is essential.

In 3 lines: Precipitate and weigh. Mass loss on heating. Accurate, slow.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which measurement is central to gravimetric analysis?
    • The volume of a titrant
    • The absorbance of a solution
    • The mass of a pure product or mass lost
    • The retention time of a peak
    Show the answer

    Answer: The mass of a pure product or mass lost. Gravimetry relies on accurately weighing a pure product or mass lost.

  2. Why must the precipitate be washed?
    • To dissolve the precipitate
    • To add more analyte
    • To make it react faster
    • To remove soluble impurities that would add extra mass
    Show the answer

    Answer: To remove soluble impurities that would add extra mass. Impurities would give a falsely high mass.

  3. Why does gravimetric analysis not need calibration?
    • Mass is measured directly and related to amount by stoichiometry
    • It relies on an internal standard to correct results
    • Gravimetry actually measures absorbance of the sample
    • It has no relationship to reaction stoichiometry at all
    Show the answer

    Answer: Mass is measured directly and related to amount by stoichiometry. Weighing gives an absolute measurement linked to amount by stoichiometry.

Key terms

Gravimetric analysis
Analysis based on measuring mass
Constant mass
Mass that no longer changes on further heating
Thermogravimetric analysis
Recording mass against temperature
