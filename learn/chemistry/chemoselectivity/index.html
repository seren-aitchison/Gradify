Chemoselectivity

Chemoselectivity comes from choosing reagents that react with only the most reactive group or from protecting groups such as acetals, silyl ethers and carbamates that temporarily mask a group before being removed.

Chemistry · Organic Reaction Mechanisms · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain how reagent choice gives chemoselectivity
  • Describe the use of protecting groups
  • Plan chemoselective steps in synthesis

Reagent control

  • Mild reagents react with the most reactive group only.
  • NaBH₄ reduces aldehydes and ketones but not esters or acids.
  • Hydrogen with Lindlar's catalyst reduces alkynes but not alkenes.
  • Reactivity order of carbonyls: acyl chlorides > aldehydes > ketones > esters > amides.

Protecting groups

  • A protecting group temporarily masks a reactive group.
  • Ketones and aldehydes are protected as acetals.
  • Alcohols are protected as silyl ethers; amines as carbamates.
  • The group is removed after the other step is complete.

To reduce an ester with LiAlH₄ without touching a ketone in the same molecule, the ketone is first turned into an acetal, which LiAlH₄ ignores. After reduction, dilute acid removes the acetal. Protecting groups add steps, so chemists prefer selective reagents when available.

Worked example

Using a protecting group.

  1. A molecule has a ketone and an ester.
  2. The ketone is protected as an acetal with ethane-1,2-diol.
  3. LiAlH₄ reduces the ester to an alcohol.
  4. Dilute acid removes the acetal, restoring the ketone.
Common mistakeThinking protecting groups are always needed. A selective reagent is better when one exists.

In 3 lines: Choose mild reagents. Protect, react, deprotect. Fewer steps is better.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which carbonyl compound is most reactive towards nucleophiles?
    • An acyl chloride
    • An amide
    • An ester
    • A carboxylate ion
    Show the answer

    Answer: An acyl chloride. Chloride is a good leaving group and strongly withdraws electrons.

  2. Why are protecting groups avoided when possible?
    • They always destroy the molecule
    • They add extra steps, lowering overall yield
    • They make every reaction faster
    • They cannot be removed
    Show the answer

    Answer: They add extra steps, lowering overall yield. Each extra step costs time, material and yield.

  3. Why does LiAlH₄ leave an acetal untouched?
    • Acetals are more reactive than esters
    • LiAlH₄ only reduces acetals
    • Acetals have no electrophilic carbonyl carbon to attack
    • Acetals are acids that destroy LiAlH₄
    Show the answer

    Answer: Acetals have no electrophilic carbonyl carbon to attack. Without C=O, hydride has no site to add to.

Key terms

Chemoselectivity
Reaction of one functional group in preference to others
Protecting group
A temporary group that masks reactivity
Acetal
A protected form of an aldehyde or ketone
