Metallic Bonding

Metallic bonding is the attraction between positive ions and delocalised electrons, explaining conductivity, malleability and ductility; it strengthens with more delocalised electrons and smaller ions, and alloys are harder because layers slide less easily.

Chemistry · Chemical Bonding and Molecular Structure · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the model of metallic bonding
  • Explain the properties of metals using the model
  • Explain factors affecting the strength of metallic bonding

The model

  • Metal atoms lose outer electrons to form a lattice of positive ions.
  • The electrons are delocalised across the whole structure.
  • Bonding is the attraction between the ions and the delocalised electrons.
  • The bonding is non-directional.

Properties and strength

  • Electrical conductivity: delocalised electrons move through the structure.
  • Thermal conductivity: electrons transfer energy.
  • Malleable and ductile: layers of ions slide without breaking bonding.
  • Strength increases with more delocalised electrons and smaller ions, so Mg melts higher than Na.

Alloys are harder than pure metals because different-sized atoms disrupt the layers, making sliding harder. Metallic bonding also explains the shiny lustre of metals, as delocalised electrons interact with light.

Worked example

Sodium versus magnesium.

  1. Sodium contributes one electron per atom; magnesium contributes two.
  2. Mg²⁺ ions are smaller and more highly charged than Na⁺.
  3. Magnesium has stronger metallic bonding.
  4. Magnesium melts at 650 °C; sodium at 98 °C.
Common mistakeThinking metals conduct because ions move. Delocalised electrons carry the current.

In 3 lines: Ions in a sea of electrons. Conductivity and malleability. Strength depends on charge and size.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What carries electric current in a metal?
    • Delocalised electrons
    • Positive metal ions
    • Protons in the nucleus
    • Neutrons in the lattice
    Show the answer

    Answer: Delocalised electrons. Free electrons move through the structure when a voltage is applied.

  2. Why are metals malleable?
    • Metal atoms are held together by weak forces
    • Layers of ions slide without breaking the metallic bonding
    • Metals have ionic bonds
    • Electrons are fixed between atoms
    Show the answer

    Answer: Layers of ions slide without breaking the metallic bonding. Non-directional bonding allows layers to move.

  3. Why does magnesium have stronger metallic bonding than sodium?
    • It has fewer delocalised electrons per atom
    • Its ions are larger
    • It contributes more electrons and has smaller, more charged ions
    • It is a non-metal
    Show the answer

    Answer: It contributes more electrons and has smaller, more charged ions. More electrons and greater charge increase the attraction.

Key terms

Delocalised electrons
Electrons free to move throughout a structure
Malleable
Able to be hammered into shape
Alloy
A mixture of a metal with other elements
