Equilibrium Constants

The equilibrium constant K = [products]^coefficients ÷ [reactants]^coefficients uses equilibrium concentrations and omits pure solids and liquids; large K favours products, small K reactants, and K changes only with temperature.

Chemistry · Chemical Equilibrium · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Write the expression for an equilibrium constant
  • Interpret the magnitude of an equilibrium constant
  • Explain what affects the value of an equilibrium constant

The equilibrium law

  • For aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD, K = [C]ᶜ[D]ᵈ ÷ [A]ᵃ[B]ᵇ.
  • Products on top, reactants below, each raised to its coefficient.
  • Concentrations are equilibrium values.
  • Pure solids and liquids are left out.

Magnitude and changes

  • K ≫ 1: products favoured at equilibrium.
  • K ≪ 1: reactants favoured.
  • K changes only with temperature.
  • Concentration, pressure and catalysts do not change K.

Reversing a reaction gives K' = 1/K, and doubling the equation squares K. K values can range from tiny to enormous; for example, the formation of water from hydrogen and oxygen has an extremely large K at 298 K. Unlike rate constants, K tells us how far, not how fast.

Worked example

Writing K.

  1. For N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃:
  2. K = [NH₃]² ÷ ([N₂][H₂]³).
  3. Each concentration is raised to its coefficient.
  4. Products are on top, reactants below.
Common mistakeThinking adding more reactant changes K. Only temperature changes K; the position shifts but K stays the same.

In 3 lines: Products over reactants. Size shows extent. Only temperature changes K.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which substances are left out of equilibrium expressions?
    • Gases at high pressure
    • Aqueous ions
    • Pure solids and liquids
    • All products
    Show the answer

    Answer: Pure solids and liquids. Their concentrations are effectively constant.

  2. How does K for a reversed reaction relate to the original K?
    • It is the same value
    • It is the negative of K
    • It is K squared
    • It is the reciprocal, 1/K
    Show the answer

    Answer: It is the reciprocal, 1/K. Swapping products and reactants inverts the ratio.

  3. Why does adding a catalyst not change K?
    • It speeds forward and reverse reactions equally
    • It increases only the forward reaction
    • It is used up in the reaction
    • It changes the temperature
    Show the answer

    Answer: It speeds forward and reverse reactions equally. Equal acceleration leaves the equilibrium ratio unchanged.

Key terms

Equilibrium constant
A ratio of equilibrium concentrations raised to their coefficients
Equilibrium law
The expression defining K
Position of equilibrium
The relative amounts of reactants and products
