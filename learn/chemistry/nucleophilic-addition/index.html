Nucleophilic Addition

Nucleophiles attack the δ+ carbonyl carbon, forming an alkoxide that is protonated to an alcohol; aldehydes are more reactive than ketones, and attack on either face of the planar carbonyl gives racemic products.

Chemistry · Organic Reaction Mechanisms · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Draw the mechanism of nucleophilic addition to carbonyls
  • Compare the reactivity of aldehydes and ketones
  • Explain the stereochemistry of addition to carbonyls

Mechanism

  • A nucleophile attacks the δ+ carbonyl carbon.
  • The π electrons move onto oxygen, forming an alkoxide.
  • The alkoxide is protonated to give an alcohol.
  • Examples: HCN gives hydroxynitriles; NaBH₄ gives alcohols.

Reactivity and stereochemistry

  • Aldehydes are more reactive than ketones.
  • Ketones have two alkyl groups that donate electrons and crowd the carbon.
  • The planar carbonyl can be attacked from either face.
  • Unsymmetrical carbonyls give racemic products.

Adding HCN to ethanal makes 2-hydroxypropanenitrile, extending the carbon chain. In teaching labs KCN with acid is used rather than HCN gas, for safety; either way the cyanide ion is the nucleophile, and the alkoxide is then protonated by HCN or by H⁺ from the acid. Grignard reagents add to carbonyls to make larger alcohols.

Worked example

HCN with propanal.

  1. CN⁻ attacks the carbonyl carbon from above or below.
  2. An alkoxide intermediate forms.
  3. H⁺ protonates the oxygen.
  4. A racemic hydroxynitrile forms.
Common mistakeThinking nucleophilic addition to ethanal gives one enantiomer. Equal attack from both faces gives a racemate.

In 3 lines: Attack, then protonate. Aldehydes beat ketones. Racemic products.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is formed when HCN adds to ethanal?
    • Ethanoic acid
    • Propanal
    • 2-hydroxypropanenitrile
    • Ethanol
    Show the answer

    Answer: 2-hydroxypropanenitrile. Cyanide adds a carbon and the oxygen becomes –OH.

  2. Why are ketones less reactive than aldehydes towards nucleophiles?
    • Ketones contain no carbonyl group at all
    • Aldehydes carry two alkyl groups on C=O
    • Ketone carbonyl carbons are more strongly δ+
    • Two alkyl groups donate electrons and crowd the carbonyl carbon
    Show the answer

    Answer: Two alkyl groups donate electrons and crowd the carbonyl carbon. Both electronic and steric effects slow attack on ketones.

  3. Why does adding HCN to propanal give a racemic mixture?
    • The planar carbonyl is attacked equally from both faces
    • Cyanide attacks only from one face
    • Propanal is already a chiral molecule
    • The product has no stereocentre at all
    Show the answer

    Answer: The planar carbonyl is attacked equally from both faces. Equal attack forms equal amounts of each enantiomer.

Key terms

Nucleophilic addition
Addition initiated by a nucleophile
Alkoxide
An anion RO⁻
Hydroxynitrile
A molecule with –OH and –CN on the same carbon
