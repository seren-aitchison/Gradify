Air Pollution

Major air pollutants include particulates, NOₓ, SO₂, CO and VOCs; SO₂ and NOₓ cause acid rain, NOₓ and VOCs form photochemical smog and catalytic converters, desulfurisation and clean fuels reduce emissions.

Chemistry · Materials, Environmental and Industrial Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Identify major air pollutants and their sources
  • Explain the formation of photochemical smog and acid rain
  • Describe methods for reducing air pollution

Pollutants

  • Particulate matter (PM2.5, PM10): from combustion and dust; harms lungs.
  • Nitrogen oxides (NOₓ): from high-temperature engines.
  • Sulfur dioxide: from burning sulfur-containing fuels.
  • Carbon monoxide and volatile organic compounds (VOCs).

Effects and controls

  • Acid rain: SO₂ and NOₓ form sulfuric and nitric acids.
  • Photochemical smog: NOₓ and VOCs react in sunlight, forming ozone.
  • Catalytic converters reduce CO, NOₓ and hydrocarbons.
  • Flue gas desulfurisation removes SO₂ with lime.

The WHO estimates that air pollution causes millions of premature deaths each year. Clean air laws, catalytic converters and low-sulfur fuels have greatly reduced acid rain and lead pollution in many countries. Urban particulate pollution remains a major concern.

Worked example

A catalytic converter.

  1. Exhaust passes over platinum, palladium and rhodium.
  2. 2CO + O₂ → 2CO₂.
  3. 2NO + 2CO → N₂ + 2CO₂.
  4. Unburnt hydrocarbons are oxidised to CO₂ and water.
Common mistakeThinking catalytic converters remove all harmful emissions. They do not remove CO₂ or most particulates.

In 3 lines: Particulates, NOₓ, SO₂. Acid rain and smog. Converters and scrubbers.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which pollutant is produced mainly by burning sulfur-containing fuels?
    • Nitrogen monoxide
    • Ozone
    • Sulfur dioxide
    • Methane
    Show the answer

    Answer: Sulfur dioxide. Sulfur in coal and oil burns to form sulfur dioxide gas.

  2. Why does photochemical smog form mostly on sunny days?
    • Cold weather produces more ozone than heat
    • Rain is what creates photochemical smog
    • Smog forms only at night, away from sunlight
    • Sunlight drives reactions of NOₓ and VOCs that form ozone
    Show the answer

    Answer: Sunlight drives reactions of NOₓ and VOCs that form ozone. UV light splits NO₂, starting the chain of reactions that forms ozone.

  3. Why does flue gas desulfurisation use calcium compounds?
    • Basic CaCO₃ or CaO reacts with acidic SO₂, forming calcium sulfite or sulfate
    • Calcium oxidises SO₂ to sulfur
    • It turns SO₂ into a fuel
    • It releases more SO₂
    Show the answer

    Answer: Basic CaCO₃ or CaO reacts with acidic SO₂, forming calcium sulfite or sulfate. Acid-base neutralisation captures the gas.

Key terms

Particulate matter
Tiny solid or liquid particles suspended in air
Photochemical smog
Pollution formed by sunlight acting on NOₓ and VOCs
Flue gas desulfurisation
Removing SO₂ from exhaust gases
