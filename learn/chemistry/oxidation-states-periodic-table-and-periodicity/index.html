Oxidation States

Oxidation states are assigned using rules for elements, ions, oxygen, hydrogen and fluorine, summing to the overall charge; they follow group patterns, vary for non-metals and transition metals and appear as Roman numerals in names.

Chemistry · Periodic Table and Periodicity · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Apply rules for assigning oxidation states
  • Relate common oxidation states to group number
  • Use oxidation states to name compounds

Rules

  • Elements uncombined have oxidation state 0.
  • Simple ions: oxidation state equals the charge.
  • Oxygen is usually −2 (−1 in peroxides); hydrogen usually +1 (−1 in metal hydrides).
  • Fluorine is always −1.
  • The sum of oxidation states equals the overall charge.

Patterns and names

  • Group 1: +1; group 2: +2; aluminium: +3.
  • Non-metals can show negative and positive states, such as chlorine from −1 to +7.
  • Transition metals show variable states.
  • Roman numerals give oxidation states in names, such as iron(III) chloride.

The maximum positive oxidation state of a main group element usually equals its number of outer electrons. Oxidation states are a bookkeeping tool and do not always represent real charges. They are essential for recognising redox reactions.

Worked example

Manganese in permanganate.

  1. In MnO₄⁻, each oxygen is −2, giving −8 in total.
  2. The overall charge is −1.
  3. Mn + (−8) = −1, so Mn = +7.
  4. The ion is called manganate(VII).
Common mistakeThinking oxygen is always −2. In peroxides such as H₂O₂, oxygen is −1.

In 3 lines: Rules sum to charge. Group patterns. Roman numerals in names.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is the oxidation state of sulfur in SO₄²⁻?
    • +4
    • −2
    • +6
    • +8
    Show the answer

    Answer: +6. S + 4(−2) = −2 for the ion, so sulfur must be +6.

  2. What is the systematic name for Fe₂O₃?
    • Iron(II) oxide
    • Iron(VI) oxide
    • Iron(I) oxide
    • Iron(III) oxide
    Show the answer

    Answer: Iron(III) oxide. Each Fe is +3 because 3 O at −2 give −6 over two Fe.

  3. Why are oxidation states described as a bookkeeping tool?
    • They do not always represent real charges on atoms
    • They are always exact real charges
    • They only apply to ions
    • They measure bond length
    Show the answer

    Answer: They do not always represent real charges on atoms. In covalent compounds charges are shared, not transferred.

Key terms

Oxidation state
A number showing electron control in a compound
Peroxide
A compound with an O–O bond, oxygen −1
Roman numeral
Notation showing oxidation state in names
