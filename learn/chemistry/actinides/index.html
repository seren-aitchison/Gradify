Actinides

Actinides fill the 5f subshell and are all radioactive, with early ones showing many oxidation states; uranium-235 fission and plutonium-239 provide nuclear energy, americium is used in smoke detectors and long-lived waste needs safe long-term storage.

Chemistry · Periodic Table and Periodicity · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the position and radioactivity of the actinides
  • Explain the use of uranium and plutonium in nuclear power
  • Describe the hazards and management of actinide waste

Chemistry

  • Elements 89–103, filling the 5f subshell.
  • All are radioactive; only thorium and uranium occur in large amounts naturally.
  • Early actinides show many oxidation states, such as U from +3 to +6.
  • Elements beyond uranium are transuranic and mostly made artificially.

Nuclear uses and waste

  • Uranium-235 undergoes fission, releasing energy in reactors.
  • Natural uranium is enriched to raise the proportion of U-235.
  • Plutonium-239 is formed in reactors and can also be used as fuel.
  • Spent fuel is highly radioactive and must be stored safely for very long times.

The 5f orbitals extend further than 4f, so early actinides use them in bonding, giving more varied chemistry than lanthanides. Americium-241 is used in smoke detectors. The UK plans a geological disposal facility for long-lived radioactive waste.

Worked example

Fission of uranium-235.

  1. A neutron is absorbed by a uranium-235 nucleus.
  2. The nucleus splits into two smaller nuclei and releases two or three neutrons.
  3. A large amount of energy is released.
  4. The released neutrons can cause further fissions in a chain reaction.
Common mistakeThinking all actinides occur naturally in large amounts. Most are made artificially in reactors.

In 3 lines: 5f and radioactive. Fission for energy. Long-lived waste.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which isotope of uranium undergoes fission in reactors?
    • Uranium-238
    • Uranium-235
    • Uranium-232
    • Uranium-240
    Show the answer

    Answer: Uranium-235. U-235 fissions readily with slow neutrons.

  2. Why is uranium enriched for reactors?
    • To remove all radioactivity
    • To turn it into plutonium
    • To increase the proportion of fissile U-235
    • To make it non-radioactive
    Show the answer

    Answer: To increase the proportion of fissile U-235. Natural uranium has too little U-235 to sustain a chain reaction.

  3. Why do early actinides show more oxidation states than lanthanides?
    • They have no f electrons
    • Lanthanides have more electrons
    • Actinides are not radioactive
    • Their 5f orbitals extend further and can join in bonding
    Show the answer

    Answer: Their 5f orbitals extend further and can join in bonding. More available orbitals allow varied bonding.

Key terms

Actinide
An element filling 5f orbitals
Fission
Splitting of a heavy nucleus releasing energy
Transuranic element
An element heavier than uranium
