S-block Chemistry

s-block metals form oxides, peroxides and superoxides as cation size increases; carbonate and nitrate stability increases down group 2 because smaller cations polarise the anions more, and lithium and beryllium are anomalous due to high charge density.

Chemistry · Inorganic Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the oxides formed by s-block metals with oxygen
  • Explain trends in thermal stability of carbonates and nitrates
  • Explain the anomalous behaviour of lithium and beryllium

Oxides and peroxides

  • Lithium forms oxide (Li₂O); sodium forms peroxide (Na₂O₂); potassium forms superoxide (KO₂).
  • Larger cations stabilise larger anions.
  • Group 2 metals mainly form oxides, MO.
  • Superoxides are used in breathing apparatus to release oxygen.

Thermal stability

  • Group 2 carbonates become more stable down the group: MgCO₃ decomposes more easily than BaCO₃.
  • Small, highly charged cations polarise the carbonate ion, weakening it.
  • Group 1 nitrates (except lithium) decompose to nitrites and oxygen.
  • Group 2 nitrates decompose to oxides, NO₂ and oxygen.

Lithium and beryllium behave unusually because their small ions have high charge density: lithium carbonate decomposes like group 2 carbonates, and beryllium compounds are largely covalent. Potassium superoxide absorbs carbon dioxide and releases oxygen in rebreathers.

Worked example

Decomposing carbonates.

  1. MgCO₃ decomposes at about 350 °C.
  2. CaCO₃ decomposes at about 840 °C.
  3. BaCO₃ decomposes at about 1,360 °C.
  4. Larger cations polarise carbonate less, making it more stable.
Common mistakeThinking all group 1 metals form the same oxide. Lithium, sodium and potassium form different oxygen compounds.

In 3 lines: Oxide, peroxide, superoxide. Stability rises down group 2. Li and Be anomalies.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which compound does sodium mainly form when burned in oxygen?
    • Sodium oxide only
    • Sodium superoxide
    • Sodium peroxide, Na₂O₂
    • Sodium hydroxide
    Show the answer

    Answer: Sodium peroxide, Na₂O₂. Na⁺ is large enough to stabilise the peroxide ion.

  2. Why does MgCO₃ decompose at a lower temperature than BaCO₃?
    • Ba²⁺ is smaller than Mg²⁺
    • MgCO₃ is a covalent gas
    • Barium has a higher charge
    • Mg²⁺ is smaller and polarises carbonate more strongly
    Show the answer

    Answer: Mg²⁺ is smaller and polarises carbonate more strongly. Greater polarisation weakens the C–O bonds in carbonate.

  3. Why does lithium carbonate decompose like a group 2 carbonate?
    • The small Li⁺ ion has a high charge density
    • Lithium is in group 2
    • Lithium carbonate is very large
    • Li⁺ is not polarising
    Show the answer

    Answer: The small Li⁺ ion has a high charge density. High charge density polarises carbonate, as Mg²⁺ does.

Key terms

Peroxide
A compound containing O₂²⁻
Superoxide
A compound containing O₂⁻
Polarisation
Distortion of an anion's electron cloud by a cation
