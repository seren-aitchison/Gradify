Weak Acids and Bases

Weak acids and bases ionise only partially, with equilibrium to the left; at equal concentration weak acids have higher pH, conduct less and react more slowly, but neutralise the same amount of base per mole.

Chemistry · Acids, Bases and Aqueous Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define weak acids and bases
  • Write equilibrium equations for weak acids and bases
  • Compare properties of weak and strong acids

Partial ionisation

  • Weak acids ionise partially: CH₃COOH ⇌ CH₃COO⁻ + H⁺.
  • Most molecules stay un-ionised.
  • Weak bases, such as ammonia, accept protons partially.
  • Equilibrium lies to the left.

Comparison

  • Same concentration: a weak acid has higher pH than a strong acid.
  • Weak acids conduct electricity less well.
  • They react more slowly with metals and carbonates at the same concentration.
  • But they neutralise the same amount of base per mole.

Ethanoic acid at 0.10 mol dm⁻³ is only about 1% ionised. Carboxylic acids, carbonic acid and many acids in food are weak. A weak acid still neutralises the same amount of alkali because the equilibrium shifts as H⁺ is removed.

Worked example

Comparing reactions.

  1. Equal volumes of 1 mol dm⁻³ HCl and ethanoic acid react with magnesium.
  2. HCl fizzes much faster.
  3. Both eventually produce the same volume of hydrogen.
  4. Ethanoic acid releases H⁺ gradually as it is used.
Common mistakeThinking a weak acid neutralises less alkali than a strong acid. Per mole, both neutralise the same amount.

In 3 lines: Partial ionisation. Higher pH than strong. Same neutralising capacity.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which acid is weak?
    • Ethanoic acid
    • Hydrochloric acid
    • Nitric acid
    • Sulfuric acid
    Show the answer

    Answer: Ethanoic acid. Ethanoic acid only partially ionises in water.

  2. Why do weak and strong acids of equal moles neutralise the same amount of alkali?
    • Weak acids contain more hydrogen atoms
    • The weak acid's equilibrium shifts to release more H⁺ as it is used
    • Strong acids lose H⁺ to the air
    • Weak acids react faster
    Show the answer

    Answer: The weak acid's equilibrium shifts to release more H⁺ as it is used. Removing H⁺ drives further ionisation until all reacts.

  3. Why does a weak acid react more slowly with magnesium than a strong acid?
    • It contains no hydrogen
    • It is more concentrated
    • It has a lower concentration of H⁺ ions at any moment
    • Magnesium does not react with weak acids
    Show the answer

    Answer: It has a lower concentration of H⁺ ions at any moment. Fewer H⁺ ions means fewer successful collisions.

Key terms

Weak acid
An acid that partially ionises in water
Partial ionisation
Only a fraction of molecules ionising
Weak base
A base that partially accepts protons
