Electrophilic Addition

Electrophilic addition of HBr goes through a carbocation, with the more stable one giving the major product, while Br₂ forms a cyclic bromonium ion attacked from the opposite face to give anti addition; capture by other anions supports these intermediates.

Chemistry · Organic Reaction Mechanisms · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Draw the mechanism for addition of HBr to an alkene
  • Explain the bromonium ion mechanism for adding bromine
  • Explain evidence for the carbocation and bromonium intermediates

Adding HBr

  • The π electrons attack H of H–Br, which is δ+.
  • A carbocation forms; Br⁻ is released.
  • Br⁻ attacks the carbocation.
  • The more stable carbocation leads to the major product: with propene, H adds to the end carbon and Br to the middle one, giving 2-bromopropane (Markovnikov's rule).

Adding Br₂

  • Br₂ is polarised as the π bond approaches.
  • A cyclic bromonium ion forms.
  • Br⁻ attacks from the opposite face: anti addition.
  • Cyclohexene gives trans-1,2-dibromocyclohexane.

Evidence for the intermediate: bromine in the presence of chloride ions gives some products containing both Br and Cl, showing that a positive intermediate is captured by whichever anion is nearby. Hydration with steam and acid follows the HBr pathway, with water as the nucleophile.

Worked example

Bromine with ethene in brine.

  1. Br₂ adds to form a bromonium ion.
  2. Br⁻ or Cl⁻ can attack the intermediate.
  3. Products include 1,2-dibromoethane and 1-bromo-2-chloroethane.
  4. A two-step mechanism with an intermediate is confirmed.
Common mistakeThinking Br₂ adds both bromines at once from the same side. It adds in two steps from opposite faces.

In 3 lines: HBr via carbocation. Br₂ via bromonium. Anti addition.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which atom in HBr does the alkene's π bond attack first?
    • The δ− bromine atom
    • Both atoms at the same time
    • Neither atom reacts
    • The δ+ hydrogen atom
    Show the answer

    Answer: The δ+ hydrogen atom. Electrons flow towards the electron-poor hydrogen.

  2. Why does bromine in sodium chloride solution give some chlorinated products?
    • Chloride ions capture the positive intermediate
    • Chlorine replaces bromine in Br₂
    • Sodium ions add to the alkene
    • The alkene reacts with water only
    Show the answer

    Answer: Chloride ions capture the positive intermediate. A two-step mechanism lets any nearby anion attack.

  3. Why does bromination of cyclohexene give only the trans product?
    • Both bromines add together from the same face
    • Bromide attacks the bromonium ion from the face opposite the bridging bromine
    • The ring flips during reaction to give cis product
    • A flat carbocation forms and is attacked equally from both faces
    Show the answer

    Answer: Bromide attacks the bromonium ion from the face opposite the bridging bromine. The bridged intermediate blocks one face.

Key terms

Bromonium ion
A cyclic intermediate with a positive bromine bridging two carbons
Anti addition
Addition of two groups to opposite faces
Induced dipole
Polarity created in Br₂ by an approaching π bond
