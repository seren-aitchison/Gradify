Ligand Field Theory

Ligand field theory adds covalent orbital overlap to crystal field theory, with σ bonding raising eg* and π effects altering t2g: π-donors decrease and π-acceptors increase splitting, explaining the spectrochemical series and back-bonding in metal carbonyls.

Chemistry · Inorganic Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain the limitations of crystal field theory
  • Describe ligand field theory using molecular orbitals
  • Explain π-donor and π-acceptor effects on splitting

Beyond point charges

  • Ligand field theory combines crystal field ideas with molecular orbital theory.
  • Metal and ligand orbitals overlap to form bonding and antibonding orbitals.
  • The eg* orbitals are σ-antibonding; t2g orbitals can take part in π bonding.
  • This explains why neutral ligands such as CO produce large splitting.

π effects

  • π-donor ligands (halides) raise t2g, decreasing Δo.
  • π-acceptor ligands (CO, CN⁻) lower t2g, increasing Δo.
  • This explains the order of the spectrochemical series.
  • π-acceptance (back-bonding) stabilises low oxidation states.

Crystal field theory cannot explain why neutral CO produces a larger splitting than negative halides. Ligand field theory explains this through back-bonding, where metal d electrons are donated into empty π* orbitals of CO. This also explains stable metal carbonyls such as Ni(CO)₄ with nickel in oxidation state 0.

Worked example

Back-bonding in metal carbonyls.

  1. CO donates a lone pair to the metal: σ bond.
  2. The metal donates d electrons back into CO's empty π* orbitals.
  3. This strengthens the metal-CO bond and weakens the C≡O bond slightly.
  4. Infrared spectra show a lower C≡O stretching frequency in carbonyls.
Common mistakeThinking negative ligands always give larger splitting. Neutral π-acceptors such as CO give the largest splitting.

In 3 lines: Covalent overlap added. π-donors and π-acceptors. Explains spectrochemical series.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which type of ligand increases Δo?
    • π-donor ligands such as halides
    • π-acceptor ligands such as CO
    • Ligands with no lone pairs
    • Noble gas atoms
    Show the answer

    Answer: π-acceptor ligands such as CO. π-acceptors lower the t2g energy, which widens the gap Δo.

  2. Why can crystal field theory not explain CO's large splitting?
    • CO has a large negative charge
    • CO is not a ligand
    • CO is neutral, so point charges cannot account for it
    • Crystal field theory ignores all ligands
    Show the answer

    Answer: CO is neutral, so point charges cannot account for it. Only covalent π interactions explain CO's effect.

  3. How does back-bonding affect the C≡O bond in metal carbonyls?
    • It strengthens the C≡O triple bond
    • It breaks the C≡O bond completely
    • It has no effect
    • It weakens it slightly by filling CO π* orbitals
    Show the answer

    Answer: It weakens it slightly by filling CO π* orbitals. Electrons in antibonding orbitals lower the bond order.

Key terms

Ligand field theory
A bonding model combining crystal field and MO theory
Back-bonding
Donation of metal d electrons into ligand π* orbitals
π-acceptor
A ligand accepting electrons into empty π orbitals
