Solid-state Bonding

Solids are ionic, molecular, giant covalent or metallic, each with characteristic properties; band theory explains conductors with overlapping bands, insulators with large gaps and semiconductors with small gaps whose conductivity can be tuned by doping.

Chemistry · Chemical Bonding and Molecular Structure · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Classify solids as ionic, molecular, giant covalent or metallic
  • Relate solid type to properties
  • Explain conductors, semiconductors and insulators using band theory

Types of solid

  • Ionic: ions in a lattice; high melting, brittle, conduct when molten.
  • Molecular: molecules held by intermolecular forces; low melting, non-conducting.
  • Giant covalent: atoms in a covalent network; very high melting, usually non-conducting.
  • Metallic: ions in delocalised electrons; conducting, malleable.

Band theory

  • In solids, atomic orbitals merge into energy bands.
  • The valence band holds bonding electrons; the conduction band allows electrons to move.
  • Conductors: bands overlap. Insulators: large band gap.
  • Semiconductors: small band gap; conductivity rises with temperature or doping.

Silicon is a semiconductor with a band gap of about 1.1 eV. Doping with phosphorus adds electrons (n-type) and doping with boron creates holes (p-type), forming the basis of transistors and solar cells. Diamond is an insulator with a large band gap of about 5.5 eV.

Worked example

Identifying an unknown solid.

  1. A solid melts at 1,600 °C, does not conduct as a solid or liquid and is insoluble.
  2. It is not ionic, as it does not conduct when molten.
  3. Its very high melting point rules out a molecular solid.
  4. It is a giant covalent solid, such as silica.
Common mistakeThinking all non-metal solids are insulators. Silicon and graphite conduct to some degree.

In 3 lines: Four types of solid. Properties follow bonding. Band theory.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which type of solid has a very high melting point and does not conduct?
    • Metallic
    • Giant covalent
    • Molecular
    • Ionic when molten
    Show the answer

    Answer: Giant covalent. Strong network bonds and no mobile charges give these properties.

  2. Why does a semiconductor's conductivity rise with temperature?
    • Its band gap disappears completely
    • It becomes a metal
    • More electrons gain enough energy to cross the small band gap
    • Heat removes electrons
    Show the answer

    Answer: More electrons gain enough energy to cross the small band gap. Thermal energy promotes electrons into the conduction band.

  3. How does doping silicon with phosphorus increase conductivity?
    • It removes all electrons
    • It widens the band gap
    • It makes silicon ionic
    • It adds extra electrons that can move (n-type)
    Show the answer

    Answer: It adds extra electrons that can move (n-type). Phosphorus has one more outer electron than silicon.

Key terms

Band gap
Energy difference between valence and conduction bands
Semiconductor
A material with a small band gap
Doping
Adding impurities to change conductivity
