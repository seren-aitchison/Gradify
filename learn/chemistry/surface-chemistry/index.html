Surface Chemistry

Surface atoms have fewer neighbours and higher energy, making surfaces reactive; high surface area, especially in nanoparticles, increases activity, and techniques such as STM, XPS and BET reveal surface structure and composition.

Chemistry · Physical Chemistry and Molecular Science · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain why surface atoms behave differently from bulk atoms
  • Describe the importance of surface area
  • Describe techniques for studying surfaces

Why surfaces matter

  • Surface atoms have fewer neighbours, so they are more reactive.
  • Surface energy makes surfaces tend to minimise their area.
  • Nanoparticles have a high proportion of surface atoms.
  • Many reactions happen only at surfaces, as in heterogeneous catalysis.

Studying surfaces

  • Scanning tunnelling microscopy (STM) images individual atoms.
  • X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) identifies surface elements and oxidation states.
  • Ultra-high vacuum keeps surfaces clean.
  • BET analysis measures surface area by gas adsorption.

Gerhard Ertl's studies of reactions on surfaces, including the Haber process on iron, earned the 2007 Nobel Prize. Surface science explains catalysis, corrosion, sensors and semiconductor devices.

Worked example

Surface area of nanoparticles.

  1. A 1 cm cube of gold has a tiny fraction of atoms at the surface.
  2. Divided into 5 nm particles, a large fraction is at the surface.
  3. Gold nanoparticles become catalytically active.
  4. Bulk gold is famously unreactive.
Common mistakeThinking materials behave the same at all sizes. Nanoparticles can behave very differently from bulk.

In 3 lines: Surface atoms differ. Surface area matters. STM, XPS, BET.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which technique can image individual surface atoms?
    • Infrared spectroscopy
    • Thin-layer chromatography
    • Scanning tunnelling microscopy
    • Flame testing
    Show the answer

    Answer: Scanning tunnelling microscopy. STM measures a tunnelling current at atomic resolution.

  2. Why are gold nanoparticles catalytically active while bulk gold is not?
    • Nanoparticles contain completely different elements
    • Bulk gold has much more surface area per gram
    • Gold changes into another metal when divided
    • A large fraction of their atoms are at the surface with unusual properties
    Show the answer

    Answer: A large fraction of their atoms are at the surface with unusual properties. Size changes surface structure and electronic properties.

  3. Why are surface experiments often done in ultra-high vacuum?
    • To keep surfaces free of contaminating gas molecules
    • To make surfaces hotter
    • Vacuum increases surface energy
    • Air improves all measurements
    Show the answer

    Answer: To keep surfaces free of contaminating gas molecules. Even trace gases cover a surface quickly.
