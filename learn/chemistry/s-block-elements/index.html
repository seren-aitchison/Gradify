S-block Elements

s-block elements have outer s electrons, are reactive metals forming +1 or +2 ions in mostly ionic compounds, act as reducing agents and become more reactive down each group, with group 1 more reactive than group 2.

Chemistry · Periodic Table and Periodicity · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the electron configurations of s-block elements
  • Describe typical properties of s-block metals
  • Compare the reactivity of groups 1 and 2

Features

  • Outer electrons are in an s orbital: ns¹ for group 1 and ns² for group 2.
  • They are metals with low ionisation energies.
  • They form ions with charges +1 (group 1) and +2 (group 2).
  • Their compounds are mostly ionic and white or colourless.

Reactivity

  • Group 1 metals are more reactive than group 2 metals in the same period.
  • Reactivity increases down each group.
  • Many react with water to form hydroxides and hydrogen.
  • They are strong reducing agents.

Group 1 metals are soft and stored under oil because they react with air and water. Group 2 elements include magnesium and calcium, important in biology and construction. Many s-block compounds give characteristic flame colours.

Worked example

Comparing sodium and magnesium.

  1. Sodium (3s¹) loses one electron to form Na⁺.
  2. Magnesium (3s²) must lose two electrons to form Mg²⁺.
  3. Removing two electrons needs more energy in total.
  4. Sodium reacts vigorously with cold water; magnesium reacts very slowly.
Common mistakeThinking s-block metals are found as pure metals in nature. They are too reactive and occur as compounds.

In 3 lines: Outer s electrons. +1 and +2 ions. Reactivity increases down groups.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What charge do group 2 ions have?
    • +2
    • +1
    • −2
    • 0
    Show the answer

    Answer: +2. Group 2 atoms lose two outer s electrons.

  2. Why is sodium more reactive than magnesium?
    • It has more protons
    • It needs to lose only one electron
    • It has more electron shells
    • It is a non-metal
    Show the answer

    Answer: It needs to lose only one electron. Losing one electron takes less total energy than losing two.

  3. Why do s-block metals act as reducing agents?
    • They readily accept electrons
    • They have full outer shells
    • They readily donate their outer electrons
    • They form covalent bonds only
    Show the answer

    Answer: They readily donate their outer electrons. Low ionisation energies make electron loss easy.

Key terms

s-block
Groups 1 and 2, with outer s electrons
Reducing agent
A substance that donates electrons
Ionic compound
A compound of oppositely charged ions
