Experimental Kinetics

Kinetics experiments use continuous methods such as colorimetry or gas collection, discontinuous sampling with quenching and titration, or clock reactions, controlling temperature and other variables and repeating runs to reduce errors.

Chemistry · Chemical Kinetics · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Plan a kinetics experiment controlling variables
  • Choose continuous or discontinuous monitoring methods
  • Evaluate errors and uncertainties in kinetics data

Methods

  • Continuous monitoring: measuring a property throughout, such as colorimetry, gas volume or conductivity.
  • Discontinuous (sampling): withdrawing samples, quenching them and titrating.
  • Clock reactions: timing to a sudden colour change.
  • Temperature is controlled with a water bath.

Errors

  • Keep all variables constant except the one being changed.
  • Start timing accurately when reactants mix.
  • Repeat runs to check reliability.
  • Uncertainties in timing and volume measurements limit precision.

Quenching stops a reaction in a sample, for example by rapid cooling or by neutralising a catalyst, so the concentration at that moment can be measured. Data loggers and computers allow many readings and accurate graphs. Fast reactions need special methods such as stopped-flow spectroscopy.

Worked example

Sampling and quenching.

  1. An ester is hydrolysed with acid catalyst at 25 °C.
  2. Every 5 minutes, 5 cm³ is withdrawn into ice-cold water.
  3. Cooling quenches the reaction.
  4. Each sample is titrated to find the acid concentration at that time.
Common mistakeLetting the temperature drift during a kinetics run. Temperature strongly affects rate, so it must be controlled.

In 3 lines: Continuous or sampling. Control temperature. Repeat and evaluate.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which is a continuous monitoring method?
    • Using a colorimeter throughout the reaction
    • Titrating quenched samples
    • Weighing products at the end only
    • Measuring pH once at the start
    Show the answer

    Answer: Using a colorimeter throughout the reaction. Colorimeters record absorbance throughout.

  2. How can a sample be quenched?
    • By heating it strongly
    • By rapid cooling or neutralising the catalyst
    • By adding more reactant
    • By stirring vigorously
    Show the answer

    Answer: By rapid cooling or neutralising the catalyst. Stopping the reaction freezes its composition.

  3. Why are fast reactions studied using stopped-flow methods?
    • They slow reactions down chemically
    • They avoid measuring anything
    • They mix and measure within milliseconds
    • They use only titrations
    Show the answer

    Answer: They mix and measure within milliseconds. Ordinary mixing is too slow for rapid reactions.

Key terms

Quenching
Stopping a reaction in a sample
Continuous monitoring
Measuring a property throughout a reaction
Stopped-flow
A method for studying very fast reactions
