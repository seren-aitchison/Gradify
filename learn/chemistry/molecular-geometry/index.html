Molecular Geometry

Two to six domains give linear, trigonal planar, tetrahedral, trigonal bipyramidal and octahedral shapes; lone pairs produce trigonal pyramidal, bent and square planar shapes, and shape governs polarity and interactions.

Chemistry · Chemical Bonding and Molecular Structure · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Name shapes for two to six electron domains
  • Predict shapes of molecules with lone pairs
  • Link molecular shape to properties

Shapes without lone pairs

  • 2 domains: linear, 180°, such as CO₂.
  • 3 domains: trigonal planar, 120°, such as BF₃.
  • 4 domains: tetrahedral, 109.5°, such as CH₄.
  • 5 domains: trigonal bipyramidal, 90° and 120°, such as PCl₅.
  • 6 domains: octahedral, 90°, such as SF₆.

Shapes with lone pairs

  • 3 bonding + 1 lone: trigonal pyramidal, such as NH₃.
  • 2 bonding + 2 lone: bent, such as H₂O.
  • 2 bonding + 1 lone (3 domains): bent, about 118°, such as SO₂.
  • 4 bonding + 2 lone: square planar, such as XeF₄.

Shape determines polarity, intermolecular forces and how molecules fit together, including drug-receptor binding. In trigonal bipyramidal arrangements, lone pairs occupy equatorial positions to reduce repulsion, giving shapes such as seesaw (SF₄).

Worked example

Predicting the shape of XeF₄.

  1. Xenon has 8 valence electrons; four bond to fluorine.
  2. That leaves two lone pairs: six domains in total.
  3. The electron geometry is octahedral.
  4. Lone pairs sit opposite each other, giving a square planar shape.
Common mistakeNaming the shape from the electron geometry. The shape describes the atoms, not the lone pairs.

In 3 lines: Domain number sets geometry. Lone pairs change shape. Shape affects properties.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is the bond angle in a linear molecule?
    • 120°
    • 109.5°
    • 180°
    • 90°
    Show the answer

    Answer: 180°. Two domains point in opposite directions in a straight line.

  2. What shape is water?
    • Linear
    • Tetrahedral
    • Trigonal planar
    • Bent
    Show the answer

    Answer: Bent. Two bonding pairs and two lone pairs give a bent molecular shape.

  3. Why is XeF₄ square planar rather than tetrahedral?
    • Its two lone pairs occupy opposite octahedral positions
    • Xenon has no lone pairs
    • It has only four domains
    • Fluorine atoms repel the lone pairs away
    Show the answer

    Answer: Its two lone pairs occupy opposite octahedral positions. Six domains give an octahedral arrangement with lone pairs opposite.

Key terms

Tetrahedral
A shape with four bonds at 109.5°
Trigonal pyramidal
A pyramid shape with a lone pair at the top
Square planar
Four bonds in a square around a central atom
