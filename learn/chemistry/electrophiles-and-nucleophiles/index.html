Electrophiles and Nucleophiles

Nucleophiles donate lone pairs or π electrons and are stronger with negative charge and less electronegative donor atoms; electrophiles have empty orbitals or δ+ atoms and are stronger with positive charge, and polar reactions pair the most nucleophilic and electrophilic sites.

Chemistry · Organic Reaction Mechanisms · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Identify nucleophilic and electrophilic sites in molecules
  • Explain factors affecting nucleophile strength
  • Explain factors affecting electrophile strength

Nucleophiles

  • Have a lone pair or π bond to donate.
  • Strength rises with negative charge: OH⁻ is stronger than H₂O.
  • Less electronegative donor atoms are often stronger: NH₃ outdoes H₂O.
  • Bulky nucleophiles react more slowly at crowded carbons.

Electrophiles

  • Have an empty orbital or a δ+ atom.
  • Carbonyl carbons and carbons bonded to halogens are δ+.
  • Positive charge increases strength: carbocations are strong.
  • Lewis acids such as AlCl₃ and BF₃ are electrophiles.

Most polar reactions pair the most nucleophilic site of one molecule with the most electrophilic site of another. Bond polarity, from electronegativity differences, identifies these sites. Solvent also matters: polar aprotic solvents leave anions "naked" and more reactive.

Worked example

Finding the sites in a reaction.

  1. In bromoethane, Br is more electronegative, so C is δ+.
  2. In cyanide, CN⁻, carbon carries a lone pair.
  3. The cyanide lone pair attacks the δ+ carbon.
  4. Bromide leaves, forming propanenitrile.
Common mistakeThinking nucleophiles must be negatively charged. Neutral molecules such as ammonia and water are nucleophiles too.

In 3 lines: Lone pairs and π bonds. δ+ and empty orbitals. Match the sites.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which carbon in bromoethane is electrophilic?
    • The carbon of the methyl group
    • The carbon bonded to bromine
    • Neither carbon is electrophilic
    • Both carbons equally
    Show the answer

    Answer: The carbon bonded to bromine. Bromine pulls electron density, leaving its carbon δ+.

  2. Why does a polar aprotic solvent speed up reactions of anionic nucleophiles?
    • It adds extra negative charge to the nucleophile
    • It removes all the electrophile from solution
    • It does not hydrogen bond to the anion, leaving it more reactive
    • It converts the nucleophile into a radical
    Show the answer

    Answer: It does not hydrogen bond to the anion, leaving it more reactive. Protic solvents cage anions in hydrogen bonds.

  3. Why is a carbonyl carbon electrophilic?
    • Carbon is more electronegative than oxygen
    • The carbon carries a lone pair
    • Carbonyl groups have no polarity
    • Oxygen pulls electron density from the C=O, leaving carbon δ+
    Show the answer

    Answer: Oxygen pulls electron density from the C=O, leaving carbon δ+. Polarisation of C=O makes carbon open to attack.

Key terms

Nucleophilicity
How quickly a nucleophile reacts
Lewis acid
An electron-pair acceptor
Polar aprotic solvent
A polar solvent without O–H or N–H bonds
