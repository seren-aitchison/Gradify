Electron Configurations

Configurations list subshells in filling order (1s 2s 2p 3s 3p 4s 3d 4p); transition metals lose 4s electrons first, and Cr and Cu are exceptions.

Chemistry · Atomic Structure and Quantum Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Write full and abbreviated electron configurations for elements up to krypton
  • Write configurations for ions, including transition metal ions
  • Identify the exceptional configurations of chromium and copper

Writing configurations

Subshells are listed in order of filling, with the number of electrons as a superscript. The order up to krypton is 1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 4s, 3d, 4p. Chlorine (17 electrons) is 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁵. The abbreviated form starts from the previous noble gas: [Ne] 3s² 3p⁵.

Worked example

Write the electron configuration of iron (Z = 26).

  1. Fill in order: 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ (18 electrons), then 4s² (20), then 3d⁶ (26).
  2. Iron: 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d⁶ 4s², or [Ar] 3d⁶ 4s².

Ions

For main-group ions, add or remove electrons from the outermost subshell: Na⁺ is 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ and Cl⁻ is [Ne] 3s² 3p⁶, both with noble gas configurations. For transition metals, the 4s electrons are removed before the 3d: Fe²⁺ is [Ar] 3d⁶ and Fe³⁺ is [Ar] 3d⁵. 4s fills first, but once 3d is occupied the 4s electrons are the outermost and highest in energy.

Chromium and copper

Two elements in the first transition series break the filling pattern. Chromium is [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹, not 3d⁴ 4s², and copper is [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹, not 3d⁹ 4s². The 4s and 3d energies are very close, and a half-filled or filled d subshell, with its reduced electron repulsion, gives the lower overall energy.

Configurations explain the periodic table: elements in a group share the same outer configuration, such as ns¹ for the alkali metals and ns² np⁵ for the halogens.

Common mistakeRemoving 3d electrons first when forming a transition metal ion. The 4s electrons are lost first: Fe²⁺ is [Ar] 3d⁶, not [Ar] 3d⁴ 4s².

In 3 lines: Fill 1s 2s 2p 3s 3p 4s 3d 4p in order, writing the number in each. Abbreviate with a noble gas core; main-group ions often reach noble gas configurations. Transition metals lose 4s before 3d; Cr is 3d⁵ 4s¹ and Cu 3d¹⁰ 4s¹.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which is the electron configuration of oxygen (Z = 8)?
    • 1s² 2s² 2p⁶
    • 1s² 2s⁴ 2p²
    • 1s² 2p⁶
    • 1s² 2s² 2p⁴
    Show the answer

    Answer: 1s² 2s² 2p⁴. Oxygen has 8 electrons: 2 in 1s, 2 in 2s and 4 in 2p.

  2. What is the electron configuration of Fe³⁺?
    • [Ar] 3d⁵
    • [Ar] 3d³ 4s²
    • [Ar] 3d⁶ 4s²
    • [Ar] 4s² 3d³
    Show the answer

    Answer: [Ar] 3d⁵. Iron [Ar] 3d⁶ 4s² loses its two 4s electrons and then one 3d electron.

  3. Why is chromium's configuration [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹ rather than [Ar] 3d⁴ 4s²?
    • 4s orbitals can hold only one electron in chromium
    • The 3d and 4s energies are so close that a half-filled 3d subshell, with less electron repulsion, gives a lower total energy
    • Chromium has 23 electrons
    • The 3d subshell must always fill before 4s
    Show the answer

    Answer: The 3d and 4s energies are so close that a half-filled 3d subshell, with less electron repulsion, gives a lower total energy. Small energy differences let the more stable half-filled arrangement win; copper's full 3d¹⁰ is the similar case.

Key terms

Electron configuration
The arrangement of an atom's electrons in subshells, written as 1s² 2s² and so on
Noble gas core
The abbreviation [He], [Ne], [Ar]… for the inner electrons matching the previous noble gas
Valence configuration
The configuration of the outer electrons, such as 3s² 3p⁵ for chlorine
