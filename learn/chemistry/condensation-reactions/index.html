Condensation Reactions

Condensation reactions join molecules with loss of water or HCl, as in esterification, amide formation and aldol condensation; repeating them with bifunctional monomers gives polyesters, polyamides and proteins.

Chemistry · Organic Reaction Mechanisms · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define condensation reactions
  • Describe esterification and amide formation
  • Describe condensation polymerisation

Small-molecule condensations

  • Two molecules join and lose a small molecule, usually water or HCl.
  • Esterification: acid + alcohol ⇌ ester + water (acid catalyst).
  • Amide formation: acyl chloride + amine → amide + HCl.
  • Aldol condensation: two carbonyls join and lose water, forming C=C.

Polymers

  • Polyesters: diacid + diol, such as PET.
  • Polyamides: diacid or diacyl chloride + diamine, such as nylon-6,6.
  • Proteins are natural polyamides from amino acids.
  • Each link releases a small molecule.

Condensations are often reversible, so removing water drives the reaction forward. Nylon-6,6 can be pulled as a rope from the interface between solutions of hexanedioyl dichloride and hexane-1,6-diamine. Condensation polymers can be broken down by hydrolysis, unlike most addition polymers.

Worked example

Making ethyl ethanoate.

  1. Ethanoic acid and ethanol are warmed with concentrated H₂SO₄.
  2. The acid catalyst activates the C=O group.
  3. Water is lost as the ester linkage forms.
  4. Ethyl ethanoate, a fruity-smelling ester, forms.
Common mistakeConfusing condensation polymers with addition polymers. Condensation releases a small molecule at each link.

In 3 lines: Join and lose water. Esters and amides. Polyesters and polyamides.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which product forms when ethanoic acid reacts with ethanol?
    • Ethyl ethanoate
    • Ethanal
    • Ethene
    • Ethanamide
    Show the answer

    Answer: Ethyl ethanoate. The acid and alcohol join to form an ester.

  2. Why is water removed during esterification?
    • Water catalyses the reverse reaction only
    • Removing a product shifts the equilibrium towards the ester
    • Water is a reactant that must be destroyed
    • The ester dissolves only in water
    Show the answer

    Answer: Removing a product shifts the equilibrium towards the ester. Le Chatelier's principle drives the condensation forward.

  3. Why are condensation polymers easier to break down than addition polymers?
    • They contain only C–C bonds
    • They are never biodegradable
    • Their ester and amide links can be hydrolysed
    • Addition polymers have ester links
    Show the answer

    Answer: Their ester and amide links can be hydrolysed. Hydrolysis reverses the condensation at each link.

Key terms

Condensation reaction
Joining with loss of a small molecule
Esterification
Formation of an ester from an acid and alcohol
Condensation polymer
A polymer formed with loss of small molecules
