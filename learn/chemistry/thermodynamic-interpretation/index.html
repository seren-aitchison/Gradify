Thermodynamic Interpretation

ΔG⦵ = −RT ln K links equilibrium to thermodynamics; combining with ΔG⦵ = ΔH⦵ − TΔS⦵ gives the van 't Hoff relation, so a plot of ln K against 1/T yields ΔH⦵ and ΔS⦵, and thermodynamic data predict K.

Chemistry · Chemical Equilibrium · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Relate K to ΔG⦵
  • Explain the temperature dependence of K using ΔH⦵ and ΔS⦵
  • Use thermodynamic data to predict equilibrium positions

Linking K and ΔG⦵

  • ΔG⦵ = −RT ln K.
  • Combined with ΔG⦵ = ΔH⦵ − TΔS⦵: ln K = −ΔH⦵/RT + ΔS⦵/R.
  • Plotting ln K against 1/T gives gradient −ΔH⦵/R (the van 't Hoff plot).
  • Intercept gives ΔS⦵/R.

Interpreting

  • Large negative ΔG⦵ means K is large.
  • ΔG⦵ of about −6 kJ mol⁻¹ at 298 K gives K of about 10.
  • Each change of about 5.7 kJ mol⁻¹ in ΔG⦵ changes K by a factor of 10 at 298 K.
  • Thermodynamic tables allow K to be predicted without experiments.

The van 't Hoff plot is analogous to the Arrhenius plot for rate constants. Thermodynamic interpretation explains Le Chatelier's temperature effects quantitatively and links equilibrium to entropy and enthalpy.

Worked example

Predicting K from data.

  1. A reaction has ΔH⦵ = −40 kJ mol⁻¹ and ΔS⦵ = −100 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹.
  2. At 298 K: ΔG⦵ = −40 − 298(−0.100) = −10.2 kJ mol⁻¹.
  3. ln K = 10,200 ÷ (8.314 × 298) = 4.12.
  4. K ≈ 62, so products are favoured.
Common mistakeThinking K depends only on ΔH⦵. Both enthalpy and entropy determine K.

In 3 lines: ΔG⦵ = −RT ln K. Van 't Hoff plot. Predict K from tables.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What does a large negative ΔG⦵ indicate about K?
    • K is small, so reactants are favoured
    • K is large, favouring products
    • K is negative
    • K equals one
    Show the answer

    Answer: K is large, favouring products. ln K = −ΔG⦵/RT is large and positive, so K is large.

  2. Which two quantities together determine K?
    • Rate and concentration
    • Volume and pressure
    • ΔH⦵ and ΔS⦵
    • Mass and colour
    Show the answer

    Answer: ΔH⦵ and ΔS⦵. ΔG⦵ combines both, and K follows from ΔG⦵.

  3. How is the van 't Hoff plot similar to the Arrhenius plot?
    • Both plot rate against concentration
    • Both measure pressure
    • They are unrelated
    • Both plot a log against 1/T to extract an energy term
    Show the answer

    Answer: Both plot a log against 1/T to extract an energy term. Each plots a logarithm against 1/T, and the gradient gives an energy term.
