Industrial Inorganic Chemistry

Major industrial inorganic processes include the Haber, Contact and Ostwald processes and chlor-alkali electrolysis; compromise conditions, catalysts and recycling balance rate, yield, cost and safety.

Chemistry · Inorganic Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the manufacture of ammonia, sulfuric acid and nitric acid
  • Describe the chlor-alkali process
  • Explain the role of catalysts and conditions in industry

Major processes

  • Haber process: N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃, iron catalyst.
  • Contact process: SO₂ oxidised to SO₃ over vanadium(V) oxide, then made into H₂SO₄.
  • Ostwald process: ammonia oxidised over platinum-rhodium to make nitric acid.
  • Chlor-alkali: electrolysis of brine gives Cl₂, H₂ and NaOH.

Choosing conditions

  • Compromise conditions balance rate, yield and cost.
  • Catalysts allow lower temperatures and faster rates.
  • Recycling unreacted gases increases overall conversion.
  • Safety and energy efficiency are key concerns.

Ammonia, sulfuric acid and chlorine are among the most produced chemicals. Ammonia feeds fertiliser production, and sulfuric acid is used in fertilisers, batteries and metal processing. Membrane cells in the chlor-alkali process have replaced older mercury cells, reducing pollution.

Worked example

The Contact process.

  1. Sulfur is burned to SO₂.
  2. 2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃ over V₂O₅ at about 450 °C.
  3. SO₃ is absorbed in concentrated sulfuric acid to form oleum.
  4. Oleum is diluted with water to give sulfuric acid.
Common mistakeThinking SO₃ is dissolved directly in water. This forms a corrosive mist, so it is absorbed in sulfuric acid first.

In 3 lines: Haber, Contact, Ostwald. Chlor-alkali. Compromise conditions.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which process makes nitric acid?
    • The Contact process
    • The Haber process
    • The Ostwald process
    • The chlor-alkali process
    Show the answer

    Answer: The Ostwald process. Ammonia is oxidised over platinum-rhodium gauze.

  2. Why is SO₃ absorbed in sulfuric acid rather than water?
    • SO₃ does not react with water under any conditions
    • Sulfuric acid is far cheaper than using water
    • Water would convert the SO₃ back into sulfur
    • Direct reaction with water forms a hard-to-handle acid mist
    Show the answer

    Answer: Direct reaction with water forms a hard-to-handle acid mist. Absorbing SO₃ in sulfuric acid forms oleum safely, avoiding an acid mist.

  3. Why did industry replace mercury cells in chlor-alkali production?
    • Membrane cells avoid toxic mercury pollution and use less energy
    • Mercury cells made no chlorine
    • Membrane cells use more mercury
    • Mercury cells were too cheap
    Show the answer

    Answer: Membrane cells avoid toxic mercury pollution and use less energy. Environmental and efficiency gains drove the change.
