Addition Reactions

Addition reactions break a π bond and form two σ bonds; non-polar, electron-rich C=C bonds undergo electrophilic addition, while polar C=O bonds undergo nucleophilic addition at the δ+ carbon, and additions have high atom economy.

Chemistry · Organic Reaction Mechanisms · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe addition across C=C and C=O bonds
  • Compare electrophilic and nucleophilic addition
  • Explain why the two bonds attract different reagents

Two kinds of π bond

  • C=C is non-polar and electron-rich: it attracts electrophiles.
  • C=O is polar with a δ+ carbon: it attracts nucleophiles.
  • In both, the π bond breaks and two new σ bonds form.
  • Addition turns sp² carbons into sp³ carbons.

Examples

  • Ethene + HBr → bromoethane (electrophilic addition).
  • Ethanal + HCN → 2-hydroxypropanenitrile (nucleophilic addition).
  • Alkenes + H₂ and carbonyls + hydride both add hydrogen.
  • Polymerisation is repeated addition of alkene monomers.

The polarity of the π bond decides which reagents attack. Alkenes add bromine and hydrogen halides, while aldehydes and ketones add cyanide, hydride and Grignard reagents. Addition reactions have high atom economy because all atoms end up in the product.

Worked example

Comparing two additions.

  1. Propene + H₂O (acid catalyst) → propan-2-ol: electrophile H⁺ attacks first.
  2. Propanone + NaBH₄ → propan-2-ol: nucleophile H⁻ attacks first.
  3. Both give the same alcohol.
  4. The first attacking species differs because the π bonds differ.
Common mistakeThinking C=O bonds react like C=C bonds. Their polarity means nucleophiles attack, not electrophiles.

In 3 lines: C=C electrophiles. C=O nucleophiles. High atom economy.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What type of addition occurs when HCN adds to ethanal?
    • Nucleophilic addition to the carbonyl
    • Electrophilic addition to an alkene
    • Free-radical substitution of hydrogen
    • Nucleophilic substitution of a halide
    Show the answer

    Answer: Nucleophilic addition to the carbonyl. Cyanide attacks the δ+ carbonyl carbon, so this is nucleophilic addition.

  2. Why do addition reactions have high atom economy?
    • They always produce water as a waste product
    • All the reactant atoms end up in the single product
    • They need large amounts of catalyst
    • They release carbon dioxide
    Show the answer

    Answer: All the reactant atoms end up in the single product. Nothing is lost when two molecules join into one.

  3. Why do alkenes not react readily with cyanide ions?
    • Cyanide is too strong an electrophile
    • Alkenes have no π electrons
    • The non-polar, electron-rich C=C repels nucleophiles
    • Cyanide only reacts with metals
    Show the answer

    Answer: The non-polar, electron-rich C=C repels nucleophiles. Nucleophiles need an electron-poor site to attack.

Key terms

Addition reaction
Two molecules combine to form one product
Atom economy
Percentage of reactant atoms ending up in the desired product
π bond
A bond formed by sideways overlap of p orbitals
