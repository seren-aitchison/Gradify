Hydrogen Bonding

Hydrogen bonds form between H bonded to N, O or F and a lone pair on N, O or F, giving water anomalous properties such as a high boiling point and less dense ice, and they hold DNA strands together and shape proteins.

Chemistry · Chemical Bonding and Molecular Structure · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • State the conditions for hydrogen bonding
  • Explain the anomalous properties of water
  • Describe the importance of hydrogen bonding in biology

Conditions

  • Hydrogen must be bonded to a highly electronegative atom: N, O or F.
  • This makes hydrogen strongly δ+.
  • It is attracted to a lone pair on N, O or F of another molecule.
  • The three atoms involved are usually in a straight line.

Effects

  • H₂O, NH₃ and HF have unusually high boiling points for their size.
  • Ice is less dense than water: hydrogen bonds hold molecules in an open lattice.
  • Water has high surface tension and a high specific heat capacity.
  • Alcohols and carboxylic acids are soluble in water due to hydrogen bonding.

Hydrogen bonds hold the two strands of DNA together and help fold proteins into shape. Because ice floats, lakes freeze from the top, insulating the water below and allowing aquatic life to survive winter. A hydrogen bond is roughly a tenth as strong as a covalent bond.

Worked example

Group 16 hydrides.

  1. H₂S boils at −60 °C, H₂Se at −41 °C and H₂Te at −2 °C.
  2. Following the trend, H₂O might boil near −80 °C.
  3. It actually boils at 100 °C.
  4. Hydrogen bonding explains this anomaly.
Common mistakeThinking hydrogen bonds are covalent bonds to hydrogen. They are intermolecular attractions between molecules.

In 3 lines: H on N, O or F. Water's anomalies. DNA and proteins.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which molecule can form hydrogen bonds between its own molecules?
    • Methane, CH₄
    • Hydrogen sulfide, H₂S
    • Hydrogen chloride, HCl
    • Ammonia, NH₃
    Show the answer

    Answer: Ammonia, NH₃. NH₃ has hydrogen bonded to nitrogen and a lone pair on nitrogen.

  2. Why does water have a much higher boiling point than H₂S?
    • Water forms hydrogen bonds between molecules
    • H₂S has fewer electrons
    • Water has stronger O–H covalent bonds
    • H₂S forms hydrogen bonds
    Show the answer

    Answer: Water forms hydrogen bonds between molecules. Extra energy is needed to break hydrogen bonds in water.

  3. Why is it important for aquatic life that ice floats?
    • Floating ice makes water warmer than 4 °C
    • Lakes freeze from the top, insulating water below
    • Ice sinking would heat the lake
    • Fish need ice to breathe
    Show the answer

    Answer: Lakes freeze from the top, insulating water below. Surface ice protects the liquid water underneath.

Key terms

Hydrogen bond
An attraction between δ+ H on N, O or F and a lone pair
Anomalous
Different from the expected trend
Specific heat capacity
Energy needed to raise 1 g by 1 °C
