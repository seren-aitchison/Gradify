E and Z Nomenclature

E/Z nomenclature uses CIP priorities on each alkene carbon: higher priorities on the same side give Z and on opposite sides give E, working where cis/trans fails, with Z often but not always matching cis.

Chemistry · Organic Chemistry Foundations · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain the limits of cis/trans naming
  • Assign E or Z using CIP priorities
  • Name alkenes with E/Z descriptors

Why E/Z

  • Cis/trans works when each carbon has one identical group.
  • It fails when all four groups are different.
  • E/Z uses CIP priorities on each alkene carbon.
  • It applies to any alkene showing geometric isomerism.

Assigning E or Z

  • Find the higher-priority group on each carbon of the C=C.
  • If both are on the same side: Z (zusammen, together).
  • If on opposite sides: E (entgegen, opposite).
  • Z is often, but not always, the same as cis.

For 1-bromo-1-chloropropene, cis/trans is ambiguous, but E/Z gives a clear answer. Names include the descriptor at the start, such as (E)-but-2-ene. Fatty acids in natural oils are mostly Z (cis), while trans fats produced by partial hydrogenation are E.

Worked example

Naming CHCl=CBrCH₃.

  1. On carbon 1: Cl outranks H.
  2. On carbon 2: Br outranks CH₃.
  3. If Cl and Br are on the same side, the isomer is Z.
  4. If they are on opposite sides, it is E.
Common mistakeAssuming Z always means cis. The two can differ when priorities do not match the identical groups.

In 3 lines: Cis/trans has limits. CIP on each carbon. Same side Z.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. In an E isomer, where are the higher-priority groups?
    • On opposite sides of the double bond
    • On the same side of the double bond
    • On the same carbon atom
    • Not attached to the double bond
    Show the answer

    Answer: On opposite sides of the double bond. Entgegen means opposite in German, so higher priorities lie on opposite sides.

  2. Why is E/Z naming preferred over cis/trans?
    • It only works for cyclic compounds
    • It works for any alkene, even with four different groups
    • It ignores the groups attached
    • It is used only for alkanes
    Show the answer

    Answer: It works for any alkene, even with four different groups. CIP priorities give an unambiguous result in every case.

  3. Why can a Z isomer be trans?
    • Z and trans always mean exactly the same thing
    • Z isomers do not contain a carbon double bond
    • Priorities may not follow the identical groups used for cis/trans
    • Trans isomers can never be given the label E
    Show the answer

    Answer: Priorities may not follow the identical groups used for cis/trans. Each system looks at different groups, so the labels need not match.

Key terms

Z isomer
Higher-priority groups on the same side
E isomer
Higher-priority groups on opposite sides
Geometric isomers
Isomers due to restricted rotation about C=C
