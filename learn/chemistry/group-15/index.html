Group 15

Group 15 elements have ns²np³ configurations; N₂ is unreactive due to its triple bond, ammonia from the Haber process supplies fertilisers, phosphorus has white and red allotropes, nitrogen oxides cause pollution and metallic character increases down the group.

Chemistry · Periodic Table and Periodicity · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain why nitrogen gas is unreactive
  • Describe important compounds of nitrogen and phosphorus
  • Describe the trend in metallic character down group 15

Nitrogen and phosphorus

  • Outer configuration ns² np³.
  • N₂ has a strong triple bond, making it very unreactive.
  • Ammonia (NH₃), made by the Haber process, is used for fertilisers.
  • Phosphorus exists as white (P₄, very reactive) and red allotropes.

Compounds and trends

  • Nitrogen oxides form in engines and cause acid rain and smog.
  • Phosphoric acid and phosphates are used in fertilisers and foods.
  • Oxidation states range from −3 to +5.
  • Metallic character increases down: nitrogen and phosphorus are non-metals, bismuth is a metal.

Phosphorus does not form triple bonds easily, so it exists as P₄ tetrahedra rather than P₂. White phosphorus ignites in air and is stored under water. Nitrogen and phosphorus are key nutrients in DNA, proteins and ATP.

Worked example

Why nitrogen is inert.

  1. The N≡N triple bond has a bond enthalpy of about 945 kJ mol⁻¹.
  2. This is one of the strongest bonds known.
  3. Breaking it requires a lot of energy.
  4. So the Haber process uses an iron catalyst, high temperature and pressure.
Common mistakeThinking nitrogen is unreactive because it is a noble gas. It is unreactive because of its very strong triple bond.

In 3 lines: Strong N≡N bond. Ammonia and phosphates. Non-metal to metal.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is the main use of ammonia from the Haber process?
    • Making plastics only
    • Fuel for cars
    • Cleaning water
    • Making fertilisers
    Show the answer

    Answer: Making fertilisers. Most ammonia becomes nitrogen fertilisers.

  2. Why does phosphorus form P₄ rather than P₂ molecules?
    • Phosphorus does not form strong triple bonds
    • Phosphorus atoms are too small
    • P₂ is a noble gas
    • P₄ has triple bonds
    Show the answer

    Answer: Phosphorus does not form strong triple bonds. Larger atoms prefer single bonds in a tetrahedron.

  3. Why do nitrogen oxides form in car engines?
    • Petrol contains nitrogen oxides
    • High temperatures allow N₂ and O₂ to react
    • Engines make nitrogen from oxygen
    • Catalytic converters produce them
    Show the answer

    Answer: High temperatures allow N₂ and O₂ to react. Extreme heat in engines breaks the strong N≡N bond so N₂ reacts.

Key terms

Haber process

White phosphorus
