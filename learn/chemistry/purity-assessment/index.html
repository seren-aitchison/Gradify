Purity Assessment

Purity is shown by sharp melting points matching literature, constant boiling points, single spots or peaks in TLC, GC or HPLC, clean NMR spectra and elemental analysis or HRMS agreeing with expected values, combining several methods.

Chemistry · Synthetic Chemistry and Laboratory Methods · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Use melting and boiling points to assess purity
  • Use chromatography to detect impurities
  • Use spectroscopy and elemental analysis to confirm purity

Physical tests

  • A pure solid has a sharp melting point matching the literature value.
  • Impurities lower and broaden the melting range.
  • A mixed melting point with an authentic sample confirms identity.
  • Pure liquids boil at a constant temperature.

Instrumental tests

  • TLC, GC or HPLC show impurity peaks or spots.
  • NMR reveals impurity signals; integration estimates amounts.
  • Elemental analysis compares %C, %H and %N with calculated values.
  • HRMS confirms the molecular formula.

No single test proves purity, so chemists combine methods. HPLC purity is often reported as the area percentage of the main peak. Journals typically require evidence such as NMR spectra and elemental analysis or HPLC for new compounds.

Worked example

Melting point check.

  1. Literature melting point of aspirin: 135 °C.
  2. Crude sample melts at 128–132 °C.
  3. After recrystallisation it melts at 134–135 °C.
  4. The sharper, higher range shows improved purity.
Common mistakeRelying on one test alone. Some impurities do not show up in every method.

In 3 lines: Sharp melting point. Chromatography and NMR. Combine methods.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which observation suggests a pure solid?
    • A broad melting range well below the literature value
    • Several spots on a TLC plate
    • A sharp melting point matching the literature value
    • Extra unassigned peaks in its NMR spectrum
    Show the answer

    Answer: A sharp melting point matching the literature value. Pure solids melt over a narrow range at the expected literature value.

  2. Why does a mixed melting point confirm identity?
    • Mixing always raises the melting point
    • Mixing makes every sample melt sharply
    • It measures molecular mass
    • If the sample matches the standard, the melting point stays sharp and unchanged
    Show the answer

    Answer: If the sample matches the standard, the melting point stays sharp and unchanged. A different compound would act as an impurity.

  3. Why are several purity tests combined?
    • Some impurities are invisible to particular methods
    • One test always detects everything
    • Tests always disagree with each other
    • Combining tests lowers purity
    Show the answer

    Answer: Some impurities are invisible to particular methods. Different methods detect different impurities.

Key terms

Melting range
Temperature range over which a solid melts
Mixed melting point
Melting point of a sample mixed with an authentic standard
Elemental analysis
Measuring percentages of C, H and N in a compound
