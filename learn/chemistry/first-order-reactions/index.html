First-order Reactions

First-order reactions have rate = k[A], straight rate-concentration graphs, exponential concentration decay, k in s⁻¹ and a constant half-life t½ = 0.693 ÷ k, as in radioactive decay and many drug eliminations.

Chemistry · Chemical Kinetics · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the rate equation for first-order reactions
  • Interpret concentration-time and rate-concentration graphs
  • Explain why first-order half-life is constant

First order

  • Rate = k[A].
  • Rate-concentration graph: straight line through the origin with gradient k.
  • Concentration-time graph: exponential decay.
  • Units of k: s⁻¹.

Half-life

  • The half-life is constant: t½ = ln 2 ÷ k ≈ 0.693 ÷ k.
  • Independent of starting concentration.
  • Radioactive decay is a first-order process.
  • Many drug eliminations from the body are first order.

A constant half-life is the signature of a first-order reaction and can be used to identify it from a concentration-time graph. The decomposition of N₂O₅ and hydrolysis of some halogenoalkanes are first-order examples. Carbon-14 dating relies on its constant half-life of 5,730 years.

Worked example

Drug elimination.

  1. A drug has a half-life of 4 hours.
  2. The initial blood level is 80 mg dm⁻³.
  3. After 4 h: 40; after 8 h: 20; after 12 h: 10 mg dm⁻³.
  4. Each 4-hour period halves the concentration.
Common mistakeThinking half-life depends on starting concentration for first-order reactions. It is constant.

In 3 lines: Rate = k[A]. Exponential decay. Constant half-life.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which process is first order?
    • Ammonia decomposing on saturated platinum
    • Alcohol removal by a saturated liver
    • A reaction with rate = k
    • Radioactive decay
    Show the answer

    Answer: Radioactive decay. Decay rate is proportional to the number of nuclei.

  2. How can you identify a first-order reaction from a concentration-time graph?
    • Successive half-lives are equal
    • The graph is a straight line
    • The half-life doubles each time
    • The rate is constant
    Show the answer

    Answer: Successive half-lives are equal. Constant half-life is characteristic of first order.

  3. Why is half-life constant for first-order reactions?
    • Rate depends on concentration squared at all times
    • Rate is proportional to concentration, so the same fraction reacts per unit time
    • Rate depends on concentration squared
    • The reaction stops halfway
    Show the answer

    Answer: Rate is proportional to concentration, so the same fraction reacts per unit time. Proportional rate gives exponential decay with fixed halving time.

Key terms

First order
Rate proportional to concentration
Half-life
Time for concentration to halve
Exponential decay
Decrease by a constant fraction per unit time
