Climate Chemistry

Human activity moves fossil carbon rapidly into the atmosphere, shown by isotope and ice-core evidence; feedbacks such as water vapour and permafrost thaw amplify warming and mitigation includes renewable energy, efficiency and carbon capture.

Chemistry · Materials, Environmental and Industrial Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the carbon cycle and human disruption
  • Explain climate feedbacks involving chemistry
  • Describe chemical approaches to mitigation

Carbon cycle and evidence

  • Carbon moves between atmosphere, oceans, biosphere and rocks.
  • Burning fossil fuels moves carbon from rocks to the atmosphere quickly.
  • Isotope evidence: fossil carbon lacks ¹⁴C and is depleted in ¹³C.
  • Ice cores record past CO₂ and temperature.

Feedbacks and mitigation

  • Water vapour feedback: warmer air holds more water, amplifying warming.
  • Permafrost thaw releases CH₄ and CO₂.
  • Mitigation: renewable energy, efficiency, carbon capture and storage.
  • Aerosols reflect sunlight, partly masking warming.

Changing isotope ratios in atmospheric CO₂ show that the extra carbon comes from fossil fuels. Carbon capture can trap CO₂ from power stations using amine solutions, then store it underground. Removing CO₂ directly from air is being developed but is energy intensive.

Worked example

Isotope fingerprint.

  1. Fossil fuels formed millions of years ago, so they contain no ¹⁴C.
  2. Plants favour ¹²C, so fossil carbon is also depleted in ¹³C.
  3. Atmospheric CO₂'s ¹⁴C and ¹³C fractions are falling.
  4. This points to fossil fuel combustion as the source.
Common mistakeThinking the rise in CO₂ could be natural. Isotope evidence identifies fossil fuels as the source.

In 3 lines: Fossil carbon released. Feedbacks amplify. Mitigation options.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which feedback amplifies warming as air warms?
    • Water vapour feedback
    • Aerosol cooling
    • Increased ice reflection
    • Ocean CO₂ uptake
    Show the answer

    Answer: Water vapour feedback. Warmer air holds more water vapour, a greenhouse gas.

  2. How do ice cores provide climate evidence?
    • They measure ocean pH directly from the ice
    • Trapped air bubbles record past atmospheric composition
    • They show only modern temperatures, not past
    • Ice contains no trapped gases at all
    Show the answer

    Answer: Trapped air bubbles record past atmospheric composition. Layers of ice preserve small samples of ancient air in bubbles.

  3. Why is falling ¹³C in atmospheric CO₂ evidence for fossil fuel burning?
    • Volcanoes emit carbon rich in ¹³C only
    • Oceans remove only ¹²C
    • Fossil carbon came from plants that favoured ¹²C
    • Isotopes do not vary in nature
    Show the answer

    Answer: Fossil carbon came from plants that favoured ¹²C. The isotopic signature matches ancient plant material.

Key terms

Carbon capture and storage
Trapping CO₂ and storing it underground
Feedback
A process that amplifies or reduces a change
Suess effect
Dilution of atmospheric ¹⁴C by fossil carbon
