Hydrocarbons

Hydrocarbons contain only carbon and hydrogen and include alkanes, alkenes, alkynes and arenes; crude oil is separated by fractional distillation and cracked into useful products, and combustion gives CO₂ and water or, when incomplete, CO and soot.

Chemistry · Organic Chemistry Foundations · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define hydrocarbons and their main classes
  • Describe crude oil as a source of hydrocarbons
  • Describe the combustion of hydrocarbons

Classes

  • Alkanes: single bonds only, saturated, CₙH₂ₙ₊₂.
  • Alkenes: at least one C=C double bond, CₙH₂ₙ.
  • Alkynes: at least one C≡C triple bond.
  • Arenes: aromatic rings such as benzene.

Sources and use

  • Crude oil is a mixture of hydrocarbons separated by fractional distillation.
  • Cracking breaks long alkanes into shorter alkanes and alkenes.
  • Complete combustion gives CO₂ and water.
  • Incomplete combustion gives CO and soot.

Hydrocarbons supply most of the world's fuels and the feedstocks for plastics. Burning them releases carbon dioxide, a greenhouse gas, so alternatives and carbon capture are being developed. Saturated hydrocarbons have only single bonds; unsaturated ones have multiple bonds.

Worked example

Fractional distillation.

  1. Crude oil is heated and vaporised.
  2. Vapours rise up a column that is cooler at the top.
  3. Fractions condense at different heights by boiling point.
  4. Small molecules such as LPG leave at the top; bitumen at the bottom.
Common mistakeThinking any compound with carbon and hydrogen is a hydrocarbon. Hydrocarbons contain only those two elements.

In 3 lines: Four classes. Crude oil and cracking. Combustion.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which compound is a hydrocarbon?
    • Ethanol, C₂H₅OH
    • Chloromethane, CH₃Cl
    • Ethanoic acid, CH₃COOH
    • Propane, C₃H₈
    Show the answer

    Answer: Propane, C₃H₈. Propane contains only carbon and hydrogen atoms.

  2. Why do crude oil fractions separate in a fractionating column?
    • Fractions with different boiling points condense at different heights
    • Heavier molecules rise to the top of the column
    • All fractions boil at exactly the same temperature
    • The column filters molecules by colour
    Show the answer

    Answer: Fractions with different boiling points condense at different heights. The column is hotter at the bottom, so fractions condense by boiling point.

  3. Why is incomplete combustion of hydrocarbons dangerous?
    • It produces only water and oxygen
    • It produces toxic carbon monoxide and soot
    • It releases more energy than complete combustion
    • It forms nitrogen gas only
    Show the answer

    Answer: It produces toxic carbon monoxide and soot. Too little oxygen gives CO, which blocks oxygen transport in blood.

Key terms

Hydrocarbon
A compound of carbon and hydrogen only
Saturated
Containing only single bonds
Cracking
Breaking long hydrocarbons into shorter ones
