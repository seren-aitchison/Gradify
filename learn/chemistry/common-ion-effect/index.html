Common-ion Effect

Adding a common ion shifts solubility or ionisation equilibria towards the solid or un-ionised form while Ksp is unchanged, greatly reducing solubility, which is used in analysis, purification and water treatment.

Chemistry · Acids, Bases and Aqueous Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain the common-ion effect using Le Chatelier's principle
  • Calculate solubility in the presence of a common ion
  • Describe applications of the common-ion effect

The effect

  • Adding a common ion shifts the equilibrium towards the solid or un-ionised form.
  • AgCl is less soluble in NaCl solution than in pure water.
  • Weak acid ionisation is suppressed by adding its conjugate base, which is the basis of buffers.
  • Ksp stays the same.

Calculations and uses

  • In 0.10 mol dm⁻³ NaCl, [Cl⁻] ≈ 0.10, so s(AgCl) = Ksp ÷ 0.10.
  • Used to precipitate salts more completely in gravimetric analysis.
  • Used to purify sodium chloride by adding HCl gas.
  • Explains reduced solubility of some drugs.

The common-ion effect follows directly from Le Chatelier's principle and from Ksp: if one ion concentration is fixed high, the other must be low. It is used in water softening and in removing toxic metal ions from waste water.

Worked example

AgCl in salt water.

  1. In pure water, s(AgCl) = √(1.8 × 10⁻¹⁰) = 1.3 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³.
  2. In 0.10 mol dm⁻³ NaCl, [Cl⁻] ≈ 0.10.
  3. s = 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁰ ÷ 0.10 = 1.8 × 10⁻⁹ mol dm⁻³.
  4. Solubility falls by about 7,000 times.
Common mistakeThinking adding a common ion changes Ksp. Ksp stays constant; solubility changes.

In 3 lines: Shifts towards solid. Ksp unchanged. Analysis and purification.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. In which liquid is AgCl least soluble?
    • Pure water
    • Dilute nitric acid
    • 0.10 mol dm⁻³ NaCl solution
    • Ethanol-water mixture
    Show the answer

    Answer: 0.10 mol dm⁻³ NaCl solution. Extra chloride pushes the equilibrium towards solid AgCl.

  2. How does adding sodium ethanoate affect ethanoic acid ionisation?
    • It increases ionisation
    • It has no effect
    • It converts the acid to a strong acid
    • It suppresses ionisation by adding ethanoate
    Show the answer

    Answer: It suppresses ionisation by adding ethanoate. The common ion shifts the equilibrium left.

  3. Why is excess precipitating reagent used in gravimetric analysis?
    • The common-ion effect makes precipitation more complete
    • Excess reagent increases solubility
    • It changes Ksp
    • It dissolves the precipitate
    Show the answer

    Answer: The common-ion effect makes precipitation more complete. Lower solubility leaves less product in solution.

Key terms
