Group 16

Group 16 elements have ns²np⁴ configurations and often form 2− ions; oxygen forms O₂ and ozone, sulfur forms S₈ and many oxidation states, sulfur dioxide causes acid rain, sulfuric acid is made by the Contact process and metallic character increases down the group.

Chemistry · Periodic Table and Periodicity · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Compare the structures and properties of oxygen and sulfur
  • Describe important compounds of oxygen and sulfur
  • Describe trends down group 16

Oxygen and sulfur

  • Outer configuration ns² np⁴; they often gain two electrons to form 2− ions.
  • Oxygen exists as O₂ and ozone, O₃.
  • Sulfur exists as S₈ rings and shows oxidation states from −2 to +6.
  • Oxygen is the second most electronegative element.

Compounds and trends

  • Sulfur dioxide forms from burning fossil fuels and causes acid rain.
  • Sulfuric acid, made by the Contact process, is a major industrial chemical.
  • Ozone in the stratosphere absorbs harmful UV radiation.
  • Metallic character increases down: oxygen and sulfur are non-metals, polonium is a metal.

Oxygen can only expand its bonding to two bonds in most compounds, but sulfur can use more orbitals and form compounds such as SF₆. Hydrogen sulfide smells of rotten eggs and is toxic. Desulfurisation of fuels and flue gases has greatly reduced UK sulfur dioxide emissions.

Worked example

Making sulfuric acid.

  1. Sulfur is burned to make SO₂.
  2. SO₂ is oxidised to SO₃ using a vanadium(V) oxide catalyst.
  3. SO₃ is absorbed in concentrated sulfuric acid.
  4. Water is then added to produce more sulfuric acid.
Common mistakeThinking ozone is always helpful. Stratospheric ozone protects from UV, but ground-level ozone is a pollutant.

In 3 lines: ns²np⁴ and 2− ions. Oxygen and sulfur compounds. Non-metal to metal.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What ion do group 16 elements usually form?
    • A 2+ ion
    • A 1− ion
    • A 2− ion
    • A 3+ ion
    Show the answer

    Answer: A 2− ion. Gaining two electrons completes the outer shell.

  2. Why can sulfur form SF₆ while oxygen cannot form OF₆?
    • Oxygen has more electrons
    • Sulfur is a metal
    • Fluorine does not bond to oxygen
    • Sulfur is larger and can accommodate more bonds
    Show the answer

    Answer: Sulfur is larger and can accommodate more bonds. Larger atoms can bond to more neighbours.

  3. Why did UK sulfur dioxide emissions fall sharply after 1970?
    • Coal use fell and flue gases and fuels were desulfurised
    • More coal was burned
    • Sulfur stopped being found in fuels naturally
    • SO₂ became a greenhouse gas
    Show the answer

    Answer: Coal use fell and flue gases and fuels were desulfurised. Cleaner fuels and scrubbers removed sulfur.
