Matter and Its Classification

Pure substances (elements and compounds) have fixed compositions; mixtures don't, and their parts aren't chemically joined.

Chemistry · Foundations of Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Classify matter as elements, compounds or mixtures
  • Distinguish homogeneous from heterogeneous mixtures
  • Draw particle diagrams for each category

Pure substances and mixtures

  • Pure substance: has a fixed composition and fixed properties, such as a sharp melting point.
  • Element: contains only one type of atom (iron, oxygen gas O₂).
  • Compound: two or more elements chemically combined in a fixed ratio (water H₂O, sodium chloride NaCl).
  • Mixture: two or more substances not chemically combined; the composition can vary and the parts keep their own properties (air, sea water).

Kinds of mixture

A homogeneous mixture has the same composition throughout (a solution such as salt water, or an alloy such as brass). A heterogeneous mixture has visibly different parts (sand in water, granite).

Particle diagrams

In diagrams, each circle is an atom. An element is drawn as identical circles (single atoms, or identical molecules such as O₂ pairs). A compound shows identical groups containing different atoms joined together. A mixture shows two or more different kinds of particle not joined to each other.

Worked example

Classify: (a) bronze, (b) carbon dioxide, (c) nitrogen gas, (d) orange juice.

  1. (a) Bronze: a homogeneous mixture (alloy) of copper and tin.
  2. (b) Carbon dioxide: a compound, CO₂, carbon and oxygen chemically combined.
  3. (c) Nitrogen gas: an element made of N₂ molecules.
  4. (d) Orange juice: a heterogeneous mixture (it contains pulp and settles).
Common mistakeCalling O₂ a compound because it contains two atoms. Both atoms are oxygen, so it is an element made of molecules.

In 2 lines: Matter is pure (elements, compounds) or a mixture (homogeneous or heterogeneous). Molecules can be elements (O₂) or compounds (H₂O).

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which is a compound?
    • Sodium chloride
    • Chlorine gas, Cl₂
    • Brass (an alloy)
    • Salty sea water
    Show the answer

    Answer: Sodium chloride. NaCl contains two elements chemically combined; O₂ is an element, and brass and sea water are mixtures.

  2. A white solid melts between 120 and 128 °C. What does this suggest?
    • It is pure: a compound melts over a range
    • It is impure: a mixture melts over a range
    • It is a pure element with two melting points
    • It is pure, and the range is just measuring error
    Show the answer

    Answer: It is impure: a mixture melts over a range. Pure substances melt at a sharp, fixed temperature; a melting range indicates impurities.

  3. Which particle diagram describes a mixture of an element and a compound?
    • Only groups of two different atoms joined together, every group the same, with no separate pairs of identical atoms
    • Some single identical atoms and some single atoms of another element, mixed together but none joined to another
    • Some pairs of identical atoms and some groups of two different atoms joined together, not linked to each other
    • Some pairs of identical atoms and some single atoms of a different element, all mixed but none chemically joined
    Show the answer

    Answer: Some pairs of identical atoms and some groups of two different atoms joined together, not linked to each other. Pairs of identical atoms are an element's molecules, groups of different joined atoms are a compound, and the two kinds being separate makes a mixture.

Key terms

Pure substance
A single element or compound with a fixed composition and properties
Homogeneous mixture
A mixture with uniform composition throughout, such as a solution
Heterogeneous mixture
A mixture whose composition varies, with visibly different parts
