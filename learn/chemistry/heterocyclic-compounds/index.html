Heterocyclic Compounds

Heterocycles contain ring atoms other than carbon; pyridine is aromatic and basic, pyrrole is aromatic but not basic because its lone pair is in the π system and heterocycles are central to DNA, vitamins and most medicines.

Chemistry · Functional Group Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define heterocyclic compounds
  • Describe aromatic heterocycles such as pyridine and pyrrole
  • Explain the importance of heterocycles in biology and medicine

Key heterocycles

  • Pyridine: a six-membered ring with one N; aromatic and weakly basic.
  • Pyrrole: a five-membered ring with NH; aromatic but not basic.
  • Furan (O) and thiophene (S) are five-membered aromatic heterocycles.
  • Saturated heterocycles include piperidine and tetrahydrofuran.

Basicity and importance

  • Pyridine's lone pair is in the ring plane, so it is available for H⁺.
  • Pyrrole's lone pair is part of the aromatic sextet, so it is not basic.
  • DNA bases are purines and pyrimidines.
  • Most drugs contain at least one heterocycle.

Heterocycles appear in vitamins, chlorophyll, haem, caffeine and nicotine. Their nitrogen atoms form hydrogen bonds that help drugs bind to target proteins. Pyridine is used as a solvent and base in synthesis.

Worked example

Comparing pyridine and pyrrole.

  1. Both are aromatic with six π electrons.
  2. In pyridine, N contributes one π electron; its lone pair points outward.
  3. In pyrrole, N contributes its lone pair to the π system.
  4. So pyridine is basic and pyrrole is not.
Common mistakeAssuming every nitrogen heterocycle is basic. In pyrrole, the lone pair is needed for aromaticity.

In 3 lines: Rings with N, O, S. Pyridine versus pyrrole. DNA and drugs.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which heterocycle contains oxygen in a five-membered aromatic ring?
    • Pyridine
    • Pyrrole
    • Furan
    • Thiophene
    Show the answer

    Answer: Furan. Furan's five-membered aromatic ring contains one oxygen atom.

  2. Why is pyrrole not basic?
    • It has no nitrogen atom
    • It is a six-membered ring
    • Its lone pair points out of the ring
    • Its nitrogen lone pair is part of the aromatic π system
    Show the answer

    Answer: Its nitrogen lone pair is part of the aromatic π system. Protonating it would destroy aromaticity.

  3. Why do many drugs contain nitrogen heterocycles?
    • Ring nitrogens form hydrogen bonds that help binding to target proteins
    • Heterocycles make drugs completely insoluble
    • Ring nitrogen atoms prevent any binding to proteins
    • Heterocycles contain no polar atoms at all
    Show the answer

    Answer: Ring nitrogens form hydrogen bonds that help binding to target proteins. Hydrogen bonding and shape aid specific recognition.

Key terms

Heterocycle
A ring containing at least one non-carbon atom
Pyridine
A six-membered aromatic ring with one nitrogen
Pyrrole
A five-membered aromatic ring with an NH group
