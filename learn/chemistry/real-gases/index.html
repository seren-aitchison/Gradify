Real Gases

The ideal model assumes negligible particle volume, no intermolecular forces and elastic collisions; real gases deviate at high pressure and low temperature, measured by the compressibility factor and modelled by the van der Waals equation.

Chemistry · States of Matter and Physical Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • State the assumptions of the ideal gas model
  • Explain when and why real gases deviate from ideal behaviour
  • Describe the van der Waals equation

Ideal assumptions

  • Gas particles have negligible volume.
  • There are no intermolecular forces between particles.
  • Collisions are perfectly elastic.
  • Particles move randomly in straight lines.

Deviations

  • Real gases deviate at high pressure and low temperature.
  • At high pressure, particle volume becomes significant compared with the container.
  • At low temperature, intermolecular attractions reduce pressure and can cause condensation.
  • The compressibility factor Z = pV/nRT equals 1 for an ideal gas.

The van der Waals equation corrects for particle volume and attraction: (p + an²/V²)(V − nb) = nRT, where a reflects attraction and b particle volume. Gases with strong intermolecular forces, such as ammonia, deviate more than helium. Real gases behave most ideally at low pressure and high temperature.

Worked example

Ammonia versus helium.

  1. At high pressure, helium's Z stays close to 1.
  2. Ammonia's Z falls below 1 at moderate pressures.
  3. Ammonia molecules attract each other through hydrogen bonds.
  4. These attractions make ammonia occupy less volume than predicted.
Common mistakeThinking real gases always behave ideally. Deviations are significant at high pressure and low temperature.

In 3 lines: Ideal assumptions. High pressure and low temperature deviate. Van der Waals corrections.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is the compressibility factor of an ideal gas?
    • 0
    • 8.314
    • 1
    • 22.4
    Show the answer

    Answer: 1. Z = pV/nRT equals exactly one when the gas is ideal.

  2. Why do real gases deviate at low temperature?
    • Particles become larger as they cool down
    • Collisions become more elastic when cold
    • Particle volume disappears at low temperature
    • Intermolecular attractions become significant as particles slow
    Show the answer

    Answer: Intermolecular attractions become significant as particles slow. Slower particles are held by attractions, lowering pressure.

  3. Why does ammonia deviate from ideal behaviour more than helium?
    • Ammonia has much stronger intermolecular forces
    • Helium has a larger molecular volume
    • Ammonia molecules are perfectly elastic
    • Helium forms hydrogen bonds
    Show the answer

    Answer: Ammonia has much stronger intermolecular forces. Stronger attractions make ammonia less ideal.

Key terms

Ideal gas
A gas obeying pV = nRT exactly
Compressibility factor
Z = pV/nRT
Van der Waals equation
A gas equation correcting for size and attraction
