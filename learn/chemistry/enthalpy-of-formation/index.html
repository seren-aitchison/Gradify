Enthalpy of Formation

Standard enthalpy of formation is the enthalpy change forming one mole of a compound from its elements in standard states, zero for elements; ΔrH⦵ = ΣΔfH⦵(products) − ΣΔfH⦵(reactants), using coefficients.

Chemistry · Chemical Energetics and Thermodynamics · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define standard enthalpy of formation
  • Write equations representing enthalpies of formation
  • Calculate enthalpy of reaction from enthalpies of formation

Definition

  • ΔfH⦵: enthalpy change when one mole of a compound forms from its elements in their standard states under standard conditions.
  • For elements in standard states, ΔfH⦵ = 0.
  • Example: C(s) + 2H₂(g) → CH₄(g), ΔfH⦵ = −75 kJ mol⁻¹.
  • Fractions may be needed to make exactly one mole.

Using values

  • ΔrH⦵ = ΣΔfH⦵(products) − ΣΔfH⦵(reactants).
  • Multiply each value by its coefficient in the equation.
  • This is an application of Hess's law.
  • Negative ΔfH⦵ usually indicates an energetically stable compound.

Many formation reactions cannot be carried out directly, such as forming methane from carbon and hydrogen, so values are found indirectly from combustion data. Tables of ΔfH⦵ allow enthalpy changes of thousands of reactions to be calculated.

Worked example

Calculating ΔrH⦵.

  1. CH₄(g) + 2O₂(g) → CO₂(g) + 2H₂O(l).
  2. ΔfH⦵: CH₄ −75, CO₂ −394, H₂O(l) −286, O₂ 0 kJ mol⁻¹.
  3. Products: −394 + 2(−286) = −966; reactants: −75.
  4. ΔrH⦵ = −966 − (−75) = −891 kJ mol⁻¹.
Common mistakeForgetting to multiply ΔfH⦵ by the coefficients. Each value must be multiplied by its number of moles.

In 3 lines: One mole from elements. Elements are zero. Products minus reactants.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What is the standard enthalpy of formation of an element in its standard state?
    • Positive
    • Zero
    • Negative
    • It cannot be defined
    Show the answer

    Answer: Zero. No change occurs in forming an element from itself.

  2. Why are fractions sometimes used in formation equations?
    • Fractions make values smaller
    • Elements cannot be whole numbers
    • The definition requires exactly one mole of product
    • It shows the reaction is slow
    Show the answer

    Answer: The definition requires exactly one mole of product. One mole of compound may need half a mole of O₂.

  3. Why are many enthalpies of formation found indirectly?
    • Direct measurements are always more accurate
    • Elements never react together
    • They are calculated from bond lengths
    • Many formation reactions cannot be carried out directly
    Show the answer

    Answer: Many formation reactions cannot be carried out directly. Hess's law lets chemists use combustion data instead of direct measurement.
