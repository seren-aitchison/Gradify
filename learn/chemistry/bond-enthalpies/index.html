Bond Enthalpies

Bond enthalpy calculations give approximate values because mean values are averages and apply only to gases, whereas formation data are exact for each compound; the bond enthalpy equation can be rearranged to estimate unknown bond enthalpies.

Chemistry · Chemical Energetics and Thermodynamics · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Compare mean bond enthalpy calculations with data-based values
  • Explain sources of discrepancy
  • Find a bond enthalpy from enthalpy of reaction data

Two methods compared

  • Bond enthalpy method: ΔH ≈ Σ(bonds broken) − Σ(bonds made).
  • Formation data method: ΔH = ΣΔfH(products) − ΣΔfH(reactants).
  • Formation data are more accurate because they are measured for the exact compounds.
  • Bond enthalpy values only apply to gaseous species.

Discrepancies and back-calculation

  • Mean bond enthalpies are averages over many molecules.
  • Liquids and solids require extra energy for changes of state, not included in bond calculations.
  • Rearranging the bond enthalpy equation allows an unknown bond enthalpy to be estimated.
  • Such estimates show how bond strength varies with environment.

For methane combustion, bond enthalpies give about −818 kJ mol⁻¹ but the accepted value is −891 kJ mol⁻¹. Part of the difference is that water is formed as a liquid, releasing extra energy on condensation. The C–H bond in methane differs slightly from the mean C–H value.

Worked example

Finding an unknown bond enthalpy.

  1. H₂(g) + F₂(g) → 2HF(g), ΔH = −542 kJ.
  2. H–H = 436 and F–F = 158 kJ mol⁻¹.
  3. −542 = (436 + 158) − 2x.
  4. 2x = 1,136, so H–F ≈ 568 kJ mol⁻¹.
Common mistakeUsing bond enthalpies for reactions involving liquids without adjustment. Bond enthalpies apply to gaseous species.

In 3 lines: Two methods. Averages and states cause error. Back-calculate bonds.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which method gives more accurate enthalpies of reaction?
    • Using enthalpies of formation
    • Using mean bond enthalpies
    • Guessing from bond lengths
    • Using melting points
    Show the answer

    Answer: Using enthalpies of formation. Formation data are specific to each compound.

  2. Why does the bond enthalpy estimate for methane combustion differ from −891 kJ mol⁻¹?
    • The reaction does not occur
    • Water forms as a liquid and mean values are averages
    • Methane has no C–H bonds
    • Oxygen bonds have no energy
    Show the answer

    Answer: Water forms as a liquid and mean values are averages. Both condensation energy and averaging cause the gap.

  3. What does back-calculating a bond enthalpy reveal?
    • The mean value over all molecules
    • The rate of the reaction
    • The strength of that bond in a particular molecule
    • The activation energy
    Show the answer

    Answer: The strength of that bond in a particular molecule. Specific values show how environment changes bond strength.

Key terms

Mean bond enthalpy
Average bond enthalpy across many compounds
Discrepancy
A difference between calculated and accepted values
Gaseous state
The state required for bond enthalpy calculations
