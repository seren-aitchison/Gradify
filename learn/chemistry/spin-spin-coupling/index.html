Spin-spin Coupling

Spin-spin coupling splits a signal into n + 1 peaks for n equivalent neighbours, with intensities from Pascal's triangle; coupled protons share the same coupling constant J, which helps link signals and distinguish isomers.

Chemistry · Analytical Chemistry and Spectroscopy · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Apply the n + 1 rule to predict splitting
  • Explain coupling constants
  • Use splitting patterns to deduce structure

The n + 1 rule

  • A proton with n equivalent neighbours on adjacent carbons is split into n + 1 peaks.
  • 0 → singlet, 1 → doublet, 2 → triplet, 3 → quartet.
  • Peak intensities follow Pascal's triangle: triplet 1:2:1, quartet 1:3:3:1.
  • Equivalent protons do not split each other.

Coupling constants

  • The spacing between peaks is the coupling constant J, in Hz.
  • Coupled protons share the same J.
  • J does not depend on magnet strength.
  • O–H protons often appear as singlets due to fast exchange.

An ethyl group gives a classic triplet-quartet pattern. Matching J values show which signals are coupled to each other. In alkenes, trans couplings (about 15 Hz) are larger than cis couplings (about 10 Hz), helping distinguish E and Z isomers.

Worked example

Splitting in 1-chloropropane.

  1. CH₃ has 2 neighbours: triplet.
  2. Middle CH₂ has 5 neighbours: sextet.
  3. CH₂Cl has 2 neighbours: triplet.
  4. Matching J values link the signals.
Common mistakeCounting hydrogens on the same carbon as neighbours. Only hydrogens on adjacent carbons cause splitting.

In 3 lines: n + 1 peaks. Pascal's triangle. Matching J.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What are the relative intensities of a triplet?
    • 1 : 1 : 1
    • 1 : 3 : 3 : 1
    • 1 : 2 : 1
    • 1 : 4 : 6 : 4 : 1
    Show the answer

    Answer: 1 : 2 : 1. Pascal's triangle gives the intensities 1 : 2 : 1 for a triplet.

  2. Why do two coupled signals share the same coupling constant?
    • Each signal has a different J by definition
    • J depends only on the solvent
    • Coupling constants change with magnet strength
    • The interaction between the two sets of protons is mutual
    Show the answer

    Answer: The interaction between the two sets of protons is mutual. Each set splits the other by the same amount.

  3. How can coupling constants distinguish E and Z alkenes?
    • Trans couplings are larger (about 15 Hz) than cis (about 10 Hz)
    • E and Z isomers always have identical J
    • Only Z isomers show coupling
    • Coupling constants depend only on mass
    Show the answer

    Answer: Trans couplings are larger (about 15 Hz) than cis (about 10 Hz). Geometry changes the through-bond interaction.
