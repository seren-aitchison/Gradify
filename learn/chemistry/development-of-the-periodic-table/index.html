Development of the Periodic Table

Early classifications included Döbereiner's triads and Newlands' octaves; Mendeleev's 1869 table grouped elements by properties, left gaps and swapped orders where needed, and his successful predictions confirmed it.

Chemistry · Periodic Table and Periodicity · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe early attempts to classify the elements
  • Explain how Mendeleev arranged his periodic table
  • Explain why Mendeleev's predictions supported his table

Early ideas

  • Döbereiner's triads (1829): groups of three similar elements, such as Cl, Br and I, where the middle mass is about the average.
  • Newlands' law of octaves (1865): similar properties every eighth element, which failed beyond calcium.
  • These attempts arranged elements by atomic mass.
  • Many elements were still undiscovered.

Mendeleev

  • In 1869 Dmitri Mendeleev arranged elements by atomic mass and grouped those with similar properties.
  • He left gaps for undiscovered elements.
  • He sometimes swapped the order where properties demanded it, such as iodine and tellurium.
  • He predicted properties of missing elements, such as "eka-silicon".

When gallium (1875), scandium (1879) and germanium (1886) were discovered, their properties closely matched Mendeleev's predictions, which convinced chemists his table was correct. The noble gases, discovered in the 1890s, fitted in as a new group. The later discovery of atomic number explained why his order swaps were right.

Worked example

Predicting germanium.

  1. Mendeleev predicted eka-silicon would have a relative atomic mass of about 72.
  2. He predicted a density of about 5.5 g cm⁻³ and an oxide EO₂.
  3. Germanium, found in 1886, has a mass of 72.6 and density 5.3 g cm⁻³.
  4. Its oxide is GeO₂, matching the prediction closely.
Common mistakeThinking Mendeleev ordered elements strictly by atomic mass. He broke mass order when properties required it.

In 3 lines: Triads and octaves came first. Mendeleev left gaps. Predictions confirmed the table.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Who published a periodic table with gaps for undiscovered elements in 1869?
    • John Newlands
    • Johann Döbereiner
    • Henry Moseley
    • Dmitri Mendeleev
    Show the answer

    Answer: Dmitri Mendeleev. Mendeleev's gaps let him predict new elements.

  2. Why did Mendeleev place tellurium before iodine despite its greater mass?
    • Their properties matched the groups better in that order
    • Tellurium was discovered later
    • He made an error in the masses
    • Iodine is a metal
    Show the answer

    Answer: Their properties matched the groups better in that order. Grouping by properties was more important than strict mass order.

  3. Why did the discovery of germanium support Mendeleev's table?
    • It disproved all his predictions
    • Its properties closely matched his predictions for eka-silicon
    • It had no properties in common with silicon
    • It was already known in 1869
    Show the answer

    Answer: Its properties closely matched his predictions for eka-silicon. Successful predictions are strong evidence for a theory.

Key terms

Periodic table
An arrangement of elements showing repeating properties
Triad
A group of three elements with similar properties
Eka-silicon
Mendeleev's name for the predicted element germanium
