Nuclear Magnetic Resonance

NMR uses nuclei with spin, which align with or against a magnetic field and absorb radio waves at resonance; shielding by electrons changes the resonance frequency, giving structural information, with TMS as reference and deuterated solvents.

Chemistry · Analytical Chemistry and Spectroscopy · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain nuclear spin and its behaviour in a magnetic field
  • Describe how an NMR spectrum is obtained
  • Explain why NMR gives structural information

Principles

  • Nuclei with odd mass number, such as ¹H and ¹³C, have spin.
  • In a magnetic field, spins align with or against the field, at different energies.
  • Radio waves of the right frequency flip spins: resonance.
  • The energy gap depends on the field strength and the nucleus's environment.

Obtaining spectra

  • Samples are dissolved in deuterated solvents such as CDCl₃.
  • TMS (tetramethylsilane) is the reference at δ = 0.
  • Modern instruments use pulses and Fourier transformation.
  • Stronger magnets give better resolution.

Electrons around a nucleus shield it from the applied field, so nuclei in different environments resonate at slightly different frequencies. NMR is the main tool for determining organic structures, and the same physics underlies MRI scanning in medicine.

Worked example

Why TMS is the reference.

  1. TMS, Si(CH₃)₄, has 12 identical hydrogens.
  2. It gives one strong signal.
  3. Its protons are highly shielded, so the signal is upfield of most others.
  4. It is inert and volatile, so it is easily removed.
Common mistakeThinking all nuclei are NMR active. Nuclei such as ¹²C and ¹⁶O have no spin.

In 3 lines: Spin in a field. Radio waves flip spins. Environment shifts frequency.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which compound is used as the NMR reference?
    • Water
    • Benzene
    • Tetramethylsilane, TMS
    • Ethanol
    Show the answer

    Answer: Tetramethylsilane, TMS. TMS gives a single strong signal defined as δ = 0.

  2. Why do nuclei in different environments resonate at different frequencies?
    • All nuclei experience exactly the same field
    • The magnet changes strength for each nucleus
    • Different nuclei have different masses only
    • Surrounding electrons shield them to different extents
    Show the answer

    Answer: Surrounding electrons shield them to different extents. Shielding changes the effective field felt by each nucleus.

  3. Why do stronger magnets give better NMR spectra?
    • They increase the energy gap and spread out signals for clearer resolution
    • They remove all electron shielding from the nuclei
    • They make every nucleus resonate identically
    • They only work for nuclei such as ¹²C
    Show the answer

    Answer: They increase the energy gap and spread out signals for clearer resolution. Larger fields improve both sensitivity and separation.

Key terms

Nuclear spin
A property of some nuclei making them behave like tiny magnets
Resonance
Absorption of radio waves that flips nuclear spin
Shielding
Reduction of the field felt by a nucleus due to electrons
