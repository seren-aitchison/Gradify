Bond Enthalpy

Bond enthalpy is the energy to break a mole of bonds in the gas phase; mean values are averages, multiple and shorter bonds are stronger, and ΔH ≈ bonds broken − bonds made, giving approximate values.

Chemistry · Chemical Bonding and Molecular Structure · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define bond enthalpy and mean bond enthalpy
  • Relate bond enthalpy to bond length and multiplicity
  • Calculate enthalpy changes from bond enthalpies

Definitions

  • Bond enthalpy is the energy needed to break one mole of a bond in gaseous molecules.
  • Bond breaking is endothermic; bond making is exothermic.
  • Mean bond enthalpy is averaged over many compounds.
  • Triple bonds > double bonds > single bonds in strength.

Calculations

  • ΔH = Σ(bonds broken) − Σ(bonds made).
  • Results are approximate because mean values are used.
  • Calculations require all species to be gaseous.
  • Shorter bonds are generally stronger.

For example, C–C is 347 kJ mol⁻¹, C=C 612 kJ mol⁻¹ and C≡C 838 kJ mol⁻¹. Calculated values differ from experimental values because mean bond enthalpies are averages and substances may not be gaseous. Bond enthalpies help explain why fuels release energy.

Worked example

Combustion of methane.

  1. CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O.
  2. Broken: 4 C–H (4 × 413) + 2 O=O (2 × 498) = 2,648 kJ.
  3. Made: 2 C=O (2 × 805) + 4 O–H (4 × 464) = 3,466 kJ.
  4. ΔH = 2,648 − 3,466 = −818 kJ mol⁻¹.
Common mistakeSubtracting bonds broken from bonds made. ΔH = bonds broken − bonds made.

In 3 lines: Breaking needs energy. Multiple bonds stronger. Broken minus made.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Is bond breaking endothermic or exothermic?
    • Exothermic
    • Neither
    • Endothermic
    • Both at once
    Show the answer

    Answer: Endothermic. Energy must be supplied to separate bonded atoms.

  2. Why is C≡C stronger than C=C?
    • It is longer than a double bond
    • It has fewer electrons
    • Triple bonds are ionic
    • More shared electron pairs hold the atoms more strongly
    Show the answer

    Answer: More shared electron pairs hold the atoms more strongly. Three shared pairs give a shorter, stronger bond.

  3. Why do fuels release energy when they burn?
    • Bonds made in the products release more energy than bonds broken
    • Breaking bonds releases energy
    • Fuels store heat directly
    • Oxygen contains free energy
    Show the answer

    Answer: Bonds made in the products release more energy than bonds broken. Strong C=O and O–H bonds release more energy than is used.

Key terms

Mean bond enthalpy

Endothermic
