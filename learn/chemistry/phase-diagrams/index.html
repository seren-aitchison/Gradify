Phase Diagrams

Phase diagrams show stable phases at each temperature and pressure, with lines of coexistence, a triple point and a critical point; water's negative solid-liquid slope reflects less dense ice, while CO₂ sublimes at 1 atm because its triple point is above 1 atm.

Chemistry · States of Matter and Physical Chemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Identify regions, lines and points on a phase diagram
  • Explain the triple point and critical point
  • Compare the phase diagrams of water and carbon dioxide

Features

  • Regions show where solid, liquid and gas are stable.
  • Lines show conditions where two phases coexist in equilibrium.
  • The triple point is where all three phases coexist.
  • The critical point is beyond which liquid and gas become a single supercritical fluid.

Water and CO₂

  • Water's solid-liquid line slopes backwards: increasing pressure can melt ice, because ice is less dense.
  • CO₂'s triple point is at 5.1 atm, so at 1 atm solid CO₂ sublimes.
  • Dry ice sublimes at −78 °C.
  • Supercritical CO₂ is used to decaffeinate coffee.

Phase diagrams explain why ice skates glide partly because pressure and friction melt a thin layer, and why liquid CO₂ only exists above 5.1 atm. Supercritical fluids combine gas-like diffusion with liquid-like dissolving power.

Worked example

Reading CO₂'s diagram.

  1. At 1 atm and −100 °C, CO₂ is solid.
  2. As it warms at 1 atm, it crosses into the gas region at −78 °C.
  3. It never passes through the liquid region.
  4. So dry ice sublimes rather than melting.
Common mistakeThinking every substance melts at atmospheric pressure. CO₂ sublimes because its triple point is above 1 atm.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. What do lines on a phase diagram represent?
    • Conditions where only gas exists
    • Conditions where two phases coexist
    • The boiling points of mixtures
    • The density of each phase
    Show the answer

    Answer: Conditions where two phases coexist. Each line marks an equilibrium between two phases.

  2. Why does water's solid-liquid line slope backwards?
    • Ice is denser than liquid water
    • Water has no triple point
    • Ice is less dense than liquid water
    • Pressure always freezes water
    Show the answer

    Answer: Ice is less dense than liquid water. Raising pressure favours the denser liquid phase.

  3. Why is supercritical CO₂ useful for extraction?
    • It is a solid at room temperature
    • It reacts with caffeine chemically
    • It is more toxic than organic solvents
    • It diffuses like a gas but dissolves like a liquid
    Show the answer

    Answer: It diffuses like a gas but dissolves like a liquid. Combining properties gives efficient, clean extraction.

Key terms

Triple point
Conditions where solid, liquid and gas coexist
Critical point
Conditions beyond which liquid and gas become one phase
Supercritical fluid
A phase beyond the critical point
