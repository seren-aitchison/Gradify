Reaction Order

Orders are found by varying one concentration at a time and comparing initial rates, or from rate-concentration graph shapes; k is then calculated, with units depending on overall order.

Chemistry · Chemical Kinetics · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Determine orders using the initial rates method
  • Identify orders from rate-concentration graphs
  • Calculate the rate constant and its units

Initial rates method

  • Run several experiments, changing one concentration at a time.
  • Compare how the initial rate changes.
  • Doubling [A] with no change in rate: zero order; rate doubles: first order; rate quadruples: second order.
  • Then substitute into the rate equation to find k.

Graphs and units

  • Rate-concentration graphs: zero order is horizontal, first order a straight line through the origin, second order a curve.
  • Units of k depend on overall order.
  • Zero order: mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹; first order: s⁻¹; second order: dm³ mol⁻¹ s⁻¹.
  • Units are derived by rearranging the rate equation.

The initial rates method avoids complications from products as the reaction proceeds. Clock reactions, such as the iodine clock, measure the time to reach a fixed point, and 1/time is proportional to initial rate.

Worked example

Using initial rates.

  1. Exp 1: [A] = 0.10, [B] = 0.10, rate = 2.0 × 10⁻⁴.
  2. Exp 2: [A] = 0.20, [B] = 0.10, rate = 8.0 × 10⁻⁴ (×4, so second order in A).
  3. Exp 3: [A] = 0.10, [B] = 0.20, rate = 4.0 × 10⁻⁴ (×2, so first order in B).
  4. Rate = k[A]²[B]; k = 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ ÷ (0.01 × 0.10) = 0.20 dm⁶ mol⁻² s⁻¹.
Common mistakeChanging two concentrations at once in initial rate experiments. Only one variable should change at a time.

In 3 lines: Change one concentration. Graph shapes. Units of k.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. When [A] doubles, the rate quadruples. What is the order in A?
    • First order
    • Second order
    • Zero order
    • Third order
    Show the answer

    Answer: Second order. 2² = 4, so the rate depends on [A] squared.

  2. Why is the initial rate used rather than a later rate?
    • The rate is slowest at the start
    • Later rates are always zero
    • Concentrations are known exactly and products have not built up
    • It removes the need for a rate equation
    Show the answer

    Answer: Concentrations are known exactly and products have not built up. Starting conditions are controlled and uncomplicated.

  3. What does 1/time measure in a clock reaction?
    • The equilibrium constant
    • The activation energy
    • The concentration of the product
    • A value proportional to the initial rate
    Show the answer

    Answer: A value proportional to the initial rate. A fixed amount of product formed in time t gives rate ∝ 1/t.

Key terms

Initial rates method
Finding orders by comparing initial rates
Clock reaction
A reaction timed to a sudden visible change
Overall order
The sum of individual orders
