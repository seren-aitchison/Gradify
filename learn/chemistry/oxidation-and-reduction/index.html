Oxidation and Reduction

Oxidation is loss of electrons or increase in oxidation state and reduction is gain of electrons or decrease in oxidation state (OIL RIG); they always occur together in redox reactions, which include combustion, respiration and corrosion.

Chemistry · Redox and Electrochemistry · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define oxidation and reduction in terms of oxygen, electrons and oxidation states
  • Identify oxidation and reduction in reactions
  • Explain why oxidation and reduction occur together

Definitions

  • Oxidation is gain of oxygen, loss of electrons or increase in oxidation state.
  • Reduction is loss of oxygen, gain of electrons or decrease in oxidation state.
  • Memory aid: OIL RIG (Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain of electrons).
  • The electron definition is the most general.

Redox

  • Electrons lost by one species are gained by another.
  • So oxidation and reduction occur together: a redox reaction.
  • Example: Zn + Cu²⁺ → Zn²⁺ + Cu; zinc is oxidised and copper ions reduced.
  • Combustion, respiration and corrosion are redox reactions.

The oxygen definition came first historically, but many redox reactions involve no oxygen, such as sodium burning in chlorine. The electron definition covers all cases. Redox reactions power batteries and biological energy production.

Worked example

Sodium in chlorine.

  1. 2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl.
  2. Each Na loses one electron: Na → Na⁺ + e⁻ (oxidation).
  3. Each Cl gains one electron: Cl₂ + 2e⁻ → 2Cl⁻ (reduction).
  4. No oxygen is involved, but it is still a redox reaction.
Common mistakeThinking oxidation always involves oxygen. Oxidation is fundamentally loss of electrons.

In 3 lines: OIL RIG. Always together. Everyday redox.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. In Zn + Cu²⁺ → Zn²⁺ + Cu, which species is oxidised?
    • Copper(II) ions
    • Copper metal
    • Zinc ions
    • Zinc
    Show the answer

    Answer: Zinc. Zinc loses two electrons to become Zn²⁺, so zinc is oxidised.

  2. Why is sodium burning in chlorine a redox reaction?
    • Sodium loses electrons and chlorine gains them
    • Oxygen is added to sodium
    • No electrons are transferred
    • Chlorine loses electrons to sodium
    Show the answer

    Answer: Sodium loses electrons and chlorine gains them. Electron transfer defines redox even without oxygen.

  3. Why is the electron-transfer definition preferred?
    • It only applies to combustion reactions
    • It covers redox reactions that involve no oxygen
    • It ignores electrons
    • It is the oldest definition
    Show the answer

    Answer: It covers redox reactions that involve no oxygen. Many redox reactions involve electrons but not oxygen.
