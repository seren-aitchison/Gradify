The Therapeutic Relationship

Bordin's working alliance involves goals, tasks and bond; meta-analyses show a consistent moderate link between alliance and outcome; ruptures can be repaired; relationship and techniques both matter.

Psychology · Clinical Psychology and Interventions · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe Bordin's model of the working alliance
  • Explain evidence linking the alliance to outcomes
  • Evaluate the importance of the relationship compared with specific techniques
Competence and safetyThis lesson is educational and is not a guide to therapy. Psychological therapies should only be delivered by qualified, registered professionals.

The working alliance

Bordin (1979) described the working alliance as having three parts: agreement on the goals of therapy, agreement on the tasks used to reach them, and the emotional bond between client and therapist. The concept applies across therapies, from CBT to psychodynamic approaches.

Evidence

Flückiger et al. (2018) meta-analysed nearly 300 studies and found a consistent, moderate association between the strength of the alliance and therapy outcome. Norcross and Wampold (2011) concluded that the therapy relationship makes substantial and consistent contributions to outcome, independent of the specific type of treatment.

Worked example

Ruptures and repair

  1. A rupture is a strain in the alliance, such as a client feeling misunderstood or disagreeing with a task.
  2. Therapists can repair ruptures by noticing them, inviting discussion and adjusting their approach.
  3. Successfully repaired ruptures may strengthen the relationship. This example is illustrative.

Evaluation

The alliance is one of the best-established predictors of outcome. But correlation doesn't show causation: early improvement may strengthen the alliance rather than the reverse. Most researchers conclude that both relationship factors and specific techniques matter, and the best therapists combine them.

Common mistakeAssuming techniques alone make therapy work. The relationship contributes substantially across approaches.

In 2 lines: Bordin's alliance: goals, tasks and bond. Alliance predicts outcome; ruptures can be repaired; techniques also matter.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Agreement on goals and tasks plus an emotional bond describes the…
    • Transference
    • Working alliance
    • Formulation
    • Fear hierarchy
    Show the answer

    Answer: Working alliance. Bordin defined the working alliance this way.

  2. A client seems withdrawn after a therapist sets homework; the therapist asks how the task felt and adapts it. This is…
    • Free association
    • Flooding
    • Repairing a rupture
    • Shaping
    Show the answer

    Answer: Repairing a rupture. Noticing and discussing strain repairs the alliance.

  3. Why can't alliance–outcome correlations prove the alliance causes improvement?
    • Alliance is never measured
    • Correlations always show causes
    • Outcomes are never measured
    • Early improvement may strengthen the alliance
    Show the answer

    Answer: Early improvement may strengthen the alliance. Direction of influence may run both ways.

Key terms

Working alliance
Agreement on goals and tasks, plus the emotional bond, between client and therapist
Rupture
A strain or breakdown in the therapeutic alliance
Common factors
Elements shared across therapies, such as the relationship
