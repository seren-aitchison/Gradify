Culture and Development

Childrearing goals and practices vary (Keller); attachment rates differ between countries; Vygotsky and Rogoff show development is cultural participation; developmental norms are not universal.

Psychology · Cultural and Cross-Cultural Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe cultural variation in childrearing and attachment
  • Explain sociocultural theories of development
  • Evaluate the universality of developmental theories

Childrearing across cultures

Childrearing goals vary: some cultures prioritise independence and self-expression, others obedience, relatedness and responsibility. Keller (2007) contrasted distal parenting (face-to-face interaction and object play, common in Western middle-class families) with proximal parenting (body contact and carrying, common in many rural societies). Van IJzendoorn and Kroonenberg (1988) found that rates of attachment types varied between countries.

Sociocultural theories

Vygotsky argued development occurs through social interaction and cultural tools such as language. Rogoff (2003) described guided participation: children learn by taking part in everyday cultural activities alongside more skilled people. In some communities, children learn mainly by observing and pitching in to adult work rather than through direct instruction.

Worked example

Learning by observing

  1. Rogoff and colleagues found Mayan children from Guatemala were more likely than European American children to attend to activities around them while doing their own task.
  2. They learned by observing and participating rather than waiting for explanation.
  3. This shows that "normal" learning styles depend on cultural practice.

Evaluation

Cross-cultural research shows that many developmental norms, such as ages for milestones or ideal parenting styles, are culturally shaped. Piaget's stages appear in many cultures, but the timing of formal operations varies with schooling. Developmental science increasingly recognises that WEIRD samples are not the global standard.

Common mistakeAssuming Western middle-class childrearing is the universal norm. Practices and goals vary widely.

In 2 lines: Childrearing styles and attachment rates vary across cultures. Vygotsky and Rogoff: development through cultural participation.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Childrearing based on body contact and carrying is…
    • Distal parenting
    • Proximal parenting
    • Authoritative parenting
    • Guided participation
    Show the answer

    Answer: Proximal parenting. Keller described body contact and carrying as proximal parenting.

  2. A child learns to weave by watching her grandmother and helping with simple steps. Rogoff would call this…
    • Distal parenting
    • Formal operations
    • Guided participation
    • Imposed etic
    Show the answer

    Answer: Guided participation. Learning through joint activity is guided participation.

  3. Why are developmental norms said to be culturally shaped?
    • Development is identical everywhere
    • Culture never affects children
    • Only biology matters
    • Milestones and ideal parenting differ across communities
    Show the answer

    Answer: Milestones and ideal parenting differ across communities. Practices and expectations about milestones and parenting vary widely between communities.

Key terms

Guided participation
Learning through taking part in activities with more skilled people
Proximal parenting
Childrearing focused on body contact and carrying
Distal parenting
Childrearing focused on face-to-face interaction and object play
