Genetics and Behaviour

Twin and adoption studies show genes contribute to most traits; heritability describes populations, not individuals, and genes act with environments.

Psychology · Biological Psychology and Neuroscience · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain how twin and adoption studies estimate genetic influence
  • Define heritability and explain common misunderstandings
  • Evaluate genome-wide and polygenic approaches to behaviour

Family, twin and adoption studies

Twin studies compare monozygotic (MZ, identical) twins, who share nearly all their genes, with dizygotic (DZ, fraternal) twins, who share about 50% on average. If MZ twins are more similar than DZ twins on a trait, genes probably contribute. Concordance is the probability that if one twin has a trait, the other does too. Adoption studies compare adopted children with their biological and adoptive parents.

Heritability

Heritability is the proportion of variation in a trait within a population that is linked to genetic differences. A meta-analysis of 50 years of twin studies, covering over 14 million twin pairs, found the average heritability across all traits studied was about 49% (Polderman et al., 2015). Heritability doesn't tell us how much of one person's trait is "due to genes", and it can change when environments change.

Worked example

Height and nutrition

  1. Height is highly heritable within well-fed populations.
  2. Yet average height rose substantially in many countries over the 20th century, as nutrition and health improved.
  3. High heritability doesn't mean a trait can't change with the environment.

Molecular genetics

Most behavioural traits are polygenic: influenced by thousands of genetic variants, each with a tiny effect. Genome-wide association studies (GWAS) scan the genomes of very large samples. Polygenic scores add up many small effects; they predict a modest share of variation in traits such as educational attainment and can't determine an individual's outcome.

Common mistakeTreating heritability as fixed or individual. It describes variation in a population and depends on environment.

In 2 lines: Twin and adoption studies estimate genetic influence; average heritability across traits is about 49%. Heritability is a population statistic; most traits are polygenic and shaped with environments.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Identical twins are…
    • Dizygotic
    • Polygenic
    • Heterozygous
    • Monozygotic
    Show the answer

    Answer: Monozygotic. Monozygotic twins develop from one fertilised egg.

  2. A trait has 60% heritability in a population. What can we conclude?
    • 60% of population variation is linked to genes
    • 60% of each person's trait is genetic
    • The trait can't change
    • The environment has no role
    Show the answer

    Answer: 60% of population variation is linked to genes. Heritability describes population variation, not individuals.

  3. What is the main criticism of the equal environments assumption?
    • DZ twins share all genes
    • MZ twins may be treated more alike
    • Twins are never studied
    • Environments are always identical
    Show the answer

    Answer: MZ twins may be treated more alike. If MZ twins share more similar environments, heritability may be overestimated.

Key terms

Concordance
The probability that both twins share a trait
Heritability
The proportion of population variation linked to genetic differences
Polygenic
Influenced by many genes, each with small effects
