Analysis of Variance (ANOVA)

ANOVA compares several means with one F test; a significant F needs post hoc tests to locate the differences, and η² gives the effect size.

Psychology · Statistics · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain why ANOVA is used instead of multiple t-tests
  • Interpret an F ratio, its degrees of freedom and eta squared
  • Explain the role of post hoc tests and factorial designs

The logic of ANOVA

Running many t-tests inflates the chance of a false positive. Analysis of variance tests all group means in one test. It compares between-groups variance (how much group means differ) with within-groups variance (how much individuals vary inside groups). The ratio is F: if groups differ more than chance variation predicts, F is large.

Worked example

A worked one-way ANOVA

  1. Three groups score 1, 2, 3; 4, 5, 6; and 7, 8, 9 (means 2, 5, 8).
  2. Between-groups sum of squares = 54; within-groups = 6.
  3. F(2, 6) = (54 ÷ 2) ÷ (6 ÷ 6) = 27, p ≈ .001; η² = 54 ÷ 60 = .90.
  4. A significant F says at least one mean differs, not which.

Repeated measures ANOVA extends the paired t-test to three or more conditions with the same participants, removing individual differences from the error term.

After a significant F

Post hoc tests (such as Tukey's HSD) compare pairs of groups while controlling the overall error rate; planned contrasts test specific comparisons decided in advance. Factorial ANOVA tests two or more IVs at once and their interaction: whether the effect of one IV depends on the level of another.

Common mistakeConcluding from a significant F that every group differs from every other. F only shows that the means aren't all equal.

In 2 lines: ANOVA compares three or more means using the F ratio. Post hoc tests locate differences; factorial ANOVA tests interactions.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Why use ANOVA rather than several t-tests for four groups?
    • It always gives larger effects
    • It controls the overall Type I error rate
    • It needs no assumptions
    • It works only with two groups
    Show the answer

    Answer: It controls the overall Type I error rate. Many t-tests inflate the chance of a false positive.

  2. A three-group ANOVA gives F(2, 57) = 6.4, p = .003. What is the next step?
    • Conclude all three groups differ
    • Conclude no groups differ
    • Run post hoc tests to find which groups differ
    • Run a chi-square test
    Show the answer

    Answer: Run post hoc tests to find which groups differ. A significant omnibus F needs follow-up comparisons.

  3. A drug improves memory in young adults but not older adults. In a factorial design this is…
    • A main effect of age only
    • A Type II error
    • Multicollinearity
    • An interaction between drug and age
    Show the answer

    Answer: An interaction between drug and age. The drug's effect depends on age group, which is the definition of an interaction.

Key terms

F ratio
Between-groups variance divided by within-groups variance
Post hoc test
A test comparing pairs of groups after a significant ANOVA, controlling error rate
Interaction
When the effect of one IV depends on the level of another IV
