Short-term Memory

Short-term memory has limited capacity (a few chunks), brief duration without rehearsal, and mainly acoustic coding.

Psychology · Cognitive Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the capacity, duration and coding of short-term memory
  • Explain the studies of Miller, Peterson and Peterson, and Baddeley on short-term memory
  • Evaluate the evidence for a separate short-term store

Capacity

Miller (1956) argued that short-term memory holds about seven plus or minus two items, but that the items can be chunks: meaningful groups. "BBCFBIUSA" is nine letters but three chunks for someone who recognises the acronyms. Later work suggests the true limit is nearer four chunks when rehearsal and chunking are controlled (Cowan, 2001).

Duration and coding

Peterson and Peterson (1959) gave participants trigrams of consonants (such as "KTX") and prevented rehearsal by having them count backwards in threes. Recall fell sharply, to around 10% after 18 seconds, suggesting short-term memory lasts under 20 seconds without rehearsal.

Baddeley (1966) found that lists of words that sound alike were harder to recall immediately than lists of dissimilar words, while words with similar meanings caused more trouble after a delay. This suggests short-term memory is mainly acoustically coded and long-term memory semantically coded.

Worked example

The serial position curve

  1. Participants hear a list of 20 words and recall them in any order.
  2. Recall is best for the first words (primacy effect) and the last words (recency effect).
  3. A 30-second distraction task before recall removes the recency effect but leaves primacy, suggesting recency reflects short-term memory and primacy long-term memory (Glanzer and Cunitz, 1966).

Investigate with the lab. Step through the multi-store model, then the working memory model, and compare what each says about short-term memory.

Common mistakeTreating "seven items" as fixed. Chunking, item type and rehearsal all change the apparent capacity.

In 2 lines: Short-term memory holds a few chunks for under about 20 seconds without rehearsal. It is mainly acoustically coded; serial position effects support a short-term/long-term distinction.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Miller's estimate of short-term memory capacity was…
    • Seven plus or minus two
    • Two plus or minus one
    • Twenty items
    • Unlimited
    Show the answer

    Answer: Seven plus or minus two. Miller's 1956 paper proposed about seven items or chunks.

  2. In Peterson and Peterson's study, why did participants count backwards in threes?
    • To test arithmetic
    • To prevent rehearsal
    • To increase attention
    • To build chunks
    Show the answer

    Answer: To prevent rehearsal. Counting blocked rehearsal so duration could be measured.

  3. Which is a limitation of the Peterson and Peterson study?
    • It used too many participants
    • It measured long-term memory
    • Consonant trigrams are unlike everyday material
    • It had no control of rehearsal
    Show the answer

    Answer: Consonant trigrams are unlike everyday material. Artificial stimuli limit ecological validity.

  4. How long does information last in short-term memory without rehearsal?
    • About 1 second
    • About 1 hour
    • Indefinitely
    • About 18 to 30 seconds
    Show the answer

    Answer: About 18 to 30 seconds. Peterson and Peterson found recall dropped sharply after about 18 seconds.

Key terms

Chunking
Grouping items into meaningful units to increase capacity
Acoustic coding
Storing information by its sound
Recency effect
Better recall of the last items in a list
