Sleep

Sleep cycles through non-REM and REM stages; it supports restoration and memory; sleep loss impairs performance more than people realise.

Psychology · Biological Psychology and Neuroscience · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the stages of sleep and how they are measured
  • Explain restoration, memory consolidation and evolutionary theories of sleep
  • Evaluate evidence on the effects of sleep deprivation

Stages

Sleep is measured with EEG (brain waves), plus eye movements and muscle activity. It cycles through non-REM stages N1 (light), N2 (sleep spindles) and N3 (deep, slow-wave sleep with large delta waves), and REM sleep, with rapid eye movements, vivid dreams and near-paralysis of the muscles. A full cycle lasts about 90 minutes; there are four to six cycles a night, with more deep sleep early and more REM late (Aserinsky and Kleitman, 1953).

Why sleep?

  • Restoration: sleep repairs and restores the body and brain. During sleep, the space between brain cells expands and waste products are cleared faster (Xie et al., 2013).
  • Memory consolidation: sleep strengthens new memories; slow-wave sleep supports declarative memory.
  • Evolutionary (energy conservation and safety): sleep keeps animals inactive when activity is dangerous or wasteful; sleep patterns vary with diet and predation.
Worked example

Sleep deprivation

  1. Van Dongen et al. (2003) restricted participants to 4 or 6 hours a night for two weeks.
  2. Their attention lapses rose steadily, reaching levels similar to one or two nights of total deprivation.
  3. Yet participants rated themselves as only slightly sleepy, underestimating their impairment.

Chronic short sleep is associated with poorer mood, impaired learning, accidents and increased health risks. Teenagers' body clocks shift later, which is one argument for later school start times.

Common mistakeAssuming you adapt to short sleep. Performance keeps declining even when people feel they've adjusted.

In 2 lines: Sleep cycles through N1–N3 and REM about every 90 minutes. Theories stress restoration, memory and energy conservation; restriction impairs attention cumulatively.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Vivid dreaming is most associated with…
    • REM sleep
    • Stage N1
    • Slow-wave sleep
    • Being awake
    Show the answer

    Answer: REM sleep. REM sleep features vivid dreams, rapid eye movements and muscle paralysis.

  2. A participant woken in the first two hours of sleep is most likely to be in…
    • REM sleep only
    • Slow-wave sleep
    • Wakefulness
    • A dream state
    Show the answer

    Answer: Slow-wave sleep. Deep sleep is concentrated early in the night.

  3. What does Xie et al.'s research suggest about the function of sleep?
    • Sleep has no function
    • Sleep only saves energy
    • Sleep helps clear brain waste
    • Sleep damages memory
    Show the answer

    Answer: Sleep helps clear brain waste. Faster waste clearance during sleep supports restoration.

Key terms

REM sleep
Sleep with rapid eye movements, vivid dreams and muscle paralysis
Slow-wave sleep
Deep non-REM sleep with large, slow delta waves
Sleep spindles
Bursts of brain activity characteristic of stage N2
