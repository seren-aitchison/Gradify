What Is Psychology?

Psychology is the scientific study of mind and behaviour: it infers unobservable mental processes from careful measurement of what people and animals do.

Psychology · Foundations of Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define psychology and explain what "mind" and "behaviour" refer to
  • Explain why psychology uses scientific methods rather than intuition alone
  • Evaluate the claim that psychology is "just common sense"

A working definition

Psychology is the scientific study of mind and behaviour. Behaviour is anything an organism does that can be observed and measured: speaking, pressing a button, a change in heart rate. Mind covers inner processes that can't be observed directly, such as perception, memory, thinking and emotion. Psychologists study the mind indirectly, by measuring behaviour and physiology and drawing inferences from them.

Psychology studies people (and other animals) at many scales: a single neurone firing, an individual making a decision, a crowd reacting to an emergency, cultures differing in how they express emotion.

Why not just ask common sense?

Everyone holds intuitive theories about other people, and many feel obviously true. But common sense often contains contradictions ("birds of a feather flock together" against "opposites attract") and is shaped by memorable cases rather than representative evidence. Psychology tests claims systematically, under conditions where they could turn out to be wrong.

Worked example

A popular belief holds that people use only 10% of their brains.

  1. Brain imaging shows activity across virtually the whole brain over a day, though not all regions at once.
  2. Damage to almost any brain region produces some loss of function, which wouldn't happen if 90% were unused.
  3. Conclusion: the claim is a myth; testing it against evidence is what makes psychology a science.
Common mistakeThinking psychology is mainly therapy. Clinical work is one part; most psychologists research or apply knowledge about perception, learning, development, social behaviour and more.

The questions psychology asks

  • Describe: what do people do, and how often?
  • Explain: why do they do it?
  • Predict: under what conditions will it happen?
  • Change: how can behaviour be improved, for example in education, health or work?

In 2 lines: Psychology studies mind and behaviour scientifically, inferring mental processes from measurable behaviour. It tests intuitions rather than accepting them, because common sense is often contradictory or biased.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which is the best definition of psychology?
    • The study of mental illness and its treatment
    • The scientific study of mind and behaviour
    • The study of the brain's anatomy
    • The interpretation of dreams and personality
    Show the answer

    Answer: The scientific study of mind and behaviour. Psychology covers all mental processes and behaviour, studied scientifically; therapy, anatomy and dreams are narrower topics within or beside it.

  2. A newspaper claims that "everyone knows" stress causes stomach ulcers. How would a psychologist respond?
    • Accept it because it is common knowledge
    • Reject it because newspapers are unreliable
    • Treat it as a hypothesis and look at controlled evidence
    • Ask a few friends whether they agree
    Show the answer

    Answer: Treat it as a hypothesis and look at controlled evidence. Psychology treats widely held beliefs as claims to test. (In fact most ulcers are caused by Helicobacter pylori infection, though stress may play a role: a good example of why testing matters.)

  3. Which is the strongest reply to the claim that psychology is "just common sense"?
    • Psychology uses technical words that common sense lacks
    • Psychologists have degrees and other people don't
    • Common sense is always wrong, so psychology is always right
    • Common sense contains contradictory sayings and is often wrong when tested, as myths show
    Show the answer

    Answer: Common sense contains contradictory sayings and is often wrong when tested, as myths show. The key point is that intuition must be tested; it is sometimes right and sometimes wrong, and only evidence tells us which. Jargon or credentials don't make a claim true.

Key terms

Psychology
The scientific study of mind and behaviour
Behaviour
Any observable, measurable action or response of an organism
Mind
Inner mental processes such as perception, memory, thought and emotion, inferred from behaviour
