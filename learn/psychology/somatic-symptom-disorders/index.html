Somatic Symptom Disorders

Somatic symptom disorders involve distressing physical symptoms with excessive related thoughts and behaviours; attention, interpretation, stress and services contribute; modern approaches avoid mind–body dualism.

Psychology · Psychopathology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe somatic symptom and related disorders
  • Explain psychological and biological factors in persistent physical symptoms
  • Evaluate approaches that avoid mind–body dualism
Competence and safetyThis lesson is educational and is not a guide to diagnosis or treatment. Anyone with physical or psychological symptoms should speak to a GP or another qualified professional.

What are somatic symptom disorders?

In DSM-5-TR, somatic symptom disorder involves one or more distressing physical symptoms together with excessive thoughts, feelings or behaviours about them, such as persistent worry or time spent on health concerns. ICD-11 uses the term bodily distress disorder. Illness anxiety involves preoccupation with having a serious illness despite few symptoms. Functional neurological disorder involves real neurological symptoms, such as weakness or seizures, linked to how the nervous system is functioning rather than structural disease.

Explanations

Factors include attention to bodily sensations, catastrophic interpretations of symptoms, anxiety and depression, past illness experiences, stress and trauma, and how health services respond. Henningsen et al. (2018) describe functional somatic syndromes as arising from interactions between bodily processes, perception and psychological and social factors.

Worked example

Avoiding dualism

  1. Telling someone their symptoms are "all in the mind" can feel dismissive and damage trust.
  2. A more helpful message is that symptoms are real and involve interactions between body and brain.
  3. Psychological approaches, such as CBT, and graded activity can help alongside medical care.

Evaluation

Modern approaches avoid mind–body dualism and the older idea that symptoms are "medically unexplained" or imagined. Diagnosis can be difficult and must avoid missing physical disease. Critics argue the DSM-5 criteria may label people with genuine physical illness as having a mental disorder because of understandable worry.

Common mistakeAssuming these symptoms are imagined. They are real and involve body–brain interactions.

In 2 lines: Physical symptoms with excessive related thoughts and behaviours. Attention, interpretation and stress contribute; avoid mind–body dualism.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. ICD-11's term for distressing bodily symptoms with excessive attention to them is…
    • Panic disorder
    • Dissociative amnesia
    • Hypomania
    • Bodily distress disorder
    Show the answer

    Answer: Bodily distress disorder. ICD-11 introduced bodily distress disorder.

  2. A man with ongoing stomach pain constantly checks symptoms online and fears each twinge means cancer, despite reassurance. This involves…
    • Catastrophic interpretation of bodily sensations
    • Hypomania with racing thoughts
    • Displacement of anger onto others
    • Learned helplessness after failure
    Show the answer

    Answer: Catastrophic interpretation of bodily sensations. Excessive worry about symptoms fits this pattern.

  3. Why do critics question DSM-5 somatic symptom disorder criteria?
    • The criteria require no physical symptoms at all
    • People with physical illness may be labelled for worrying
    • They exclude any form of health worry
    • They apply only to young children under five
    Show the answer

    Answer: People with physical illness may be labelled for worrying. Understandable distress about illness may be pathologised.

Key terms

Somatic symptom disorder
Distressing physical symptoms with excessive related thoughts, feelings or behaviours
Functional neurological disorder
Neurological symptoms from altered nervous system functioning rather than structural disease
Mind–body dualism
Treating mind and body as separate
