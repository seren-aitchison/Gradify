Evolutionary Developmental Psychology

Evolutionary developmental psychology explains why childhood and immaturity may be adaptive and how evolved predispositions develop through experience.

Psychology · Evolutionary Psychology and Behavioural Ecology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain the aims of evolutionary developmental psychology
  • Explain why a long childhood and immaturity might be adaptive
  • Apply evolutionary developmental ideas to play and learning

Development and evolution

Evolutionary developmental psychology (Bjorklund and Pellegrini, 2002) studies how evolved predispositions develop through interaction with the environment. It asks not only what adults' minds are like, but why children develop as they do, and why humans have such a long period of dependency.

Adaptive immaturity

Bjorklund (1997) argued some features of children's immaturity are adaptive for their stage, not just incomplete adulthood. For example, young children's overestimation of their own abilities may encourage them to keep trying new skills; limited working memory may make language learning easier by simplifying input ("less is more", Newport, 1990).

Worked example

Play

  1. Many young mammals play, especially in species with long juvenile periods.
  2. Rough-and-tumble play, more common among boys on average, may help practise physical skills and social competition safely.
  3. Pretend play may help children explore social roles and possibilities; across cultures, children's play reflects the adult activities of their society.

Developmental plasticity

Evolved development is plastic: children adjust to their environments, as life history theory suggests. Experience-expectant processes rely on typical experiences, while experience-dependent processes allow learning of local culture. This view rejects simple nature versus nurture.

Common mistakeTreating childhood as merely a stage of incompleteness. Some childhood features may be adapted to childhood itself.

In 2 lines: Evolutionary developmental psychology explains why humans have long childhoods. Immaturity, play and plasticity may be adaptive for children's stage and environment.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Playful fighting and chasing is called…
    • Imaginative pretend play
    • Rough-and-tumble play
    • Quiet solitary play
    • Side-by-side parallel play
    Show the answer

    Answer: Rough-and-tumble play. It may practise physical and social skills.

  2. A child keeps trying to ride a bike, believing they are nearly expert despite many falls. Bjorklund might see this as…
    • A cognitive failure only
    • Mismatch
    • Adaptive overestimation of ability
    • Kin selection
    Show the answer

    Answer: Adaptive overestimation of ability. Overestimating ability encourages children to keep practising new skills.

  3. How does evolutionary developmental psychology challenge simple nature–nurture debates?
    • Genes determine everything
    • Environment determines everything
    • Development doesn't change
    • Evolved predispositions develop through experience
    Show the answer

    Answer: Evolved predispositions develop through experience. Development is a plastic interaction between evolved predispositions and experience.
