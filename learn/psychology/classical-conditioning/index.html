Classical Conditioning

In classical conditioning, repeated pairing of a neutral stimulus with an unconditioned stimulus makes it a conditioned stimulus that triggers a conditioned response.

Psychology · Learning and Behaviour · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define the key terms of classical conditioning: UCS, UCR, NS, CS and CR
  • Apply the terms to identify the elements of a conditioning example
  • Evaluate the Little Albert study, including its ethics

Pavlov's discovery

Ivan Pavlov, a Russian physiologist who won the 1904 Nobel Prize for work on digestion, noticed that dogs began salivating before food arrived, at the sight of the person who fed them. He studied this systematically: a sound (such as a metronome or bell) was presented just before food, many times. Eventually the sound alone produced salivation (Pavlov, 1927).

The terms

  • Unconditioned stimulus (UCS): triggers a response without learning (food).
  • Unconditioned response (UCR): the natural response to the UCS (salivating to food).
  • Neutral stimulus (NS): produces no relevant response at first (the bell).
  • Conditioned stimulus (CS): the former NS, after pairing with the UCS.
  • Conditioned response (CR): the learned response to the CS (salivating to the bell).
Worked example

Little Albert (Watson and Rayner, 1920)

  1. An 11-month-old infant showed no fear of a white rat (NS).
  2. Each time he touched the rat, a steel bar was struck loudly behind him (UCS), causing fear (UCR).
  3. After seven pairings, the rat alone produced crying and avoidance (CS and CR), and the fear generalised to similar objects such as a rabbit and a fur coat.

Evaluation

The study suggested phobias could be learned, which later supported behaviour therapies. But it was a single case, the fear was never removed before Albert left, the measures were informal, and it would be judged unethical today. Historians have also debated who Albert was. Modern accounts see classical conditioning as learning that one event predicts another, not just a reflex link (Rescorla, 1988).

Common mistakeMixing up UCS and CS. Ask: did this stimulus trigger the response before any learning? If yes, it is unconditioned.

In 2 lines: Pairing a neutral stimulus with an unconditioned stimulus makes it a conditioned stimulus. Little Albert suggested fears can be learned, but the study was a poorly controlled, unethical single case.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. In Pavlov's experiment, salivation to the bell after conditioning is the…
    • Conditioned response
    • Unconditioned response
    • Unconditioned stimulus
    • Neutral stimulus
    Show the answer

    Answer: Conditioned response. It is learned, and triggered by the bell, so it is the conditioned response.

  2. A child is stung by a wasp in a garden and afterwards feels anxious in that garden. What is the conditioned stimulus?
    • The sting
    • The garden
    • The pain of the sting
    • The anxiety
    Show the answer

    Answer: The garden. The garden was neutral until paired with the sting (UCS); now it triggers anxiety (CR).

  3. Which is the strongest methodological criticism of the Little Albert study?
    • It used too many participants
    • It took place outside a laboratory
    • It was a single, poorly measured case
    • It studied adults rather than infants
    Show the answer

    Answer: It was a single, poorly measured case. One infant, informal observations and no follow-up limit what can be concluded.

Key terms

Unconditioned stimulus
A stimulus that triggers a response without learning
Conditioned stimulus
A formerly neutral stimulus that triggers a response after pairing with an unconditioned stimulus
Conditioned response
The learned response to a conditioned stimulus
