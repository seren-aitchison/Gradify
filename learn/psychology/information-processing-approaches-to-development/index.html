Information-processing Approaches to Development

Information-processing approaches explain development through gains in speed, capacity, strategies and knowledge, with strategies competing and gradually improving.

Psychology · Developmental Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain how processing speed, working memory and strategies develop
  • Describe Siegler's overlapping waves model
  • Apply information-processing research to children's learning

What changes with age

Information-processing approaches see development as continuous rather than stage-like. Several things improve with age:

  • Processing speed increases through childhood into adolescence (Kail, 1991).
  • Working memory capacity grows: digit span rises from about 2–3 items at age 2 to about 7 in adulthood.
  • Strategies: children increasingly use rehearsal, organisation and elaboration to remember.
  • Knowledge: a rich knowledge base makes new information easier to encode.
Worked example

Chess experts

  1. Chi (1978) compared 10-year-old chess experts with adult chess novices.
  2. The children recalled chess positions better than the adults, but the adults recalled lists of digits better.
  3. Knowledge in a domain can outweigh age differences in general memory capacity.

Overlapping waves

Siegler's overlapping waves model (1996) proposes that children have several strategies available at any age and gradually shift towards more efficient ones. For example, in adding 3 + 5, a child may count all, count on from the larger number, or retrieve the answer, choosing differently on different trials. Strategy choice improves with experience as children learn which strategies work.

Executive function and metacognition

Development of inhibitory control and metacognition (knowing when and how to use strategies) helps children use their abilities. Teaching strategies explicitly, and helping children monitor them, can improve learning.

Common mistakeTreating children as small adults with less memory. Strategy use and knowledge differ, not just capacity.

In 2 lines: Speed, working memory, strategies and knowledge improve gradually. Siegler shows children use several strategies that shift over time; domain knowledge can outweigh age.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Information-processing approaches see development as…
    • Strictly stage-like
    • Mostly continuous
    • Fixed at birth
    • Purely social
    Show the answer

    Answer: Mostly continuous. Gains are gradual rather than in discrete stages.

  2. A child sometimes counts on her fingers and sometimes recalls the answer to 4 + 3. Which model fits?
    • Piaget's stages
    • Ecological systems
    • Overlapping waves
    • Dual systems
    Show the answer

    Answer: Overlapping waves. Several strategies coexist and shift gradually.

  3. What does Chi's study imply for teaching?
    • Memory is fixed by age
    • Children can't learn chess
    • Digits are best for teaching
    • Building knowledge improves memory
    Show the answer

    Answer: Building knowledge improves memory. Domain knowledge supports encoding new information.

Key terms

Processing speed
How quickly cognitive operations are carried out
Overlapping waves model
Siegler's model of multiple strategies gradually shifting in use
Knowledge base
Domain knowledge that aids encoding and recall
