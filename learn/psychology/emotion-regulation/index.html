Emotion Regulation

Emotion regulation can occur at several points; reappraisal generally has better outcomes than suppression, but flexibility and context matter.

Psychology · Motivation, Emotion and Wellbeing · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain Gross's process model of emotion regulation
  • Compare cognitive reappraisal and expressive suppression
  • Apply emotion regulation research to everyday situations

The process model

Gross (1998) proposed that emotions unfold over time and can be regulated at different points: situation selection (choosing whether to enter a situation), situation modification (changing it), attentional deployment (shifting attention), cognitive change (reappraising its meaning) and response modulation (changing the response, such as suppressing expression).

Reappraisal versus suppression

Cognitive reappraisal means reinterpreting a situation to change its emotional impact ("this interview is a chance to learn"). Expressive suppression means hiding emotional expression. Gross and John (2003) found people who habitually used reappraisal reported more positive emotion, better relationships and wellbeing, while habitual suppression was linked to more negative emotion and poorer social functioning.

Worked example

Watching a disgusting film

  1. Gross (1998) showed participants a disgusting film clip and asked some to reappraise it (view it with detachment), some to suppress expressions and others to just watch.
  2. Reappraisal reduced both disgust expression and reported disgust, without increasing physiological arousal.
  3. Suppression reduced visible expression but not reported disgust, and increased sympathetic arousal.

Context matters

No strategy is always best. Regulatory flexibility (Bonanno and Burton, 2013) means using strategies that fit the situation. Suppression may be useful in a short professional encounter; reappraisal may be less effective in uncontrollable situations; and cultural norms affect the costs of suppression (Butler, Lee and Gross, 2007).

Common mistakeThinking suppressing emotions removes them. Suppression hides expression but often leaves feelings and increases arousal.

In 2 lines: Gross's model identifies five points for regulating emotion. Reappraisal generally works better than suppression; flexibility and culture matter.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Hiding your emotional expression is…
    • Cognitive reappraisal
    • Situation selection
    • Expressive suppression
    • Attentional deployment
    Show the answer

    Answer: Expressive suppression. Masking outward expression is suppression.

  2. Before a presentation, a student reframes nerves as excitement. This is…
    • Expressive suppression
    • Situation selection
    • Response modulation
    • Cognitive reappraisal
    Show the answer

    Answer: Cognitive reappraisal. Reinterpreting what a situation means is cognitive reappraisal.

  3. Why is regulatory flexibility important?
    • The best strategy depends on context
    • One strategy always works
    • Emotions can't be regulated
    • Suppression is always harmful
    Show the answer

    Answer: The best strategy depends on context. Effective regulation chooses strategies that fit the situation.
