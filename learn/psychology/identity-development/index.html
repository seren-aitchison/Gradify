Identity Development

Identity develops through exploration and commitment, producing four statuses that can change over time and differ across life areas.

Psychology · Developmental Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe Marcia's four identity statuses
  • Explain how exploration and commitment shape identity
  • Evaluate research on identity development, including cultural and ethnic identity

Exploration and commitment

Building on Erikson, Marcia (1966) interviewed young men about occupation, religion and politics and classified them by two dimensions: exploration (actively considering options) and commitment (making firm choices). Combining them gives four identity statuses:

  • Identity diffusion: no exploration, no commitment.
  • Foreclosure: commitment without exploration, often adopting parents' values.
  • Moratorium: active exploration, commitment not yet made.
  • Identity achievement: commitment after exploration.
Worked example

Changing status

  1. A 16-year-old plans to be a doctor because her parents are doctors (foreclosure).
  2. At university she tries several subjects and volunteering roles, unsure what she wants (moratorium).
  3. At 23 she chooses to train as a teacher after weighing options (identity achievement).

Identity status isn't a fixed stage: people can move between statuses and may hold different statuses in different areas of life. Longitudinal research suggests the proportion of identity achievers increases from adolescence to young adulthood, but many people haven't reached achievement by their mid-20s (Kroger, Martinussen and Marcia, 2010).

Ethnic and cultural identity

Phinney (1989) applied Marcia's ideas to ethnic identity, describing how young people from minority ethnic groups explore and commit to the meaning of their ethnicity. A secure, positive ethnic identity is associated with higher self-esteem and well-being. Digital and social media now provide new spaces for exploring identity, with both opportunities and risks.

Common mistakeTreating moratorium as a problem. Exploring options is a normal, often valuable part of identity development.

In 2 lines: Marcia's statuses combine exploration and commitment: diffusion, foreclosure, moratorium and achievement. Statuses change with age and differ by domain; ethnic identity is an important part of identity.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. A person actively exploring options without commitment is in…
    • Foreclosure
    • Diffusion
    • Moratorium
    • Achievement
    Show the answer

    Answer: Moratorium. Exploration without commitment is moratorium.

  2. A student isn't thinking about a career and hasn't chosen one. Which status is this?
    • Moratorium
    • Foreclosure
    • Identity achievement
    • Identity diffusion
    Show the answer

    Answer: Identity diffusion. Neither exploration nor commitment is diffusion.

  3. Why might Marcia's original findings not generalise?
    • They came from US young men
    • They used too many interviews
    • They studied only children
    • They measured no commitment
    Show the answer

    Answer: They came from US young men. Gender and cultural differences may change identity processes.

Key terms

Identity achievement
Commitment to an identity after exploring alternatives
Foreclosure
Commitment without exploration, often adopting others' values
Moratorium
Active exploration of options before commitment
