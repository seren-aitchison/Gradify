The Psychodynamic Model

The psychodynamic model links distress to unconscious conflict, defences and early experience; later theorists stressed relationships; key concepts are influential but many claims are hard to test.

Psychology · Psychopathology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the main ideas of the psychodynamic model
  • Explain how defence mechanisms and early experiences may relate to distress
  • Evaluate the scientific status of psychodynamic explanations
Competence and safetyThis lesson is educational and is not a guide to diagnosis or treatment. Anyone concerned about their own or someone else's mental health should speak to a GP or another qualified professional.

Freud's model

Freud proposed that the mind includes the id (instinctive drives), the ego (reality-oriented self) and the superego (internalised morals). Psychological difficulties arise from unconscious conflicts between these parts, often rooted in childhood experiences. Defence mechanisms, such as repression, denial and projection, protect against anxiety but can become maladaptive.

Later developments

Later theorists placed more emphasis on relationships. Object relations theorists such as Klein and Winnicott focused on early relationships with caregivers. Attachment theory (Bowlby) drew on psychodynamic ideas but used more systematic observation. Modern psychodynamic approaches focus on recurring relationship patterns and emotions.

Worked example

Defence mechanisms in everyday life

  1. A person who is angry with their boss but can't express it might snap at their family instead: displacement.
  2. Someone who can't accept a painful truth may refuse to acknowledge it: denial.
  3. Psychodynamic theory suggests such defences reduce anxiety in the short term but may cause problems if rigid. This example is illustrative.

Evaluation

The psychodynamic model highlighted the importance of early experience, unconscious processes and relationships, ideas supported in some modern research. But many of Freud's specific claims are hard to falsify, were based on small numbers of cases, and reflected the culture of his time. Some modern psychodynamic therapies have evidence of effectiveness.

Common mistakeTreating all psychodynamic ideas as discredited. Some, such as unconscious processes and attachment, have research support.

In 2 lines: Id, ego, superego, unconscious conflict and defences. Object relations and attachment developed the model; falsifiability is a key criticism.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Redirecting feelings onto a safer target is the defence mechanism of…
    • Denial
    • Repression
    • Sublimation
    • Displacement
    Show the answer

    Answer: Displacement. Displacement shifts feelings to a substitute.

  2. A man insists he isn't upset about his divorce, despite crying often and avoiding the subject. A psychodynamic view would see this as…
    • Denial
    • Displacement
    • Projection
    • Moral treatment
    Show the answer

    Answer: Denial. Refusing to acknowledge painful feelings is denial.

  3. Why are Freud's theories criticised as unscientific?
    • They were tested on large samples
    • Many claims are hard to falsify
    • They have no influence
    • They ignore childhood
    Show the answer

    Answer: Many claims are hard to falsify. Claims that can't be shown to be false can't be tested scientifically.

Key terms
