Individualism and Collectivism

Hofstede identified individualism–collectivism among national culture dimensions; Markus and Kitayama distinguished independent and interdependent selves; the concepts are useful but can oversimplify.

Psychology · Cultural and Cross-Cultural Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Distinguish individualism and collectivism
  • Explain Markus and Kitayama's independent and interdependent self
  • Evaluate the use of national culture dimensions

Hofstede's dimensions

Hofstede (1980) surveyed IBM employees in many countries and identified dimensions of national culture, including individualism–collectivism, power distance, uncertainty avoidance and masculinity–femininity. Individualist cultures, such as the US and UK, emphasise personal goals and independence; collectivist cultures, such as many East Asian and Latin American societies, emphasise group harmony and obligations.

Self-construal

Markus and Kitayama (1991) distinguished an independent self, defined by unique internal attributes, from an interdependent self, defined by relationships and roles. They argued self-construal shapes cognition, emotion and motivation. Heine et al. (1999) argued that the strong motive for positive self-regard found in North American samples is much weaker in Japan, where self-criticism and self-improvement are valued.

Worked example

Applying the concepts

  1. Asked "Who am I?", people from more individualist backgrounds tend to list traits ("I am creative").
  2. People from more collectivist backgrounds more often list roles and relationships ("I am a daughter", "I am a student at…").
  3. Conformity studies have found higher conformity in some collectivist samples (Bond and Smith, 1996).

Evaluation

The dimension is useful for comparing cultures, but critics argue it oversimplifies: countries contain many subcultures, and people can be individualist in some contexts and collectivist in others. Hofstede's data came from one company in the 1960s and 1970s. Oyserman et al. (2002) found differences between groups were smaller and less consistent than often assumed.

Common mistakeAssuming everyone in a country shares one cultural orientation. Variation within countries is large.

In 2 lines: Hofstede's dimensions include individualism–collectivism. Independent and interdependent selves; critics stress within-country variation.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. A culture emphasising group harmony and obligations is…
    • Individualist
    • Collectivist
    • Analytic
    • WEIRD
    Show the answer

    Answer: Collectivist. Collectivism prioritises group harmony and obligations over personal goals.

  2. Asked to describe herself, a woman writes "a mother, a nurse, a member of my church". This suggests…
    • An independent self-construal
    • Analytic thinking
    • An interdependent self-construal
    • Display rules
    Show the answer

    Answer: An interdependent self-construal. Defining the self by roles is interdependent.

  3. Why do critics question national culture dimensions?
    • Countries are culturally uniform
    • Hofstede surveyed every citizen
    • Dimensions never predict any behaviour
    • Countries vary internally, and data may be dated
    Show the answer

    Answer: Countries vary internally, and data may be dated. Within-country variation and dated samples limit them.

Key terms

Individualism
Emphasis on personal goals, independence and uniqueness
Collectivism
Emphasis on group harmony, roles and obligations
Interdependent self
A self defined by relationships and social roles
