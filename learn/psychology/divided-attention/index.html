Divided Attention

Divided attention is limited by capacity and shared resources; similar, difficult and unpractised tasks interfere most.

Psychology · Cognitive Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe capacity and multiple-resource theories of divided attention
  • Explain how task similarity, difficulty and practice affect dual-task performance
  • Apply dual-task research to real-world multitasking

Why multitasking is hard

Kahneman (1973) proposed that attention is a limited pool of capacity shared between tasks: when combined demands exceed capacity, performance falls. Wickens proposed multiple resources: tasks interfere most when they use the same resources (both visual, both verbal, both manual) and less when they use different ones (listening while looking).

What affects dual-task performance

  • Similarity: similar tasks interfere more.
  • Difficulty: harder tasks need more capacity.
  • Practice: practised tasks become automatic, needing less attention.

Even simple tasks show a psychological refractory period: when two stimuli need quick responses one after another, the second response is delayed, suggesting a bottleneck in choosing responses (Pashler, 1994).

Worked example

Phones and driving

  1. In a simulated driving task, participants talking on a phone missed more simulated red lights and reacted more slowly than when listening to the radio (Strayer and Johnston, 2001).
  2. Hands-free phones gave similar impairment to hand-held ones.
  3. The problem is attentional (conversation demands central resources), not just holding the phone.
Common mistakeBelieving people can truly do two demanding tasks at once. Most "multitasking" is rapid switching with costs.

In 2 lines: Capacity, multiple-resource and bottleneck theories explain dual-task costs. Similarity, difficulty and practice affect interference; phone use impairs driving even hands-free.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which theory says tasks interfere most when they use the same type of resource?
    • Early selection theory
    • Multiple-resource theory
    • Hick's law
    • Load theory
    Show the answer

    Answer: Multiple-resource theory. Wickens proposed separate pools for different modalities and responses.

  2. Which pair of tasks is likely to interfere least?
    • Reading while listening to a lecture
    • Writing an email while dictating another
    • Listening to music while walking a familiar route
    • Two mental arithmetic tasks
    Show the answer

    Answer: Listening to music while walking a familiar route. Different resources and practised tasks interfere less.

  3. Why do expert typists find it easier to talk while typing than beginners do?
    • Experts have more capacity overall
    • Talking uses no attention
    • Beginners type faster
    • Practice has made typing largely automatic
    Show the answer

    Answer: Practice has made typing largely automatic. Automatic processes need fewer attentional resources.

Key terms

Divided attention
Attending to two or more tasks at once
Automaticity
Performing a task with little attention after extensive practice
Psychological refractory period
The delay in responding to a second stimulus presented soon after a first
