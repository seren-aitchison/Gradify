Reciprocal Altruism

Reciprocal altruism explains helping non-kin when help is returned; it needs repeated interaction and protection against cheaters, which emotions and reputation provide.

Psychology · Evolutionary Psychology and Behavioural Ecology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain reciprocal altruism and the conditions it requires
  • Describe evidence of reciprocity in animals and humans
  • Evaluate how cheating is prevented in reciprocal relationships

You scratch my back

Trivers (1971) proposed reciprocal altruism: helping a non-relative can evolve if the help is likely to be returned later, so both gain over time. Conditions include repeated interactions, the ability to recognise individuals, memory of past interactions and a benefit to the recipient greater than the cost to the helper.

Worked example

Vampire bats

  1. Wilkinson (1984) studied vampire bats, which die if they fail to feed for two or three nights.
  2. Bats that had fed regurgitated blood to hungry roost-mates, especially those who had shared with them before or were close associates.
  3. Carter and Wilkinson (2013) found food sharing was predicted by past reciprocity more than by relatedness.

The cheating problem

Reciprocity is vulnerable to cheaters who take help without returning it. Protection comes from memory of who helped, reputation, punishment of cheaters and moralistic emotions such as gratitude, guilt and anger, which Trivers suggested evolved to regulate reciprocity. Cosmides's cheater-detection research links to this idea.

In humans

Humans engage in extensive reciprocity, from favours between friends to gift exchange. Experiments show people tend to return favours even to strangers (Regan, 1971). Cultural norms of reciprocity (Gouldner, 1960) reinforce the tendency. Critics note humans also help strangers who will never reciprocate, which reciprocal altruism alone can't explain.

Common mistakeTreating reciprocal altruism as conscious bargaining. Emotions like gratitude and guilt can sustain reciprocity without calculation.

In 2 lines: Reciprocal altruism requires repeated interaction, recognition and memory. Vampire bats show reciprocity; cheating is controlled by memory, reputation and emotions.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Who proposed the theory of reciprocal altruism?
    • W. D. Hamilton
    • Charles Darwin
    • Leda Cosmides
    • Robert Trivers
    Show the answer

    Answer: Robert Trivers. Robert Trivers published the theory of reciprocal altruism in 1971.

  2. A bat shares blood mainly with roost-mates that shared with it before. This supports…
    • Reciprocal altruism
    • Kin selection only
    • Sexual selection
    • Mismatch
    Show the answer

    Answer: Reciprocal altruism. Past reciprocity predicted who received shared blood, supporting reciprocal altruism.

  3. Why is cheating a problem for reciprocal altruism?
    • Cheating never happens
    • Cheaters gain without paying costs
    • Reciprocity needs no memory
    • Cheaters always lose
    Show the answer

    Answer: Cheaters gain without paying costs. Unchecked cheaters would outcompete reciprocators.

Key terms

Reciprocal altruism
Helping non-relatives when help is likely to be returned
Cheater
An individual who takes help without returning it
Norm of reciprocity
A social norm that people should return favours
