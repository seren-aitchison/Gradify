Culture and Social Behaviour

Cultures differ in self-construal and thinking style; findings from WEIRD samples may not generalise.

Psychology · Social Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe individualism–collectivism and independent–interdependent selves
  • Explain cultural differences in social behaviour and cognition
  • Evaluate the generalisability of social psychology, including WEIRD samples

Dimensions of culture

Hofstede (1980) surveyed IBM employees in many countries and identified dimensions on which national cultures differ, including individualism–collectivism. Individualist cultures (such as the US, UK and Australia) emphasise personal goals and independence; collectivist cultures (such as China, Japan and Guatemala) emphasise group goals, harmony and obligations. Markus and Kitayama (1991) described independent and interdependent self-construals.

Culture and cognition

Worked example

The fish tank

  1. Masuda and Nisbett (2001) showed Japanese and American students animated underwater scenes.
  2. Japanese participants mentioned the background and relationships between objects more; American participants described the large, focal fish first.
  3. This supports the idea of more holistic thinking in East Asian cultures and more analytic thinking in Western ones.

Cultural differences also appear in conformity (higher in collectivist cultures; Bond and Smith, 1996), attribution (the FAE is weaker in some collectivist cultures) and self-enhancement. Differences are averages: individuals vary within any culture, and countries are not culturally uniform.

WEIRD samples

Henrich, Heine and Norenzayan (2010) noted that most psychology participants come from Western, Educated, Industrialised, Rich and Democratic (WEIRD) societies, often university students, yet such societies are unusual on many measures, from visual illusions to fairness. Findings from WEIRD samples can't be assumed to be universal. Researchers increasingly use cross-cultural and multi-site studies.

Common mistakeStereotyping whole countries from cultural dimensions. Dimensions describe averages with large within-culture variation.

In 2 lines: Cultures differ in individualism–collectivism and self-construal, affecting cognition and social behaviour. WEIRD samples limit generalisation; cross-cultural research is needed.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. A culture emphasising group harmony and obligation is described as…
    • Individualist
    • WEIRD
    • Analytic
    • Collectivist
    Show the answer

    Answer: Collectivist. Collectivism stresses group goals and harmony.

  2. A finding from US undergraduates is assumed to apply worldwide. What is the main problem?
    • WEIRD samples may not generalise
    • The sample is too diverse
    • Culture never matters
    • Students are always typical
    Show the answer

    Answer: WEIRD samples may not generalise. WEIRD populations are unusual on many measures.

  3. How does Bond and Smith's meta-analysis support cultural explanations?
    • Conformity was identical everywhere
    • Conformity was higher in collectivist cultures
    • It studied only one country
    • It found no conformity
    Show the answer

    Answer: Conformity was higher in collectivist cultures. Cross-cultural variation in Asch-type conformity fits cultural values.

Key terms

Individualism
Cultural emphasis on personal goals and independence
Collectivism
Cultural emphasis on group goals, harmony and obligation
WEIRD
Western, educated, industrialised, rich and democratic samples
