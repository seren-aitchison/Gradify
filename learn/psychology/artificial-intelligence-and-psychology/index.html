Artificial Intelligence and Psychology

Psychology and AI share roots in cognitive science and connectionism; psychologists test LLMs with cognitive tasks; AI performance differs from human cognition in important ways; anthropomorphism shapes how people treat AI.

Psychology · Contemporary Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe how psychology and AI have influenced each other
  • Explain how psychologists study large language models
  • Evaluate claims that AI systems think like humans

A shared history

Early cognitive science treated the mind as an information processor, inspiring AI. Connectionist neural network models from the 1980s were developed partly as models of human learning. Modern deep learning grew from these ideas. In turn, AI provides psychologists with new tools for analysing language, images and behaviour.

Studying AI with psychology

Psychologists apply cognitive tasks to large language models (LLMs). Binz and Schulz (2023) tested GPT-3 on classic tasks and found it performed well on some decision-making and reasoning problems but failed on small changes to others and showed no signs of directed exploration. Lake et al. (2017) argued that human-like intelligence requires abilities such as causal models, intuitive physics and learning from few examples, which many AI systems lacked.

Worked example

Anthropomorphism

  1. People readily attribute minds, emotions and intentions to chatbots and robots, a tendency called anthropomorphism.
  2. This can increase trust and engagement but may lead people to overestimate what systems understand.
  3. Psychologists study how design features, such as human-like language, shape these attributions.

Evaluation

AI systems can match or exceed humans on some tasks, but success on benchmarks may reflect training data rather than human-like understanding. Comparing AI and human cognition helps clarify what intelligence involves. Psychological research on trust, bias and over-reliance is important as AI is used more widely.

Common mistakeAssuming good performance on a task means an AI understands it as humans do. Mechanisms may differ.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Attributing human feelings and intentions to a chatbot is…
    • Connectionism
    • Overfitting
    • Anthropomorphism
    • Reverse inference
    Show the answer

    Answer: Anthropomorphism. Anthropomorphism attributes minds to non-humans.

  2. An AI solves a famous logic puzzle but fails when the names and numbers are changed slightly. This suggests…
    • It understands the puzzle deeply
    • It reasons exactly like a human
    • The puzzle has no correct answer
    • It may rely on training data, not understanding
    Show the answer

    Answer: It may rely on training data, not understanding. Fragility to small changes suggests memorisation.

  3. Why is research on trust in AI important for psychology?
    • People may over-rely on systems they overestimate
    • AI systems never make mistakes
    • Trust has no effect on how AI is used
    • People never use AI in daily life
    Show the answer

    Answer: People may over-rely on systems they overestimate. Over-trust in AI can lead people to accept errors and cause harm.

Key terms

Connectionism
Modelling cognition with networks of simple units
Large language model
An AI system trained on large amounts of text to generate language
Anthropomorphism
Attributing human characteristics to non-human things
