Independent T-tests

The independent t-test compares two group means; report t, degrees of freedom, p and an effect size, and prefer Welch's version when variances may differ.

Psychology · Statistics · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Identify when an independent t-test is appropriate
  • Interpret and report a t-test result with degrees of freedom, p and effect size
  • Evaluate the assumptions of the t-test and the case for Welch's test

When to use it

Use an independent t-test when comparing the means of two independent groups (different participants in each) on an interval or ratio DV. The test, devised by W. S. Gosset writing as "Student" (1908), calculates t: the difference between the means divided by the standard error of the difference. Larger |t| means the difference is large relative to the variability.

Assumptions

  • Independent observations.
  • Interval or ratio DV.
  • Approximately normal distributions within groups (less important with larger samples).
  • Equal variances (Student's version). Welch's t-test doesn't assume equal variances; many methodologists recommend it as the default (Delacre, Lakens and Leys, 2017).
Worked example

A worked example

  1. Condition A scores: 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 (mean 7). Condition B: 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 (mean 5). Both SDs ≈ 1.58.
  2. Student's t(8) = 2.00, p = .081 two-tailed; Cohen's d ≈ 1.26.
  3. Report: "Condition A (M = 7.0, SD = 1.58) scored higher than condition B (M = 5.0, SD = 1.58), but the difference was not significant, t(8) = 2.00, p = .081, d = 1.26." The large d with a non-significant p shows how little information five per group provides.

Reporting the confidence interval for the difference between means shows the range of plausible effects, which is especially useful when p is close to .05.

Investigate with the lab. Run an independent t-test simulation and see how sample size changes the power to detect a real difference.

Common mistakeUsing an independent t-test on repeated measures data. The same people in both conditions need a paired t-test.

In 2 lines: The independent t-test compares two separate groups' means. Report t, df, p and d; check assumptions and consider Welch's test.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Who first published the t-test?
    • Ronald Fisher
    • Karl Pearson
    • Jacob Cohen
    • W. S. Gosset ("Student")
    Show the answer

    Answer: W. S. Gosset ("Student"). Gosset published it in 1908 under the pen name Student.

  2. Which test fits comparing anxiety scores of 30 therapy clients with 30 waiting-list controls?
    • Independent t-test
    • Paired t-test
    • Chi-square test
    • Pearson's r
    Show the answer

    Answer: Independent t-test. Two separate groups compared on an interval DV call for an independent t-test.

  3. Group A has 10 participants with SD 2; group B has 40 with SD 9. Which test is most appropriate?
    • Student's t-test
    • Welch's t-test
    • Paired t-test
    • Spearman's rho
    Show the answer

    Answer: Welch's t-test. Unequal variances and group sizes make Welch's test safer.

  4. When is an independent t-test appropriate?
    • Comparing the same group twice
    • Testing an association between categories
    • Comparing two separate groups on interval data
    • Comparing more than two groups at once
    Show the answer

    Answer: Comparing two separate groups on interval data. It compares the means of two independent groups.

Key terms

Independent t-test
A test comparing the means of two independent groups
Degrees of freedom
The number of values free to vary; for an independent t-test, n₁ + n₂ − 2
Welch's t-test
A version of the t-test that doesn't assume equal variances
