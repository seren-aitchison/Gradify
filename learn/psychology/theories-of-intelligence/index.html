Theories of Intelligence

Strong evidence supports a general factor within a hierarchy of abilities; broader theories highlight valued skills but have weaker evidence for separate intelligences.

Psychology · Cognitive Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe Spearman's g and hierarchical models of intelligence
  • Explain Gardner's and Sternberg's broader theories
  • Evaluate theories of intelligence using evidence

The general factor

Spearman (1904) noticed that people who did well on one mental test tended to do well on others: scores were positively correlated. He proposed a general intelligence factor (g) underlying performance on all tests, plus specific abilities. Factor analysis of many test batteries still finds a strong general factor.

Hierarchical models such as the Cattell–Horn–Carroll (CHC) model place g at the top, broad abilities in the middle (including fluid intelligence, reasoning with new problems, and crystallised intelligence, accumulated knowledge) and narrow skills at the bottom (Carroll, 1993).

Broader theories

Gardner's multiple intelligences (1983) proposed separate intelligences: linguistic, logical-mathematical, spatial, musical, bodily-kinaesthetic, interpersonal, intrapersonal and naturalistic. It has been popular in education, but there is little evidence that these are independent abilities or that teaching to "intelligence types" helps learning (Waterhouse, 2006).

Sternberg's triarchic theory distinguishes analytical, creative and practical intelligence, emphasising success in real life.

Worked example

Fluid and crystallised intelligence across age

  1. Fluid reasoning tends to peak in early adulthood and decline gradually.
  2. Crystallised knowledge such as vocabulary often keeps rising into later adulthood.
  3. This pattern supports distinguishing the two abilities.
Common mistakeEquating intelligence with worth. Intelligence tests measure particular cognitive abilities, not a person's value.

In 2 lines: Spearman's g and hierarchical models describe intelligence as general plus specific abilities. Gardner and Sternberg broaden intelligence; evidence for Gardner's separate intelligences is weak.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Who proposed the general intelligence factor g?
    • Howard Gardner
    • Charles Spearman
    • Robert Sternberg
    • Alfred Binet
    Show the answer

    Answer: Charles Spearman. Spearman noticed positive correlations among test scores.

  2. An older adult has a large vocabulary but finds new puzzles harder than when young. This illustrates…
    • Gardner's multiple intelligences
    • Sternberg's practical intelligence
    • Crystallised versus fluid ability
    • The Flynn effect over decades
    Show the answer

    Answer: Crystallised versus fluid ability. Knowledge rises while new reasoning declines.

  3. What is the main criticism of Gardner's theory?
    • It includes too few abilities
    • It relies on g alone
    • It leaves out musical ability
    • Weak evidence they are separate
    Show the answer

    Answer: Weak evidence they are separate. Measures of his intelligences are often correlated or untested.

Key terms

g (general intelligence)
A general factor underlying performance on many cognitive tests
Fluid intelligence
The ability to reason with and solve new problems
Crystallised intelligence
Accumulated knowledge and skills
