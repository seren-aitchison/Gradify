The Biological Model

The biological model links difficulties to genes, neurochemistry and brain structure; it informed medications; simple neurotransmitter theories such as low serotonin have been challenged; correlation isn't causation.

Psychology · Psychopathology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe genetic, neurochemical and structural explanations
  • Explain how biological explanations inform treatments
  • Evaluate the strengths and limitations of the biological model
Competence and safetyThis lesson is educational and is not a guide to diagnosis or treatment. Anyone concerned about their own or someone else's mental health should speak to a GP or another qualified professional.

Main claims

The biological model proposes that psychological difficulties are associated with genetic influences, neurochemical differences and brain structure or function. Twin and family studies show many conditions are partly heritable. Neurochemical theories, such as dopamine involvement in psychosis, guided the development of medications.

Evidence and treatment

Antipsychotic medications that block dopamine receptors can reduce some symptoms of psychosis, supporting a role for dopamine. Imaging studies show average group differences in brain structure and activity in some conditions. However, the serotonin hypothesis of depression, that depression is caused by low serotonin, has been strongly challenged; Moncrieff et al. (2022) reviewed the evidence and found no consistent support for it, though antidepressants can still help some people.

Worked example

Correlation and causation

  1. Finding a brain difference in people with a condition doesn't show the difference caused it.
  2. The difference might result from the condition, from medication, or from shared causes such as stress.
  3. Longitudinal and experimental designs are needed to establish direction.

Evaluation

The biological model has produced effective treatments for some conditions and reduces blame by framing difficulties as health conditions. But it can be reductionist, overlooking psychological and social causes, and biological explanations can increase perceptions of permanence and stigma. Most researchers now favour integrated models.

Common mistakeAssuming a brain difference proves a biological cause. Direction and confounds must be considered.

In 2 lines: Genes, neurochemistry and brain structure are linked to difficulties. Medications developed from it; simple neurotransmitter theories challenged; reductionism critique.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Explaining complex behaviour by its simplest parts is called…
    • Holism
    • Formulation
    • Reductionism
    • Comorbidity
    Show the answer

    Answer: Reductionism. Reductionism breaks behaviour into basic components.

  2. A study finds a brain difference in people with a condition and concludes the difference caused it. This ignores…
    • The heritability of the condition
    • The benefits of reductionism
    • The history of moral treatment
    • Possible reverse causation and confounds
    Show the answer

    Answer: Possible reverse causation and confounds. The condition or medication may cause the difference.

  3. Why might biological explanations increase stigma?
    • They can make difficulties seem permanent
    • They always reduce stigma for everyone
    • They blame individuals for their choices
    • They ignore the role of genes
    Show the answer

    Answer: They can make difficulties seem permanent. Perceived permanence can increase social distance.

Key terms

Reductionism
Explaining complex behaviour by its simplest parts
Neurotransmitter
A chemical messenger between neurons
Heritability
The proportion of variation in a trait associated with genetic variation
