Leadership

Leadership depends on traits, style, situation and the leader's ability to represent the group's identity.

Psychology · Social Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe trait and behavioural approaches to leadership
  • Explain contingency and transformational leadership
  • Evaluate the social identity approach to leadership

Traits and styles

Early trait approaches searched for qualities that make a leader. Meta-analyses find modest links between leadership emergence and traits such as extraversion and conscientiousness (Judge et al., 2002), but no trait guarantees leadership. Behavioural approaches studied styles. Lewin, Lippitt and White (1939) compared boys' clubs led in autocratic, democratic and laissez-faire styles: democratic groups were more cooperative and kept working when the leader left, while autocratic groups worked hard only when the leader was present.

It depends

Contingency theories argue the best style depends on the situation. Fiedler (1967) proposed that task-oriented leaders do best in very favourable or very unfavourable situations, and relationship-oriented leaders in moderate ones. Transformational leaders inspire followers with a vision, attend to individuals and encourage new thinking; transactional leaders use rewards and corrections. Transformational leadership is associated with higher follower satisfaction and performance (Judge and Piccolo, 2004).

Social identity and leadership

Haslam, Reicher and Platow (2011) argue effective leaders are seen as prototypical of the group ("one of us"), champion the group's interests, craft a shared sense of who "we" are and embed that identity in practice. Leaders gain influence by representing the group, not just by their personal qualities.

Worked example

Two head teachers

  1. One head teacher announces new rules by email and checks compliance.
  2. Another discusses the school's shared values with staff, presents changes as "how we do things here" and stands up for staff in disputes.
  3. The second fits transformational and social identity approaches and is more likely to win commitment.
Common mistakeThinking leaders are simply "born". Traits help modestly; situation and group identity matter greatly.

In 2 lines: Trait and style approaches give partial explanations of leadership. Contingency, transformational and social identity approaches stress situation, vision and shared identity.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. A leader who makes all decisions alone uses which style?
    • Autocratic
    • Democratic
    • Laissez-faire
    • Transformational
    Show the answer

    Answer: Autocratic. Autocratic leaders decide without consulting the group.

  2. A manager inspires staff with a vision and supports their development. This is…
    • Transactional leadership
    • Transformational leadership
    • Laissez-faire leadership
    • Autocratic leadership
    Show the answer

    Answer: Transformational leadership. Vision and individual attention define transformational leadership.

  3. Why might a leader who is very different from the group struggle, according to the social identity approach?
    • They are too democratic
    • They have no traits
    • They aren't seen as prototypical
    • Groups don't need leaders
    Show the answer

    Answer: They aren't seen as prototypical. Leaders gain influence by representing "us".

Key terms

Transformational leadership
Inspiring followers with a vision and attention to their development
Contingency theory
The view that effective leadership depends on the situation
Prototypicality
How far a leader represents what is typical of the group
