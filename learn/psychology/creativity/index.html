Creativity

Creativity combines originality and usefulness; it can be measured by divergent thinking and is supported by incubation, intrinsic motivation and openness.

Psychology · Cognitive Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define creativity and describe divergent-thinking measures
  • Explain stage models of the creative process and incubation
  • Evaluate factors that encourage or hinder creativity

Defining and measuring creativity

Most researchers define creativity as producing work that is both original and appropriate (useful or valuable) (Runco and Jaeger, 2012). Guilford (1950) distinguished convergent thinking (finding the single correct answer) from divergent thinking (generating many possible answers). Divergent-thinking tests such as the Alternative Uses Task ("list uses for a brick") score fluency (number of ideas), flexibility (number of categories) and originality (rarity).

The creative process

Wallas (1926) proposed four stages: preparation (studying the problem), incubation (setting it aside), illumination (the idea appears) and verification (testing it). A meta-analysis found incubation periods modestly improve creative problem solving, especially for divergent tasks (Sio and Ormerod, 2009).

Worked example

Motivation and creativity

  1. Amabile (1985) asked creative writers to write poems after reflecting on either intrinsic reasons (enjoyment) or extrinsic reasons (money, recognition) for writing.
  2. Poems written after considering extrinsic reasons were judged less creative.
  3. Intrinsic motivation tends to support creativity, though rewards for creative performance can sometimes help.

Creativity is linked to openness to experience, domain knowledge and environments that allow risk-taking and time. Intelligence correlates modestly with creativity, more strongly at lower levels of IQ.

Common mistakeAssuming creativity is only for artists. Creativity matters in science, business and everyday problem solving.

In 2 lines: Creativity is novel and useful; divergent-thinking tests score fluency, flexibility and originality. Wallas's stages and incubation describe the process; intrinsic motivation and openness support it.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. "List as many uses for a paperclip as you can" measures…
    • Convergent thinking
    • Crystallised intelligence
    • Divergent thinking
    • Working memory
    Show the answer

    Answer: Divergent thinking. Generating many ideas is divergent thinking.

  2. A scientist solves a problem in the shower after a week away from it. Which stage of Wallas's model came just before?
    • Preparation
    • Verification
    • Convergence
    • Incubation
    Show the answer

    Answer: Incubation. Time away from the problem precedes illumination.

  3. Which is the main limitation of divergent-thinking tests?
    • Weak links to real achievement
    • They count no ideas at all
    • They measure only memory
    • They take far too long
    Show the answer

    Answer: Weak links to real achievement. Lab fluency may not predict real-world creative work.

Key terms

Divergent thinking
Generating many possible solutions to an open problem
Convergent thinking
Finding the single best answer to a problem
Incubation
Setting a problem aside, after which solutions may come more easily
