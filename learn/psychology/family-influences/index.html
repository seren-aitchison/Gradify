Family Influences

Family conflict, economic stress and relationships shape development; divorce has small average effects, much of which reflects conflict and economic change.

Psychology · Developmental Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain how parental conflict and divorce relate to child outcomes
  • Describe sibling influences and the family stress model
  • Evaluate research on family influences, including methodological limits

Conflict and divorce

Children whose parents divorce show, on average, slightly poorer outcomes in well-being, behaviour and achievement than those whose parents remain together, but the differences are small and most children adjust well within a few years (Amato, 2001). Much of the difference is linked to conflict before and after separation, economic loss and changes in parenting. Ongoing, intense interparental conflict, especially conflict children are drawn into, is consistently linked to poorer child outcomes, whether or not parents separate.

Siblings

Siblings are companions, rivals, teachers and models. Older siblings may help younger ones learn, including theory of mind. Parents may treat siblings differently (differential treatment), and children who perceive they are treated less favourably tend to have poorer adjustment.

Worked example

The family stress model

  1. Conger and colleagues studied farm families in Iowa during the economic crisis of the 1980s.
  2. Economic hardship increased parents' emotional distress and conflict between parents.
  3. These in turn led to harsher, less involved parenting, which was associated with adolescent problems (Conger et al., 1994).

Methodological issues

Family research is mostly correlational. Families aren't randomly assigned to divorce or poverty, and genes shared by parents and children can explain some associations. Designs such as adoption studies, twin studies and natural experiments help. Costello et al. (2003) found that when a casino opened on a Native American reservation in North Carolina and families received income payments, children's behaviour problems decreased among families lifted out of poverty.

Common mistakeAssuming divorce inevitably harms children. Average effects are small and most children adjust well, while ongoing high conflict is more harmful.

In 2 lines: Divorce has small average effects; conflict and economic stress matter more. Siblings and differential treatment shape adjustment; natural experiments help test causes.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which family factor is most consistently linked to poorer child outcomes?
    • Having exactly one sibling
    • Living in a large city
    • The parents' ages at birth
    • Intense parental conflict
    Show the answer

    Answer: Intense parental conflict. Persistent conflict is strongly linked to difficulties.

  2. Families on a reservation received casino income, and children's behaviour problems fell. Which design is this?
    • A natural experiment
    • A twin study
    • A randomised trial
    • A case study
    Show the answer

    Answer: A natural experiment. A real-world event changed income for some families.

  3. Why are family influence studies often hard to interpret?
    • Families are randomly assigned
    • Shared genes and selection confound associations
    • Children are never asked
    • All findings are causal
    Show the answer

    Answer: Shared genes and selection confound associations. Without random assignment, other factors may explain links.

Key terms

Interparental conflict
Conflict between parents or carers
Family stress model
The model linking economic hardship to parental distress, conflict and harsher parenting
Natural experiment
A real-world event that changes circumstances for some people but not others
