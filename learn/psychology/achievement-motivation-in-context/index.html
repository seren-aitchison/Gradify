Achievement Motivation in Context

Achievement motivation is shaped by classroom goal structures, cultural beliefs and stereotypes; brief interventions can help in some contexts but effects vary.

Psychology · Motivation, Emotion and Wellbeing · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain how classroom goal structures shape achievement motivation
  • Explain cultural and social influences on achievement motivation, including stereotype threat
  • Evaluate interventions that aim to improve achievement motivation

Classroom climates

Achievement goal theory distinguishes mastery goals (learning and improving) from performance goals (outperforming others). Classrooms also have goal structures: a mastery-oriented classroom emphasises effort, improvement and understanding; a performance-oriented one emphasises grades, rankings and comparison. Meece, Anderman and Anderman (2006) reviewed evidence that mastery-oriented classrooms are associated with deeper learning and engagement.

Culture and expectations

Cultures differ in beliefs about effort and ability. Stevenson and Stigler (1992) found Japanese and Chinese parents and students emphasised effort more than ability when explaining success, compared with American families. Stereotype threat (Steele and Aronson, 1995), concern about confirming a negative group stereotype, can reduce performance in some conditions, though replications suggest effects are smaller than first reported.

Worked example

A values-affirmation study

  1. Cohen et al. (2006) asked some middle-school students to write briefly about values important to them (values affirmation), and others about unimportant values.
  2. Among African American students, affirmation was associated with higher grades over the term, reducing the racial achievement gap in the study.
  3. Later large replications found smaller or inconsistent effects (Hanselman et al., 2017), showing the importance of context.

Evaluation

Context-focused approaches show achievement motivation isn't just a personal trait. Brief "wise" interventions can help in some settings, but effects vary and are often small at scale. Structural factors, such as school resources and inequality, also matter.

Common mistakeTreating achievement motivation as only an individual trait. Classrooms and culture shape it strongly.

In 2 lines: Classroom goal structures and cultural beliefs shape achievement motivation. Stereotype threat and values affirmation show context effects, though replications show smaller effects.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. A classroom that ranks students publicly by test scores has a…
    • Mastery goal structure
    • Performance goal structure
    • Learning goal
    • Values affirmation
    Show the answer

    Answer: Performance goal structure. Public ranking emphasises comparison, creating a performance goal structure.

  2. Students write about their most important values before a term, and some groups' grades improve. This is…
    • Overjustification
    • Drive reduction
    • Values affirmation
    • Introjection
    Show the answer

    Answer: Values affirmation. Writing about important values may reduce threat and support performance.

  3. Why do brief interventions often show smaller effects at scale?
    • They always fail
    • They are always large
    • Context never matters
    • Effects depend on context
    Show the answer

    Answer: Effects depend on context. Interventions work only where conditions suit them.

Key terms

Goal structure
The emphasis a classroom places on mastery or performance
Values affirmation
Writing about personally important values to reduce threat
Stereotype threat
Concern about confirming a negative group stereotype that can impair performance
