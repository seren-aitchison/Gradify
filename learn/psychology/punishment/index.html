Punishment

Punishment decreases behaviour, but it is less effective than reinforcing alternatives and has side effects, especially physical punishment.

Psychology · Learning and Behaviour · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define positive and negative punishment
  • Explain the conditions under which punishment is most and least effective
  • Evaluate the use of physical punishment, with reference to evidence

Two kinds

  • Positive punishment: adding an unpleasant stimulus after a behaviour, to decrease it (a reprimand, a fine).
  • Negative punishment: removing a pleasant stimulus after a behaviour, to decrease it (losing phone time, time out from positive reinforcement).

When punishment works and fails

Punishment suppresses behaviour most when it is immediate, consistent and paired with reinforcement of an alternative behaviour. Its problems: it shows what not to do, not what to do; it can produce fear, anxiety and avoidance of the punisher; suppressed behaviour may return when the punisher is absent; and it models aggression.

Worked example

Two responses to a child's tantrum in a shop

  1. Positive punishment: shouting at the child. The tantrum may stop briefly, but the child learns little about what to do instead.
  2. Negative punishment plus reinforcement: calmly removing a privilege, and praising the child later when they ask calmly.
  3. Research favours the second approach.

Physical punishment

Meta-analyses have found physical punishment (spanking) associated with more child aggression, worse mental health and poorer parent–child relationships, and not with better long-term compliance (Gershoff, 2002; Gershoff and Grogan-Kaylor, 2016). Most of this evidence is correlational, so causation is inferred cautiously, but longitudinal studies controlling for earlier behaviour show similar patterns. Physical punishment of children was made unlawful in Scotland from 2020 and in Wales from 2022; in England a defence of "reasonable punishment" remains.

Common mistakeEquating negative punishment with negative reinforcement. Negative punishment decreases behaviour; negative reinforcement increases it.

In 2 lines: Positive punishment adds something unpleasant; negative punishment removes something pleasant; both decrease behaviour. Punishment works best when immediate and paired with reinforcement of alternatives; physical punishment is linked to harm.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Taking away a teenager's phone for breaking curfew is…
    • Positive punishment
    • Negative reinforcement
    • Negative punishment
    • Positive reinforcement
    Show the answer

    Answer: Negative punishment. Removing something pleasant to decrease a behaviour is negative punishment.

  2. Which approach is most likely to reduce a pupil's shouting out in class?
    • Shout back each time
    • Punish only occasionally
    • Give sweets after shouting
    • Ignore shouting and praise hand-raising
    Show the answer

    Answer: Ignore shouting and praise hand-raising. Reinforcing an alternative behaviour while not reinforcing the problem behaviour is most effective.

  3. Which is the best evaluation of research on physical punishment?
    • Mostly correlational, but longitudinal studies also link it to harm
    • It proves physical punishment improves compliance
    • It shows no association with any outcome
    • It is based only on animal studies
    Show the answer

    Answer: Mostly correlational, but longitudinal studies also link it to harm. Correlational designs limit causal claims, but longitudinal controls and consistent findings point to harm.
