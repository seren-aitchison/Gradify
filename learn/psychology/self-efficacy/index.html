Self-efficacy

Self-efficacy is task-specific belief in capability, built mainly by mastery experiences, and predicts effort, persistence and success.

Psychology · Personality and Individual Differences · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define self-efficacy and distinguish it from self-esteem
  • Explain Bandura's four sources of self-efficacy
  • Apply self-efficacy theory to education and health behaviour change

Belief in capability

Bandura (1977) defined self-efficacy as belief in one's capability to organise and carry out actions needed for a specific goal. Unlike self-esteem, it is task-specific: a person may have high self-efficacy for maths but low for public speaking. Higher self-efficacy predicts the choice of challenging tasks, more effort and greater persistence.

Four sources

  • Mastery experiences: past successes, the strongest source.
  • Vicarious experiences: seeing similar others succeed.
  • Verbal persuasion: encouragement from others.
  • Physiological and emotional states: interpreting anxiety as a sign of inability can lower self-efficacy; calm or excitement can raise it.
Worked example

Snake phobia

  1. Bandura, Adams and Beyer (1977) treated adults with a snake phobia by guided participant modelling.
  2. Participants watched a therapist handle a snake, then gradually did so themselves with support.
  3. Gains in self-efficacy predicted how far participants could approach snakes afterwards, even in new tasks.

Applications

In education, a meta-analysis found self-efficacy was related to academic performance (Multon, Brown and Lent, 1991). In health, self-efficacy predicts success in quitting smoking and increasing exercise, and is part of models such as the theory of planned behaviour (perceived behavioural control). Interventions build it through small achievable goals, modelling and feedback.

Common mistakeThinking self-efficacy is general confidence. It is specific to tasks and situations.

In 2 lines: Self-efficacy is task-specific belief in capability. It comes from mastery, vicarious experience, persuasion and emotional states, and predicts persistence and success.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. The strongest source of self-efficacy is…
    • Verbal persuasion
    • Vicarious experience
    • Physiological states
    • Mastery experiences
    Show the answer

    Answer: Mastery experiences. Past successes build the strongest beliefs.

  2. A student gains confidence in maths after watching a classmate like her solve a hard problem. Which source is this?
    • Vicarious experience
    • Mastery experience
    • Verbal persuasion
    • Physiological state
    Show the answer

    Answer: Vicarious experience. Seeing a similar other succeed raises self-efficacy.

  3. Why did Bandura's phobia treatment work through self-efficacy?
    • It removed all fear chemically
    • Guided success raised belief in coping
    • It used persuasion only
    • It avoided snakes
    Show the answer

    Answer: Guided success raised belief in coping. Mastery with support built task-specific confidence.

Key terms

Self-efficacy
Belief in one's capability to succeed at a specific task
Mastery experience
Past success at a task, the strongest source of self-efficacy
Vicarious experience
Gaining self-efficacy by seeing similar others succeed
