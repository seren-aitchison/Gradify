The Humanistic Model

Rogers linked distress to incongruence and conditions of worth; Maslow proposed a hierarchy of needs; the model stresses growth and relationships but its concepts are hard to measure.

Psychology · Psychopathology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain Rogers's concepts of congruence and conditions of worth
  • Describe Maslow's hierarchy of needs and self-actualisation
  • Evaluate the humanistic model
Competence and safetyThis lesson is educational and is not a guide to diagnosis or treatment. Anyone concerned about their own or someone else's mental health should speak to a GP or another qualified professional.

Rogers

Rogers proposed that people have a natural tendency towards growth and self-actualisation. Distress arises from incongruence: a gap between the self-concept and the ideal self or actual experience. Conditions of worth, when love and approval depend on meeting others' expectations, lead people to deny parts of themselves. Unconditional positive regard supports congruence.

Maslow

Maslow (1943) proposed a hierarchy of needs: physiological, safety, love and belonging, esteem, and self-actualisation. When lower needs are unmet, people focus on meeting them. Psychological difficulties may arise when needs for belonging or esteem are frustrated.

Worked example

Conditions of worth

  1. A child praised only when they get top marks may learn they are valued only for achievement.
  2. As an adult, they may feel worthless after any failure.
  3. Rogers would see this as incongruence created by conditions of worth. This example is illustrative.

Evaluation

The humanistic model emphasises free will, personal meaning and the therapeutic relationship, and person-centred therapy is widely used. Its concepts, such as self-actualisation, are hard to measure, and it may suit some difficulties better than others. Maslow's hierarchy has weak evidence for a fixed order of needs.

Common mistakeAssuming humanistic ideas lack influence. The therapeutic relationship they emphasised is central in many therapies.

In 2 lines: Rogers: incongruence and conditions of worth. Maslow's hierarchy; strengths in relationships, weaknesses in measurement.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Acceptance without conditions is called…
    • Conditions of worth
    • Incongruence
    • Unconditional positive regard
    • Displacement
    Show the answer

    Answer: Unconditional positive regard. Rogers stressed unconditional positive regard.

  2. A young man feels he must hide his interests because his parents only approve of sport. Rogers would describe this as the effect of…
    • Unconditional positive regard
    • Self-actualisation
    • Learned helplessness
    • Conditions of worth
    Show the answer

    Answer: Conditions of worth. Conditional approval leads people to deny parts of themselves.

  3. Why is the humanistic model hard to test scientifically?
    • Concepts such as self-actualisation are hard to measure
    • It makes very precise predictions
    • It rejects the role of relationships
    • It focuses mainly on biology
    Show the answer

    Answer: Concepts such as self-actualisation are hard to measure. Vague concepts such as self-actualisation resist reliable measurement and testing.
