Culture and Cognition

Holistic and analytic thinking styles differ on average between East Asian and Western samples; Masuda and Nisbett found differences in attention to context; the WEIRD critique challenges assumptions of universality.

Psychology · Cultural and Cross-Cultural Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe analytic and holistic thinking styles
  • Explain evidence that culture shapes attention and reasoning
  • Evaluate the WEIRD critique of psychological research

Analytic and holistic thinking

Nisbett and colleagues proposed that people from East Asian cultures tend towards holistic thinking, attending to context, relationships and change, while people from Western cultures tend towards analytic thinking, focusing on objects, categories and rules. These are tendencies, not fixed traits, and vary within cultures.

Evidence on attention

Masuda and Nisbett (2001) showed Japanese and American students animated underwater scenes. When describing them, Japanese participants made more statements about the background and relationships between objects, while Americans focused more on the large, central fish. Japanese participants were also more affected when the main fish appeared against a new background, suggesting they had encoded it together with its context.

Worked example

The WEIRD critique

  1. Henrich, Heine and Norenzayan (2010) noted that most psychological research uses participants from WEIRD societies: Western, Educated, Industrialised, Rich and Democratic.
  2. They showed that WEIRD participants are often unusual compared with the rest of humanity on measures such as visual perception, fairness and reasoning.
  3. They argued that findings from WEIRD samples can't be assumed to be universal.

Evaluation

Cross-cultural cognition research shows that some processes once assumed universal are shaped by experience. But comparisons between nations can stereotype cultures and overlook variation within them, such as by region, class or generation. Effects are often moderate, and some findings have been hard to replicate. Researchers increasingly study how cultural practices, such as language and social structure, produce differences.

Common mistakeTreating cultural differences as fixed national traits. They are average tendencies that vary within cultures.

In 2 lines: Holistic and analytic thinking styles differ on average across cultures. Masuda and Nisbett showed attention to context; WEIRD samples limit generalisation.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Focusing on objects, categories and rules rather than context is…
    • Analytic thinking
    • Holistic thinking
    • Collectivism
    • Acculturation
    Show the answer

    Answer: Analytic thinking. Analytic thinking isolates objects from their context.

  2. Shown an aquarium scene, a participant describes the plants, rocks and how fish interact rather than the biggest fish. This fits…
    • Analytic attention
    • Holistic attention
    • Ethnocentrism
    • Cultural relativism
    Show the answer

    Answer: Holistic attention. Attending to context and relationships is holistic.

  3. Why must cross-cultural comparisons of thinking style be interpreted cautiously?
    • Culture never affects cognition at all
    • All samples are fully representative
    • They can stereotype nations and hide variation
    • Effects are always extremely large
    Show the answer

    Answer: They can stereotype nations and hide variation. National averages hide regional, class and generational variation.
