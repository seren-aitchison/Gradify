Action Potentials

An action potential is an all-or-none spike of depolarisation and repolarisation; intensity is coded by firing rate.

Psychology · Biological Psychology and Neuroscience · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the stages of an action potential
  • Explain threshold, the all-or-none law and the refractory period
  • Explain how signal strength is coded by firing rate

Stages

When input pushes the membrane potential up to the threshold (about −55 mV), voltage-gated sodium channels open. Na⁺ rushes in and the inside becomes positive, peaking near +30 to +40 mV: depolarisation. Sodium channels then close and potassium channels open; K⁺ flows out and the potential falls: repolarisation. It briefly dips below resting level (hyperpolarisation) before the pump restores the resting state. The whole cycle takes about 1–2 milliseconds.

Rules of firing

The all-or-none law states that if threshold is reached, an action potential fires at full strength; if not, it doesn't fire. During the refractory period, just after firing, the neurone can't fire again (absolute) or needs stronger input (relative). This makes the impulse travel in one direction and limits how fast a neurone can fire.

Worked example

Coding intensity

  1. A gentle touch and a firm press both produce action potentials of the same size.
  2. The firm press produces a higher rate of firing and activates more neurones.
  3. The brain reads intensity from firing rate and the number of neurones active, not from the size of each spike.

Hodgkin and Huxley (1952) worked out the ionic basis of the action potential using the giant axon of the squid, which is large enough to place electrodes inside; they shared the 1963 Nobel Prize for this work.

Common mistakeThinking stronger stimuli produce bigger action potentials. They produce more frequent ones.

In 2 lines: Reaching threshold opens sodium channels, causing depolarisation; potassium outflow repolarises. Firing is all-or-none; intensity is coded by firing rate; refractory periods make signals one-way.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which ion rushes into the neurone during depolarisation?
    • Sodium
    • Potassium
    • Chloride
    • Calcium
    Show the answer

    Answer: Sodium. Voltage-gated sodium channels open at threshold.

  2. Input raises a neurone's potential from −70 mV to −60 mV. What happens?
    • A small action potential fires
    • No action potential fires
    • A full action potential fires
    • The neurone dies
    Show the answer

    Answer: No action potential fires. −60 mV is below the threshold of about −55 mV.

  3. Why do action potentials travel only in one direction along the axon?
    • Sodium flows backwards
    • Myelin blocks the cell body
    • The area just behind is refractory
    • Dendrites send signals out
    Show the answer

    Answer: The area just behind is refractory. Recently fired membrane can't immediately fire again.

Key terms

Threshold
The membrane potential at which an action potential is triggered, about −55 mV
Depolarisation
The membrane potential becoming less negative or positive
Refractory period
A brief time after firing when a neurone can't fire or needs stronger input
