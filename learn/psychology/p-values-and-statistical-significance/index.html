P-values and Statistical Significance

A p-value is the probability of data this extreme if the null is true; it isn't the probability the null is true, and it doesn't measure importance.

Psychology · Statistics · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define a p-value correctly
  • Identify common misinterpretations of p-values
  • Evaluate the use of statistical significance in research

What a p-value is

A p-value is the probability of obtaining data at least as extreme as those observed, if the null hypothesis were true. A small p-value means the data would be surprising under the null. If p is below α (usually .05), the result is called statistically significant.

What a p-value is not

  • It is not the probability that the null hypothesis is true.
  • It is not the probability that the result happened "by chance".
  • It does not measure the size or importance of an effect.
  • p = .049 and p = .051 are not meaningfully different evidence.

The American Statistical Association's 2016 statement warned that scientific conclusions shouldn't rest only on whether p passes a threshold, and that p-values should be reported with effect sizes and confidence intervals.

Worked example

Significant but trivial

  1. A survey of 100,000 people finds that one group sleeps 2 minutes more per night, p < .001.
  2. The tiny p-value reflects the huge sample, not an important effect.
  3. The effect size (2 minutes) shows the finding has little practical significance.
Common mistakeWriting "p = .03, so there is a 97% chance the hypothesis is true". p-values don't give the probability of hypotheses.

In 2 lines: The p-value measures how surprising the data are under the null. It isn't the probability of a hypothesis, nor a measure of effect size; report effects and intervals too.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. A result is usually called statistically significant when…
    • p is greater than .05
    • The effect is large
    • p is less than .05
    • The sample is small
    Show the answer

    Answer: p is less than .05. By convention, results with p below .05 are called statistically significant.

  2. A study reports p = .002 with 50,000 participants and a tiny difference between groups. What should a reader conclude?
    • The effect must be large and important
    • The null hypothesis has a 0.2% chance of being true
    • The study was badly designed
    • The effect is detectable but may be too small to matter
    Show the answer

    Answer: The effect is detectable but may be too small to matter. Large samples make small effects significant; check effect size.

  3. Which practice best addresses problems with p-values?
    • Reporting effect sizes and confidence intervals with p
    • Reporting only whether p < .05
    • Using p-values only for large samples
    • Removing all statistics from papers
    Show the answer

    Answer: Reporting effect sizes and confidence intervals with p. Estimates with uncertainty give fuller information than a threshold decision.

Key terms

p-value
The probability of data at least as extreme as those observed, if the null hypothesis were true
Statistical significance
A p-value below the chosen threshold α
Practical significance
Whether an effect is large enough to matter in the real world
