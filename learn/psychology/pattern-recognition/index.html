Pattern Recognition

Pattern recognition combines feature analysis, structural descriptions and context; face recognition appears to rely on holistic processing.

Psychology · Cognitive Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe template, feature-analysis and recognition-by-components theories
  • Explain evidence for feature detection and for holistic face processing
  • Evaluate theories of pattern recognition

Theories

  • Template matching: compare input with stored templates. Simple, but can't cope with the endless variation of real objects (different fonts, angles, sizes).
  • Feature analysis: identify basic features (lines, curves, angles) and combine them. Supported by feature-detecting neurones (Hubel and Wiesel) and by visual search, where letters sharing features take longer to tell apart.
  • Recognition-by-components (Biederman, 1987): objects are recognised from a small set of basic 3D shapes ("geons") and their arrangement, which stay recognisable from many viewpoints.

Faces

Faces seem to be processed holistically, as wholes rather than features. Faces are much harder to recognise upside down (the face inversion effect). Bruce and Young (1986) proposed a model in which face recognition units connect to person identity information and names, explaining why we can know a face is familiar without retrieving a name. Prosopagnosia (face blindness) can occur after damage to the fusiform gyrus while other object recognition is spared.

Worked example

Context in recognition

  1. A smudged letter between "T" and "E" is read as "H" in "THE".
  2. The same smudge is read as "A" in "CAT".
  3. Top-down context interacts with feature analysis.

Computer vision systems now recognise objects and faces using deep neural networks with layers of feature detectors, loosely inspired by the visual cortex. They can match human accuracy on some tasks but make errors humans don't, such as being fooled by small changes to an image.

Common mistakeAssuming one theory explains all recognition. Letters, objects and faces may rely on different processes.

In 2 lines: Template, feature and component theories explain different aspects of recognition. Faces are processed holistically; context interacts with features.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Biederman's basic 3D shapes are called…
    • Templates
    • Features
    • Pixels
    • Geons
    Show the answer

    Answer: Geons. Recognition-by-components builds objects from geons and their arrangement.

  2. A person can recognise cars and chairs but not familiar faces after a stroke. This is…
    • Prosopagnosia
    • Inattentional blindness
    • Aphasia
    • Amnesia
    Show the answer

    Answer: Prosopagnosia. Selective face recognition impairment is prosopagnosia.

  3. Why does the face inversion effect suggest faces are processed holistically?
    • Faces have no features
    • Inversion disrupts configuration
    • Inverted faces are always blurred
    • Objects are recognised holistically too
    Show the answer

    Answer: Inversion disrupts configuration. Large inversion costs suggest reliance on whole-face configuration.

Key terms

Feature analysis
Recognising patterns by detecting and combining basic features
Geons
Biederman's basic 3D shapes from which objects are recognised
Prosopagnosia
Impaired face recognition, often after brain damage
