General Intelligence (g)

The positive manifold produces g, which predicts educational, occupational and health outcomes; what g is remains debated.

Psychology · Personality and Individual Differences · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain the positive manifold and how g is extracted by factor analysis
  • Describe evidence on what g predicts in education, work and health
  • Evaluate interpretations of g, including alternatives such as mutualism

The positive manifold

Scores on very different cognitive tests (vocabulary, mental rotation, arithmetic, memory span) are all positively correlated: people who do well on one tend to do well on others. Spearman (1904) called this pattern the positive manifold and proposed a general factor, g, to explain it. Factor analysis extracts g as the factor accounting for the most shared variance; in typical test batteries, g accounts for roughly 40–50% of the variance in scores (Deary, 2012).

What g predicts

Worked example

Scottish school children

  1. In 1947, almost all 11-year-olds in Scotland took the same intelligence test.
  2. Deary et al. (2007) linked over 70,000 English pupils' test scores at 11 with their GCSE results at 16.
  3. The correlation between a general cognitive factor and a general educational achievement factor was about 0.81.

g also predicts job performance, especially in complex jobs (Schmidt and Hunter, 1998), and is associated with health and longevity: higher childhood IQ is linked to lower mortality in later life (Calvin et al., 2011). These are average associations; many other factors influence individual outcomes.

What is g?

Some see g as a real property of the brain, such as processing speed or neural efficiency. Others argue g is a statistical summary. The mutualism model (van der Maas et al., 2006) proposes that separate abilities develop and reinforce each other over time, producing positive correlations without a single underlying cause. Thomson's sampling theory made a similar argument in 1916.

Common mistakeTreating g as a fixed amount of "brain power". g is a statistical factor whose cause is debated, and scores change with environment.

In 2 lines: The positive manifold led Spearman to propose g, extracted by factor analysis. g predicts education, work and health; its nature is debated.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Who first proposed the general factor g?
    • Charles Spearman
    • Howard Gardner
    • Raymond Cattell
    • Daniel Goleman
    Show the answer

    Answer: Charles Spearman. Spearman proposed g in 1904 to explain the positive manifold.

  2. A student good at vocabulary is also above average at puzzles and arithmetic. This pattern illustrates…
    • Multiple intelligences
    • The positive manifold
    • Emotional intelligence
    • The Flynn effect
    Show the answer

    Answer: The positive manifold. Positive correlations across different tests form the positive manifold.

  3. Why does the mutualism model challenge a single-cause view of g?
    • It denies abilities correlate
    • It shows g is genetic
    • Correlations can arise from interacting abilities
    • It proves g is processing speed
    Show the answer

    Answer: Correlations can arise from interacting abilities. Mutual reinforcement can produce the manifold without one cause.

Key terms

Positive manifold
The pattern of positive correlations among all cognitive tests
g
A general factor accounting for shared variance across cognitive tests
Mutualism model
The idea that abilities reinforce each other, producing g without a single cause
