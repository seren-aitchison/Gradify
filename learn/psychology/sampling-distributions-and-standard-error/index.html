Sampling Distributions and Standard Error

The standard error measures how much sample means vary from sample to sample; it shrinks with larger samples.

Psychology · Statistics · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe a sampling distribution of the mean
  • Calculate and interpret the standard error of the mean
  • Explain the central limit theorem and its importance

Many samples, many means

Each sample from a population gives a slightly different mean, because of sampling error. If we took every possible sample of the same size and plotted their means, we would get the sampling distribution of the mean. Its centre is the population mean; its spread is the standard error (SE).

Standard error

The standard error of the mean is SE = SD ÷ √n. It shrinks as the sample grows: quadrupling the sample halves the SE. A small SE means a sample mean is likely to be close to the population mean.

Worked example

Calculating a standard error

  1. Anxiety scores: SD = 12, n = 36.
  2. SE = 12 ÷ √36 = 12 ÷ 6 = 2.
  3. Sample means from studies of this size would typically differ from the population mean by about 2 points.

The central limit theorem

The central limit theorem says that, for reasonably large samples (often about 30 or more), the sampling distribution of the mean is approximately normal even if the population is not. This is why many tests based on the normal or t distribution work for skewed data with large enough samples.

Common mistakeConfusing the SD with the SE. The SD describes spread of individual scores; the SE describes spread of sample means.

In 2 lines: Sample means vary; their distribution is the sampling distribution. SE = SD ÷ √n; the central limit theorem justifies many common tests.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. As sample size increases, the standard error…
    • Increases
    • Stays the same
    • Decreases
    • Becomes zero immediately
    Show the answer

    Answer: Decreases. SE = SD ÷ √n, so larger n gives a smaller SE.

  2. A researcher wants to halve the standard error of a study with 50 participants. How many are needed?
    • 100
    • 75
    • 25
    • 200
    Show the answer

    Answer: 200. Halving SE needs four times the sample, because SE depends on √n.

  3. Why can a t-test be used on skewed data when samples are large?
    • The sampling distribution of the mean is approximately normal
    • Skewed data become normal when collected
    • Large samples remove all outliers
    • The t-test doesn't use means
    Show the answer

    Answer: The sampling distribution of the mean is approximately normal. The central limit theorem applies to the distribution of means, not the raw data.

Key terms

Sampling distribution
The distribution of a statistic, such as the mean, across all possible samples of a given size
Standard error
The standard deviation of a sampling distribution; for the mean, SD ÷ √n
Central limit theorem
The principle that sample means are approximately normally distributed for large samples
