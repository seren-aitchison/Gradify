Behavioural Therapy

Behavioural therapies apply conditioning through reinforcement, shaping, exposure and token economies; behavioural activation schedules rewarding activities and is as effective as CBT in trials.

Psychology · Clinical Psychology and Interventions · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe behavioural therapy techniques based on conditioning
  • Explain behavioural activation for depression
  • Evaluate behavioural approaches
Competence and safetyThis lesson is educational and is not a guide to therapy. Psychological therapies should only be delivered by qualified, registered professionals.

Learning-based techniques

Behavioural therapies apply classical and operant conditioning. Techniques include reinforcement of helpful behaviours, shaping (reinforcing steps towards a goal), exposure to reduce fear, and token economies in some institutional settings, where tokens earned for target behaviours can be exchanged for rewards. Ethical concerns have reduced the use of aversive methods.

Behavioural activation

Behavioural activation (BA) targets the cycle of low mood, withdrawal and loss of reward in depression. People monitor activities and mood, then schedule activities that bring pleasure or a sense of achievement, gradually re-engaging with life. Jacobson et al. (1996) found BA alone was as effective as full cognitive therapy in a component analysis. The COBRA trial (Richards et al., 2016) found BA delivered by less experienced staff was no less effective than CBT and cheaper.

Worked example

Breaking the cycle

  1. Low mood leads to doing less; doing less reduces rewarding experiences; fewer rewards worsen mood.
  2. BA reverses this by scheduling small, achievable activities, such as a short walk or calling a friend.
  3. Mood often improves after activity, not before. This example is illustrative.

Evaluation

Behavioural therapies are practical, measurable and effective for many problems, and BA can be delivered widely at lower cost. Critics argue purely behavioural approaches may not address thoughts or underlying causes, and token economies raise ethical questions about control and generalisation outside institutions.

Common mistakeAssuming behavioural approaches ignore people's experience. Behavioural activation is collaborative and personalised.

In 2 lines: Conditioning-based techniques change behaviour directly. Behavioural activation is effective and cheaper; ethical limits on some methods.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Reinforcing successive steps towards a target behaviour is called…
    • Flooding
    • Formulation
    • Free association
    • Shaping
    Show the answer

    Answer: Shaping. Shaping builds a new behaviour step by step by reinforcing closer and closer attempts.

  2. A man with low mood plans a 10-minute walk each morning and rates his mood afterwards. This uses…
    • Behavioural activation
    • Free association
    • Exposure and response prevention
    • Token economy
    Show the answer

    Answer: Behavioural activation. Scheduling rewarding activity is behavioural activation.

  3. Why do critics raise concerns about token economies?
    • They are always unethical in every case
    • They raise concerns about control and generalisation
    • They never change behaviour at all
    • They are used in every kind of therapy
    Show the answer

    Answer: They raise concerns about control and generalisation. Behaviour may not transfer beyond the token system.

Key terms

Behavioural activation
Scheduling rewarding activities to improve mood
Shaping
Reinforcing successive steps towards a target behaviour
Token economy
A system of earning tokens for target behaviours
