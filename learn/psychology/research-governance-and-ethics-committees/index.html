Research Governance and Ethics Committees

Ethics committees independently review research before it starts, applying professional codes and law to protect participants.

Psychology · Research Methods · about 2 minutes · sources checked 2026-11

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the role of research ethics committees and the review process
  • Explain the frameworks that govern psychological research in the UK
  • Evaluate the strengths and limitations of ethical review

Ethics committees

Before a study begins, researchers submit a proposal to a research ethics committee (REC): a university committee for most student and staff research, or an NHS REC, coordinated by the Health Research Authority, for research involving NHS patients or staff. The committee weighs risks against benefits and checks consent procedures, confidentiality, data management, deception, debriefing and safeguards for vulnerable participants. It can approve, request changes or reject.

The wider framework

  • Professional codes: the BPS Code of Human Research Ethics, based on respect, scientific integrity, social responsibility and maximising benefit while minimising harm.
  • Law: data protection (UK GDPR and Data Protection Act 2018), the Mental Capacity Act 2005, and laws on research with human tissue.
  • International statements: the World Medical Association's Declaration of Helsinki on medical research involving people.
Worked example

A student project's journey

  1. A student plans an online survey on stress.
  2. They complete an ethics form: aims, procedure, information sheet, consent form, debrief and data plan.
  3. The committee asks them to add support contacts for distressed participants; after revision, the study is approved.

Evaluation

Strengths: independent scrutiny protects participants and researchers and maintains public trust. Limitations: review can be slow and bureaucratic; committees can be overcautious, blocking valuable research; review happens before the study, so ongoing monitoring depends on researchers; and standards differ between institutions and countries.

Common mistakeTreating ethical approval as the end of ethical responsibility. Researchers must act ethically throughout and report problems.

In 2 lines: Ethics committees review proposals against professional codes and law before research begins. Review protects participants but can be slow or overcautious, and ethical responsibility continues during the study.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which document sets out ethical principles for psychological research in the UK?
    • The Mental Health Act
    • The Equality Act
    • The National Curriculum
    • The BPS research ethics code
    Show the answer

    Answer: The BPS research ethics code. The BPS Code is the professional standard for research.

  2. A study involves interviewing NHS patients on a hospital ward. Which review is needed?
    • An NHS research ethics committee, via the HRA
    • No review, if the patients agree
    • Only the researcher's line manager
    • A newspaper editor
    Show the answer

    Answer: An NHS research ethics committee, via the HRA. NHS patient research needs NHS ethical review.

  3. Which is the strongest criticism of ethics review systems?
    • They provide independent scrutiny
    • They can be slow and overcautious
    • They protect vulnerable participants
    • They check consent procedures
    Show the answer

    Answer: They can be slow and overcautious. Critics argue bureaucracy can hinder valuable research, though scrutiny is necessary.

Key terms

Research ethics committee
An independent group that reviews research proposals for ethical acceptability
Research governance
The framework of rules, review and accountability for conducting research
