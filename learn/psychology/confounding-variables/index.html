Confounding Variables

A confound varies systematically with the IV and provides an alternative explanation; control techniques aim to remove or balance such variables.

Psychology · Research Methods · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define extraneous and confounding variables
  • Identify confounds in study descriptions
  • Explain techniques for controlling confounds, including randomisation and standardisation

Extraneous and confounding

An extraneous variable is any variable other than the IV that might affect the DV. It becomes a confounding variable when it varies systematically with the IV, so we can't tell whether the IV or the confound caused the result.

  • Participant variables: differences between people (ability, age, motivation).
  • Situational variables: features of the setting (noise, time of day, instructions).
  • Demand characteristics: cues that tell participants the hypothesis (Orne, 1962).
  • Investigator effects: the researcher's expectations influencing results.
Worked example

Spotting a confound

  1. A teacher compares a new reading scheme (used in the morning class) with the old one (afternoon class).
  2. The morning class reads better.
  3. Time of day varies with the scheme, so alertness, not the scheme, might explain the result: a confound.

Investigate with the lab. Spot the confounding variables in each study design and choose a control for each.

Control techniques

  • Random allocation spreads participant variables evenly across conditions.
  • Standardisation: same instructions, setting and procedure for everyone.
  • Counterbalancing controls order effects in repeated measures designs.
  • Single-blind (participants don't know the condition) and double-blind (nor do researchers) procedures reduce demand characteristics and investigator effects.
Common mistakeCalling every extraneous variable a confound. It is only a confound if it changes systematically with the IV.

In 2 lines: Extraneous variables may affect the DV; confounds vary with the IV and threaten causal conclusions. Random allocation, standardisation, counterbalancing and blinding control them.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which technique spreads participant variables evenly across conditions?
    • Counterbalancing
    • Debriefing
    • Operationalisation
    • Random allocation
    Show the answer

    Answer: Random allocation. Random allocation balances individual differences on average.

  2. Volunteers who chose a mindfulness course score lower on stress than those who didn't. What is the main confound?
    • Self-selection into the course
    • The stress questionnaire
    • The length of the course
    • The time of year
    Show the answer

    Answer: Self-selection into the course. Without random allocation, motivation or other traits may differ between groups.

  3. Rosenthal and Fode told students their rats were "bright" or "dull" (they were the same). The "bright" rats learned faster. What does this show?
    • Rats differ greatly in intelligence
    • Experimenter expectations can bias results, so blinding matters
    • Students are better trainers than professors
    • Random allocation is unnecessary
    Show the answer

    Answer: Experimenter expectations can bias results, so blinding matters. Expectations changed handling and scoring; double-blind procedures guard against this.

  4. What is a confounding variable?
    • The variable the researcher deliberately changes
    • The variable that is measured
    • A variable other than the IV that varies with it and affects the DV
    • A variable that has no effect at all
    Show the answer

    Answer: A variable other than the IV that varies with it and affects the DV. Confounds make it unclear whether the IV caused the change.

Key terms

Confounding variable
A variable that changes systematically with the IV, offering an alternative explanation
Demand characteristics
Cues that reveal the study's aim and may change participants' behaviour
Double-blind
Neither participants nor the researchers running the study know who is in which condition
