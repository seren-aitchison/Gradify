Indigenous Psychologies

Indigenous psychologies build knowledge from within cultures; examples include Sikolohiyang Pilipino (kapwa), Chinese concepts of face and Māori models such as Te Whare Tapa Whā; they challenge Western dominance.

Psychology · Cultural and Cross-Cultural Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain the aims of indigenous psychologies
  • Describe examples such as Sikolohiyang Pilipino
  • Evaluate the contribution of indigenous psychologies to psychology as a whole

Aims

Kim, Yang and Hwang (2006) defined indigenous psychology as the scientific study of human behaviour and mind that is native, not transported from other regions, and designed for its people. It develops concepts, theories and methods from the culture's own understanding, aiming for psychology that is relevant to local lives. It responds to the dominance of Western, especially American, psychology.

Examples

Sikolohiyang Pilipino (Filipino psychology), developed by Enriquez from the 1970s, centres on kapwa, a shared sense of self with others, and uses methods suited to Filipino social life. Chinese indigenous psychology has studied concepts such as face and guanxi (relationships). Māori psychologists in Aotearoa New Zealand have developed frameworks grounded in Māori knowledge, such as holistic models of health.

Worked example

Te Whare Tapa Whā

  1. Durie (1984) proposed Te Whare Tapa Whā, a Māori model of health represented as a house with four walls.
  2. The walls are physical, mental and emotional, family and social, and spiritual well-being.
  3. The model is used in health services in New Zealand and illustrates an indigenous framework influencing practice.

Evaluation

Indigenous psychologies make psychology more relevant and challenge the assumption that Western theories are universal. Critics argue they risk fragmenting psychology into separate local sciences. Many indigenous psychologists respond that local findings can contribute to a more genuinely global psychology by testing which ideas generalise.

Common mistakeTreating indigenous psychology as anti-scientific. It applies scientific inquiry to local concepts.

In 2 lines: Indigenous psychologies build knowledge from local concepts. Kapwa, face and Te Whare Tapa Whā; they challenge Western dominance.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. A Māori model of health represented as a house with four walls is…
    • Kapwa
    • Te Whare Tapa Whā
    • Guanxi
    • The Big Five
    Show the answer

    Answer: Te Whare Tapa Whā. Mason Durie proposed Te Whare Tapa Whā as a holistic Māori model of health.

  2. Researchers in the Philippines develop methods based on informal conversation and shared identity rather than Western questionnaires. This reflects…
    • An imposed etic
    • Ethnocentrism
    • Indigenous psychology
    • Linguistic determinism
    Show the answer

    Answer: Indigenous psychology. Locally grounded methods reflect indigenous psychology.

  3. How can indigenous psychologies contribute to global psychology?
    • By replacing all other psychology
    • By ignoring Western research entirely
    • By studying only one culture forever
    • By testing which ideas generalise and adding local concepts
    Show the answer

    Answer: By testing which ideas generalise and adding local concepts. Local findings can test and enrich general theories.
