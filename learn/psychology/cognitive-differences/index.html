Cognitive Differences

Most cognitive sex differences are small and culturally variable; socioeconomic factors shape cognitive development; group differences must be interpreted with care.

Psychology · Personality and Individual Differences · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe research on sex similarities and differences in cognitive abilities
  • Explain socioeconomic and environmental influences on cognitive development
  • Evaluate how group differences should be interpreted

Sex similarities and differences

Hyde's (2005) gender similarities hypothesis, based on a review of 46 meta-analyses, found most psychological differences between males and females are small or near zero. In cognition, average differences in general intelligence are negligible. Some specific differences are reported, such as a male advantage in mental rotation and a female advantage in some verbal tasks, but distributions overlap greatly and differences vary by culture and over time.

Worked example

Maths and culture

  1. Else-Quest, Hyde and Linn (2010) meta-analysed international maths assessments from 69 countries.
  2. Average sex differences in maths performance were very small overall.
  3. Where differences existed, they were linked to gender equity in education and women's representation in research, suggesting social influences.

Socioeconomic influences

Children from lower-income families score lower on average on cognitive tests, a gap that appears early and is linked to nutrition, stress, home learning environments and school quality. Heritability of IQ appears lower in poorer families in some US studies (Turkheimer et al., 2003), suggesting environment constrains development more when resources are scarce, though this pattern is less clear in other countries.

Interpreting group differences

Average differences between groups say nothing about any individual, and within-group variation is much larger than between-group differences. High heritability within groups doesn't mean differences between groups are genetic, because groups may differ systematically in environment. The history of intelligence testing includes misuse to justify discrimination, so responsible interpretation matters.

Common mistakeUsing group averages to judge individuals. Distributions overlap greatly, and individual variation far exceeds group differences.

In 2 lines: Sex differences in cognition are mostly small and vary with culture and gender equity. Socioeconomic factors affect cognitive development; group differences need careful interpretation.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Hyde's 2005 review found most psychological sex differences were…
    • Small or near zero
    • Very large
    • Always favouring males
    • Always favouring females
    Show the answer

    Answer: Small or near zero. Most of the 46 meta-analyses showed small or near-zero sex differences.

  2. Maths sex differences are smaller in countries with more gender equity. What does this suggest?
    • Differences are purely genetic
    • Social factors influence the differences
    • Maths can't be measured
    • Equity increases differences
    Show the answer

    Answer: Social factors influence the differences. Variation with equity points to social influences.

  3. Why is it wrong to judge an individual by a group average?
    • Group averages are always zero
    • Individuals don't differ
    • Within-group variation is much larger
    • Averages are secret
    Show the answer

    Answer: Within-group variation is much larger. Overlapping distributions mean averages don't describe individuals.

Key terms

Gender similarities hypothesis
Hyde's proposal that males and females are similar on most psychological variables
Mental rotation
Imagining an object rotating in space
Within-group variation
Differences among individuals within the same group
