Moral Development: Kohlberg

Kohlberg described moral reasoning developing from punishment-avoidance to principled ethics; the sequence is supported but culture, care and behaviour are underplayed.

Psychology · Developmental Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe Kohlberg's levels and stages of moral reasoning
  • Explain how moral dilemmas were used to assess reasoning
  • Evaluate Kohlberg's theory, including gender and cultural critiques

Moral dilemmas

Worked example

The Heinz dilemma

  1. Heinz's wife is dying, and a druggist charges far more for a drug than Heinz can pay. Heinz steals the drug. Should he have done so?
  2. Kohlberg (1958) was interested less in the answer than in the reasoning behind it.
  3. He interviewed 72 boys in Chicago aged 10–16 and followed many of them for over 20 years (Colby et al., 1983).

Three levels, six stages

  • Pre-conventional: Stage 1, avoiding punishment; Stage 2, self-interest and exchange.
  • Conventional: Stage 3, being a good person and gaining approval; Stage 4, maintaining law and social order.
  • Post-conventional: Stage 5, social contract and individual rights; Stage 6, universal ethical principles.

Kohlberg argued the stages follow a fixed sequence, depend on cognitive development and perspective-taking, and that few people reach the post-conventional level. The longitudinal follow-up found participants moved through stages in order, without skipping (Colby et al., 1983).

Criticisms

Gilligan (1982) argued Kohlberg's original all-male sample and justice focus undervalued an ethic of care, which she associated with women's moral reasoning. A meta-analysis found few gender differences in Kohlberg stage scores (Walker, 1984), and both justice and care orientations appear in both sexes. Other criticisms: the theory measures reasoning, not behaviour; dilemmas are hypothetical; and post-conventional stages reflect Western, individualist values, while other cultures emphasise community or divinity.

Common mistakeAssuming higher stages mean better behaviour. Kohlberg measured reasoning, which predicts behaviour only modestly.

In 2 lines: Kohlberg's three levels and six stages describe developing moral reasoning, assessed through dilemmas. The sequence is supported; gender, culture and the reasoning–behaviour gap are criticisms.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Reasoning that "Heinz shouldn't steal because he'll go to prison" reflects…
    • Stage 4, law and order
    • Stage 5, social contract
    • Stage 6, universal principles
    • Stage 1, punishment avoidance
    Show the answer

    Answer: Stage 1, punishment avoidance. Avoiding punishment is the pre-conventional first stage.

  2. Someone argues "Heinz should steal because the right to life outweighs property laws". Which level is this?
    • Post-conventional
    • Pre-conventional
    • Conventional
    • No level applies
    Show the answer

    Answer: Post-conventional. Appeals to rights above law are post-conventional.

  3. Why is the gap between reasoning and behaviour a problem for Kohlberg?
    • People never act morally at all
    • Reasoning doesn't ensure action
    • Reasoning is never measured
    • Behaviour always matches the stage
    Show the answer

    Answer: Reasoning doesn't ensure action. Knowing what is right doesn't ensure doing it.

Key terms

Pre-conventional level
Moral reasoning based on punishment and self-interest
Conventional level
Moral reasoning based on approval and law and order
Ethic of care
Gilligan's moral orientation focusing on relationships and responsibility
