Group Decision-making

Group discussion can polarise views and, in cohesive isolated groups, produce groupthink; structured procedures improve decisions.

Psychology · Social Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain group polarisation and its causes
  • Describe the symptoms and causes of groupthink
  • Evaluate strategies for improving group decisions

Group polarisation

Early research found groups sometimes made riskier decisions than individuals (the "risky shift"). Later work showed a broader pattern, group polarisation: discussion tends to strengthen the initial leaning of the group. Moscovici and Zavalloni (1969) found French students' moderately positive views of President de Gaulle became more positive after discussion. Explanations include persuasive arguments (hearing new arguments for the favoured side) and social comparison (wanting to appear a good member by taking a slightly stronger position).

Groupthink

Janis (1972) proposed groupthink: a cohesive group's desire for agreement overrides realistic appraisal. He studied policy failures such as the 1961 Bay of Pigs invasion. Symptoms include an illusion of invulnerability, collective rationalisation, self-censorship, an illusion of unanimity, pressure on dissenters and mindguards who shield the group from contrary information. Antecedents include high cohesion, isolation, a directive leader and high stress.

Worked example

The Challenger decision

  1. On the night before the 1986 Challenger launch, engineers warned that seals might fail in cold weather.
  2. Managers pressed for a launch decision, and engineers' concerns were set aside.
  3. The shuttle broke apart shortly after launch. Some analysts describe the decision as showing features of groupthink, though others stress organisational pressures.

Improving decisions

Strategies include appointing a devil's advocate, having leaders withhold their views at first, inviting outside experts, collecting views independently before discussion, and dividing into subgroups. Laboratory tests of groupthink have produced mixed support, and historical case studies are open to interpretation (Esser, 1998).

Common mistakeAssuming cohesive groups always decide badly. Cohesion mainly harms decisions when combined with isolation, directive leadership and stress.

In 2 lines: Group discussion often polarises views through arguments and social comparison. Groupthink describes poor decisions in cohesive, isolated groups; devil's advocates and independent views help.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Who proposed the concept of groupthink?
    • Serge Moscovici
    • Irving Janis
    • Solomon Asch
    • Stanley Milgram
    Show the answer

    Answer: Irving Janis. Irving Janis analysed US policy failures such as the Bay of Pigs in 1972.

  2. A jury that leans towards guilty becomes more certain after discussion. This is…
    • Social loafing
    • Groupthink
    • Group polarisation
    • Deindividuation
    Show the answer

    Answer: Group polarisation. Discussion strengthened the initial leaning.

  3. Why is evidence for groupthink considered mixed?
    • It has never been studied
    • Lab studies all support it
    • It only applies to juries
    • It relies heavily on case studies
    Show the answer

    Answer: It relies heavily on case studies. Historical cases are open to interpretation and lab tests vary.

Key terms

Group polarisation
Discussion strengthening the group's initial position
Groupthink
A cohesive group's pursuit of agreement overriding realistic appraisal
Devil's advocate
A member assigned to argue against the group's preferred view
