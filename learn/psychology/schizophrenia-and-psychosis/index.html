Schizophrenia and Psychosis

Psychosis involves positive symptoms such as hallucinations and delusions and negative symptoms; biological (dopamine, genes) and social (adversity, urbanicity, migration) factors interact; the concept of schizophrenia is debated.

Psychology · Psychopathology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe positive and negative symptoms of psychosis
  • Explain biological and social explanations of psychosis
  • Evaluate debates about the concept of schizophrenia
Competence and safetyThis lesson is educational and is not a guide to diagnosis or treatment. Anyone concerned about psychosis should seek urgent advice from a GP, NHS 111 or a qualified professional.

Symptoms

Positive symptoms add to experience: hallucinations (perceiving things others don't, such as hearing voices) and delusions (fixed beliefs not shared by others). Negative symptoms reduce experience: reduced motivation, emotional expression and speech. Schizophrenia is diagnosed when psychotic symptoms persist and affect functioning. The Adult Psychiatric Morbidity Survey identified psychotic disorder in about 0.5% of adults in England in 2023/4.

Explanations

Biological factors include genetic risk and the dopamine hypothesis: Howes and Kapur (2009) argued that increased dopamine activity leads people to attach unusual significance to experiences. Social factors include urban upbringing, migration, childhood adversity and cannabis use. Higher rates in some ethnic minority groups in England, found in the AESOP study, have been linked partly to social adversity and discrimination.

Worked example

Hearing voices

  1. Surveys suggest some people in the general population hear voices without distress or a diagnosis.
  2. The Hearing Voices Movement supports people to understand and cope with voices rather than only seeking to eliminate them.
  3. This suggests psychotic experiences lie on a continuum.

Evaluation

Psychosis involves interacting biological and social factors, consistent with the diathesis–stress model. Critics argue "schizophrenia" may group together different conditions with varied causes and outcomes, and some propose replacing it with broader concepts such as psychosis spectrum. Recovery is possible, and outcomes vary widely.

Common mistakeAssociating psychosis with violence. Most people with psychosis are not violent and are more likely to be victims.

In 2 lines: Positive and negative symptoms; psychotic disorder in 0.5% of adults (2023/4). Dopamine, genes and social adversity interact; the concept of schizophrenia is debated.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Hearing a voice that others can't hear is a…
    • Delusion
    • Negative symptom
    • Compulsion
    • Hallucination
    Show the answer

    Answer: Hallucination. Perceiving without a stimulus is a hallucination.

  2. A man firmly believes his neighbours are broadcasting his thoughts, despite all evidence. This is an example of…
    • A delusion
    • A hallucination
    • A negative symptom
    • Rumination
    Show the answer

    Answer: A delusion. A fixed belief not shared by others is a delusion.

  3. Why do some researchers want to rethink the concept of schizophrenia?
    • It describes one simple, known cause
    • It may group conditions with different causes and outcomes
    • It has no recognised symptoms
    • Everyone diagnosed has identical outcomes
    Show the answer

    Answer: It may group conditions with different causes and outcomes. Heterogeneity questions whether it is one condition.

Key terms

Positive symptoms
Symptoms that add to experience, such as hallucinations
Negative symptoms
Symptoms involving reduced motivation, emotion or speech
Delusion
A fixed belief not shared by others and resistant to evidence
