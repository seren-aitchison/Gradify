Preregistration

Preregistration records hypotheses and analyses in advance, separating confirmatory tests from exploration and reducing p-hacking.

Psychology · Research Methods · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define preregistration and describe what a preregistration includes
  • Explain how preregistration distinguishes confirmatory from exploratory analysis
  • Evaluate the benefits and limitations of preregistration

What gets preregistered

A preregistration is a time-stamped public record, often on the Open Science Framework, made before data collection or analysis. It typically states the hypotheses, design, sample size and its justification, how variables will be measured, exclusion rules and the exact analysis plan.

Confirmatory and exploratory

Preregistration makes clear which analyses were planned (confirmatory) and which were decided after seeing the data (exploratory). Both are valuable, but only confirmatory tests have the error rates their p-values claim. Without a plan, researchers can unconsciously choose analyses that produce significance (Simmons et al., 2011).

Worked example

Preregistering a study on sleep and memory

  1. Hypothesis: participants who sleep after learning recall more words than those who stay awake.
  2. Plan: 60 per group (power analysis for d = 0.5); exclude participants who fail an attention check; independent t-test, one-tailed, α = .05.
  3. After data collection, an extra unplanned analysis by age is reported clearly as exploratory.

Limitations

Preregistration doesn't prevent poor designs, may be vague, and deviations are sometimes not reported. It suits hypothesis-testing research better than exploratory or qualitative work, though adapted forms exist. Registered reports go further by having peer review before data collection.

Common mistakeThinking preregistration forbids exploratory analysis. It allows it, as long as it is labelled as exploratory.

In 2 lines: Preregistration records hypotheses, design and analysis plans in advance. It separates confirmatory from exploratory analysis and reduces p-hacking, but isn't a cure-all.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which is usually included in a preregistration?
    • The final results
    • The discussion section
    • The analysis plan and sample size
    • Participants' names
    Show the answer

    Answer: The analysis plan and sample size. Plans, not results, are registered in advance.

  2. After seeing the data, a researcher tests whether the effect differs by gender, which wasn't planned. How should this be reported?
    • As the main confirmatory test
    • Not at all
    • As if it had been preregistered
    • As an exploratory analysis
    Show the answer

    Answer: As an exploratory analysis. Unplanned analyses are legitimate if labelled exploratory.

  3. Why does preregistration help keep p-values meaningful?
    • The analysis is fixed before seeing data
    • It makes all results significant
    • It increases sample sizes automatically
    • It removes the need for statistics
    Show the answer

    Answer: The analysis is fixed before seeing data. Choosing analyses after seeing data inflates false positives beyond the nominal α.

Key terms

Preregistration
A public, time-stamped record of hypotheses and analysis plans made before data collection
Confirmatory analysis
An analysis planned in advance to test a specific hypothesis
Exploratory analysis
An analysis decided after seeing the data, used to generate hypotheses
