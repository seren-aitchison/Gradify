Freud's Psychodynamic Theory of Personality

Freud explained personality through the id, ego, superego, unconscious conflict, defences and psychosexual stages; the theory is influential but largely untestable.

Psychology · Personality and Individual Differences · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the id, ego and superego and the role of the unconscious
  • Explain defence mechanisms and the psychosexual stages
  • Evaluate Freud's theory and its legacy

Structure of personality

Freud proposed much mental life is unconscious. Personality has three parts: the id (present from birth, seeking immediate gratification: the pleasure principle), the ego (developing in infancy, balancing demands using the reality principle) and the superego (internalised moral standards, developing around age 5). Conflict between them creates anxiety.

Defence mechanisms

  • Repression: pushing distressing thoughts out of awareness.
  • Denial: refusing to accept reality.
  • Displacement: redirecting feelings onto a safer target.
  • Projection: attributing one's own unacceptable feelings to others.
  • Sublimation: channelling impulses into socially acceptable activities.

Psychosexual stages

Freud proposed five stages, each focused on a body area: oral (0–1), anal (1–3), phallic (3–6, including the Oedipus complex), latency (6 to puberty) and genital (puberty onwards). Unresolved conflict could cause fixation, shaping adult personality, such as an "anal-retentive" orderliness.

Worked example

Little Hans

  1. Freud (1909) analysed a five-year-old boy's fear of horses, mainly through letters from the boy's father.
  2. He interpreted the phobia as displaced fear of the father, linked to the Oedipus complex.
  3. Critics note Hans had seen a horse fall in the street, a simpler explanation, and Freud met him only briefly.

Evaluation

Strengths: Freud highlighted unconscious processes, the importance of childhood and defence mechanisms, influencing therapy, literature and later psychodynamic theories. Limitations: key ideas are unfalsifiable, evidence came from case studies of mostly middle-class Viennese patients, and the psychosexual stages have little empirical support. Modern research supports some broader ideas, such as unconscious processing and defensive biases, in revised forms (Westen, 1998).

Common mistakeTreating Freud's specific claims as established. His broad ideas influenced psychology, but most specific claims lack support.

In 2 lines: Freud proposed the id, ego and superego, unconscious conflict and defence mechanisms. Psychosexual stages and fixation shape personality; the theory is influential but hard to test.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which part of personality operates on the pleasure principle?
    • Ego
    • Superego
    • Preconscious
    • Id
    Show the answer

    Answer: Id. The id seeks immediate gratification, following the pleasure principle.

  2. After a bad day at work, a person shouts at their dog. Which defence mechanism is this?
    • Displacement
    • Projection
    • Denial
    • Sublimation
    Show the answer

    Answer: Displacement. Feelings are redirected onto a safer target.

  3. Why is Freud's theory described as unfalsifiable?
    • It makes precise predictions
    • Any outcome can be explained by it
    • It has been disproved
    • It uses experiments
    Show the answer

    Answer: Any outcome can be explained by it. Opposite behaviours can both be explained, so it can't be tested.

Key terms

Id
The unconscious part of personality seeking immediate gratification
Defence mechanism
An unconscious strategy for reducing anxiety
Fixation
Remaining focused on an earlier psychosexual stage due to unresolved conflict
