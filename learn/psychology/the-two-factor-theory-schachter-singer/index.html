The Two-factor Theory: Schachter–Singer

The two-factor theory says emotion is arousal plus a cognitive label; evidence is mixed, but misattribution and appraisal research support a role for cognition.

Psychology · Motivation, Emotion and Wellbeing · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain the two-factor theory of emotion
  • Describe Schachter and Singer's adrenaline experiment and its limitations
  • Evaluate the theory using misattribution research and appraisal theory

Arousal plus a label

Schachter and Singer (1962) proposed that emotion requires two factors: physiological arousal and a cognitive label explaining it. Arousal is similar across emotions, so we look to the situation to decide which emotion we feel.

Worked example

The adrenaline experiment

  1. Participants were told they were receiving a vitamin injection; most actually received adrenaline (epinephrine), which causes arousal.
  2. Some were informed of the real side effects; others were misinformed or told nothing.
  3. Uninformed participants who waited with a confederate acting euphoric or angry tended to report and show more euphoria or anger than informed participants, who could attribute their arousal to the injection.

The study had problems: effects were modest, some results didn't fit, and participants' baseline arousal wasn't measured. Marshall and Zimbardo (1979) failed to replicate the euphoria effect, finding that unexplained arousal tended to feel negative.

Misattribution

Dutton and Aron (1974) had an attractive female interviewer approach men on either a high, swaying suspension bridge or a low, stable bridge in Canada. More men on the high bridge called her afterwards and wrote more sexual content in a story task, consistent with misattributing fear arousal to attraction. Alternative explanations exist, such as differences in the men who crossed each bridge.

Appraisal theories

Appraisal theories (Lazarus, 1991) developed the cognitive idea: emotions arise from evaluations of events' significance for our goals, such as threat, loss or achievement. Different appraisals produce different emotions even with similar arousal.

Common mistakeAssuming arousal automatically becomes whatever emotion others display. Replications found unexplained arousal often feels negative.

In 2 lines: Schachter and Singer proposed arousal plus labelling; their study had limitations. Misattribution (Dutton and Aron) and appraisal theories support cognition's role.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. In Schachter and Singer's study, participants were injected with…
    • A sedative
    • Caffeine
    • Saline only
    • Adrenaline
    Show the answer

    Answer: Adrenaline. Most participants received adrenaline (epinephrine), told it was a vitamin.

  2. Informed participants didn't take on the confederate's mood. Why?
    • They attributed arousal to the injection
    • They received no arousal
    • They disliked the confederate
    • They were tired
    Show the answer

    Answer: They attributed arousal to the injection. Knowing the cause removed the need for a situational label.

  3. What is an alternative explanation of the bridge study?
    • Bridges have no effect
    • Different men chose each bridge
    • The interviewer was absent
    • Everyone called her
    Show the answer

    Answer: Different men chose each bridge. Thrill-seeking men may have chosen the high bridge.

Key terms

Two-factor theory
The theory that emotion requires arousal and a cognitive label
Misattribution of arousal
Attributing arousal to the wrong source
Appraisal
Evaluating an event's significance for one's goals and wellbeing
