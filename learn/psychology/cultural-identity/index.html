Cultural Identity

Cultural identity develops through socialisation and exploration (Phinney); bicultural people frame switch; a secure identity is linked to well-being.

Psychology · Cultural and Cross-Cultural Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain how cultural identity develops
  • Describe bicultural and multiple identities
  • Evaluate the links between cultural identity and well-being

Developing cultural identity

Cultural identity develops through socialisation in families, communities, schools and media. Phinney (1989) described stages of ethnic identity development: unexamined identity, exploration (searching and learning about one's group) and achieved identity (a secure, committed sense of belonging). Social identity theory suggests group membership contributes to self-esteem.

Bicultural identities

Many people belong to more than one cultural group. Bicultural individuals may frame switch: using different cultural frames depending on context. Hong et al. (2000) showed that bicultural Chinese Americans primed with Chinese or American cultural images gave more culturally typical explanations of behaviour. Benet-Martínez described bicultural identity integration: how far people see their two identities as compatible.

Worked example

Identity and well-being

  1. A strong, secure ethnic identity has been linked to higher self-esteem and well-being in many studies of minority adolescents.
  2. Experiences of discrimination can be stressful, but a strong group identity may buffer some effects.
  3. People who feel their identities conflict may experience more stress than those who integrate them.

Evaluation

Research shows cultural identity is dynamic and context-dependent rather than fixed. Stage models may be too linear, and much research has been done in the US. Identity can also be a basis for exclusion. Intersectionality reminds us that cultural identity interacts with gender, class, religion and sexuality.

Common mistakeAssuming people have one fixed cultural identity. Many have multiple, context-dependent identities.

In 2 lines: Phinney's stages: unexamined, exploration, achieved. Bicultural frame switching; a secure identity links to well-being.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Seeing one's two cultural identities as compatible is called…
    • Bicultural identity integration
    • Acculturative stress
    • Ethnocentrism
    • Collectivism
    Show the answer

    Answer: Bicultural identity integration. Benet-Martínez described bicultural identity integration.

  2. A teenager starts reading about her family's history and attending cultural events to learn more about her heritage. Phinney would call this…
    • Unexamined identity
    • Exploration
    • Achieved identity
    • Assimilation
    Show the answer

    Answer: Exploration. Searching and learning about one's group is exploration.

  3. Why are stage models of ethnic identity criticised?
    • They describe all cultures perfectly
    • They ignore development
    • They may be too linear and US-centred
    • They focus only on adults
    Show the answer

    Answer: They may be too linear and US-centred. Identity can be non-linear and context-specific.

Key terms

Cultural identity
A sense of belonging to cultural groups
Frame switching
Shifting between cultural frames depending on context
Bicultural identity integration
How compatible a person sees their two cultural identities as being
