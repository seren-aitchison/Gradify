Bipolar Disorder

Bipolar disorder involves manic or hypomanic and depressive episodes; it is strongly heritable, with sleep and stress affecting episodes; management combines medication, psychoeducation and routine.

Psychology · Psychopathology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the features of bipolar disorder
  • Explain biological and psychosocial factors in bipolar disorder
  • Evaluate approaches to long-term management
Competence and safetyThis lesson is educational and is not a guide to diagnosis or treatment. Anyone concerned about their mood should speak to a GP or another qualified professional.

Features

Bipolar disorder involves episodes of mania or hypomania: periods of elevated or irritable mood, increased energy, reduced need for sleep, racing thoughts, and risky behaviour. Bipolar I involves at least one manic episode; bipolar II involves hypomanic and depressive episodes. Between episodes, many people function well. The Adult Psychiatric Morbidity Survey found about one in fifty adults in England screened positive for bipolar disorder in 2023/4.

Explanations

Bipolar disorder is strongly heritable according to twin studies, and biological rhythms, such as sleep and circadian cycles, appear important: disrupted sleep can trigger episodes. Psychosocial factors, including stressful life events and family stress, influence the timing and course of episodes. Some theories focus on sensitivity of the reward system to goal-related events.

Worked example

Managing bipolar disorder

  1. NICE guidance recommends long-term management combining medication, such as mood stabilisers, with psychological support.
  2. Psychoeducation helps people recognise early warning signs and plan responses.
  3. Regular routines, especially for sleep, can help reduce the risk of episodes.

Evaluation

Bipolar disorder shows how biology and environment interact: strong genetic risk combines with sleep, stress and life events. Diagnosis can be delayed because people often first seek help during depression. Psychological approaches complement medication and support self-management.

Common mistakeUsing "bipolar" to mean ordinary mood swings. The disorder involves distinct episodes that last days or longer.

In 2 lines: Bipolar disorder involves mania or hypomania and depression. Strongly heritable; sleep and stress matter; medication plus psychological support.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. A period of elevated mood, high energy and reduced need for sleep is…
    • Anhedonia
    • Mania
    • Rumination
    • Catastrophising
    Show the answer

    Answer: Mania. Elevated mood, high energy and reduced need for sleep together define a manic episode.

  2. A man notices he sleeps less and talks faster before past episodes, and agrees a plan with his care team. This uses…
    • Exposure and response prevention
    • Post-event processing
    • Psychoeducation about early warning signs
    • Inflated responsibility
    Show the answer

    Answer: Psychoeducation about early warning signs. Recognising warning signs is part of psychoeducation.

  3. Why is bipolar disorder often diagnosed late?
    • Mania is always obvious to everyone
    • It never involves depressive episodes
    • It is never diagnosed by clinicians
    • People often first seek help when depressed
    Show the answer

    Answer: People often first seek help when depressed. Hypomanic periods may feel good and go unreported, so depression is seen first.

Key terms

Mania
A period of elevated or irritable mood with high energy and reduced need for sleep
Hypomania
A milder form of mania without severe impairment
Psychoeducation
Teaching people about a condition to support self-management
