Globalisation and Psychology

Arnett described bicultural identities and identity confusion from globalisation; hybrid identities and reactions both occur; values change but cultural differences persist.

Psychology · Cultural and Cross-Cultural Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain Arnett's account of the psychology of globalisation
  • Describe bicultural and hybrid identities in a globalised world
  • Evaluate whether globalisation is making psychology more similar across cultures

Arnett's psychology of globalisation

Arnett (2002) argued that globalisation leads many young people to develop a bicultural identity: one part rooted in local culture and another linked to global culture, often through media and consumer products. Some experience identity confusion when local and global values clash. Others form self-selected cultures with like-minded people across borders, for example online.

Hybrid identities

Migration and digital media create hybrid and multicultural identities that combine elements from different sources. Research on biculturalism (Benet-Martínez) shows that integrating identities is linked to better adjustment. Globalisation also produces reactions: some people strengthen local, national or religious identities in response to perceived threats.

Worked example

Cultural change over time

  1. Santos, Varnum and Grossmann (2017) found measures of individualism had risen in most of the 78 countries they studied over recent decades.
  2. Economic development, urbanisation and education are linked to these shifts.
  3. Yet differences between cultures persist, and change is not uniform.

Evaluation

Globalisation may make some aspects of psychology more similar, especially among urban, educated young people. But local cultures adapt rather than disappear, and resistance can strengthen differences. Cultural psychology must study change and hybridity, not treat cultures as fixed.

Common mistakeAssuming globalisation makes everyone psychologically the same. Cultures adapt and sometimes resist.

In 2 lines: Arnett: bicultural identities, identity confusion and self-selected cultures. Hybridity and reaction; values change but differences persist.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. A group formed across borders around shared interests is a…
    • Self-selected culture
    • National culture
    • Culture-bound syndrome
    • Collectivist culture
    Show the answer

    Answer: Self-selected culture. Arnett described self-selected cultures formed by choice around shared interests.

  2. A teenager in a small town follows global music and fashion online while keeping family traditions at home. Arnett would describe this as…
    • Identity confusion only
    • A bicultural identity
    • Marginalisation
    • Linguistic determinism
    Show the answer

    Answer: A bicultural identity. Combining local and global elements is bicultural identity.

  3. Why may globalisation not make psychology uniform?
    • All cultures are merging fully
    • Cultural values never change
    • Local cultures adapt, and some people resist
    • Everyone consumes the same media
    Show the answer

    Answer: Local cultures adapt, and some people resist. Adaptation and resistance preserve differences.

Key terms

Bicultural identity
An identity combining local and global cultural elements
Identity confusion
Uncertainty when local and global values clash
Self-selected culture
A group formed by choice across borders around shared interests
