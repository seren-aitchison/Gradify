Self-determination Theory

SDT proposes autonomy, competence and relatedness as basic needs; autonomous motivation supports persistence and wellbeing; autonomy-supportive environments help.

Psychology · Motivation, Emotion and Wellbeing · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain SDT's three basic psychological needs
  • Describe the continuum from external regulation to intrinsic motivation
  • Apply SDT to education, work and health

Three basic needs

Ryan and Deci's (2000) self-determination theory (SDT) proposes three universal psychological needs: autonomy (feeling one's actions are self-endorsed), competence (feeling effective) and relatedness (feeling connected and cared for). When these needs are supported, people show more autonomous motivation, persistence and wellbeing; when they are thwarted, motivation and wellbeing suffer.

A continuum of regulation

  • External regulation: acting for rewards or to avoid punishment.
  • Introjected regulation: acting to avoid guilt or gain approval ("I should").
  • Identified regulation: acting because one values the outcome.
  • Integrated regulation: the value is fully part of one's identity.
  • Intrinsic motivation: acting for enjoyment itself.

More autonomous forms (identified, integrated, intrinsic) are associated with better persistence and wellbeing than controlled forms (external, introjected).

Worked example

Supporting autonomy in schools

  1. Reeve (2009) reviewed research on autonomy-supportive teaching: offering choices, explaining reasons, acknowledging feelings and using informational language.
  2. Students of autonomy-supportive teachers showed more engagement and better learning.
  3. Controlling teaching, such as pressure and commands, was associated with lower engagement.

Applications and evaluation

SDT has been applied in education, workplaces, sport, health behaviour change and therapy. Meta-analyses link need satisfaction with wellbeing across cultures (Chen et al., 2015). Critics question whether autonomy means the same thing in collectivist cultures; SDT replies that autonomy means self-endorsement, not independence from others.

Common mistakeEquating autonomy with independence. In SDT, autonomy means acting from self-endorsed values, which can include following others' guidance.

In 2 lines: SDT's three needs are autonomy, competence and relatedness. Motivation ranges from external to intrinsic; autonomous forms and autonomy support improve outcomes.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Feeling effective at what you do satisfies the need for…
    • Autonomy
    • Relatedness
    • Homeostasis
    • Competence
    Show the answer

    Answer: Competence. Feeling effective at tasks satisfies the basic need for competence.

  2. A student revises because she'd feel guilty if she didn't. This is…
    • Introjected regulation
    • Intrinsic motivation
    • Integrated regulation
    • External regulation
    Show the answer

    Answer: Introjected regulation. Acting to avoid guilt or gain approval is introjected regulation.

  3. Why might autonomy-supportive teaching improve learning?
    • It removes all structure
    • It boosts engagement
    • It uses more punishment
    • It avoids giving feedback
    Show the answer

    Answer: It boosts engagement. Choice and reasons support autonomous motivation.
