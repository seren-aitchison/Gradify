Bowlby's Attachment Theory

Bowlby proposed an evolved, monotropic attachment formed in a sensitive period, creating an internal working model; evidence supports much of it, but monotropy and maternal deprivation are contested.

Psychology · Developmental Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe Bowlby's monotropic theory of attachment
  • Explain the evidence from Lorenz, Harlow and Bowlby's own studies
  • Evaluate Bowlby's theory, including the maternal deprivation hypothesis

An evolved bond

Bowlby (1969) proposed that attachment is an evolved system that keeps infants close to caregivers for protection. Infants have social releasers (crying, smiling, cooing) that trigger caregiving. Key ideas include:

  • Monotropy: one special attachment, usually to the mother, is more important than others.
  • Critical period: attachments must form within roughly the first 2½ years (later called a sensitive period).
  • Internal working model: the first attachment provides a mental template for later relationships.

Animal evidence

Worked example

Imprinting and comfort

  1. Lorenz (1935) found goslings followed the first moving object they saw after hatching, even Lorenz himself: imprinting, which occurred within a critical window.
  2. Harlow (1958) raised infant rhesus monkeys with two "mothers": a wire one with a feeding bottle and a soft cloth one without. The monkeys spent most time clinging to the cloth mother, going to the wire one only to feed.
  3. Comfort, not food, seemed central to the bond, contrary to learning theory explanations.

Maternal deprivation

Bowlby's (1944) study of 44 juvenile thieves found 14 were "affectionless psychopaths", of whom 12 had experienced prolonged early separation from their mothers, compared with 2 of 44 non-offending controls. His maternal deprivation hypothesis proposed that prolonged early separation causes lasting harm. Rutter (1972) argued much harm came from privation (never forming an attachment) and family discord rather than separation itself.

Evaluation

Strengths: the theory is supported by animal studies, has wide influence on childcare (for example, parents staying with children in hospital) and inspired Ainsworth's research. Limitations: monotropy is challenged by evidence of multiple attachments (Schaffer and Emerson, 1964); Bowlby's 1944 study was retrospective, correlational and conducted by Bowlby himself; generalising from geese and monkeys is limited; and the theory has been criticised for placing responsibility on mothers.

Investigate with the lab. Classify invented infants' attachment types from their reunion behaviour, then compare with Bowlby's phases on the timeline.

Common mistakeConcluding that any separation from the mother causes lasting harm. Quality of care and lack of attachment matter more than brief separations.

In 2 lines: Bowlby saw attachment as evolved, monotropic and formed in a sensitive period. Lorenz and Harlow support evolutionary and comfort-based views; maternal deprivation and monotropy are contested.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Goslings following the first moving object they see is called…
    • Imprinting
    • Monotropy
    • Social referencing
    • Privation
    Show the answer

    Answer: Imprinting. Lorenz described goslings imprinting on the first moving object they saw.

  2. Infant monkeys cling to a cloth mother and visit a wire mother only to feed. Which explanation does this challenge?
    • That comfort matters
    • That attachment is based on food
    • That attachment evolved
    • That monkeys form bonds
    Show the answer

    Answer: That attachment is based on food. Learning theory linked attachment to feeding; comfort mattered more.

  3. Why is the 44 thieves study weak evidence for maternal deprivation?
    • It was a randomised trial
    • It used monkeys
    • It was retrospective and correlational
    • It studied only adults
    Show the answer

    Answer: It was retrospective and correlational. Recall of early separation and other family factors confound the link.

  4. What did Bowlby call the special attachment to one main caregiver?
    • Imprinting
    • Object permanence
    • Reciprocity
    • Monotropy
    Show the answer

    Answer: Monotropy. Bowlby argued infants form one primary attachment, which he called monotropy.

Key terms

Monotropy
Bowlby's idea that one primary attachment is more important than others
Internal working model
A mental template of relationships based on early attachment
Privation
Never forming an attachment bond
