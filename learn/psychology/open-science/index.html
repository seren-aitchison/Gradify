Open Science

Open science makes research transparent and checkable, helping errors to be found and findings to be built on.

Psychology · Research Methods · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the main open science practices
  • Explain how open science improves the reliability of research
  • Evaluate the challenges of open science, including privacy and equity

Practices

  • Open data: sharing anonymised datasets so others can check and reuse them.
  • Open materials and code: sharing questionnaires, stimuli and analysis scripts so studies can be repeated exactly.
  • Preregistration and registered reports.
  • Open access: making papers free to read.
  • Preprints: sharing papers publicly before peer review.
  • Transparent reporting: following reporting guidelines and stating all measures and exclusions.

Why it matters

Openness lets others detect errors, reproduce analyses, combine data in meta-analyses and build on work efficiently. A manifesto for reproducible science (Munafò et al., 2017) argued that changes to methods, reporting, incentives and training were needed together. Many journals now award badges or require data availability statements.

Worked example

Reproducing an analysis

  1. A published study shares its data and analysis code.
  2. Another researcher reruns the code and gets the same results (computational reproducibility).
  3. They notice an outlier that drives the effect and publish a comment; the field learns faster.

Challenges

Participant privacy limits what data can be shared, especially in clinical research; anonymisation must be careful. Sharing takes time and skills that aren't always rewarded. Some argue open access fees shift costs onto researchers and may disadvantage those with less funding.

Common mistakeThinking open data means sharing everything. Data must be anonymised, and participants must have consented to sharing.

In 2 lines: Open science shares data, materials, code, plans and papers. It improves error detection and cumulative knowledge, but raises privacy and equity challenges.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Sharing a study's analysis scripts online is an example of…
    • Publication bias
    • Open code
    • p-hacking
    • Attrition
    Show the answer

    Answer: Open code. Sharing scripts lets others check and reproduce analyses.

  2. A clinical trial team wants to share data openly. What must they ensure first?
    • Results are significant
    • Only positive findings are shared
    • Anonymisation and consent to share
    • Participants' names are included
    Show the answer

    Answer: Anonymisation and consent to share. Privacy and consent come before openness.

  3. Which is the strongest challenge to open data in psychology?
    • Data can't be stored digitally
    • Nobody would ever reuse data
    • Open data makes studies impossible to replicate
    • Protecting participants' privacy, especially with sensitive data
    Show the answer

    Answer: Protecting participants' privacy, especially with sensitive data. Privacy, consent and re-identification risks limit what can be shared.

Key terms

Open data
Research data shared publicly, after anonymisation, for checking and reuse
Preprint
A paper shared publicly before peer review
Computational reproducibility
Getting the same results by rerunning the original analysis on the original data
