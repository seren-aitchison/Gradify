Language Acquisition

Children acquire language in predictable stages; nativist, learning and interactionist theories each explain part of the evidence.

Psychology · Cognitive Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the main stages of language acquisition
  • Compare behaviourist, nativist and interactionist theories of language acquisition
  • Evaluate evidence on critical or sensitive periods for language

Stages

  • Cooing (from about 2 months) and babbling (about 6 months): repeated syllables such as "bababa".
  • One-word stage (around 12 months): single words, often naming things.
  • Two-word stage (around 18–24 months): "telegraphic speech" such as "more milk".
  • Vocabulary spurt and rapid growth in grammar from about 2 years.

Children make overregularisation errors such as "goed" and "mouses", applying grammatical rules too widely. These errors are useful evidence: children don't hear adults say "goed", so they must be generating rules, not just copying.

Theories

Behaviourist (Skinner, 1957): language is learned through imitation and reinforcement. It struggles to explain overregularisation and the speed of learning. Nativist (Chomsky): humans have an innate capacity for language, a "language acquisition device" with universal grammar, explaining why children learn complex grammar from limited input (the "poverty of the stimulus" argument). Interactionist views (Bruner, Tomasello) stress social interaction, joint attention and child-directed speech, alongside general learning abilities.

Worked example

Sensitive periods

  1. Genie, a girl isolated and neglected until she was found at 13 in 1970, learned many words but never mastered grammar (Curtiss, 1977).
  2. Her case is consistent with a sensitive period, but her severe deprivation means it can't isolate the effect of age.
  3. Studies of second-language learners find later starters rarely reach native-like grammar (Johnson and Newport, 1989), supporting a gradual sensitive period rather than a sharp cut-off.
Common mistakeTreating nativism and learning as all-or-nothing. Most researchers accept both innate capacities and rich learning from input.

In 2 lines: Language develops from babbling to words, word combinations and grammar. Learning, nativist and interactionist theories each explain part; evidence supports a sensitive period.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Who proposed that humans have an innate language acquisition device?
    • B. F. Skinner
    • Jean Piaget
    • Noam Chomsky
    • Jerome Bruner
    Show the answer

    Answer: Noam Chomsky. Chomsky argued for innate universal grammar.

  2. A child says "I runned fast". What does this error suggest?
    • The child is copying adults
    • The child has a hearing problem
    • The child is in the cooing stage
    • The child is applying a rule
    Show the answer

    Answer: The child is applying a rule. Adults don't say "runned", so the child generates rules.

  3. Why is Genie's case weak evidence for a critical period?
    • Her severe deprivation confounds age
    • She learned fluent grammar
    • She was studied in a lab
    • She was an adult learner
    Show the answer

    Answer: Her severe deprivation confounds age. Abuse and neglect, not just age, could explain her difficulties.

Key terms

Overregularisation
Applying a grammatical rule too widely, as in "goed"
Universal grammar
Chomsky's proposed innate set of principles common to all languages
Child-directed speech
Simplified, expressive speech adults use with young children
