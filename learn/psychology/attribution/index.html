Attribution

Attributions explain behaviour as internal or external; covariation information and Weiner's dimensions shape conclusions and motivation.

Psychology · Social Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Distinguish internal (dispositional) and external (situational) attributions
  • Apply Kelley's covariation model using consensus, consistency and distinctiveness
  • Explain Weiner's dimensions of attribution and their effects on motivation

Explaining behaviour

Heider (1958) described people as "naive scientists" who try to explain behaviour. An internal (dispositional) attribution explains behaviour by the person's traits or effort ("she's rude"). An external (situational) attribution explains it by circumstances ("she was rushing to the hospital").

Kelley's covariation model

Kelley (1967) proposed that people use three kinds of information:

  • Consensus: do other people behave the same way in this situation?
  • Consistency: does this person behave this way in this situation at other times?
  • Distinctiveness: does this person behave this way in other situations?
Worked example

Applying the model

  1. Sam laughs at a comedian. Everyone else laughs (high consensus); Sam always laughs at this comedian (high consistency); Sam rarely laughs at other comedians (high distinctiveness).
  2. The likely attribution is external: the comedian is funny.
  3. If no one else laughs (low consensus), Sam laughs at everything (low distinctiveness) and always laughs here (high consistency), the attribution is internal: something about Sam.

Weiner's model

Weiner (1985) classified attributions for success and failure on three dimensions: locus (internal or external), stability (stable or unstable) and controllability. Attributing failure to lack of effort (internal, unstable, controllable) supports trying again; attributing it to lack of ability (internal, stable, uncontrollable) may lead to giving up. Attributional retraining encourages effort attributions and has improved some students' performance.

Common mistakeThinking people always use covariation information systematically. People often have incomplete information and use shortcuts.

In 2 lines: People attribute behaviour to internal or external causes. Kelley's model uses consensus, consistency and distinctiveness; Weiner's dimensions affect motivation.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. "He failed because he didn't revise" is an attribution to…
    • Effort
    • Luck
    • Task difficulty
    • Other people
    Show the answer

    Answer: Effort. Effort is internal, unstable and controllable.

  2. Everyone dislikes a restaurant, Jo always dislikes it, and Jo likes most restaurants. Kelley's model suggests…
    • An internal attribution to Jo
    • An external attribution to the restaurant
    • No attribution is possible
    • Jo is lying
    Show the answer

    Answer: An external attribution to the restaurant. High consensus, consistency and distinctiveness point to the restaurant.

  3. Why might attributional retraining help struggling students?
    • It raises ability directly
    • It removes all failure
    • Effort attributions encourage persistence
    • It blames teachers
    Show the answer

    Answer: Effort attributions encourage persistence. Seeing failure as changeable through effort supports trying again.

Key terms

Dispositional attribution
Explaining behaviour by the person's characteristics
Situational attribution
Explaining behaviour by external circumstances
Distinctiveness
Whether a person behaves this way in other situations
