Insight Learning

Insight learning is sudden problem solving by mentally restructuring the situation, contrasting with gradual trial and error.

Psychology · Learning and Behaviour · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define insight learning
  • Describe Köhler's studies with chimpanzees
  • Evaluate the evidence for insight as distinct from trial-and-error learning

Köhler's chimpanzees

Wolfgang Köhler, a Gestalt psychologist, studied chimpanzees on Tenerife during the First World War (The Mentality of Apes, 1925). In one problem, bananas hung out of reach and boxes lay around the enclosure. Köhler reported that a chimpanzee called Sultan, after unsuccessful attempts, paused and then stacked boxes to reach the fruit. In another, Sultan joined two short sticks to reach food beyond the bars.

What insight is

Insight learning is a sudden understanding of the relationships in a problem (an "aha" moment), leading to a solution that appears complete, is retained and transfers to similar problems. It contrasts with Thorndike's gradual trial-and-error learning. For Gestalt psychologists, insight involved restructuring the problem as a whole.

Worked example

Insight in humans

  1. In the nine-dot problem, people must join nine dots in a square with four straight lines without lifting the pen.
  2. Most fail because they assume lines must stay inside the square.
  3. The solution comes when they restructure the problem and extend lines beyond the square, often suddenly.

Evaluation

Strengths: showed that animals can solve problems in ways that look cognitive, and inspired research on human problem solving; brain imaging studies have found distinctive activity associated with moments of insight. Limitations: Köhler's chimpanzees may have used prior experience with boxes and sticks; later studies found that chimpanzees without prior play experience with sticks rarely solved such problems; and "suddenness" can hide earlier gradual learning.

Common mistakeAssuming insight means learning without any previous experience. Insight often recombines things learned earlier.

In 2 lines: Köhler reported chimpanzees solving problems suddenly by insight. Insight contrasts with trial and error, though prior experience may contribute.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Köhler was a member of which school of psychology?
    • Gestalt psychology
    • Behaviourism
    • Psychoanalysis
    • Humanistic psychology
    Show the answer

    Answer: Gestalt psychology. Köhler was a leading Gestalt psychologist, interested in perceiving problems as wholes.

  2. After struggling with a puzzle for an hour, a student suddenly sees the solution and can solve similar puzzles easily. This is…
    • Classical conditioning
    • Insight learning
    • Habituation
    • Latent learning
    Show the answer

    Answer: Insight learning. A sudden, complete, transferable solution is characteristic of insight.

  3. What is the strongest criticism of Köhler's evidence for insight?
    • Köhler used too many chimpanzees
    • The problems were too easy for any animal
    • The apes may have used prior play experience
    • Köhler measured only reaction times
    Show the answer

    Answer: The apes may have used prior play experience. Later studies found prior play experience mattered, so "insight" may recombine earlier learning.

Key terms

Insight learning
Sudden understanding of a problem's structure, leading to a complete solution
Restructuring
Mentally reorganising how a problem is represented, central to Gestalt accounts of insight
