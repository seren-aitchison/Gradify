Autism

Autism involves differences in social communication, repetitive behaviours and sensory experience; cognitive theories explain some features; the double empathy problem and neurodiversity challenge deficit views.

Psychology · Psychopathology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the characteristics of autism
  • Explain cognitive theories of autism
  • Evaluate deficit and neurodiversity perspectives
Competence and safetyThis lesson is educational and is not a guide to diagnosis. Autism assessment should be carried out by qualified professionals.

Characteristics

Autism involves differences in social communication and interaction and restricted, repetitive patterns of behaviour, interests or activities, including sensory differences such as over- or under-sensitivity to sound or touch. These are present from early development, though they may not be recognised until later. Autism is a spectrum: support needs vary widely. Autistic people often have co-occurring conditions such as anxiety or ADHD.

Cognitive theories

Theory of mind accounts proposed that autistic people have difficulty understanding others' mental states. Weak central coherence proposed a focus on detail over global meaning. Executive function accounts point to differences in planning and flexibility. Each explains some features but not all, and not all autistic people show these patterns.

Worked example

The double empathy problem

  1. Milton (2012) proposed the double empathy problem: communication difficulties between autistic and non-autistic people are mutual, arising from different ways of experiencing the world.
  2. Studies have found autistic people often communicate effectively with each other.
  3. This challenges the idea that difficulties lie only within autistic individuals.

Evaluation

The neurodiversity perspective sees autism as a natural variation in human neurology, emphasising strengths, acceptance and adapting environments, while recognising that many autistic people need support. Deficit-based theories have been criticised for describing autism only from non-autistic perspectives. Rising diagnoses reflect greater awareness, broader criteria and better recognition, especially in women and adults.

Common mistakeAssuming autism can be "cured" or that all autistic people are alike. It is lifelong and diverse.

In 2 lines: Autism involves social communication, repetitive and sensory differences. Cognitive theories; double empathy and neurodiversity challenge deficit views.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Understanding that others have thoughts and beliefs different from our own is…
    • Central coherence
    • Executive function
    • Neurodiversity
    • Theory of mind
    Show the answer

    Answer: Theory of mind. Theory of mind concerns understanding mental states.

  2. Two autistic friends communicate easily with each other but find conversations with non-autistic classmates difficult. This supports…
    • The double empathy problem
    • Weak central coherence only
    • The disease model
    • Learned helplessness
    Show the answer

    Answer: The double empathy problem. Communication difficulty is mutual between groups, not located only in autistic people.

  3. Why have diagnoses of autism increased?
    • Autism is caused by modern technology
    • Greater awareness, broader criteria and better recognition
    • Diagnosis has become random
    • Fewer people are autistic
    Show the answer

    Answer: Greater awareness, broader criteria and better recognition. Recognition, especially in women and adults, has improved.

Key terms

Neurodiversity
The view that neurological differences are natural human variation
Double empathy problem
Mutual communication difficulties between autistic and non-autistic people
Theory of mind
Understanding others' mental states
