Infancy

Infants are born with reflexes and perceptual preferences, reach motor milestones in a broad window and are studied through looking and habituation.

Psychology · Developmental Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe newborn reflexes and perceptual abilities
  • Explain motor development and the role of experience
  • Describe methods for studying infant perception and cognition

Newborn abilities

Newborns have reflexes such as rooting (turning towards a touch on the cheek), sucking, grasping and the Moro (startle) reflex, most of which fade within months. Vision is blurry at first but improves rapidly; newborns prefer face-like patterns (Johnson et al., 1991). Hearing is well developed at birth, and newborns prefer their mother's voice.

Motor milestones

Most infants hold their head up by about 2–4 months, sit without support around 6 months, crawl around 8–10 months and walk around 12 months, with wide normal variation. The WHO Multicentre Growth Reference Study found healthy children in six countries reached milestones such as walking alone within a broad window of roughly 8 to 18 months. Experience matters: in cultures where infants are given practice, some milestones arrive earlier.

Worked example

Studying infants

  1. Infants can't answer questions, so researchers use looking, sucking and heart rate.
  2. In habituation, an infant is shown a stimulus until looking time declines; if looking recovers for a new stimulus, the infant can tell them apart.
  3. In the visual cliff (Gibson and Walk, 1960), most crawling infants wouldn't cross a glass surface over an apparent drop, suggesting depth perception by the time they crawl.

Brain growth

The brain roughly doubles in size in the first year. Synapses form rapidly and are later pruned according to use. Infancy is a period of high plasticity and sensitivity to experience, including care, nutrition and stimulation.

Common mistakeTreating milestone ages as deadlines. Normal development varies widely, and many differences even out.

In 2 lines: Newborns have reflexes, perceptual preferences and good hearing. Motor milestones vary widely; habituation and looking methods reveal infant abilities.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Most infants walk alone at around…
    • 12 months
    • 3 months
    • 3 years
    • 6 weeks
    Show the answer

    Answer: 12 months. Walking alone typically appears around the first birthday, with wide variation.

  2. An infant looks less at a repeated picture but looks longer when a new one appears. This shows…
    • The infant is blind
    • The infant can tell the pictures apart
    • The infant prefers old pictures
    • Habituation has failed
    Show the answer

    Answer: The infant can tell the pictures apart. Recovery of looking shows discrimination.

  3. Why do some cultures see earlier sitting or walking?
    • Their genes are different
    • Milestones are fixed
    • Infants are given more practice
    • Their brains are larger
    Show the answer

    Answer: Infants are given more practice. Experience and practice can speed motor milestones.

Key terms

Reflex
An automatic response present at birth
Habituation
Declining response to a repeated stimulus
Visual cliff
An apparatus testing infants' depth perception
