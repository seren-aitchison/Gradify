Evolution of Cognition

Evolutionary psychology proposes specialised evolved mechanisms such as cheater detection; critics stress flexible, general and culturally learned cognition.

Psychology · Evolutionary Psychology and Behavioural Ecology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain the idea of evolved cognitive mechanisms and modularity
  • Describe evidence for specialised mechanisms, such as cheater detection
  • Evaluate modular and domain-general views of the evolved mind

An evolved mind

Tooby and Cosmides (1992) argued the mind contains many evolved psychological mechanisms, each designed to solve a specific ancestral problem, such as choosing food, avoiding predators, finding mates or cooperating. They described the mind as a "Swiss army knife" of specialised modules, shaped in the environment of evolutionary adaptedness (EEA).

Cheater detection

Worked example

The Wason task revisited

  1. Cosmides (1989) found people perform poorly on abstract versions of the Wason selection task but well when the rule is a social contract ("If you take the benefit, you must pay the cost").
  2. She argued humans evolved a specialised mechanism for detecting cheaters in social exchange.
  3. Critics argue the improvement reflects familiarity or reasoning about permissions and obligations generally, not a cheater module (Cheng and Holyoak, 1985).

Other proposed mechanisms

Other proposed mechanisms include face recognition, theory of mind, language acquisition, fear of snakes and spiders and disgust to avoid contamination. Some have support from developmental and neuropsychological evidence; others remain debated.

Modular or general?

Critics argue the human mind is notable for flexibility: general learning, reasoning and culture let humans adapt to new environments. Heyes (2018) argued many capacities thought to be innate modules, such as imitation and mind-reading, are "cognitive gadgets" learned through culture. Most researchers now see a mix of specialised and general processes.

Common mistakeAssuming evolved means fixed or present at birth. Evolved mechanisms can develop through experience.

In 2 lines: Evolutionary psychology proposes specialised mechanisms shaped in the EEA. Cheater detection is a key example; critics stress flexibility and cultural learning.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Tooby and Cosmides compared the mind to a…
    • Blank slate
    • Computer only
    • Swiss army knife
    • Telephone exchange
    Show the answer

    Answer: Swiss army knife. Many specialised tools, each for a problem.

  2. People easily spot who broke the rule "If you borrow the car, you must fill the tank". Cosmides would explain this by…
    • General logic only
    • Random guessing
    • Cultural gadgets only
    • A cheater-detection mechanism
    Show the answer

    Answer: A cheater-detection mechanism. Social contract framing activates cheater detection.

  3. What is the main criticism of strong modularity?
    • Human cognition is highly flexible
    • Modules are proven everywhere
    • The mind has no structure
    • Culture has no role
    Show the answer

    Answer: Human cognition is highly flexible. Flexibility and general learning challenge many specialised modules.

Key terms

Modularity
The idea that the mind contains specialised mechanisms for specific problems
Environment of evolutionary adaptedness
The ancestral environment in which adaptations evolved
Cheater detection
A proposed mechanism for identifying people who take benefits without paying costs
