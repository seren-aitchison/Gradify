Relapse Prevention

Relapse prevention plans identify warning signs, triggers and strategies; MBCT reduces depressive relapse; Marlatt's model addresses high-risk situations and the abstinence violation effect.

Psychology · Clinical Psychology and Interventions · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the components of a relapse prevention plan
  • Explain mindfulness-based cognitive therapy for recurrent depression
  • Evaluate relapse prevention approaches
Competence and safetyThis lesson is educational and is not a guide to therapy. Psychological therapies should only be delivered by qualified, registered professionals.

Relapse prevention plans

Near the end of therapy, people often create a relapse prevention plan (sometimes called a blueprint) that summarises what they learned, identifies early warning signs and triggers, lists helpful strategies and supports, and sets out what to do if difficulties return. Distinguishing a lapse (a temporary setback) from a relapse (a full return of difficulties) helps people respond calmly.

Mindfulness-based cognitive therapy

Mindfulness-based cognitive therapy (MBCT) combines mindfulness practice with cognitive therapy, helping people notice early signs of low mood and relate to negative thoughts differently. It is designed for people who have recovered from recurrent depression. Kuyken et al. (2016) found in an individual patient data meta-analysis that MBCT reduced the risk of depressive relapse compared with usual care over 60 weeks.

Worked example

Relapse prevention in addiction

  1. Marlatt's relapse prevention model identifies high-risk situations, such as stress or social pressure, and teaches coping skills.
  2. It warns against the abstinence violation effect: after a lapse, thinking "I've blown it" can lead to full relapse.
  3. Seeing a lapse as a learning opportunity reduces this risk (Witkiewitz and Marlatt, 2004).

Evaluation

Relapse prevention recognises that many conditions recur and gives people tools to manage them. MBCT has good evidence for recurrent depression, particularly for people with several previous episodes. But relapse prevention depends on continued practice, and life stressors can overwhelm plans without wider support.

Common mistakeTreating a lapse as total failure. A lapse is a setback that can be learned from.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Thinking "I've blown it" after one drink and then drinking heavily is the…
    • Thought–action fusion
    • Universality
    • Abstinence violation effect
    • Expressed emotion
    Show the answer

    Answer: Abstinence violation effect. Marlatt described the abstinence violation effect, where one lapse is treated as total failure.

  2. Near the end of therapy, a client writes down early warning signs and what to do if low mood returns. This is…
    • A fear hierarchy
    • A formulation only
    • Free association
    • A relapse prevention plan
    Show the answer

    Answer: A relapse prevention plan. Planning for warning signs is relapse prevention.

  3. Why might relapse prevention plans fail?
    • Major stressors can overwhelm plans without support
    • Relapse prevention plans always work
    • Nobody ever experiences a relapse
    • Practising the plan is unnecessary
    Show the answer

    Answer: Major stressors can overwhelm plans without support. Plans need regular practice and wider support to hold up under major stress.
