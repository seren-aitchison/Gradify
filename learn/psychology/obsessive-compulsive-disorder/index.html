Obsessive-compulsive Disorder

OCD involves obsessions and compulsions; Salkovskis explained OCD through inflated responsibility and neutralising; ERP is an effective, recommended approach; biological factors also matter.

Psychology · Psychopathology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe obsessions and compulsions
  • Explain Salkovskis's cognitive-behavioural model of OCD
  • Evaluate explanations and approaches to OCD
Competence and safetyThis lesson is educational and is not a guide to diagnosis or treatment. Anyone concerned about OCD should speak to a GP or another qualified professional.

Obsessions and compulsions

Obsessions are unwanted, intrusive thoughts, images or urges that cause distress, such as fears of contamination or of causing harm. Compulsions are repetitive behaviours or mental acts, such as washing, checking or counting, performed to reduce distress or prevent a feared event. OCD is diagnosed when these are time-consuming, distressing or impair daily life. People often recognise the thoughts as excessive.

Salkovskis's model

Salkovskis (1985) noted that most people have occasional intrusive thoughts. In OCD, these are interpreted as meaningful and dangerous, especially through inflated responsibility: the belief that one is responsible for preventing harm. This leads to anxiety and neutralising behaviours (compulsions), which bring short-term relief and so are reinforced, while preventing the person from learning the feared outcome wouldn't happen.

Worked example

Exposure and response prevention

  1. Exposure and response prevention (ERP) involves gradually facing feared situations, such as touching a "contaminated" object, without performing compulsions.
  2. Over time, anxiety reduces and the person learns that the feared outcome doesn't occur.
  3. NICE guidance recommends CBT including ERP for OCD.

Evaluation

The cognitive-behavioural model explains why compulsions persist and has informed effective treatment. Biological factors also contribute: OCD is partly heritable, and some medications help. Not all people with OCD report inflated responsibility, and the model may fit some presentations better than others.

Common mistakeUsing "OCD" to mean liking things tidy. OCD involves distressing obsessions and time-consuming compulsions.

In 2 lines: OCD involves obsessions and compulsions that cause distress. Salkovskis: inflated responsibility and neutralising; ERP is recommended.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. A repetitive act performed to reduce distress from an obsession is a…
    • Compulsion
    • Obsession
    • Safety signal
    • Delusion
    Show the answer

    Answer: Compulsion. Compulsions are acts that neutralise distress.

  2. A woman checks the oven ten times before leaving because she believes she'd be to blame for a fire. Salkovskis would highlight…
    • Learned helplessness
    • Inflated responsibility
    • Conditions of worth
    • Displacement
    Show the answer

    Answer: Inflated responsibility. A sense of responsibility for harm drives checking.

  3. Why do compulsions persist despite being distressing?
    • They never reduce anxiety at all
    • They are always enjoyable to do
    • They bring short-term relief, which reinforces them
    • They have no consequences of any kind
    Show the answer

    Answer: They bring short-term relief, which reinforces them. Relief negatively reinforces the behaviour.

Key terms

Obsession
An unwanted, intrusive and distressing thought, image or urge
Compulsion
A repetitive behaviour or mental act performed to reduce distress
Inflated responsibility
Believing one is responsible for preventing harm
