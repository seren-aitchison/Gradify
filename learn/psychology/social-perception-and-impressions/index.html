Social Perception and Impressions

Impressions form quickly, are shaped by central traits, order and halo effects, and expectations can be partly self-fulfilling.

Psychology · Social Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe how first impressions are formed, including thin slices
  • Explain central traits, primacy and the halo effect
  • Evaluate research on self-fulfilling prophecies

Fast impressions

People judge traits such as trustworthiness from faces after very brief exposure: Willis and Todorov (2006) found judgements after 100 milliseconds correlated strongly with judgements made without time limits. Ambady and Rosenthal (1993) found that ratings of teachers from thin slices (silent 10-second clips) predicted end-of-term student evaluations. Speed isn't the same as accuracy: snap judgements from faces are often wrong.

Central traits and primacy

Worked example

Warm and cold

  1. Asch (1946) gave participants a list of traits describing a person: intelligent, skilful, industrious, warm (or cold), determined, practical, cautious.
  2. Changing just "warm" to "cold" transformed the overall impression: the warm person was seen as generous and happy, the cold person as less so.
  3. Swapping "polite" for "blunt" had much less effect, so some traits are central.

Asch also found a primacy effect: a person described as "intelligent, industrious, impulsive, critical, stubborn, envious" was rated more positively than the same list in reverse order. The halo effect is the tendency for one positive trait (such as attractiveness) to colour judgements of unrelated traits.

Self-fulfilling prophecies

Expectations can change behaviour in ways that confirm them. In the "Pygmalion" study, Rosenthal and Jacobson (1968) told teachers that some randomly chosen pupils were about to "bloom"; these pupils showed larger IQ gains, especially in the youngest years. The study was criticised for its methods, and later reviews found real but generally small expectancy effects (Jussim and Harber, 2005).

Common mistakeTreating fast impressions as accurate. Confidence in snap judgements often exceeds their accuracy.

In 2 lines: People form impressions quickly; central traits, primacy and halo effects shape them. Expectations can be self-fulfilling, though effects are usually small.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. In Asch's study, which pair of traits changed impressions most?
    • Polite and blunt
    • Warm and cold
    • Tall and short
    • Fast and slow
    Show the answer

    Answer: Warm and cold. Swapping warm for cold changed the whole impression, so they were central traits.

  2. An interviewer rates an attractive candidate as more competent with the same CV. This is the…
    • Primacy effect
    • Spotlight effect
    • Halo effect
    • False consensus effect
    Show the answer

    Answer: Halo effect. Attractiveness spilled over into competence judgements.

  3. Which is a fair conclusion about teacher expectancy effects?
    • They are always huge
    • They don't exist
    • They only affect adults
    • They are real but usually small
    Show the answer

    Answer: They are real but usually small. Reviews suggest small, real effects, larger in some groups.

Key terms

Central trait
A trait that strongly shapes the overall impression of a person
Halo effect
One positive trait influencing judgements of unrelated traits
Self-fulfilling prophecy
An expectation that leads to behaviour that confirms it
