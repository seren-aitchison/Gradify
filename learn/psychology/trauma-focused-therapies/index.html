Trauma-focused Therapies

Trauma-focused CBT updates trauma meanings and memories; EMDR uses recall with bilateral stimulation; both are supported for PTSD and recommended by NICE; demands and mechanisms are debated.

Psychology · Clinical Psychology and Interventions · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe trauma-focused CBT and EMDR
  • Explain how trauma-focused therapies aim to reduce PTSD symptoms
  • Evaluate the evidence for trauma-focused therapies
Competence and safetyThis lesson is educational and is not a guide to therapy. Trauma-focused therapies should only be delivered by qualified, registered professionals.

Trauma-focused CBT

Trauma-focused CBT includes approaches such as cognitive therapy for PTSD, based on Ehlers and Clark's model. It helps people update the meaning of the trauma (for example, changing "it was my fault" to a more accurate understanding), elaborate the trauma memory so it is placed in context, and reduce avoidance and safety behaviours. Prolonged exposure involves repeatedly recounting the memory and gradually approaching avoided reminders.

EMDR

Eye movement desensitisation and reprocessing (EMDR) involves recalling traumatic memories while attending to bilateral stimulation, such as following the therapist's finger movements. It aims to help the brain process memories so they become less distressing. Whether the eye movements themselves add to its effectiveness is debated.

Worked example

Evidence and guidance

  1. A Cochrane review (Bisson et al., 2013) found trauma-focused CBT and EMDR were more effective than waiting list or usual care for chronic PTSD.
  2. NICE guidance recommends trauma-focused CBT for adults and children with PTSD, and EMDR for adults with PTSD after non-combat trauma.
  3. Stabilisation and safety may be needed first for people with complex difficulties.

Evaluation

Trauma-focused therapies are among the best-supported treatments for PTSD. But they can be emotionally demanding, some people drop out, and evidence for complex trauma is less clear. Debate continues about what in EMDR is effective.

Common mistakeAssuming talking about trauma is always harmful. Carefully structured trauma-focused therapy is effective for PTSD.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Repeatedly recounting a trauma memory and approaching avoided reminders is…
    • EMDR
    • Prolonged exposure
    • Interpersonal therapy
    • Free association
    Show the answer

    Answer: Prolonged exposure. Prolonged exposure is a trauma-focused technique.

  2. A survivor of an assault learns to replace "I should have fought back" with a more accurate understanding of what happened. This reflects…
    • Exposure therapy for a spider phobia
    • Free association about early childhood
    • Updating trauma meanings in trauma-focused CBT
    • Behavioural activation for low mood
    Show the answer

    Answer: Updating trauma meanings in trauma-focused CBT. Changing the meaning of trauma is central to trauma-focused CBT.

  3. Why is the role of eye movements in EMDR debated?
    • EMDR has no effect on PTSD at all
    • Eye movements are always harmful
    • EMDR is never used in the NHS
    • Studies question whether they add to recall's effect
    Show the answer

    Answer: Studies question whether they add to recall's effect. Benefits may come mainly from processing memories.

Key terms

Trauma-focused CBT
CBT that targets trauma memories and their meanings
EMDR
Recalling trauma memories with bilateral stimulation to reduce distress
Prolonged exposure
Repeatedly recounting trauma and approaching avoided reminders
