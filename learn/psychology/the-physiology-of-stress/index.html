The Physiology of Stress

Stress activates the fast SAM pathway and slower HPA axis; Selye described alarm, resistance and exhaustion; chronic stress can impair immunity and healing.

Psychology · Motivation, Emotion and Wellbeing · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the SAM and HPA pathways
  • Explain Selye's general adaptation syndrome
  • Evaluate evidence linking chronic stress to immune function and illness

Two pathways

The sympathetic adrenal medullary (SAM) pathway responds within seconds: the hypothalamus activates the sympathetic nervous system, and the adrenal medulla releases adrenaline and noradrenaline, raising heart rate and blood pressure. The hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal (HPA) axis responds over minutes: the hypothalamus releases CRH, the pituitary releases ACTH, and the adrenal cortex releases cortisol, which mobilises energy and suppresses non-essential functions.

General adaptation syndrome

Selye (1936, 1956), working with rats exposed to various stressors, described the general adaptation syndrome (GAS): alarm (initial arousal), resistance (the body adapts and copes) and exhaustion (resources are depleted, increasing vulnerability to illness). He argued the same pattern occurred regardless of the stressor.

Worked example

Stress and wound healing

  1. Kiecolt-Glaser et al. (1995) gave small standard wounds to women caring for relatives with dementia and to matched controls.
  2. Wounds took on average about nine days longer to heal in the caregivers.
  3. Chronic stress appeared to impair immune processes involved in healing.

Evaluation

Research links chronic stress to suppressed immune function, cardiovascular disease and susceptibility to infection: Cohen, Tyrrell and Smith (1991) found people reporting more stress were more likely to develop colds after exposure to a virus. But Selye's claim of a single response to all stressors has been revised: responses differ by stressor type and individual, and cognitive appraisal matters.

Common mistakeThinking stress hormones are only harmful. Short-term responses are adaptive; problems arise with chronic activation.

In 2 lines: SAM and HPA pathways produce fast and slower stress responses. Selye's GAS describes stages; chronic stress impairs immunity and healing.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which pathway releases adrenaline within seconds?
    • HPA
    • Parasympathetic
    • Somatic
    • SAM
    Show the answer

    Answer: SAM. The sympathetic adrenal medullary pathway acts fast.

  2. People reporting more stress are more likely to catch colds after virus exposure. This supports…
    • Stress suppressing immunity
    • Stress improving immunity
    • No link between stress and health
    • Drive reduction
    Show the answer

    Answer: Stress suppressing immunity. Cohen et al. linked stress to susceptibility.

  3. Why are short-term stress responses adaptive?
    • They cause illness
    • They prepare the body for action
    • They suppress energy
    • They are always harmful
    Show the answer

    Answer: They prepare the body for action. Acute arousal helps meet immediate demands.

Key terms

SAM pathway
The fast stress pathway releasing adrenaline from the adrenal medulla
HPA axis
The slower stress pathway releasing cortisol from the adrenal cortex
General adaptation syndrome
Selye's three-stage model of alarm, resistance and exhaustion
