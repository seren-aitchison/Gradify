Human–computer Interaction Today

Norman's principles (affordances, signifiers, feedback, mappings) guide usable design; dark patterns exploit biases; automation bias makes appropriate trust in AI a design goal.

Psychology · Contemporary Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain psychological principles of usable design
  • Describe dark patterns and their effects
  • Evaluate challenges in designing human–AI interaction

Usable design

Norman (1988) argued good design makes the right actions visible and natural, using affordances (what an object allows you to do), signifiers (cues showing how to use it), feedback and mappings between controls and effects. Designs should fit human limits on attention and memory, for example through recognition rather than recall, consistent layouts and clear error recovery.

Dark patterns

Dark patterns are design choices that steer users into actions they might not otherwise take, such as hard-to-cancel subscriptions, pre-ticked boxes or misleading buttons. They exploit known cognitive biases, such as defaults and loss aversion. Regulators in the UK and EU have taken action against some practices.

Worked example

Human–AI interaction

  1. Automation bias is the tendency to over-rely on automated suggestions, even when they are wrong.
  2. In healthcare and aviation, over-reliance on automation has contributed to errors.
  3. Good design aims for appropriate trust: showing uncertainty, explaining outputs and keeping humans in control.

Evaluation

HCI shows how psychology can improve everyday technology and safety. But commercial incentives can encourage designs that exploit rather than support users. As AI systems become more capable, designing for appropriate trust and accountability becomes more important.

Common mistakeAssuming user errors always reflect user failings. Poor design often causes errors.

In 2 lines: Norman's principles make designs usable. Dark patterns exploit biases; automation bias challenges human–AI design.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. A cue showing how to use something, such as a "push" label on a door, is a…
    • Dark pattern
    • Automation bias
    • Signifier
    • Mapping error
    Show the answer

    Answer: Signifier. Signifiers are cues, such as labels, that show users how to act.

  2. A website makes it easy to subscribe but hides the cancel button several menus deep. This is…
    • Good usability
    • An affordance
    • Appropriate trust
    • A dark pattern
    Show the answer

    Answer: A dark pattern. Hiding cancellation steers users against their interests.

  3. Why is appropriate trust a design goal for AI systems?
    • Over-trust and under-trust both cause errors
    • Users should trust AI completely
    • Trust doesn't matter
    • AI never makes errors
    Show the answer

    Answer: Over-trust and under-trust both cause errors. Calibrated trust supports good decisions.
