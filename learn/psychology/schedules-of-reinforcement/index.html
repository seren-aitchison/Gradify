Schedules of Reinforcement

Ratio schedules produce higher rates than interval schedules, and variable schedules produce steadier responding and greater resistance to extinction.

Psychology · Learning and Behaviour · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe continuous and partial schedules, including FR, VR, FI and VI
  • Explain the response patterns each schedule produces
  • Apply schedules of reinforcement to real-world behaviour, such as gambling

The schedules

  • Continuous reinforcement (CRF): every response is reinforced. Fast learning, fast extinction.
  • Fixed ratio (FR): reinforcement after a set number of responses (piecework pay). High rate, with a pause after each reinforcer.
  • Variable ratio (VR): after a varying number of responses averaging a set value (gambling machines). High, steady rate; very resistant to extinction.
  • Fixed interval (FI): the first response after a set time is reinforced (a weekly test). A "scallop": slow after reinforcement, speeding up as the time nears.
  • Variable interval (VI): the first response after a varying time (checking for messages). Moderate, steady rate.

In everyday life schedules are often mixed: a salesperson may earn a basic wage (interval) plus commission (ratio).

Worked example

Why are gambling machines so compelling?

  1. They pay out on a variable ratio schedule: any play might win.
  2. The unpredictability sustains high, steady rates of play.
  3. Behaviour learned under partial reinforcement persists through long losing runs (the partial reinforcement extinction effect).
  4. Near-misses and sounds may act as additional conditioned reinforcers.

Partial reinforcement extinction effect

Behaviour reinforced only some of the time persists longer in extinction than behaviour reinforced every time. One explanation: under partial reinforcement, unrewarded responses are part of the normal pattern, so it takes longer to detect that rewards have stopped.

Common mistakeThinking continuous reinforcement produces the most durable behaviour. It produces the fastest learning but the fastest extinction.

In 2 lines: Schedules can be continuous or partial; partial schedules are ratio or interval, fixed or variable. Variable ratio schedules give high, persistent responding, as in gambling.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Reinforcement after every fifth response is a…
    • Variable ratio schedule
    • Fixed ratio schedule
    • Fixed interval schedule
    • Variable interval schedule
    Show the answer

    Answer: Fixed ratio schedule. A set number of responses is a fixed ratio (FR5).

  2. A student revises little after each fortnightly test and then crams just before the next. Which schedule does this resemble?
    • Variable ratio
    • Continuous
    • Fixed interval
    • Variable interval
    Show the answer

    Answer: Fixed interval. Responding that rises as a predictable time approaches is the fixed-interval scallop.

  3. Why is behaviour learned on a variable ratio schedule so hard to extinguish?
    • Every response was reinforced during learning
    • The reinforcer was always delivered on time
    • Variable ratio schedules produce low response rates
    • Unrewarded responses are normal, so a stop in rewards is hard to detect
    Show the answer

    Answer: Unrewarded responses are normal, so a stop in rewards is hard to detect. The partial reinforcement extinction effect: unpredictability makes extinction slower.

Key terms

Variable ratio schedule
Reinforcement after an unpredictable number of responses, averaging a set value
Fixed interval schedule
Reinforcement for the first response after a fixed period of time
Partial reinforcement extinction effect
Behaviour reinforced intermittently persists longer in extinction than behaviour reinforced every time
