Future Directions

Future psychology involves big team science, computational and AI methods, precision approaches, interdisciplinary work and global participation; ethical and equity challenges accompany these opportunities.

Psychology · Contemporary Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe emerging directions in psychological research
  • Explain the role of big team science and global samples
  • Evaluate opportunities and risks of future directions

Emerging directions

  • Big team science: large collaborations run the same study across many countries.
  • Computational and AI methods: modelling behaviour and analysing language, images and passive data.
  • Precision approaches: tailoring interventions to individuals' profiles.
  • Interdisciplinary work: combining psychology with genetics, neuroscience, economics and sociology.
  • Open and global science: diverse samples and researchers.

Big team science

The Psychological Science Accelerator (Moshontz et al., 2018) is a network of laboratories worldwide that run studies together, increasing sample size, diversity and generalisability. Multi-lab projects such as the Many Labs studies test whether effects replicate across cultures and settings.

Worked example

Opportunities and risks

  1. Passive data from phones and wearables could track mood and behaviour in daily life.
  2. This raises privacy, consent and bias concerns, especially if data are used commercially.
  3. Researchers argue that ethical frameworks must develop alongside technical capabilities.

Evaluation

Future directions promise more reliable, generalisable and useful psychology. But large-scale and digital approaches raise ethical questions and risk concentrating resources in wealthy institutions. Psychology's future depends on combining methodological rigour with ethical responsibility and openness to diverse perspectives.

Common mistakeAssuming new technology automatically improves psychology. Rigour and ethics matter as much as tools.

In 2 lines: Big team science, computation, precision and global participation shape the future. Opportunities come with ethical and equity challenges.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Large collaborations running the same study across many sites are called…
    • Precision psychiatry
    • Registered Reports
    • Reverse inference
    • Big team science
    Show the answer

    Answer: Big team science. Big team science pools sites and samples.

  2. A study runs the same experiment in 40 countries to test whether an effect generalises. This illustrates…
    • Big team science
    • Reverse inference
    • Overfitting
    • Cognitive offloading
    Show the answer

    Answer: Big team science. Multi-site studies test generalisability.

  3. Why might big team science risk inequality?
    • It always includes everyone equally
    • Resources may concentrate in wealthy institutions
    • It has no costs
    • It uses tiny samples
    Show the answer

    Answer: Resources may concentrate in wealthy institutions. Funding and leadership can remain unequal.
