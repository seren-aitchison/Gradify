Personality, Behaviour and Mental Health

Personality traits relate to health behaviours and mental health risk, especially high neuroticism and low conscientiousness; several causal models apply together.

Psychology · Personality and Individual Differences · about 2 minutes · sources checked 2026-11

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe links between personality traits and health behaviours
  • Explain associations between traits and mental health difficulties
  • Evaluate models explaining personality–mental health links

Education, not diagnosis

This lesson explains research associations for learning purposes. Personality scores can't diagnose a mental health condition. If you are concerned about your own or someone else's mental health, talk to a GP or another qualified professional; in an emergency in the UK, call 999 or contact Samaritans on 116 123.

Traits and health behaviour

Conscientiousness predicts healthier behaviour: less smoking, more exercise, better diet and adherence to medication (Bogg and Roberts, 2004). This may help explain why conscientious people live longer. Neuroticism is linked to more health complaints and, through stress, to some health risks.

Traits and mental health

A meta-analysis found people with diagnosed anxiety, depressive and substance use disorders tended to score higher in neuroticism and lower in conscientiousness than controls, and lower in extraversion for some disorders (Kotov et al., 2010). Neuroticism is one of the strongest known predictors of later common mental health problems.

Worked example

Four models

  1. Vulnerability: traits such as high neuroticism increase risk of later difficulties.
  2. Scar: experiencing a mental health difficulty changes personality afterwards.
  3. Common cause: shared genes or experiences influence both traits and mental health.
  4. Spectrum: some conditions are extreme ends of normal trait dimensions. Longitudinal evidence suggests several of these processes operate together.

Modern classifications reflect these links: ICD-11 (World Health Organization, 2019) describes personality disorder using severity and trait domains (such as negative affectivity and detachment) rather than separate types.

Common mistakeTreating high neuroticism as a diagnosis. Traits describe risk tendencies in populations, not conditions in individuals.

In 2 lines: Conscientiousness predicts healthier behaviour; neuroticism predicts mental health risk. Vulnerability, scar, common-cause and spectrum models explain links; ICD-11 uses trait domains. Not diagnostic.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which trait is most strongly linked to risk of anxiety and depression?
    • Neuroticism
    • Openness
    • Agreeableness
    • Extraversion
    Show the answer

    Answer: Neuroticism. Neuroticism is a strong predictor of common mental health problems.

  2. After recovering from depression, a person scores higher on neuroticism than before. Which model fits?
    • Vulnerability model
    • Scar model
    • Spectrum model
    • Common cause model
    Show the answer

    Answer: Scar model. The difficulty changed personality afterwards.

  3. Why might shared genes explain the link between neuroticism and depression?
    • They prove depression causes neuroticism
    • Genes have no role
    • They can influence both
    • They make traits irrelevant
    Show the answer

    Answer: They can influence both. Shared genes are a common cause that can raise both neuroticism and depression risk.

Key terms

Neuroticism
The tendency to experience negative emotions
Vulnerability model
The idea that traits increase risk of later difficulties
Spectrum model
The idea that some conditions are extremes of normal trait dimensions
