Contemporary Psychology

Contemporary psychology is organised by topic and method rather than by school, with integrative models that combine biological, psychological and social explanations.

Psychology · Foundations of Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the main sub-fields of contemporary psychology
  • Explain how integrative frameworks such as the biopsychosocial model combine approaches
  • Analyse the trends changing psychology today

From schools to fields

Few psychologists now describe themselves as behaviourists or humanists. Instead they work in fields defined by topic, each drawing on several approaches: cognitive, developmental, social, biological, clinical, health, educational, occupational, forensic and more. The APA has more than 50 divisions, and the BPS has sections and divisions for its professional and research areas.

Integration

The biopsychosocial model (Engel, 1977) argues that health and illness arise from the interaction of biological, psychological and social factors. It replaced a purely biomedical model in much of health care and is now a common framework for mental health. Cognitive neuroscience similarly integrates cognitive models with biology.

Worked example

Explaining chronic pain biopsychosocially

  1. Biological: tissue damage and changes in pain processing in the nervous system.
  2. Psychological: catastrophising thoughts, fear of movement and low mood amplify pain.
  3. Social: work stress, isolation and access to care shape how pain develops.
  4. Treatment combines physical rehabilitation, psychological therapy and social support.

Forces of change

  • Open science: preregistration and data sharing after replication failures.
  • Diversity: recognising that most research samples have been Western, educated, industrialised, rich and democratic (WEIRD; Henrich, Heine and Norenzayan, 2010).
  • Technology: big data, smartphones, computational modelling and AI.
  • Applied impact: psychology in policy, health systems and technology design.
Common mistakeThinking integration means "everything matters equally". The biopsychosocial model asks which factors matter, how much and how they interact, which can be tested.

In 2 lines: Contemporary psychology is organised into topic-based fields that combine approaches. Integrative models, open science, diversity and technology are reshaping it.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Who proposed the biopsychosocial model?
    • Sigmund Freud
    • B. F. Skinner
    • George Engel
    • Abraham Maslow
    Show the answer

    Answer: George Engel. Engel's 1977 paper in Science argued for a model beyond the biomedical.

  2. A clinician explains a patient's depression through a family history of depression, negative thinking patterns and recent job loss. Which framework is this?
    • Radical behaviourism
    • Structuralism
    • Psychoanalysis
    • The biopsychosocial model
    Show the answer

    Answer: The biopsychosocial model. It combines biological (family history), psychological (thinking) and social (job loss) factors.

  3. Why does reliance on WEIRD samples matter for psychology?
    • Findings may not generalise to most of the world's population
    • WEIRD participants give less honest answers
    • It makes experiments too expensive
    • It means psychology can't use questionnaires
    Show the answer

    Answer: Findings may not generalise to most of the world's population. WEIRD societies are a minority globally and sometimes unusual on psychological measures, so universal claims need broader samples.

Key terms

Biopsychosocial model
A framework explaining health and behaviour through interacting biological, psychological and social factors
WEIRD
Western, educated, industrialised, rich and democratic: describing the populations most psychology research has sampled
