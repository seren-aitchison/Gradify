Emotional Intelligence

EI can be defined as an ability or a trait; ability EI modestly predicts outcomes in emotional jobs, while popular claims overstate its importance.

Psychology · Personality and Individual Differences · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the ability model of emotional intelligence
  • Distinguish ability EI from trait EI and their measures
  • Evaluate evidence on what EI predicts and the claims made for it

The ability model

Salovey and Mayer (1990) defined emotional intelligence as the ability to monitor one's own and others' feelings and use this to guide thinking and action. Their four-branch model includes: perceiving emotions, using emotions to facilitate thought, understanding emotions and managing emotions. It is measured with the MSCEIT, a performance test with right and wrong answers, such as identifying emotions in faces.

Trait EI

Trait EI (Petrides and Furnham, 2001) treats EI as a set of self-perceptions about emotional abilities, measured by self-report questionnaires such as the TEIQue. Trait EI overlaps strongly with Big Five traits, especially low neuroticism, extraversion and agreeableness. Goleman's (1995) popular book broadened EI to include motivation and social skills, and made strong claims that EI matters "more than IQ".

Worked example

EI at work

  1. Joseph and Newman (2010) meta-analysed EI and job performance.
  2. Ability EI predicted performance modestly, mainly in jobs high in emotional labour, such as customer service.
  3. Mixed and trait EI measures predicted performance but overlapped heavily with personality and cognitive ability.

Evaluation

EI has drawn attention to emotional skills and can be taught: some school social and emotional learning programmes improve social behaviour and attainment (Durlak et al., 2011). But definitions vary, self-report measures overlap with personality, and claims that EI matters more than IQ are not supported by the evidence.

Common mistakeAssuming self-reported EI measures the same thing as ability EI. Self-report scores largely reflect personality.

In 2 lines: Ability EI has four branches and is measured by performance tests; trait EI uses self-report. EI predicts some outcomes modestly; claims it outweighs IQ aren't supported.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which test measures ability emotional intelligence?
    • Rorschach
    • MSCEIT
    • TAT
    • EPQ
    Show the answer

    Answer: MSCEIT. The MSCEIT is a performance test of ability EI, with right and wrong answers.

  2. A customer service worker calms an angry customer by recognising and responding to their feelings. Which branch is most involved?
    • Perceiving shapes
    • Using numbers
    • Managing emotions
    • Spatial reasoning
    Show the answer

    Answer: Managing emotions. Managing others' emotions is a key branch.

  3. Why are claims that EI matters "more than IQ" criticised?
    • EI can't be defined
    • IQ predicts nothing
    • EI has no relation to work
    • Evidence shows modest EI effects
    Show the answer

    Answer: Evidence shows modest EI effects. Meta-analyses find EI adds modestly beyond IQ and personality.

Key terms

Emotional intelligence
The ability to perceive, use, understand and manage emotions
MSCEIT
A performance-based test of ability emotional intelligence
Trait EI
Self-perceptions of emotional abilities, measured by self-report
