Eysenck's Theory

Eysenck described extraversion, neuroticism and psychoticism with biological bases; the theory is testable and partly supported, but arousal and crime links are mixed.

Psychology · Personality and Individual Differences · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe Eysenck's three dimensions of personality
  • Explain the proposed biological basis of extraversion and neuroticism
  • Evaluate Eysenck's theory, including his link between personality and crime

Three dimensions

Eysenck (1947, 1967) proposed personality could be described by a few broad dimensions: extraversion–introversion (sociable and lively versus quiet and reserved), neuroticism–stability (anxious and moody versus calm) and, later, psychoticism (cold, aggressive, impulsive). He measured them with the Eysenck Personality Questionnaire (EPQ).

Biological basis

Eysenck argued extraversion reflects differences in cortical arousal, regulated by the ascending reticular activating system. Introverts are chronically more aroused, so they avoid extra stimulation; extraverts are under-aroused, so they seek stimulation. Neuroticism was linked to reactivity of the limbic system and sympathetic nervous system. He argued these differences are largely inherited.

Worked example

Lemon juice

  1. In a classic demonstration, drops of lemon juice placed on the tongue produced more saliva in introverts than in extraverts (Corcoran, 1964).
  2. This was taken as evidence of greater physiological reactivity in introverts.
  3. Later physiological studies found mixed support for simple arousal differences.

Personality and crime

Eysenck (1964) proposed people high in extraversion, neuroticism and psychoticism are harder to condition and more likely to offend. Some studies found offenders score higher on psychoticism and neuroticism, but support for extraversion is mixed (Farrington, Biron and LeBlanc, 1982), and the theory underplays social causes of crime.

Evaluation

Eysenck's theory was influential and testable, linking traits to biology and heritability. Twin studies support substantial heritability of extraversion and neuroticism. But his arousal model has received mixed support, psychoticism is a poorly defined dimension, and many researchers prefer the Big Five's broader description.

Common mistakeAssuming introverts dislike people. Eysenck's introverts prefer less stimulation, not necessarily less company.

In 2 lines: Eysenck proposed extraversion, neuroticism and psychoticism, measured by the EPQ. He linked them to arousal and inheritance; evidence for arousal and criminality links is mixed.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Eysenck measured personality with the…
    • Costa and McCrae's NEO-PI-R
    • Eysenck Personality Questionnaire
    • The Rorschach inkblot test
    • Ainsworth's Strange Situation
    Show the answer

    Answer: Eysenck Personality Questionnaire. The Eysenck Personality Questionnaire measured his three dimensions.

  2. According to Eysenck, why might an introvert prefer a quiet library?
    • They are under-aroused
    • They dislike books
    • They are already highly aroused
    • They are high in psychoticism
    Show the answer

    Answer: They are already highly aroused. Introverts are already highly aroused, so they avoid extra stimulation.

  3. Why is Eysenck's theory of crime criticised?
    • It ignores biology
    • It uses no measures
    • It denies personality
    • It underplays social causes
    Show the answer

    Answer: It underplays social causes. Poverty and environment are important causes of offending.

Key terms

Extraversion
Sociability and stimulation-seeking
Psychoticism
Eysenck's dimension of coldness, aggression and impulsivity
Cortical arousal
The level of activity in the cortex, proposed to differ by extraversion
