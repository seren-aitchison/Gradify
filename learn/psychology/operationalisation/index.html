Operationalisation

Operationalisation makes concepts measurable; every operational definition captures only part of the concept, so the choice shapes the conclusions.

Psychology · Research Methods · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define operationalisation and give examples
  • Operationalise abstract variables for a study
  • Evaluate operational definitions for validity and reliability

From concept to measure

Psychology studies concepts that can't be seen directly: intelligence, anxiety, memory, aggression. To study them, researchers give each an operational definition: a precise statement of how it will be measured or manipulated. "Stress" might be operationalised as a score on the Perceived Stress Scale, as salivary cortisol, or as heart rate during a task.

What makes a good operationalisation

  • It is precise: anyone could repeat it exactly.
  • It is valid: it captures the concept, not something else.
  • It is reliable: it gives consistent results.
  • It is appropriate to the population and setting.
Worked example

Operationalising aggression in children

  1. Option 1: number of times a child hits a Bobo doll in 20 minutes. Precise and reliable, but hitting a toy may not be real aggression.
  2. Option 2: teacher ratings on a standard aggression scale. Covers everyday behaviour, but depends on teachers' judgement.
  3. Using both and comparing them gives a better picture than either alone.

Manipulated variables need operationalising too: "sleep deprivation" might mean staying awake for 24 hours under supervision, and "positive mood" might mean watching a five-minute comedy clip.

Investigate with the lab. Turn each study's vague variables into measurable ones.

Common mistakeTreating the measure as if it were the concept. A memory test score measures performance on that test, not "memory" in every sense.

In 2 lines: Operationalisation turns abstract constructs into precise measures or manipulations. Good operational definitions are precise, valid, reliable and appropriate; each captures only part of a construct.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Measuring "hunger" as hours since the last meal is an example of…
    • Counterbalancing
    • Operationalisation
    • Debriefing
    • Generalisation
    Show the answer

    Answer: Operationalisation. It defines the concept in measurable terms.

  2. Which is the most precise operationalisation of "helpfulness"?
    • Whether someone seems helpful
    • A person's own feeling of kindness
    • Pens picked up for a stranger in five minutes
    • Being a good person
    Show the answer

    Answer: Pens picked up for a stranger in five minutes. A precise, countable behaviour can be measured and replicated.

  3. Why might two studies of "anxiety" reach different conclusions?
    • Anxiety can't be studied scientifically
    • Both studies must have made errors
    • Anxiety is the same as stress
    • They may have operationalised anxiety differently
    Show the answer

    Answer: They may have operationalised anxiety differently. Different measures capture different aspects of a construct.

  4. What does operationalising a variable mean?
    • Defining it in a measurable way
    • Changing it during the study
    • Removing it from the study
    • Testing it on animals
    Show the answer

    Answer: Defining it in a measurable way. For example, aggression might be operationalised as the number of hits on a doll.

Key terms

Operationalisation
Defining a variable in terms of how it will be measured or manipulated
Construct
An abstract concept, such as intelligence, that is inferred rather than directly observed
