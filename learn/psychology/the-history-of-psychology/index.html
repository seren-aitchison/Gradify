The History of Psychology

Each major approach arose partly as a reaction to the limits of the one before: behaviourism rejected unreliable introspection; cognitive psychology rejected behaviourism's refusal to study the mind.

Psychology · Foundations of Psychology · about 3 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe key events in the development of psychology as a discipline
  • Explain how each new approach responded to the limits of the one before
  • Evaluate the "great founders" account of psychology's history

Philosophical and physiological roots

Philosophers had long asked psychological questions: Descartes (1641) argued mind and body are separate substances (dualism); Locke (1690) argued the mind begins as a blank slate filled by experience. In the 19th century, physiologists began measuring the senses: Fechner's Elements of Psychophysics (1860) related physical stimulus intensity to sensation, showing mental events could be measured.

A new science

  • 1879: Wilhelm Wundt opened a laboratory for experimental psychology in Leipzig, usually taken as psychology's start as a separate science. His students used trained introspection to report the elements of conscious experience.
  • 1885: Ebbinghaus published experiments on his own memory using nonsense syllables, producing the forgetting curve.
  • 1890: William James's Principles of Psychology set out functionalism: the mind as adaptive, serving survival.
  • 1900: Freud's The Interpretation of Dreams introduced psychoanalysis, emphasising unconscious motives.
  • 1913: Watson's "behaviourist manifesto" argued psychology should study only observable behaviour.
  • 1950s–60s: the cognitive revolution (Miller, 1956; Chomsky's 1959 critique of Skinner; Neisser, 1967) returned to mental processes, now modelled as information processing.
Worked example

Why did introspection fall out of favour?

  1. Trained observers in different laboratories reported different results for the same task.
  2. With no way to check private reports, disagreements couldn't be resolved.
  3. Behaviourists responded by studying only what could be observed by others; later, cognitive psychologists found indirect, testable ways to study mental processes.

Whose history?

Textbook histories often feature a few founders, mostly white Western men. Women and minority psychologists were present but often excluded from posts: Mary Whiton Calkins completed the requirements for a Harvard PhD but was refused the degree because she was a woman, and later became the first woman president of the American Psychological Association (1905). Kenneth and Mamie Phipps Clark's research on Black children's self-image was cited in the US Supreme Court's 1954 school desegregation ruling.

Common mistakeTreating dates such as 1879 as the moment psychology "began". They are useful markers, but ideas developed gradually and in many countries at once.

In 2 lines: Psychology grew from philosophy and physiology; Wundt's 1879 laboratory marks its emergence as an experimental science. Behaviourism, psychoanalysis, humanism and cognitive psychology each responded to the limits of earlier approaches.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which event is usually taken as the start of psychology as a separate experimental science?
    • Wundt's Leipzig laboratory, 1879
    • Freud's The Interpretation of Dreams, 1900
    • Watson's behaviourist manifesto, 1913
    • Neisser's Cognitive Psychology, 1967
    Show the answer

    Answer: Wundt's Leipzig laboratory, 1879. The Leipzig laboratory is the conventional marker: the first institution devoted to experimental psychology. The others are later turning points.

  2. Cognitive psychologists in the 1960s studied memory using reaction times and recall tests. Why was this acceptable to scientists who had rejected introspection?
    • It relied on participants' descriptions of their thoughts
    • It inferred mental processes from objective, repeatable measurements
    • It avoided the mind and studied only behaviour
    • It used the same trained observers as Wundt
    Show the answer

    Answer: It inferred mental processes from objective, repeatable measurements. The key change was method: mental processes became theoretical constructs tested through observable, repeatable data, rather than private reports.

  3. Which is the best criticism of the "great founders" account of psychology's history?
    • It wrongly includes Wundt, who in fact carried out no experiments of his own
    • It gives far too much credit to the cognitive revolution of the 1960s
    • It overlooks contributors excluded from academic posts and the gradual spread of ideas
    • It relies on primary sources, which historians always treat as unreliable
    Show the answer

    Answer: It overlooks contributors excluded from academic posts and the gradual spread of ideas. Historians argue such accounts simplify a gradual, international process and neglect people (often women and minorities) who were denied recognition, such as Calkins.

Key terms

Introspection
Wundt's method of trained observers reporting the contents of their own conscious experience
Cognitive revolution
The return, from the 1950s, to studying mental processes as information processing
