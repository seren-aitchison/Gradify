Critiques of Evolutionary Psychology

Evolutionary psychology faces critiques about testability, modularity, samples and determinism; responses stress rigorous, multi-method testing and integration with culture.

Psychology · Evolutionary Psychology and Behavioural Ecology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the main scientific critiques of evolutionary psychology
  • Explain ethical and political concerns, including determinism and the naturalistic fallacy
  • Evaluate responses to these critiques

Scientific critiques

  • Testability: hypotheses about the EEA are hard to test because ancestral environments are only partly known; explanations can become just-so stories (Gould and Lewontin, 1979).
  • Modularity: critics argue the brain is more flexible and plastic than strong modularity suggests (Buller, 2005).
  • Samples: many studies use Western undergraduates (WEIRD samples).
  • Genetics: specific genes for proposed mechanisms are rarely identified.

Ethical and political critiques

Critics worry evolutionary explanations can be used to justify inequality, such as gender roles or aggression, through biological determinism. The naturalistic fallacy is the mistake of concluding that what is natural is good or right. Rose and Rose (2000) argued evolutionary psychology often reflects social assumptions presented as science.

Worked example

Responses

  1. Evolutionary psychologists reply that explaining a tendency isn't justifying it, and that understanding evolved tendencies can help design better environments.
  2. They point to cross-cultural studies, multiple methods (experiments, physiology, comparative data) and predictions that were confirmed before data were collected.
  3. Many now emphasise interaction with culture, plasticity and individual differences (Confer et al., 2010).

A balanced view

Evolutionary psychology adds an important question: why might a mind work this way? Its value depends on rigorous testing, diverse samples and integration with developmental, cultural and neuroscientific evidence. Its claims should be judged case by case rather than accepted or rejected wholesale.

Common mistakeRejecting or accepting evolutionary psychology wholesale. Each hypothesis needs evaluating on its evidence.

In 2 lines: Critiques address testability, modularity, samples, genetics and determinism. Responses stress multi-method testing and interaction with culture; claims should be judged case by case.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Arguing that aggression is acceptable because it evolved commits the…
    • Naturalistic fallacy
    • Ecological fallacy
    • Gambler's fallacy
    • Base-rate fallacy
    Show the answer

    Answer: Naturalistic fallacy. Concluding that what is natural is right is the naturalistic fallacy.

  2. A study of mate preferences uses only US university students. Which critique applies?
    • Modularity
    • WEIRD samples
    • Kin selection
    • Mismatch
    Show the answer

    Answer: WEIRD samples. Narrow WEIRD samples limit how far findings generalise to all humans.

  3. How do evolutionary psychologists respond to the just-so story critique?
    • By avoiding all testing
    • By relying on stories alone
    • Multi-method testing
    • By rejecting evolution
    Show the answer

    Answer: Multi-method testing. Prior predictions and convergent methods strengthen claims.

Key terms

Biological determinism
The view that biology fixes behaviour, ignoring environment and choice
Naturalistic fallacy
Concluding that what is natural is good or right
Just-so story
An untested, plausible-sounding adaptive explanation
