Psychological Treatment of PTSD

Early responses offer support and monitoring; debriefing is not recommended; trauma-focused therapies are first-line; ICD-11 complex PTSD may need stabilisation, though its length is debated.

Psychology · Clinical Psychology and Interventions · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe how PTSD treatment is organised over time after trauma
  • Explain why debriefing is not recommended and trauma-focused therapy is
  • Evaluate approaches to complex PTSD
Competence and safetyThis lesson is educational and is not a guide to diagnosis or therapy. Anyone affected by trauma should speak to a GP or another qualified professional.

Over time after trauma

Many people recover naturally after trauma, so early responses focus on practical support, information and monitoring, sometimes called watchful waiting. NICE guidance advises against offering psychological debriefing (a single session soon after trauma) to prevent PTSD, as evidence shows it doesn't help and may harm some people. For people with symptoms, trauma-focused psychological therapies are recommended, including in the first month for those with clinically important symptoms.

Complex PTSD

ICD-11 includes complex PTSD, which involves PTSD symptoms plus difficulties with emotion regulation, negative self-concept and relationships, often after prolonged or repeated trauma such as childhood abuse. Treatment may involve a phase of stabilisation and skills building before trauma processing, though how long stabilisation should last is debated.

Worked example

Barriers to treatment

  1. Avoidance, a core PTSD symptom, can make people reluctant to seek or continue trauma-focused therapy.
  2. Shame, mistrust and practical barriers also reduce access.
  3. Explaining the rationale and building trust helps engagement. This example is illustrative.

Evaluation

Trauma-focused therapies are effective for PTSD, and guidance has moved away from debriefing. Evidence for complex PTSD is less developed, and some argue extended stabilisation delays effective treatment, while others argue it is needed for safety. Services must balance evidence with individual readiness.

Common mistakeAssuming everyone needs therapy straight after trauma. Many recover with support and time.

In 2 lines: Support and monitoring early; debriefing not recommended; trauma-focused therapy first-line. Complex PTSD may need stabilisation, with its length debated.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Monitoring symptoms while offering support after trauma is called…
    • Watchful waiting
    • Debriefing
    • Flooding
    • Free association
    Show the answer

    Answer: Watchful waiting. Watchful waiting allows natural recovery.

  2. After years of childhood abuse, a woman has PTSD symptoms plus intense shame and difficulty trusting others. ICD-11 might describe this as…
    • Panic disorder
    • Complex PTSD
    • Specific phobia
    • Bipolar disorder
    Show the answer

    Answer: Complex PTSD. Added self and relationship difficulties suggest complex PTSD.

  3. Why is extended stabilisation in complex PTSD debated?
    • It always speeds up recovery for everyone
    • It has been banned by NICE guidance
    • It may support safety but delay trauma processing
    • It replaces all other forms of therapy
    Show the answer

    Answer: It may support safety but delay trauma processing. The safety benefits of stabilisation must be balanced against delaying effective treatment.

Key terms

Watchful waiting

Complex PTSD
