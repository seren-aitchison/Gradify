Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT)

ACT aims for psychological flexibility through acceptance, defusion, present-moment awareness, self-as-context, values and committed action; it changes the relationship with thoughts rather than their content.

Psychology · Clinical Psychology and Interventions · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the core processes of ACT
  • Explain how ACT differs from traditional CBT
  • Evaluate the evidence for ACT
Competence and safetyThis lesson is educational and is not a guide to therapy. ACT should only be delivered by qualified, registered professionals.

Psychological flexibility

ACT (Hayes et al., 2006) aims to increase psychological flexibility: being open to experiences and acting on what matters. Its six core processes include acceptance (allowing difficult feelings), cognitive defusion (seeing thoughts as thoughts rather than facts), present-moment awareness, self-as-context, values and committed action.

How ACT differs from CBT

Traditional CBT often challenges the content of thoughts to change them. ACT focuses on changing the relationship with thoughts: rather than disputing "I'm a failure", a person might notice "I'm having the thought that I'm a failure" and choose to act on their values anyway. ACT views attempts to control or avoid unwanted experiences, called experiential avoidance, as a source of difficulty.

Worked example

Cognitive defusion

  1. A person might repeat a distressing word aloud quickly until it loses its meaning, or imagine thoughts as leaves floating down a stream.
  2. The aim is not to make thoughts disappear but to reduce their grip.
  3. This creates space to act in line with values. This example is illustrative.

Evaluation

ACT has a growing evidence base across conditions, including chronic pain, anxiety and depression, and is flexible across settings. Critics argue many trials are small, and it is unclear whether ACT works differently from CBT or through similar mechanisms. Its concepts can be hard to measure.

Common mistakeAssuming acceptance means giving up. ACT combines acceptance with committed action towards values.

In 2 lines: ACT builds psychological flexibility through six core processes. It changes the relationship with thoughts; evidence is growing but mechanisms are debated.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Being open to experiences while acting on what matters is called…
    • Experiential avoidance
    • Psychological flexibility
    • Cognitive restructuring
    • Free association
    Show the answer

    Answer: Psychological flexibility. ACT aims to increase psychological flexibility.

  2. Instead of arguing with the thought "I'm useless", a client says "I notice I'm having the thought that I'm useless". This is…
    • Cognitive restructuring
    • Exposure
    • Cognitive defusion
    • Behavioural activation
    Show the answer

    Answer: Cognitive defusion. Stepping back to notice a thought as a thought, rather than a fact, is cognitive defusion.

  3. Why do critics question how distinct ACT is from CBT?
    • It has no evidence at all
    • It is identical to psychoanalysis
    • It never mentions values
    • It may work through similar mechanisms
    Show the answer

    Answer: It may work through similar mechanisms. Shared mechanisms would blur the difference.
