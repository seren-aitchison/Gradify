Behaviour Change

COM-B explains behaviour through capability, opportunity and motivation; BCTs such as self-monitoring and goal setting help; habits take time; environments matter.

Psychology · Applied Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain the COM-B model and the behaviour change wheel
  • Describe common behaviour change techniques and how habits form
  • Evaluate the effectiveness of behaviour change interventions

COM-B

Michie, van Stralen and West (2011) proposed the COM-B model: behaviour depends on capability (physical and psychological), opportunity (physical and social) and motivation (reflective and automatic). Their behaviour change wheel links these to intervention types such as education, training, incentives, environmental restructuring and enablement.

Techniques and habits

Michie et al. (2013) published a taxonomy of 93 behaviour change techniques (BCTs), including goal setting, self-monitoring, feedback, prompts, social support and implementation intentions. Habits form through repetition in a stable context: Lally et al. (2010) found it took a median of 66 days for a new behaviour to become automatic, with wide variation (18 to 254 days).

Worked example

Self-monitoring

  1. Self-monitoring (recording a behaviour, such as steps or food) is among the most widely used techniques.
  2. A meta-regression of interventions for healthy eating and physical activity found that interventions combining self-monitoring with other self-regulation techniques were more effective (Michie et al., 2009).
  3. Wearable devices make self-monitoring easier, though long-term use often declines.

Evaluation

Behaviour change science provides a shared language and systematic design. But many interventions have small, short-lived effects, and individual-level change is limited when environments (such as food availability, transport or income) work against it. Combining individual techniques with environmental and policy change tends to work better.

Common mistakeAssuming habits form in 21 days. Lally et al. found a median of 66 days, with large variation.

In 2 lines: COM-B and the behaviour change wheel structure intervention design. BCTs include goal setting and self-monitoring; habits took a median of 66 days to form; environments matter.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Recording your daily steps is the behaviour change technique of…
    • Self-monitoring
    • Environmental restructuring
    • Incentivisation
    • Modelling
    Show the answer

    Answer: Self-monitoring. Tracking your own behaviour is self-monitoring.

  2. A workplace moves bikes racks to the front door and adds showers to encourage cycling. In COM-B terms this increases…
    • Psychological capability
    • Physical opportunity
    • Reflective motivation
    • Automatic motivation
    Show the answer

    Answer: Physical opportunity. Bike racks and showers change the physical opportunity to cycle.

  3. Why might individual behaviour change be limited?
    • Individuals never change
    • Techniques are always ineffective
    • Environments can work against change
    • Habits can't form
    Show the answer

    Answer: Environments can work against change. Food availability, income and transport shape behaviour.

Key terms

COM-B
A model in which behaviour depends on capability, opportunity and motivation
Behaviour change technique
An active ingredient of an intervention, such as self-monitoring
Habit
A behaviour performed automatically in response to a context
