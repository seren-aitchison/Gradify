Biological Explanations of Mental Disorders

Genes, neurochemistry and brain structure contribute to mental disorders, but interact with psychological and social factors.

Psychology · Biological Psychology and Neuroscience · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe genetic, neurochemical and neuroanatomical explanations of mental disorders
  • Explain the diathesis–stress and biopsychosocial models
  • Evaluate biological explanations, including reductionism and determinism

Not a diagnosis

This lesson explains research about mental health conditions for learning purposes. It can't be used to diagnose anyone. If you are worried about your own or someone else's mental health, speak to a GP or another qualified professional; in an emergency in the UK, call 999 or contact Samaritans on 116 123.

Types of biological explanation

  • Genetic: family and twin studies show many disorders run in families. Schizophrenia's heritability is estimated at around 80% (Hilker et al., 2018), though many genes of small effect are involved.
  • Neurochemical: the dopamine hypothesis proposes that schizophrenia involves overactive dopamine in some pathways, supported by antipsychotics blocking D2 receptors and by amphetamines worsening psychotic symptoms.
  • Neuroanatomical: brain imaging finds average differences, such as enlarged ventricles in some people with schizophrenia, though overlap with the general population is large.

Diathesis–stress

The diathesis–stress model proposes that a disorder develops when a vulnerability (diathesis), which may be genetic or from early experience, combines with stress (life events, trauma, substance use). The biopsychosocial model extends this: biological, psychological and social factors interact. Tienari et al. (2004) found adopted children with a biological mother with schizophrenia were more likely to develop schizophrenia-spectrum disorders if raised in adoptive families with poor communication.

Worked example

Why biology alone is incomplete

  1. MZ twin concordance for schizophrenia is well below 100%, so genes don't determine the disorder.
  2. Social factors such as urban upbringing, migration and childhood adversity are associated with higher risk.
  3. Effective treatments include medication, psychological therapy and social support, each with evidence.

Investigate with the lab. Look up dopamine and serotonin in the explorer and note the disorders and drugs linked to each.

Common mistakeTreating biological findings as proof that a disorder is "only in the brain" or that someone is destined to develop it. Risk is probabilistic and multifactorial.

In 2 lines: Genetic, neurochemical and neuroanatomical factors contribute to mental disorders. Diathesis–stress and biopsychosocial models explain why biology alone is incomplete; this lesson isn't diagnostic.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which model says biological, psychological and social factors interact?
    • Dopamine hypothesis
    • Biopsychosocial model
    • Medical model only
    • Multi-store model
    Show the answer

    Answer: Biopsychosocial model. The biopsychosocial model integrates all three levels.

  2. MZ twin concordance for a disorder is about 40%. What does this suggest?
    • Genes have no role
    • The disorder is fully genetic
    • Genes contribute but don't determine it
    • Twins share no genes
    Show the answer

    Answer: Genes contribute but don't determine it. Concordance below 100% shows environment matters too.

  3. Why is the dopamine hypothesis described as reductionist?
    • It includes too many factors
    • It ignores biology
    • It denies treatment works
    • It reduces it to one chemical
    Show the answer

    Answer: It reduces it to one chemical. Reducing schizophrenia to dopamine omits other systems and social factors.

  4. The dopamine hypothesis links which disorder to dopamine activity?
    • Schizophrenia
    • Dyslexia
    • Insomnia
    • Phobias
    Show the answer

    Answer: Schizophrenia. It proposes excess dopamine activity in some pathways underlies symptoms of schizophrenia.

Key terms

Diathesis–stress model
The idea that disorders result from vulnerability combined with stress
Dopamine hypothesis
The proposal that schizophrenia involves dopamine overactivity in some pathways
Biopsychosocial model
A model in which biological, psychological and social factors interact
