Decision Science

Kahneman's heuristics and biases and dual-process view contrast with Gigerenzer's ecological rationality; checklists, pre-mortems and natural frequencies improve decisions.

Psychology · Contemporary Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Compare dual-process and ecological rationality accounts of decision-making
  • Describe methods for improving decisions
  • Evaluate debates about human rationality

Two views of rationality

The heuristics and biases programme (Kahneman and Tversky) showed that people use mental shortcuts that can lead to systematic errors. Kahneman (2011) popularised a dual-process account: fast, intuitive System 1 and slow, deliberative System 2. Gigerenzer argued for ecological rationality: simple heuristics can be accurate when they fit the structure of the environment, so "less can be more".

Improving decisions

Methods include debiasing training, using checklists, considering the opposite, pre-mortems (imagining a plan has failed and asking why), and structured decision tools. Presenting risks as natural frequencies (for example, "8 out of 1,000") rather than percentages improves understanding of probabilities, including among doctors (Gigerenzer).

Worked example

Natural frequencies

  1. A test detects a condition in 90% of people who have it and gives false positives in 9% of people who don't; 1% of people have the condition.
  2. In natural frequencies: of 1,000 people, 10 have it and 9 test positive; of 990 without it, about 89 test positive.
  3. So only about 9 of 98 positive results are true positives, about 9%. This example is illustrative.

Evaluation

Both views capture part of the truth: heuristics can fail in some contexts and succeed in others. Dual-process models are criticised as oversimplified, since processes may not divide neatly into two systems. Practical tools such as checklists and natural frequencies show decision science can improve real decisions.

Common mistakeAssuming heuristics are always errors. They can be accurate when they fit the environment.

In 2 lines: Heuristics and biases versus ecological rationality. Checklists, pre-mortems and natural frequencies improve decisions.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Fast, intuitive thinking is described by Kahneman as…
    • System 2
    • Ecological rationality
    • System 1
    • A pre-mortem
    Show the answer

    Answer: System 1. Kahneman describes System 1 as fast and intuitive, and System 2 as slow and deliberate.

  2. A doctor misreads a 9% false-positive rate as meaning most positive tests are correct, but understands when the figures are given as counts. This supports…
    • System 1 thinking
    • Loss aversion
    • Framing effects only
    • Using natural frequencies
    Show the answer

    Answer: Using natural frequencies. Counts make base rates easier to understand.

  3. Why are dual-process models criticised?
    • Processes may not divide neatly into two systems
    • They explain everything perfectly
    • They ignore intuition
    • They reject deliberation
    Show the answer

    Answer: Processes may not divide neatly into two systems. Thinking may involve more than two systems.

Key terms

Dual-process theory
The view that thinking involves fast intuitive and slow deliberative systems
Ecological rationality
Heuristics being accurate when they fit the environment
Natural frequencies
Expressing probabilities as counts out of a whole number of cases
