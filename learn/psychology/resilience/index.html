Resilience

Resilience is common and arises from ordinary protective factors; it is both outcome and process; critics warn against using it to ignore harmful conditions.

Psychology · Motivation, Emotion and Wellbeing · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define resilience as an outcome and a process
  • Describe research on resilience after adversity and trauma
  • Evaluate factors that support resilience and critiques of the concept

Defining resilience

Resilience is positive adaptation despite significant adversity. It can be seen as an outcome (doing well after adversity) or a process (the dynamic interaction of a person with supportive relationships and resources). Masten (2001) described it as "ordinary magic", arising from common protective systems rather than rare traits.

Common after trauma

Bonanno (2004) argued that resilience is the most common response to potentially traumatic events such as bereavement or violence: many people show stable, healthy functioning, rather than prolonged distress. He distinguished trajectories: resilience, recovery (distress then return to normal), chronic distress and delayed reactions.

Worked example

Trajectories after loss

  1. Bonanno et al. (2002) followed older adults before and after the death of a spouse.
  2. The largest group (about 46%) showed low distress throughout: a resilient trajectory.
  3. Others showed chronic grief or improvement after a difficult marriage, showing varied responses.

Supporting resilience

Protective factors include supportive relationships, self-regulation, problem-solving skills, meaning and community resources. Resilience-building programmes in schools and workplaces aim to strengthen these. Critics argue an emphasis on individual resilience can shift responsibility away from addressing harmful conditions such as poverty or poor working environments, and that people who struggle shouldn't be seen as failing.

Common mistakeTreating resilience as a fixed personal trait. It depends on relationships, resources and context.

In 2 lines: Resilience is common, ordinary and both outcome and process. Bonanno identified varied trajectories; protective factors support resilience; critics warn against individual blame.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Who described resilience as "ordinary magic"?
    • George Bonanno
    • Ann Masten
    • Hans Selye
    • Richard Lazarus
    Show the answer

    Answer: Ann Masten. Masten argued resilience arises from common protective systems.

  2. After a loss, someone is very distressed for months and then returns to normal functioning. Bonanno would call this…
    • Resilience
    • Chronic distress
    • Recovery
    • Delayed reaction
    Show the answer

    Answer: Recovery. Distress followed by return to baseline is recovery.

  3. Why is resilience described as a process?
    • It is a fixed personal trait
    • It can never change
    • It ignores context entirely
    • It depends on interactions
    Show the answer

    Answer: It depends on interactions. Resilience emerges from ongoing interactions.

Key terms

Resilience
Positive adaptation despite significant adversity
Protective factor
A factor that reduces the impact of risk
Trajectory
A pattern of change in functioning over time
