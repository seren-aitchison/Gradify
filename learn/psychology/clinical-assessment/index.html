Clinical Assessment

Clinical assessment aims to understand difficulties, context, strengths and risks collaboratively; methods include interviews, questionnaires, observation, testing and information from others.

Psychology · Clinical Psychology and Interventions · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the aims of clinical assessment
  • Explain the main methods used in assessment
  • Evaluate principles of good assessment practice
Competence and safetyThis lesson is educational and is not a guide to diagnosis or therapy. Assessment should only be carried out by qualified, registered professionals.

Aims of assessment

Clinical assessment aims to understand the person's difficulties, their history and context, their strengths and goals, and any risks, in order to agree a plan. It may lead to a diagnosis, a formulation, or both. Assessment is collaborative: the person's own account and priorities are central, and assessment continues throughout care.

Methods

  • Clinical interviews, structured or unstructured.
  • Questionnaires and rating scales, such as the PHQ-9 for depressive symptoms and the GAD-7 for anxiety.
  • Observation of behaviour in clinic or everyday settings.
  • Psychometric tests of cognition, such as memory or attention.
  • Information from others, such as family or other professionals, with consent.
Worked example

Assessing risk

  1. Assessment includes asking about risks, such as self-harm or harm from others, in a sensitive and direct way.
  2. Asking about suicidal thoughts does not increase risk, and it allows support to be offered.
  3. Risk assessment informs a safety plan developed with the person.

Evaluation

Combining methods gives a fuller picture than any single source. But assessment can be affected by clinician bias, cultural misunderstanding and the person's trust in services. Good practice includes cultural humility, transparency about purpose and attention to strengths as well as difficulties.

Investigate with the lab. Sort the client's difficulties into the 5 Ps of a case formulation.

Common mistakeAssuming assessment is a one-off event. Understanding develops throughout care.

In 2 lines: Assessment explores difficulties, context, strengths and risks collaboratively. Interviews, questionnaires, observation, testing and others' information combine.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. A plan agreed with someone for staying safe in a crisis is a…
    • Formulation
    • Safety plan
    • Diagnosis
    • Questionnaire
    Show the answer

    Answer: Safety plan. Safety plans are developed collaboratively.

  2. A psychologist uses an interview, the PHQ-9 and a conversation with a GP (with consent). This shows…
    • Relying on one source
    • Diagnostic overshadowing
    • Combining assessment methods
    • Exposure therapy
    Show the answer

    Answer: Combining assessment methods. Combining several sources of information gives a fuller and more reliable picture.

  3. Why is cultural humility important in assessment?
    • Culture never affects any assessment
    • It replaces the need for questionnaires
    • It removes the need for interviews
    • Misreading culture can bias how difficulties are interpreted
    Show the answer

    Answer: Misreading culture can bias how difficulties are interpreted. Assessment must understand the person's context.

  4. In the 5 Ps model, what is a precipitating factor?
    • Something that triggered the current difficulty
    • Something that keeps the difficulty going
    • A strength that protects the client
    • A long-standing vulnerability
    Show the answer

    Answer: Something that triggered the current difficulty. Precipitating factors are recent triggers; perpetuating factors maintain problems.

Key terms

Clinical assessment
Gathering information to understand difficulties and plan help
Risk assessment
Exploring risks such as self-harm or harm from others
Safety plan
A plan agreed with a person for staying safe in a crisis
