Basal Ganglia and Cerebellum

The basal ganglia select and initiate movements; the cerebellum coordinates and refines them.

Psychology · Biological Psychology and Neuroscience · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the structure and functions of the basal ganglia
  • Describe the functions of the cerebellum
  • Explain Parkinson's and Huntington's diseases in terms of the basal ganglia

The basal ganglia

The basal ganglia are a group of structures deep in the brain, including the striatum (caudate and putamen), globus pallidus and substantia nigra. They help select and start wanted movements and suppress unwanted ones, and they are involved in habit learning and reward. Dopamine from the substantia nigra is crucial to their function.

Worked example

Two movement disorders

  1. In Parkinson's disease, dopamine neurones in the substantia nigra die. Movements become slow and hard to start, with tremor and stiffness.
  2. In Huntington's disease, an inherited condition, neurones in the striatum degenerate, producing involuntary jerky movements and cognitive changes.
  3. One disorder involves too little movement and the other too much, consistent with the basal ganglia's role in selecting movement.

The cerebellum

The cerebellum ("little brain") contains more than half the brain's neurones. It coordinates timing, balance and precision of movement, compares intended with actual movements and corrects errors, and supports motor learning such as riding a bike. Damage causes ataxia: clumsy, unsteady movement. Alcohol affects the cerebellum, which is why sobriety tests check balance and coordination.

The cerebellum also contributes to some cognitive functions, including timing, language and attention, as imaging and patient studies show.

Investigate with the lab. Select the basal ganglia and the cerebellum and compare their roles in movement.

Common mistakeThinking the cerebellum only matters for movement. It also supports aspects of cognition.

In 2 lines: The basal ganglia select movements and depend on dopamine; Parkinson's and Huntington's affect them. The cerebellum coordinates timing, balance and motor learning, and contributes to cognition.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Parkinson's disease involves loss of dopamine neurones in the…
    • Cerebellum
    • Hippocampus
    • Occipital lobe
    • Substantia nigra
    Show the answer

    Answer: Substantia nigra. Dopamine neurones in the substantia nigra degenerate.

  2. A person staggers and can't touch their nose accurately with eyes closed after an injury. Which structure is most likely damaged?
    • Cerebellum
    • Amygdala
    • Thalamus
    • Wernicke's area
    Show the answer

    Answer: Cerebellum. Poor coordination and balance suggest cerebellar damage.

  3. Why do Parkinson's and Huntington's diseases support the idea that the basal ganglia select movements?
    • Both cause blindness
    • Their symptoms are opposite
    • Neither involves the basal ganglia
    • Both affect only memory
    Show the answer

    Answer: Their symptoms are opposite. Opposite symptoms fit a role in selecting and suppressing movement.

  4. Which condition involves loss of dopamine neurons supplying the basal ganglia?
    • Down's syndrome
    • Schizophrenia
    • Parkinson's disease
    • Dyslexia
    Show the answer

    Answer: Parkinson's disease. Parkinson's disease is caused by loss of dopamine-producing neurons in the substantia nigra.

Key terms

Basal ganglia
Deep brain structures that help select and initiate movement
Substantia nigra
A midbrain region supplying dopamine to the striatum
Ataxia
Lack of coordination caused by cerebellar damage
