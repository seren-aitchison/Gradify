Attachment in Evolutionary Perspective

Attachment evolved to keep infants close to caregivers; life history approaches suggest attachment variation may calibrate children to their environments, though evidence is mixed.

Psychology · Evolutionary Psychology and Behavioural Ecology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain Bowlby's evolutionary basis for attachment
  • Explain how insecure attachment might be adaptive in some environments
  • Evaluate evolutionary approaches to attachment

An evolved system

Bowlby (1969) argued attachment evolved because infants who stayed close to caregivers were more likely to survive predators and other dangers in the environment of evolutionary adaptedness. Social releasers such as crying and smiling elicit care, and the attachment system activates when the infant is frightened, tired or separated.

Adaptive variation

Classic attachment research treated secure attachment as optimal and insecure attachment as a failure. Evolutionary life history approaches (Belsky, Steinberg and Draper, 1991; Simpson and Belsky, 2008) suggest attachment patterns may calibrate children to their likely environment. In harsh, unpredictable environments, insecure strategies such as earlier independence or heightened vigilance might have been adaptive in ancestral conditions.

Worked example

Early adversity and development

  1. Belsky, Steinberg and Draper (1991) proposed that early family stress and insecure attachment predict earlier puberty and earlier sexual activity in girls.
  2. Longitudinal studies found some support: harsher early environments were associated with earlier menarche (Belsky et al., 2007).
  3. The theory reframes these outcomes as calibrated strategies, while recognising they can carry costs.

Evaluation

Evolutionary approaches explain why attachment is universal and why attachment patterns vary. Critics argue "adaptive" explanations for insecurity risk normalising harmful environments; links between attachment and life history outcomes are often modest; and cultural variation in caregiving is large. Practitioners stress that "adaptive" in evolutionary terms doesn't mean good for well-being.

Common mistakeReading "adaptive" as "desirable". Evolutionary adaptiveness concerns reproductive outcomes, not well-being.

In 2 lines: Bowlby saw attachment as an evolved protective system. Life history theory reframes attachment variation as calibration; evidence is mixed and "adaptive" isn't "good".

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Bowlby's term for crying and smiling that elicit care is…
    • Display rules
    • Exaptations
    • Social releasers
    • Mismatch
    Show the answer

    Answer: Social releasers. Bowlby called these social releasers because they trigger caregiving.

  2. Girls from stressful early environments reach puberty earlier. Belsky would interpret this as…
    • A purely random effect
    • Kin selection at work
    • Sexual selection only
    • A calibrated strategy
    Show the answer

    Answer: A calibrated strategy. Development adjusts to cues about the environment.

  3. Why are some practitioners cautious about adaptive accounts of insecure attachment?
    • They may normalise harmful environments
    • They deny attachment exists
    • They reject evolution
    • They focus only on adults
    Show the answer

    Answer: They may normalise harmful environments. Calling insecurity adaptive could obscure harm.

Key terms

Environment of evolutionary adaptedness
The ancestral environment in which adaptations evolved
Life history strategy
A pattern of allocating effort to growth, reproduction and parenting
Calibration
Adjusting development to cues about the likely environment
