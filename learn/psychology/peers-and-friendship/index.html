Peers and Friendship

Friendship shifts from play to trust to intimacy; peer acceptance matters for well-being, and peers and parents both shape development.

Psychology · Developmental Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe how children's understanding of friendship changes with age
  • Explain sociometric status and the effects of peer rejection
  • Evaluate the claim that peers matter more than parents in shaping development

What friends mean

Young children see friends as playmates who are nearby and share toys. By middle childhood, friendship involves trust and mutual help. In adolescence, friendship centres on intimacy, loyalty and sharing thoughts and feelings. Friends provide companionship, support and a context to practise social skills and resolve conflicts.

Acceptance and rejection

Sociometric methods ask children whom they like most and least. Coie, Dodge and Coppotelli (1982) classified children as popular, rejected, neglected, controversial or average. Rejected children, especially those who are aggressive, are at higher risk of later difficulties such as academic problems and loneliness. Being bullied is associated with anxiety, depression and lower well-being, sometimes into adulthood (Arseneault, 2018).

Worked example

Peers or parents?

  1. Harris (1995) proposed group socialisation theory: children's personalities are shaped more by their peer groups than by their parents.
  2. She noted that children of immigrants usually adopt the accent of their peers, not their parents.
  3. Critics argue parents shape which peers children meet and that parent and peer influences interact.

Peer influence in adolescence

Peer influence peaks in early to mid-adolescence. It can encourage risk-taking, but also prosocial behaviour, academic motivation and healthy habits. Children tend to choose friends who are similar to them (selection) and then become more similar through influence; both processes contribute.

Common mistakeAssuming peer influence is always negative. Peers also encourage positive behaviour and support.

In 2 lines: Friendship changes from play to trust to intimacy with age. Rejection and bullying carry risks; peers and parents both shape development.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which sociometric group is liked by few and disliked by many?
    • Rejected
    • Popular
    • Neglected
    • Average
    Show the answer

    Answer: Rejected. Rejected children receive many dislike nominations.

  2. Children of immigrant parents speak with their classmates' accent, not their parents'. Which theory uses this as evidence?
    • Attachment theory
    • Group socialisation theory
    • Social exchange theory
    • Dual-systems model
    Show the answer

    Answer: Group socialisation theory. Harris argued peers shape such characteristics.

  3. What is the main criticism of group socialisation theory?
    • Peers have no effect at all
    • Parents have no influence
    • Parents shape peer choices
    • It ignores children's accents
    Show the answer

    Answer: Parents shape peer choices. Parental choices of neighbourhood and school shape peer groups.

Key terms

Sociometric status
A child's standing in a peer group based on peer ratings
Group socialisation theory
Harris's theory that peers shape personality more than parents do
Selection and influence
Choosing similar friends, then becoming more similar to them
