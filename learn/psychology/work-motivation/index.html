Work Motivation

Herzberg separates motivators from hygiene factors; equity theory highlights fairness; the job characteristics model shows how job design motivates.

Psychology · Applied Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain Herzberg's two-factor theory
  • Explain equity theory and the job characteristics model
  • Evaluate the evidence for theories of work motivation

Herzberg's two factors

Herzberg (1959) interviewed engineers and accountants about times they felt good or bad at work. He proposed motivators (achievement, recognition, responsibility, the work itself) that increase satisfaction, and hygiene factors (pay, conditions, supervision, company policy) that prevent dissatisfaction but don't motivate. Critics note his method relied on people's recollections, which may be biased: people credit themselves for good times and blame circumstances for bad ones.

Equity theory

Adams (1963) proposed equity theory: people compare their inputs (effort, skills) and outputs (pay, recognition) with those of others. If they perceive inequity, they may reduce effort, ask for more or leave. Research supports the importance of perceived fairness, now studied as organisational justice: distributive (fair outcomes), procedural (fair processes) and interactional (respectful treatment).

Worked example

Designing motivating jobs

  1. Hackman and Oldham's (1976) job characteristics model identified five core job characteristics: skill variety, task identity, task significance, autonomy and feedback.
  2. These create critical psychological states, such as experienced meaningfulness, which lead to motivation and satisfaction.
  3. A meta-analysis found job characteristics were linked to satisfaction and motivation (Humphrey, Nahrgang and Morgeson, 2007).

Evaluation

Theories of work motivation give practical guidance on job design, fairness and recognition. But individual differences, culture and economic pressures (such as insecure work) also shape motivation, and some theories rely on self-report.

Common mistakeAssuming pay is the only motivator. Fairness, autonomy, meaningful work and recognition also matter.

In 2 lines: Herzberg separated motivators and hygiene factors; equity theory stresses fairness. Job characteristics such as autonomy and feedback motivate; context and individual differences also matter.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. In Herzberg's theory, recognition and responsibility are…
    • Motivators
    • Hygiene factors
    • Inputs
    • Outputs
    Show the answer

    Answer: Motivators. Herzberg classed these as motivators that increase satisfaction.

  2. An employee reduces effort after learning a colleague doing the same work earns more. This fits…
    • The job characteristics model
    • Equity theory
    • Scientific management
    • Hygiene theory only
    Show the answer

    Answer: Equity theory. Perceived unfairness in input-output comparisons reduces effort.

  3. What is the main methodological criticism of Herzberg's research?
    • It used too many people
    • It measured pay only
    • It relied on biased recollections
    • It was an experiment
    Show the answer

    Answer: It relied on biased recollections. People may credit themselves for good events and blame circumstances for bad.

Key terms

Hygiene factors
Features such as pay and conditions that prevent dissatisfaction but don't motivate
Equity theory
The theory that people compare their input-output ratio with others'
Job characteristics model
Hackman and Oldham's model of five core job features that motivate
