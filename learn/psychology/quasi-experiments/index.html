Quasi-experiments

Quasi-experiments compare pre-existing groups under controlled conditions; they can show differences but can't isolate the cause, because people can't be randomly allocated.

Psychology · Research Methods · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define a quasi-experiment
  • Distinguish quasi-experiments from true and natural experiments
  • Evaluate the causal conclusions quasi-experiments can support

Groups that already differ

In a quasi-experiment, the IV is a characteristic participants already have: age group, sex, having a diagnosis of depression, being a smoker. The researcher measures the DV under controlled conditions but can't randomly allocate people to the IV.

How it differs

  • True experiment: IV manipulated, random allocation.
  • Natural experiment: IV is an event or change that occurs naturally (television arriving).
  • Quasi-experiment: IV is an existing difference between people.
Worked example

Comparing memory in older and younger adults

  1. IV: age group (20–30 versus 65–75).
  2. DV: recall of a word list under identical lab conditions.
  3. Older adults recall fewer words, but they also differ in education history, health and familiarity with computers, any of which might explain the difference.

Quasi-experiments are still valuable: they show whether and how groups differ, and suggest hypotheses that other designs can test.

Common mistakeConcluding that the grouping variable caused the difference. Other variables that come with it may be responsible.

In 2 lines: Quasi-experiments use existing characteristics as the IV. They control testing conditions but can't establish that the characteristic itself caused the effect.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Comparing anxiety in people with and without a diagnosis of insomnia is a…
    • True experiment
    • Quasi-experiment
    • Field experiment
    • Natural experiment
    Show the answer

    Answer: Quasi-experiment. Diagnosis is a pre-existing characteristic, not manipulated or naturally changing during the study.

  2. Women outperform men on a verbal task in a controlled study. What is the strongest conclusion?
    • Sex causes verbal ability
    • Verbal ability causes sex differences
    • There is a difference, but its cause isn't established
    • The task must be biased
    Show the answer

    Answer: There is a difference, but its cause isn't established. A quasi-experiment shows a difference; socialisation, education and other factors may explain it.

  3. Which addition would most strengthen a quasi-experiment comparing older and younger adults' memory?
    • Testing the groups in different rooms
    • Allowing older adults more time only
    • Removing the comparison group
    • Measuring and controlling likely confounds
    Show the answer

    Answer: Measuring and controlling likely confounds. Measuring and statistically controlling likely confounds reduces alternative explanations, though it can't remove them all.

Key terms

Quasi-experiment
A study comparing groups that differ on a pre-existing characteristic not assigned by the researcher
Random allocation
Assigning participants to conditions by chance, which quasi-experiments can't do
