Non-parametric Tests

Non-parametric tests use ranks and suit ordinal, skewed or outlier-prone data; each has a parametric equivalent.

Psychology · Statistics · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Match non-parametric tests to their parametric equivalents
  • Explain when non-parametric tests are appropriate
  • Evaluate the trade-offs between parametric and non-parametric tests

Tests on ranks

  • Mann–Whitney U: two independent groups (equivalent to the independent t-test).
  • Wilcoxon signed-rank: two related conditions (equivalent to the paired t-test).
  • Kruskal–Wallis: three or more independent groups (equivalent to one-way ANOVA).
  • Friedman: three or more related conditions (equivalent to repeated measures ANOVA).
  • Spearman's rho: correlation (equivalent to Pearson's r).

These tests replace scores with ranks, so they suit ordinal data and data with outliers or heavy skew, and don't assume normality.

Worked example

Choosing a test for pain ratings

  1. Pain is rated 0–10 by two independent groups after different treatments; the ratings are ordinal and skewed.
  2. Mann–Whitney U compares the groups' rank distributions.
  3. Report U, z or exact p, the medians and an effect size such as r = z ÷ √N.

Trade-offs

When parametric assumptions hold, parametric tests are a little more powerful. When they don't, non-parametric tests can be more powerful and more robust. With large samples, the t-test is often robust to non-normality, so the choice depends on the level of measurement, sample size and outliers more than on normality alone.

Common mistakeThinking non-parametric tests compare medians exactly. Mann–Whitney tests whether values in one group tend to be larger, which only equals a median comparison under extra assumptions.

In 2 lines: Non-parametric tests use ranks and match parametric tests one to one. Use them for ordinal, skewed or outlier-prone data; parametric tests are slightly more powerful when assumptions hold.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. The non-parametric equivalent of one-way independent ANOVA is…
    • Friedman
    • Wilcoxon
    • Kruskal–Wallis
    • Mann–Whitney
    Show the answer

    Answer: Kruskal–Wallis. Kruskal–Wallis compares three or more independent groups on ranks.

  2. Twelve patients rate fatigue (1–7) before and after treatment. Which test fits best?
    • Mann–Whitney U
    • Independent t-test
    • Chi-square
    • Wilcoxon signed-rank
    Show the answer

    Answer: Wilcoxon signed-rank. Related ordinal ratings suit the Wilcoxon test.

  3. With 200 participants per group and mildly skewed data, why might a t-test still be acceptable?
    • The sampling distribution of the mean is close to normal
    • Non-parametric tests can't handle large samples
    • Skew disappears with large samples
    • Ranks can't be calculated for 200 people
    Show the answer

    Answer: The sampling distribution of the mean is close to normal. The central limit theorem makes the t-test robust with large samples.

Key terms

Non-parametric test
A test that makes few assumptions about the population distribution, often using ranks
Mann–Whitney U
A non-parametric test comparing two independent groups
Wilcoxon signed-rank test
A non-parametric test comparing two related conditions
