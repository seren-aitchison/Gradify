Natural Selection

Natural selection requires variation, inheritance and differential reproduction; fitness is reproductive success; behaviour can evolve if heritable and fitness-relevant.

Psychology · Evolutionary Psychology and Behavioural Ecology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain the conditions for natural selection
  • Describe the modern synthesis and fitness
  • Apply natural selection to behaviour, with examples

Darwin's theory

Darwin (1859) argued that natural selection follows from three conditions: variation (individuals differ), inheritance (some differences are passed to offspring) and differential reproduction (some variants survive and reproduce more than others). Over many generations, beneficial variants become more common. Wallace independently developed the same idea.

Genes and fitness

The modern synthesis (1930s–1940s) combined Darwin's theory with Mendelian genetics. Fitness means reproductive success: how many copies of one's genes are passed on, not physical strength. Selection acts on phenotypes (observable traits), changing the frequency of genes in a population.

Worked example

The peppered moth

  1. In industrial England, soot darkened tree bark.
  2. Dark (melanic) peppered moths became more common because birds ate pale moths more easily on dark bark.
  3. After clean air laws reduced pollution, pale moths became common again, a classic observed example of natural selection (Cook et al., 2012).

Selection and behaviour

Behaviour can be shaped by selection if it varies, is partly heritable and affects reproduction. Selection doesn't create perfect organisms; it works on existing variation, involves trade-offs and lags behind environmental change. It also doesn't plan ahead or aim at "progress".

Common mistakeThinking "survival of the fittest" means the strongest win. Fitness is about reproductive success.

In 2 lines: Selection needs variation, inheritance and differential reproduction. Fitness means reproductive success; the peppered moth illustrates selection; selection isn't perfect or planned.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Who published On the Origin of Species in 1859?
    • Charles Darwin
    • Gregor Mendel
    • Alfred Russel Wallace
    • Richard Dawkins
    Show the answer

    Answer: Charles Darwin. Charles Darwin published On the Origin of Species in 1859.

  2. Dark moths became more common when tree bark was darkened by soot. This shows…
    • Sexual selection
    • Natural selection
    • Genetic drift only
    • Cultural evolution
    Show the answer

    Answer: Natural selection. Birds ate more pale moths on dark bark, so dark moths survived and reproduced more.

  3. Why doesn't natural selection produce perfect organisms?
    • It aims for perfection
    • Genes never vary at all
    • Variation and trade-offs limit it
    • Environments never change
    Show the answer

    Answer: Variation and trade-offs limit it. Selection is constrained by variation, trade-offs and time lags.

Key terms

Natural selection
The process by which heritable traits that improve reproduction become more common
Fitness
Reproductive success, measured in gene copies passed on
Phenotype
The observable characteristics of an organism
