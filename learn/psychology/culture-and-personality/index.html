Culture and Personality

The Big Five structure replicates in many cultures (McCrae and Terracciano); emic research finds culture-specific factors such as interpersonal relatedness; national character stereotypes are often inaccurate.

Psychology · Cultural and Cross-Cultural Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe evidence for the Big Five across cultures
  • Explain culture-specific personality dimensions
  • Evaluate whether personality structure is universal

The Big Five across cultures

The Big Five (openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness and neuroticism) has been replicated in translated questionnaires in many countries. McCrae and Terracciano (2005) analysed observer ratings from about 50 cultures and found the five-factor structure was broadly replicated, supporting the view that it is a universal feature of personality.

Culture-specific dimensions

Researchers using emic methods have found additional dimensions. Cheung et al. (1996) developed the Chinese Personality Assessment Inventory and identified an interpersonal relatedness factor, including harmony and face, not well captured by the Big Five. In some non-literate or rural samples, the Big Five structure replicates less clearly, possibly because of translation and questionnaire unfamiliarity.

Worked example

National character stereotypes

  1. Terracciano et al. (2005) compared people's beliefs about the typical personality of their national group with average measured personality.
  2. National character stereotypes generally did not match aggregate personality scores.
  3. This suggests stereotypes about national personality are often inaccurate.

Evaluation

The Big Five's replication across many cultures supports some universality, but questionnaire methods may impose a Western framework. Emic research shows important culture-specific traits. Personality expression also depends on context: behaviour seen as assertive in one culture may be seen as rude in another.

Common mistakeAssuming national stereotypes describe real personality averages. Evidence suggests they often don't.

In 2 lines: The Big Five replicates widely across cultures. Emic research adds culture-specific factors; national stereotypes are often inaccurate.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness and neuroticism are the…
    • Basic emotions
    • Acculturation strategies
    • Big Five
    • Hofstede dimensions
    Show the answer

    Answer: Big Five. These five broad dimensions make up the Big Five model of personality traits.

  2. Researchers build a personality test from local concepts and find a factor about maintaining harmony. This illustrates…
    • An imposed etic from the West
    • Back-translation of a Western test
    • Ethnocentrism in test design
    • An emic approach finding culture-specific traits
    Show the answer

    Answer: An emic approach finding culture-specific traits. Local concepts revealed interpersonal relatedness.

  3. Why might questionnaire research overstate the universality of the Big Five?
    • Translated Western questionnaires may impose a framework
    • Questionnaires are fully culture-free
    • Personality never varies between people
    • All cultures use identical trait words
    Show the answer

    Answer: Translated Western questionnaires may impose a framework. Imposed measures can find the structure they assume.

Key terms

Big Five
Five broad personality traits: openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness and neuroticism
Interpersonal relatedness
A Chinese personality factor including harmony and face
National character stereotype
A belief about the typical personality of a nation
