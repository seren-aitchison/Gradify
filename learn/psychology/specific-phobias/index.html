Specific Phobias

Specific phobias involve excessive fear and avoidance; fears are learned through conditioning, observation and information and maintained by avoidance; evolved preparedness explains common phobias.

Psychology · Psychopathology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the features of specific phobias
  • Explain behavioural and evolutionary explanations
  • Evaluate explanations of phobias
Competence and safetyThis lesson is educational and is not a guide to diagnosis or treatment. Anyone concerned about a phobia should speak to a GP or another qualified professional.

Features

A specific phobia involves marked fear or anxiety about a particular object or situation, such as animals, heights, injections or flying. The fear is out of proportion to the actual danger, lasts for months, and leads to avoidance or intense distress, interfering with daily life. Adults often recognise their fear is excessive.

Explanations

Behavioural explanations use Mowrer's two-process model: fear is learned through classical conditioning and maintained by avoidance. Fears can also be learned through observation (seeing others' fear) and information (being told something is dangerous). Öhman and Mineka (2001) proposed an evolved fear module: humans are prepared to learn fears of things that threatened our ancestors, such as snakes and spiders, more easily than of modern dangers like cars.

Worked example

Pathways to fear

  1. A child may develop a fear of dogs after being bitten (direct conditioning).
  2. Another may develop it after seeing a parent scream at a dog (observational learning).
  3. A third may develop it after repeatedly being told dogs are dangerous (information transmission). This example is illustrative.

Evaluation

Learning explanations are supported by the success of exposure therapy, which works by gradually confronting feared situations. Preparedness explains why phobias cluster around certain objects. But many people with phobias recall no learning event, and genetic factors contribute to anxiety sensitivity. Combined models fit the evidence best.

Common mistakeAssuming phobias are just strong dislikes. They involve intense fear and avoidance that interfere with life.

In 2 lines: Specific phobias involve excessive fear and avoidance. Conditioning, observation and information; preparedness explains common phobias.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Learning to fear something by watching another person's fear is…
    • Classical conditioning
    • Negative reinforcement
    • Observational learning
    • Preparedness
    Show the answer

    Answer: Observational learning. Watching others' reactions is observational learning.

  2. Spider phobias are common, but phobias of electric sockets are rare, despite sockets being dangerous. This supports…
    • Moral treatment
    • Learned helplessness
    • Conditions of worth
    • Preparedness
    Show the answer

    Answer: Preparedness. Ancestral threats are learned more easily.

  3. What does the success of exposure therapy suggest about phobias?
    • Fear learning can be reduced through new learning
    • Phobias are purely genetic in origin
    • Avoidance is the best cure for phobias
    • Fear can never change once learned
    Show the answer

    Answer: Fear learning can be reduced through new learning. Gradual exposure allows extinction of fear.

Key terms

Specific phobia
An intense, persistent fear of a particular object or situation
Observational learning
Learning by watching others
Preparedness
An evolved readiness to learn certain fears easily
