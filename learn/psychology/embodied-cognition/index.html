Embodied Cognition

Embodied cognition grounds thinking in bodily experience (Barsalou); neuroimaging supports sensorimotor involvement; some behavioural effects, such as the pen facial feedback study, failed to replicate.

Psychology · Contemporary Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain the claims of embodied and grounded cognition
  • Describe evidence for and against embodiment effects
  • Evaluate the role of replication in testing embodiment claims

Grounded cognition

Traditional cognitive psychology treated the mind as manipulating abstract symbols, like a computer. Embodied or grounded cognition (Barsalou, 2008) argues that concepts are grounded in sensory and motor experience: understanding the word "kick" partly reactivates motor systems involved in kicking. Some versions go further, arguing cognition extends into the body and environment.

Evidence and replication

Evidence includes neuroimaging showing motor areas active when reading action words. Some striking behavioural findings have not replicated: a large registered replication of Strack et al.'s (1988) facial feedback pen study, in which holding a pen in the teeth was said to make cartoons seem funnier, found no effect (Wagenmakers et al., 2016). The later Many Smiles Collaboration (2022) found some support for facial feedback using other methods, but not robustly for the pen technique.

Worked example

A mixed picture

  1. Motor and sensory system involvement in understanding language is well supported by neuroimaging.
  2. Some behavioural "priming" effects linked to embodiment, such as warmth or posture effects, have failed to replicate.
  3. This illustrates the importance of distinguishing robust core claims from fragile individual findings.

Evaluation

Embodied cognition has challenged purely abstract models of the mind and influenced robotics and education. But some claims are vague, and several high-profile findings have failed to replicate. Researchers now emphasise preregistered, large-sample studies to test specific predictions.

Common mistakeAssuming every embodiment finding is robust. Some have failed large replications.

In 2 lines: Embodied cognition grounds concepts in sensorimotor experience. Neuroimaging supports it; some behavioural effects failed to replicate.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. The idea that facial expressions influence how we feel is the…
    • Facial feedback hypothesis
    • Free-energy principle
    • Prospect theory
    • Dual-process theory
    Show the answer

    Answer: Facial feedback hypothesis. Expressions feeding back into emotion is facial feedback.

  2. Reading the word "grasp" activates parts of the brain involved in hand movement. This supports…
    • Abstract symbol processing only
    • Grounded cognition
    • Reverse inference
    • Loss aversion
    Show the answer

    Answer: Grounded cognition. Motor activation during reading supports grounding.

  3. Why is replication important for embodiment research?
    • Replication always confirms findings
    • Embodiment has never been tested
    • Some striking findings have failed large replications
    • Small studies are most reliable
    Show the answer

    Answer: Some striking findings have failed large replications. Distinguishing robust and fragile claims needs replication.

Key terms

Embodied cognition
The view that thinking depends on the body and its interaction with the world
Facial feedback hypothesis
The idea that facial expressions influence emotional experience
Registered replication
A preregistered, multi-lab repeat of an earlier study
