Culture and Diagnosis

The category fallacy warns against exporting diagnoses; DSM-5 moved from culture-bound syndromes to cultural concepts of distress; ethnic differences in psychosis diagnosis in England reflect debated causes.

Psychology · Cultural and Cross-Cultural Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain the category fallacy and culture-bound syndromes
  • Describe evidence of ethnic differences in diagnosis in the UK
  • Evaluate attempts to make diagnosis culturally sensitive
Competence and safetyThis lesson is educational and is not a guide to diagnosis or treatment. Diagnosis should only be made by qualified clinicians.

The category fallacy

Kleinman described the category fallacy: applying diagnostic categories developed in one culture to another where they may lack meaning. The DSM and ICD were developed mainly in Western settings. Earlier editions listed culture-bound syndromes, conditions said to occur only in particular cultures, such as koro. DSM-5 replaced this with cultural concepts of distress, recognising that all diagnoses, including Western ones, are shaped by culture.

Ethnic differences in diagnosis

In England, Black Caribbean and Black African people have been diagnosed with psychotic disorders at much higher rates than White British people. The AESOP study (Fearon et al., 2006) found higher incidence of schizophrenia and other psychoses in these groups in three English cities. Explanations debated include social adversity, discrimination and migration stress, as well as possible diagnostic bias and differences in pathways into care.

Worked example

The Cultural Formulation Interview

  1. DSM-5 includes a Cultural Formulation Interview to help clinicians explore a person's cultural identity, explanatory models, stressors and supports.
  2. It encourages clinicians to understand the person's own account of their difficulties.
  3. Its use in routine practice varies.

Evaluation

Cultural research has made diagnostic systems more aware of culture, but critics argue categories remain rooted in Western concepts. Higher diagnosis rates in some minority groups may reflect both real differences in risk linked to social adversity and bias in services. Disentangling these requires careful research.

Common mistakeAssuming ethnic differences in diagnosis prove differences in illness. Bias and pathways into care may also play a role.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. DSM-5 replaced culture-bound syndromes with…
    • Imposed etic categories
    • Universal display rules
    • Big Five personality traits
    • Cultural concepts of distress
    Show the answer

    Answer: Cultural concepts of distress. DSM-5 recognised culture shapes all diagnoses.

  2. A clinician asks a patient how they understand their difficulties and what their community thinks causes them. This uses…
    • A cultural formulation
    • The category fallacy
    • An imposed etic
    • Somatisation
    Show the answer

    Answer: A cultural formulation. The Cultural Formulation Interview explores the person's own account.

  3. Why is it hard to interpret ethnic differences in diagnosis?
    • Diagnosis is always completely unbiased
    • Real risk differences and service bias may both matter
    • Risk never differs between any groups
    • Ethnicity data are never collected
    Show the answer

    Answer: Real risk differences and service bias may both matter. Several explanations can operate together.

Key terms

Culture-bound syndrome

Cultural formulation
