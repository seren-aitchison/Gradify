Motivation and Individual Differences

Motivation varies from intrinsic to extrinsic; SDT stresses autonomy, competence and relatedness, and expected tangible rewards can undermine interest.

Psychology · Personality and Individual Differences · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Distinguish intrinsic and extrinsic motivation
  • Explain self-determination theory and its three basic needs
  • Apply motivation research to education and work

Why do we act?

Intrinsic motivation is doing something for its own sake, because it is interesting or enjoyable. Extrinsic motivation is doing something for an outcome separate from the activity, such as pay, grades or approval. People differ in their typical balance of the two, and the same person may be intrinsically motivated for some activities and extrinsically for others.

Self-determination theory

Deci and Ryan's self-determination theory (SDT; Ryan and Deci, 2000) proposes three basic psychological needs: autonomy (feeling one's actions are self-chosen), competence (feeling effective) and relatedness (feeling connected to others). Environments that support these needs foster intrinsic and well-internalised motivation, well-being and persistence. SDT describes a continuum from external regulation to more autonomous forms in which people value the activity.

Worked example

Rewards and intrinsic motivation

  1. Deci, Koestner and Ryan (1999) meta-analysed 128 experiments on rewards.
  2. Tangible rewards expected for doing an interesting task reduced later free-choice engagement with the task (the undermining effect).
  3. Verbal praise tended to increase intrinsic motivation, especially when it conveyed competence.

Individual differences

People differ in causality orientations: some are autonomy-oriented (seeking interest and choice), others control-oriented (focused on rewards and pressures). Personality traits such as openness and conscientiousness also relate to motivation. Need for cognition, the enjoyment of effortful thinking, predicts deeper engagement with arguments and learning.

Common mistakeAssuming rewards always increase motivation. Expected tangible rewards can reduce interest in tasks people already enjoy.

In 2 lines: Intrinsic and extrinsic motivation differ in source; people vary in their balance. SDT's three needs support autonomous motivation; rewards can undermine interest.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Reading a novel purely because you enjoy it shows…
    • Extrinsic motivation
    • External regulation
    • Intrinsic motivation
    • The undermining effect
    Show the answer

    Answer: Intrinsic motivation. Doing something for its own sake is intrinsic.

  2. Children who enjoy drawing draw less after being paid for drawings. This illustrates…
    • Positive reinforcement only
    • The halo effect
    • Social loafing
    • The undermining effect
    Show the answer

    Answer: The undermining effect. Expected rewards reduced later free-choice drawing.

  3. Why might giving pupils choice improve motivation, according to SDT?
    • It supports autonomy
    • It removes competence
    • It reduces relatedness
    • It adds pressure
    Show the answer

    Answer: It supports autonomy. Giving choice satisfies the need for autonomy, supporting self-determined motivation.
