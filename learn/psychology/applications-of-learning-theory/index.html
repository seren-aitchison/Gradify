Applications of Learning Theory

Learning theory underpins exposure-based therapies, parenting programmes, education and technology design; its ethical use depends on consent and whose goals it serves.

Psychology · Learning and Behaviour · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe applications of classical, operant and social learning theory
  • Explain how exposure therapy applies extinction
  • Evaluate the ethics and effectiveness of applying learning theory, including in technology design

Therapy

  • Systematic desensitisation (Wolpe, 1958): pairing relaxation with a graded hierarchy of feared situations.
  • Exposure therapy: confronting feared stimuli without escape so fear extinguishes; a core part of CBT for phobias, OCD and PTSD.
  • Aversion therapy: pairing an unwanted behaviour with an unpleasant stimulus. It has been used for alcohol dependence, and historically, and harmfully, to try to change sexual orientation, a practice now condemned by professional bodies.

Education and parenting

Praise and feedback (operant reinforcement), modelling of skills and behaviours by teachers, classroom token systems and clear routines all apply learning principles. Parenting programmes based on social learning theory, which teach reinforcement of positive behaviour and consistent, non-harsh discipline, have good evidence for reducing children's conduct problems.

Technology

Variable rewards in apps and games, streaks, badges and notifications apply operant principles to keep users engaged. This can support learning and healthy habits, as in this course's XP and streaks, but can also encourage compulsive use. Debates about "persuasive" or "addictive" design concern whether techniques serve users' goals or exploit them.

Investigate with the lab. Read the ABAB graph by level, trend and variability, and decide whether the intervention worked.

Worked example

Applying exposure therapy to a fear of dogs

  1. Build a hierarchy: pictures of dogs, a dog across the street, a calm dog on a lead, stroking a dog.
  2. Work up the hierarchy, staying in each situation until anxiety falls, without escaping.
  3. Fear extinguishes; practising in varied places and returning for booster sessions reduces relapse.
Common mistakeTreating these therapy descriptions as self-help instructions. Exposure therapy for clinical problems should be guided by a qualified practitioner.

In 2 lines: Learning theory is applied in therapy, education, parenting and technology. Exposure therapies and parenting programmes have strong evidence; aversion therapy and persuasive design raise ethical concerns.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Who developed systematic desensitisation?
    • B. F. Skinner
    • Albert Bandura
    • John Watson
    • Joseph Wolpe
    Show the answer

    Answer: Joseph Wolpe. Wolpe described systematic desensitisation in 1958, building on relaxation and hierarchies.

  2. A therapist helps a client with a fear of lifts ride one floor, then two, then ten, staying until anxiety falls each time. This applies…
    • Graded exposure and extinction
    • Aversion therapy
    • Vicarious punishment
    • Insight learning
    Show the answer

    Answer: Graded exposure and extinction. Gradually confronting the feared situation without escape allows fear to extinguish.

  3. Which is the strongest ethical concern about engagement techniques in apps?
    • They use principles from learning theory
    • They can foster compulsive use that serves companies
    • They can support learning and habits
    • They give feedback to users
    Show the answer

    Answer: They can foster compulsive use that serves companies. The concern is whose goals are served and whether users can meaningfully consent.

  4. Why is an ABAB design stronger than a simple AB design?
    • It uses a much larger sample of participants
    • It needs no baseline phase
    • Reversing and reapplying the intervention shows the change follows it
    • It removes all individual differences
    Show the answer

    Answer: Reversing and reapplying the intervention shows the change follows it. Behaviour changing each time the intervention starts and stops suggests a causal effect.

Key terms

Systematic desensitisation
Gradually pairing relaxation with feared situations in a hierarchy (Wolpe)
Exposure therapy
Confronting feared stimuli without escape so that fear extinguishes
Aversion therapy
Pairing an unwanted behaviour with an unpleasant stimulus to reduce it
