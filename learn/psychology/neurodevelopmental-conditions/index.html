Neurodevelopmental Conditions

Autism, ADHD and dyslexia are common, highly heritable neurodevelopmental conditions; vaccines and parenting don't cause them; medical and neurodiversity views both inform support.

Psychology · Developmental Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the main features of autism, ADHD and dyslexia
  • Explain genetic and environmental contributions to neurodevelopmental conditions
  • Evaluate the medical and neurodiversity perspectives

Not a diagnosis

This lesson describes research for learning purposes. It can't be used to identify a condition in anyone. Assessment is carried out by qualified professionals such as paediatricians, psychiatrists, clinical or educational psychologists and speech and language therapists. If you have concerns, a GP or school special educational needs coordinator (SENCo) is a good starting point.

Main conditions

  • Autism: differences in social communication and interaction, with restricted or repetitive behaviours, interests or sensory sensitivities. A systematic review estimated a median global prevalence of about 1 in 100 children (Zeidan et al., 2022).
  • ADHD: persistent patterns of inattention and/or hyperactivity–impulsivity that affect daily life. A meta-analysis estimated worldwide prevalence in children at about 5% (Polanczyk et al., 2007).
  • Dyslexia: persistent difficulty with accurate or fluent word reading and spelling, often linked to phonological processing, not to general intelligence.
  • Others include developmental language disorder, developmental coordination disorder and intellectual disability. Conditions often co-occur.

Causes

Twin studies show strong genetic influence: heritability of around 80% for autism (Bai et al., 2019) and about 70–80% for ADHD (Faraone and Larsson, 2019), involving many genes. Prenatal factors such as preterm birth also contribute. Large studies have found no link between vaccines and autism (Taylor, Swerdfeger and Eslick, 2014). Parenting style doesn't cause autism or ADHD, though support affects outcomes.

Worked example

Two perspectives

  1. The medical model describes conditions as disorders defined by criteria in manuals such as DSM-5 and ICD-11, enabling diagnosis and access to support.
  2. The neurodiversity perspective sees autism and ADHD as natural variations in human brains, with strengths as well as difficulties, and stresses changing environments rather than people.
  3. Many people and services combine both: recognising real difficulties while respecting difference and adapting environments.
Common mistakeRepeating the myth that vaccines cause autism. Large studies have found no link.

In 2 lines: Autism, ADHD and dyslexia affect communication, attention and reading; they often co-occur. They are highly heritable, not caused by vaccines or parenting; medical and neurodiversity views inform support. Not diagnostic.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Persistent difficulty with fluent word reading, not explained by general ability, describes…
    • Dyslexia
    • ADHD
    • Autism
    • Intellectual disability
    Show the answer

    Answer: Dyslexia. Dyslexia affects word reading and spelling.

  2. A school redesigns a classroom with quiet spaces so autistic pupils can learn comfortably. This reflects…
    • A vaccine theory
    • A neurodiversity approach
    • Parent-blaming
    • Ignoring difference
    Show the answer

    Answer: A neurodiversity approach. Adapting environments is central to the neurodiversity view.

  3. What is a strength of the medical model?
    • It denies any difficulties
    • It removes all differences
    • It opens access to support
    • It rejects genetic findings
    Show the answer

    Answer: It opens access to support. Clear criteria help people get assessment and services.

Key terms

Neurodevelopmental condition
A condition beginning in development that affects brain function
Neurodiversity
The view that neurological differences are natural human variation
Co-occurrence
Having more than one condition at the same time
