Personality Disorders

Personality disorders involve enduring patterns causing difficulties; ICD-11 uses severity and trait domains; childhood adversity and the biosocial theory explain some difficulties; stigma and trauma framing are key debates.

Psychology · Psychopathology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe how personality disorders are classified in ICD-11
  • Explain developmental explanations of personality difficulties
  • Evaluate debates about the concept and stigma of personality disorder
Competence and safetyThis lesson is educational and is not a guide to diagnosis or treatment. Anyone concerned about these difficulties should speak to a GP or another qualified professional.

Classification

Personality disorders involve enduring patterns that affect how people see themselves and relate to others, causing distress or impairment across many situations. DSM-5-TR retains named categories, such as borderline personality disorder. ICD-11 (2022) replaced most categories with a dimensional approach: clinicians rate severity (mild, moderate, severe) and can describe prominent trait domains, such as negative affectivity, detachment, dissociality, disinhibition and anankastia, with an optional borderline pattern specifier.

Explanations

Many people given a personality disorder diagnosis report childhood adversity, such as abuse, neglect or invalidating environments. Linehan's biosocial theory of borderline difficulties proposes that emotional sensitivity combined with an invalidating environment leads to problems regulating emotion. Temperament and genetic factors also contribute. Difficulties often reduce over time, challenging the idea that personality patterns are fixed.

Worked example

Approaches in guidance

  1. NICE guidance on borderline personality disorder recommends structured psychological approaches and warns against using medication as the main treatment.
  2. Dialectical behaviour therapy (DBT) and mentalisation-based therapy have evidence of benefit.
  3. A consistent, trusting relationship with services is emphasised.

Evaluation

Personality disorder diagnoses can help people access specialist support. But critics argue they can be stigmatising, may describe responses to trauma, and are disproportionately given to women (borderline) and men in forensic settings (antisocial). Dimensional approaches aim to describe difficulties more accurately and reduce stigma.

Common mistakeAssuming personality disorders can't change. Many people's difficulties reduce over time and with support.

In 2 lines: ICD-11 rates severity and trait domains; DSM-5-TR keeps categories. Adversity and biosocial theory; DBT helps; stigma and trauma framing debated.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Surroundings that dismiss or punish a child's emotions are called…
    • A trait domain
    • A safety behaviour
    • An invalidating environment
    • A vulnerability factor
    Show the answer

    Answer: An invalidating environment. Linehan described invalidating environments.

  2. A clinician describes someone's difficulties as "moderate, with prominent negative affectivity" rather than giving a named category. This follows…
    • DSM-5-TR named categories
    • The humoral model
    • Moral treatment
    • The ICD-11 dimensional approach
    Show the answer

    Answer: The ICD-11 dimensional approach. ICD-11 rates the severity of personality difficulties and notes prominent trait domains.

  3. Why do critics argue personality disorder diagnoses can stigmatise?
    • They may label trauma responses as flaws of character
    • They always reduce stigma for everyone
    • They are never given to women at all
    • They only describe temporary moods
    Show the answer

    Answer: They may label trauma responses as flaws of character. Framing trauma responses as personality can blame individuals.

Key terms

Personality disorder
Enduring patterns of thinking, feeling and relating that cause significant difficulties
Invalidating environment
Surroundings that dismiss or punish a person's emotional experiences
Trait domain
A broad dimension of personality difficulty in ICD-11
