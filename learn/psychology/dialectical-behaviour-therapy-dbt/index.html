Dialectical Behaviour Therapy (DBT)

DBT combines individual therapy, skills groups, phone coaching and a team; it teaches mindfulness, distress tolerance, emotion regulation and interpersonal effectiveness; it balances acceptance and change; evidence supports reduced self-harm.

Psychology · Clinical Psychology and Interventions · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the structure and skills modules of DBT
  • Explain the dialectic of acceptance and change
  • Evaluate the evidence for DBT
Competence and safetyThis lesson is educational and is not a guide to therapy. DBT should only be delivered by qualified, registered professionals.

Origins and structure

Linehan developed DBT for people diagnosed with borderline personality disorder who repeatedly self-harmed. Standard DBT combines individual therapy, group skills training, telephone coaching and a consultation team for therapists. It draws on CBT and on mindfulness practice.

Skills and dialectics

DBT teaches four skills modules: mindfulness, distress tolerance (getting through crises without making things worse), emotion regulation and interpersonal effectiveness. Its central dialectic balances acceptance (validating the person's experience as understandable) with change (learning new skills and behaviours).

Worked example

Evidence

  1. Linehan et al. (1991) found women with borderline difficulties who received DBT had fewer episodes of self-harm and fewer days in hospital than those receiving usual treatment.
  2. Later trials have supported DBT for reducing self-harm, including in adolescents.
  3. NICE guidance on borderline personality disorder notes DBT may be considered for women with frequent self-harm.

Evaluation

DBT is one of the best-supported therapies for self-harm and emotional dysregulation. But it is intensive and costly, requiring trained teams, which limits access. Some trials compare DBT with weaker control treatments, which may overstate its advantage.

Common mistakeAssuming DBT is only for one diagnosis. Its skills have been adapted for other difficulties with emotion regulation.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Skills for getting through a crisis without making it worse are…
    • Emotion regulation
    • Interpersonal effectiveness
    • Cognitive defusion
    • Distress tolerance
    Show the answer

    Answer: Distress tolerance. Distress tolerance targets crisis survival.

  2. A therapist says "It makes sense you felt that way" and then helps the client practise a new way to respond. This shows…
    • DBT's balance of acceptance and change
    • Free association about the past
    • Flooding with a feared situation
    • A token economy for behaviour
    Show the answer

    Answer: DBT's balance of acceptance and change. Validation plus change reflects the dialectic.

  3. Why may some DBT trials overstate its advantage?
    • DBT has never been tested
    • They compared DBT with weaker control treatments
    • All trials were identical
    • Controls always outperform DBT
    Show the answer

    Answer: They compared DBT with weaker control treatments. Weak comparisons inflate apparent superiority.

Key terms

Distress tolerance
Skills for getting through crises without making things worse
Emotion regulation
Skills for understanding and managing emotions
Dialectic
Balancing opposites, such as acceptance and change
