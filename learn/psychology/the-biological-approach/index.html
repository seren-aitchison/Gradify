The Biological Approach

The biological approach treats behaviour as the product of genes, brain and body, studied with methods from twin studies to brain imaging.

Psychology · Foundations of Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the main assumptions of the biological approach
  • Explain how genetic, neural and hormonal factors can influence behaviour, using examples
  • Evaluate the biological approach, including its reductionism and determinism

Assumptions

  • Everything psychological has a physical basis in the brain and body.
  • Behaviour can be influenced by genes, neurochemistry (neurotransmitters and hormones) and brain structure.
  • Many behavioural characteristics have evolved because they helped survival and reproduction.

Methods

  • Twin and adoption studies compare identical (MZ) and non-identical (DZ) twins, or adopted children with biological and adoptive relatives, to estimate genetic influence.
  • Brain imaging (fMRI, PET, EEG) and lesion studies link functions to brain systems.
  • Drug studies manipulate neurotransmitters, and hormone studies measure, for example, cortisol under stress.
Worked example

Reading a twin study

  1. If identical twins (who share all their genes) are more alike on a trait than non-identical twins (who share about half), genes probably contribute.
  2. A meta-analysis of over 2,700 twin studies found the average heritability across all traits studied was about 49% (Polderman et al., 2015).
  3. Heritability describes variation in a population, not how much of one person's trait is "genetic".

Evaluation

Strengths: scientific, objective methods; practical applications such as drug treatments for some mental disorders; increasingly precise links between brain and behaviour. Limitations: reductionism, explaining complex experience only by chemicals or genes, can miss social and psychological causes; determinism sits uneasily with personal responsibility; and correlations between brain activity and behaviour don't show which causes which. Most modern accounts are interactionist: genes and biology work together with the environment (see the nature–nurture debate).

Common mistakeReading heritability as "how much a trait is caused by genes in an individual". It describes differences between people in a particular population and environment.

In 2 lines: The biological approach explains behaviour through genes, brain structure, neurochemistry and evolution. It is scientific and useful but can be reductionist and deterministic; most evidence supports interaction with the environment.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which method compares identical and non-identical twins to estimate genetic influence?
    • Lesion studies
    • Brain imaging
    • Twin studies
    • Case studies
    Show the answer

    Answer: Twin studies. Comparing MZ and DZ similarity separates the effects of shared genes from shared environment, under some assumptions.

  2. Identical twins have a concordance of 50% for a disorder and non-identical twins 15%. What can be concluded?
    • The disorder is entirely genetic, since identical twins share all their genes
    • The disorder is entirely environmental, since concordance is below 100%
    • Twin data can't tell us anything at all about psychological disorders
    • Genes contribute, but environment matters too, since concordance isn't 100%
    Show the answer

    Answer: Genes contribute, but environment matters too, since concordance isn't 100%. Higher MZ concordance suggests a genetic contribution; the 50% shortfall shows non-genetic factors.

  3. Which is the strongest criticism of a purely biological explanation of depression?
    • It neglects psychological and social causes such as stressful events and thinking styles
    • It relies on brain scans rather than detailed clinical case studies
    • It has never led to any effective treatments for depression at all
    • It assumes that depression is learned from the environment
    Show the answer

    Answer: It neglects psychological and social causes such as stressful events and thinking styles. Purely biological accounts are reductionist: life events, cognition and social circumstances also predict depression.

Key terms

Heritability
The proportion of variation in a trait within a population that is associated with genetic differences
Neurotransmitter
A chemical messenger that carries signals between neurones
Monozygotic (MZ) twins
Identical twins, developed from one fertilised egg and sharing nearly all their genes
