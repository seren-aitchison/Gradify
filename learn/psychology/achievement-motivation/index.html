Achievement Motivation

Achievement motivation involves need for achievement, mastery goals and beliefs about ability; mindset and grit effects are real but smaller than popular claims suggest.

Psychology · Personality and Individual Differences · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe McClelland's need for achievement and its measurement
  • Explain mastery and performance goals and Dweck's mindset theory
  • Evaluate the evidence for growth mindset and grit interventions

Need for achievement

McClelland and Atkinson measured need for achievement (nAch) by scoring stories people told about TAT pictures for achievement themes. People high in nAch prefer moderately difficult tasks, where success is possible but not guaranteed, and seek feedback. Atkinson's model balanced the motive to succeed against the fear of failure.

Goals and mindsets

Achievement goal theory distinguishes mastery goals (developing competence and learning) from performance goals (demonstrating ability compared with others). Mastery goals are linked to deeper learning and persistence. Dweck (2006) proposed that people with a growth mindset believe ability can be developed, while those with a fixed mindset believe it is fixed; a growth mindset was linked to resilience after failure.

Worked example

Testing mindset interventions

  1. Yeager et al. (2019) ran a national randomised trial of a brief online growth mindset intervention with over 12,000 US ninth-graders.
  2. It slightly improved grades among lower-achieving students, and increased enrolment in harder maths courses.
  3. Sisk et al. (2018) meta-analysed mindset studies and found weak overall associations with achievement and small average intervention effects.

Grit

Duckworth et al. (2007) defined grit as passion and perseverance for long-term goals. A meta-analysis found grit correlated strongly with conscientiousness and only modestly predicted performance beyond it (Credé, Tynan and Harms, 2017).

Common mistakeTreating growth mindset as a cure-all. Average effects of interventions are small, with benefits concentrated in some groups.

In 2 lines: McClelland's nAch, Atkinson's fear of failure and goal theory explain achievement motivation. Mindset and grit have attracted attention, but evidence suggests modest effects.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. People high in need for achievement prefer tasks that are…
    • Very easy indeed
    • Moderately difficult
    • Almost impossible
    • Completely random
    Show the answer

    Answer: Moderately difficult. Moderate difficulty gives a realistic chance of meaningful success.

  2. A student focuses on beating classmates' scores rather than understanding. This is a…
    • Mastery goal
    • Growth mindset
    • Performance goal
    • Intrinsic motive
    Show the answer

    Answer: Performance goal. Demonstrating ability relative to others is a performance goal.

  3. Why did Credé et al. question grit as a new construct?
    • It doesn't predict anything
    • It is the same as IQ
    • It can't be measured
    • It overlaps strongly with conscientiousness
    Show the answer

    Answer: It overlaps strongly with conscientiousness. Grit added little beyond conscientiousness.

Key terms

Need for achievement
A motive to succeed and meet standards of excellence
Mastery goal
A goal of developing competence and learning
Growth mindset
The belief that ability can be developed through effort and strategy
