Attitude Change and Persuasion

Persuasion works through careful thought (central route) or cues (peripheral route); source, message and audience factors shape it.

Psychology · Social Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the central and peripheral routes of the elaboration likelihood model
  • Explain source, message and audience factors in persuasion
  • Apply persuasion research to health campaigns

Two routes to persuasion

Petty and Cacioppo's elaboration likelihood model (ELM, 1986) proposes two routes. In the central route, people think carefully about the arguments; strong arguments persuade and weak ones don't, and resulting attitudes are lasting and resistant to change. In the peripheral route, people rely on cues such as the speaker's attractiveness or the number of arguments; change is weaker and shorter-lived. People take the central route when they are motivated (the issue is personally relevant) and able (not distracted, have knowledge).

Worked example

Exams and expertise

  1. Petty, Cacioppo and Goldman (1981) told students about a proposal to introduce comprehensive exams, either at their own university next year (high relevance) or in ten years (low relevance).
  2. With high relevance, argument strength mattered most.
  3. With low relevance, whether the source was an expert (a Princeton professor) or a high-school class mattered most.

The Yale approach

  • Source: credible (expert, trustworthy) and attractive speakers are more persuasive.
  • Message: two-sided arguments work better with educated or initially opposed audiences; repetition helps up to a point.
  • Audience: people with low self-esteem or who are distracted may be more easily persuaded by peripheral cues; people forewarned of persuasion resist more.

Fear appeals

Fear appeals ("smoking kills") can work when they also give people a clear, achievable way to reduce the threat. A meta-analysis found fear appeals are generally effective, especially with high efficacy messages that tell people what to do (Tannenbaum et al., 2015).

Common mistakeAssuming more fear always persuades more. Fear works best paired with clear steps people believe they can take.

In 2 lines: The ELM proposes central and peripheral routes depending on motivation and ability. Credible sources, well-matched messages and efficacy information improve persuasion.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Persuasion based on a celebrity's attractiveness uses the…
    • Central route
    • Peripheral route
    • Covariation model
    • Mere exposure effect
    Show the answer

    Answer: Peripheral route. A speaker's attractiveness is a peripheral cue, not an argument.

  2. Students hear a proposal affecting them next year. What most determines whether they're persuaded?
    • Speaker attractiveness
    • Number of slides
    • Argument strength
    • Background music
    Show the answer

    Answer: Argument strength. High relevance leads to central route processing.

  3. Why do two-sided messages work better with opposed audiences?
    • They are shorter
    • They use only emotion
    • They hide the message
    • They anticipate counter-arguments
    Show the answer

    Answer: They anticipate counter-arguments. Acknowledging opposing views seems fair and pre-empts objections.

Key terms

Elaboration likelihood model
Petty and Cacioppo's model of central and peripheral routes to persuasion
Central route
Persuasion through careful evaluation of arguments
Peripheral route
Persuasion through superficial cues such as attractiveness or expertise
