Depression

Depression involves persistent low mood or anhedonia with other symptoms; cognitive, social and biological factors interact; NICE recommends a choice of psychological and medical treatments.

Psychology · Psychopathology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the main features of depression
  • Explain cognitive and social explanations of depression
  • Evaluate an integrated understanding of depression
Competence and safetyThis lesson is educational and is not a guide to diagnosis or treatment. Anyone concerned about low mood should speak to a GP or another qualified professional. In an emergency, call 999 or contact NHS 111.

Features

Depression involves persistent low mood and/or loss of interest or pleasure (anhedonia) for at least two weeks, with other symptoms such as changes in sleep and appetite, fatigue, poor concentration, feelings of worthlessness or guilt, and in some cases thoughts of death or suicide. It ranges in severity and causes significant distress or impairment.

Explanations

Cognitive explanations (Beck) highlight negative schemas and the cognitive triad; rumination prolongs low mood. Social explanations focus on life events and circumstances: Brown and Harris (1978) found that women in London who experienced severe life events were more likely to become depressed if they had vulnerability factors, such as lack of a confiding relationship. Biological factors include genetic risk and stress-related changes, though simple chemical imbalance theories have been challenged.

Worked example

Approaches in guidance

  1. NICE guidance on depression in adults (2022) recommends offering a choice of treatments, discussed with the person.
  2. Options for less severe depression include guided self-help, group CBT and group exercise; for more severe depression, individual CBT, behavioural activation, antidepressant medication or combinations.
  3. The person's preferences and previous experience should guide decisions.

Evaluation

Depression is best understood through interacting factors. Cognitive models explain maintenance and have produced effective therapies; social models show the importance of adversity and support; biological research shows partial heritability. Overemphasis on any single level can lead to narrow treatment.

Common mistakeAssuming depression is simply sadness or a chemical imbalance. It is a complex condition with several interacting causes.

In 2 lines: Low mood or anhedonia with other symptoms for at least two weeks. Cognitive, social and biological factors; NICE offers a choice of treatments.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Repetitively dwelling on distress and its causes is…
    • Anhedonia
    • Displacement
    • Rumination
    • Self-actualisation
    Show the answer

    Answer: Rumination. Repeatedly dwelling on distress, called rumination, tends to prolong low mood.

  2. After losing her job, a woman with no close confidant becomes depressed, while a colleague with strong support doesn't. Brown and Harris would explain this by…
    • Inflated responsibility
    • Catastrophic misinterpretation
    • Preparedness
    • Vulnerability factors
    Show the answer

    Answer: Vulnerability factors. Lack of a confiding relationship increased vulnerability.

  3. Why is an integrated understanding of depression preferred?
    • Biological, psychological and social factors interact
    • One single cause explains all depression
    • Social factors never matter in depression
    • Biology is irrelevant to depression
    Show the answer

    Answer: Biological, psychological and social factors interact. Single-level accounts miss interacting causes.

Key terms

Anhedonia
Loss of interest or pleasure in activities
Rumination
Repetitively focusing on distress and its causes
Vulnerability factor
A circumstance that increases the risk of depression after stress
