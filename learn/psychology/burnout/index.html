Burnout

Burnout involves exhaustion, cynicism and reduced efficacy from chronic work stress; ICD-11 calls it an occupational phenomenon; organisational causes and solutions are central.

Psychology · Applied Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define burnout using Maslach's three dimensions and the ICD-11 description
  • Explain organisational causes of burnout
  • Evaluate approaches to preventing burnout

Education, not diagnosis

This lesson explains research for learning purposes. If you are worried about your own wellbeing, speaking to a GP, occupational health service or another qualified professional can help.

Defining burnout

Maslach and Jackson (1981) described burnout as having three dimensions: emotional exhaustion, depersonalisation or cynicism (detachment from people or the job) and reduced personal accomplishment (efficacy). It is measured with the Maslach Burnout Inventory (MBI). The World Health Organization's ICD-11 (2019) describes burnout as an occupational phenomenon resulting from chronic workplace stress that hasn't been successfully managed, not as a medical condition.

Causes

Maslach and Leiter (2016) identified six areas of work life linked to burnout: workload, control, reward, community, fairness and values. Burnout is common in helping professions such as healthcare, teaching and social work, where emotional demands are high.

Worked example

Burnout in healthcare

  1. Surveys of NHS staff, such as the annual NHS Staff Survey, include questions on burnout and work-related stress.
  2. Researchers link burnout among healthcare staff to staffing shortages, workload and emotional demands.
  3. Burnout is associated with staff leaving and can affect patient care (Panagioti et al., 2018).

Prevention

Interventions can target individuals (stress management, mindfulness) or organisations (workload, staffing, schedules, team support). A meta-analysis of interventions for physicians found both types reduced burnout, with organisational interventions showing somewhat larger effects (Panagioti et al., 2017). Critics argue burnout is often treated as an individual problem when its causes are organisational.

Common mistakeTreating burnout as only an individual weakness. Workload, control, fairness and resources are key causes.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which tool measures burnout?
    • Rorschach test
    • NEO-PI-R
    • Maslach Burnout Inventory
    • Strange Situation
    Show the answer

    Answer: Maslach Burnout Inventory. The MBI measures the three dimensions of burnout.

  2. A teacher becomes detached and cynical about pupils after years of overwork. This reflects…
    • Psychological safety
    • Job enrichment
    • Flow
    • Depersonalisation
    Show the answer

    Answer: Depersonalisation. Detached, cynical attitudes are depersonalisation.

  3. Why do critics say burnout is often wrongly treated as individual?
    • Its main causes are organisational
    • Individuals cause all burnout
    • Workload never matters
    • Burnout is genetic
    Show the answer

    Answer: Its main causes are organisational. Workload, control and fairness are workplace factors.

Key terms

Burnout
A response to chronic workplace stress involving exhaustion, cynicism and reduced efficacy
Depersonalisation
Detached, cynical attitudes towards people or the job
Occupational phenomenon
ICD-11's description of burnout as work-related, not a medical condition
