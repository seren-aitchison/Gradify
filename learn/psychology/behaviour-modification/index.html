Behaviour Modification

Behaviour modification changes behaviour by identifying what maintains it and changing antecedents and consequences, especially by reinforcing alternatives.

Psychology · Learning and Behaviour · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe behaviour modification techniques, including functional assessment and token economies
  • Explain how a token economy works using operant principles
  • Evaluate behaviour modification, including ethical concerns

Techniques

  • Functional assessment: identify the antecedents (A), behaviour (B) and consequences (C) to find what maintains a behaviour (the ABC model).
  • Reinforcement of alternative behaviours: teach and reward a behaviour that serves the same function.
  • Extinction: withhold the reinforcer that maintained the problem behaviour.
  • Token economies: tokens (secondary reinforcers) earned for target behaviours and exchanged for backup rewards.
  • Self-management: monitoring and reinforcing one's own behaviour, such as rewarding study sessions.
Worked example

A functional assessment

  1. A pupil throws work on the floor (behaviour) when given a writing task (antecedent).
  2. They are then sent out of the room (consequence), escaping the task: negative reinforcement.
  3. Intervention: break the task into smaller steps, teach the pupil to ask for a break, and reinforce asking rather than throwing.

Token economies

Ayllon and Azrin (1968) used token economies in a psychiatric hospital: patients earned tokens for self-care and work tasks, increasing those behaviours. Token economies are now used in schools and youth settings. Critics note behaviour often declines when tokens stop, and that rewards can institutionalise people or undermine intrinsic motivation.

Ethics

Behaviour modification raises questions of consent, dignity and control, particularly for people with less power, such as children and people with learning disabilities. Aversive methods have caused harm. Modern positive behaviour support emphasises understanding the function of behaviour, improving quality of life and avoiding restrictive practices, and is recommended in UK guidance for people with learning disabilities whose behaviour challenges.

Common mistakeAssuming behaviour modification means punishment. Effective programmes rely mainly on reinforcing alternative behaviours.

In 2 lines: Behaviour modification uses functional assessment and reinforcement to change behaviour. Token economies work while in place; ethical practice favours positive behaviour support.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. In a token economy, tokens are…
    • Unconditioned stimuli
    • Punishers
    • Secondary reinforcers
    • Primary reinforcers
    Show the answer

    Answer: Secondary reinforcers. Tokens have value because they can be exchanged for rewards, making them secondary reinforcers.

  2. A child shouts to get attention, and adults always respond. Which intervention is most likely to help?
    • Give more attention after shouting
    • Punish the child at random
    • Ignore the child completely at all times
    • Attend to calm requests and stop rewarding shouting
    Show the answer

    Answer: Attend to calm requests and stop rewarding shouting. Reinforcing an alternative that achieves the same function (attention) while extinguishing shouting works best.

  3. Which is the strongest ethical concern about behaviour modification?
    • It can control less powerful people without consent
    • It is based on learning theory
    • It uses observation of behaviour
    • It aims to teach new skills
    Show the answer

    Answer: It can control less powerful people without consent. The concern is about power and consent, especially with children and people with learning disabilities.

Key terms

Functional assessment
Identifying the antecedents and consequences that maintain a behaviour
Token economy
A system in which tokens earned for target behaviours are exchanged for rewards
Positive behaviour support
An approach that understands the function of behaviour and improves quality of life without restrictive practices
