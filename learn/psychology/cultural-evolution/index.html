Cultural Evolution

Culture evolves through variation, transmission biases and selection; dual inheritance and gene–culture coevolution, such as lactase persistence, show genes and culture interact.

Psychology · Evolutionary Psychology and Behavioural Ecology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain cultural evolution and dual inheritance theory
  • Describe transmission biases in cultural learning
  • Evaluate gene–culture coevolution, using lactase persistence as an example

Culture evolves

Cultural evolution applies evolutionary thinking to culture: ideas, skills and practices vary, are transmitted through social learning and some spread more than others. Dual inheritance theory (Boyd and Richerson, 1985) proposes humans inherit both genes and culture, which interact. Culture can change much faster than genes, allowing rapid adaptation to new environments.

Transmission biases

  • Conformist bias: copying the majority.
  • Prestige bias: copying successful or respected individuals.
  • Content bias: some ideas spread because they are easier to remember or more emotionally striking, such as stories involving disgust or threat.

Cumulative culture, building on previous innovations over generations, allows technologies no single person could invent, like boats or computers.

Worked example

Milk and genes

  1. Most mammals lose the ability to digest lactose after weaning.
  2. In populations with a long history of dairy farming, such as northern Europeans and some East African pastoralists, many adults carry gene variants that keep lactase active (lactase persistence).
  3. The cultural practice of dairying created selection for these genes: a classic example of gene–culture coevolution (Tishkoff et al., 2007).

Evaluation

Cultural evolution offers testable models of how culture spreads and explains human success. Critics argue cultural "units" aren't as discrete as genes, that human creativity and intention make cultural change unlike biological evolution, and that "meme" analogies can be loose. Researchers increasingly use formal models and experiments to address this.

Common mistakeAssuming culture and biology are separate. Culture can shape genetic evolution, as with lactase persistence.

In 2 lines: Culture evolves through variation, transmission and selection, faster than genes. Transmission biases shape spread; lactase persistence shows gene–culture coevolution.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Copying what most people around you do is…
    • Prestige bias
    • Content bias
    • Conformist bias
    • Kin selection
    Show the answer

    Answer: Conformist bias. Following the majority is conformist bias.

  2. An urban legend spreads quickly because it involves something disgusting. This illustrates…
    • Prestige bias
    • Conformist bias
    • Natural selection of genes
    • Content bias
    Show the answer

    Answer: Content bias. Memorable, emotional content spreads more.

  3. What is one criticism of cultural evolution analogies?
    • Cultural units blur together
    • Culture never changes at all
    • Genes are purely cultural
    • Learning doesn't occur
    Show the answer

    Answer: Cultural units blur together. Ideas blend and change in ways genes don't.

Key terms

Dual inheritance theory
The theory that humans inherit both genes and culture, which interact
Conformist bias
A tendency to copy the majority
Gene–culture coevolution
Mutual influence between cultural practices and genetic evolution
