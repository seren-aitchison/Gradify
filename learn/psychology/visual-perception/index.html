Visual Perception

Vision builds a stable 3D world from retinal images using depth cues and constancies, processed in partly separate streams for recognition and action.

Psychology · Cognitive Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the visual pathway from retina to cortex
  • Explain depth cues and perceptual constancy
  • Explain the "what" and "where/how" visual streams, with neuropsychological evidence

From eye to cortex

Light passes through the lens to the retina, where rods (sensitive to dim light) and cones (colour and detail, concentrated in the fovea) transduce it. Signals travel via the optic nerve and thalamus to the primary visual cortex in the occipital lobe, where Hubel and Wiesel (1962) found neurones tuned to edges and orientations.

Depth and constancy

  • Binocular cues: retinal disparity (slightly different images from each eye) and convergence.
  • Monocular cues: relative size, linear perspective, texture gradient, overlap (occlusion), height in the visual field, motion parallax.

Perceptual constancy means objects look the same size, shape, colour and brightness despite changes in the retinal image: a door looks rectangular as it opens, though its retinal image becomes a trapezium.

Worked example

Two visual streams

  1. Goodale and Milner (1992) described patient DF, who couldn't recognise the orientation of a slot (ventral "what" stream damage).
  2. Yet she could post a card through it accurately (intact dorsal "how" stream).
  3. This suggests partly separate pathways for recognising objects and guiding action.

Investigate with the lab. Find the orange X: does it pop out among one kind of distractor, but need a slow search among mixed ones? Then try the Müller-Lyer illusion.

Common mistakeAssuming we see with our eyes alone. The eyes transduce light; seeing depends on the brain's interpretation.

In 2 lines: Rods and cones transduce light; visual cortex detects features. Depth cues and constancies create a stable world; ventral and dorsal streams support recognition and action.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which receptors are specialised for colour and fine detail?
    • Rods
    • Hair cells
    • Cones
    • Taste buds
    Show the answer

    Answer: Cones. Cones are concentrated in the fovea and support colour and detail.

  2. Railway lines appear to meet in the distance. Which depth cue is this?
    • Retinal disparity
    • Convergence
    • Motion parallax
    • Linear perspective
    Show the answer

    Answer: Linear perspective. Parallel lines converging with distance is linear perspective.

  3. Why does a door still look rectangular as it swings open?
    • Shape constancy uses knowledge and depth cues
    • The retinal image stays rectangular
    • Doors have no shape
    • Rods correct the image
    Show the answer

    Answer: Shape constancy uses knowledge and depth cues. The brain compensates for the changing retinal image.

  4. In visual search, which kind of target usually pops out?
    • One that combines two features shared with distractors
    • One that differs by a single feature, such as colour
    • One that is always in the centre
    • One that appears last
    Show the answer

    Answer: One that differs by a single feature, such as colour. Single-feature targets are found in parallel; conjunction targets need serial search.

Key terms

Retinal disparity
The difference between the two eyes' images, used as a depth cue
Perceptual constancy
Perceiving objects as stable despite changes in their retinal images
Ventral and dorsal streams
Visual pathways for object recognition ("what") and spatial action ("where/how")
