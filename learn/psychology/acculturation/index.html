Acculturation

Berry's model has four acculturation strategies: integration, assimilation, separation and marginalisation; integration is often linked to better adaptation; acculturative stress and host society attitudes shape outcomes.

Psychology · Cultural and Cross-Cultural Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe Berry's four acculturation strategies
  • Explain acculturative stress and its causes
  • Evaluate Berry's model

Berry's model

Berry (1997) proposed that people in contact with a new culture face two questions: is it valuable to maintain one's heritage culture, and is it valuable to have relationships with the wider society? The answers give four strategies: integration (both), assimilation (wider society only), separation (heritage only) and marginalisation (neither). Research has often found integration is associated with the best psychological and social adaptation.

Acculturative stress

Acculturative stress arises from the challenges of adapting to a new culture, such as language barriers, discrimination, loss of social support, and changing family roles. Children often acculturate faster than parents, which can create acculturation gaps and family conflict.

Worked example

The role of the host society

  1. Berry stressed that individuals can't choose strategies freely: the receiving society's attitudes matter.
  2. If the wider society expects assimilation or discriminates against newcomers, integration becomes harder.
  3. Policies on language support, recognition of qualifications and anti-discrimination shape adaptation.

Evaluation

Berry's model is influential and widely tested. Critics argue the four categories are too static, that people use different strategies in different areas of life (for example, integration at work and separation at home), and that the model can underplay power and discrimination. Measurement of the strategies has also been questioned.

Common mistakeAssuming acculturation is only a migrant's choice. The host society's attitudes constrain options.

In 2 lines: Berry's four strategies; integration is often most adaptive. Acculturative stress and host attitudes matter; the model may be too static.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Maintaining one's heritage culture while engaging with the wider society is…
    • Assimilation
    • Separation
    • Marginalisation
    • Integration
    Show the answer

    Answer: Integration. Integration combines keeping heritage culture with engaging in the wider society.

  2. A migrant stops speaking his first language and avoids contact with his heritage community to fit in. Berry would call this…
    • Assimilation
    • Integration
    • Separation
    • Marginalisation
    Show the answer

    Answer: Assimilation. Adopting the new culture and dropping the old is assimilation.

  3. Why is Berry's model criticised as too static?
    • Everyone uses one strategy for ever
    • People use different strategies in different areas
    • It ignores heritage culture entirely
    • It has only a single category
    Show the answer

    Answer: People use different strategies in different areas. People may integrate at work but separate at home, so strategies vary by context.

Key terms

Acculturation
Cultural and psychological change through contact between cultures
Integration
Maintaining heritage culture while engaging with the wider society
Acculturative stress
Stress caused by adapting to a new culture
