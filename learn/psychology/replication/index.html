Replication

Replication tests whether findings are reliable; large projects found many published effects were weaker than reported, prompting reforms.

Psychology · Research Methods · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Distinguish direct and conceptual replication
  • Describe the findings of the Open Science Collaboration (2015)
  • Evaluate the causes of and responses to the replication crisis

Why replicate?

A single study can be wrong by chance, through bias or because of unnoticed features of its setting. Direct (close) replication repeats the original procedure as closely as possible with new participants. Conceptual replication tests the same idea with different methods or measures. Both are needed: direct replication checks the finding; conceptual replication checks the theory.

The replication crisis

In 2015 the Open Science Collaboration attempted close replications of 100 studies published in three psychology journals in 2008. About 97% of the original studies had reported significant results, but only about 36% of the replications did, and replication effect sizes were on average about half the originals. Other large projects (such as Many Labs) found that some effects replicated robustly while others didn't.

Worked example

Why might a published effect fail to replicate?

  1. The original was a false positive, helped by small samples and flexible analysis.
  2. The original effect was real but smaller than reported (the winner's curse).
  3. Differences in sample, setting or procedure (hidden moderators) changed the effect.

Investigate with the lab. Find the underpowered and p-hacked studies, and consider why they might not replicate.

Responses

Reforms include larger samples and power analysis, preregistration, registered reports, open data and materials, and valuing replication studies. Some researchers argue the crisis was overstated, because replications can differ in important ways from originals; others see it as science correcting itself.

Common mistakeConcluding that a failed replication proves the original was false. It lowers confidence; several replications and meta-analysis give a clearer picture.

In 2 lines: Direct and conceptual replication test findings and theories. Large projects found low replication rates, prompting reforms in methods and publishing.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Testing the same hypothesis with a different measure is a…
    • Direct replication
    • Pilot study
    • Meta-analysis
    • Conceptual replication
    Show the answer

    Answer: Conceptual replication. Changing methods while keeping the idea is conceptual replication.

  2. A famous priming effect fails to replicate in a large preregistered study. What is the most reasonable conclusion?
    • Confidence in the original effect should fall, pending further evidence
    • The original researchers must have committed fraud
    • The replication must be wrong
    • Priming effects can never be studied
    Show the answer

    Answer: Confidence in the original effect should fall, pending further evidence. One failure lowers confidence; patterns across studies decide.

  3. Which reform most directly reduces false positives in original studies?
    • Publishing only significant results
    • Preregistration with adequate power
    • Using smaller samples
    • Avoiding replication studies
    Show the answer

    Answer: Preregistration with adequate power. Preregistration limits analytic flexibility and power reduces chance findings.

  4. Why might a study with a small sample fail to replicate?
    • Small samples always give exact results
    • Replications always use smaller samples
    • Low power makes significant results more likely to be chance
    • Small samples remove all bias
    Show the answer

    Answer: Low power makes significant results more likely to be chance. Underpowered studies that find effects often overestimate them.

Key terms

Direct replication
Repeating a study's procedure as closely as possible with new participants
Conceptual replication
Testing the same hypothesis with different methods or measures
Replication crisis
The discovery that many published findings failed to replicate
