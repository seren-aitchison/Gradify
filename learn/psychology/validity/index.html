Validity

Valid studies show real causes (internal validity) that generalise (external validity); valid measures capture the intended construct.

Psychology · Research Methods · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define internal, external, ecological and population validity
  • Describe face, concurrent, predictive and construct validity of measures
  • Evaluate the validity of a study or measure and suggest improvements

Validity of studies

  • Internal validity: whether the IV, rather than something else, caused the change in the DV. Threatened by confounds, demand characteristics and investigator effects.
  • External validity: whether findings generalise beyond the study: to other settings (ecological validity), other people (population validity) and other times (temporal validity).

Validity of measures

  • Face validity: does the measure look as if it measures the construct? The weakest form.
  • Concurrent validity: do scores agree with an established measure taken at the same time?
  • Predictive validity: do scores predict future outcomes they should predict?
  • Construct validity: does the measure behave as theory says the construct should, relating to some measures and not others (Cronbach and Meehl, 1955)?
Worked example

Validating a new depression questionnaire

  1. Face: items ask about low mood and loss of interest.
  2. Concurrent: scores correlate strongly with an established depression scale.
  3. Predictive: high scores predict later contact with mental health services.
  4. Construct: scores relate more strongly to depression measures than to anxiety measures.

Validity and reliability are linked: an unreliable measure can't be valid, but a reliable one may still measure the wrong thing.

Investigate with the lab. Judge each invented study's internal and external validity.

Common mistakeTreating face validity as enough. A measure that looks right can still fail to predict or relate to anything as it should.

In 2 lines: Internal validity concerns causation; external validity concerns generalisation. Measures are validated through face, concurrent, predictive and construct evidence.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Whether findings from a 1960s study still apply today concerns…
    • Face validity
    • Internal validity
    • Temporal validity
    • Inter-rater reliability
    Show the answer

    Answer: Temporal validity. Generalising across time is temporal validity.

  2. A new aptitude test correlates highly with an established test given on the same day. Which validity does this show?
    • Predictive validity
    • Face validity
    • Ecological validity
    • Concurrent validity
    Show the answer

    Answer: Concurrent validity. Agreement with an established measure at the same time is concurrent validity.

  3. Which would most improve the ecological validity of a laboratory memory study?
    • Meaningful material in an everyday setting
    • Using more nonsense syllables
    • Adding a stricter time limit
    • Recruiting only psychology students
    Show the answer

    Answer: Meaningful material in an everyday setting. Realistic materials and settings increase ecological validity, possibly at some cost to control.

  4. What does ecological validity concern?
    • Whether a test is consistent over time
    • Whether findings generalise to real-life settings
    • Whether the sample is large enough
    • Whether the hypothesis is directional
    Show the answer

    Answer: Whether findings generalise to real-life settings. Highly artificial tasks may not reflect everyday behaviour.

Key terms

Internal validity
The extent to which the IV caused the change in the DV
External validity
The extent to which findings generalise beyond the study
Construct validity
The extent to which a measure behaves as the theory of the construct predicts
