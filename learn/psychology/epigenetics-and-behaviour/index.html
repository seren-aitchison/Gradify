Epigenetics and Behaviour

Epigenetic marks such as DNA methylation regulate gene expression and can respond to experience; Weaver et al. and the Dutch Hunger Winter provide evidence; transgenerational inheritance in humans is contested.

Psychology · Contemporary Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain epigenetic mechanisms such as DNA methylation
  • Describe animal and human evidence linking experience to epigenetic change
  • Evaluate claims about epigenetic inheritance in humans

Mechanisms

Epigenetic mechanisms, such as DNA methylation and changes to histone proteins, affect whether genes are switched on or off. These marks can respond to the environment, offering a possible route by which experience "gets under the skin". Epigenetic patterns differ between tissues, so changes in blood may not reflect changes in the brain.

Evidence

Weaver et al. (2004) found that rat pups who received more licking and grooming from their mothers had different methylation of a stress-related gene in the hippocampus and calmer stress responses as adults; cross-fostering showed the effect followed the caregiving, not the birth mother. In humans, Heijmans et al. (2008) found people prenatally exposed to the Dutch Hunger Winter of 1944–45 had different methylation of a growth-related gene about six decades later.

Worked example

Inheritance across generations?

  1. Some animal studies suggest stress effects can pass to offspring through epigenetic marks.
  2. In humans, evidence for transgenerational epigenetic inheritance, across generations not directly exposed, is limited and contested.
  3. Most epigenetic marks are reset during early development, which makes inheritance difficult.

Evaluation

Epigenetics shows genes and environment are intertwined, undermining simple nature–nurture divisions. But many human studies are small, correlational and measure marks in blood rather than brain. Popular claims about inherited trauma often go far beyond current evidence.

Common mistakeAssuming trauma is commonly inherited through epigenetic marks. Human evidence is limited.

In 2 lines: Epigenetic marks regulate gene expression and respond to experience. Animal and Dutch Hunger Winter evidence; human transgenerational claims are contested.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Changes in gene expression without changes to the DNA sequence are studied in…
    • Heritability
    • Polygenic scoring
    • Predictive processing
    • Epigenetics
    Show the answer

    Answer: Epigenetics. Epigenetics concerns gene expression, not sequence.

  2. Cross-fostered rat pups show stress responses matching their foster mother's care, not their birth mother's. This suggests…
    • Caregiving shaped gene expression
    • Genes alone determined behaviour
    • No epigenetic effect
    • Inheritance through DNA sequence
    Show the answer

    Answer: Caregiving shaped gene expression. The stress response followed the foster mother's caregiving, not the birth mother's genes.

  3. Why are popular claims about inherited trauma criticised?
    • It has been proven in all families
    • Human evidence for transgenerational effects is limited
    • Epigenetics doesn't exist at all
    • Epigenetic marks are never reset at all
    Show the answer

    Answer: Human evidence for transgenerational effects is limited. Popular claims about inherited trauma often outrun the current human evidence.

Key terms

Epigenetics
Changes in gene expression without changes to the DNA sequence
DNA methylation
Chemical marks on DNA that affect gene expression
Transgenerational inheritance
Effects passing to generations not directly exposed
