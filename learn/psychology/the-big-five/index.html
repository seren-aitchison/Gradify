The Big Five

The Big Five (OCEAN) are broad traits measured by questionnaires, broadly replicated across cultures and predictive of important life outcomes.

Psychology · Personality and Individual Differences · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the five factors of the Big Five model
  • Explain how the Big Five are measured and their cross-cultural replication
  • Evaluate evidence on what the Big Five predict

Five factors

  • Openness to experience: curiosity, imagination, interest in ideas and art.
  • Conscientiousness: organisation, self-discipline, reliability.
  • Extraversion: sociability, assertiveness, positive emotion.
  • Agreeableness: compassion, trust, cooperativeness.
  • Neuroticism: tendency to experience negative emotions such as anxiety and moodiness (the opposite pole is emotional stability).

The acronym OCEAN helps remember them. Each factor is broad and contains narrower facets; conscientiousness, for example, includes orderliness and dutifulness.

Measurement and replication

The Big Five are measured with questionnaires such as the NEO-PI-R (Costa and McCrae, 1992) and the shorter Big Five Inventory. McCrae and Costa (1997) found a similar five-factor structure in translated questionnaires across many cultures, including Chinese, Korean and German samples. Some studies of small-scale, non-literate societies have found weaker replication (Gurven et al., 2013).

Worked example

What the Big Five predict

  1. Conscientiousness predicts job performance across many occupations (Barrick and Mount, 1991) and longer life (Roberts et al., 2007).
  2. Neuroticism predicts risk of anxiety and depression.
  3. Roberts et al. (2007) found the effects of personality on mortality, divorce and occupational attainment were comparable in size to those of socioeconomic status and cognitive ability.

Evaluation

Strengths: the model organises many traits into a widely replicated framework with strong predictive validity. Limitations: it is descriptive rather than explanatory, relies on self-report, and some argue a sixth factor, honesty–humility (the HEXACO model), is needed (Ashton and Lee, 2007).

Common mistakeTreating Big Five scores as types. People vary continuously on each dimension, and most are near the middle.

In 2 lines: The Big Five are openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness and neuroticism. They replicate widely and predict outcomes such as job performance, health and longevity.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. A tendency to feel anxious and moody reflects high…
    • Agreeableness
    • Openness
    • Neuroticism
    • Conscientiousness
    Show the answer

    Answer: Neuroticism. Neuroticism is the tendency to experience anxiety, moodiness and other negative emotions.

  2. A colleague is curious, imaginative and loves new ideas. Which trait is likely high?
    • Neuroticism
    • Agreeableness
    • Conscientiousness
    • Openness to experience
    Show the answer

    Answer: Openness to experience. Curiosity and imagination define openness.

  3. What does the HEXACO model add to the Big Five?
    • An honesty–humility factor
    • A neuroticism factor
    • Intelligence
    • Temperament types
    Show the answer

    Answer: An honesty–humility factor. HEXACO adds honesty–humility as a sixth dimension.

Key terms

Conscientiousness

Neuroticism

Facet
