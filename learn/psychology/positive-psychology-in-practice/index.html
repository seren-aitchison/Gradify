Positive Psychology in Practice

Positive psychology studies strengths and wellbeing; brief interventions have small but real effects; critics warn against overclaiming and ignoring social causes.

Psychology · Motivation, Emotion and Wellbeing · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain the aims and origins of positive psychology
  • Describe common positive psychology interventions
  • Evaluate the evidence for positive psychology interventions and criticisms of the field

Origins

Seligman and Csikszentmihalyi (2000) argued psychology had focused mainly on illness and should also study positive emotions, strengths and positive institutions. Positive psychology draws on earlier humanistic psychology but emphasises empirical research. Peterson and Seligman (2004) classified 24 character strengths, such as kindness, curiosity and perseverance.

Interventions

Worked example

Three good things

  1. Seligman et al. (2005) ran an online randomised study of several brief exercises.
  2. Writing down three good things each day and why they happened, and using signature strengths in new ways, increased happiness and reduced depressive symptoms for up to six months compared with a placebo exercise.
  3. Effects depended on participants continuing the exercises.

A meta-analysis found positive psychology interventions had small effects on wellbeing and depression (Bolier et al., 2013). Effects were larger for people with some depressive symptoms and when interventions were delivered individually.

Criticisms

Critics argue positive psychology can encourage "tyranny of positive thinking", making people feel they should always be happy; that it underplays social and economic causes of unhappiness; that some early findings were overstated (the "positivity ratio" claim was retracted after criticism by Brown, Sokal and Friedman, 2013); and that many studies use WEIRD samples. Second-wave positive psychology emphasises balancing positive and negative experiences.

Common mistakeAssuming positive thinking can solve all problems. Effects are modest, and social conditions matter.

In 2 lines: Positive psychology studies strengths, positive emotions and institutions. Interventions have small effects; critics warn of overclaiming and neglecting social causes.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Who launched positive psychology as a field in 2000?
    • Sigmund Freud and Carl Jung
    • Abraham Maslow and Carl Rogers
    • Seligman and Csikszentmihalyi
    • Ed Diener and Richard Lucas
    Show the answer

    Answer: Seligman and Csikszentmihalyi. Seligman and Csikszentmihalyi's 2000 article set out the field's agenda.

  2. Each evening a person writes down three things that went well and why. This is…
    • Expressive suppression
    • Drive reduction
    • The day reconstruction method
    • A positive psychology intervention
    Show the answer

    Answer: A positive psychology intervention. Writing three good things each day is a classic positive psychology intervention.

  3. What is the main social criticism of positive psychology?
    • It may underplay social causes of unhappiness
    • It focuses too much on poverty
    • It rejects research
    • It studies only illness
    Show the answer

    Answer: It may underplay social causes of unhappiness. Individual exercises can't fix structural problems.

Key terms

Positive psychology
The scientific study of what helps people thrive
Character strengths
Positive traits such as kindness and curiosity
Positive psychology intervention
An activity designed to increase wellbeing, such as three good things
