Language Processing

Language processing involves rapid word recognition, incremental parsing and knowledge-driven inference.

Psychology · Cognitive Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain how spoken and written words are recognised
  • Describe how sentences are parsed, using garden-path sentences
  • Explain how inferences and knowledge build comprehension

Recognising words

Our mental lexicon stores tens of thousands of words. Frequent words are recognised faster (the word frequency effect), and words are recognised faster after a related word (semantic priming: "nurse" is read faster after "doctor"; Meyer and Schvaneveldt, 1971). In reading, the eyes move in jumps (saccades) with short fixations; skilled readers decode print quickly and largely automatically.

Building sentence structure

Parsing assigns grammatical structure to a sentence as it unfolds. Garden-path sentences show parsing happens word by word: in "The horse raced past the barn fell", readers first take "raced" as the main verb, then must reanalyse when "fell" arrives. Context, word meaning and probability all guide parsing.

Worked example

Comprehension and inference

  1. Bransford and Johnson (1972) read participants a passage about a procedure ("First you arrange items into different groups…").
  2. Those told beforehand it was about washing clothes understood and remembered it much better.
  3. Comprehension depends on activating the right background knowledge (schemas).

Brain studies link language production to Broca's area in the left frontal lobe and comprehension to Wernicke's area in the left temporal lobe, though language relies on a wider network than these two regions.

Common mistakeAssuming comprehension simply decodes words. Readers constantly infer meaning using background knowledge.

In 2 lines: Word recognition depends on frequency and priming; parsing proceeds word by word. Comprehension relies on inference and background knowledge.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Common words are recognised faster than rare words. This is the…
    • Garden-path effect
    • Word frequency effect
    • McGurk effect
    • Stroop effect
    Show the answer

    Answer: Word frequency effect. Words we meet often are recognised faster: the word frequency effect.

  2. Readers stumble at the end of "The old man the boat". Why?
    • They don't know the words
    • They have poor eyesight
    • They parsed "man" as a noun first
    • The sentence is ungrammatical
    Show the answer

    Answer: They parsed "man" as a noun first. Initial parsing must be revised: "man" is a verb.

  3. What does Bransford and Johnson's study imply for teaching?
    • Remove titles from all texts
    • Teach only new vocabulary
    • Train pupils to read faster
    • Activate prior knowledge first
    Show the answer

    Answer: Activate prior knowledge first. Comprehension improves when relevant schemas are activated.

Key terms

Mental lexicon
The store of words and their meanings in memory
Semantic priming
Faster recognition of a word after a related word
Garden-path sentence
A sentence that leads readers to an initial wrong interpretation
