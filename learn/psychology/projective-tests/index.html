Projective Tests

Projective tests interpret responses to ambiguous stimuli; most indices have weak reliability and validity, though a few show support.

Psychology · Personality and Individual Differences · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the rationale and procedure of projective tests
  • Describe the Rorschach and the Thematic Apperception Test
  • Evaluate the reliability and validity of projective tests

The projective hypothesis

Projective tests rest on the projective hypothesis: when people interpret ambiguous material, they project their own needs, conflicts and feelings onto it. The approach comes from psychodynamic theory, which emphasises unconscious processes that people can't report directly on questionnaires.

Two well-known tests

The Rorschach inkblot test (Rorschach, 1921) presents ten inkblots and asks "What might this be?" Responses are scored for content, location and features such as colour and movement. Exner's Comprehensive System (1974) standardised scoring. The Thematic Apperception Test (TAT; Morgan and Murray, 1935) shows ambiguous pictures of people and asks for a story; themes are used to infer needs, such as the need for achievement.

Worked example

The illusory correlation

  1. Chapman and Chapman (1969) showed that clinicians and students "saw" links between certain Rorschach responses and homosexuality that didn't exist in the data.
  2. The links matched popular stereotypes rather than evidence.
  3. This illusory correlation shows how expectations can bias interpretation of projective responses.

Evaluation

Critics argue that many Rorschach indices have weak reliability and validity and that norms over-pathologised normal people (Lilienfeld, Wood and Garb, 2000). Defenders note some indices, such as measures of disordered thinking, show validity, and a newer system (R-PAS) addresses some problems. TAT measures of achievement motivation predict some long-term behaviour, but scoring is time-consuming.

Common mistakeAssuming projective tests reveal hidden truths. Their interpretations are vulnerable to bias and illusory correlation.

In 2 lines: Projective tests assume people project inner states onto ambiguous material. The Rorschach and TAT are well known, but most indices have weak psychometric support.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which test uses ten inkblots?
    • TAT
    • Rorschach
    • NEO-PI-R
    • MMPI
    Show the answer

    Answer: Rorschach. Rorschach introduced the inkblot test in 1921.

  2. A clinician "sees" a link between certain responses and a trait because of a stereotype. This is…
    • Construct validity
    • Faking good
    • An illusory correlation
    • Test–retest reliability
    Show the answer

    Answer: An illusory correlation. The perceived link reflects the clinician's expectation, not the data: an illusory correlation.

  3. Why are projective tests linked to psychodynamic theory?
    • They measure only behaviour
    • They were designed by behaviourists
    • They use reaction times
    • They aim to reveal unconscious processes
    Show the answer

    Answer: They aim to reveal unconscious processes. They assume unconscious content appears in interpretations.

Key terms

Projective hypothesis
The idea that people project their own needs onto ambiguous stimuli
Rorschach test
A projective test using ten inkblots
Illusory correlation
Perceiving a relationship that doesn't exist
