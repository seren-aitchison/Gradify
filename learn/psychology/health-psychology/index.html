Health Psychology

Health depends on biological, psychological and social factors; health beliefs and intentions shape behaviour; adherence depends on beliefs, communication and practical barriers.

Psychology · Applied Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain the biopsychosocial model of health
  • Describe factors affecting health behaviours and adherence
  • Evaluate psychological approaches to improving health

The biopsychosocial model

Engel (1977) proposed the biopsychosocial model: health and illness result from interactions between biological factors (genes, physiology), psychological factors (beliefs, emotions, behaviour) and social factors (relationships, income, culture). This contrasts with a purely biomedical model focused only on disease processes.

Health behaviours

Behaviours such as smoking, diet, physical activity and alcohol use strongly affect health. The health belief model (Rosenstock, 1966) proposes people act to protect health if they perceive susceptibility and severity, believe benefits outweigh barriers, and receive cues to action. The theory of planned behaviour (Ajzen, 1991) adds attitudes, subjective norms and perceived control.

Worked example

Adherence to medication

  1. The World Health Organization (2003) estimated that, on average, around 50% of people with long-term conditions in developed countries don't take medicines as prescribed.
  2. Reasons include forgetting, side effects, cost, not understanding instructions and doubts about need.
  3. Horne's necessity–concerns framework found adherence is higher when people believe a medicine is necessary and have fewer concerns about it (Horne et al., 2013).

Doctor–patient communication

Clear communication, shared decision-making and checking understanding improve satisfaction and adherence. Health psychologists also study pain, chronic illness, coping with diagnosis and the psychological effects of illness.

Common mistakeAssuming non-adherence is just carelessness. Beliefs, side effects, costs and understanding all play a part.

In 2 lines: The biopsychosocial model replaces a narrow biomedical view. Health belief and planned behaviour models explain health behaviour; adherence depends on beliefs and barriers.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which model contrasts with a purely biomedical view of illness?
    • The health belief model
    • The biopsychosocial model
    • The theory of planned behaviour
    • The necessity–concerns framework
    Show the answer

    Answer: The biopsychosocial model. Engel's 1977 biopsychosocial model adds psychological and social factors to the biomedical view.

  2. A person doesn't get a flu jab because they think they're unlikely to catch flu. The health belief model calls this low…
    • Perceived barriers
    • Cues to action
    • Perceived susceptibility
    • Self-efficacy
    Show the answer

    Answer: Perceived susceptibility. Believing you're unlikely to be affected is low perceived susceptibility.

  3. Why is non-adherence not simply carelessness?
    • People always forget
    • Medicines have no side effects
    • Doctors never explain
    • Beliefs and barriers matter
    Show the answer

    Answer: Beliefs and barriers matter. Beliefs, side effects, costs and understanding give rational or practical reasons.

Key terms

Biopsychosocial model
The view that health results from biological, psychological and social factors
Health belief model
A model linking health behaviour to perceived susceptibility, severity, benefits, barriers and cues
Adherence
Taking medicines or following advice as agreed with health professionals
