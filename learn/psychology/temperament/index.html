Temperament

Temperament is early, partly biological reactivity and regulation; goodness of fit with the environment shapes how it develops into personality.

Psychology · Personality and Individual Differences · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define temperament and describe Thomas and Chess's types
  • Explain Kagan's research on behavioural inhibition
  • Evaluate the links between temperament, environment and later personality

Early differences

Babies differ from birth in activity level, mood and how they react to new things. Temperament refers to these early-appearing, partly biological differences in reactivity and self-regulation (Rothbart, 2007). It is thought of as the foundation on which personality develops.

Models

In the New York Longitudinal Study, Thomas and Chess (1977) followed 133 children from infancy. They classified about 40% as easy (regular, adaptable, positive mood), 10% as difficult (irregular, intense, negative) and 15% as slow-to-warm-up (cautious, gradually adapting); the rest didn't fit a type. They stressed goodness of fit: outcomes depend on how well a child's temperament matches their environment.

Worked example

Behavioural inhibition

  1. Kagan and colleagues observed toddlers meeting unfamiliar people and objects.
  2. About 15–20% were consistently shy and cautious (behaviourally inhibited), and showed higher heart rates and physiological arousal.
  3. Many inhibited toddlers remained relatively reserved later, and as a group they had a higher risk of anxiety in adolescence, though many changed (Kagan and Snidman, 1999).

Temperament and personality

Rothbart's model of effortful control, negative affectivity and surgency maps onto later conscientiousness, neuroticism and extraversion. Links between infant temperament and adult personality are moderate, and parenting, culture and experience shape how temperament develops.

Common mistakeLabelling a child "difficult" as if it were permanent. Temperament interacts with care and often changes.

In 2 lines: Temperament describes early differences in reactivity and self-regulation. Thomas and Chess's types, Kagan's inhibition and Rothbart's dimensions link to later personality, moderated by environment.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Thomas and Chess classified about 40% of children as…
    • Difficult
    • Easy
    • Slow-to-warm-up
    • Inhibited
    Show the answer

    Answer: Easy. Easy children, about 40% of the sample, were the largest group.

  2. A cautious toddler thrives with patient parents who allow time to adjust. This illustrates…
    • Fixation
    • Self-efficacy
    • Goodness of fit
    • Social comparison
    Show the answer

    Answer: Goodness of fit. Outcomes depend on the match between child and environment.

  3. Why are infant temperament–adult personality links only moderate?
    • Temperament is not biological
    • Adults have no personality
    • Babies can't be measured
    • Experience and parenting shape development
    Show the answer

    Answer: Experience and parenting shape development. Parenting, culture and experience modify early tendencies, so links are moderate.

Key terms

Temperament
Early-appearing, biologically based differences in reactivity and regulation
Goodness of fit
The match between a child's temperament and their environment
Behavioural inhibition
A tendency to be cautious and withdrawn with unfamiliar people and situations
