The Diathesis–stress Model

The diathesis–stress model combines vulnerability and stress; diatheses can be biological or psychological; candidate gene interaction findings such as Caspi et al. (2003) have not replicated in large samples.

Psychology · Psychopathology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain the diathesis–stress model
  • Apply the model to examples of psychological difficulty
  • Evaluate evidence from gene–environment interaction research
Competence and safetyThis lesson is educational and is not a guide to diagnosis or treatment. Anyone concerned about their own or someone else's mental health should speak to a GP or another qualified professional.

Vulnerability and stress

Zubin and Spring (1977) proposed that people differ in their vulnerability (diathesis) to psychological difficulties. A diathesis may be biological, such as genetic risk, or psychological, such as negative thinking styles or early adversity. Difficulties develop when stress exceeds a person's threshold: people with high vulnerability may be affected by modest stress, while those with low vulnerability may need severe stress.

Gene–environment interaction

Caspi et al. (2003) reported that people with a particular variant of the serotonin transporter gene were more likely to become depressed after stressful life events, an example of gene–environment interaction. This influential finding has not held up well: Border et al. (2019) analysed large samples and found no support for this or other historical candidate gene interactions for depression.

Worked example

Applying the model

  1. Two students face the same stressful exams.
  2. One has a family history of anxiety and a tendency to catastrophise, and develops severe anxiety; the other copes.
  3. The model explains the difference through differing vulnerability meeting the same stress. This example is illustrative.

Evaluation

The diathesis–stress model integrates biological and environmental factors and fits the observation that not everyone exposed to stress develops difficulties. But it can be hard to measure diathesis independently of outcome. Differential susceptibility theory suggests some "vulnerable" people also benefit more from supportive environments, not only suffer more from bad ones.

Common mistakeAssuming vulnerability means difficulties are inevitable. Stress and support shape outcomes.

In 2 lines: Vulnerability plus stress produces difficulties. Caspi et al. (2003) didn't replicate; differential susceptibility extends the model.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. When the effect of stress depends on a person's genes, this is called…
    • Heritability of depression
    • Gene–environment interaction
    • Comorbidity of conditions
    • Biological reductionism
    Show the answer

    Answer: Gene–environment interaction. Effects that depend on genes and environment together are interactions.

  2. Two people lose their jobs; only the one with a history of childhood adversity develops depression. The diathesis–stress model explains this through…
    • Identical vulnerability in both people
    • Stress having no effect on either
    • Different vulnerability meeting the same stress
    • Genes alone causing the outcome
    Show the answer

    Answer: Different vulnerability meeting the same stress. The same stress affects people with different diatheses differently.

  3. What does differential susceptibility add to the diathesis–stress model?
    • Vulnerability only ever causes harm
    • Environments never matter for outcomes
    • Genes alone decide every outcome
    • Vulnerable people may benefit more from good environments
    Show the answer

    Answer: Vulnerable people may benefit more from good environments. Sensitivity can work for better as well as worse.

Key terms

Diathesis
An underlying vulnerability to difficulties
Gene–environment interaction
When the effect of the environment depends on genes, or vice versa
Differential susceptibility
The idea that some people are more affected by both bad and good environments
