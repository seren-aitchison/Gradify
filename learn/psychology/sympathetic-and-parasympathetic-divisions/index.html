Sympathetic and Parasympathetic Divisions

The sympathetic branch prepares the body for action via adrenaline; the parasympathetic restores rest and digestion.

Psychology · Biological Psychology and Neuroscience · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the effects of sympathetic and parasympathetic activation
  • Explain the fight-or-flight response and the role of adrenaline
  • Evaluate the fight-or-flight account, including the tend-and-befriend alternative

Two opposing branches

The sympathetic division prepares the body for action. It increases heart rate and blood pressure, widens airways, dilates pupils, releases glucose and slows digestion. The parasympathetic division ("rest and digest") does the opposite: slows heart rate, stimulates digestion and conserves energy. Most organs receive input from both, and the balance shifts with circumstances.

Fight or flight

When a threat is perceived, the hypothalamus activates the sympathetic system, which signals the adrenal medulla to release adrenaline (and noradrenaline) into the blood. Adrenaline reinforces the sympathetic effects within seconds. Cannon (1932) called this the fight-or-flight response: it prepares the body to fight or escape.

Worked example

An exam room

  1. A student turns over the exam paper and sees an unfamiliar question.
  2. Heart rate rises, breathing quickens, hands sweat and the stomach "churns" as digestion slows.
  3. After a few minutes of working through familiar questions, the parasympathetic system brings heart rate back down.

Evaluation

The response is adaptive for physical threats but less so for modern psychological stressors that can't be fought or fled, and repeated activation may harm health. Taylor et al. (2000) proposed that females, in particular, may often respond to stress by tend and befriend: protecting offspring and seeking social support, linked to the hormone oxytocin. People may also freeze.

Common mistakeThinking the two branches switch on and off like a light. Both are active continuously; their balance shifts.

In 2 lines: Sympathetic activation prepares for action; parasympathetic activity restores calm. Fight-or-flight is driven by the hypothalamus and adrenaline; tend-and-befriend and freezing are alternatives.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which division is described as "rest and digest"?
    • Sympathetic
    • Somatic
    • Central
    • Parasympathetic
    Show the answer

    Answer: Parasympathetic. The parasympathetic division conserves energy.

  2. A person's mouth goes dry and heart pounds before a speech. Which division is most active?
    • Sympathetic
    • Parasympathetic
    • Somatic
    • Spinal reflex
    Show the answer

    Answer: Sympathetic. Dry mouth and fast heart reflect sympathetic arousal.

  3. Why is the fight-or-flight response sometimes called maladaptive today?
    • It no longer occurs
    • Modern stressors are rarely physical
    • It slows the heart
    • It only occurs in sleep
    Show the answer

    Answer: Modern stressors are rarely physical. Repeated arousal to psychological stressors may harm health without helping.

Key terms

Sympathetic division
The autonomic branch that prepares the body for action
Parasympathetic division
The autonomic branch that conserves energy and supports rest and digestion
Adrenaline
A hormone from the adrenal medulla that amplifies the fight-or-flight response
