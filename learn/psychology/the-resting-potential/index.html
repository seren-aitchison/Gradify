The Resting Potential

The resting potential (about −70 mV) comes from uneven ion distribution and potassium leak, maintained by the sodium–potassium pump.

Psychology · Biological Psychology and Neuroscience · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define the resting potential and give its typical value
  • Explain how ion distribution and the sodium–potassium pump maintain it
  • Apply the resting potential to explain readiness to fire

A charged membrane

The inside of a resting neurone is negative compared with the outside, typically about −70 millivolts (mV). This difference is the resting potential. The membrane is said to be polarised.

How it is maintained

Ions are unevenly distributed. There are more sodium ions (Na⁺) outside the cell and more potassium ions (K⁺) inside, plus large negatively charged proteins that can't leave. At rest the membrane is much more permeable to K⁺ than to Na⁺, so some K⁺ leaks out, leaving the inside more negative.

The sodium–potassium pump uses energy (ATP) to move 3 Na⁺ out for every 2 K⁺ in, keeping the gradients steady. This takes a large share of the brain's energy, which is one reason the brain uses about a fifth of the body's energy at rest despite being about 2% of its weight.

Worked example

A charged battery

  1. Think of the resting neurone like a charged battery: energy is stored in the difference across the membrane.
  2. When ion channels open, ions rush down their gradients, releasing that stored energy as an electrical signal.
  3. The pump then recharges the battery.
Common mistakeThinking the resting neurone is inactive. It spends energy constantly to stay ready.

In 2 lines: At rest, the inside of a neurone is about −70 mV relative to outside. Ion gradients, potassium leak and the sodium–potassium pump maintain it.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. At rest, the inside of a neurone is…
    • Positive relative to outside
    • Negative relative to outside
    • Neutral
    • Filled with sodium
    Show the answer

    Answer: Negative relative to outside. The resting potential is about −70 mV: the inside is more negative.

  2. At rest, which ion is most concentrated inside the neurone?
    • Sodium
    • Chloride
    • Potassium
    • Calcium
    Show the answer

    Answer: Potassium. Potassium is concentrated inside the cell and sodium outside it.

  3. Why does the brain use a large share of the body's energy at rest?
    • Neurones are always firing at maximum
    • The brain is very heavy
    • Glial cells burn sugar for heat
    • Pumps work constantly to keep gradients
    Show the answer

    Answer: Pumps work constantly to keep gradients. Maintaining ion gradients is energetically expensive.

Key terms

Resting potential
The charge across a neurone's membrane at rest, about −70 mV
Polarised
Having a charge difference across the membrane
Sodium–potassium pump
A membrane protein using energy to move 3 Na⁺ out and 2 K⁺ in
