Human–computer Interaction

HCI applies psychology to design usable, accessible technology through heuristics, user-centred design and testing; dark patterns exploit biases.

Psychology · Applied Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain usability and its main components
  • Describe user-centred design and usability testing
  • Evaluate design choices, including dark patterns and accessibility

Usability

Usability is how effectively, efficiently and satisfyingly people can use a product to achieve their goals (ISO 9241-11). Nielsen (1994) proposed heuristics such as visibility of system status, match between system and real world, user control, consistency, error prevention and recognition rather than recall. Applying psychology, good interfaces reduce working memory load and use familiar mental models.

User-centred design

User-centred design involves understanding users' needs, designing iteratively, and testing with real users. Usability testing asks people to complete tasks while researchers observe difficulties; A/B testing compares versions with live users. Nielsen and Landauer (1993) argued that testing with about five users can reveal many usability problems in each round, though more are needed for diverse users.

Worked example

Dark patterns

  1. Dark patterns are designs that steer users into choices they may not want, such as hiding the cancel button for subscriptions or pre-ticking consent boxes.
  2. They exploit cognitive biases such as defaults and limited attention.
  3. Regulators, including the UK Competition and Markets Authority, have warned businesses about harmful online choice architecture.

Accessibility

Accessible design ensures people with disabilities can use technology, for example through screen-reader compatibility, captions, sufficient colour contrast and keyboard navigation. The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) set standards, and UK public sector websites must meet accessibility regulations. Good accessibility often improves usability for everyone.

Common mistakeAssuming "user error" means the user is at fault. Poor design often causes errors.

In 2 lines: Usability depends on effectiveness, efficiency and satisfaction; heuristics and user testing improve it. Dark patterns exploit biases; accessible design benefits everyone.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Showing a loading bar while a file uploads follows the heuristic of…
    • Prevention of user errors
    • Consistency and standards
    • Flexibility and efficiency
    • Visibility of system status
    Show the answer

    Answer: Visibility of system status. Users should know what the system is doing.

  2. A subscription is easy to start but the cancel option is hidden several menus deep. This is…
    • A dark pattern
    • Good accessibility
    • A usability heuristic
    • Error prevention
    Show the answer

    Answer: A dark pattern. The design steers users against their interests.

  3. Why test interfaces with real users?
    • Designers know everything
    • Designers can't predict all problems
    • Users never make errors
    • Testing slows nothing
    Show the answer

    Answer: Designers can't predict all problems. Observing users reveals unexpected difficulties.

Key terms

Usability
How effectively, efficiently and satisfyingly people can use a product
Dark pattern
A design that steers users into unwanted choices
Accessibility
Designing so people with disabilities can use technology
