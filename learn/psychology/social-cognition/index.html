Social Cognition

Social cognition relies on schemas and a mix of automatic and controlled thinking, which is efficient but can bias judgement.

Psychology · Social Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define social cognition and explain the role of schemas
  • Distinguish automatic and controlled social thinking
  • Apply social cognition concepts to everyday judgements of people

Making sense of the social world

Social cognition studies how we notice, interpret, remember and use information about people. Because the social world is complex, we rely on schemas: organised knowledge about people (person schemas), roles (a doctor, a teacher), events (scripts, such as how a restaurant meal unfolds) and ourselves (self-schemas). Schemas speed up judgement but can bias what we notice and remember.

Automatic and controlled thinking

Much social thinking is automatic: fast, effortless and often unconscious. Controlled thinking is slow, effortful and deliberate. Bargh, Chen and Burrows (1996) reported that participants primed with words linked to old age walked more slowly afterwards, though later replications failed to find the effect (Doyen et al., 2012), and the priming literature has been heavily questioned.

Worked example

Cognitive misers and motivated tacticians

  1. Fiske and Taylor described people as cognitive misers who conserve mental effort by using shortcuts.
  2. Later they described people as motivated tacticians, who choose shortcuts or careful thought depending on goals.
  3. For example, a manager may glance at CVs quickly, but think carefully about a candidate they will work with closely.

Social judgements also use heuristics. The false consensus effect is overestimating how far others share our views. The spotlight effect is overestimating how much others notice us: Gilovich et al. (2000) found students wearing an embarrassing T-shirt thought about half of observers noticed, when about a quarter did.

Common mistakeAssuming famous priming findings are solid. Several high-profile social priming effects haven't replicated.

In 2 lines: Social cognition uses schemas and heuristics to make sense of people. Thinking is often automatic, sometimes controlled; some famous priming effects haven't replicated.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Overestimating how many people share your opinions is the…
    • Spotlight effect
    • Halo effect
    • False consensus effect
    • Primacy effect
    Show the answer

    Answer: False consensus effect. We assume others think as we do more often than they do.

  2. A new employee assumes meetings will start with small talk, then an agenda. Which concept is this?
    • A self-schema
    • The spotlight effect
    • Controlled thinking
    • A script
    Show the answer

    Answer: A script. Expectations about event sequences are scripts.

  3. Why is the "motivated tactician" view more complete than the "cognitive miser" view?
    • People switch strategy depending on goals
    • People never use shortcuts
    • Shortcuts are always wrong
    • Thinking is always controlled
    Show the answer

    Answer: People switch strategy depending on goals. People choose effortful or quick thinking as needed.

Key terms

Social cognition
How people think about themselves, others and social situations
Script
A schema for a familiar sequence of events
Cognitive miser
The idea that people conserve mental effort by using shortcuts
