Formulation

Formulation is a collaborative, revisable explanation of difficulties; the 5 Ps framework covers presenting, predisposing, precipitating, perpetuating and protective factors; it complements diagnosis.

Psychology · Clinical Psychology and Interventions · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define psychological formulation
  • Apply the 5 Ps framework to a case
  • Evaluate formulation compared with diagnosis
Competence and safetyThis lesson is educational and is not a guide to diagnosis or therapy. Formulation in clinical practice should only be carried out by qualified, registered professionals.

What is formulation?

A formulation is a hypothesis, developed with the person, that explains how their difficulties came about and what keeps them going, drawing on psychological theory and the person's own story. It guides intervention and is revised as understanding develops. The British Psychological Society's Division of Clinical Psychology describes formulation as a core skill of clinical psychologists.

The 5 Ps

A common framework is the 5 Ps: Presenting problems (what the difficulties are), Predisposing factors (what made the person vulnerable), Precipitating factors (what triggered the difficulties), Perpetuating factors (what keeps them going) and Protective factors (strengths and supports).

Worked example

A 5 Ps example

  1. Presenting: low mood and withdrawal. Predisposing: harsh criticism in childhood and a family history of depression.
  2. Precipitating: redundancy six months ago. Perpetuating: staying in bed, self-critical thinking, avoiding friends.
  3. Protective: a supportive sibling and enjoyment of walking. This example is illustrative.

Evaluation

Formulation is individual, collaborative and directly links understanding to intervention. It can reduce stigma by explaining difficulties as understandable responses. But formulations may vary between clinicians, are hard to test for reliability, and depend on the theories chosen. Many services use formulation alongside diagnosis.

Common mistakeTreating formulation as fixed. It is a working hypothesis revised over time.

In 2 lines: Formulation explains difficulties collaboratively and guides help. The 5 Ps; formulation complements diagnosis but varies between clinicians.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Something that keeps difficulties going is a…
    • Predisposing factor
    • Perpetuating factor
    • Precipitating factor
    • Protective factor
    Show the answer

    Answer: Perpetuating factor. Perpetuating factors maintain difficulties.

  2. In a formulation, losing a job just before low mood started would be listed as a…
    • Predisposing factor
    • Protective factor
    • Precipitating factor
    • Perpetuating factor
    Show the answer

    Answer: Precipitating factor. Events that trigger the onset of difficulties are precipitating factors.

  3. What is a limitation of formulation?
    • They are always identical between clinicians
    • They never guide intervention at all
    • They exclude the person's own view
    • They may vary between clinicians and are hard to test
    Show the answer

    Answer: They may vary between clinicians and are hard to test. Reliability of formulation is hard to establish.

Key terms

Formulation
A shared explanation of how difficulties developed and are maintained
Perpetuating factor
Something that keeps difficulties going
Protective factor
A strength or support that helps
