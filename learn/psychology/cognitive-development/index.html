Cognitive Development

Piaget described stages of self-constructed understanding; Vygotsky stressed social support; later research shows children are often more capable than Piaget's tasks suggested.

Psychology · Cognitive Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe Piaget's stages of cognitive development and key concepts
  • Explain Vygotsky's zone of proximal development and scaffolding
  • Evaluate Piaget's and Vygotsky's theories using later evidence

Piaget

Piaget argued children actively construct knowledge through schemas, adapted by assimilation (fitting new experience into existing schemas) and accommodation (changing schemas to fit new experience). He proposed four stages:

  • Sensorimotor (0–2 years): learning through senses and actions; object permanence develops.
  • Pre-operational (2–7): language and symbolic thought, but egocentrism and lack of conservation.
  • Concrete operational (7–11): logical thinking about concrete things; conservation achieved.
  • Formal operational (11+): abstract and hypothetical reasoning.
Worked example

Conservation and its critics

  1. In Piaget's conservation task, a child sees two identical rows of counters, then one row is spread out. Pre-operational children say the longer row has more.
  2. McGarrigle and Donaldson (1974) had a "naughty teddy" spread the counters "by accident". Many more 4–6-year-olds then said the number was the same.
  3. Children may understand more than Piaget thought; his tasks may have confused them.

Vygotsky

Vygotsky emphasised social and cultural influences. The zone of proximal development (ZPD) is the gap between what a child can do alone and what they can do with help from a more knowledgeable other. Scaffolding (Wood, Bruner and Ross, 1976) is support adjusted to the child's needs and gradually withdrawn. Language is a key tool of thought: children's private speech becomes inner speech.

Common mistakeTreating Piaget's stage ages as fixed. Development varies, and abilities emerge earlier with child-friendly tasks.

In 2 lines: Piaget proposed four stages driven by assimilation and accommodation. Vygotsky stressed the ZPD and scaffolding; later research suggests children's abilities appear earlier.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Understanding that objects exist when out of sight is…
    • Conservation
    • Egocentrism
    • Object permanence
    • Accommodation
    Show the answer

    Answer: Object permanence. Object permanence develops in the sensorimotor stage.

  2. A teacher gives hints on a maths problem, then fewer hints as the pupil improves. This is…
    • Assimilation
    • Egocentrism
    • Conservation
    • Scaffolding
    Show the answer

    Answer: Scaffolding. Gradually withdrawn support is scaffolding.

  3. What does the "naughty teddy" study suggest about Piaget's methods?
    • His tasks may have underestimated children
    • Children can't conserve before 11
    • Piaget overestimated children
    • Teddy bears distract children
    Show the answer

    Answer: His tasks may have underestimated children. Children may have misread the adult's intentions in the original task.

Key terms

Accommodation
Changing schemas to fit new experience
Conservation
Understanding that quantity stays the same despite changes in appearance
Zone of proximal development
The gap between what a learner can do alone and with help
