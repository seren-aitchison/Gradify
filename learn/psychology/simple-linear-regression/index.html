Simple Linear Regression

Regression predicts an outcome from a predictor with ŷ = a + bx; the slope gives the change per unit, and R² the proportion of variance explained.

Psychology · Statistics · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the regression equation and the meaning of its slope and intercept
  • Use a regression equation to make a prediction
  • Evaluate regression predictions, including extrapolation and causation

The regression line

Regression finds the line ŷ = a + bx that best predicts the outcome (y) from the predictor (x), by minimising the squared distances of the points from the line (least squares). The slope (b) is the predicted change in y for each one-unit increase in x. The intercept (a) is the predicted y when x = 0. Residuals are the differences between actual and predicted values.

Worked example

Predicting test scores from revision

  1. With the data 1–5 revision hours and scores 2, 4, 5, 4, 5, the least-squares line is ŷ = 2.2 + 0.6x.
  2. Each extra hour predicts 0.6 more marks; the intercept 2.2 is the predicted score with no revision.
  3. For 3.5 hours: ŷ = 2.2 + 0.6 × 3.5 = 4.3. R² = .60: the line accounts for 60% of the variance.

R² in simple regression equals r² from the correlation of the same two variables, so the two analyses tell the same story about strength.

Investigate with the lab. Add your own points and watch the regression line and R² change.

Cautions

Predictions are reliable only within the range of the data: extrapolating (predicting a score for 40 hours of revision) can be absurd. A regression slope doesn't show causation unless the predictor was manipulated. Residual plots should be checked for curves, outliers and uneven spread. The term "regression" comes from Galton's (1886) observation that children of very tall parents tend to be less extreme: regression towards the mean.

Common mistakeTreating the predictor as a cause. Regression describes prediction; causation needs a suitable design.

In 2 lines: Regression fits ŷ = a + bx by least squares; the slope and intercept make predictions. R² shows variance explained; avoid extrapolation and causal claims without a suitable design.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. In ŷ = 10 + 3x, what is the intercept?
    • 3
    • 10
    • 13
    • x
    Show the answer

    Answer: 10. The intercept is the constant term, the prediction when x = 0.

  2. A regression of happiness on income is based on incomes from £15,000 to £60,000. Why shouldn't it be used to predict happiness at £2 million?
    • The slope would be negative
    • Regression can't use money
    • That is extrapolation beyond the data
    • The intercept would be zero
    Show the answer

    Answer: That is extrapolation beyond the data. Predictions outside the observed range may not follow the same line.

  3. Pupils with the lowest test scores improve most on retest, with no intervention. What explains this?
    • A strong treatment effect
    • Restricted range
    • A Type II error
    • Regression towards the mean
    Show the answer

    Answer: Regression towards the mean. Extreme scores partly reflect chance and tend to be less extreme next time.

  4. What does R² tell you in simple linear regression?
    • The proportion of variance in one variable explained by the other
    • The slope of the regression line
    • The number of data points
    • Whether the relationship is causal
    Show the answer

    Answer: The proportion of variance in one variable explained by the other. R² of 0.64 means 64% of the variance is explained.

Key terms

Slope
The predicted change in the outcome for each one-unit increase in the predictor
Intercept
The predicted outcome when the predictor equals zero
Residual
The difference between an observed value and the value predicted by the regression line
