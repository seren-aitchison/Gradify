Data Visualisation

Choose graphs that match the data and show distributions, not just means; always check scales and axes.

Psychology · Statistics · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Choose an appropriate graph for different types of data
  • Interpret histograms, box plots and scatterplots
  • Evaluate graphs for misleading features

Choosing a graph

  • Bar chart: categories (nominal data) or comparing group means, with error bars.
  • Histogram: the distribution of a continuous variable, bars touching.
  • Box plot: median, IQR and outliers; good for comparing distributions.
  • Scatterplot: the relationship between two continuous variables.
  • Line graph: change over time or across ordered conditions.

Show the data

Bar charts of means hide the distribution: very different data sets can produce the same bars. Plotting individual data points, or using box or violin plots, lets readers see spread, skew and outliers. Anscombe's quartet (1973) showed four data sets with near-identical summary statistics but very different scatterplots.

Worked example

Two bar charts, one story

  1. Group A: all participants score 50. Group B: half score 20, half score 80.
  2. Both have a mean of 50, so the bar chart looks identical.
  3. A dot plot reveals that Group B is split into two clusters, which may matter more than the mean.

Investigate with the lab. Paste in a data set and look at its histogram; then think about which graph suits each kind of data.

How graphs mislead

Truncated y-axes exaggerate small differences; missing error bars hide uncertainty; 3D effects distort size; and cherry-picked time ranges create false trends. Readers should check axis labels, scales and what the bars or error bars represent.

Common mistakeStarting a bar chart's y-axis well above zero. It makes small differences look large.

In 2 lines: Bar charts suit categories; histograms distributions; box plots comparisons; scatterplots relationships. Showing individual data and checking axes prevent misleading impressions.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which graph best shows the distribution of reaction times?
    • A histogram
    • A pie chart
    • A scatterplot
    • A table of means
    Show the answer

    Answer: A histogram. Histograms show the shape of a continuous distribution.

  2. A bar chart's y-axis runs from 48 to 52, making a difference of 1 point look huge. What is the problem?
    • Error bars are too large
    • A truncated axis
    • It should be a scatterplot
    • The bars should be 3D
    Show the answer

    Answer: A truncated axis. Starting the axis far above zero magnifies small differences.

  3. Why do critics argue against bar charts of means for continuous data?
    • They are hard to draw
    • They can't show more than two groups
    • They hide distributions, outliers and sample size
    • They always use a truncated axis
    Show the answer

    Answer: They hide distributions, outliers and sample size. Plotting points or distributions reveals what bars conceal.

  4. Which graph suits continuous data such as reaction times?
    • A bar chart of categories
    • A pie chart
    • A scattergram of one variable
    • A histogram
    Show the answer

    Answer: A histogram. Histograms show the distribution of continuous data.

Key terms

Histogram
A graph showing the distribution of a continuous variable, with touching bars
Box plot
A graph showing the median, interquartile range and outliers
Scatterplot
A graph of pairs of values showing the relationship between two variables
