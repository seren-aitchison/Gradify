Occupational Psychology

Occupational psychology applies psychology to work in selection, development, wellbeing, design and leadership; it developed from scientific management to human relations.

Psychology · Applied Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the scope and roles of occupational psychology
  • Explain the historical development from scientific management to human relations
  • Evaluate the contribution of psychology to the workplace

What occupational psychologists do

Occupational (work or organisational) psychology applies psychology to people at work. In the UK, occupational psychologists are registered with the Health and Care Professions Council. The BPS has described key areas including selection and assessment, learning and development, wellbeing at work, work design, leadership and organisational change.

From Taylor to human relations

Scientific management (Taylor, 1911) broke jobs into simple, timed tasks and used pay incentives to maximise efficiency, treating workers like parts of a machine. The human relations movement, influenced by the Hawthorne studies at the Western Electric company in the 1920s and 1930s, emphasised social relationships, attention and group norms in productivity.

Worked example

The Hawthorne studies

  1. Researchers changed lighting and working conditions for workers at the Hawthorne plant and observed productivity.
  2. Output seemed to rise with almost any change, which was interpreted as workers responding to being observed (the Hawthorne effect).
  3. Later reanalysis (Levitt and List, 2011) found the original lighting data showed weaker evidence of this effect than commonly claimed.

Evaluation

Occupational psychology has improved selection methods, job design and wellbeing practices. Critics argue it has sometimes served employers' interests over workers', for example by focusing on productivity, and that interventions don't always address structural issues such as low pay or insecure contracts.

Common mistakeRepeating the Hawthorne effect as settled fact. Reanalysis suggests the original evidence was weaker than often claimed.

In 2 lines: Occupational psychology covers selection, development, wellbeing, work design and leadership. Taylor's scientific management gave way to human relations; the Hawthorne effect is debated.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Who developed scientific management?
    • Elton Mayo
    • Frederick Taylor
    • Abraham Maslow
    • Douglas McGregor
    Show the answer

    Answer: Frederick Taylor. Taylor published his principles in 1911, timing tasks for efficiency.

  2. Productivity rises when workers know researchers are watching. This is called…
    • Scientific management
    • Burnout
    • The Hawthorne effect
    • Job enrichment
    Show the answer

    Answer: The Hawthorne effect. Behaviour changing under observation is the Hawthorne effect.

  3. What is a criticism of occupational psychology?
    • It ignores productivity
    • It has no methods
    • It never studies wellbeing
    • It may favour employers' interests
    Show the answer

    Answer: It may favour employers' interests. Critics argue it can prioritise productivity over workers' needs.

Key terms

Scientific management
Taylor's approach of simplifying and timing tasks to maximise efficiency
Human relations movement
An approach emphasising social relationships and group norms at work
Hawthorne effect
Changes in behaviour because people know they are being observed
