Subjective Wellbeing

Subjective wellbeing combines life satisfaction, positive and negative affect; the ONS measures four aspects; self-reports are useful but sensitive to context.

Psychology · Motivation, Emotion and Wellbeing · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Define subjective wellbeing and its components
  • Describe how the ONS measures personal wellbeing in the UK
  • Evaluate the reliability and usefulness of self-reported wellbeing

Components

Diener (1984) defined subjective wellbeing (SWB) as people's own evaluations of their lives. It has three components: life satisfaction (a cognitive judgement of life as a whole), positive affect (frequent pleasant emotions) and low negative affect (infrequent unpleasant emotions). Eudaimonic approaches add a sense of meaning and purpose, as opposed to purely hedonic pleasure.

Measuring national wellbeing

Since 2011, the ONS has asked four questions in UK surveys, each rated from 0 to 10: How satisfied are you with your life nowadays? To what extent do you feel the things you do in your life are worthwhile? How happy did you feel yesterday? How anxious did you feel yesterday? The ONS reported that average ratings declined across all four measures in the year ending March 2023, and that self-reported health made the largest contribution to differences in wellbeing (ONS, 2023).

Worked example

The day reconstruction method

  1. Kahneman et al. (2004) asked 909 working women in Texas to reconstruct the previous day as a series of episodes and rate their feelings in each.
  2. Socialising and relaxing were rated most pleasant; commuting and working were among the least.
  3. The method captures experienced wellbeing across the day, which can differ from overall life evaluations.

Evaluation

Self-reports of wellbeing show reasonable reliability and correlate with reports by friends and family, health and behaviour. But they are influenced by mood at the time of asking, question order and cultural norms about expressing happiness. Many governments now use wellbeing alongside GDP to evaluate progress.

Common mistakeTreating wellbeing as one single number. Life satisfaction, daily happiness, meaning and anxiety can differ.

In 2 lines: SWB includes life satisfaction and positive and negative affect. The ONS uses four questions; self-reports are useful but context-sensitive.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. A cognitive judgement of life as a whole is…
    • Life satisfaction
    • Positive affect
    • Negative affect
    • Flow
    Show the answer

    Answer: Life satisfaction. Life satisfaction is an overall evaluation.

  2. A person feels their work is meaningful even though it is often stressful. This reflects…
    • Hedonic wellbeing only
    • Eudaimonic wellbeing
    • Negative affect only
    • The day reconstruction method
    Show the answer

    Answer: Eudaimonic wellbeing. Meaning and purpose, even alongside stress, reflect eudaimonic wellbeing.

  3. Why might wellbeing self-reports be influenced by context?
    • They are perfectly stable
    • People never answer honestly
    • Mood and question order affect answers
    • Context has no effect
    Show the answer

    Answer: Mood and question order affect answers. Current mood and framing can shift ratings.

Key terms

Subjective wellbeing
People's own evaluations and experiences of their lives
Hedonic wellbeing
Wellbeing as pleasure and satisfaction
Eudaimonic wellbeing
Wellbeing as meaning, purpose and fulfilment
