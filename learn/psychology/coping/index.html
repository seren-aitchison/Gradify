Coping

Coping can be problem-focused or emotion-focused; matching coping to controllability helps; social support strongly predicts health; coping depends on resources.

Psychology · Motivation, Emotion and Wellbeing · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Distinguish problem-focused and emotion-focused coping
  • Explain the role of social support in coping
  • Evaluate the effectiveness of different coping strategies

Two main types

Lazarus and Folkman (1984) distinguished problem-focused coping (tackling the stressor directly: planning, seeking information, taking action) from emotion-focused coping (managing the emotional response: reappraisal, seeking comfort, acceptance, distraction). Problem-focused coping tends to work best when a situation is controllable; emotion-focused coping can be more helpful when it is uncontrollable, such as bereavement.

Avoidance and approach

Some emotion-focused strategies, such as avoidance, denial and substance use, are associated with poorer outcomes over time. Approach strategies, such as acceptance, positive reappraisal and seeking support, tend to be more helpful. The goodness of fit hypothesis proposes that coping works best when it matches the controllability of the stressor.

Worked example

Social support

  1. Social support can be instrumental (practical help), emotional (care and listening) or informational (advice).
  2. Holt-Lunstad, Smith and Layton (2010) meta-analysed 148 studies and found stronger social relationships were associated with a 50% increased likelihood of survival over the study periods.
  3. Social connection is linked to health outcomes, partly by buffering stress.

Interventions

Stress inoculation training (Meichenbaum, 1985) teaches people to reconceptualise stressors, learn coping skills and practise them in increasingly difficult situations. Cognitive behavioural stress management and exercise also show benefits. Coping depends on resources: money, time and support affect which strategies are available.

Common mistakeAssuming emotion-focused coping is always worse. It can be the best approach when a stressor can't be changed.

In 2 lines: Problem-focused coping suits controllable stressors; emotion-focused suits uncontrollable ones. Social support is protective; stress inoculation and other interventions help; resources shape coping.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Making a revision timetable to tackle exam stress is…
    • Emotion-focused coping
    • Avoidance
    • Problem-focused coping
    • Suppression
    Show the answer

    Answer: Problem-focused coping. Tackling the stressor directly is problem-focused.

  2. After a bereavement, a person focuses on accepting the loss and talking with friends. This is…
    • Problem-focused coping
    • Avoidance
    • Drive reduction
    • Emotion-focused coping
    Show the answer

    Answer: Emotion-focused coping. Managing emotions suits an uncontrollable stressor.

  3. What does the goodness of fit hypothesis propose?
    • Coping works best when matched to controllability
    • One strategy suits all stressors
    • Coping never matters
    • Avoidance is best
    Show the answer

    Answer: Coping works best when matched to controllability. Matching strategy to situation improves outcomes.
