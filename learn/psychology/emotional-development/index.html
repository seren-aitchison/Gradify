Emotional Development

Basic emotions appear in the first year, self-conscious emotions in the second and third; children use others' emotions to guide action and gradually learn to regulate their own.

Psychology · Developmental Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the development of basic and self-conscious emotions
  • Explain social referencing using the visual cliff evidence
  • Explain how emotion regulation develops and why it matters

Emerging emotions

Newborns show distress, contentment and interest. The social smile appears around 6–8 weeks. Basic emotions such as joy, anger, sadness, fear and surprise appear during the first year; fear of strangers often appears around 8–10 months. Self-conscious emotions such as embarrassment, pride, guilt and shame appear in the second and third years, once children recognise themselves as separate people. In the rouge test, most children touch a red mark on their own nose when seeing it in a mirror by about 18–24 months (Lewis and Brooks-Gunn, 1979).

Reading others' emotions

Worked example

Social referencing on the visual cliff

  1. Sorce et al. (1985) placed 12-month-olds on a visual cliff with a moderate apparent drop and their mothers on the far side.
  2. When mothers posed a happy face, most infants (about 74%) crossed; when mothers posed fear, none did.
  3. Infants used their mothers' emotional expressions to decide how to act in an uncertain situation: social referencing.

Emotion regulation

Emotion regulation is managing the intensity and expression of feelings. Infants rely on caregivers to soothe them (co-regulation); toddlers start to use simple strategies like looking away; school-age children increasingly use thinking strategies ("it's only a film"). Children learn display rules, such as hiding disappointment at a gift. Good emotion regulation is associated with better social and academic outcomes.

Temperament, a child's characteristic emotional style, also shapes emotional development. Thomas and Chess (1977) described easy, difficult and slow-to-warm-up children. The goodness of fit between a child's temperament and the caregiving environment matters more than temperament alone.

Common mistakeAssuming young children can regulate emotions like adults. Regulation develops slowly and depends at first on caregivers.

In 2 lines: Basic emotions emerge in the first year; self-conscious emotions follow self-recognition. Social referencing guides infants' actions; regulation develops from co-regulation to self-regulation.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Around what age does the social smile appear?
    • 6 months
    • 2 years
    • 6–8 weeks
    • At birth
    Show the answer

    Answer: 6–8 weeks. The social smile emerges in the first two months.

  2. A toddler touches a red mark on her nose after seeing it in a mirror. What does this show?
    • Social referencing
    • Emotion regulation
    • Stranger anxiety
    • Self-recognition
    Show the answer

    Answer: Self-recognition. Touching her own nose shows she recognises herself.

  3. Why do self-conscious emotions appear later than basic emotions?
    • They require self-awareness
    • They are less intense
    • They are learned at school
    • Infants can't feel anything
    Show the answer

    Answer: They require self-awareness. Pride and guilt depend on seeing oneself as separate and evaluated.

Key terms

Social referencing
Using others' emotional reactions to guide one's own behaviour
Self-conscious emotions
Emotions such as pride and guilt that depend on self-awareness
Emotion regulation
Managing the intensity and expression of emotions
