Brain Anatomy: an Overview

The brain has cortex, subcortical structures, brainstem and cerebellum; anatomical terms describe locations; regions work as networks.

Psychology · Biological Psychology and Neuroscience · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Identify the main divisions and structures of the brain
  • Use anatomical terms to describe locations in the brain
  • Explain why the brain is best understood as interacting networks

Main structures

  • Cerebrum: the two cerebral hemispheres, covered by the folded cerebral cortex; responsible for perception, thought, language and voluntary action.
  • Corpus callosum: a thick band of axons linking the two hemispheres.
  • Subcortical structures: the thalamus, hypothalamus, basal ganglia and limbic structures beneath the cortex.
  • Brainstem: midbrain, pons and medulla, controlling vital functions and relaying signals.
  • Cerebellum: at the back, under the cerebrum, coordinating movement and balance.

Describing locations

Anatomists use direction terms. Anterior (front) and posterior (back); superior or dorsal (top) and inferior or ventral (bottom); medial (towards the middle) and lateral (towards the side). Brain images are often shown in three planes: sagittal (side view), coronal (front view) and axial or horizontal (top view).

The cortex is folded into ridges (gyri) and grooves (sulci), which greatly increase its surface area. The deep central sulcus separates the frontal and parietal lobes; the lateral fissure separates the temporal lobe from the frontal and parietal lobes.

Worked example

Networks, not just spots

  1. Reading a word aloud involves visual areas (seeing it), language areas (recognising it), motor areas (speaking) and attention networks.
  2. Brain imaging shows many regions active together for most tasks.
  3. Regions specialise, but behaviour emerges from their interaction.

Investigate with the lab. Play the identification game on the side and midline views until you can find each major region without hints.

Common mistakeAssuming each behaviour has a single brain "centre". Most behaviours depend on distributed networks.

In 2 lines: The brain comprises cortex, subcortical structures, brainstem and cerebellum. Direction and plane terms describe locations; behaviour depends on networks.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which structure coordinates movement and balance?
    • Corpus callosum
    • Medulla
    • Thalamus
    • Cerebellum
    Show the answer

    Answer: Cerebellum. The cerebellum sits at the back, below the cerebrum.

  2. A brain scan shows the brain from the side, cut down the middle. Which plane is this?
    • Sagittal
    • Coronal
    • Axial
    • Lateral
    Show the answer

    Answer: Sagittal. A sagittal section divides left from right.

  3. Which part of the brain coordinates balance and fine movement?
    • The hippocampus
    • The cerebellum
    • The occipital lobe
    • The hypothalamus
    Show the answer

    Answer: The cerebellum. The cerebellum fine-tunes movement and balance.

Key terms

Cerebral cortex
The folded outer layer of the cerebrum
Corpus callosum
The band of axons connecting the two hemispheres
Gyrus and sulcus
A ridge and a groove on the surface of the cortex
