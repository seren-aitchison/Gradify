Psychology and the Law

Psychology has shaped line-up procedures, interviewing (PEACE) and understanding of false confessions and juries, though ecological validity limits some findings.

Psychology · Applied Psychology · about 2 minutes · sources checked 2026-11

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain psychological research on eyewitness identification procedures
  • Explain research on investigative interviewing and false confessions
  • Evaluate research on jury decision-making

Identification procedures

Mistaken eyewitness identification is a leading factor in wrongful convictions. Research has shaped identification procedures: line-ups should be conducted double-blind (the officer doesn't know which person is the suspect), include fillers who match the description, and warn witnesses the offender may not be present. In England and Wales, PACE Code D governs identification, now mostly by video identification (VIPER).

Interviewing and false confessions

Some people confess to crimes they didn't commit, especially under pressure, when vulnerable (young, with learning disabilities or mental health conditions) or after long interrogation (Kassin et al., 2010). In England and Wales, the PEACE model (Planning and preparation, Engage and explain, Account, Closure, Evaluation), introduced in the 1990s, replaced confrontational questioning with information-gathering interviews, informed by research on false confessions such as the Guildford Four case.

Worked example

Mock juries

  1. Researchers use mock juries to study decision-making, since real jury deliberations in England and Wales can't legally be researched in detail.
  2. Thomas (2010), working with the Ministry of Justice using case simulations with real jurors at court, found all-white juries didn't discriminate against Black and Asian defendants in the simulated cases.
  3. Mock jury research raises questions of ecological validity because decisions have no real consequences.

Evaluation

Psychological research has improved legal procedures, but courts change slowly, research settings differ from real cases, and some reforms are applied inconsistently. Collaboration between psychologists, police and lawyers has been productive, as in the development of PEACE and the cognitive interview.

Common mistakeAssuming confessions are always reliable. People can falsely confess under pressure, especially when vulnerable.

In 2 lines: Research shaped line-up procedures (PACE Code D, double-blind, warnings). PEACE replaced coercive interviewing; false confessions and jury research inform the law.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Which code governs identification procedures in England and Wales?
    • The Equality Act
    • The Children Act
    • The SEND Code
    • PACE Code D
    Show the answer

    Answer: PACE Code D. PACE Code D covers identification procedures.

  2. A young suspect with a learning disability admits a crime after hours of pressured questioning, but evidence later clears him. This illustrates…
    • A false confession
    • A double-blind line-up
    • The PEACE model
    • Geographical profiling
    Show the answer

    Answer: A false confession. Vulnerability and pressure increase false confession risk.

  3. Why is mock jury research limited?
    • It uses real trials
    • Decisions have no real consequences
    • It is never controlled
    • Juries always agree
    Show the answer

    Answer: Decisions have no real consequences. Decisions without real consequences reduce the ecological validity of mock juries.

Key terms

Double-blind line-up
A line-up where the administrator doesn't know which person is the suspect
PEACE model
An information-gathering interview model used by police in England and Wales
False confession
An admission of guilt for a crime one didn't commit
