Retrieval Failure

Forgetting often reflects missing retrieval cues; reinstating the context or state of learning aids recall.

Psychology · Cognitive Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain retrieval failure and the encoding specificity principle
  • Describe research on context-dependent and state-dependent forgetting
  • Apply retrieval cues to improve remembering

Cues and encoding specificity

Tulving's encoding specificity principle (Tulving and Thomson, 1973) holds that a cue helps retrieval if it was present when the information was encoded. Information isn't so much lost as unreachable: the tip-of-the-tongue state, when you know you know a word but can't produce it, is a familiar example.

Context and state

Context-dependent forgetting occurs when the external environment at recall differs from that at learning. State-dependent forgetting occurs when the internal state (mood, alertness, intoxication) differs.

Worked example

The divers' study

  1. Godden and Baddeley (1975) asked divers to learn word lists on land or underwater, and recall them on land or underwater.
  2. Recall was about 40% lower when the learning and recall contexts didn't match.
  3. Matching context provided cues that aided retrieval.

Carter and Cassaday (1998) found participants who learned and recalled under the same antihistamine-induced drowsiness, or both while alert, did better than those whose states differed. Effects of context are often smaller in everyday life, where contexts are less distinct, and in recognition tests, which provide their own cues.

Common mistakeOver-applying context effects. They are strongest with very different contexts and weaker for recognition and meaningful material.

In 2 lines: Retrieval failure means memories are stored but inaccessible without cues. Matching context and state aids recall, though everyday effects are often small.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Godden and Baddeley's participants were…
    • Rugby players
    • Taxi drivers
    • Divers
    • Pilots
    Show the answer

    Answer: Divers. Divers learned and recalled words on land or underwater.

  2. A student can't remember a fact in an exam but recalls it walking back into their bedroom. Which explanation fits best?
    • Proactive interference
    • Trace decay
    • Sensory adaptation
    • Context-dependent forgetting
    Show the answer

    Answer: Context-dependent forgetting. Returning to the learning context provided cues.

  3. Why might context effects be small when students revise at home and sit exams in a hall?
    • Home and hall contexts are not very distinct
    • Exams use no cues
    • Students have no memories
    • Context effects are always huge
    Show the answer

    Answer: Home and hall contexts are not very distinct. Everyday contexts differ less than land versus underwater.

Key terms

Encoding specificity
The principle that cues aid retrieval if present at encoding
Context-dependent forgetting
Forgetting when the external setting at recall differs from learning
State-dependent forgetting
Forgetting when the internal state at recall differs from learning
