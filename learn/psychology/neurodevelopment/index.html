Neurodevelopment

The brain develops through synapse formation, pruning and myelination; some development expects typical experience in sensitive periods, and severe deprivation can have lasting effects.

Psychology · Developmental Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Describe the main processes of brain development
  • Explain experience-expectant and experience-dependent development
  • Evaluate evidence on sensitive periods from deprivation studies

Building and refining

After neurogenesis and migration before birth, the brain develops through synaptogenesis (rapid formation of synapses in infancy), pruning (removal of unused synapses, continuing through adolescence) and myelination (insulating axons, which proceeds from back to front and continues into the 20s). Sensory and motor regions mature first; the prefrontal cortex matures last.

Expecting experience

Greenough, Black and Wallace (1987) distinguished experience-expectant development, which relies on experiences almost all members of a species encounter (patterned light, speech sounds) during a sensitive period, from experience-dependent development, which depends on individual experiences throughout life (learning a skill or a language).

Worked example

Kittens and vision

  1. Wiesel and Hubel (1963) covered one eye of kittens during the first months of life.
  2. Afterwards few cells in the visual cortex responded to the deprived eye, and the kittens were functionally blind in it.
  3. The same deprivation in adult cats had little effect, showing a sensitive period for visual development.

Early deprivation in humans

The English and Romanian Adoptees (ERA) study followed children adopted into UK families from severely depriving Romanian institutions in the early 1990s. Those adopted before 6 months showed few lasting problems; those who spent longer in institutions had higher rates of difficulties into young adulthood, including in social relationships, attention and cognition, though many recovered substantially (Sonuga-Barke et al., 2017).

Common mistakeThinking the brain is "finished" in early childhood. Pruning and myelination continue into adulthood.

In 2 lines: Synaptogenesis, pruning and myelination shape the developing brain into the 20s. Experience-expectant and experience-dependent processes differ; deprivation studies show sensitive periods.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. Removing unused synapses is called…
    • Myelination
    • Pruning
    • Synaptogenesis
    • Migration
    Show the answer

    Answer: Pruning. Pruning refines networks by removing unused connections.

  2. Learning to play the violin at 30 changes motor areas of the brain. This is…
    • Experience-expectant development
    • Neurogenesis before birth
    • Experience-dependent development
    • Pruning in infancy
    Show the answer

    Answer: Experience-dependent development. Individual learning at any age is experience-dependent.

  3. Why is the ERA study especially informative?
    • It was a randomised trial
    • It studied only animals
    • It had no follow-up at all
    • Adoption age varied naturally
    Show the answer

    Answer: Adoption age varied naturally. Differences in age at adoption allowed comparison of deprivation lengths.

Key terms

Synaptogenesis
The formation of synapses
Myelination
Insulation of axons with myelin, speeding conduction
Experience-expectant development
Development relying on typical species-wide experiences in sensitive periods
