Behavioural Ecology

Behavioural ecology tests whether behaviour maximises fitness under current conditions, using optimality models; HBE and evolutionary psychology differ in emphasis.

Psychology · Evolutionary Psychology and Behavioural Ecology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain the aims of behavioural ecology and optimality models
  • Apply optimal foraging theory to animal and human examples
  • Compare human behavioural ecology with evolutionary psychology

Behaviour fitted to conditions

Behavioural ecology asks how behaviour helps organisms survive and reproduce in their current environments. It uses optimality models: predicting the behaviour that would maximise fitness given costs and benefits, then testing whether animals behave that way. Organisms are seen as flexible strategists, adjusting behaviour to conditions.

Optimal foraging

Optimal foraging theory predicts animals choose foods and patches that maximise energy gained per unit time. The marginal value theorem (Charnov, 1976) predicts how long an animal should stay in a food patch before moving on as returns diminish. Krebs et al. (1977) found great tits chose prey in ways consistent with optimal foraging predictions.

Worked example

Human foragers

  1. Hawkes, Hill and O'Connell (1982) studied Aché foragers in Paraguay.
  2. The foods they chose broadly matched predictions of optimal foraging models based on energy returns and search time.
  3. Some deviations, such as men hunting large game that's shared widely, suggested additional goals such as status and sharing.

Human behavioural ecology vs evolutionary psychology

Human behavioural ecology (HBE) assumes humans flexibly adjust behaviour to maximise fitness in current environments and tests this with field data, often in small-scale societies. Evolutionary psychology focuses on specific psychological mechanisms adapted to ancestral environments, which may misfit modern conditions. Critics of HBE note modern humans often don't maximise reproduction (for example, low fertility in wealthy societies).

Common mistakeAssuming optimality models claim animals calculate. They predict outcomes of selection, not conscious decisions.

In 2 lines: Behavioural ecology tests whether behaviour is optimal for fitness in current conditions. Optimal foraging is supported in animals and some human foragers; HBE and evolutionary psychology differ.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. The model predicting when to leave a depleting food patch is the…
    • Hamilton's rule
    • Tit-for-tat
    • Prisoner's Dilemma
    • Marginal value theorem
    Show the answer

    Answer: Marginal value theorem. Charnov's marginal value theorem predicts when to leave a depleting patch.

  2. Aché hunters pursue large game that is widely shared, beyond what energy models predict. This suggests…
    • Additional goals such as status
    • Optimal foraging always fails
    • Foraging is random
    • Energy doesn't matter
    Show the answer

    Answer: Additional goals such as status. Deviations from energy predictions point to social goals such as status and sharing.

  3. Why are optimality models useful even if animals don't calculate?
    • They prove animals think
    • They predict outcomes of selection to test
    • They describe conscious plans
    • They avoid testing
    Show the answer

    Answer: They predict outcomes of selection to test. Optimality models generate testable predictions about the outcomes of selection.

Key terms

Behavioural ecology
The study of how behaviour is adapted to current ecological conditions
Optimal foraging theory
The prediction that animals maximise energy gained per unit time when foraging
Marginal value theorem
A model predicting when a forager should leave a depleting patch
