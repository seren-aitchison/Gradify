Piaget's Theory of Cognitive Development

Piaget described children constructing knowledge through stages; later research shows earlier competence and a greater role for social context.

Psychology · Developmental Psychology · about 2 minutes

What you'll be able to do

  • Explain schemas, adaptation and equilibration
  • Describe the key achievements and limits of each stage with Piaget's tasks
  • Evaluate Piaget's theory using later research

Building knowledge

Piaget saw children as active "little scientists". Knowledge is organised in schemas. When experience fits, it is assimilated; when it doesn't, the schema is accommodated. Imbalance between schema and experience (disequilibrium) motivates change until equilibration restores balance at a higher level.

Stages and tasks

  • Sensorimotor (0–2): object permanence emerges around 8 months; Piaget hid a toy under a cloth and found younger infants stopped searching.
  • Pre-operational (2–7): symbolic thought and pretend play; egocentrism shown in the three mountains task (children chose their own view when asked what a doll saw); failure on conservation tasks; centration (focusing on one feature).
  • Concrete operational (7–11): conservation, classification and seriation of concrete objects; reversibility.
  • Formal operational (11+): abstract and hypothetical reasoning, tested by problems such as the pendulum task.
Worked example

Policeman doll

  1. Hughes (1975) asked children to hide a boy doll from two policeman dolls positioned at a crossroads of walls.
  2. Around 90% of 3½–5-year-olds hid it correctly, much better than in the three mountains task.
  3. When a task makes sense to children, they show less egocentrism than Piaget reported.

Evaluation

Strengths: Piaget transformed the study of children's thinking, and his tasks are widely replicated in their original forms. Criticisms: infants show signs of object understanding earlier when tested with looking-time methods (Baillargeon, 1987); child-friendly tasks reveal earlier competence; many adults don't reach formal operations consistently; and Piaget underplayed social and cultural influences. His theory influenced discovery learning in education.

Investigate with the lab. Place Piaget's four stages on the timeline and note the key ability that marks each.

Common mistakeAssuming children fail tasks because they lack the concept. Task demands, language and context can hide what they know.

In 2 lines: Piaget proposed assimilation, accommodation and equilibration driving four stages. Later research shows earlier competence with child-friendly tasks and more social influence than he allowed.

Practice questions

Answer each one in your head before you open it: recalling beats rereading.

  1. The three mountains task tests…
    • Conservation
    • Object permanence
    • Abstract reasoning
    • Egocentrism
    Show the answer

    Answer: Egocentrism. Children choose the doll's view, testing perspective-taking.

  2. A child calls every four-legged animal "doggy", then learns cats are different. Changing the schema is…
    • Accommodation
    • Assimilation
    • Centration
    • Egocentrism
    Show the answer

    Answer: Accommodation. Modifying a schema to fit new information is accommodation.

  3. What is the strongest criticism of Piaget's methods?
    • They used too many children
    • Tasks may underestimate children
    • Tasks were too easy
    • He studied only adults
    Show the answer

    Answer: Tasks may underestimate children. Confusing language and context can mask competence.

  4. In which Piagetian stage does object permanence develop?
    • Preoperational
    • Concrete operational
    • Sensorimotor
    • Formal operational
    Show the answer

    Answer: Sensorimotor. Object permanence develops in the sensorimotor stage, from birth to about two years.

Key terms

Equilibration
Restoring balance between schemas and experience, driving development
Egocentrism
Difficulty taking another's perspective
Centration
Focusing on one feature of a situation while ignoring others
