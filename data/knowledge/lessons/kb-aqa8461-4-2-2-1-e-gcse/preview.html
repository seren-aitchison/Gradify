The human digestive system

Enzymes are biological catalysts: proteins that speed up reactions without being used up.

GCSE

Learning objectives

  • Define enzyme and use the term correctly
  • Explain the active site
  • Explain how an enzyme works: the lock and key model

The idea

Enzymes are biological catalysts: proteins that speed up chemical reactions in living organisms without being used up, so each enzyme molecule can be used again and again.

For example:

  • Amylase breaks starch down into sugars.
  • Proteases break proteins down into amino acids.

Each enzyme has an active site: a region of its surface with a particular shape. The molecule an enzyme acts on, its substrate, binds there.

Enzyme: a protein that acts as a biological catalyst, speeding up a reaction without being used up.

Enzymes are specific

Enzymes are specific: each one catalyses only one reaction, or one type of reaction, because only a substrate whose shape is complementary to its active site can fit into it.

For example:

  • Amylase breaks down starch, but not proteins or fats.

Denaturation

High temperatures and extreme pH change the shape of an enzyme's active site, so the substrate no longer fits and the enzyme stops working. The enzyme is denatured, and this is usually permanent.

How an enzyme works: the lock and key model

The lock and key theory is a simplified model of how an enzyme works: the substrate fits the active site like a key in a lock.

  • The substrate collides with the enzyme's active site.
  • Because their shapes are complementary, the substrate fits into the active site, forming an enzyme-substrate complex.
  • The reaction happens while the substrate is held in the active site: it is broken down or joined to another molecule.
  • The products are a different shape, so they leave the active site.
  • The enzyme is unchanged and its active site is free to bind another substrate molecule.
Common mistake"High temperatures kill enzymes." Enzymes are molecules, not living things, so they can't be killed: heat changes the shape of the active site and denatures them.
Common mistake"An enzyme is used up in the reaction it catalyses." An enzyme is unchanged at the end of the reaction, so it can catalyse the same reaction again and again.
Common mistake"The substrate is the same shape as the active site." The substrate's shape is complementary to the active site, fitting into it the way a key fits a lock, not identical to it.
