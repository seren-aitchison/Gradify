## The human digestive system

H: The idea
P: **Enzymes** are biological catalysts: proteins that speed up chemical reactions in living organisms without being used up, so each enzyme molecule can be used again and again.
P: For example:
- Amylase breaks starch down into sugars.
- Proteases break proteins down into amino acids.
P: Each enzyme has an **active site**: a region of its surface with a particular shape. The molecule an enzyme acts on, its **substrate**, binds there.
K: **Enzyme**: a protein that acts as a biological catalyst, speeding up a reaction without being used up.
H: Enzymes are specific
P: Enzymes are **specific**: each one catalyses only one reaction, or one type of reaction, because only a substrate whose shape is complementary to its active site can fit into it.
P: For example:
- Amylase breaks down starch, but not proteins or fats.
H: Denaturation
P: High temperatures and extreme pH change the shape of an enzyme's active site, so the substrate no longer fits and the enzyme stops working. The enzyme is **denatured**, and this is usually permanent.
H: How an enzyme works: the lock and key model
P: The lock and key theory is a simplified model of how an enzyme works: the substrate fits the active site like a key in a lock.
- The substrate collides with the enzyme's active site.
- Because their shapes are complementary, the substrate fits into the active site, forming an enzyme-substrate complex.
- The reaction happens while the substrate is held in the active site: it is broken down or joined to another molecule.
- The products are a different shape, so they leave the active site.
- The enzyme is unchanged and its active site is free to bind another substrate molecule.

!: "High temperatures kill enzymes." Enzymes are molecules, not living things, so they can't be killed: heat changes the shape of the active site and denatures them.
!: "An enzyme is used up in the reaction it catalyses." An enzyme is unchanged at the end of the reaction, so it can catalyse the same reaction again and again.
!: "The substrate is the same shape as the active site." The substrate's shape is complementary to the active site, fitting into it the way a key fits a lock, not identical to it.
