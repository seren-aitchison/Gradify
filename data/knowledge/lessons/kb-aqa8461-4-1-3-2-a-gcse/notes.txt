## Osmosis
S: Osmosis is the overall movement of water across a partially permeable membrane, out of a dilute solution and into a more concentrated one.
LV: GCSE
O: Define osmosis and use the term correctly
O: Explain why water moves overall into the concentrated solution
O: Predict and explain what osmosis does to animal and plant cells
H: The idea
P: **Diffusion** is the net movement of particles from where they are more concentrated to where they are less concentrated. It happens because particles move about randomly: more of them leave a crowded region than arrive in it, so they spread out until they are evenly mixed.
P: A **solution** is a **solute** (such as sugar or salt) dissolved in a **solvent**; in living things the solvent is water. A **dilute solution** contains little solute, so most of its molecules are water. A **concentrated solution** contains a lot of solute, so a smaller share of its molecules are water.
P: A **partially permeable membrane** lets some molecules cross but not others. A cell membrane lets small molecules such as water pass through, but holds back larger solute molecules such as sucrose.
P: **Osmosis** is the net movement of water molecules across a partially permeable membrane, from a dilute solution into a more concentrated one. It is diffusion of water: water moves from where its molecules are more concentrated to where they are less concentrated.
K: **Osmosis**: the diffusion of water: its overall movement across a partially permeable membrane, out of a dilute solution and into a more concentrated one.
H: Osmosis is passive
P: Osmosis is a passive process: the cell uses no energy from respiration to move water this way. Water simply moves down its own concentration gradient.
H: When there is no net movement
P: If both sides have the same concentration of water, water molecules still cross in both directions, but equally often. There is no net movement of water.
H: Why water moves overall into the concentrated solution
P: A particle model explains the direction.
- Water molecules move about randomly, and some pass through the membrane in each direction.
- In the dilute solution a bigger share of the molecules are water, so more water molecules reach the membrane from that side each second.
- So more water crosses from the dilute side to the concentrated side than crosses back.
- The overall (net) movement of water is into the concentrated solution.
- The solute molecules are too large to pass through the membrane, so they stay on their own side.

!: "In osmosis, the sugar or salt moves across the membrane." In osmosis it is water that moves overall; the partially permeable membrane holds the solute molecules back.
!: "Water moves from the concentrated solution to the dilute solution." Water moves overall from the dilute solution, which has more water, to the concentrated solution, which has less.
!: "Water moves from a high concentration to a low concentration." Say which concentration you mean: water moves from a high concentration of water (a dilute solution) to a low concentration of water (a concentrated solution). The two are opposites, so leaving it out makes the answer ambiguous.
!: "Osmosis needs energy from respiration." Osmosis is passive: water moves down its own gradient, and the cell spends no energy on it. Moving substances against a gradient (active transport) is what needs energy.
!: "When both solutions have the same concentration, water stops moving." Water molecules keep crossing in both directions; they just cross equally often, so there is no net movement.
!: "A plant cell in pure water swells until it bursts, like a red blood cell." The cell wall pushes back and stops the plant cell bursting; it becomes turgid instead.
H: What osmosis does to animal and plant cells
P: Whether water enters or leaves a cell depends on the solution around it. Plant cells have a cell wall; animal cells do not, and that changes what happens.
- **A red blood cell is placed in pure water:** water enters by osmosis, and the cell swells and bursts. The cell's contents are more concentrated than pure water, and an animal cell has no wall to stop it expanding.
- **A red blood cell is placed in a concentrated salt solution:** water leaves by osmosis, and the cell shrinks. The solution has a lower concentration of water than the cell's contents.
- **A plant cell is placed in pure water:** water enters by osmosis, and the cell becomes turgid without bursting. The cell wall pushes back on the swelling cell and stops it bursting; a firm, turgid cell helps hold the plant up.
- **A plant cell is placed in a concentrated sugar solution:** water leaves by osmosis, and the cell becomes flaccid, then plasmolysed. As the cell loses water its contents shrink and the membrane pulls away from the cell wall.
- **A cell is placed in a solution with the same concentration of water as its contents:** there is no net movement of water, and the cell stays the same. Water crosses both ways equally often.

T: Plasmolysis | The shrinking of a plant cell's contents so the membrane pulls away from the cell wall, when the cell loses a lot of water
T: Cell wall | A layer outside the cell membrane of plant cells (and bacteria) that strengthens the cell and stops it bursting
