The effect of lifestyle on some non-communicable diseases

The idea

A risk factor is anything linked to an increased chance of developing a disease. It can be an aspect of a person's lifestyle, such as smoking or diet, or a substance in their body or environment.

For example:

  • Smoking is a risk factor for lung cancer and cardiovascular disease.
  • Being obese increases the chance of developing Type 2 diabetes.

Two variables are correlated when they tend to change together. In a positive correlation, as one increases the other tends to increase too; in a negative correlation, as one increases the other tends to decrease. The closer the points on a scatter diagram lie to a straight line, the stronger the correlation.

Correlation doesn't prove cause

A correlation doesn't show that one variable causes the other. Both could be caused by a third factor, or the link could be chance. A risk factor is shown to cause a disease only when scientists find a causal mechanism: how one leads to the other.

For example:

  • A causal mechanism is known for smoking and lung cancer: chemicals in tobacco smoke damage the DNA of lung cells.

Calculations

A scatter diagram shows whether two variables are correlated.

  • Plot one variable on each axis, each with a sensible, evenly spaced scale and a label with units.
  • Plot each pair of values as a point; don't join the points up.
  • If there's a trend, draw a straight line of best fit with about as many points above it as below.
  • Describe the pattern: positive, negative or no correlation, and how strong it is.
  • Use the line to estimate values between your measurements.

  5. A line of best fit should go through every point. true or false

    Answer: False

    A line of best fit shows the overall trend: it passes as close as possible to the points, with about as many above it as below.

  6. If a few points don't fit the pattern, there is no correlation. true or false

    Answer: False

    A correlation is about the overall trend. A few points off the line make it weaker, not absent; check whether they are anomalies.

  7. A strong correlation proves that one variable causes the other. true or false

    Answer: False

    A correlation shows only that the variables change together; a third factor or chance could explain it. Cause needs a mechanism.

  8. These data show that a larger waist causes higher blood pressure. true or false

    Answer: False

    They show a correlation only. A third factor, such as diet or lack of exercise, could raise both; showing cause needs a causal mechanism.
