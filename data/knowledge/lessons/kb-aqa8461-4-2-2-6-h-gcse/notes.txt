H: The idea
P: A **risk factor** is anything linked to an increased chance of developing a disease. It can be an aspect of a person's lifestyle, such as smoking or diet, or a substance in their body or environment.
P: For example:
- Smoking is a risk factor for lung cancer and cardiovascular disease.
- Being obese increases the chance of developing Type 2 diabetes.
P: Two variables are **correlated** when they tend to change together. In a **positive correlation**, as one increases the other tends to increase too; in a **negative correlation**, as one increases the other tends to decrease. The closer the points on a scatter diagram lie to a straight line, the stronger the correlation.
H: Correlation doesn't prove cause
P: A correlation doesn't show that one variable causes the other. Both could be caused by a third factor, or the link could be chance. A risk factor is shown to cause a disease only when scientists find a causal mechanism: how one leads to the other.
P: For example:
- A causal mechanism is known for smoking and lung cancer: chemicals in tobacco smoke damage the DNA of lung cells.
H: Calculations
P: A scatter diagram shows whether two variables are correlated.
- Plot one variable on each axis, each with a sensible, evenly spaced scale and a label with units.
- Plot each pair of values as a point; don't join the points up.
- If there's a trend, draw a straight line of best fit with about as many points above it as below.
- Describe the pattern: positive, negative or no correlation, and how strong it is.
- Use the line to estimate values between your measurements.

!: "A strong correlation proves that one variable causes the other." A correlation shows only that the variables change together; a third factor or chance could explain it. Cause needs a mechanism.
!: "A line of best fit should go through every point." A line of best fit shows the overall trend: it passes as close as possible to the points, with about as many above it as below.
!: "If a few points don't fit the pattern, there is no correlation." A correlation is about the overall trend. A few points off the line make it weaker, not absent; check whether they are anomalies.
