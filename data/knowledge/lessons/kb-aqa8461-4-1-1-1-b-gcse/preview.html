Eukaryotes and prokaryotes

Prokaryotic (bacterial) cells have a cell membrane, cytoplasm and a cell wall, but no nucleus.

GCSE

Learning objectives

  • Define prokaryotic cell and use the term correctly
  • Predict and explain telling prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells apart

What you need to know first

  • Diffusion

The idea

Plant and animal cells are eukaryotic cells. Each has a cell membrane around its cytoplasm, and its genetic material is enclosed in a nucleus.

Bacterial cells are prokaryotic cells. Like plant and animal cells they have cytoplasm and a cell membrane, which is surrounded by a cell wall. Unlike them, they have no nucleus: their DNA lies loose in the cytoplasm.

Many bacteria also have one or more plasmids: small rings of DNA separate from the main loop, which carry a few extra genes.

Prokaryotic cell: a cell with no nucleus, such as a bacterial cell.

A bacterium's DNA

A bacterium's genetic material is a single loop of DNA, lying free in the cytoplasm rather than inside a nucleus.

How big prokaryotic cells are

Bacterial cells are much smaller in comparison with plant and animal cells: a typical bacterium is about 1–5 µm long, while most plant and animal cells are about 10–100 µm across.

For example:

  • E. coli, a gut bacterium: about 2 µm long
  • A human cheek cell: about 60 µm across
Common mistake"Bacteria have no DNA because they have no nucleus." Bacteria do have DNA, just not inside a nucleus: a loop of it lies in the cytoplasm, often with plasmids as well.
Common mistake"Plasmids are the bacterium's main DNA." The main DNA is the large loop; plasmids are small extra rings with only a few genes.
Common mistake"A bacterial cell wall is made of cellulose, like a plant cell wall." Plant cell walls are made of cellulose; bacterial cell walls are made of a different material.

Telling prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells apart

To decide what kind of cell you are looking at, check for a nucleus first, then a cell wall.

  • A cell has a cell wall, no nucleus and a single loop of DNA: it is a prokaryotic cell, such as a bacterium. It has no nucleus: its DNA lies loose in the cytoplasm.
  • A cell has a nucleus and a cell membrane but no cell wall: it is a eukaryotic animal cell. It has a nucleus, and animal cells have no cell wall.
  • A cell has a nucleus, a cell wall and chloroplasts: it is a eukaryotic plant cell. It has a nucleus, and only plant (and algal) cells have chloroplasts.
  • A cell is about 2 µm long and has small rings of DNA in its cytoplasm: it is almost certainly a bacterium. Its size and plasmids are typical of a prokaryotic cell.

Key terms

Eukaryotic cell
A cell whose genetic material is enclosed in a nucleus, such as a plant or animal cell
Prokaryotic cell
A cell with no nucleus, such as a bacterial cell
Cell wall
A layer outside the cell membrane that strengthens the cell and stops it bursting
Plasmid
A small ring of DNA, separate from the main DNA, found in many bacteria

Summary

  • Prokaryotic (bacterial) cells have a cell membrane, cytoplasm and a cell wall, but no nucleus.
  • Eukaryotic cells (plant and animal cells) keep their DNA in a nucleus.
  • A bacterium's main DNA is a single loop, free in the cytoplasm.
  • Plasmids are small extra rings of DNA in many bacteria.
  • Bacteria (about 1–5 µm) are much smaller than plant and animal cells (about 10–100 µm).
