H: Units for small lengths
P: Cells are measured in **micrometres** (µm): 1 mm = 1000 µm. Smaller structures are measured in **nanometres** (nm): 1 µm = 1000 nm. Before comparing or dividing two lengths, convert them to the same unit.

H: Calculations
P: **Magnification** says how many times bigger an image is than the real object. Use the formula: divide the size of the image by the real size of the object, with both in the same unit. Magnification has no unit: write it as, for example, ×400.
E: magnification = size of image ÷ size of real object :: both sizes in the same unit
P: Rearrange the magnification equation to find a real size from an image and its magnification, or an image size from a real size.
E: size of real object = size of image ÷ magnification :: and size of image = size of real object × magnification
P: Very small sizes are often written in standard form. To express a number in standard form, write it as a number between 1 and 10 multiplied by a power of ten.
- Write the number with one non-zero digit before the decimal point.
- Count how many places the decimal point moved: that is the power of ten.
- Moving it to the right gives a negative power: 0.000 025 m = 2.5 × 10⁻⁵ m.
X: In a drawing at a magnification of ×1500, a cell is 30 mm long. What is its real length, in µm and in metres in standard form?
> Convert the image size: 30 mm = 30 000 µm.
> Real size = size of image ÷ magnification = 30 000 µm ÷ 1500 = 20 µm.
> In metres: 20 µm = 0.000 020 m = 2.0 × 10⁻⁵ m.
X: A cell is 15 µm long. In a micrograph it is 45 mm long. What is the magnification?
> Convert to the same unit: 45 mm = 45 × 1000 = 45 000 µm.
> Magnification = size of image ÷ size of real object = 45 000 µm ÷ 15 µm.
> Magnification = ×3000.
!: "Magnification = size of real object ÷ size of image." Divide the image size by the real size; a magnification is bigger than 1 because the image is bigger than the object.
!: "A 45 mm image of a 15 µm cell is magnified ×3, because 45 ÷ 15 = 3." Convert to the same unit first: 45 mm is 45 000 µm, so the magnification is 45 000 ÷ 15 = ×3000.
!: "The magnification is 400 mm." Magnification is a ratio of two lengths, so it has no unit: write ×400.
