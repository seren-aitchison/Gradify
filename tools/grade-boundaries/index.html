Grade boundary checker

Paste the grade boundaries your exam board published for that paper (for example A*: 162) and enter your raw mark. You'll see your grade and exactly how many marks you are from the next one. We don't ship boundaries: they change every year, so use the official ones for your paper.

Best after marking a past paper

Worked example

With made-up boundaries of A 140 and B 118, a raw mark of 137 out of 200 (68.5%) is a B, 3 marks off an A: often one or two questions, which is why logging dropped marks pays off.

How do I read the result?

  1. The grade comes from the boundaries you pasted for that exact paper.
  2. "Marks off" is the gap to the next grade up.
  3. A few marks is often one question: log what you dropped.

Questions

Where do I find grade boundaries?

Exam boards (AQA, OCR, Edexcel/Pearson, WJEC, CCEA, IB) publish them for each paper on their websites after results day. Use the boundaries for the exact paper and year you sat.
