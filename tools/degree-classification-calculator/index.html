How UK degree classifications are calculated

Most universities take a credit-weighted average of each year that counts. A 40-credit dissertation counts twice as much as a 20-credit module. The year averages are then combined with a weighting: common schemes are 40/60, 33/67, 30/70 and 25/75 between year 2 and the final year, and some universities count the final year only. First year almost never counts.

AverageClassification
70% and aboveFirst-class honours (1st)
60–69%Upper second-class (2:1)
50–59%Lower second-class (2:2)
40–49%Third-class honours (3rd)

What is a borderline mark?

If your average lands just under a boundary (often within 1 or 2 marks, such as 68.5%), many universities look again. A common rule is preponderance: if more than half of your final-year credits are in the higher class, you're moved up. Others use your final-year average alone, or an exam board's discretion. The calculator flags anything within 2 marks; your own university's regulations decide.

Worked example

Year 2 average 62%, final-year average 68%, weighted 40/60: (62 × 0.4) + (68 × 0.6) = 24.8 + 40.8 = 65.6%, a 2:1.

How do I read the result?

  1. The big number is your weighted degree average; the line under it is the class it falls in.
  2. Check the year averages: the final year usually matters more, so that's where extra marks count most.
  3. If it says borderline, look up your university's rule (often preponderance) before assuming the lower class.

Questions

Does first year count towards my degree?

At most UK universities, no: first year has to be passed but its marks don't count towards your classification. Some four-year courses and a few universities are exceptions, so check your programme handbook.

What percentage is a 2:1?

A 2:1 (upper second-class honours) is an overall average of 60% to 69%. A First starts at 70%.

Is a 69.5% a First?

Often yes, after rounding: many universities round to the nearest whole mark, so 69.5% becomes 70%. Others don't round, but review it as a borderline case. Check your regulations.
