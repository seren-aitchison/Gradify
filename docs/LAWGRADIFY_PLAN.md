# LawGradify plan

LawGradify (getgradify.com/lawgradify/) is a law-learning platform for England and Wales, with the differences in Scotland and Northern Ireland taught explicitly. It is built on the shared Gradify Kit; nothing in it is a separate system.

## Curriculum

Schools 1–16 are exactly the brief's sixteen schools and topics. Three schools were added to meet the brief's other requirements:

- **School 17, Scotland and Northern Ireland** (16 lessons): the "jurisdiction-specific learning pathways" requirement: Scots law as a mixed system, Scottish courts, devolved law, promise and contract, delict, Scots criminal law, property and succession; Northern Ireland's courts, Assembly, criminal law and land law; cross-border issues; qualifying in each.
- **School 18, Routes to Qualification** (13 lessons): separates academic study from professional qualification: the SQE route, QWE, character and suitability, the Bar's academic, vocational and pupillage components, other careers, and how to answer SBAs.
- **School 19, SQE2 Skills Practice** (15 lessons): case-file simulations for each SQE2 skill: interviews and attendance notes, advocacy, case and matter analysis, research, writing, drafting, and ethics in the skills assessments.

### A correction to the brief

The brief's FLK1 list omits **Constitutional and Administrative Law and EU Law**, which the SRA's SQE1 specification includes in FLK1 (with Legal Services and the Legal System). The FLK1 pathway includes Schools 2 and 14 for this reason. The SRA revises its specification; School 18 tells learners to check the current version before they sit.

## Assessment

- **Every check question is a single best answer** (one best option, four plausible distractors, an explanation), mostly as short client scenarios, so every lesson is SQE1-style practice. Rated easy / medium / hard.
- **School quizzes**: ten random SBAs from the school; 75% to pass.
- **Problem bank**: every SBA in the course, filterable by school and difficulty.
- **Flashcards**: in every lesson, spaced review across completed lessons, and saveable to the learner's account so they join the study app and the Mastery Engine.

## Safety and honesty

- A standing notice on every course view: study material, not legal advice; law stated for England and Wales; Scotland and Northern Ireland differ; not a legal qualification and no authorisation to practise; check current sources.
- Content standard: no invented authorities; reforms and contested points flagged; date-sensitive figures dated (`docs/CURRICULUM_CONTENT_STANDARD.md`).
- The Bar pathway's description says it is study, not Bar training.

## Next steps, in order

1. **Finish the notes**, school by school, one commit each (the physics course was built the same way). Priority: Schools 3, 4, 5, 7, 9 (core of LLB, GDL and SQE1), then 11, 12, 15, 8, 10, 6, then 2, 13, 14, 16, 17, 18, 19.
2. **Timed SQE1 mocks** (`#/mock` view): 90 SBAs across a pathway's schools, timed, with a per-school breakdown. Front end only.
3. **AI marking of written answers** (problem questions, SQE2 letters, attendance notes, drafting) through a new `mark` mode on the existing Edge Function, using rubrics stored with each task. Clear labelling that AI feedback is study feedback.
4. **AI role-play client interviews** for SQE2 practice (Edge Function, rate-limited), with a fixed case file per scenario.
5. **Case and statute cards**: case name → facts, held, principle; statute section → elements. As a flashcard family in the notes format.
6. **Course progress in the account** (`course_progress` table) so lesson completion follows the learner across devices.
7. **Entitlements**, once the shared subscriptions system exists: a free set of schools for everyone, the rest by subscription.

## Risks

- **Legal accuracy and currency.** Mitigation: content standard, dated notes, flagged reforms, a review pass by a qualified lawyer before marketing any pathway as exam preparation.
- **Implied qualification.** Mitigation: the notice, School 18, and no "certificate" language anywhere.
- **SQE specification changes.** Mitigation: pathways reference schools, not SRA wording; update the path descriptions when the SRA changes the split.

## Status after clearing the QA warnings (2026-11-03)

All seven written law schools (1, 3, 4, 15, 17, 18 and 19; 165 lessons) are now held to content standard 2. Every lesson has learning objectives, a summary, key terms and dated sources, and every question is linked to an objective. Answer options were rebalanced so the right answer is no longer conspicuously the longest, and each school now has at least 10% hard (M3) questions. LawGradify QA reports **0 warnings**.

Content added to lesson bodies (not just metadata):
- School 1: an authority in What is law? (Theft Act 1968), Court structures (Courts Act 1971; Constitutional Reform Act 2005), Parliament and the legislative process (Parliament Acts 1911 and 1949; R (Jackson) v Attorney General), Legal research and authority (Anderton v Ryan overruled by R v Shivpuri), Legal writing (CPR Part 16).
- School 4, Assault: a short section on the Protection from Harassment Act 1997.
- School 15, Conflicts of interest: the source paragraphs (SRA Code 6.1–6.2) and Prince Jefri Bolkiah v KPMG.
- School 17: Stair's Institutions and Donoghue v Stevenson (Scots law), the Courts Reform (Scotland) Act 2014 (Scottish courts), youth conferencing under the Justice (Northern Ireland) Act 2002 (NI criminal justice), and the Solicitors (Scotland) Act 1980 and Solicitors (NI) Order 1976 (qualifying).

Expert review flags:
- Sources and citations were added after the event from memory; check every case citation, year and statute section against a primary source before the "sources" QA stage is signed off.
- Scotland: check the Victims, Witnesses, and Justice Reform (Scotland) Act 2025's commencement and its effect on jury size and majority before relying on "15-person juries" and the not proven abolition.
- SQE and Bar pages (Schools 18–19) cite SRA and BSB pages dated 2026; recheck against the current specifications.
- Rewritten answer options are shorter paraphrases; check none has changed the legal meaning, especially in the School 15 conduct scenarios.
