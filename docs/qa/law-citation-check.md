# LawGradify: citation check (2026-11-03)

**What this is:** a check of the law course's citations by Claude, using web searches against BAILII, the UK Supreme Court and JCPC sites, the National Archives' Find Case Law and legislation.gov.uk. It is **not** an expert review and it does not complete the `sources` stage in `kit/courses/law/qa.js`. That stage still needs every authority checked against a primary source by a person.

## Scope

- **Searched individually:** 36 authorities, mostly post-2015 cases where an error made from memory is most likely, plus a few that looked doubtful. Each one was confirmed against a court or BAILII record. 2 were wrong and are now corrected.
- **Read through without searching:** about 110 citations from 2000–2014, about 400 older reported citations, about 280 statute-and-section references, and all legislation years and URLs. These were checked for internal consistency (the same case cited the same way everywhere) and against well-established citations. No errors were found, but they were not each confirmed against a primary source.
- **Not checked:** regulator guidance, Civil Procedure Rules and Criminal Procedure Rules rule numbers, and figures that change (fees, thresholds, limits).

## Corrections

1. **R v Shayler.** The notes cited "[2001] UKHL 11", which doesn't exist. The necessity and duress-of-circumstances statement comes from the Court of Appeal, [2001] EWCA Crim 1977. The House of Lords decision ([2002] UKHL 11) said the necessity point didn't arise on the facts. Lesson text and source updated (u05, necessity).
2. **R (Nicklinson) v Ministry of Justice.** The notes attributed the rejection of a necessity defence to the Supreme Court ([2014] UKSC 38). It was the Court of Appeal, [2013] EWCA Civ 961, and the point wasn't pursued in the Supreme Court. Corrected (u05).
3. **u09-01 question:** a wrong option named a statute that doesn't exist ("Judicature Act 1925"). It was replaced with the real, repealed Supreme Court of Judicature Act 1873, s 25.

## Minor points, not changed

- Benkharbouche [2017] UKSC 62: the Supreme Court title is *Benkharbouche v Secretary of State for Foreign and Commonwealth Affairs* (heard with *Janah v Libya*). The notes use the earlier title, against the Embassy of Sudan.
- Times Travel [2021] UKSC 40: the Supreme Court title is *Pakistan International Airline Corp v Times Travel (UK) Ltd*.
- Re Allister [2023] UKSC 5: the Supreme Court title is *Re Allister and Peeples*.
