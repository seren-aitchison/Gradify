# Biology curriculum mapping pilot: audit and implementation

_November 2026. This is the Phase 1 audit, written once, plus a record of what was built. Current figures are in the generated report, `docs/curriculum/biology-england-coverage.md`. Don't update this file's numbers by hand: they are a snapshot from 23 November 2026._

## The question: is there a working curriculum mapping engine?

**No.** Before this pilot, Gradify had three pieces:

1. A course syllabus system, with permanent lesson ids.
2. A course-level claim-strength registry.
3. For four courses only, a unit-level tag list of framework topic names.

Nothing mapped individual lessons or resources to individual official requirements, and nothing held official requirement wording, ids or sources. Biology had no mapping of any kind.

## 1. What curriculum infrastructure exists

| File | What it does | Mapping? |
|---|---|---|
| `kit/course/syllabus.js` | Parses a text syllabus into units (schools), modules and lessons, and keeps every lesson's permanent id through the id lock (`ids.js`), whatever its position. | No: the course's own structure |
| `kit/courses/biology/syllabus.js` | SciGradify Biology's syllabus: 14 schools, 82 modules, 391 lessons, plus tracks, paths and blurbs. Lesson titles come verbatim from the Biology brief. | No |
| `kit/courses/biology/ids.js`, `registry.js` | The id lock for lessons, and generated versions and fingerprints for every lesson and question (`biology-u02-01-q3`). | No: identity and versioning |
| `scripts/curriculum-registry.mjs` | Writes question ids into the notes and keeps the versions in `registry.js` up to date. Run by `npm run qa`. | No |
| `kit/curriculum/coverage.js` | The claim-strength registry: countries → systems → bodies → qualifications, and for each course the qualifications it serves at one of four levels (verified, mapped, aligned, gradify). `quality.html` reads it. ## 2. What `curriculum.js` does

**There is no `curriculum.js`.** It isn't on `main`, on this branch (the only two branches) or in the working tree, and no code imports a module by that name. The file you are probably remembering is `kit/course/syllabus.js` or `kit/curriculum/coverage.js` (table above).

## 3. Previous England curriculum research that can be reused

- **Maths** (`kit/courses/maths/map.js`): the DfE GCSE and A level subject content areas, recorded by name only.
- **Nothing for science or Biology.** The "UK Coverage" work you described (ten England government specifications extracted and mapped to lessons, with Maths and English mappings under way) **isn't in this repository**, on any branch. It may be in another repository, a local working tree or a Claude project. If you can share it, its records can be merged into `data/curriculum/requirements/`, which uses source ids and preserved wording for exactly this purpose.

## 7. What was missing

- Statement-level requirement data with provenance
- A lesson-level mapping with match strength, confidence, evidence and gaps
- Resource-level mapping
- Coverage reporting generated from data
- A progress tracker

## 8. The smallest safe implementation (built)

Everything is new data, scripts and one test file. **No runtime code, course config, lesson content, URL, database or deployment changed.**

- `scripts/curriculum/`: `extract-specs.py` (requirements), `inventory.mjs`, `candidates.mjs` (shortlists), `build-mapping.mjs` and `report.mjs`. See its `README.md`.
- `data/curriculum/`: the requirements, inventory, mapping (`.map` source and built `.json`), resource mapping, coverage report data, and `progress.json`.
- `tests/curriculum-mapping.test.mjs`: 7 tests covering ids, provenance, no invented years, a current inventory, a clean build, evidence on every link, no "verified" claims, and a consistent report.

**Relation to the existing architecture.** The new data sits beside `coverage.js` and `map.js` and doesn't replace them:

- `coverage.js` stays the place for the course-level claim. Biology is still at the default, because changing it changes what learners see on `quality.html`. That is your decision (below).
- `map.js` stays the unit-level summary for the courses that have one. A Biology `map.js` could be generated from the lesson-level data if you want Biology in `npm run qa`'s framework report. I didn't wire one in, because that would change Biology's course config.

Lessons and resources:

- 256 of 391 lessons have a full or partial match.

- 3,197 of 4,879 resources are mapped through their lessons, and 991 match a requirement's wording directly.

The largest gaps are topics with no lesson at all:

- the eye and its defects
- diet and nutrition, including food tests and calorimetry
- respiration in animals: lactic acid, oxygen debt and exercise
- plant disease and plant defences
- health as a concept, non-communicable disease and its treatment
- selective breeding and food security
- decomposition rates
- primary-age identification: common plants and animals, classification keys, teeth, animal life cycles and the senses

## Integrity notes

- **Status: AI-drafted, unreviewed.** No link is expert-verified, and the mapping says so in its data.
- Some extraction defects were found and fixed with tests: Edexcel sub-items, OCR page breaks, two-column tables in OCR J257, and two OCR J247 outcomes transcribed by hand. These are marked `corrected` in the data.
- Not yet extracted:
  - OCR J247 topic B7 and OCR J257 chapters B7–B8 (practical skills and Ideas about Science)
  - the boards' working-scientifically and maths-skills appendices
  - the Combined Science specifications as separate documents. For AQA, Edexcel and OCR J257, shared content is flagged statement by statement instead.
- 54 links have no wording overlap between the requirement and the lesson. Some may be right but use different words. `build-mapping.mjs --review` lists them for the reviewer.

## Decisions for you

1. **`coverage.js`:** should Biology move from the default to `mapped`, for GCSE (`uk-eng`), with `evidence: "docs/curriculum/biology-england-coverage.md"` and a note that the mapping is AI-drafted? This changes `quality.html`.
2. **Review:** who reviews the mapping, and in what order? I suggest low-confidence links first, then the `--review` list.
3. **Gaps:** write new lessons for the gap themes, or leave Biology's GCSE scope as it is?
4. **The UK Coverage dataset:** where it lives, so it can be merged rather than duplicated.
