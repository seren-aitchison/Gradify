# PsychGradify and SocGradify

Two subject courses built on the shared Gradify course engine: **Psychology** at `getgradify.com/psychgradify/course/` and **Sociology** at `getgradify.com/socgradify/course/`. Each site's homepage (`/psychgradify/`, `/socgradify/`) keeps its existing study app, map and LifeOS pages, and now links to its course from the nav, the hero button and a course card. Each course links to the other.

Status on 2 November 2026. This page is the honest record of what exists; `docs/CURRICULUM_PROGRESS.md` (generated by `npm run qa`) is the live per-school table.

## Architecture: what was reused, what was added

Nothing here is a second engine or design system. Both courses are configuration and content handed to `kit/course/course.js`, the engine behind Physics, Biology, Chemistry, Law and Nursing.

| Part | Where |
|---|---|
| Curriculum | `kit/courses/{psychology,sociology}/syllabus.js`: schools, modules (`> `) and lessons (`* `), stages (`TRACKS`), pathways (`PATHS`) and school summaries |
| Permanent lesson ids | `ids.js`, `u01-01` style, locked. Tests fail if an id disappears or changes title |
| Question ids and versions | `registry.js`, written by `npm run qa`; every question carries `#psychology-…` or `#sociology-…` |
| Quality record | `qa.js` (written → tested → reviewed → sources → sign-off) plus `WRITTEN_ON` dates |
| Notes | `notes/uNN.js` in the standard notes format |
| Interactives | `interactives.js` registry → modules in `kit/labs/psychology/`, `kit/labs/sociology/` and the shared `kit/labs/social/` |
| Pages | `psychgradify/course/index.html`, `socgradify/course/index.html` |
| Styles | `kit/styles/social-labs.css` (new, loaded only by these two pages) |
| Tests | `tests/social-sciences.test.mjs` (curriculum, ids, lesson standard, registry, data provenance), `tests/social-labs.test.mjs` (statistics and models pinned to known values) |

**Engine additions, all opt-in** (every other course renders as before; the full test suite passes):

- `EV: established | claim` (also `contested`, `hypothetical`): evidence-status lines, drawn at the end of a lesson under "How strong is the evidence?".
- `LV: Introductory · Foundation`: the lesson's level and difficulty, shown as a chip under the title.
- A `data` source kind ("Data source") for datasets.
- `reviewLine: true` in a course config: each lesson shows "Last reviewed … / Not yet reviewed · sources checked … / pending verification · written …", from `qa.js` and `writtenOn`.
- `scripts/qa-courses.mjs` now covers both courses (subject rules: evidence-status lines, all three levels, and an education-not-diagnosis statement in the psychopathology and clinical schools). Its citation register lists each lesson's `SRC:` lines.
- `scripts/check-sources.mjs <course>`: checks every linked source. Web pages must load; every DOI must exist in Crossref with a matching year and title. Reports go to `docs/qa/<course>-source-check.md`.
- `scripts/fetch-social-data.mjs`: refetches the World Bank data used by the sociology labs.

Shared features come from the engine unchanged:

- lessons, flashcards and spaced review
- check questions, school quizzes and the problem bank
- timed exams: one per stage, a methods or theory paper and a whole-course paper
- pathways, search, XP and streaks, saving to the Gradify account, attempt records and the Lab tab

That is **734 of 831 lessons** (296 of 393 psychology, 438 of 438 sociology: every sociology school), with 3,802 questions and 1,468 flashcards. The other 4 schools (Psychology 8, 9, 15 and 16) are registered but unwritten.

**Every written lesson has:**

- a level, at least two objectives (with command words) and prerequisites where relevant
- two or more sections and a key point, usually with a worked example
- key terms, a summary, and at least two typed and dated sources
- evidence-status lines saying which claims are established and which contested
- two or more flashcards
- five or more questions covering all three levels (Foundation, Application, Evaluation), each linked to an objective, including at least one written (short or extended) answer

Question types used are single best answer and multiple choice, true or false, multi-select, ordering, matching, numerical and short or extended written answers. Scenario questions are written as multiple choice; there is no separate data-interpretation item type yet.

**Sources.** All 62 linked psychology sources and 38 linked sociology sources pass `check-sources.mjs`: pages load, and each DOI matches Crossref on year and title. Books and older works without links are on the citation registers (`docs/qa/psychology-sources.md`, `docs/qa/sociology-sources.md`) for a person to check. One citation (the ASA's "What is sociology?" page) has no link because the site's bot check blocks automated access.

**What "draft" means.** No school has been content-reviewed, had every claim checked against its source, or been signed off by a subject expert (stages 3 to 5 of `docs/CURRICULUM_QA.md`). The automated checks confirm that each reference exists and is described correctly, not that the lesson reports it accurately. Every lesson shows the draft notice and "Not yet reviewed" until `qa.js` records those stages. Sign-off needs a university psychology lecturer or chartered psychologist (Psychology), or a university sociology lecturer (Sociology); AI review can't complete it.

## Interactives (61 registered)

Every lab says what kind it is and what it simplifies. Data is labelled either **real** (with source and retrieval date) or **simulated**. Experiment data stays in the browser (`localStorage`); nothing is uploaded. Every lab mounts with no console errors (checked in the browser), and models with numbers behind them are pinned in `tests/social-labs.test.mjs`.

**Psychology (32 entries, 30 modules; two reused from SciGradify Biology).**

- **Experiments** (your own responses): Stroop (colour and spatial), visual search, free recall and the serial position curve, digit span, choice reaction time (Hick's law), the Müller-Lyer illusion, and an Asch-style line-judgement procedure.
- **Simulations**:
  - Rescorla–Wagner conditioning
  - signal detection with an ROC curve
  - the two-process model of sleep
  - a hypothesis-test simulator
  - ABAB single-case data
  - the Hodgkin–Huxley action potential and synapse, reused from SciGradify Biology
- **Explorers**: brain anatomy (side and midline views), neurotransmitters and receptors, memory models, a developmental timeline, and the Big Five (explicitly not a test).
- **Exercises**:
  - brain-region identification and split-brain predictions
  - cognitive and attribution biases
  - Strange Situation scenarios
  - a CBT thought record and a 5 Ps formulation, both educational with invented clients
  - an experiment builder and a study critique
- **Tools**: a statistics workspace, a correlation and regression visualiser (with Anscombe's real quartet) and psychometric score interpretation.

**Sociology (29 entries, 13 modules).**

- **Real-data explorers** (World Bank WDI, 14 indicators, 22 countries, 1990 onwards, retrieved 1 November 2026): inequality and development, gender, education, crime (homicide, from UNODC), work, climate (CO₂ against income) and urbanisation. There are also Lorenz curves from real income quintiles, the demographic transition in real birth and death rates, and real population pyramids for 1960, 1990 and 2023.
- **Simulations and models**: absolute versus relative poverty, a social mobility Markov model, sampling bias, and a demographic transition model.
- **Theory**: a timeline of 30 theorists, a Marx–Durkheim–Weber comparison with a "whose idea?" sorter, and a social movements timeline.
- **Research studio**:
  - a methodology decision tool
  - a survey design builder
  - qualitative coding into themes
  - a content analysis tool
  - ethnographic fieldnotes
  - BSA ethics scenarios
  - a social network visualiser (Brandes betweenness)
  - the shared statistics workspace with a crosstab and chi-square mode

**Not built from the brief's interactive list:**

- **Psychology:** reinforcement shaping as its own lab (it is covered in lessons), lateralisation beyond the split-brain exercise, and a dedicated attention-span task.
- **Sociology:** a socialisation and identity mapping tool, a cultural capital explorer, a policy comparison tool, a globalisation network, migration flows, and urbanisation and gentrification models.
- **UK data:** there is no UK crime-by-class or education-by-ethnicity explorer, because that needs ONS and DfE data the labs don't hold yet. The crime and education explorers use international World Bank data instead.

## Safety framing

- PsychGradify carries a standing notice: education, not diagnosis or therapy, with UK help routes (GP, NHS 111, 999, Samaritans 116 123).
- The personality, psychometric, thought-record and formulation labs each say they are educational demonstrations with invented people, not tests or treatment.
- The QA script warns if any lesson in the psychopathology or clinical schools lacks an education-not-diagnosis statement.
