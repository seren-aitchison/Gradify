# Curriculum quality assurance

How LawGradify and NurseGradify content moves from written to published. Quality comes before lesson count: a school is not complete until it has passed every stage below.

The live status of every school is in **`docs/CURRICULUM_PROGRESS.md`**, generated by `npm run qa`. Citation registers for source checking are in **`docs/qa/law-sources.md`** and **`docs/qa/nursing-sources.md`**. The record of each school's review is in **`kit/courses/<slug>/qa.js`**.

## The five stages

| Stage | Definition | Evidence | Who sets it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Written** | Notes exist for every planned lesson in the school, in the notes format | `written` in `course.js` | Author |
| **2. Technically tested** | `npm test` passes and `npm run qa` reports no hard failures for the school | Generated each run | The checks |
| **3. Content reviewed** | A structured review against the checklist below is complete; every finding is fixed or recorded as open | `reviewed: { date, by, findings, open }` | Reviewer |
| **4. Sources verified** | Every authority in the school's citation register checked against a primary source; corrections made | `sources: { date, by, method, checked, corrected, open }` | Reviewer with access to primary sources |
| **5. Ready for publication** | Stages 1–4 complete, no open review items, and approved by a qualified subject expert | `signoff: { date, by }` | A practising solicitor or barrister (law); a registered nurse educator (nursing) |

Until a school reaches stage 5, every lesson in it shows a **Draft** label in the course (`kit/course/course.js`, driven by `cfg.qa`). `tests/qa.test.mjs` enforces the order: sources can't be recorded before review, and sign-off can't be recorded before both, or with open items.

AI review (for example, by Claude) can complete stage 3 if it is recorded as such in `by`. It can never complete stage 5.

## Batch procedure

For every completed batch (normally one school):

1. **Registration**: every planned lesson has notes and is registered (`written`, `qa.js`); `npm run qa` finds no missing or extra lessons.
2. **Objectives**: each lesson's lead states what it teaches; the content covers the syllabus topic and, where mapped, the SQE or NMC outcomes (see the mapping documents).
3. **Depth, clarity, accuracy**: work through the checklist below lesson by lesson.
4. **Sources**: check every entry in the citation register against a primary source; fix errors; record the method.
5. **Assessment**: check every flashcard and check question (see below), including the warnings from `npm run qa`.
6. **Level**: right for the stated audience (LLB/SQE/Bar; pre-registration/NQN/CPD), with professional limits clear.
7. **Technical**: `npm test`, `npm run qa`, and a browser check of a sample of lessons and the school quiz at desktop and phone widths.

Record the outcome in `qa.js`, rerun `npm run qa`, and commit.

## Review checklist

**Content**
- Correct, current and complete for the topic at the stated level; no invented authorities, figures or doses.
- Jurisdiction (law) or nation (nursing) stated where the rule differs.
- Date-sensitive material (tax bands, thresholds, guideline versions, fees) dated or described without the number.
- Recent changes and contested points flagged in a `!:` line.
- Law: authority given for each legal proposition that needs one; Scotland and Northern Ireland differences noted where material.
- Nursing: escalation and local-policy statements where care is clinical; procedures framed as education within competence, scope and supervision; medicines examples labelled illustrative; School 5 conditions cover all thirteen headings.

**Assessment**
- Flashcards test one fact each, with answers under 40 words.
- Check questions: one clearly best answer; plausible distractors drawn from real misconceptions; no answer leaked by wording or length; explanation says why the answer is right (and why the tempting distractor is wrong, where useful).
- Law: five-option single best answer in client-scenario form wherever possible, as in SQE1.
- A mix of difficulty: aim for at least 15% hard (M3) questions that test application across more than one rule.
- No duplicate questions across the course.

**Language and accessibility**
- British English, plain language, terms defined at first use, inclusive and person-first.

## The automated checks (`npm run qa`)

**Hard failures** (block stage 2): missing or extra lessons; a lead of 60 characters or fewer; fewer than two sections; no key point; fewer than two flashcards or check questions; a question with too few options, duplicate options or an explanation of 40 characters or fewer; an answer that appears in its own question; a duplicate question anywhere in the course.

**Warnings** (must be resolved or explicitly accepted at stage 3): short lessons; law lessons with no authority cited (acceptable for conceptual lessons, but say so); law questions with fewer than five options; nursing clinical lessons with no escalation, safety or local-policy statement; School 5 condition lessons missing any of the thirteen headings; medicines calculations not labelled illustrative; the right answer being much the longest option in more than a quarter of a school's questions; fewer than 10% hard questions.

## Current limitation: primary sources

This development environment can't reach legislation.gov.uk, the National Archives' Find Case Law, BAILII, the SRA, the NMC, NICE, the BNF, the Resuscitation Council UK, the British Thoracic Society, gov.uk or the MHRA. Stage 4 can't be completed here. To enable it, allow those domains in the cloud environment's network settings, or verify the citation registers outside this environment and record the results in `qa.js`.
