Free Medicine course: revision notes, flashcards and questions

MedGradify's free Medicine course runs from Foundations of Medical Science to Professional Practice, Prescribing and Population Health: 234 lessons in 12 schools, each with notes, flashcards and check questions at three levels (Foundation, Application, Evaluation). It's written for medical students and junior doctors.

Education for medical students, not medical advice or clinical guidance. MedGradify teaches medicine for study and exam revision. The lessons were drafted with AI assistance and haven't yet been reviewed by a doctor, so they may contain errors: check important points against your medical school's teaching, current NICE guidance and the BNF. Nothing here can diagnose, treat or advise on a real person, and it gives no doses for real patients. If you or someone else is unwell, contact a doctor, call NHS 111, or in an emergency call 999.

Schools and lessons

School 1: Foundations of Medical Science

17 lessons

Foundations of Medical Science: key terms, sample flashcards and a quick quiz

  1. Cell structure and function in health: Every disease is ultimately a disorder of cells.
  2. Membranes, transport and the resting potential: Cells keep a different ionic composition from the fluid around them, and that difference powers nerve, muscle and heart.
  3. Homeostasis and negative feedback: The body holds temperature, glucose, blood pressure and hormone levels within narrow limits by negative feedback.
  4. Body fluids, osmolality and electrolytes: Where water and salt sit in the body explains dehydration, oedema, hyponatraemia and how intravenous fluids behave.
  5. Acid–base physiology: Blood pH is held between 7.35 and 7.45 by buffers, the lungs and the kidneys.
  6. Cell injury, adaptation and death: Cells under stress first adapt, then suffer reversible injury, and finally die.
  7. Acute inflammation: Acute inflammation is the body's rapid, stereotyped response to infection and injury.
  8. Chronic inflammation and repair: When injury persists, inflammation becomes chronic and repair runs alongside destruction.
  9. Thrombosis, embolism and infarction: A thrombus is a clot in a living vessel; an embolus is anything that travels in the blood and lodges; infarction is the tissue death that follows.
  10. Neoplasia: benign and malignant tumours: A neoplasm is an abnormal mass of tissue whose growth is uncoordinated and persists after the stimulus stops.
  11. The hallmarks and genetics of cancer: Cancer is a genetic disease of somatic cells, built up mutation by mutation.
  12. Innate immunity: Innate immunity is the fast, inborn defence that responds within minutes to hours and recognises broad classes of danger.
  13. Adaptive immunity: Adaptive immunity recognises specific antigens with enormous diversity and remembers them.
  14. Hypersensitivity and autoimmunity: The immune system can harm the body it protects, by over-reacting to harmless antigens or attacking self.
  15. Patterns of inheritance in clinical genetics: Many diseases run in families in predictable ways.
  16. Pharmacodynamics: receptors and dose–response: Pharmacodynamics is what a drug does to the body.
  17. Pharmacokinetics: absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion: Pharmacokinetics is what the body does to a drug.

School 2: Clinical Method and Reasoning

17 lessons

Clinical Method and Reasoning: key terms, sample flashcards and a quick quiz

  1. The medical history: Most diagnoses are made, or strongly suggested, by the history.
  2. Consultation models and patient-centred care: Consultation models turn communication into teachable, observable skills.
  3. Communicating risk and shared decision-making: Patients can only decide well if risks are explained in ways they understand.
  4. Breaking bad news: How bad news is given affects patients for years.
  5. Principles of physical examination: Examination is a skill of observation, technique and interpretation, built on consent and dignity.
  6. Vital signs and the early warning score (NEWS2): Deterioration is usually preceded by hours of abnormal observations.
  7. The cardiovascular examination: The cardiovascular examination looks for the signs of heart failure, valve disease, endocarditis and vascular disease, from the hands to the heart.
  8. The respiratory examination: The respiratory examination combines inspection, palpation, percussion and auscultation to locate disease in the airways, lung tissue or pleura.
  9. The abdominal examination: The abdominal examination detects organ enlargement, masses, fluid, tenderness and the signs of chronic liver disease.
  10. The neurological examination: The neurological examination localises a lesion, which narrows the causes dramatically.
  11. Clinical reasoning and diagnostic error: Diagnosis is a reasoning process that can be studied and improved.
  12. Interpreting tests: sensitivity, specificity and predictive values: No test is perfect, and the same result means different things in different patients.
  13. Interpreting a full blood count and electrolytes: The full blood count and urea and electrolytes are the most requested tests in hospital.
  14. Arterial blood gas interpretation: An arterial blood gas tells you about oxygenation, ventilation and acid–base status within minutes.
  15. The 12-lead ECG: a systematic approach: The ECG is cheap, fast and diagnostic for arrhythmias, ischaemia and electrolyte disturbance.
  16. Chest X-ray interpretation: The chest X-ray is the most common imaging test in medicine.
  17. Presenting a patient and documenting care: Clinical information only helps patients if it is communicated clearly.

School 3: Cardiovascular Medicine

18 lessons

Cardiovascular Medicine: key terms, sample flashcards and a quick quiz

  1. The cardiac cycle and cardiac output: The cardiac cycle is the sequence of pressure and volume changes in one heartbeat, and cardiac output is the volume the heart pumps each minute.
  2. Cardiac electrophysiology and conduction: The heartbeat is generated and coordinated by specialised conducting tissue.
  3. Blood pressure regulation: Blood pressure is regulated over seconds by the baroreceptor reflex and over hours to days by the kidney and hormones.
  4. Atherosclerosis and cardiovascular risk: Atherosclerosis is a chronic inflammatory disease of arteries and the main cause of heart attack, stroke and peripheral arterial disease.
  5. Stable angina: Stable angina is chest discomfort caused by myocardial ischaemia on exertion, relieved by rest.
  6. Acute coronary syndromes: Acute coronary syndromes (ACS) are unstable angina, NSTEMI and STEMI, caused by sudden reduction of coronary blood flow.
  7. Secondary prevention after myocardial infarction: After a myocardial infarction, the aim is to prevent another event and help the person recover.
  8. Heart failure: causes and classification: Heart failure is a clinical syndrome in which the heart can't pump enough blood to meet the body's needs at normal filling pressures.
  9. Managing chronic heart failure: Treatment of chronic heart failure aims to relieve symptoms, reduce admissions and prolong life.
  10. Valvular heart disease: Valve disease causes narrowing (stenosis) or leaking (regurgitation).
  11. Infective endocarditis: Infective endocarditis is infection of the heart's inner lining, usually the valves.
  12. Cardiomyopathies and pericardial disease: Cardiomyopathies are diseases of the heart muscle not explained by coronary disease or loading conditions; pericardial disease affects the sac around the heart.
  13. Atrial fibrillation: Atrial fibrillation (AF) is the most common sustained arrhythmia and a major cause of stroke.
  14. Bradyarrhythmias and heart block: Bradyarrhythmias are slow heart rhythms, from sinus node disease to complete heart block.
  15. Tachyarrhythmias: Tachyarrhythmias are fast heart rhythms.
  16. Hypertension: Hypertension is a major modifiable risk factor for stroke, heart attack, heart failure and kidney disease.
  17. Peripheral arterial disease and aortic aneurysm: Atherosclerosis in the leg arteries causes peripheral arterial disease; weakening of the aortic wall causes abdominal aortic aneurysm.
  18. Venous thromboembolism: Venous thromboembolism (VTE) covers deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism.

School 4: Respiratory Medicine

16 lessons

Respiratory Medicine: key terms, sample flashcards and a quick quiz

  1. Ventilation, gas exchange and oxygen transport: The lungs move air in and out, exchange oxygen and carbon dioxide with the blood, and haemoglobin carries oxygen to the tissues.
  2. Lung function tests: Lung function tests measure how much air the lungs hold and how quickly it can be moved.
  3. Respiratory failure: type 1 and type 2: Respiratory failure occurs when the lungs fail to oxygenate the blood or remove carbon dioxide adequately.
  4. Asthma: Asthma is a chronic inflammatory airway disease with variable symptoms and airflow obstruction.
  5. Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD): COPD is a common, progressive lung disease with airflow obstruction, mainly caused by smoking.
  6. Bronchiectasis and cystic fibrosis: Bronchiectasis is permanent dilatation of the airways with chronic infection; cystic fibrosis is an inherited cause.
  7. Community-acquired pneumonia: Community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) is infection of the lung tissue acquired outside hospital.
  8. Tuberculosis: Tuberculosis (TB) is a bacterial infection, usually of the lungs, that remains a major global cause of death.
  9. Pleural effusion: A pleural effusion is fluid in the pleural space.
  10. Pneumothorax: A pneumothorax is air in the pleural space, causing the lung to collapse.
  11. Interstitial lung disease: Interstitial lung diseases (ILDs) are a group of conditions causing inflammation and scarring of the lung tissue.
  12. Sarcoidosis: Sarcoidosis is a multisystem disease of unknown cause characterised by non-caseating granulomas.
  13. Pulmonary embolism: Pulmonary embolism (PE) ranges from small, low-risk clots to massive PE causing shock.
  14. Pulmonary hypertension: Pulmonary hypertension is raised pressure in the pulmonary arteries, which strains the right heart.
  15. Lung cancer: Lung cancer is the leading cause of cancer death in the UK.
  16. Obstructive sleep apnoea: Obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA) is repeated collapse of the upper airway during sleep, causing disturbed sleep and daytime sleepiness.

School 5: Gastroenterology and Hepatology

18 lessons

Gastroenterology and Hepatology: key terms, sample flashcards and a quick quiz

  1. Digestion and absorption: The gut breaks food down into molecules small enough to absorb, and different nutrients are absorbed at different sites.
  2. Nutrition, malnutrition and refeeding: Malnutrition is common in hospital patients and worsens outcomes.
  3. Dysphagia and oesophageal disease: Dysphagia, difficulty swallowing, can be the first sign of serious disease.
  4. Gastro-oesophageal reflux disease: Gastro-oesophageal reflux disease (GORD) is very common and usually managed in primary care.
  5. Peptic ulcer disease and Helicobacter pylori: Peptic ulcers are breaks in the lining of the stomach or duodenum.
  6. Upper gastrointestinal bleeding: Upper GI bleeding is a common medical emergency.
  7. Coeliac disease: Coeliac disease is an immune reaction to gluten that damages the small intestine.
  8. Inflammatory bowel disease: Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) comprises Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis, chronic relapsing conditions often starting in young adults.
  9. Irritable bowel syndrome: Irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) is a common disorder of gut–brain interaction causing pain and altered bowel habit.
  10. Diarrhoea and gastroenteritis: Diarrhoea is among the most common reasons for seeking medical help.
  11. Colorectal cancer and screening: Colorectal cancer is one of the most common cancers in the UK.
  12. Diverticular disease: Diverticula, small pouches in the colon wall, are very common with age.
  13. Liver function tests and jaundice: Liver blood tests and jaundice are common clinical problems.
  14. Viral hepatitis: Viral hepatitis is a major cause of liver disease worldwide.
  15. Alcohol-related and metabolic liver disease: Alcohol and metabolic dysfunction are the two leading causes of chronic liver disease in the UK.
  16. Cirrhosis and its complications: Cirrhosis is the end stage of chronic liver injury, with scarring and nodules that disrupt liver structure.
  17. Gallstone disease: Gallstones are very common and often silent, but they can cause pain, infection and pancreatitis.
  18. Acute and chronic pancreatitis: Pancreatitis ranges from a self-limiting illness to a life-threatening emergency.

School 6: Renal, Endocrine and Metabolic Medicine

20 lessons

Renal, Endocrine and Metabolic Medicine: key terms, sample flashcards and a quick quiz

  1. Renal physiology and glomerular filtration: The kidneys filter the blood, regulate fluids and electrolytes, and produce hormones.
  2. Acute kidney injury: Acute kidney injury (AKI) is a sudden fall in kidney function, common in hospital and often preventable.
  3. Chronic kidney disease: Chronic kidney disease (CKD) is common, often silent, and increases cardiovascular risk.
  4. Glomerulonephritis and nephrotic syndrome: Glomerular diseases damage the kidney's filters and present with nephrotic or nephritic patterns.
  5. Urinary tract infection: Urinary tract infections are among the most common bacterial infections.
  6. Hyperkalaemia and hyponatraemia: Potassium and sodium disorders are common and can be life-threatening.
  7. Type 1 diabetes: Type 1 diabetes is an autoimmune condition in which the pancreas stops producing insulin.
  8. Type 2 diabetes: Type 2 diabetes is the most common form of diabetes and is closely linked with obesity and insulin resistance.
  9. Diabetic ketoacidosis and hyperosmolar hyperglycaemic state: Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) and the hyperosmolar hyperglycaemic state (HHS) are life-threatening emergencies.
  10. Hypoglycaemia: Hypoglycaemia is the most common acute complication of diabetes treatment.
  11. Complications of diabetes: Long-term high glucose damages small and large blood vessels.
  12. The hypothalamus and pituitary: The hypothalamus and pituitary control much of the endocrine system.
  13. Hypothyroidism: Hypothyroidism is common, especially in women, and easily treated once recognised.
  14. Hyperthyroidism: Hyperthyroidism speeds up metabolism and can affect the heart, bones and eyes.
  15. Adrenal insufficiency: Adrenal insufficiency means the adrenal glands do not make enough cortisol.
  16. Cushing's syndrome: Cushing's syndrome is caused by prolonged exposure to excess cortisol.
  17. Primary hyperaldosteronism and phaeochromocytoma: Some cases of hypertension have endocrine causes that can be treated or cured.
  18. Calcium disorders and the parathyroid glands: Calcium is tightly regulated by parathyroid hormone and vitamin D. This lesson covers calcium homeostasis, the causes and features of hypercalcaemia and hypocalcaemia, corrected calcium, and…
  19. Osteoporosis and metabolic bone disease: Osteoporosis weakens bones and causes fragility fractures, a major cause of disability in older people.
  20. Lipid disorders: Raised cholesterol is a major modifiable cause of cardiovascular disease.

School 7: Neurology and Psychiatry

24 lessons

Neurology and Psychiatry: key terms, sample flashcards and a quick quiz

  1. Headache: Headache is one of the commonest reasons for seeking medical help; most headaches are benign, but some signal serious disease.
  2. Stroke and transient ischaemic attack: Stroke is a medical emergency in which every minute counts.
  3. Epilepsy and seizures: Epilepsy is a tendency to recurrent unprovoked seizures.
  4. Multiple sclerosis: Multiple sclerosis (MS) is an immune-mediated disease of the central nervous system and a leading cause of disability in young adults.
  5. Parkinson's disease and movement disorders: Parkinson's disease is the commonest movement disorder of later life.
  6. Dementia: Dementia is a progressive decline in cognitive function affecting daily life.
  7. Motor neurone disease: Motor neurone disease (MND) is a progressive, fatal disease of the motor neurons.
  8. Peripheral neuropathy: Peripheral neuropathy is damage to nerves outside the brain and spinal cord.
  9. Myasthenia gravis and muscle disease: Myasthenia gravis affects the junction between nerves and muscles, causing fatigable weakness.
  10. Meningitis and encephalitis: Meningitis and encephalitis are infections of the nervous system that can kill or disable within hours.
  11. Spinal cord compression and cauda equina syndrome: Compression of the spinal cord or cauda equina can cause permanent paralysis and incontinence if not treated urgently.
  12. Raised intracranial pressure and head injury: Head injuries are common, and some cause bleeding that raises pressure inside the skull.
  13. The psychiatric assessment and mental state examination: The psychiatric assessment gathers a full history and examines the person's current mental state.
  14. Depression: Depression is common in all clinical settings and can be life-threatening.
  15. Bipolar affective disorder: Bipolar disorder involves episodes of mania or hypomania and depression.
  16. Anxiety disorders and obsessive-compulsive disorder: Anxiety disorders and obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD) are common and treatable.
  17. Schizophrenia and psychosis: Schizophrenia is a severe mental illness involving psychosis.
  18. Self-harm and suicide risk assessment: Self-harm is common and a strong predictor of suicide.
  19. Eating disorders: Eating disorders are serious mental illnesses with high physical risk.
  20. Alcohol and substance misuse: Alcohol and drug misuse cause physical, psychological and social harm and often present in hospital.
  21. Personality disorders: Personality disorders are long-standing patterns of thinking, feeling and relating that cause distress and difficulty.
  22. Delirium: Delirium is an acute, fluctuating disturbance of attention and awareness, common in hospital and often missed.
  23. Psychiatry of childhood and adolescence: Many mental disorders begin in childhood and adolescence.
  24. Mental health law in the UK: Mental health law allows compulsory assessment and treatment in limited circumstances while protecting rights.

School 8: Infection, Haematology and Oncology

20 lessons

Infection, Haematology and Oncology: key terms, sample flashcards and a quick quiz

  1. Principles of microbiology: Choosing the right investigation and treatment for an infection starts with basic microbiology.
  2. Antimicrobial therapy and stewardship: Antibiotics save lives, but every prescription selects for resistance.
  3. Sepsis: Sepsis is life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated response to infection.
  4. Fever in the returning traveller: Fever after travel may be a routine infection or something life-threatening.
  5. HIV infection: HIV is now a manageable long-term condition, but late diagnosis remains common.
  6. Healthcare-associated infection and infection control: Infections acquired in hospital harm patients and are often preventable.
  7. Vaccination and immunisation: Immunisation prevents disease in individuals and populations.
  8. Anaemia: Anaemia is a reduced haemoglobin concentration, and its cause must always be sought.
  9. Haemoglobinopathies: sickle cell disease and thalassaemia: Inherited disorders of haemoglobin are common worldwide and in the UK.
  10. Haemostasis and bleeding disorders: Normal haemostasis balances bleeding and clotting.
  11. Anticoagulation: Anticoagulants prevent stroke and treat venous thromboembolism but carry a risk of bleeding.
  12. Blood transfusion: Blood transfusion can save lives but carries risks, and many transfusions are avoidable.
  13. Leukaemia: Leukaemias are cancers of blood-forming cells.
  14. Lymphoma: Lymphomas are cancers of lymphocytes, usually presenting with enlarged lymph nodes.
  15. Myeloma: Myeloma is a cancer of plasma cells in the bone marrow, often diagnosed late because its symptoms are non-specific.
  16. Myeloproliferative neoplasms: Myeloproliferative neoplasms are chronic blood cancers in which the marrow makes too many blood cells.
  17. Principles of cancer staging and treatment: Cancer care depends on accurate diagnosis, staging and a plan agreed by a multidisciplinary team.
  18. Systemic anticancer therapy and its toxicities: Systemic anticancer therapy includes chemotherapy, targeted therapy and immunotherapy, each with characteristic side effects.
  19. Oncological emergencies: Cancer and its treatment can cause emergencies that need rapid recognition.
  20. Cancer screening programmes: Screening aims to detect cancer or pre-cancer before symptoms appear.

School 9: Musculoskeletal, Skin and the Special Senses

21 lessons

Musculoskeletal, Skin and the Special Senses: key terms, sample flashcards and a quick quiz

  1. Osteoarthritis: Osteoarthritis is the commonest joint disease, a process of cartilage loss and joint remodelling that causes pain and stiffness.
  2. Rheumatoid arthritis: Rheumatoid arthritis is a chronic autoimmune inflammatory arthritis that destroys joints if not treated early.
  3. Gout and pseudogout: Gout and pseudogout are crystal arthropathies that cause sudden, very painful joint inflammation.
  4. Seronegative spondyloarthropathies: The spondyloarthropathies are inflammatory arthritides linked to HLA-B27 that affect the spine, entheses and joints, usually with negative rheumatoid factor.
  5. Systemic lupus erythematosus and connective tissue disease: Systemic lupus erythematosus is a multisystem autoimmune disease driven by autoantibodies and immune complexes.
  6. Vasculitis and polymyalgia rheumatica: Vasculitis is inflammation of blood vessel walls, classified by the size of vessel affected.
  7. Septic arthritis and osteomyelitis: Bacterial infection of a joint or bone can destroy tissue within days and cause sepsis.
  8. Back pain: Low back pain is very common and usually non-specific and self-limiting, but a few patients have serious causes that must not be missed.
  9. Fractures and orthopaedic trauma: Fractures range from minor wrist injuries to life-threatening trauma.
  10. Describing skin lesions: Accurate description of skin lesions is the basis of dermatological diagnosis and referral.
  11. Eczema and psoriasis: Eczema and psoriasis are the two commonest chronic inflammatory skin diseases.
  12. Skin cancer: Skin cancer is the commonest cancer in the UK.
  13. Skin infections and infestations: Bacterial, viral and fungal skin infections and infestations such as scabies are common in every setting.
  14. Urticaria and drug reactions: Urticaria and cutaneous drug reactions range from a mild itchy rash to life-threatening emergencies.
  15. The red eye: A red eye is usually benign, but some causes threaten sight within hours.
  16. Sudden loss of vision: Sudden loss of vision is an emergency until proven otherwise.
  17. Glaucoma, cataract and diabetic eye disease: Chronic glaucoma, cataract and diabetic retinopathy are among the commonest causes of visual impairment.
  18. Hearing loss and tinnitus: Hearing loss is common and often under-recognised, and some causes need urgent action.
  19. Vertigo: Vertigo is the illusion of movement, usually rotational.
  20. Ear, nose and throat infections: Sore throat, earache and sinusitis are among the commonest reasons for consultation, and most are viral and self-limiting.
  21. Head and neck cancer: Head and neck cancers arise mainly in the mouth, pharynx and larynx and are usually squamous cell carcinomas.

School 10: Acute, Surgical and Palliative Care

21 lessons

Acute, Surgical and Palliative Care: key terms, sample flashcards and a quick quiz

  1. The ABCDE assessment of the acutely ill patient: The ABCDE approach is a systematic way to assess and treat an acutely unwell patient, dealing with life-threatening problems in order of urgency.
  2. Shock: Shock is circulatory failure leading to inadequate oxygen delivery to the tissues.
  3. Anaphylaxis: Anaphylaxis is a severe, life-threatening systemic hypersensitivity reaction.
  4. Cardiac arrest and advanced life support: Cardiac arrest needs immediate, coordinated resuscitation.
  5. Poisoning and overdose: Poisoning is a common reason for hospital attendance, often after intentional self-harm.
  6. Major trauma: Major trauma is a leading cause of death in younger adults.
  7. Burns: Burns range from minor scalds to life-threatening injuries with airway compromise and major fluid loss.
  8. The acute abdomen: The acute abdomen is sudden, severe abdominal pain that may need urgent surgery.
  9. Appendicitis: Appendicitis is the commonest surgical emergency of the abdomen.
  10. Bowel obstruction: Bowel obstruction is a common surgical emergency in which intestinal contents cannot pass.
  11. Hernias: A hernia is the protrusion of a structure through a defect in the wall that normally contains it.
  12. Preoperative assessment: Preoperative assessment identifies and reduces risk before surgery and helps patients decide whether to proceed.
  13. Anaesthesia and airway management: Anaesthesia makes surgery possible, and airway management is a core skill in anaesthesia and resuscitation.
  14. Postoperative complications: Complications after surgery can be general or specific to the operation and tend to occur at characteristic times.
  15. Fluid prescribing: Intravenous fluids are among the most commonly prescribed treatments, and errors cause real harm.
  16. Urological emergencies and urinary retention: Several urological conditions need urgent recognition and treatment.
  17. Breast disease: Breast symptoms are common and most are benign, but breast cancer is the commonest cancer in the UK.
  18. Frailty and comprehensive geriatric assessment: Frailty is a state of reduced reserve that makes older people vulnerable to sudden decline after minor stresses.
  19. Falls: Falls are common in older people and cause injury, fear and loss of independence.
  20. Palliative care and symptom control: Palliative care aims to improve the quality of life of people with life-limiting illness and their families.
  21. Care in the last days of life: Good care in the last days of life focuses on comfort, dignity and the needs of the person and those close to them.

School 11: Obstetrics, Gynaecology and Paediatrics

22 lessons

Obstetrics, Gynaecology and Paediatrics: key terms, sample flashcards and a quick quiz

  1. Normal pregnancy and antenatal care: Most pregnancies are healthy, and antenatal care aims to keep them so while detecting problems early.
  2. Bleeding in early pregnancy: Bleeding in the first trimester is common and usually due to miscarriage, but ectopic pregnancy is a life-threatening cause that must not be missed.
  3. Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy: Hypertensive disorders are a major cause of maternal and fetal harm.
  4. Diabetes in pregnancy: Diabetes in pregnancy, whether present before pregnancy or developing during it, increases risks for mother and baby.
  5. Labour and delivery: Labour is the process by which the baby, placenta and membranes are delivered.
  6. Obstetric emergencies: Obstetric emergencies can threaten the lives of both mother and baby within minutes.
  7. The postnatal period and perinatal mental health: The weeks after birth bring physical recovery and major psychological adjustment.
  8. The menstrual cycle and its disorders: The menstrual cycle is controlled by the hypothalamic–pituitary–ovarian axis, and its disorders are among the most common reasons women see a doctor.
  9. Contraception: Effective contraception allows people to plan pregnancies.
  10. Subfertility: Subfertility affects around 1 in 7 couples in the UK.
  11. The menopause: The menopause is the permanent end of menstruation, and its symptoms can greatly affect quality of life.
  12. Gynaecological cancers: Cancers of the cervix, uterus, ovary and vulva differ in their causes, presentation and prevention.
  13. Sexually transmitted infections: Sexually transmitted infections are common and often have no symptoms, yet they can cause serious complications.
  14. Child development and the developmental assessment: Children develop skills in a predictable sequence, and delays can be the first sign of a medical, sensory or social problem.
  15. The sick child: Recognising a seriously ill child is one of the most important skills in paediatrics.
  16. The newborn: The first days of life include important checks and screening, and some common problems such as jaundice need careful assessment.
  17. Respiratory illness in children: Respiratory infections and wheeze are the most common reasons children see a doctor or are admitted to hospital.
  18. Childhood infections and immunisation: Many childhood infections can be prevented by vaccination, and others need prompt recognition.
  19. Growth and nutrition in children: Growth is a sensitive marker of a child's health.
  20. Congenital heart disease: Congenital heart disease is the most common major birth defect.
  21. Safeguarding children: Every doctor has a duty to protect children from abuse and neglect.
  22. Common paediatric emergencies: Children can deteriorate quickly, and a structured approach saves lives.

School 12: Professional Practice, Prescribing and Population Health

20 lessons

Professional Practice, Prescribing and Population Health: key terms, sample flashcards and a quick quiz

  1. Good medical practice and professionalism: The General Medical Council sets the standards every UK doctor must meet, and medical students are held to the same values.
  2. Medical ethics: principles and frameworks: Ethical reasoning helps doctors make and justify difficult decisions.
  3. Consent and capacity: Valid consent and an understanding of capacity underpin every examination and treatment.
  4. Confidentiality and information governance: Patients must be able to trust doctors with personal information.
  5. Raising concerns and the duty of candour: Doctors and students have a duty to act when patient safety is at risk and to be open when things go wrong.
  6. Writing a safe prescription: Prescribing errors are common and can cause serious harm.
  7. Adverse drug reactions and interactions: Adverse drug reactions are a major cause of hospital admission and harm.
  8. Prescribing in renal and hepatic impairment: The kidneys and liver clear most drugs, so impairment of either can lead to accumulation and toxicity.
  9. Prescribing in pregnancy, children and older people: Pregnancy, childhood and older age each change how medicines act and the harms they can cause.
  10. Therapeutic drug monitoring: For a small number of medicines, measuring blood levels helps keep treatment effective and safe.
  11. Patient safety and human factors: Most harm in healthcare comes from flawed systems rather than careless individuals.
  12. Clinical governance and audit: Clinical governance is the framework through which NHS organisations are accountable for improving quality.
  13. Teamwork, handover and leadership: Safe care depends on how well people work together.
  14. Epidemiology: measures of disease frequency and association: Epidemiology measures how common disease is and how strongly it is linked to exposures.
  15. Study design and critical appraisal: Different research questions need different study designs, each with its own strengths and biases.
  16. Evidence-based medicine: Evidence-based medicine combines the best research evidence with clinical expertise and patient values.
  17. Screening: principles and criteria: Screening aims to find disease or risk early in people without symptoms, but it can harm as well as help.
  18. Health inequalities and the social determinants of health: Health differs systematically between social groups, and much of that difference is avoidable.
  19. Health promotion and behaviour change: Many leading causes of ill health are linked to behaviours such as smoking, inactivity and alcohol.
  20. The NHS and the organisation of healthcare: Doctors work within a complex health system that shapes what care patients receive.
