Privacy

A plain-English account of what Gradify keeps about you and where. It describes how the site works today. Last updated 24 November 2026.

Who's responsible. Gradify, getgradify.com and every Gradify site are run by Gradify Ltd, which is the controller of the personal data described on this page. See also the terms of use.

Contact. For anything about your personal data (a copy of it, a correction, removal from a launch list, or a question), email privacy@getgradify.com. For anything else about Gradify, email hello@getgradify.com.

No account: everything stays in this browser

Gradify works without an account. Everything you do is saved in this browser's storage, on this device:

  • notes, flashcards, reviews and plans (gradify.v1)
  • progress in each course (gradify.course.…)
  • your set-up answers and display settings (gradify.learner)

Gradify itself doesn't set cookies. The only cookies are Google's, for analytics and advertising measurement, and only if you accept them (below). Clearing this site's data in your browser deletes all of it.

With a Gradify account

When accounts are switched on and you sign in, the same things are saved to your account so they follow you between devices:

  • your profile and set-up answers
  • notes you upload, with their extracted text
  • study packs, flashcards and reviews
  • course progress, your answers to questions, and the lab challenges you meet or try (which challenge, whether you met it, and how long you worked at it)
  • study plans and activity
  • any SportGradify training logs or NurseGradify revalidation records you add

The data is stored with Supabase. Every table is protected so that only you can read your own rows: other learners can't, and neither can visitors who aren't signed in.

What you've opened on Free. To apply Free's allowances (such as 10 lessons in each course), Gradify keeps a list of the lessons, flashcard sets, mind maps and tool uses you've opened: in your account when you're signed in, so the allowances are the same on every device, or otherwise in your browser's storage on that device. It's used for nothing else, and it's deleted with your account.

Tools. Flashcard decks (with their review schedule), mind maps and images you make in Tools are kept in your browser's storage, and, when you're signed in, in your account, so they're on every device you use. Only you can see them, and they're deleted with your account.

Your study device. On Free you study on one device at a time, so Gradify keeps a random identifier for the device you study on, made by the app and stored in that browser, and when you last moved it. It identifies a browser, not you, and isn't used for anything else.

Why Gradify can keep it: you made an account so that Gradify can do this for you (the contract between you and Gradify). How long it's kept: until you delete it. Delete all my data, in Account in the Gradify app, removes your notes, study packs, cards, progress, plans and activity straight away. Your sign-in (your email address) and your set-up answers stay so you can still sign in; to close your account completely, email privacy@getgradify.com and it will be deleted within 30 days.

Your course details (MyGradify)

You can tell Gradify what and where you study, so it can organise your revision, plan and tracker around your actual course. It's optional, and every question can be skipped. Gradify asks for these eight things only:

  • the country where you study;
  • your university or college;
  • your subject;
  • your course or qualification;
  • the year you started;
  • which year or stage you're in now;
  • your study mode (for example full-time or part-time);
  • this year's modules.

Alongside them Gradify stores when you last changed them, and a note that they're entered by you and not checked with your university.

  • Where it's kept: with your Gradify profile, in your account when you're signed in (Supabase; only you can read your own profile), and in this browser so pages can use it straight away. If you aren't signed in, it stays in this browser.
  • What it's used for: suggesting what to study and shaping your plan and tracker. It isn't sent to Google Analytics, used for advertising, sent to Gradify AI unless a feature you choose says so, or shared with your university or anyone else.
  • While you're filling it in: your answers are kept in this browser tab's session storage until you save them, so going back a step loses nothing. They're cleared when you save, choose “Not now”, or close the tab. The nation or state you pick to narrow the search isn't saved.
  • Handbooks: if you import a handbook to find your modules, it's read in your browser and not uploaded or kept. Only the modules you tick are saved.
  • Changing or removing it: change it any time in Account, or choose Remove there. Removing it doesn't affect your notes, progress or plans.
  • Who it's for: university and college students. It isn't offered on JuniorGradify or YouthGradify.

Why Gradify can keep it: you chose to add it so Gradify can personalise your studying (the contract between you and Gradify). How long it's kept: until you remove it, or delete your data or account.

Calendar files

You can import a calendar file (.ics) into Smart Plan in the Gradify app, and into the degree tracker's Connections page. Both are optional, and neither is needed to use Gradify.

  • The file is read on your device, in your browser. It isn't uploaded to Gradify or anyone else, and it isn't kept after it's read.
  • Smart Plan keeps only when you're busy: the start and end of each event, labelled “Busy”, so your plan works around it. An event keeps its name only if you tick it as an exam or deadline on the review list. Other events' names, and every event's location, notes, guests and categories, are never kept. All-day events are kept only when they say you're away, as “Away”. These busy times are saved like the rest of your plan: in this browser, or in your account when you're signed in. Remove imported events, in Smart Plan, deletes them all.
  • The degree tracker keeps only the due dates you tick, in your tracker on this device. Nothing else from the file is kept.
  • Calendar information is never sent to Gradify AI, Google Analytics or anyone else, and isn't used for advertising.

No calendar links or sign-ins. Gradify doesn't take calendar links, Google, Microsoft, Moodle or Canvas sign-ins, or access tokens. A calendar link works like a password to your calendar, so Gradify stopped accepting them in November 2026. Links added before then are no longer used, and are removed from your account the next time you open the Gradify app (and for everyone by a clean-up of Gradify's database). Events already imported from them stay in your plan until you remove them. Connections is for university students, and isn't offered on JuniorGradify or YouthGradify.

Why Gradify can keep it: you chose to import the file so Gradify can plan around it (the contract between you and Gradify). How long it's kept: until you remove it, or delete your data or account.

Gradify AI

When you use a Gradify AI feature, the text it needs is sent from Gradify's server to Anthropic's Claude API to produce the answer. Depending on the feature, that's:

  • your notes
  • your written answer
  • the lesson's notes, which the server reads from the site itself
  • what an interactive lab on the page shows when you ask about it: its settings, results and challenges
  • the conversation so far
  • a summary of your progress
  • when you ask about your degree: the degree tracker's results (your averages, class, what each class needs, your modules' names and due dates, your university's rules if you chose one, and the tracker's insights), worked out in your browser. Not your files, and not a list of your marks: a single mark appears only where an insight names it (such as your lowest one)

Gradify saves the study packs it makes from your notes to your account, with a fingerprint of the notes so the same notes are never processed twice. It doesn't keep tutor conversations. For each AI request it records which feature you used, on which site, whether it worked, how much text it took (in tokens) and its estimated cost, so it can apply your monthly allowance and keep Gradify's spending in check. AI features need an account and only run when you choose to use them. Gradify AI is not switched on yet.

Don't put personal details about other people, such as patients or clients, into notes, answers or questions.

Launch lists

If you ask to be told when a Gradify site or Gradify Ultra opens, Gradify keeps:

  • your email address (stored in lower case),
  • which list you joined (for example "Gradify Ultra"),
  • when you joined.

Nothing else is collected: no account, no payment details, no name. The list is kept in Gradify's database (Supabase, in the UK).

What it's for: one email to tell you that thing has opened. It isn't used for newsletters or marketing, and it isn't shared or sold. Why Gradify can keep it: you asked to be told (your consent), and you can withdraw that at any time.

Study tips emails

If you ask for the free study plan and weekly tips on one of Gradify's guides, tools or templates pages, Gradify keeps the same three things in the same place: your email address, the list ("Study tips") and when you joined. It's used only to send that plan and one study tip a week. It isn't shared or sold. Why Gradify can keep it: you asked for it (your consent). Reply "stop" to any email, or email privacy@getgradify.com, and you're removed.

How long it's kept: your entry is deleted once that launch email has been sent, and in any case within 12 months of joining if it never opens.

Nobody can read the list through the website, including you, and joining the same list twice keeps one entry. To have your entry removed before then, email privacy@getgradify.com from the address you joined with.

Usage counts

To see whether Gradify is used, the site counts page views, finished lessons, finished exams and tutor requests. To see how people come to a paid plan, it also counts when an offer of Gradify Pro is shown or pressed, when the pricing page is opened, and when a checkout is started, paid or cancelled. On the free study pages (tools, guides, topics) it also counts using a tool, finishing a quiz, sharing, printing, exporting and opening the app, and whether the page loaded and responded quickly (just "good", "needs improvement" or "poor" for each of three speed measures, no timings). Each count records:

  • the site
  • the page address, without anything after # or ?
  • the website that linked to it, if any
  • whether you're on a phone, tablet or computer
  • the date

It records no name, account, cookie or identifier of any kind, and no time of day, so counts can't be linked to you or to each other. Nothing is counted if your browser sends Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control, or if you turn counting off here.

Google Analytics and Google Ads measurement (only if you accept)

Gradify asks before using Google's cookies, with two separate choices, both off until you say yes:

  • Analytics (Google Analytics): to see which pages and features help people study, and to find what's broken or confusing.
  • Advertising measurement (Google Ads): to see whether Gradify's own ads on Google led to a sign-up or a purchase, so Gradify spends less on ads that don't help anyone. It is never used to show you personalised ads or to follow you round other sites (remarketing).

“Accept all” turns both on; “Reject all”, or not choosing, keeps both off; “Manage preferences” lets you pick one. Until you accept one, no Google code loads and no Google cookie is set. Gradify doesn't sell this data.

Where neither ever runs. Not on JuniorGradify or YouthGradify, whatever you choose. Not if your browser sends Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control: Gradify treats those as a no and doesn't ask. Not if you've turned off the usage counts above.

What analytics collects, if you accept it. Google Analytics sets cookies named _ga and _ga_… holding a random identifier for this browser. With them, Google Analytics records:

  • the pages you visit, without anything after ? except campaign tags (utm_…), and without sign-in codes after #
  • the website that linked to Gradify (its address only, not the full link)
  • your device type, browser, screen size, language and approximate location (country and city, worked out by Google from your IP address, which Google Analytics 4 doesn't store)
  • study events: a lesson started or finished, a quiz or exam started or finished (passed or not, never your score or answers), a flashcard session started or finished (how many cards), the tutor opened, a free tool used, sharing, printing or exporting, a planned study block finished, signing up or signing in (only whether by email or Google), and, once per browser, the first time you finish a piece of learning (only which kind: a lesson, quiz, exam, flashcard session or study block). Clicks into the app from a free page say which kind of page it was (a tool, a guide, a feature page …)
  • if Gradify is testing two versions of something (for example the words on a button), which version you saw, kept in this browser (gradify.exp) and sent with the events above. Only if you accept analytics; otherwise you always see the normal version and nothing is kept
  • on the pricing page, starting checkout and a new subscription (the plan and its price, never payment details)

It never receives your name, email address, password, payment details, notes, flashcards, answers or scores. Google signals and ad personalisation are off.

What advertising measurement adds, if you accept it. When you arrive from one of Gradify's Google ads, Google's click identifier is kept in a cookie named _gcl_… (for up to 90 days), and Google is told when that visit leads to a sign-up, a checkout or a new subscription (with the plan and price). Google matches this to the ad click in its own systems. Nothing about your studying is used, and ad personalisation stays off, whatever you choose.

Why Gradify can use them: you agreed (your consent), separately for each. How long it's kept: the analytics cookies last up to 13 months, and Google Analytics keeps the detailed records for 2 months before deleting them; totals (such as visits per page per month) are kept. Google's role: for analytics, Google Ireland Limited and Google LLC process this for Gradify under Google's data processing terms. For advertising measurement, Google uses the ad-click data under its own controller terms and privacy policy (policies.google.com/privacy). The data can be handled in the United States. That transfer is covered by the UK safeguards described below.

Change your mind any time. Use the button here or “Cookie settings” at the bottom of the site's pages. Turning a choice off deletes its Google cookies in this browser and stops it from the next page. Your choice is kept in this browser (gradify.consent), so you'll be asked again in another browser or if you clear this site's data.

Who else handles your data

Gradify uses a few services to run. Each only gets what it needs to do its job:

  • Supabase stores accounts, study data and launch lists, and handles sign-in.
  • Google signs you in, only if you choose “Sign in with Google”.
  • Google Analytics counts visits and feature use, and Google Ads measures whether Gradify's ads lead to sign-ups and purchases, each only if you accept it, as described above.
  • Google Fonts supplies the fonts on every page, so Google sees your device's IP address when a page loads.
  • GitHub Pages serves the website itself, and sees your IP address like any web host.
  • Anthropic (Claude) writes Gradify AI's answers, once Gradify AI is switched on, as described above. Anthropic is in the United States.
  • Stripe takes payments for Gradify Pro and Gradify Ultra. It receives your email address and your payment details; Gradify keeps your Stripe customer number, your plan, its status and renewal date, never your card number.

Where one of these services handles personal data outside the UK, the transfer is covered by the safeguards UK data protection law requires, such as the UK's international data transfer agreement or addendum.

Children

Accounts are for people aged 13 and over. Children under 13, including JuniorGradify learners, can use Gradify without an account, where everything stays in the browser on that device, with a parent or guardian. If you think a child under 13 has made an account, email privacy@getgradify.com and it will be deleted.

Your choices

  • Download your data, or delete your notes, cards and progress, from Account in the Gradify app. Your sign-in and set-up answers stay so you can still sign in; to close your account completely, email privacy@getgradify.com.
  • Ask about, correct or remove anything Gradify holds about you: privacy@getgradify.com.
  • Use Gradify without an account, and nothing leaves this browser except the anonymous counts above, and Google analytics or advertising measurement if you accept them.
  • Turn the counts off on this page (that turns Google analytics and advertising measurement off too).
  • Accept, reject or withdraw analytics and advertising measurement with “Cookie settings”.

Under UK data protection law you can ask for a copy of your data, ask for it to be corrected or deleted, object to how it's used, and withdraw consent you've given. If you're unhappy with how Gradify has handled your data, please tell Gradify first; you can also complain to the Information Commissioner's Office (ico.org.uk).
