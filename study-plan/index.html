Art and Design

  • GCSE Art and DesignThe visual elements, drawing, colour, a range of media and the creative process behind the portfolio and the externally set assignment.
  • A level Art and DesignDeeper practice and critical study: media, contextual art history, the personal investigation and the written related study.
  • History of artArt from prehistory to now, and the methods art historians use to read it: formalism, iconography, social and feminist histories.
  • Graphic and digital designVisual language, colour, typography, photography and digital image-making for communication design.
  • Portfolio and art schoolFrom sketchbook to portfolio: drawing, process, critical writing, documentation and applying to foundation and degree courses.

Biology

  • GCSE BiologyThe core of secondary-school biology: cells, biomolecules, genetics, the human body and ecosystems.
  • A level BiologyEverything for A level and equivalent exams, from cells to ecosystems.
  • University year 1The first-year core of a biological sciences degree.
  • Pre-medicine and human biologyThe biology behind medicine: cells, physiology, microbes and immunity.
  • Genetics and genomicsFrom DNA and inheritance to genome sequencing and gene editing.
  • Computational biologySequences, alignment, phylogenetics, omics and reproducible data science.
  • Ecology and conservationEvolution, plants, ecosystems and protecting biodiversity.
  • Research-readyExperimental design, laboratory techniques, statistics and research integrity.

Business and Management

  • A level BusinessThe A level content: the environment, management, the four functions and strategy.
  • New managerLeading people, managing money, making strategy and governing well.
  • Starting a businessFrom an idea to a funded, legal, growing venture.
  • Business degree, first yearEvery school, in the order a business school teaches them.

Careers and Employability

  • Landing a graduate jobFrom self-assessment and research to CVs, applications, tests, interviews and assessment centres.
  • Winning an internship or placementFinding experience, building a network and getting through selection for a summer internship or placement year.
  • Starting work wellContracts, pay, rights, probation and the first months in a job, with resilience for the long run.
  • Working for yourselfSelf-knowledge, freelancing, enterprise and planning a career that you build yourself.

Chemistry

  • GCSE ChemistryThe core of secondary-school chemistry: atoms, the periodic table, bonding, moles, energy and rates.
  • A level ChemistryEverything for A level and equivalent exams, physical, inorganic and organic.
  • University year 1The first-year core of a chemistry degree.
  • Organic chemistryStructure, stereochemistry, mechanisms, functional groups and synthesis.
  • Medicinal chemistryFrom organic mechanisms to drug targets, ADME, docking and drug design.
  • Computational chemistryQuantum chemistry, molecular modelling, cheminformatics and machine learning.
  • Analytical chemistMeasurement, titration, spectroscopy, chromatography and laboratory practice.
  • Materials and sustainabilityStates of matter, inorganic materials, the environment and green chemistry.

Dentistry

  • The BDS coreEvery school in order, from anatomy to professional practice, following the GDC's Preparing for Practice outcomes.
  • BDS year 1The biomedical foundations: anatomy, teeth and their tissues, oral physiology, microbiology and infection control.
  • Hygiene and therapyThe core for dental hygienists and therapists: tissues, prevention, caries, periodontology, radiography and professionalism.
  • Before your first clinicInfection control, radiation protection, pain control, the medically complex patient and the GDC Standards.
  • Restorative and materialsCaries, materials, operative dentistry, endodontics and prosthodontics.

Design Thinking and Creativity

  • GCSE and A level Design and TechnologyIterative design, briefs and specifications, prototyping, materials, manufacture, sustainability and designers, as in D&T specifications.
  • UX and interaction designUser research, problem definition, prototyping and usability testing, interface principles and WCAG accessibility.
  • Design thinking for anyoneThe creative process and the Double Diamond for any problem: creativity, ideation, empathy, framing and testing.
  • Responsible designDesigning for everyone and for the planet: inclusive design, services and systems, circular design and ethics.
  • Working as a designerCollaboration, critique, pitching, portfolios and protecting your ideas, with the visual and prototyping skills behind them.

Ecology and Environmental Science

  • A level Biology ecologyThe ecology of A level Biology: populations, communities, ecosystems, nutrient cycles, biodiversity and the fieldwork and statistics behind them.
  • A level Environmental ScienceThe AQA A level Environmental Science course: the living and physical environment, energy, pollution, resources, sustainability and research methods.
  • University ecology, years 1 and 2The core of an ecology degree: from individuals and populations to communities and ecosystems, with field methods and statistics.
  • ConservationWhat biodiversity is, why it is being lost and what the evidence says works to protect and restore it.
  • Climate and the carbon cycleHow the climate system works, how we know it is changing, what that does to ecosystems and how societies respond.

Engineering

  • University year 1The common first year of most engineering degrees: maths, statics, dynamics, materials, circuits and an introduction to design and professional practice.
  • Mechanical engineeringMechanics, materials, thermofluids, control and machine design: the core of a mechanical degree.
  • Civil and structural engineeringStatics, mechanics of materials, materials, fluids and the safety, sustainability and project skills infrastructure depends on.
  • Electrical and electronic engineeringCircuits, electronics, machines, signals and control, built on the mathematics they need.
  • The professional engineerEthics, safety, sustainability, projects and the road to registration as EngTech, IEng or CEng.

Accounting and Finance

  • A level AccountingThe core of A level Accounting: double entry, final accounts, partnerships, company accounts, ratio analysis, costing, budgeting and decision-making.
  • Professional exams: knowledge levelThe ground covered by the first professional accountancy papers: financial accounting, management accounting and the business and legal context.
  • University year 1The first-year core of an accounting and finance degree: recording, reporting, costing, financial management and markets.
  • Financial reportingFrom the double-entry system to IFRS company and group accounts, their analysis and the wider corporate report.
  • Corporate financeInvestment appraisal, working capital, the cost of capital, valuation, markets and risk. Education, not investment advice.
  • Audit, tax and lawThe professional practice papers: audit and ethics, UK taxation and the law that governs companies and accountants.

GCSE Core

  • The GCSE coreEvery school in order, from how to revise to life after Year 11.
  • English LanguageRevision skills, then both papers: reading fiction and non-fiction, and writing to describe, narrate and argue.
  • Combined ScienceRevision skills, then biology, chemistry and physics with every required practical and equation.
  • GeographyRevision skills, physical and human geography, map skills, fieldwork and issue evaluation.
  • Year 11 exam yearRevision and exam technique first, then the subjects, then next steps after GCSEs.

Health and Wellbeing

  • BTEC and T Level Health and Social CareThe core of BTEC Nationals and the T Level: human body systems, health and wellbeing, illness, public health, services and the life course.
  • Public healthDeterminants, inequalities, epidemiology, prevention and behaviour change: the public health core of a health studies degree.
  • Healthy livingThe evidence behind eating well, moving more, sleeping well and looking after your mental health. Educational, not personal advice.
  • Health studies: university year 1The first-year core of a health studies or public health degree, aligned with the QAA Health Studies benchmark.
  • Body and diseaseHow the body works and what happens when it goes wrong: systems, infection and long-term conditions.

History

  • The whole courseEvery school in order, from historical method to exam technique.
  • GCSE pathwayMethod, then the periods most GCSE specifications study: medieval, Elizabethan, the world wars, Germany, the Cold War and modern Britain, ending with exam practice. Check your own board's specification.
  • A level pathwayMethod, the Tudors and Stuarts, Russia, Germany, the Cold War, the USA and revolutions, with the independent investigation and exam practice. Check your own board's specification.
  • Britain, 1066 to todayA continuous story of British history.
  • The modern worldTwentieth-century conflict, ideology and empire beyond Britain.

Investing

  • The whole courseEvery school in order, from money basics to a written plan.
  • First-time investorSaving first, how markets work, the main assets, funds and costs, UK wrappers, protection and a plan.
  • Investment analysisThe theory behind portfolios and prices: risk and return, portfolio theory, valuation and company accounts.
  • Industry foundationsThe topics of the CISI Fundamentals of Financial Services and CFA Investment Foundations: markets, assets, risk, regulation and ethics.
  • UK saver and pensionsCash, ISAs, pensions, tax, consumer protection and planning through life.

Japanese

  • GCSE JapaneseKana, the first kanji, core grammar from desu to the te-form and plain forms, the GCSE themes and exam skills.
  • JLPT N5Hiragana, katakana, around a hundred kanji, the masu form, particles, adjectives and counters.
  • JLPT N4The te-form, plain forms, potential, volitional, conditionals, giving and receiving, the passive and basic keigo.
  • JLPT N3Aspect, hearsay and appearance, purpose and reasoning, keigo in use, kanji to around 650 and reading longer texts.
  • Grammar in orderFrom X wa Y desu to the causative-passive and N3 nuance, one structure at a time.
  • Japanese for travelKana, greetings, numbers, prices, directions, transport, food, polite shop Japanese and etiquette.
  • Japan: culture and societyHistory, religion, festivals, the arts, popular culture, work, an ageing society, dialects and the writing system.

Law

  • LLB: the foundations of legal knowledgeThe core of a qualifying law degree in England and Wales, in the order most universities teach it.
  • Graduate Diploma in Law (conversion)The seven foundation subjects for non-law graduates, compressed into one year, with the skills to study them.
  • SQE1 · FLK1Business Law and Practice, Dispute Resolution, Contract, Tort, the Legal System, Constitutional and Administrative Law, EU Law and Legal Services, with ethics throughout. 180 single-best-answer questions.
  • SQE1 · FLK2Property Practice, Wills and the Administration of Estates, Solicitors' Accounts, Land Law, Trusts, and Criminal Law and Practice, with ethics throughout. 180 single-best-answer questions.
  • SQE2: practical legal skillsInterviewing and attendance notes, advocacy, case and matter analysis, research, writing and drafting, in the five practice contexts, with realistic case files.
  • Preparing for the BarAcademic preparation for barristers: legal foundations, criminal and civil advocacy, evidence, procedure, opinion writing, drafting, conference skills and ethics. This is study, not Bar training.
  • Law across the UKWhat changes in Scotland and Northern Ireland: courts, legislatures, contract, delict, crime, property, succession and qualification.
  • Postgraduate and advanced studyThe public, international and theoretical subjects LLM and research students build on.
  • Understanding the lawFor anyone curious about how law works: what it is, who makes it, how courts decide and what rights you have.

English Literature

  • GCSE English LiteratureClose reading, poetry, Shakespeare, the nineteenth-century novel, war and Romantic poetry, unseen poetry and exam essays.
  • A level English LiteratureThe A level core: forms, literary history, critical views and theory, comparison, unseen analysis and the coursework essay.
  • University year 1The first-year core of an English degree: close reading, poetics, literary history from Chaucer to now, and theory.
  • PoetryHow poems work, from metre and form to the Romantics, the Victorians and the poets of the First World War.
  • Writing about literatureFrom annotation to a finished argument: close reading, critical views and the essay itself.

Mathematics

  • Everyday maths and Functional SkillsMoney, measures, time, data and risk, with the number skills behind them: for adult learners, Functional Skills and anyone rebuilding confidence.
  • GCSE MathematicsNumber, algebra, ratio, geometry and measures, probability and statistics, with exam technique: Foundation and Higher tier.
  • A level MathematicsProof, algebra and functions, coordinate geometry, sequences, trigonometry, exponentials and logarithms, calculus, vectors, numerical methods, statistics and mechanics.
  • A level Further MathematicsComplex numbers, matrices, proof by induction, further calculus and vectors, polar coordinates, hyperbolic functions, differential equations and further statistics.
  • University mathematics: first yearThe bridge to degree-level maths: proof and logic, sets, calculus and analysis, linear algebra, differential equations, probability and an introduction to groups.
  • Pure mathematics: degree and beyondAbstract algebra, analysis, topology, number theory, geometry, logic and foundations.
  • Applied mathematics and engineeringCalculus, vector calculus, differential equations, linear algebra, numerical methods, modelling and control.
  • Mathematics for data science and AILinear algebra, probability, statistics, optimisation, time series, information theory and the mathematics of machine learning, with the software to apply it.
  • Financial and actuarial mathematicsInterest and valuation, probability and statistics, stochastic models, risk, time series and optimisation.
  • Discrete mathematics and computer scienceLogic, sets and proof, combinatorics, graphs and networks, number theory and cryptography, information and coding.
  • University entrance tests (MAT, TMUA, STEP)Problem solving and proof on top of A level content, for Oxford, Cambridge and other competitive courses.

Medicine

  • Preclinical yearsThe science that underpins clinical medicine and the core clinical skills of the consultation and examination.
  • UKMLA preparationEvery area of the MLA content map: the clinical schools, acute care and professional knowledge. Revision, not a substitute for your school's teaching.
  • Acute and emergency medicineRecognising and managing the acutely unwell patient: ABCDE, NEWS2, sepsis, shock, arrests and the emergencies of each system.
  • General practice and long-term conditionsThe conditions managed mostly in primary care: hypertension, diabetes, asthma and COPD, mental health, children and prevention.
  • Prescribing safelyPharmacology, therapeutics and the prescribing skills assessed in the Prescribing Safety Assessment. Education, not prescribing advice.

Music

  • ABRSM Theory Grades 1–5Notation, rhythm, scales, keys, intervals, chords, cadences, terms and instruments: the ground the Grade 5 theory exam covers.
  • ABRSM Theory Grades 6–8Four-part harmony, figured bass, chromatic chords, counterpoint and stylistic composition.
  • GCSE MusicThe elements of music, set-work styles, instruments, popular and world music, with listening and appraising.
  • A level MusicHarmony, voice leading, form, the Western classical tradition from Baroque to modern, jazz and popular music, analysis and composition.
  • University year 1First-year tonal theory, aural skills, counterpoint, music history and analysis.
  • Ear and musicianshipTrain the ear: rhythm, intervals, chords, cadences and dictation, built on the theory behind them.
  • Music in history and cultureFrom plainchant to film music, jazz and the music of the wider world.

Nursing

  • Adult nursingThe shared core, then adult nursing across medical, surgical and community settings, acute and critical care, older adults and end-of-life care, with field scenarios, placement preparation and reflection.
  • Children's nursingThe shared core, then children's and young people's nursing: development, family-centred care, consent and parental responsibility, safeguarding, paediatric assessment, neonates, childhood conditions and medicines safety.
  • Mental health nursingThe shared core, then mental health nursing: assessment, therapeutic relationships, conditions, risk and crisis, mental health and capacity law, de-escalation and recovery, with physical health care for people with mental illness.
  • Learning disability nursingThe shared core, then learning disability nursing: communication, reasonable adjustments, capacity and advocacy, positive behaviour support, health inequalities, epilepsy and complex needs, families and inclusion.
  • Pre-registration year 1 (all fields)Professional practice, the body, assessment and core skills: the foundations every field of nursing builds on.
  • Recognising and responding to deteriorationAssessment, observations, escalation, acute and critical care, and the conditions behind deterioration.
  • Medicines managementPharmacology, the administration process, calculations and medicines safety. Education only: you give medicines under your local policy and supervision.
  • Newly qualified nurseAccountability, delegation, prioritisation, escalation, medicines safety and handover for your first post.
  • Internationally educated nurses: UK practiceThe NMC Code, UK law and policy across the four nations, assessment conventions and medicines governance in the UK.
  • Nursing associatesThe foundations for nursing associates, within the NMC's standards of proficiency for nursing associates and your local scope.
  • CPD and revalidationRefresh professional standards, evidence-based practice, leadership and medicines knowledge, and record it as CPD.

Pharmacy

  • MPharm: the sciencesThe professional foundations, then how drugs act, how the body handles them, their chemistry and their formulation, with calculations throughout.
  • MPharm: therapeuticsDisease, drugs and patients system by system: cardiovascular, respiratory, endocrine, mental health and neurology, infection, gastrointestinal and renal, inflammation and skin, cancer and immunology, and special populations.
  • MPharm: practice and prescribingLaw, ethics and governance, clinical pharmacy and medicines optimisation, and the clinical assessment and prescribing skills you'll need as an independent prescriber at registration.
  • Registration assessment preparationThe assessment format and framework, calculations, law and the therapeutics that come up most, with single-best-answer practice. Check the GPhC's current framework before you sit.
  • Foundation training yearWhat your foundation year involves, with the clinical pharmacy, prescribing, law and medicines optimisation you'll use every day.
  • Pharmacy techniciansThe professional foundations, medicines law, dispensing, formulation, calculations and medicines optimisation, within the GPhC's standards for pharmacy technicians and your local scope.
  • Pharmacist prescribersClinical assessment, prescribing decisions and governance, with the therapeutics and special populations behind them, for pharmacists preparing to prescribe or extending their scope.
  • Practising pharmacists: CPDRefresh law, clinical pharmacy, prescribing and the therapeutics that change most, and record it for GPhC revalidation.

Physics

  • A level PhysicsEverything for A level and equivalent exams, in exam order.
  • University year 1The first-year core of a physics degree.
  • Quantum physicsFrom the photoelectric effect to quantum computers.
  • Astrophysics and cosmologyStars, galaxies, black holes and the universe.
  • Medical physicsThe physics behind imaging, radiotherapy and the body.
  • Engineering physicsPhysics for engineers: materials, energy, vibration and electronics.
  • Theoretical physicsThe mathematics and theory behind modern physics.
  • Research-readySkills for projects, dissertations and a career in physics.

Primary

  • Key Stage 1 (ages 5 to 7)The first steps: counting, place value, adding and taking away, phonics, early reading, and plants and animals.
  • Getting ready for Year 6 SATsThe maths and English that the Key Stage 2 tests cover: number, calculation, fractions, measures, reading, grammar, punctuation and spelling.
  • Maths from Year 1 to Year 6Every maths school in order, from counting to 20 to fractions, ratio, geometry and statistics.
  • Primary scienceLiving things, materials, forces, light, sound, electricity, and the Earth in space.
  • Our world: history and geographyBritain from the Stone Age to the Vikings, ancient civilisations, and the places, maps and landscapes of the world.

Professional Skills

  • Starting your careerThe essential skills of a first professional job: communication, teamwork, productivity, problem solving and career growth.
  • New managerThe CMI Level 5 core: leading and managing people, change, decisions, projects, ethics and employment basics.
  • Project deliveryPlanning and delivering projects with APM, PRINCE2 and Agile principles, backed by data and decision skills.
  • Business and commercialHow organisations make money, measure success and stay on the right side of ethics and the law.
  • Essential skillsThe eight Skills Builder essential skills: listening, speaking, problem solving, creativity, staying positive, aiming high, leadership and teamwork.

Psychology

  • A level PsychologyThe core of A level and equivalent courses: approaches, biopsychology, memory, attachment, social influence, psychopathology and research methods.
  • University year 1The first-year core of a psychology degree: foundations, biological, cognitive, developmental and social psychology, with methods and statistics.
  • Research skillsFrom a research question to a reported result: design, ethics, measurement, statistics and open science.
  • Mind and brainNeurones, brain systems, perception, memory, learning and the new computational theories of mind.
  • Mental healthHow mental disorders are understood, classified and treated, in context and across cultures. Educational, not clinical advice.
  • Applied psychologyPsychology at school, at work, in health, sport, law and the design of technology.
  • People and societyDevelopment, social behaviour, personality, culture and evolution: psychology's bridge to SocGradify.

Research and the Dissertation

  • Master's dissertationFrom a question to a submitted dissertation: the literature review, design, methods, ethics and writing up.
  • The doctoral journeyEverything a new PhD or MPhil researcher meets, in the order they meet it: supervision, philosophy, design, integrity, the thesis and the viva.
  • Quantitative researcherDesign, measurement, inference and statistical models, reported and shared reproducibly.
  • Qualitative researcherParadigms, reflexivity, interviews, observation and the main analytic approaches, from thematic analysis to grounded theory.
  • Ethics, integrity and open researchEthical approval, the UK integrity framework, data protection and open, reproducible research.
  • Publishing and careersGetting work published, presented and used, and the careers a research degree leads to.

Sociology

  • A level SociologyThe core of A level and equivalent courses: theory, culture and identity, families, education, crime, beliefs and methods.
  • University year 1The first-year core of a sociology degree: the discipline, classical and contemporary theory, inequality and methods.
  • Social theoryFrom Comte, Marx, Durkheim and Weber to feminism, Foucault, Bourdieu, Giddens and Beck.
  • Inequality and social justiceClass, poverty, gender, race and migration, and how they shape education, health and life chances.
  • Research skillsResearch design, interviews, ethnography, content and discourse analysis, official data, reflexivity, statistics and reporting.
  • Society and policyThe state, power, welfare, crime, health and the economy: sociology for policy and public life.
  • Global and urbanGlobalisation, cities, population, migration and the environment.

Spanish

  • GCSE Spanish: FoundationThe sounds, core grammar and everyday topics of the GCSE Foundation tier, with exam skills.
  • GCSE Spanish: HigherEverything for the Higher tier: present, past and future time frames, object pronouns, the conditional and a first look at the subjunctive.
  • A level SpanishSecure the past and future, master the subjunctive, por and para and advanced structures, and study Hispanic society and culture.
  • Grammar in orderThe verb system from the present to the pluperfect subjunctive, with pronouns, prepositions and the passive.
  • Spanish for travelPronunciation, greetings, numbers, food, directions, transport, hotels and the regional Spanish you will hear.
  • Cultures of the Spanish-speaking worldSpain, Latin America and the US: history, festivals, art, film, music and how Spanish varies from place to place.

Sport and Exercise Science

  • A level Physical EducationThe theory core of AQA and OCR A level PE: applied anatomy and physiology, energy systems, biomechanics, training, skill acquisition, sport psychology and sport and society.
  • University year 1The first-year core of a sport and exercise science degree: anatomy, physiology, metabolism, biomechanics, testing and psychology.
  • Strength and conditioningThe science behind programming: muscle, energy systems, adaptation, training methods, testing, nutrition and injury risk.
  • CoachingHow people learn and perform skills, how to motivate and lead them, plan sessions and seasons, and keep athletes safe.
  • Exercise and healthPhysical activity guidelines, the physiology of health, special populations, nutrition and getting people active. Educational, not medical advice.

Teaching

  • The ITTECF coreThe Teachers' Standards in the order the Initial Teacher Training and Early Career Framework sets them out: learning, curriculum, classroom practice, adaptive teaching, assessment, behaviour and professional behaviours.
  • A PGCE yearThe ground a postgraduate teacher-training year covers, from how pupils learn to safeguarding, planning and the school system.
  • Early career teacherFor the first two years in post: behaviour, assessment, adaptive teaching, workload and using evidence well.
  • Safeguarding and welfareKeeping children safe, SEND, behaviour and professional conduct. Always follow your own school's policies.
  • Evidence-informed practiceThe science of learning, what the research does and doesn't show, and how schools put evidence to work.

University Study Skills

  • First-year essentialsEverything a new undergraduate needs in the first semester: how university works, how to learn, time, lectures, reading, writing and referencing.
  • Learn betterThe science of learning applied: memory, retrieval and spacing, time and focus, revision and exams, feedback and reflection.
  • Essays and assignmentsFrom reading and critical thinking to planning, writing, referencing and acting on feedback.
  • Research and dataFinding and judging information, small research projects, numeracy and statistics for coursework and dissertations.
  • Beyond the degreePresentations, teamwork, reflection and employability: building and evidencing the skills employers ask for.

Veterinary Science

  • The whole degreeEvery school in order, preclinical to clinical, as a UK veterinary degree runs.
  • Preclinical sciencesAnatomy, physiology, biochemistry, pathology and infection: the science behind the clinic.
  • Companion animal practiceFrom the dog and cat's anatomy to small-animal medicine, surgery and the profession.
  • Farm and equine practiceHusbandry, welfare, herd health, the horse and public health.
  • For veterinary nurses and support staffWelfare, anatomy, physiology, pharmacology principles, clinical examination and the team.
