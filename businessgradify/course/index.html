Free Business and Management course: revision notes, flashcards and questions

BusinessGradify's free Business and Management course runs from The Business Environment to Ethics, ESG and Corporate Governance: 178 lessons in 11 schools, each with notes, flashcards and check questions at three levels (Foundation, Application, Evaluation). It's written for business students and managers.

Education, not business, legal, tax or financial advice. BusinessGradify teaches business and management for study. The lessons were drafted with AI assistance and haven't yet been reviewed by subject experts, so they may contain errors; check important points against your course materials. Laws, tax rules, thresholds and economic figures change: for a decision about a real business, check GOV.UK, Companies House and HMRC, and speak to a qualified accountant, solicitor or business adviser (in England, your local Growth Hub can point you to free support).

Schools and lessons

School 1: The Business Environment

16 lessons

The Business Environment: key terms, sample flashcards and a quick quiz

  1. What is a business?: A business is an organisation that combines resources to produce goods or services that meet customers' needs, usually in exchange for payment.
  2. Business purpose and objectives: Every organisation needs to know what it is for and what it is trying to achieve.
  3. Stakeholders: Stakeholders are the individuals and groups who affect or are affected by a business.
  4. The private, public and third sectors: The UK economy is organised into three broad sectors by who owns organisations and why they exist: the private sector, the public sector and the third (voluntary and community) sector.
  5. Sole traders and partnerships: Most UK businesses are run by one person or a small group without a separate legal identity.
  6. Private and public limited companies: A company is a separate legal person, owned by shareholders and run by directors, whose owners have limited liability.
  7. Social enterprises and co-operatives: Not every business is owned by investors seeking returns.
  8. Franchising: Franchising lets a business grow by licensing others to run outlets under its brand and system.
  9. PESTLE analysis: Businesses operate in an external environment they can't control but must anticipate.
  10. Markets and competition: A market is where buyers and sellers exchange goods or services.
  11. The economic environment: Every business is affected by the state of the wider economy.
  12. The legal and regulatory environment: Law sets the rules within which businesses compete.
  13. Technology and social change: Technological and social change create new markets and destroy old ones.
  14. Globalisation and international business: Globalisation is the growing integration of the world's economies through trade, investment, technology and people.
  15. Business size and growth: Businesses range from one-person enterprises to global corporations.
  16. Why businesses fail: Many new businesses close within a few years, and even large, famous firms collapse.

School 2: Management and Organisational Behaviour

18 lessons

Management and Organisational Behaviour: key terms, sample flashcards and a quick quiz

  1. What is management?: Management is getting things done through other people and through the organisation's resources.
  2. Classical management theory: Taylor and Fayol: Classical management theory, developed around 1900–1920, tried to find the one best way to run work and organisations.
  3. Mintzberg's managerial roles: Henry Mintzberg watched what managers actually did and found work very different from the tidy functions of classical theory.
  4. The human relations movement: The human relations movement shifted attention from tasks to people.
  5. Organisational structure: Structure is how an organisation divides, groups and co-ordinates its work.
  6. Centralisation and decentralisation: Centralisation and decentralisation describe where decisions are made.
  7. Bureaucracy and Weber: Max Weber described bureaucracy as the most rational and efficient form of organisation for large-scale administration.
  8. Contingency theory and organisational design: Contingency theory says there is no one best way to organise: the right structure depends on the situation.
  9. Motivation: content theories: Content theories of motivation ask what needs drive people at work.
  10. Motivation: process theories: Process theories explain how people decide how much effort to put in.
  11. Leadership theories: Leadership theories try to explain what makes a leader effective.
  12. Leadership styles: A leadership style is a typical way a leader makes decisions and treats followers.
  13. Teams and group working: Much work is done in groups and teams.
  14. Communication in organisations: Communication is how information and meaning pass between people in an organisation.
  15. Organisational culture: Organisational culture is "the way we do things around here".
  16. Power and politics in organisations: Power is the capacity to influence others, and organisational politics is the use of power to pursue interests.
  17. Managing change: Organisations must change to survive, but change often fails.
  18. Decision-making in organisations: Managers make decisions under uncertainty and with limited time and information.

School 3: Strategy

16 lessons

Strategy: key terms, sample flashcards and a quick quiz

  1. What is strategy?: Strategy is the long-term direction of an organisation: the choices about where it competes and how it will win.
  2. Mission, vision and objectives: Mission and vision statements express why an organisation exists and what it hopes to become, and strategic objectives turn them into targets.
  3. Planned and emergent strategy: Some strategies are deliberately planned; others emerge from patterns of action.
  4. Porter's five forces: Porter's five forces framework analyses the competitive structure of an industry to judge how attractive it is.
  5. The industry life cycle: Industries, like products, pass through stages of introduction, growth, maturity and decline.
  6. SWOT analysis: SWOT analysis summarises an organisation's internal strengths and weaknesses and the external opportunities and threats it faces.
  7. Resources and capabilities: The resource-based view explains competitive advantage by what a firm has and can do, not just by the industry it is in.
  8. The value chain: Porter's value chain breaks a firm's activities into primary and support activities to show where value and cost are created.
  9. Core competences and the VRIO framework: Not every strength gives lasting advantage.
  10. Porter's generic strategies: Porter argued that a firm can achieve competitive advantage through lower cost or differentiation, across a broad market or a narrow focus.
  11. The Ansoff matrix: The Ansoff matrix sets out four directions for growth based on whether products and markets are existing or new.
  12. The Boston Matrix and portfolio analysis: Portfolio analysis helps a firm decide how to allocate resources between its products or business units.
  13. Mergers, acquisitions and alliances: Firms can grow organically or by combining with others through mergers, acquisitions, joint ventures and strategic alliances.
  14. Blue ocean strategy: Blue ocean strategy argues that firms can create uncontested market space rather than competing head-on.
  15. Strategy implementation: A strategy only creates value once it is put into practice.
  16. The balanced scorecard: The balanced scorecard measures performance from four perspectives, not just financial results.

School 4: Marketing

18 lessons

Marketing: key terms, sample flashcards and a quick quiz

  1. What is marketing?: Marketing is the process of understanding customers and creating value for them profitably.
  2. Market research: Market research gives businesses the information they need to understand customers and markets.
  3. Segmentation, targeting and positioning: Few products appeal to everyone.
  4. Consumer behaviour: Understanding why and how people buy helps businesses design and market their products.
  5. Business-to-business marketing: Business-to-business (B2B) marketing sells to organisations rather than individual consumers.
  6. Product and the product life cycle: The product is the core of the marketing mix.
  7. Pricing strategies: Price is the only element of the marketing mix that directly generates revenue.
  8. Promotion and marketing communications: Promotion communicates with customers to inform, persuade and remind them.
  9. Place and distribution channels: Place is about getting products to customers where and when they want them.
  10. The extended marketing mix: Services and customer experience need more than the four Ps.
  11. Branding and brand equity: Brands identify products and carry meaning that adds value.
  12. Customer relationship management: Keeping customers is usually cheaper than winning new ones.
  13. Services marketing: Services differ from physical goods in ways that shape how they are marketed.
  14. Digital and social media marketing: Digital channels have transformed how businesses reach and engage customers.
  15. Marketing metrics: Marketers must show that their spending creates value.
  16. Sustainable and ethical marketing: Marketing shapes consumption, so it carries responsibilities to society and the environment.
  17. International marketing: Selling across borders brings opportunities and challenges.
  18. The marketing plan: A marketing plan turns analysis into action.

School 5: Operations and Supply Chain Management

16 lessons

Operations and Supply Chain Management: key terms, sample flashcards and a quick quiz

  1. What is operations management?: Operations management is about how organisations create and deliver their goods and services.
  2. The transformation model and the four Vs: All operations transform inputs into outputs.
  3. Process types and layouts: The way work flows through an operation depends on its process type and layout.
  4. Capacity management: Capacity management matches an operation's ability to produce with demand.
  5. Quality management: Quality is central to customer satisfaction and cost.
  6. Total quality management: Total quality management (TQM) makes quality the responsibility of everyone in an organisation.
  7. Six Sigma: Six Sigma is a data-driven approach to reducing variation and defects.
  8. Lean production: Lean production, developed at Toyota, aims to deliver value with minimum waste.
  9. Supply chain management: Products reach customers through networks of suppliers, manufacturers, distributors and retailers.
  10. Inventory management: Inventory protects against uncertainty but ties up cash.
  11. Just-in-time: Just-in-time (JIT) aims to produce and deliver exactly what is needed, when it is needed.
  12. Procurement and supplier relationships: Procurement obtains the goods and services an organisation needs, and supplier relationships shape cost, quality and risk.
  13. Logistics: Logistics moves and stores goods through supply chains.
  14. Supply chain risk and resilience: Supply chains face disruptions from natural disasters, pandemics, conflicts and accidents.
  15. Productivity and efficiency: Productivity measures how well inputs are turned into outputs.
  16. Project management: Projects are temporary endeavours with defined goals.

School 6: People Management

15 lessons

People Management: key terms, sample flashcards and a quick quiz

  1. What is human resource management?: Human resource management (HRM) is the management of people at work to achieve organisational goals.
  2. Workforce planning: Workforce planning ensures an organisation has the right people, with the right skills, in the right place at the right time.
  3. Recruitment and selection: Recruitment attracts candidates and selection chooses the best.
  4. Employment law essentials: Employment law sets minimum rights for workers and duties for employers.
  5. Learning and development: Learning and development (L&D) builds the skills an organisation needs.
  6. Performance management: Performance management aligns individual work with organisational goals.
  7. Reward and pay: Reward systems attract, retain and motivate people.
  8. Talent management: Talent management attracts, develops and retains people with high performance and potential.
  9. Employee engagement and wellbeing: Engaged and well employees perform better and are more likely to stay.
  10. Employment relations and trade unions: Employment relations concerns the relationship between employers, employees and their representatives.
  11. Equality, diversity and inclusion: Equality, diversity and inclusion (EDI) aims to treat people fairly, value differences and ensure everyone can contribute.
  12. Flexible working and the future of work: Work is changing through flexible and hybrid working, technology and new forms of employment.
  13. Discipline, grievance and conflict: Fair procedures for discipline and grievances protect employees and employers.
  14. Strategic HRM: Strategic human resource management aligns people practices with organisational strategy.
  15. HR analytics: HR analytics uses data to understand and improve people management.

School 7: Finance for Managers

17 lessons

Finance for Managers: key terms, sample flashcards and a quick quiz

  1. Why managers need finance: Every management decision has a financial side.
  2. The income statement: The income statement shows whether a business made a profit over a period.
  3. The statement of financial position: The statement of financial position, or balance sheet, shows what a business owns and owes at one moment.
  4. The statement of cash flows: The statement of cash flows explains how cash changed over a period.
  5. Profitability ratios: Ratios turn raw figures into comparable measures.
  6. Liquidity and efficiency ratios: A business must be able to pay its bills and manage its working capital.
  7. Gearing and investor ratios: How a business is financed affects its risk and the returns to shareholders.
  8. Costs and break-even analysis: Knowing how costs behave helps managers set prices and targets.
  9. Contribution and short-term decisions: Many short-term decisions depend on contribution rather than full cost.
  10. Budgeting: A budget turns plans into numbers.
  11. Variance analysis: Variance analysis compares actual results with the budget to explain what went right or wrong.
  12. Cash flow forecasting: A cash flow forecast predicts when money will come in and go out.
  13. Sources of finance: Businesses fund themselves from many sources, each with different costs, risks and control implications.
  14. Investment appraisal: payback and ARR: Capital investment decisions commit large sums for years.
  15. Net present value: Net present value is the most widely recommended appraisal method because it allows for the time value of money.
  16. Working capital management: Working capital is the money tied up in day-to-day operations.
  17. The cost of capital: The cost of capital is the return investors require, and it sets the hurdle rate for new investments.

School 8: Economics for Business

15 lessons

Economics for Business: key terms, sample flashcards and a quick quiz

  1. Demand: Demand is the quantity of a good that buyers are willing and able to buy at each price.
  2. Supply and market equilibrium: Supply shows how much producers will offer at each price; together with demand it determines market prices.
  3. Price elasticity of demand: Price elasticity of demand measures how responsive quantity demanded is to price changes.
  4. Market failure: Markets do not always allocate resources efficiently.
  5. Perfect competition and monopoly: Market structure shapes prices, profits and efficiency.
  6. Oligopoly and game theory: Many important markets, from supermarkets to mobile networks, are dominated by a few large firms.
  7. Competition policy: Competition policy protects consumers from anti-competitive behaviour.
  8. Economic growth and the business cycle: The level of economic activity affects every business.
  9. Inflation: Inflation is a sustained rise in the general price level.
  10. Unemployment and the labour market: The labour market determines the availability and cost of workers.
  11. Interest rates and monetary policy: The Bank of England uses interest rates to keep inflation on target.
  12. Fiscal policy and taxation: Government spending and taxation shape demand and the business environment.
  13. Exchange rates: The value of the pound affects exporters, importers and multinational firms.
  14. International trade and protectionism: Trade lets countries specialise and exchange.
  15. Behavioural economics for business: Real people do not always behave as rational economic models assume.

School 9: Entrepreneurship and Innovation

15 lessons

Entrepreneurship and Innovation: key terms, sample flashcards and a quick quiz

  1. What is entrepreneurship?: Entrepreneurship is the process of discovering, evaluating and exploiting opportunities to create new value.
  2. Opportunity recognition and the entrepreneurial mindset: Entrepreneurs succeed by spotting and shaping opportunities.
  3. Effectuation and the lean start-up: Entrepreneurs often cannot predict the future, so they act, learn and adapt.
  4. The business model canvas: A business model describes how an organisation creates, delivers and captures value.
  5. The business plan: A business plan sets out what a venture will do, for whom, and how it will make money.
  6. Funding a start-up: New ventures need money before they earn it.
  7. Protecting ideas: intellectual property: Intellectual property rights let creators and businesses control and profit from their ideas.
  8. Small business support in the UK: A range of public and private organisations help people start and grow small businesses.
  9. Types of innovation: Innovation means turning new ideas into value, and it takes many forms.
  10. Disruptive innovation: Disruptive innovation explains how small entrants with simpler, cheaper products can displace established leaders.
  11. Diffusion of innovations: New products spread through a population over time at different speeds.
  12. Open innovation: Firms no longer rely only on their own laboratories for new ideas.
  13. Design thinking: Design thinking is a human-centred approach to solving problems and developing innovations.
  14. Scaling a business: Scaling means growing revenue much faster than costs.
  15. Social entrepreneurship: Social entrepreneurs use business methods to tackle social and environmental problems.

School 10: Digital Business and Information Systems

15 lessons

Digital Business and Information Systems: key terms, sample flashcards and a quick quiz

  1. Information systems in organisations: Every organisation runs on information.
  2. Digital transformation: Digital transformation is more than buying new technology.
  3. E-commerce business models: E-commerce has created new ways to make money online.
  4. Platforms and network effects: Some of the world's most valuable firms are platforms that connect users rather than make products.
  5. Data-driven decision-making: Many firms now aim to base decisions on evidence rather than instinct.
  6. Business intelligence and dashboards: Business intelligence tools turn data from across a firm into reports and dashboards that managers can act on.
  7. Correlation and regression for forecasting: Businesses often want to predict one variable from another, such as sales from advertising.
  8. Artificial intelligence in business: Artificial intelligence is changing how businesses operate and compete.
  9. Cybersecurity for business: Cyber attacks are among the most common threats UK businesses face.
  10. Data protection and UK GDPR: Businesses hold large amounts of personal data, and the law controls how they use it.
  11. Digital ethics: Digital technologies raise ethical questions that the law does not always answer.
  12. Enterprise systems: ERP and CRM: Large firms use enterprise systems to integrate their processes and data.
  13. Cloud computing: Cloud computing lets businesses rent computing power and software over the internet.
  14. Fintech and digital payments: Financial technology is changing how businesses pay, get paid and raise money.
  15. Agile project management: Agile methods have spread from software development to many kinds of projects.

School 11: Ethics, ESG and Corporate Governance

17 lessons

Ethics, ESG and Corporate Governance: key terms, sample flashcards and a quick quiz

  1. Ethical theories in business: Business decisions often raise questions of right and wrong that law alone does not answer.
  2. Ethical decision-making: Knowing ethical theory does not guarantee ethical behaviour.
  3. Corporate social responsibility: Corporate social responsibility asks what businesses owe society beyond making profits.
  4. Shareholder and stakeholder theories of the firm: Whose interests should a company serve?
  5. Principles of corporate governance: Corporate governance is the system by which companies are directed and controlled.
  6. The UK Corporate Governance Code: Listed companies in the UK follow the UK Corporate Governance Code on a comply or explain basis.
  7. Directors' duties: Company directors have legal duties set out in the Companies Act 2006.
  8. Agency theory: Agency theory explains the conflicts that arise when owners delegate decisions to managers.
  9. Executive pay: Executive pay is one of the most debated areas of corporate governance.
  10. ESG: environmental, social and governance: ESG describes how investors and companies assess environmental, social and governance factors.
  11. Sustainability and the triple bottom line: Sustainable development meets present needs without compromising the future.
  12. Climate change and business: Climate change creates risks and opportunities for almost every business.
  13. Sustainability reporting: Companies increasingly report on sustainability alongside their financial results.
  14. Modern slavery and supply chain ethics: Global supply chains can hide forced labour and serious abuses.
  15. Bribery and corruption: Bribery distorts markets and damages trust.
  16. Whistleblowing: Whistleblowers often expose wrongdoing that other controls miss.
  17. Corporate failures: lessons from case studies: High-profile corporate collapses reveal how ethics and governance fail in practice.
