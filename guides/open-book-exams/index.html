Open-book and take-home exams: how to prepare

By the Gradify team · Updated for the 2026–27 academic year

Open-book exams test understanding and application, not memory, and the clock is the real enemy: students who haven't revised lose time flipping through PDFs. Revise as normal, then build a one-page index of formulas, definitions and where each worked example lives, and practise one timed paper with it.

Core rule: Open book means harder questions, not less revision.

Tools for this step: Deadline planner (plan a take-home exam) · Citation helper (reference fast)

Do you need to revise for an open-book exam?

Yes. Questions are harder because notes are allowed: they ask you to apply and evaluate. If you have to look up the basics, you won't have time to answer.

Key takeaway: Open book means harder questions, not less revision.

How do I build a rapid-lookup index?

  1. One page per module: key formulas, definitions you might quote, and the page or file where each worked example is.
  2. Colour or tab by topic.
  3. Number your notes' pages and put those numbers in the index.
  4. Make a one-page summary sheet from your notes as the front page.
Test yourself: What's the most useful thing on an open-book index? Reveal

Where to find each worked example and formula fast (page numbers or file names), so you spend exam time applying, not searching.

Take-home exams: what changes?

Over 24 or 48 hours, time management and referencing matter more. Plan the hours (the deadline planner works for this), cite sources properly, and follow your university's rules on collaboration and AI tools.

Common pitfalls

  • Not revising because notes are allowed.
  • No index, so time goes on searching.

Related checklists: Exam day checklist

Questions

Are open-book exams easier?

Usually not: they're designed so looking things up doesn't give you the answer. They reward understanding, application and fast navigation of your notes.
