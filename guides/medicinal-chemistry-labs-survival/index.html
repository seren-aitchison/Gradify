Surviving first-year medicinal chemistry and pharmacology labs

By the Gradify team · Updated for the 2026–27 academic year

Labs reward preparation: read the script and do the calculations before you arrive, keep a lab book anyone could follow, and write up within 48 hours while you remember. For the exams, learn drug classes and mechanisms with flashcards and practise the calculations until the units are automatic.

Core rule: Do the calculations before the lab, not during it.

Tools for this step: Unit converter (check doses and dilutions)

How do I prepare for a lab?

  • Read the script the day before and write a one-line aim.
  • Do every calculation in advance (moles, dilutions, yields), with units.
  • Note the hazards and what you'd do if something spilled.

Key takeaway: Do the calculations before the lab, not during it.

How do I keep a good lab book?

Date, aim, what you actually did (not what the script said), raw data with units in tables, observations, and anything that went wrong. Never write on scraps.

How do I revise pharmacology?

One card per drug class: mechanism, uses, key adverse effects, monitoring. Group by system. The free pharmacy and medicine topic pages have sample cards and quizzes.

Test yourself: What four things should a drug-class flashcard cover? Reveal

Mechanism of action, main uses, key adverse effects or cautions, and monitoring or interactions.

Common pitfalls

  • Doing calculations during the lab.
  • Writing the script's method instead of what you did.

Questions

How do I get good marks on lab reports?

Answer the aim, explain how you controlled variables, present data with units and uncertainties, and discuss limitations with their effect on the result.
