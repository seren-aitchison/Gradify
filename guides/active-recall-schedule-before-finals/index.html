The best active recall schedule before finals

By the Gradify team · Based on the learning research named in the text

Spend the first four weeks covering every topic with active recall and spaced reviews, then use the last two weeks for timed past papers and your weakest topics. Every topic should be recalled at least three times, on different days, before the exam.

Tools for this step: Exam timetable planner (order several exams) · Spaced repetition schedule (date each topic's reviews)

Weeks 6 to 5: build the question bank

Turn each topic into flashcards and question lists (Gradify does this from your PDFs). Learn two or three topics a day and do the first recall the next day.

Weeks 4 to 3: cover everything, spaced

Keep adding topics, but start each session with reviews that are due. Your daily order: due reviews first, then new material. Use the schedule generator or let Gradify schedule the cards.

Week 2: past papers

One timed paper every other day. Mark it, and turn every mistake into a new flashcard. Keep daily reviews going.

Final week: weak spots and sleep

Only weak cards and the topics past papers exposed. No new material after two days out. Sleep consolidates memory: an all-nighter costs more than it gains.

A daily template

  • 25 to 40 minutes: due reviews
  • Two 50-minute blocks: new topic, learn then question
  • 15 minutes: blank-page recall of yesterday's topic
  • Evening: nothing, or a short review

Print the revision timetable or the 7-day plan, and check your hours with the exam countdown.

Common pitfalls

  • Leaving past papers until the last day.
  • Giving every exam equal time regardless of weight.

Related checklists: Final 48 hours checklist · Exam day checklist

Questions

How many times should I review a topic before an exam?

At least three times on separate days, with growing gaps, and more for topics you keep getting wrong.
