How to answer 6-mark and 8-mark questions

By the Gradify team · Updated for the 2026–27 academic year

Long-answer questions are usually marked by levels: examiners look for a logical chain of correct points, not a list. Spend 60 seconds planning: core point or mechanism, evidence or an example, a link back to the question, and (if the command word asks) an evaluation.

Core rule: Levels marking rewards a linked chain of reasoning, not the number of facts.

Tools for this step: Mark scheme checker (check your answer's points)

How are 6-mark questions marked?

Most boards use levels of response: the examiner reads the whole answer and places it in a level (for example 1–2, 3–4 or 5–6 marks) by the quality of the reasoning, then picks a mark within it. A long list of disconnected facts often sits in the middle level.

Key takeaway: Levels marking rewards a linked chain of reasoning, not the number of facts.

What does a strong outline look like?

Weak outlineStrong outline
Fact, fact, fact, fact (no links)1. Core point that answers the question → 2. How or why (mechanism) → 3. Evidence or example → 4. Second point, linked → 5. Evaluation or limitation → 6. Conclusion that answers the question
Test yourself: Your 6-mark answer lists four correct facts. Why might it still get 3/6? Reveal

Levels marking rewards a linked, logical chain that answers the question; unlinked facts usually sit in a middle level.

How long should a 6-mark answer be?

Usually 120 to 200 words, or about 7 to 9 minutes. Use the lines given as a guide, not a target.

Common pitfalls

  • Unlinked facts instead of a chain.
  • No judgement on an evaluate question.

Questions

How many points do I need for a 6-mark question?

There's no fixed number in levels-marked questions; what matters is a correct, logically linked chain that answers the command word. As a rough guide, three well-developed points beat six bare ones.
