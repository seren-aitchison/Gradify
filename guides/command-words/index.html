Exam command words: what each one wants

By the Gradify team · Updated for the 2026–27 academic year

The command word tells you what kind of answer earns the marks: 'state' wants a fact, 'describe' wants what happens, 'explain' wants why or how, and 'evaluate' wants a judgement backed by evidence on both sides. Most lost marks on short answers come from answering a different command word from the one asked.

Core rule: Describe = what happens (with data). Explain = why it happens (a cause linked to its effect).

What's the difference between describe and explain?

QuestionWeak answerFull-mark approach
Describe the trend in the graph."It goes up because more enzyme is made.""The rate increases steadily from 10 to 40 °C, peaks at 40 °C (2.4 units), then falls sharply to zero by 60 °C." (Data quoted, no reasons.)
Explain the trend."The rate increases then decreases.""Rate rises because molecules have more kinetic energy, so more successful collisions; above 40 °C hydrogen bonds break, the active site changes shape and fewer enzyme-substrate complexes form."

Key takeaway: Describe = what happens (with data). Explain = why it happens (a cause linked to its effect).

Test yourself: A question says 'Describe the results'. Should you give reasons? Reveal

No. Describe asks what the data show: trends, peaks and values with units. Reasons are for 'explain'.

What do evaluate, assess and 'to what extent' want?

All three want a judgement: weigh points on both sides, say which matter more and why, and end with a supported conclusion. A list of pros and cons with no verdict usually caps you in the middle mark band. 'To what extent' wants a clear answer on a scale ("largely", "only partly") rather than yes or no.

Key takeaway: No verdict, no top band: evaluate questions are marked on the judgement.

Every common command word

Command wordWhat to do
State / Give / NameA fact or name, no explanation.
IdentifyPick it out from the information given.
DefineThe precise meaning, in the key terms the mark scheme expects.
OutlineThe main points, briefly.
DescribeWhat happens or what it's like, step by step.
ExplainWhy or how: cause → effect, linked with 'because' or 'so'.
Calculate / DetermineA number, with working and units.
CompareSimilarities and differences, side by side ('whereas', 'both').
ContrastDifferences only, side by side.
AnalyseBreak into parts and show how they connect, with evidence.
SuggestApply what you know to an unfamiliar situation.
JustifyReasons for a decision, each backed by evidence.
DiscussArguments on more than one side, reaching a view.
Evaluate / AssessWeigh strengths and weaknesses, then judge.
To what extentHow far it's true: both sides, then a clear verdict.

Exam boards publish their own lists (AQA, OCR, Pearson Edexcel and the IB each have one). The meanings above are common to all of them, but check your board's list for subject-specific words.

How do I practise command words?

Take five past-paper questions, underline the command word, and write only the first sentence of each answer. Check: does that sentence do what the word asks? Then use the question deconstructor on anything unfamiliar, and log misreads in your mistake log.

Test yourself: What does 'compare' need that 'describe' doesn't? Reveal

Both things in every sentence, with a linking word such as 'whereas' or 'both', so the similarity or difference is explicit.

Common pitfalls

  • Explaining when the question says describe.
  • A list of pros and cons with no verdict on an evaluate question.

Questions

What does explain mean in an exam?

Give reasons: say why or how something happens, linking each cause to its effect. Each correct link usually earns a mark.

What's the difference between evaluate and discuss?

Discuss wants arguments on more than one side; evaluate also wants a final judgement about which side is stronger, backed by evidence.
