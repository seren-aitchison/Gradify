Reading graphs and data: the 4-check scan

By the Gradify team · Updated for the 2026–27 academic year

Before you describe any graph, scan four things: the axes and their units, the scale (is it linear or logarithmic, does it start at zero?), the overall trend versus any anomaly, and the caveats (error bars, sample size, control group). Most data-question marks are lost on one of these four.

Core rule: Quote numbers with units every time.

Tools for this step: Unit converter (check axis units)
Jump to: 1. Axes and units2. Scale: linear, log, or not starting at zero3. Trend vs anomaly4. Caveats: error bars, sample size, controlsCorrelation or cause

1. Axes and units

Read both axis labels and units aloud. Quote values with units in your answer: "rises from 2.0 to 6.5 mmol dm⁻³", not "goes up".

Key takeaway: Quote numbers with units every time.

2. Scale: linear, log, or not starting at zero

On a log scale each gridline is ten times the last, so a straight line means exponential change. A y-axis that starts at 90 makes small differences look huge.

3. Trend vs anomaly

Describe the overall pattern first (direction, where it changes, the peak), then point out any anomalous point separately. Don't build your conclusion on one odd value.

4. Caveats: error bars, sample size, controls

Overlapping error bars (standard deviation or confidence intervals) mean a difference may not be significant. Small samples and missing controls limit what you can conclude: say so in 'evaluate' questions.

Test yourself: Two bars differ but their error bars overlap a lot. What should you conclude? Reveal

The difference may not be significant; you can't confidently say one is higher than the other.

Correlation or cause?

A graph showing two things rising together shows correlation. Causation needs a mechanism and a controlled comparison: see correlation vs causation.

Common pitfalls

  • Describing without quoting values and units.
  • Claiming a difference when error bars overlap.

Questions

How do I describe a graph in an exam?

State the overall trend, quote specific values with units at key points (start, peak, change), and mention any anomaly separately. Only explain reasons if the command word asks.
