A winter break revision plan for January exams

By the Gradify team · Updated for the 2026–27 academic year

Rest properly first, then keep the term's material alive with 20 minutes of flashcards a day, and save the intensive revision (two 50-minute blocks plus past questions a day) for the last two weeks. That beats both ignoring revision until New Year and burning out before Christmas.

Core rule: Rest, then light daily reviews, then focus: in that order.

Tools for this step: Winter break planner (lay out the break) · Exam timetable planner (split focus days)

Should I rest or revise over Christmas?

Both, in that order. Three to five days of real rest after term reduces burnout. Then short daily reviews stop the forgetting curve wiping out the term, which makes January's focused revision much faster.

Key takeaway: Rest, then light daily reviews, then focus: in that order.

What does a light day look like?

Twenty minutes of flashcards on the cards that are due, and nothing else. It keeps memories alive at almost no cost.

What does a focus day look like?

Two 50-minute blocks: one on your weakest topic, one on timed past-paper questions. Then flashcards. Use the winter revision planner to lay out your days and the exam timetable planner if you have several exams.

Test yourself: You have 30 days until a January exam. Roughly how many should be focus days? Reveal

About the last 40%: 12 or so, after rest days and light days.

Common pitfalls

  • Revising flat out from day one and burning out.
  • Doing nothing until New Year.

Related checklists: Semester reset checklist

Questions

How many hours should I revise over Christmas?

For January exams, a common pattern is very little in the first week, 20 to 30 minutes a day in the middle, and 2 to 4 focused hours a day in the last two weeks.
