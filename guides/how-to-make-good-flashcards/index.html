How to make good flashcards: 9 rules

By the Gradify team · Based on the learning research named in the text

Good flashcards test one idea each, ask a precise question with one right answer, and come from your own course material. Small, specific cards are faster to review and tell you exactly what you don't know.

The nine rules

  1. One idea per card. "List the 8 causes of…" becomes eight cards or a cloze.
  2. Ask a question, not a topic. "Mitosis" is a topic; "What happens in anaphase?" is a question.
  3. One right answer. If you can't mark it, rewrite it.
  4. Use your course's wording. Examiners mark against your syllabus.
  5. Add cloze deletions for lists and formulae: "ΔG = ΔH − {{T}}ΔS".
  6. Add a picture when it helps: diagrams beat descriptions.
  7. Make both directions for vocabulary: term → definition and definition → term.
  8. Understand before you card. Cards are for remembering, not for learning something new.
  9. Check them against your notes. A wrong card, revised 20 times, is a wrong answer in the exam.

Should I make my own flashcards or use pre-made ones?

Making cards helps you process the material, but takes time. Pre-made decks save time but may not match your course. Gradify sits in between: it makes cards from your notes and shows the line each one came from, so you get the speed without the mismatch.

Questions

How many flashcards should I make per lecture?

Typically 15 to 40 for a one-hour lecture: one per definition, process step or key fact that the exam could ask about.
