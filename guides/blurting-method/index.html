The blurting method: how to do it properly

By the Gradify team · Updated for the 2026–27 academic year

Blurting is writing down everything you can remember about a topic on a blank page, without notes, then checking it against your notes in a different colour and filling the gaps. It's active recall for whole topics, and the gaps you fill in colour are exactly what to revise next.

Core rule: The coloured corrections are your revision list.

Tools for this step: Study timer (time each blurt)

How do I do the blurting method?

  1. Pick one topic and set a timer (10 to 15 minutes).
  2. Write or draw everything you remember: definitions, processes, examples, diagrams.
  3. Open your notes and check, adding what you missed in a different colour.
  4. Make flashcards for the coloured parts.
  5. Blurt the same topic again in two or three days: there should be less colour.

Key takeaway: The coloured corrections are your revision list.

Where can I get blurting sheets?

Every topic page has a Print blurting sheet button: it prints the topic's headings and prompts with space to write, and the answers stay on the page to check against.

Blurting or flashcards?

Blurting tests whole topics and how ideas connect; flashcards test individual facts and definitions. Use blurting to find gaps and flashcards to fix them. More in blurting vs flashcards.

Test yourself: You blurted a topic and half the page is in correction colour. What next? Reveal

Make flashcards for the corrections, review them over the next few days, and blurt the topic again in 2 or 3 days.

Common pitfalls

  • Not checking against notes afterwards.
  • Blurting the same topic twice in one day.

Related comparisons: Blurting vs flashcards

Questions

Does blurting work?

Yes: it's a form of retrieval practice, which has strong research support. Checking and correcting afterwards is essential.
