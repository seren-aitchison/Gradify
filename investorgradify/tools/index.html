Compound interest and fund fee calculator

Enter a starting amount, a monthly amount, the years and an assumed yearly return. You'll see what it could grow to, how much you paid in, and how much a yearly fund fee takes over that time. Fees compound too, so a 1% fee can cost far more than 1% of the total. This is for learning, not financial advice: real returns vary and can be negative.

Worked example

£1,000 to start and £100 a month for 20 years at an assumed 5% a year grows to about £43,800 from £25,000 paid in. A 1% yearly fee cuts that to about £38,900: nearly £4,900 gone, because the fee is taken every year from a growing pot.

Questions

What is compound interest?

Earning returns on your previous returns as well as on what you put in, so growth speeds up over time.

How much do fund fees cost?

More than they look: a yearly percentage fee is taken from a growing pot every year, so over decades a 1% fee can cost a large share of the gains. Compare the ongoing charge (OCF) when choosing funds.
