Ribosomes

Ribosomes are the cell's protein factories: they read the code in messenger RNA and join amino acids in the order it specifies. Every cell has them, and differences between bacterial and human ribosomes are the target of many antibiotics.

  • Describe the structure of a ribosome
  • Distinguish free and membrane-bound ribosomes and their products
  • Explain the difference between 70S and 80S ribosomes and why it matters

Before you start

  • Cell organelles

Structure

A ribosome is made of two subunits, each built from ribosomal RNA (rRNA) and proteins; it has no membrane. The small subunit binds the mRNA; the large subunit joins amino acids together. The rRNA itself catalyses the formation of peptide bonds, so the ribosome is a ribozyme, an RNA enzyme.

Free and bound ribosomes

  • Free ribosomes in the cytosol make proteins that stay in the cytosol or go to the nucleus, mitochondria or peroxisomes.
  • Membrane-bound ribosomes on the rough ER make proteins for secretion, for membranes and for lysosomes, threading them into the ER as they are made.

The same ribosome can be either: a signal sequence at the start of a protein directs its ribosome to the ER.

70S and 80S

Ribosomes are described by how fast they sediment in a centrifuge, in Svedberg units (S). Prokaryotic ribosomes are 70S (50S and 30S subunits); eukaryotic cytoplasmic ribosomes are 80S (60S and 40S). The values don't add up because sedimentation depends on shape as well as mass. Mitochondria and chloroplasts have 70S-type ribosomes.

Antibiotics such as streptomycin and tetracyclines bind the 30S subunit, and erythromycin the 50S subunit, blocking bacterial protein synthesis with much less effect on our 80S ribosomes.

Investigate with the lab. Watch tRNAs move through the A, P and E sites as the chain grows, until a stop codon releases it.

Key idea

Ribosomes, made of rRNA and protein, translate mRNA into protein; bacterial 70S and eukaryotic 80S ribosomes differ enough for antibiotics to tell them apart.

Common mistake
Calling ribosomes membrane-bound organelles. They have no membrane; "bound" ribosomes are simply attached to the ER.

Summary

  • Ribosomes have two subunits of rRNA and protein, and rRNA catalyses peptide bonds.
  • Free ribosomes make cytosolic proteins; bound ribosomes make proteins for export and membranes.
  • Prokaryotes have 70S ribosomes and eukaryotes 80S, a difference antibiotics exploit.

Key terms

Ribosome
A structure of rRNA and protein that translates mRNA into a polypeptide
Ribozyme
An RNA molecule that acts as an enzyme
Svedberg unit
A unit of sedimentation rate used to describe the size of ribosomes and their subunits

Flashcards

Answer from memory, then open the card to check.

Where are proteins for secretion made?

On ribosomes bound to the rough endoplasmic reticulum.

Why don't a ribosome's subunit S values add up (50S + 30S = 70S)?

Svedberg units measure sedimentation rate, which depends on shape as well as mass, so they are not additive.

Check your understanding

  1. What are ribosomes made of?
    • Cellulose and protein
    • DNA and lipids
    • Phospholipids only
    • Ribosomal RNA and proteins
    Show the answer

    Ribosomal RNA and proteins. Each subunit combines rRNA with many proteins; there is no membrane.

  2. Tetracycline binds to the 30S ribosomal subunit. Why does it harm bacteria more than human cells?
    • Bacteria have no 30S subunit
    • Human cells have no ribosomes
    • Human cytoplasmic ribosomes are 80S, with a 40S small subunit that it binds poorly
    • Tetracycline only enters the nucleus
    Show the answer

    Human cytoplasmic ribosomes are 80S, with a 40S small subunit that it binds poorly. The drug targets the bacterial small subunit, which differs from the eukaryotic one.

  3. Some antibiotics that act on bacterial ribosomes cause side effects in tissues with high energy demand. What is a likely explanation?
    • Human 80S ribosomes are identical to 70S ribosomes
    • Mitochondria have bacterial-type ribosomes, so the drug can reduce mitochondrial protein synthesis
    • The drugs block glycolysis directly
    • The drugs dissolve cell membranes
    Show the answer

    Mitochondria have bacterial-type ribosomes, so the drug can reduce mitochondrial protein synthesis. Mitochondria descend from bacteria and keep 70S-type ribosomes, so drugs aimed at bacteria can partly affect them.

  4. What does a tRNA molecule carry to the ribosome?
    • A copy of the gene from the nucleus
    • A specific amino acid, matched by its anticodon
    • An enzyme that copies DNA
    • The ribosome's two subunits
    Show the answer

    A specific amino acid, matched by its anticodon. Each tRNA's anticodon pairs with an mRNA codon and delivers the matching amino acid.
