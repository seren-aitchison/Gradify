Facilitated diffusion

Facilitated diffusion is passive movement down a concentration gradient through specific membrane proteins. It lets ions and polar molecules cross membranes quickly without the cell spending energy.

Before you start

  • Membrane transport

Channel proteins

Channel proteins form water-filled pores through the membrane. Each is specific for certain ions or molecules: potassium channels let K⁺ through but exclude the smaller Na⁺, and aquaporins pass water. Many channels are gated, opening only in response to a voltage change or a binding molecule, which is how nerve impulses are produced.

Carrier proteins

Carrier proteins bind a specific molecule on one side of the membrane, change shape, and release it on the other side. Glucose transporters (GLUT proteins) carry glucose into most body cells this way. Carriers are slower than channels, because each cycle moves only one or a few molecules.

How it differs from simple diffusion

  • Both are passive and go down the concentration gradient.
  • Facilitated diffusion needs specific proteins, so only particular substances use it.
  • Its rate levels off (saturates) at high concentrations once every carrier is busy, whereas simple diffusion keeps increasing in proportion to the gradient.

Because the number of transporters limits the rate, cells can control uptake by adding or removing transporters. Insulin, for example, makes muscle and fat cells insert more GLUT4 transporters into their plasma membranes, speeding glucose uptake after a meal.

Key idea

Facilitated diffusion is passive movement down a gradient through specific channel or carrier proteins; it saturates when all the proteins are in use.

Common mistake
Thinking facilitated diffusion uses ATP because it involves proteins. The proteins only provide a path; the gradient supplies the energy.

Summary

  • Channel proteins provide pores; carrier proteins bind and change shape.
  • Facilitated diffusion is passive and specific.
  • Its rate levels off when all transporters are occupied; cells regulate it by changing transporter numbers.

Key terms

Facilitated diffusion
Passive movement of substances down a concentration gradient through specific membrane proteins
Channel protein
A membrane protein forming a pore that lets specific ions or molecules pass
Carrier protein
A membrane protein that binds a specific molecule and changes shape to move it across

Flashcards

Answer from memory, then open the card to check.

How do carrier proteins move molecules across a membrane?

They bind a specific molecule, change shape and release it on the other side.

Why does the rate of facilitated diffusion level off at high concentration?

All the transport proteins are occupied, so adding more substance can't increase the rate.

Check your understanding

  1. Which statement about facilitated diffusion is correct?
    • It moves substances against their gradient
    • It moves substances down their concentration gradient through specific proteins
    • It only moves water
    • It uses ATP directly
    Show the answer

    It moves substances down their concentration gradient through specific proteins. It is passive, like simple diffusion, but needs channel or carrier proteins.

  2. A graph of uptake rate against external glucose concentration rises and then levels off. What does this suggest?
    • Glucose enters by facilitated diffusion through a limited number of carriers
    • Glucose enters by simple diffusion
    • Glucose is not taken up
    • The cell is dead
    Show the answer

    Glucose enters by facilitated diffusion through a limited number of carriers. Saturation is the signature of carrier-mediated transport.

  3. After a meal, insulin speeds glucose uptake into muscle cells even though the glucose gradient hardly changes. How?
    • It causes more GLUT4 carriers to be inserted into the membrane, increasing the maximum rate
    • It makes glucose pass through the lipid bilayer
    • It removes the need for a gradient
    • It switches glucose uptake to active transport using ATP
    Show the answer

    It causes more GLUT4 carriers to be inserted into the membrane, increasing the maximum rate. More carriers raise the rate of facilitated diffusion for the same gradient.
