Endoplasmic reticulum

The endoplasmic reticulum is a network of membranes spreading through the cytoplasm from the nuclear envelope. Its rough parts make and fold proteins for export and membranes; its smooth parts make lipids, detoxify drugs and store calcium.

Before you start

  • Ribosomes

Structure

The ER is a single continuous membrane enclosing a space called the lumen, folded into flattened sacs (cisternae) and tubes. It is continuous with the outer nuclear membrane. Rough ER is studded with ribosomes and is mostly flattened sacs; smooth ER has no ribosomes and is mostly tubular.

Rough ER: proteins

Proteins made on bound ribosomes are threaded into the lumen as they grow. There they fold, with help from chaperone proteins, and many have sugar chains added (glycosylation) and disulfide bonds formed. Proteins that fold badly are held back and destroyed, a quality-control step. Correct proteins leave in vesicles for the Golgi.

Smooth ER: lipids, detoxification and calcium

  • Makes phospholipids and cholesterol for membranes, and steroid hormones in cells such as the adrenal cortex and testes.
  • Detoxifies drugs and toxins, especially in liver cells, using enzymes that make them more water-soluble so they can be excreted.
  • Stores calcium ions: in muscle, the smooth ER (sarcoplasmic reticulum) releases Ca²⁺ to trigger contraction.

A plasma cell making antibodies is full of rough ER; a liver cell exposed to alcohol or some drugs expands its smooth ER.

  • The functions of each, side by side: rough ER makes, folds and modifies proteins for secretion, for membranes and for lysosomes, then sends them to the Golgi apparatus in vesicles; smooth ER makes lipids and steroid hormones, detoxifies drugs and toxins, and stores calcium ions.
Key idea

Rough ER makes and folds proteins for export and membranes; smooth ER makes lipids, detoxifies and stores calcium.

Common mistake
Thinking the rough ER makes all the cell's proteins. Proteins that stay in the cytosol are made on free ribosomes.

Summary

  • The ER is a continuous membrane network joined to the nuclear envelope.
  • Rough ER folds and modifies proteins; smooth ER makes lipids, detoxifies and stores Ca²⁺.
  • The type and amount of ER reflect each cell's job.

Key terms

Rough endoplasmic reticulum
ER with ribosomes on its surface, where proteins for secretion and membranes are made
Smooth endoplasmic reticulum
ER without ribosomes, where lipids are made and drugs detoxified
Lumen
The space enclosed by the ER membrane

Flashcards

Answer from memory, then open the card to check.

Name three functions of smooth ER.

Making lipids (phospholipids, cholesterol, steroid hormones), detoxifying drugs and storing calcium ions.

What happens to proteins inside the rough ER?

They fold with the help of chaperones and are modified, for example by glycosylation; misfolded proteins are held back and destroyed.

Check your understanding

  1. Which cell would have the most smooth ER?
    • A bacterial cell
    • A cell of the adrenal cortex making steroid hormones
    • A plasma cell making antibodies
    • A red blood cell
    Show the answer

    A cell of the adrenal cortex making steroid hormones. Steroid hormones are lipids made in the smooth ER; antibody-secreting cells are rich in rough ER instead.

  2. Regular heavy drinking increases the amount of smooth ER in liver cells. Why?
    • Alcohol is a protein made by the rough ER
    • Alcohol turns rough ER into chloroplasts
    • Smooth ER stores the alcohol permanently
    • The liver cells detoxify more alcohol and make more of the enzymes, and membrane, to do it
    Show the answer

    The liver cells detoxify more alcohol and make more of the enzymes, and membrane, to do it. Detoxification happens in the smooth ER, which expands when demand increases.

  3. A mutation causes a secreted protein to fold incorrectly. Where is the protein most likely to be found, and why?
    • In the chloroplast
    • Retained in the ER and broken down, because ER quality control stops misfolded proteins moving on
    • Secreted normally
    • Stored in the nucleolus
    Show the answer

    Retained in the ER and broken down, because ER quality control stops misfolded proteins moving on. The ER checks folding and sends misfolded proteins for destruction rather than to the Golgi; this is the basis of diseases such as one form of cystic fibrosis.
