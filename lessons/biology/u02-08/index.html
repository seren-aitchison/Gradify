Biology · School 2: Cell Biology · Organelles

Cell organelles

Organelles are the specialised compartments and structures inside eukaryotic cells. This lesson surveys them as a system: which make things, which process and ship them, which supply energy and which recycle, and how they depend on each other.

By the end of this lesson you can

  • Name the main eukaryotic organelles and state the function of each
  • Group organelles into the endomembrane system, energy organelles and others
  • Trace how organelles cooperate to make and secrete a protein

Before you start

  • Eukaryotic cells

Organelles by job

  • Information: the nucleus stores DNA; the nucleolus makes ribosomes.
  • Manufacturing: ribosomes make proteins; the rough ER makes proteins for membranes and export; the smooth ER makes lipids.
  • Processing and shipping: the Golgi apparatus modifies, sorts and packages; vesicles carry cargo between compartments.
  • Energy: mitochondria (respiration) and, in plants and algae, chloroplasts (photosynthesis).
  • Recycling and detoxifying: lysosomes digest; peroxisomes break down fatty acids and hydrogen peroxide.
  • Structure and movement: the cytoskeleton.

The endomembrane system

The nuclear envelope, ER, Golgi apparatus, lysosomes, vesicles and plasma membrane form one connected system. Membrane and cargo flow between them in vesicles that bud from one compartment and fuse with the next. Mitochondria and chloroplasts are not part of it: they make most of their own membranes and import proteins directly.

Organelles working together

To make a secreted protein such as insulin, the nucleus provides the mRNA, ribosomes on the rough ER make the chain and thread it into the ER, the Golgi modifies and packages it, a vesicle carries it to the plasma membrane, and mitochondria supply the ATP for every step.

How organelles are studied

Much of what we know comes from cell fractionation. Cells are broken open in a cold, buffered solution of the same water potential as the cells, then spun in a centrifuge at increasing speeds. Large, dense parts settle first: nuclei at low speed, then mitochondria and chloroplasts, then fragments of ER and other membranes, and ribosomes only at very high speed. Each fraction can then be tested to find which reactions it carries out.

Investigate with the lab. Go through every organelle in both cells and sort them into those that make proteins, those that release energy and those that store or transport materials.

Organelles divide the cell's work, but they function as a connected system, especially the endomembrane system.

Common mistakeLearning organelles as an unrelated list. Each depends on others: no organelle works alone.

Summary

  • Organelles can be grouped by job: information, manufacturing, processing, energy, recycling and structure.
  • The endomembrane system exchanges membrane and cargo through vesicles.
  • Making a protein for export uses several organelles in sequence.

Key terms

Endomembrane system
The connected membranes of the nuclear envelope, ER, Golgi, lysosomes, vesicles and plasma membrane
Vesicle
A small membrane-bound sac that carries materials within the cell or to its surface

Flashcards

Answer from memory, then open the card to check.

Which organelles belong to the endomembrane system?

The nuclear envelope, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, lysosomes, vesicles and the plasma membrane.

Why are mitochondria not part of the endomembrane system?

They are not connected to it by vesicle traffic; they grow and divide on their own and import proteins directly from the cytosol.

Check your understanding

  1. Which organelle packages proteins into vesicles for secretion?
    • Golgi apparatus
    • Mitochondrion
    • Nucleolus
    • Peroxisome
    Show the answer

    Golgi apparatus. The Golgi modifies, sorts and packages proteins into vesicles bound for the membrane, lysosomes or secretion.

  2. A drug stops vesicles from budding off the ER. What happens to a protein destined for secretion?
    • It builds up in the ER and is not secreted
    • It is digested by chloroplasts
    • It is made in the nucleus instead
    • It is secreted faster
    Show the answer

    It builds up in the ER and is not secreted. Without vesicle transport, proteins can't move from the ER to the Golgi and on to the surface.

  3. Cells lining the stomach secrete a lot of enzyme and also divide often. Which combination of organelles would you expect to be prominent?
    • Extensive rough ER and Golgi, many mitochondria, and active nuclei with large nucleoli
    • Few ribosomes and many peroxisomes
    • Large vacuoles and chloroplasts
    • Only lysosomes
    Show the answer

    Extensive rough ER and Golgi, many mitochondria, and active nuclei with large nucleoli. Heavy protein secretion needs ribosome production (nucleoli), rough ER, Golgi and ATP from mitochondria.

  4. Which organelle is the site of aerobic respiration?
    • The Golgi apparatus
    • The mitochondrion
    • The nucleolus
    • The ribosome
    Show the answer

    The mitochondrion. Aerobic respiration releases energy as ATP in the mitochondria.
