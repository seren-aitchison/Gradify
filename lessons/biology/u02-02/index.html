Prokaryotic cells

Prokaryotes, the bacteria and archaea, are cells without a nucleus or membrane-bound organelles. They are small and structurally simple, yet they are the most abundant and metabolically varied organisms on Earth.

Before you start

  • Cell theory

The parts of a prokaryotic cell

  • Plasma membrane enclosing the cytoplasm.
  • Nucleoid: a region where the single, usually circular chromosome sits, not enclosed by a membrane.
  • Ribosomes of the 70S type (smaller than the 80S ribosomes in eukaryotic cytoplasm).
  • Cell wall outside the membrane in almost all prokaryotes.
  • Often plasmids (small extra DNA circles), a capsule or slime layer, pili for attachment and DNA transfer, and one or more flagella for movement.

Genes without a nucleus

Because there is no nuclear envelope, transcription and translation happen in the same space: ribosomes can start translating an mRNA while it is still being made. Plasmids often carry useful genes such as antibiotic resistance and can be passed between cells, which is why resistance spreads quickly.

Small and fast

Most prokaryotes are 0.5–5 µm long. Their small size gives a large surface area relative to volume, so nutrients and wastes diffuse in and out fast enough to support rapid growth. Under good conditions E. coli can divide by binary fission about every 20 minutes.

Bacteria and archaea

Both domains are prokaryotic, but they differ deeply. Archaeal membranes are built from ether-linked lipids rather than the ester-linked lipids of bacteria and eukaryotes, archaeal walls contain no peptidoglycan, and some archaeal enzymes for copying and reading DNA resemble those of eukaryotes. Many archaea live in extreme conditions such as hot springs and salt lakes, but they are also common in soil, oceans and the human gut.

Prokaryotic cells have no nucleus or membrane-bound organelles; their DNA is in a nucleoid, often with plasmids.

Common mistakeSaying prokaryotes have no DNA organisation or no ribosomes. They have a chromosome and many ribosomes; they lack a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.

Summary

  • Prokaryotes are small cells with a nucleoid, 70S ribosomes and usually a cell wall.
  • Transcription and translation happen together; plasmids spread genes such as resistance.
  • A large surface area to volume ratio supports fast growth.

Key terms

Prokaryote
An organism whose cells have no nucleus or membrane-bound organelles: bacteria and archaea
Nucleoid
The region of a prokaryotic cell that contains its chromosome, without a surrounding membrane
Plasmid
A small, usually circular DNA molecule separate from the main chromosome

Flashcards

Answer from memory, then open the card to check.

Name four structures found in all prokaryotic cells.

Plasma membrane, cytoplasm, ribosomes and DNA (a chromosome in a nucleoid); almost all also have a cell wall.

Why can transcription and translation be coupled in bacteria?

There is no nuclear envelope separating DNA from ribosomes, so ribosomes can translate mRNA as it is being transcribed.

Check your understanding

  1. Which structure is found in prokaryotic cells?
    • 70S ribosomes
    • A nucleus
    • Golgi apparatus
    • Mitochondria
    Show the answer

    70S ribosomes. Prokaryotes have ribosomes but no membrane-bound organelles such as a nucleus, mitochondria or Golgi.

  2. Antibiotic resistance appears in a different bacterial species living in the same gut. What is the most likely route?
    • A plasmid carrying the resistance gene was transferred between the bacteria
    • Mitochondria carried the gene
    • Resistance genes moved through the nucleus
    • The antibiotic caused the same mutation in both
    Show the answer

    A plasmid carrying the resistance gene was transferred between the bacteria. Plasmids can be passed between bacteria, even of different species, through processes such as conjugation.

  3. Why are prokaryotic cells almost never larger than a few micrometres?
    • Large cells would be eaten by protists
    • Their cell wall cannot stretch
    • They have too little DNA to build a larger cell
    • Without internal transport systems they depend on diffusion, which is only fast enough when the surface area to volume ratio is large
    Show the answer

    Without internal transport systems they depend on diffusion, which is only fast enough when the surface area to volume ratio is large. Diffusion time grows with distance, and the surface area to volume ratio falls as a cell gets bigger, so small size supports fast exchange.
