Nucleus and nucleolus

The nucleus holds almost all of a eukaryotic cell's DNA and controls which genes are used. Inside it, the nucleolus builds the ribosomes the cell needs to make proteins.

Before you start

  • Cell organelles

The nuclear envelope

The nucleus is enclosed by a double membrane, the nuclear envelope, whose outer membrane is continuous with the rough ER. Thousands of nuclear pores, large protein complexes, control traffic: mRNA and ribosomal subunits leave, while proteins such as histones and transcription factors enter. Inside, a meshwork of lamin filaments supports the envelope.

Chromatin and chromosomes

Each human cell nucleus contains about 2 m of DNA packed into a space about 6 µm across. The DNA is wound around histone proteins to form nucleosomes, then folded further into chromatin. Loosely packed chromatin (euchromatin) holds active genes; tightly packed heterochromatin is mostly silent. Before division, chromatin condenses into the visible chromosomes.

The nucleolus

The nucleolus is a dense region, not surrounded by a membrane, where genes for ribosomal RNA are transcribed and combined with imported proteins to make the large and small ribosomal subunits. These leave through the pores and assemble into working ribosomes in the cytoplasm. Cells that make a lot of protein have large nucleoli.

A few cell types lose their nucleus as they mature: mammalian red blood cells, for example, which can then carry more haemoglobin but cannot make new proteins or divide.

Investigate with the lab. Play the identification game until you can find the nucleus and nucleolus in both cells without hesitating.

Key idea

The nucleus stores and controls access to DNA, and its pores regulate traffic in and out; the nucleolus makes ribosomal subunits.

Common mistake
Thinking the nucleolus is a smaller nucleus with its own membrane. It is a region inside the nucleus, defined by the work done there, with no membrane.

Summary

  • A double-membrane envelope with pores surrounds the nucleus.
  • DNA is packed with histones into chromatin; active genes lie in looser chromatin.
  • The nucleolus builds ribosomal subunits.

Key terms

Nuclear pore
A protein complex in the nuclear envelope that controls movement of molecules in and out
Chromatin
DNA wound around histone proteins, the form DNA takes in the nucleus
Nucleolus
A region inside the nucleus where ribosomal RNA is made and ribosomal subunits are assembled

Flashcards

Answer from memory, then open the card to check.

What passes out of the nucleus through the nuclear pores?

mRNA and ribosomal subunits (and other RNAs); proteins such as histones and transcription factors pass in.

What is made in the nucleolus?

Ribosomal RNA, which is combined with proteins to form ribosomal subunits.

Check your understanding

  1. What are histones?
    • Enzymes that copy DNA
    • Pores in the nuclear envelope
    • Proteins around which DNA is wound in chromatin
    • RNA molecules in ribosomes
    Show the answer

    Proteins around which DNA is wound in chromatin. DNA wraps around histone proteins to form nucleosomes, the first level of chromatin packing.

  2. A cell is treated with a chemical that blocks nuclear pores. Which process stops first?
    • DNA replication
    • Export of mRNA to the ribosomes in the cytoplasm
    • Respiration in mitochondria
    • Transcription of genes already being read
    Show the answer

    Export of mRNA to the ribosomes in the cytoplasm. Transcription happens inside the nucleus, but mRNA must leave through the pores to be translated.

  3. Why does a mature mammalian red blood cell have a limited lifespan of about 120 days?
    • It has no nucleus, so it can't make new proteins to repair itself or divide
    • It has too many mitochondria
    • Its DNA is too tightly packed
    • Its nucleolus makes too many ribosomes
    Show the answer

    It has no nucleus, so it can't make new proteins to repair itself or divide. Losing the nucleus makes room for haemoglobin but removes the genes needed for repair and renewal.

  4. What is made in the nucleolus?
    • ATP for the cell's energy needs
    • Glucose by photosynthesis
    • Lipids for the cell membrane
    • Ribosomal RNA and ribosome subunits
    Show the answer

    Ribosomal RNA and ribosome subunits. The nucleolus assembles ribosome subunits from rRNA and proteins.
