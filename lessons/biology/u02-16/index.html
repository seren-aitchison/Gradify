Cytoskeleton

The cytoskeleton is a network of protein filaments that gives the cell its shape, holds organelles in place, moves materials around and lets cells crawl, contract and divide. It is made of three kinds of filament with different jobs.

By the end of this lesson you can

  • Describe the three types of cytoskeletal filament
  • Explain how motor proteins move cargo and cells
  • Describe the structure and function of cilia and flagella in eukaryotes

Before you start

  • Cell organelles

Three kinds of filament

  • Microfilaments (about 7 nm): two twisted chains of actin. They support the cell surface, form microvilli, and with myosin cause muscle contraction, cell crawling and the furrow that divides cells in cytokinesis.
  • Intermediate filaments (about 10 nm): rope-like proteins such as keratin in skin cells and lamins in the nucleus. They resist tension and anchor cells together.
  • Microtubules (about 25 nm): hollow tubes of tubulin, growing from the centrosome. They act as tracks for transport, form the spindle that separates chromosomes, and make up cilia and flagella.

Motor proteins

Motor proteins use ATP to walk along filaments. Kinesin and dynein carry vesicles and organelles along microtubules, for example along a nerve cell's axon, which can be a metre long. Myosin pulls on actin filaments in muscle.

Cilia and flagella

Eukaryotic cilia and flagella have a core of nine pairs of microtubules around a central pair (the "9 + 2" pattern). Dynein arms slide neighbouring pairs past each other, bending the structure. Cilia lining the airways sweep mucus upwards; a sperm cell swims with a flagellum.

Eukaryotic flagella are completely different from bacterial flagella, which are rigid protein spirals turned by a rotary motor in the membrane.

Key idea

Actin microfilaments, intermediate filaments and tubulin microtubules give the cell shape and strength, and with motor proteins they move cargo, chromosomes and whole cells.

Common mistake
Thinking the cytoskeleton is fixed like a bony skeleton. Microfilaments and microtubules are built and dismantled constantly as the cell changes shape and divides.

Summary

  • The cytoskeleton has microfilaments, intermediate filaments and microtubules.
  • Motor proteins move cargo along filaments using ATP.
  • Eukaryotic cilia and flagella have a 9 + 2 arrangement of microtubules.

Key terms

Microtubule
A hollow cylinder of tubulin, used for transport tracks, the spindle, cilia and flagella
Microfilament
A thin filament of actin, involved in cell shape, movement and contraction
Motor protein
A protein, such as kinesin, dynein or myosin, that uses ATP to move along a filament

Flashcards

Answer from memory, then open the card to check.

Which cytoskeletal filament forms the spindle in cell division?

Microtubules.

What do kinesin and dynein do?

They are motor proteins that move cargo such as vesicles along microtubules, using ATP.

Check your understanding

  1. Which protein makes up microtubules?
    • Actin
    • Collagen
    • Keratin
    • Tubulin
    Show the answer

    Tubulin. Microtubules are hollow tubes of tubulin; actin forms microfilaments and keratin intermediate filaments.

  2. People with primary ciliary dyskinesia have faulty dynein arms in their cilia. Which problem would you expect?
    • Faster wound healing
    • Inability to digest starch
    • Repeated chest infections, because airway cilia can't clear mucus
    • Weak bones
    Show the answer

    Repeated chest infections, because airway cilia can't clear mucus. Without working dynein, cilia can't beat, so mucus and trapped microbes stay in the airways.

  3. Colchicine stops microtubules from forming. Why does it block cell division?
    • It destroys actin in the cleavage furrow
    • It prevents DNA replication
    • It removes the nuclear envelope
    • The spindle that separates chromosomes is made of microtubules
    Show the answer

    The spindle that separates chromosomes is made of microtubules. Without microtubules there is no spindle, so chromosomes can't be separated and the cell is stuck in mitosis.
