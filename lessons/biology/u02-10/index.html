Mitochondria

Mitochondria are the organelles where aerobic respiration releases most of the energy in food, storing it as ATP. Their folded inner membrane, their own DNA and their bacterial ancestry make them one of the cell's most distinctive organelles.

By the end of this lesson you can

  • Describe the structure of a mitochondrion and relate it to function
  • State where the stages of aerobic respiration take place
  • Explain why cells differ in how many mitochondria they contain

Before you start

  • Cell organelles

Structure

  • An outer membrane, fairly permeable to small molecules.
  • An inner membrane folded into cristae, carrying the electron transport chain and ATP synthase.
  • The intermembrane space between the two.
  • The matrix: a gel containing enzymes of the link reaction and Krebs cycle, mitochondrial DNA and 70S ribosomes.

Where respiration happens

Glycolysis happens in the cytosol. Pyruvate then enters the matrix, where the link reaction and the Krebs cycle oxidise it, releasing carbon dioxide and loading carriers (NADH and FADH₂) with electrons. On the inner membrane, the electron transport chain uses these electrons to pump protons into the intermembrane space; as protons flow back through ATP synthase, ATP is made. This is oxidative phosphorylation, and it needs oxygen as the final electron acceptor.

Form follows demand

The cristae greatly increase the area of inner membrane, and so the number of ATP synthase complexes. Cells with high energy demands, such as heart muscle, sperm tails and kidney tubule cells, are packed with mitochondria with dense cristae. Mitochondria also divide and fuse, so their number adjusts to the cell's needs.

Mitochondrial DNA is inherited almost entirely from the mother, because the egg contributes the cytoplasm of the zygote.

Investigate with the lab. Find the mitochondria in both cell types. Notice they appear in plant cells too, not only animal cells.

Key idea

Mitochondria make most of a cell's ATP by oxidative phosphorylation on their folded inner membrane; their number reflects the cell's energy demand.

Common mistake
Saying mitochondria "make energy". Energy can't be made: mitochondria transfer energy from food molecules into ATP.

Summary

  • Mitochondria have two membranes; the inner one is folded into cristae.
  • The Krebs cycle runs in the matrix; ATP is made on the inner membrane.
  • Active cells have many mitochondria with dense cristae.

Key terms

Cristae
The folds of the inner mitochondrial membrane, where ATP is made
Matrix
The fluid interior of a mitochondrion, where the Krebs cycle takes place
ATP synthase
The enzyme in the inner membrane that makes ATP as protons flow through it

Flashcards

Answer from memory, then open the card to check.

Where in the mitochondrion does the Krebs cycle take place?

In the matrix.

Why do mitochondria have folded inner membranes?

The cristae increase the surface area for the electron transport chain and ATP synthase, so more ATP can be made.

Check your understanding

  1. Which part of a mitochondrion contains ATP synthase?
    • The inner membrane
    • The intermembrane space only
    • The matrix only
    • The outer membrane
    Show the answer

    The inner membrane. ATP synthase sits in the inner membrane, using the proton gradient between the intermembrane space and the matrix.

  2. Sperm cells have many mitochondria packed around the base of the tail. What does this suggest?
    • Mitochondria make the tail longer
    • Sperm cells photosynthesise
    • Swimming uses a lot of ATP, supplied close to where it is needed
    • The mitochondria store genetic information for the embryo
    Show the answer

    Swimming uses a lot of ATP, supplied close to where it is needed. Mitochondria are concentrated where energy demand is high; the tail's beating is powered by ATP.

  3. A poison blocks the electron transport chain. Why does ATP production fall sharply even though glycolysis can continue?
    • ATP synthase is in the cytosol
    • Glycolysis happens in the mitochondria
    • Most ATP comes from oxidative phosphorylation, which depends on the electron transport chain building the proton gradient
    • The Krebs cycle makes most of the ATP directly
    Show the answer

    Most ATP comes from oxidative phosphorylation, which depends on the electron transport chain building the proton gradient. Glycolysis yields only 2 ATP per glucose; the bulk comes from proton flow through ATP synthase, which stops when electron transport stops.

  4. Why do muscle cells contain many mitochondria?
    • They carry out photosynthesis in daylight
    • They need a lot of ATP from aerobic respiration
    • They need to make a lot of protein for export
    • They store large amounts of starch
    Show the answer

    They need a lot of ATP from aerobic respiration. Active cells such as muscle need plenty of ATP, which mitochondria provide.
