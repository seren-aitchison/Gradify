Diffusion

Diffusion is the net movement of particles from where they are more concentrated to where they are less concentrated, driven by their random motion. It is how oxygen reaches your cells and carbon dioxide leaves them, and it explains why exchange surfaces look the way they do.

Before you start

  • Membrane transport

Random movement, net direction

Particles in liquids and gases move randomly. Where there are more particles, more move away than arrive, so there is a net movement down the concentration gradient until concentrations are equal. No energy from the cell is needed: the energy is the particles' own kinetic energy, so diffusion is faster when warmer.

What affects the rate

  • Concentration gradient: steeper gradient, faster diffusion.
  • Surface area: more area, faster.
  • Distance (thickness): shorter distance, much faster.
  • Temperature: higher, faster.
  • Size and nature of the particle: small, non-polar molecules cross membranes fastest.

Fick's law summarises the first three: rate ∝ (surface area × concentration difference) ÷ thickness. Lungs make the most of it with a huge alveolar area (around 70 m² in adults), walls only about 0.5 µm thick, and blood flow and breathing that keep the gradient steep.

Surface area to volume ratio

As an object gets bigger, its volume grows faster than its surface. A cell's needs depend on its volume, but its supply comes through its surface, so large organisms can't rely on diffusion across their outer surface and need specialised exchange surfaces and transport systems.

Worked example

Find the surface area to volume ratio of a cube with 2 µm sides and one with 4 µm sides.

  1. 2 µm cube: area = 6 × 2² = 24 µm²; volume = 2³ = 8 µm³; ratio = 3 : 1.
  2. 4 µm cube: area = 6 × 4² = 96 µm²; volume = 4³ = 64 µm³; ratio = 1.5 : 1. Doubling the side halves the ratio.
Key idea

Diffusion is net movement down a gradient by random motion; its rate rises with gradient and area and falls steeply with distance.

Common mistake
Saying particles "want" to spread out or "know" where to go. Their movement is random; the net direction is a statistical result.

Summary

  • Diffusion results from the random movement of particles and needs no cell energy.
  • Steeper gradients, larger areas, thinner barriers and higher temperatures speed it up.
  • Larger organisms have smaller surface area to volume ratios, so they need exchange surfaces.

Key terms

Diffusion
The net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration
Concentration gradient
A difference in concentration between two regions
Fick's law
Rate of diffusion is proportional to surface area times concentration difference, divided by thickness

Flashcards

Answer from memory, then open the card to check.

State Fick's law.

Rate of diffusion is proportional to surface area × concentration difference ÷ thickness of the exchange surface.

Why is diffusion faster at higher temperature?

Particles have more kinetic energy and move faster.

Check your understanding

  1. Which change would speed up diffusion of oxygen into the blood?
    • A lower temperature
    • A smaller concentration difference
    • A smaller surface area
    • A thinner barrier between air and blood
    Show the answer

    A thinner barrier between air and blood. Rate is inversely proportional to the distance diffused; thinner barriers speed exchange.

  2. In emphysema, alveolar walls break down into fewer, larger air spaces. Why does this make people breathless?
    • Oxygen starts to need active transport
    • The concentration gradient becomes too steep
    • The surface area for gas exchange falls, so less oxygen diffuses into the blood
    • The walls become thinner, slowing diffusion
    Show the answer

    The surface area for gas exchange falls, so less oxygen diffuses into the blood. Fewer, larger spaces have much less total surface area, reducing the rate of diffusion.

  3. Why are single-celled organisms able to rely on diffusion while a mouse cannot?
    • A small organism has a large surface area to volume ratio and short diffusion distances
    • Diffusion is faster in water than air
    • Mice have no cell membranes
    • Single cells don't need oxygen
    Show the answer

    A small organism has a large surface area to volume ratio and short diffusion distances. Diffusion is only fast over short distances; in a large animal most cells are far from the surface, so lungs and blood are needed.
