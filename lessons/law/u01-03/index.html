Law · School 1: Legal Foundations and the English Legal System · Nature and sources of law

Sources of law

The sources of law in England and Wales are legislation, case law (common law and equity), retained or assimilated law from the UK's EU membership, and, less directly, custom, conventions and international law. Knowing which source a rule comes from tells you how much weight it carries.

Primary sources

  • Acts of Parliament (primary legislation): the highest source; courts cannot strike them down.
  • Delegated (secondary) legislation: statutory instruments, byelaws and orders made under powers in an Act. Valid only within the parent Act's powers.
  • Case law: decisions of the courts, binding through the doctrine of precedent; includes the common law and equity.
  • Assimilated law: EU-derived law kept after Brexit by the European Union (Withdrawal) Act 2018, renamed "assimilated law" from 1 January 2024 by the Retained EU Law (Revocation and Reform) Act 2023.

Other sources

  • Custom: rarely a source today; a local custom must be immemorial (since 1189), reasonable, certain and continuously observed.
  • Constitutional conventions: binding in politics, not enforced by courts.
  • International law: treaties bind the UK internationally but are not part of domestic law until Parliament incorporates them (the dualist approach), as the Human Rights Act 1998 did for most ECHR rights.
  • Secondary sources: textbooks, articles, Law Commission reports. Persuasive only; never binding.

Hierarchy: Act of Parliament > delegated legislation (if within powers) > binding case law, with case law interpreting legislation. Where an Act and a common law rule conflict, the Act prevails.

Common mistakeDon't cite a textbook as authority for a legal rule. Cite the statute or the case; use the textbook only for commentary.

Summary

  • The main sources are legislation and case law.
  • Assimilated law, international law and secondary sources play lesser roles.

Flashcards

Answer from memory, then open the card to check.

What is "assimilated law"?

EU-derived law kept in UK law after Brexit, renamed from "retained EU law" from 1 January 2024 by the Retained EU Law (Revocation and Reform) Act 2023.

What does it mean that the UK is "dualist"?

A treaty does not become part of domestic law until Parliament passes legislation to incorporate it.

Why is an Act of Parliament the highest source of law?

Courts cannot strike Acts of Parliament down.

When is delegated legislation valid?

Only when it is within the powers given by the parent Act.

Name three forms of delegated legislation.

Statutory instruments, byelaws and orders.

Which Act kept EU-derived law in UK law after Brexit?

The European Union (Withdrawal) Act 2018.

Check your understanding

  1. Which is a primary source of law?
    • A Court of Appeal judgment
    • A Law Commission consultation paper
    • A law journal article
    • A leading textbook
    • A newspaper report of a trial
    Show the answer

    A Court of Appeal judgment. Judgments are primary sources; the rest are secondary and at most persuasive.

  2. A client relies on an unincorporated treaty the UK has ratified to challenge a decision in an English court. What is the best advice?
    • Only the Supreme Court may apply treaties
    • Ratification makes every treaty part of the common law
    • The treaty binds courts as if it were a statute
    • The treaty does not of itself create rights enforceable in domestic courts
    • The treaty overrides any conflicting Act
    Show the answer

    The treaty does not of itself create rights enforceable in domestic courts. Under the dualist approach, an unincorporated treaty creates no domestic rights, though courts may use it to help interpret ambiguous legislation.

  3. Delegated legislation conflicts with the Act under which it was made. What follows?
    • Both stand until Parliament repeals one
    • Only the minister can resolve the conflict
    • The courts must apply both equally
    • The delegated legislation can be declared ultra vires and invalid
    • The delegated legislation prevails because it is more recent
    Show the answer

    The delegated legislation can be declared ultra vires and invalid. Delegated legislation is valid only within the powers granted by its parent Act; courts can quash it on judicial review.
