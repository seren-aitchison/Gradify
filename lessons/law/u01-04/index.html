Common law and equity

The common law is the law developed by judges in the royal courts from the twelfth century; equity grew up in the Court of Chancery to soften its rigidity. Since the Judicature Acts 1873–75 the same courts administer both, and where they conflict, equity prevails.

Common law

  • Developed from local customs into law "common" to the whole kingdom by itinerant royal judges.
  • Worked through a writ system: no writ, no remedy. Its main remedy is damages, available as of right once liability is proved.

Equity

  • Petitioners went to the Lord Chancellor when the common law gave no fair remedy. The Court of Chancery developed rules based on conscience.
  • Equity created the trust, and remedies such as specific performance, injunctions, rescission and rectification.
  • Equitable remedies are discretionary: a court may refuse them, guided by the maxims.

The maxims (examples)

  • He who comes to equity must come with clean hands.
  • Delay defeats equity (laches).
  • Equity will not assist a volunteer.
  • Equity looks to the substance, not the form.
  • Equity follows the law.

The Judicature Acts fused the administration of law and equity, not their rules. Where the rules conflict, equity prevails (now Senior Courts Act 1981, s 49); the Earl of Oxford's Case (1615) first settled that priority.

Common mistakeDamages are available as of right; equitable remedies are not. Say "the court may grant" an injunction, not "the claimant is entitled to" one.

Summary

  • The Judicature Acts 1873–75 fused the administration of common law and equity.
  • Where they conflict, equity prevails.

Key terms

Equity
A body of rules developed in the Court of Chancery to soften the common law
Equitable remedy
A discretionary remedy such as an injunction or specific performance

Flashcards

Answer from memory, then open the card to check.

What is the main common law remedy?

Damages, available as of right once liability is established.

Which case first established that equity prevails over the common law?

The Earl of Oxford's Case (1615).

Define equity.

A body of rules developed in the Court of Chancery to soften the rigidity of the common law.

How did the common law develop?

Itinerant royal judges turned local customs into law common to the whole kingdom, from the twelfth century.

What did 'no writ, no remedy' mean in the early common law?

A claim could only be brought if it fitted a recognised writ.

Why did petitioners go to the Lord Chancellor?

Because the common law gave them no fair remedy; Chancery developed rules based on conscience.

Check your understanding

  1. Which remedy is equitable?
    • Damages for breach of contract
    • Debt
    • Liquidated damages
    • Nominal damages
    • Specific performance
    Show the answer

    Specific performance. Specific performance was developed by the Court of Chancery and is discretionary.

  2. A buyer delays four years before seeking specific performance of a contract to buy a house, with no explanation. Which maxim most threatens the claim?
    • Delay defeats equity
    • Equity acts in personam
    • Equity follows the law
    • Equity looks to the substance, not the form
    • Equity will not suffer a wrong without a remedy
    Show the answer

    Delay defeats equity. Unexplained delay (laches) can lead a court to refuse a discretionary equitable remedy.

  3. What did the Judicature Acts 1873–75 achieve?
    • All divisions of the new High Court could administer both law and equity, with equity prevailing in a conflict
    • They abolished equity
    • They gave the Court of Chancery exclusive jurisdiction over contracts
    • They made all remedies discretionary
    • They merged the rules of law and equity into one set
    Show the answer

    All divisions of the new High Court could administer both law and equity, with equity prevailing in a conflict. Administration was fused; the two bodies of rules remain distinct, with equity prevailing.

  4. A buyer seeks specific performance of a contract to buy a flat. The seller shows that the buyer misled him about her finances during negotiations, though not in a way that made the contract voidable. What is the most likely result?
    • The buyer is entitled to specific performance once breach is proved
    • The buyer loses any right to damages as well
    • The contract is automatically void
    • The court may refuse specific performance and leave the buyer to damages
    • The court must award specific performance because land is unique
    Show the answer

    The court may refuse specific performance and leave the buyer to damages. Equitable remedies are discretionary; "clean hands" can justify refusal, while the common law right to damages remains.
