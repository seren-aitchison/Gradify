Legal citation and referencing

Legal citation identifies sources precisely so a reader can find them. Most UK law schools use OSCOLA (the Oxford University Standard for the Citation of Legal Authorities); courts have their own practice directions.

By the end of this lesson you can

  • Cite cases, legislation and secondary sources using OSCOLA
  • Apply court practice on citation of judgments

Cases

  • Neutral citation (from 2001): [year] court number, for example [2019] UKSC 41, [2023] EWCA Civ 1416, [2016] EWHC 123 (Ch).
  • Law report: [year] volume report first page, for example [1932] AC 562. Square brackets where the year identifies the volume; round brackets where volume numbers do, for example (1868) LR 3 HL 330.
  • OSCOLA order: case name in italics, neutral citation, then the best report: R (Miller) v The Prime Minister [2019] UKSC 41, [2020] AC 373.
  • Pinpoint a paragraph with [paragraph number] or a page number.

Legislation

  • Acts: short title and year, no comma: Human Rights Act 1998, s 3.
  • Subsections and paragraphs: s 3(1)(a); sch 1 para 2.
  • Statutory instruments: Civil Procedure Rules 1998, SI 1998/3132, r 3.4.

Secondary sources

  • Books: Author, Title (edition, Publisher year) page.
  • Articles: Author, 'Title' (year) volume Journal page.

Court practice

In court, use the most authoritative report, which for cases reported there is the official Law Reports, as the senior courts' practice direction on citation requires.

Accurate citation is part of professional competence and academic integrity. Always cite what you have actually read.

Summary

  • OSCOLA is the standard citation style in most UK law schools.
  • Courts expect neutral citations and the most authoritative report.

Key terms

OSCOLA
The Oxford University Standard for the Citation of Legal Authorities
Neutral citation
A court-issued citation independent of any law report

Flashcards

Answer from memory, then open the card to check.

What is a neutral citation?

A court-assigned citation independent of any law report, for example [2019] UKSC 41.

How does OSCOLA cite a section of an Act?

Short title and year without a comma, then the section: Human Rights Act 1998, s 3.

What does OSCOLA stand for?

The Oxford University Standard for the Citation of Legal Authorities.

Since when have neutral citations been used?

From 2001.

When are square brackets used around the year in a law report citation?

Where the year identifies the volume.

When are round brackets used around the year in a law report citation?

Where volume numbers identify the report, for example (1868) LR 3 HL 330.

Check your understanding

  1. In [2023] EWCA Civ 1416, what does "EWCA Civ" mean?
    • Court of Appeal of England and Wales, Civil Division
    • Court of Appeal of England and Wales, Criminal Division
    • England and Wales Civil Appeals Tribunal
    • England and Wales County Court, civil list
    • High Court of England and Wales, Chancery Division
    Show the answer

    Court of Appeal of England and Wales, Civil Division. EWCA Civ identifies the Civil Division of the Court of Appeal.

  2. You are citing a Supreme Court case reported in the Appeal Cases. What is the best OSCOLA form?
    • Case name in italics, neutral citation, then the AC citation
    • Only the newspaper report
    • The AC citation followed by the neutral citation in brackets
    • The case name alone
    • The headnote's summary
    Show the answer

    Case name in italics, neutral citation, then the AC citation. OSCOLA gives the neutral citation followed by the most authoritative report.
