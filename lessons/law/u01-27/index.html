Case law analysis

Case law analysis is reading a judgment to extract the material facts, the issues, the decision, the ratio, and how the case fits with others. It is the core skill of common law study and practice.

Reading a law report

  • Citation and court: tells you the year, report and level of authority.
  • Headnote: the reporter's summary; useful, never authoritative.
  • Facts and procedural history: how the case reached this court.
  • Judgments: leading, concurring and dissenting. Note who agreed with whom.
  • Order: what the court actually did (appeal allowed or dismissed).

A method

  • Facts: which facts mattered to the decision?
  • Issue: what legal question did the court answer?
  • Decision: what was the answer and the order?
  • Reasoning: why? Which authorities were followed or distinguished?
  • Ratio and obiter (School 1 lesson on ratio).
  • Significance: how does it change or confirm the law? What questions does it leave open?
Worked example

Briefing Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co [1893] 1 QB 256 (CA).

  1. Facts: an advert promised £100 to anyone who caught flu after using the smoke ball as directed; £1,000 was deposited to show sincerity; Mrs Carlill used it and caught flu.
  2. Issue: was there a binding contract with her?
  3. Decision: yes; the company was liable.
  4. Reasoning: the advert was a unilateral offer to the world, accepted by performing the conditions; notification of acceptance was waived; using the ball was consideration; the deposit showed intention.
  5. Significance: offers can be made to the world; unilateral contracts are accepted by performance.

A good case note is short: facts that matter, the principle, and one line on significance.

Summary

  • Extract material facts, issues, decision and ratio.
  • Place the case in the line of authority.

Key terms

Law report
A published account of a judgment with headnote and citation
Material facts
The facts that the court treated as legally significant

Is a headnote part of the judgment?

No. It is the law reporter's summary and has no authority.

What should you record about each judge in an appellate case?

Whether they gave the leading judgment, concurred (and on what reasoning) or dissented.

Define a law report.

A published account of a judgment with headnote and citation.

Check your understanding

  1. Which part of a law report has no authority?
    • The concurring judgments
    • The court's order
    • The headnote
    • The leading judgment
    • The ratio decidendi
    Show the answer

    The headnote. The headnote is written by the reporter, not the court.

  2. In a three-judge Court of Appeal case, two judges allow the appeal for different reasons and one dissents. What is the main difficulty in finding the ratio?
    • The case cannot be cited in a later court
    • The dissent's reasoning becomes persuasive authority only
    • The headnote's statement of the ratio is binding
    • The leading judgment's reasoning is the ratio
    • There may be no shared majority reasoning, so no clear ratio
    Show the answer

    There may be no shared majority reasoning, so no clear ratio. When majority judges give different reasons, the binding principle is limited to what they share, if anything.
