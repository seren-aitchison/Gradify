The legal profession

The legal profession in England and Wales is split mainly between solicitors and barristers, with chartered legal executives and other authorised professionals. The roles overlap more than they used to, but differences remain.

  • Describe the roles of solicitors, barristers and other authorised professionals
  • Explain how each branch qualifies and is regulated

Solicitors

  • Advise clients directly on almost every area of law; draft documents; run transactions and litigation.
  • Work in firms, in-house for companies and public bodies, or in the CPS and government legal service.
  • May obtain higher rights of audience to appear in the higher courts as solicitor-advocates.
  • Regulated by the SRA. Since 2021 most qualify through the Solicitors Qualifying Examination (SQE) and qualifying work experience (School 18).

Barristers

  • Specialist advocates and advisers; most are self-employed in chambers, instructed by solicitors. Some take instructions directly from the public under the public access scheme.
  • Bound by the "cab-rank rule": a self-employed barrister must accept instructions within their competence and availability, whatever they think of the client or cause (subject to exceptions).
  • Regulated by the BSB; senior barristers may be appointed King's Counsel (KC).

Others

  • Chartered legal executives (CILEx) can specialise and, with the right authorisation, run their own practices and become judges.
  • Licensed conveyancers, costs lawyers, notaries, patent and trade mark attorneys.
  • Alternative business structures (Legal Services Act 2007) allow non-lawyer ownership of legal businesses.

Who you instruct depends on what the client needs: solicitors manage the matter and the client; barristers provide specialist advocacy and opinion.

Summary

  • Solicitors and barristers are the main branches of the profession.
  • The SRA, BSB and CILEx Regulation regulate them.

Key terms

Solicitors Qualifying Examination
The SRA's centralised assessment for qualifying as a solicitor
Rights of audience

Flashcards

Answer from memory, then open the card to check.

What is the cab-rank rule?

A self-employed barrister must accept instructions in their field and at a proper fee if available, regardless of the client's identity or cause.

What does KC stand for?

King's Counsel: senior barristers (and some solicitor-advocates) appointed for excellence in advocacy.

Define rights of audience.

The right to appear and conduct proceedings in court.

Define the Solicitors Qualifying Examination.

The SRA's centralised assessment for qualifying as a solicitor.

Name four places solicitors work.

In firms, in-house for companies and public bodies, in the CPS and in the government legal service.

How can solicitors appear in the higher courts?

By obtaining higher rights of audience as solicitor-advocates.

Check your understanding

  1. Who normally instructs a self-employed barrister?
    • A solicitor, or the client directly under public access
    • Only a solicitor; direct instruction is banned
    • Only the court, through the list office
    • The Bar Council, which allocates cases
    • The client, but only in criminal cases
    Show the answer

    A solicitor, or the client directly under public access. Barristers are usually instructed by solicitors; public access allows direct instruction in suitable cases.

  2. A barrister declines a brief because they find the defendant's views offensive, though the case is in their field and they are free. What principle is at stake?
    • Legal professional privilege
    • The cab-rank rule
    • The duty of candour
    • The rule in Pepper v Hart
    • Without-prejudice protection
    Show the answer

    The cab-rank rule. The cab-rank rule requires a barrister to accept work within their competence regardless of the client's identity or views.
