Access to justice

Access to justice means people can understand their rights, get advice and bring or defend claims fairly, whatever their means. It is a principle of the rule of law and a practical problem of cost, complexity and delay.

By the end of this lesson you can

  • Identify barriers to access to justice
  • Evaluate ways of widening access, including court protection

Barriers

  • Cost: legal fees, court fees and the risk of paying the other side's costs.
  • Complexity: procedure and language that unrepresented people find hard.
  • Delay: backlogs in the Crown Court, family courts and some tribunals.
  • Advice deserts: areas with little or no legal aid provision for housing or immigration advice.
  • Digital exclusion as courts move services online.

Ways of widening access

  • Legal aid (next lesson); conditional fee agreements ("no win, no fee") and damages-based agreements; after-the-event insurance; legal expenses insurance; trade union funding.
  • Pro bono advice (law centres, university law clinics, the Bar's Advocate scheme).
  • Small claims track with limited costs; online money claims; fixed recoverable costs.
  • Litigants in person supported by McKenzie friends (who may help but usually not speak).

Courts protecting access

The constitutional right of access to the courts is protected at common law: R v Lord Chancellor, ex p Witham [1998] (court fees that blocked access unlawful) and R (UNISON) v Lord Chancellor [2017] (tribunal fees unlawful).

Key idea

Access to justice is not only about courts; early advice prevents problems from escalating, which is why advice deserts matter.

Summary

  • Cost, complexity and advice deserts limit access to justice.
  • Courts have protected access as a constitutional right.

Key terms

Access to justice
People's ability to understand their rights and resolve legal problems fairly
Pro bono

Questions this page answers

Use them as flashcards: cover the answer, say it out loud, then check.

What does “access to justice” mean?

People's ability to understand their rights and resolve legal problems fairly

What does “pro bono” mean?

Free legal work done in the public interest

What is a McKenzie friend?

A person who assists a litigant in person in court, by taking notes and quietly advising, usually without rights of audience.

What is a conditional fee agreement?

A "no win, no fee" agreement: the lawyer is paid (often with a success fee) only if the case succeeds.

Define access to justice.

People's ability to understand their rights and resolve legal problems fairly, whatever their means.

Define pro bono work.

Free legal work done in the public interest.

Name the five barriers to access to justice in the lesson.

Cost, complexity, delay, advice deserts and digital exclusion.

What is an advice desert?

An area with little or no legal aid provision, for example for housing or immigration advice.

Name three funding routes other than legal aid.

Any three of: conditional fee agreements, damages-based agreements, after-the-event insurance, legal expenses insurance, trade union funding.

Name three sources of pro bono advice.

Law centres, university law clinics and the Bar's Advocate scheme.

Name three civil procedure features that widen access.

The small claims track with limited costs, online money claims and fixed recoverable costs.

What did R v Lord Chancellor, ex p Witham [1998] decide?

Court fees that blocked access to the courts were unlawful.

Why do advice deserts matter even beyond the courts?

Early advice prevents problems from escalating.

What is the cost?

Cost: legal fees, court fees and the risk of paying the other side's costs.

What is the complexity?

Complexity: procedure and language that unrepresented people find hard.

What is the delay?

Delay: backlogs in the Crown Court, family courts and some tribunals.

Check your understanding

  1. Which case held that court fees which prevented access to the courts were unlawful?
    • Donoghue v Stevenson
    • Halsey v Milton Keynes
    • Pepper v Hart
    • Porter v Magill
    • R v Lord Chancellor, ex p Witham
    Show the answer

    R v Lord Chancellor, ex p Witham. Witham established the common law right of access to the courts against prohibitive fees.

  2. A client with a strong personal injury claim cannot afford to pay a solicitor by the hour. What is the most likely funding route?
    • A conditional fee agreement, usually with after-the-event insurance
    • Civil legal aid, which covers most personal injury claims
    • Crown funding through the CPS
    • Pro bono representation by the court
    • The Legal Ombudsman's fund
    Show the answer

    A conditional fee agreement, usually with after-the-event insurance. Most personal injury claims were taken out of civil legal aid in April 2000 (Access to Justice Act 1999), so they are usually funded by CFAs.
