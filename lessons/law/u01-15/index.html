Separation of powers

The separation of powers divides state power between the legislature, the executive and the judiciary so that no one branch holds unchecked power. The UK has a partial separation with significant overlaps and strong checks and balances.

  • Describe the three branches of state power

The three branches

  • Legislature: Parliament (Commons, Lords, the King) makes law.
  • Executive: the King's government (Prime Minister, Cabinet, departments, civil service) runs the state and makes delegated legislation.
  • Judiciary: the courts interpret and apply law.

Overlaps in the UK

  • Ministers sit in Parliament, and the government usually controls the Commons.
  • Ministers make delegated legislation.
  • Before 2009, the Appellate Committee of the House of Lords was the top court, and the Lord Chancellor was judge, minister and Speaker of the Lords.

Strengthening the separation

  • The Constitutional Reform Act 2005: the Lord Chief Justice heads the judiciary; the Judicial Appointments Commission; the UK Supreme Court (from 2009); a statutory duty to uphold judicial independence.
  • Judicial review keeps the executive within its powers; courts respect Parliament's privileges and its legislative supremacy.

The UK model is checks and balances, not strict separation. Each branch limits the others in recognised ways.

Summary

  • The UK has a partial separation of powers.
  • The Constitutional Reform Act 2005 strengthened it.

Key terms

Separation of powers
Dividing state power between legislature, executive and judiciary
Checks and balances
Mechanisms allowing each branch to limit the others

Flashcards

Answer from memory, then open the card to check.

What did the Constitutional Reform Act 2005 change about the Lord Chancellor?

It removed the Lord Chancellor's judicial role, made the Lord Chief Justice head of the judiciary, and created the Supreme Court and the Judicial Appointments Commission.

Name one overlap between the legislature and the executive in the UK.

Ministers sit in and are drawn from Parliament; the government usually controls the Commons; ministers make delegated legislation.

Define checks and balances.

Mechanisms allowing each branch of the state to limit the others.

What does the legislature consist of in the UK?

Parliament: the Commons, the Lords and the King.

What does the executive consist of in the UK?

The King's government: Prime Minister, Cabinet, departments and civil service.

What is the judiciary's function?

To interpret and apply law.

Check your understanding

  1. Which body is the executive?
    • Magistrates
    • Parliament
    • The government and its departments
    • The Judicial Appointments Commission
    • The Supreme Court
    Show the answer

    The government and its departments. The executive governs and implements law.

  2. A student argues the UK has "no separation of powers at all". What is the best response?
    • Only the executive is separate; Parliament and the courts are fused
    • Separation exists only by convention and has no legal effect at all
    • The 2005 Act removed ministers from Parliament to separate the branches
    • There is a partial separation with checks and balances, strengthened in 2005
    • There is a strict, codified separation like that of the United States
    Show the answer

    There is a partial separation with checks and balances, strengthened in 2005. The branches overlap but are separated in important ways, particularly the independence of the judiciary.
