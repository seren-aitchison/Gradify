Parliament and the legislative process

Parliament makes primary legislation through a structured process of readings, committee scrutiny and Royal Assent. Understanding the stages helps you read legislative history and judge how much scrutiny a provision received.

Kinds of bill

  • Public bills: change the general law; most are government bills.
  • Private Members' bills: introduced by backbenchers; few become law without government support.
  • Private bills: affect particular people or places.
  • Hybrid bills: public bills that affect particular private interests (for example, HS2).

The stages

  • First reading: formal introduction; no debate.
  • Second reading: debate on the principle.
  • Committee stage: line-by-line scrutiny, usually in a Public Bill Committee in the Commons; amendments.
  • Report stage: the whole House considers amendments.
  • Third reading: final debate on the bill as amended.
  • The other House: the same stages.
  • Ping-pong: amendments pass between the Houses until agreement.
  • Royal Assent: the bill becomes an Act. Commencement may be on Assent, on a set date or by commencement regulations.

Limits on the Lords

Under the Parliament Acts 1911 and 1949, the Lords can delay a money bill by one month and most other public bills by about a year; the Commons can then pass them without Lords consent. The Salisbury convention means the Lords don't block manifesto bills at second reading.

The Parliament Acts 1911 and 1949 let the Commons present a bill for Royal Assent without the Lords' consent after a set delay. The Hunting Act 2004 was passed this way, and its validity was upheld in R (Jackson) v Attorney General [2005] UKHL 56.

An Act is not necessarily in force on Royal Assent. Always check commencement.

Summary

  • Bills pass through readings, committee, report and Royal Assent.
  • The Parliament Acts limit the Lords to delaying most bills.

Key terms

Royal Assent
The monarch's formal agreement that turns a bill into an Act
Parliament Acts
The 1911 and 1949 Acts limiting the Lords' power to block bills

Case and statute cards

Case

R (Jackson) v Attorney General [2005] UKHL 56

Facts
The Countryside Alliance argued that the Hunting Act 2004, passed under the Parliament Acts without Lords consent, was invalid because the 1949 Act was.
Held
The 1949 Act, and so the Hunting Act, were valid.
Principle
Acts passed under the Parliament Acts 1911 and 1949 are primary legislation; obiter dicta questioned absolute sovereignty.
Statute

Parliament Act 1911, s 2 (as amended by the Parliament Act 1949)

A public Bill (other than one extending Parliament's life) passed by the Commons in two successive sessions, with one year between second reading and final passage, and rejected by the Lords, can receive Royal Assent without Lords consent.

What happens at second reading?

The House debates the general principles of the bill.

How long can the Lords delay most public bills under the Parliament Acts?

About one year (two sessions, with one year between Commons second reading and passing).
