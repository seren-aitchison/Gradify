Public and private law

Public law governs the state's relationship with individuals and the organisation of the state itself; private law governs relationships between individuals and organisations. The line matters because it decides which procedures, remedies and principles apply.

  • Distinguish public law from private law

Public law

  • Constitutional law: the structure and powers of the state.
  • Administrative law: the control of public bodies, mainly through judicial review.
  • Criminal law: usually classed as public law because the state prosecutes.
  • Human rights law: rights against public authorities (Human Rights Act 1998, s 6).

Private law

  • Contract, tort, property, trusts, family and succession, company law.
  • Enforced by the individual whose rights are affected.

Why the line matters

  • Challenges to public bodies' decisions usually go by judicial review (CPR Part 54), with a short time limit (promptly and in any event within three months) and a permission stage.
  • Using an ordinary claim to dodge those protections can be an abuse of process (O'Reilly v Mackman [1983]), though the courts now take a flexible approach.
Key idea

The test is what kind of relationship and what kind of right is involved, not just who the parties are. A council can be sued in contract like anyone else.

Common mistake
Don't assume every claim against a public body is public law. A council's breach of a building contract is a private law claim.

Summary

  • Public law covers constitutional, administrative and criminal law.
  • Private law covers contract, tort, property and family law.

Key terms

Public law
Law governing the state and its relationship with individuals
Judicial review
The court's supervision of the lawfulness of public decisions

Case and statute cards

Case

O'Reilly v Mackman [1983] 2 AC 237

Facts
Prisoners challenged disciplinary decisions of a prison board of visitors by ordinary private law actions rather than judicial review.
Held
The claims were an abuse of process: public law challenges should normally be brought by judicial review.
Principle
The procedural exclusivity principle: public law decisions are generally challenged by judicial review (now applied flexibly).

Is criminal law public or private law?

Public law: the state prosecutes wrongs against society.

What is the time limit for a judicial review claim?

Promptly and in any event within three months of the grounds arising (CPR 54.5), shorter in some areas such as planning.

Check your understanding

  1. Which is private law?
    • Administrative law
    • Constitutional law
    • Criminal law
    • Human rights law against a public authority
    • Tort
    Show the answer

    Tort. Tort governs wrongs between private persons and is enforced by the person harmed.

  2. A council fails to pay a builder for work under a contract. Which route is most appropriate?
    • A complaint under the Human Rights Act only
    • A criminal prosecution
    • An appeal to the Supreme Court
    • An ordinary contract claim
    • Judicial review
    Show the answer

    An ordinary contract claim. The dispute is about private contractual rights, so it is a private law claim even though one party is a public body.
