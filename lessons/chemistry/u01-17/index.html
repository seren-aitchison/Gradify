Chemistry · School 1: Foundations of Chemistry · The mole and stoichiometry

Stoichiometry

Stoichiometry is the calculation of the amounts of substances that react and form, using the mole ratios in a balanced equation. It answers questions such as "how much product can I make from this much reactant?"

By the end of this lesson you can

  • Use mole ratios from a balanced equation to calculate reacting masses
  • Follow the three-step method: mass → moles → ratio → mass
  • Calculate gas volumes from moles at room temperature and pressure

Before you start

  • Molar mass ; Balancing equations

The method

  • Write the balanced equation.
  • Convert the known mass to moles: n = m ÷ M.
  • Use the mole ratio from the equation to find moles of the other substance.
  • Convert back to mass (m = n × M), or to volume or concentration as needed.
Worked example

What mass of calcium oxide forms when 25.0 g of calcium carbonate is heated? CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ (M: CaCO₃ 100.1, CaO 56.08 g/mol).

  1. n(CaCO₃) = 25.0 ÷ 100.1 = 0.250 mol.
  2. Ratio 1 : 1, so n(CaO) = 0.250 mol.
  3. m(CaO) = 0.250 × 56.08 = 14.0 g.

Gases

One mole of any gas occupies about 24.0 dm³ at room temperature and pressure (20 °C, 1 atm), the molar gas volume. More precisely, use the ideal gas equation pV = nRT.

Worked example

What volume of CO₂ is released in the example above, at room temperature and pressure?

  1. n(CO₂) = 0.250 mol; V = 0.250 × 24.0 = 6.00 dm³.

Masses don't react in simple ratios; moles do. Always go through moles.

Common mistakeUsing the mass ratio as if it were the mole ratio. Convert to moles first.

Summary

  • Mass → moles → mole ratio → moles → mass.
  • 1 mol of gas ≈ 24 dm³ at room temperature and pressure.

Key terms

Stoichiometry
The calculation of quantities of reactants and products using balanced equations
Mole ratio
The ratio of the amounts of substances in a balanced equation, given by the coefficients
Molar gas volume
The volume of one mole of gas: about 24.0 dm³ at room temperature and pressure

Flashcards

Answer from memory, then open the card to check.

What are the steps in a reacting-mass calculation?

Balanced equation; mass to moles; use the mole ratio; moles to mass.

What volume does 0.5 mol of any gas occupy at room temperature and pressure?

About 12 dm³ (0.5 × 24.0).

Check your understanding

  1. In N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃, how many moles of H₂ react with 2 mol of N₂?
    • 2
    • 3
    • 4
    • 6
    Show the answer

    6. The mole ratio N₂ : H₂ is 1 : 3, so 2 mol of N₂ needs 6 mol of H₂.

  2. What mass of magnesium oxide forms from 6.0 g of magnesium? 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO (M: Mg 24.3, MgO 40.3 g/mol)
    • 10.0 g
    • 20.0 g
    • 5.0 g
    • 6.0 g
    Show the answer

    10.0 g. n(Mg) = 6.0 ÷ 24.3 = 0.247 mol = n(MgO); 0.247 × 40.3 = 9.95 g ≈ 10.0 g.

  3. Iron is extracted by Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂. What mass of iron can be made from 1.00 tonne of Fe₂O₃ (M = 159.7 g/mol; Fe 55.85)?
    • 0.233 tonnes
    • 0.350 tonnes
    • 0.700 tonnes
    • 1.00 tonne
    Show the answer

    0.700 tonnes. 1.00 × 10⁶ g ÷ 159.7 = 6,262 mol of Fe₂O₃, giving 12,524 mol of Fe × 55.85 g = 6.99 × 10⁵ g = 0.700 t.
