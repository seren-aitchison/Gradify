Mixtures

Mixtures can be separated by physical methods that exploit differences in the properties of their parts: particle size, solubility, boiling point or how strongly they stick to a surface. This lesson matches separation methods to mixtures.

By the end of this lesson you can

  • Choose a suitable method to separate a given mixture
  • Explain filtration, crystallisation, distillation and chromatography in terms of properties
  • Calculate and interpret Rf values

Before you start

  • Matter and its classification

Separation methods

  • Filtration: separates an insoluble solid from a liquid (sand from water).
  • Evaporation and crystallisation: obtains a soluble solid from its solution (salt from salt water); crystallisation gives purer crystals by slow cooling.
  • Simple distillation: separates a liquid from a solution by boiling and condensing (pure water from sea water).
  • Fractional distillation: separates miscible liquids with different boiling points (ethanol from water; crude oil into fractions).
  • Chromatography: separates dissolved substances that move at different rates through a stationary phase (dyes in ink).
  • Separating funnel: separates immiscible liquids (oil and water).

Chromatography

A mobile phase (solvent) moves through a stationary phase (paper or silica). Substances more attracted to the mobile phase travel further. The Rf value is fixed for a substance in given conditions, so it can help identify it.

Rf = distance moved by spot / distance moved by solvent front
Worked example

A dye spot moves 3.6 cm while the solvent front moves 8.0 cm. Calculate Rf.

  1. Rf = 3.6 ÷ 8.0 = 0.45.
  2. Rf has no units and is always between 0 and 1.
Key idea

Separation methods work because components differ in a physical property: size, solubility, boiling point or affinity for a phase.

Common mistake
Calling dissolving a chemical change. Dissolving salt is a physical process: evaporating the water gets the salt back.

Summary

  • Filtration, crystallisation, distillation and chromatography each exploit a different property.
  • Rf = spot distance ÷ solvent distance, between 0 and 1.

Key terms

Mobile phase
The solvent that moves through a chromatography system
Stationary phase
The material that doesn't move in chromatography, such as paper or silica
Rf value
The ratio of the distance moved by a substance to the distance moved by the solvent front

Flashcards

Answer from memory, then open the card to check.

Which method separates ethanol (boiling point 78 °C) from water?

Fractional distillation.

What does an Rf value of 0.9 tell you about a substance in that system?

It is much more attracted to the mobile phase than the stationary phase, so it moves almost as far as the solvent.

Check your understanding

  1. Which method separates an insoluble solid from a liquid?
    • Chromatography
    • Crystallisation
    • Distillation
    • Filtration
    Show the answer

    Filtration. Filter paper traps the undissolved solid particles while the liquid passes through.

  2. Why can fractional distillation separate the hydrocarbons in crude oil?
    • They have different boiling points, so they condense at different heights in the column
    • They have different densities, so the heavier ones sink to the bottom of the column first
    • They have different solubilities, so each one dissolves out at a different height in the column
    • They react with the hot column at different rates, so each forms a new product at its own height
    Show the answer

    They have different boiling points, so they condense at different heights in the column. Larger molecules have stronger intermolecular forces and higher boiling points, so they condense lower in the column.

  3. In paper chromatography an unknown gives one spot with Rf 0.62 in solvent A, matching a known dye. In solvent B it gives two spots. What should you conclude?
    • The unknown is a mixture of two substances that happen to have the same Rf in solvent A
    • The unknown is pure, since one spot in any single solvent proves a substance has only one component
    • The unknown is the known dye, and solvent B has broken it down into two new substances
    • The unknown is the known dye, because an Rf match in one solvent is enough to identify a substance
    Show the answer

    The unknown is a mixture of two substances that happen to have the same Rf in solvent A. Different substances can share an Rf in one solvent; separation in a second solvent reveals two components.
