Chemistry · School 1: Foundations of Chemistry · Matter

What is chemistry?

Chemistry is the study of matter: what substances are made of, how their particles are arranged and bonded, and how substances change into new ones in chemical reactions. It is often called the central science because it links physics with biology, medicine, materials and the environment.

The subject

Chemists explain the properties of substances (why salt dissolves in water but wax doesn't) in terms of the particles they're made of and the forces between them, and they make new substances (medicines, polymers, fertilisers) by controlling reactions.

  • Physical chemistry: energy, rates and equilibrium, the quantitative laws of change.
  • Inorganic chemistry: the elements and their compounds, especially metals and minerals.
  • Organic chemistry: carbon compounds, from fuels to pharmaceuticals and the molecules of life.
  • Analytical chemistry: finding out what is in a sample and how much.

Three levels of description

Chemistry works at three levels at once: the macroscopic (what we see and measure, such as a colour change), the particulate (atoms, ions and molecules) and the symbolic (formulae and equations). Moving between them is a core chemical skill: a fizzing tablet (macroscopic) is carbonate ions reacting with acid to release CO₂ molecules (particulate), written as CO₃²⁻ + 2H⁺ → H₂O + CO₂ (symbolic).

Chemistry in the world

The Haber process makes ammonia for fertilisers that help feed roughly half of the world's population. Medicinal chemists design drugs to fit protein targets. Atmospheric chemistry explained the ozone hole and led to the Montreal Protocol (1987), which phased out CFCs.

Chemistry explains the properties and changes of matter in terms of particles, and describes them with symbols.

Common mistakeThinking "chemicals" are only artificial substances. Everything, including water and the food we eat, is made of chemicals.

Summary

  • Chemistry studies the composition, structure, properties and reactions of matter.
  • It connects the macroscopic, particulate and symbolic levels.

Key terms

Matter
Anything that has mass and takes up space
Chemical reaction
A process in which substances are changed into different substances by rearranging atoms
Macroscopic
The scale of things we can see and measure directly

Flashcards

Why is chemistry called the central science?

It connects physics (the behaviour of particles and energy) with biology, medicine, materials and earth science, which all depend on chemical substances and reactions.

Name the three levels at which chemists describe matter.

Macroscopic, particulate (atoms, ions, molecules) and symbolic (formulae and equations).

Check your understanding

  1. Which branch of chemistry mainly studies carbon compounds?
    • Analytical chemistry
    • Inorganic chemistry
    • Organic chemistry
    • Physical chemistry
    Show the answer

    Organic chemistry. Organic chemistry is the chemistry of carbon compounds, including most molecules in living things and medicines.

  2. A chemist measures how quickly a medicine breaks down at different temperatures. Which branch is this mostly?
    • Biochemistry (drug metabolism)
    • Inorganic chemistry (salts)
    • Organic chemistry (synthesis)
    • Physical chemistry (kinetics)
    Show the answer

    Physical chemistry (kinetics). Rates of reaction and their temperature dependence belong to physical chemistry.

  3. Which statement links all three levels of description correctly?
    • Limewater turns cloudy (macroscopic) because Ca²⁺ and CO₃²⁻ ions form insoluble calcium carbonate (particulate), Ca(OH)₂ + CO₂ → CaCO₃ + H₂O (symbolic)
    • Limewater turns cloudy (macroscopic) because the carbon dioxide gas condenses into tiny droplets (particulate), CO₂(g) → CO₂(l) (symbolic)
    • Limewater turns cloudy (particulate) because CO₂ molecules dissolve in the water (macroscopic), shown by the formula CaCO₃ alone (symbolic)
    • Limewater turns cloudy (symbolic) because calcium carbonate is a white solid (macroscopic), and Ca(OH)₂ + CO₂ → CaCO₃ + H₂O shows the ions (particulate)
    Show the answer

    Limewater turns cloudy (macroscopic) because Ca²⁺ and CO₃²⁻ ions form insoluble calcium carbonate (particulate), Ca(OH)₂ + CO₂ → CaCO₃ + H₂O (symbolic). Only this answer connects what is observed to the particles and to the equation.
