Percentage yield

The actual amount of product is almost always less than the theoretical maximum. Percentage yield compares the two and shows how efficient a preparation is.

Open this lesson in the course → Interactive version with practice, progress and spaced reviews. Free.

By the end of this lesson you can

  • Calculate theoretical yield and percentage yield
  • Explain why actual yields are below 100%
  • Distinguish yield from purity

Before you start

  • Limiting reagents

Definitions

percentage yield = actual yield / theoretical yield × 100

The theoretical yield is the maximum mass of product calculated from the limiting reagent. The actual yield is the mass obtained and weighed.

Why yields fall short

  • The reaction may be reversible and reach equilibrium before completion.
  • Side reactions make other products.
  • Product is lost in transfers, filtration, crystallisation and purification.
  • The reaction may be incomplete in the time allowed.
Worked example

8.0 g of copper(II) oxide (M 79.55) reacts with excess sulfuric acid, and 14.2 g of copper(II) sulfate pentahydrate (M 249.7) crystals are obtained. Calculate the percentage yield.

  1. n(CuO) = 8.0 ÷ 79.55 = 0.101 mol, giving 0.101 mol of CuSO₄·5H₂O at most.
  2. Theoretical yield = 0.101 × 249.7 = 25.1 g.
  3. Percentage yield = 14.2 ÷ 25.1 × 100 = 56.6%.

Yield isn't purity

A yield above 100% usually means the product is impure or wet. Purity is assessed separately, for example by melting point or chromatography.

Key idea

Percentage yield = actual ÷ theoretical × 100, and losses, side reactions and equilibria keep it below 100%.

Common mistake
Calculating the theoretical yield from a reactant in excess. Always use the limiting reagent.

Summary

  • % yield = actual ÷ theoretical × 100.
  • Over 100% means the product is impure or wet.

Key terms

Theoretical yield
The maximum mass of product that could form from the limiting reagent
Percentage yield
The actual yield as a percentage of the theoretical yield

Flashcards

Answer from memory, then open the card to check.

Give three reasons why the actual yield is less than the theoretical yield.

The reaction is reversible or incomplete, side reactions occur, and product is lost during separation and purification.

What does a percentage yield of 108% suggest?

The product is impure or still wet.

Check your understanding

  1. Which is calculated from the limiting reagent?
    • The actual yield
    • The excess reagent
    • The percentage purity
    • The theoretical yield
    Show the answer

    The theoretical yield. The theoretical yield is the maximum product the limiting reagent can give.

  2. A multi-step synthesis has three steps, each with 80% yield. What is the overall yield?
    • 240%
    • 27%
    • 51%
    • 80%
    Show the answer

    51%. Overall yield = 0.80 × 0.80 × 0.80 = 0.512, or 51%: losses multiply.

  3. A chemist recrystallises a product twice. How is this likely to affect yield and purity?
    • Both fall
    • Both rise
    • Purity rises but yield falls, because some product stays dissolved in the solvent each time
    • Yield rises but purity falls
    Show the answer

    Purity rises but yield falls, because some product stays dissolved in the solvent each time. Recrystallisation removes impurities but always loses some product in the mother liquor.
