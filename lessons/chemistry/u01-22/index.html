Dilutions

Diluting a solution adds solvent but doesn't change the amount of solute. This lesson covers the dilution formula, serial dilutions and how to carry out a dilution accurately.

By the end of this lesson you can

  • Use c₁V₁ = c₂V₂ to calculate dilutions
  • Plan a serial dilution
  • Choose suitable glassware for accurate dilutions

Before you start

  • Concentration

The dilution equation

The amount of solute stays the same, so n = c₁V₁ = c₂V₂. Any volume unit can be used as long as it's the same on both sides.

c1V1 = c2V2
Worked example

What volume of 2.00 mol/dm³ sulfuric acid is needed to make 250 cm³ of 0.100 mol/dm³ acid?

  1. V₁ = c₂V₂ ÷ c₁ = 0.100 × 250 ÷ 2.00 = 12.5 cm³.
  2. Measure 12.5 cm³ with a burette or pipette, add to a 250 cm³ volumetric flask (adding acid to water, never water to concentrated acid) and make up to the mark.

Serial dilution

A serial dilution repeats the same dilution several times, giving concentrations that fall by a constant factor. Diluting 1 cm³ into 9 cm³ of water (a 1 in 10 dilution) five times gives concentrations of 10⁻¹ to 10⁻⁵ of the original. Serial dilutions are used to make calibration standards and to count bacteria.

Dilution factor

Dilution factor = final volume ÷ initial volume (or c₁ ÷ c₂). A 1 in 10 dilution has a dilution factor of 10.

Key idea

Diluting doesn't change the moles of solute: c₁V₁ = c₂V₂.

Common mistake
Adding 10 cm³ of solution to 10 cm³ of water and calling it a 1 in 10 dilution. That's a 1 in 2 dilution; 1 in 10 is 1 part in a total of 10.

Summary

  • c₁V₁ = c₂V₂.
  • A 1 in 10 dilution means 1 part sample in 10 parts total.

Key terms

Dilution
Reducing the concentration of a solution by adding solvent
Serial dilution
A series of stepwise dilutions by the same factor
Dilution factor
The ratio of the final volume to the initial volume (or of the initial to final concentration)

Questions this page answers

Use them as flashcards: cover the answer, say it out loud, then check.

What does “dilution” mean?

Reducing the concentration of a solution by adding solvent

What does “serial dilution” mean?

A series of stepwise dilutions by the same factor

What does “dilution factor” mean?

The ratio of the final volume to the initial volume (or of the initial to final concentration)

Why does diluting a solution not change the number of moles of solute?

Only solvent is added; the solute particles already present are unchanged.

What concentration results from three successive 1 in 10 dilutions of a 0.5 mol/dm³ solution?

0.5 × 10⁻³ = 5 × 10⁻⁴ mol/dm³.

Check your understanding

  1. 10 cm³ of solution is made up to 100 cm³. What is the dilution factor?
    • 0.1
    • 10
    • 100
    • 90
    Show the answer

    10. Dilution factor = final ÷ initial volume = 100 ÷ 10 = 10.

  2. Which is the most accurate way to make 250 cm³ of a 1 in 50 dilution?
    • Add 245 cm³ of water to 5 cm³ in a beaker
    • Add 5 cm³ to 250 cm³ of water in a beaker
    • Pipette 5.00 cm³ into a 250 cm³ volumetric flask and make up to the mark
    • Pour 50 cm³ into a measuring cylinder and add water to 250 cm³
    Show the answer

    Pipette 5.00 cm³ into a 250 cm³ volumetric flask and make up to the mark. A pipette and volumetric flask give the most accurate volumes; adding to 250 cm³ of water gives 255 cm³ in total.

  3. When diluting concentrated sulfuric acid, why must the acid be added slowly to water rather than water to the acid?
    • Acid is denser than water so mixing fails
    • It makes the dilution more accurate
    • Mixing releases a lot of heat; adding water to acid can boil the water locally and spray acid
    • Water reacts to form hydrogen
    Show the answer

    Mixing releases a lot of heat; adding water to acid can boil the water locally and spray acid. The large enthalpy of dilution is safely absorbed by a large volume of water; small amounts of water added to acid can boil explosively.
