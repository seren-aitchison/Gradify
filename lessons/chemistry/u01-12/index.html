Relative atomic mass

Atoms are far too small to weigh individually in grams, so chemists compare their masses with carbon-12. The relative atomic mass of an element is the weighted average of its isotopes' masses on that scale.

Before you start

  • Atomic theory

The carbon-12 scale

The relative isotopic mass of an isotope is its mass compared with one-twelfth of the mass of a carbon-12 atom, which is defined as exactly 12. The relative atomic mass, Ar, of an element is the weighted mean of the relative isotopic masses of its naturally occurring isotopes, weighted by their abundances.

Ar = Σ (isotopic mass × abundance) / Σ abundances
Worked example

Chlorine is 75.78% ³⁵Cl (mass 34.969) and 24.22% ³⁷Cl (mass 36.966). Calculate Ar.

  1. Ar = (34.969 × 75.78 + 36.966 × 24.22) ÷ 100.
  2. = (2,650.0 + 895.3) ÷ 100 = 35.45.

Why not whole numbers?

Ar values are averages of several isotopes, so they are rarely whole numbers (chlorine 35.45). Even single isotopes don't have exactly whole-number masses (except carbon-12 by definition), because of the binding energy of the nucleus and the slightly different masses of protons and neutrons. IUPAC gives some atomic weights as ranges, because isotope abundances vary between natural sources.

Investigate with the lab. Build the two common isotopes of chlorine, chlorine-35 and chlorine-37, and think about why the relative atomic mass isn't a whole number.

Key idea

Ar is the abundance-weighted mean isotopic mass on the scale where carbon-12 = 12 exactly.

Common mistake
Taking the mass number of the most common isotope as Ar. Ar is a weighted mean of all the isotopes.

Summary

  • Carbon-12 = 12 exactly defines the scale.
  • Ar is a weighted mean of isotopic masses, so it is rarely a whole number.

Key terms

Relative atomic mass (Ar)
The weighted mean mass of an atom of an element relative to one-twelfth of the mass of a carbon-12 atom
Isotope
Atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons
Relative isotopic mass
The mass of an atom of an isotope relative to one-twelfth of the mass of a carbon-12 atom

Flashcards

Answer from memory, then open the card to check.

What standard is relative atomic mass measured against?

One-twelfth of the mass of a carbon-12 atom.

Why is the Ar of chlorine 35.45 and not 35 or 37?

It is a weighted average of ³⁵Cl (about 76%) and ³⁷Cl (about 24%).

Check your understanding

  1. Which atom is used as the standard for relative atomic masses?
    • Carbon-12
    • Carbon-14
    • Hydrogen-1
    • Oxygen-16
    Show the answer

    Carbon-12. Since 1961 one-twelfth of the mass of a carbon-12 atom has been the standard.

  2. Copper has two isotopes, ⁶³Cu and ⁶⁵Cu, and Ar = 63.55. Which is more abundant?
    • ⁶³Cu
    • ⁶⁵Cu
    • It can't be told
    • They are equally abundant
    Show the answer

    ⁶³Cu. The weighted mean is much closer to 63 than to 65, so ⁶³Cu must be more common (about 69%).

  3. Why does IUPAC now give the atomic weight of some elements, such as hydrogen, as an interval?
    • Atomic weights change over time
    • Hydrogen has no isotopes
    • Isotope abundances vary measurably between natural sources, so no single value fits all samples
    • The isotopes haven't been measured
    Show the answer

    Isotope abundances vary measurably between natural sources, so no single value fits all samples. Natural variation in isotopic composition (for example of H, C, O) means the atomic weight depends on the sample's origin.

  4. Why is the relative atomic mass of chlorine about 35.5?
    • Chlorine atoms have half a neutron each
    • Electrons add half a mass unit
    • It is a weighted mean of its isotopes' masses
    • It is rounded from the proton number
    Show the answer

    It is a weighted mean of its isotopes' masses. About three-quarters of chlorine is chlorine-35 and a quarter chlorine-37.
