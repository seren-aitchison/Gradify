Atom economy

Atom economy measures how much of the mass of the reactants ends up in the desired product. A reaction with high atom economy wastes fewer atoms, which matters for cost and the environment.

By the end of this lesson you can

  • Calculate atom economy from a balanced equation
  • Compare reactions by atom economy
  • Distinguish atom economy from percentage yield

Before you start

  • Percentage yield

Definition

atom economy = M of desired product / total M of all reactants × 100using the coefficients in the balanced equation

Addition reactions, in which everything ends up in one product, have 100% atom economy. Substitution and elimination reactions produce by-products and have lower atom economy.

Worked example

Calculate the atom economy for making ethanol by fermentation: C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂ (M: glucose 180.2, ethanol 46.07).

  1. Mass of desired product = 2 × 46.07 = 92.14.
  2. Total mass of reactants = 180.2.
  3. Atom economy = 92.14 ÷ 180.2 × 100 = 51.1%.
  4. By contrast, hydration of ethene, C₂H₄ + H₂O → C₂H₅OH, has 100% atom economy.

Atom economy and green chemistry

Atom economy, introduced by Barry Trost in 1991, is one of the principles of green chemistry. Unlike percentage yield, it is fixed by the equation, not the practical work: a reaction can have a 100% yield but a low atom economy if it makes a lot of by-product. Useful by-products (CO₂ can be captured, a by-product can be sold) make a low atom economy less wasteful.

Key idea

Atom economy is set by the balanced equation; addition reactions reach 100%.

Common mistake
Confusing atom economy with percentage yield. Yield depends on how well the reaction was carried out; atom economy depends only on the equation.

Summary

  • Atom economy = M(desired product) ÷ M(all reactants) × 100.
  • Addition reactions have 100% atom economy.

Key terms

Atom economy
The percentage of the total mass of reactants that ends up in the desired product
By-product
A substance produced in a reaction alongside the desired product

Flashcards

Answer from memory, then open the card to check.

Why do addition reactions have 100% atom economy?

All the atoms of the reactants end up in the single product; there are no by-products.

How is atom economy different from percentage yield?

Atom economy depends only on the balanced equation; percentage yield depends on how much product is actually obtained.

Check your understanding

  1. Which type of reaction always has 100% atom economy?
    • Addition with a single product
    • Combustion
    • Elimination
    • Substitution
    Show the answer

    Addition with a single product. When reactants combine into one product, no atoms are wasted.

  2. Two routes to a drug have yields of 90% and 60%, and atom economies of 35% and 85%. Which statement is fairest?
    • Route 1 is better in every way
    • Route 2 is better in every way
    • Route 2 wastes fewer atoms by design, but route 1 converts more of its reactants in practice; both measures matter
    • Yield is the only measure that matters
    Show the answer

    Route 2 wastes fewer atoms by design, but route 1 converts more of its reactants in practice; both measures matter. Atom economy measures waste built into the equation; yield measures practical efficiency.

  3. Why might a reaction with low atom economy still be chosen industrially?
    • Atom economy can't be calculated for industrial reactions
    • Because it produces more waste
    • Its by-product is valuable or easily recycled, or it uses cheap, safe reagents and gives a high yield
    • Low atom economy is always preferred
    Show the answer

    Its by-product is valuable or easily recycled, or it uses cheap, safe reagents and gives a high yield. Sustainability depends on the whole process, including the value of by-products, energy use and safety.
