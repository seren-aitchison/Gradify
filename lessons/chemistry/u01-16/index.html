Molar mass

Molar mass, M, is the mass of one mole of a substance, in g/mol. It is numerically equal to the relative formula mass and is the conversion factor between mass and amount in moles.

Before you start

  • The mole

Molar mass

The molar mass in g/mol has the same number as Mr: Mr(H₂O) = 18.02, so M(H₂O) = 18.02 g/mol. Unlike Mr, molar mass has units.

n = m / M; m = n × M; M = m / n
Worked example

0.125 mol of a metal carbonate has a mass of 12.5 g. Identify the metal, given that it forms a 2+ ion.

  1. M = m ÷ n = 12.5 ÷ 0.125 = 100 g/mol.
  2. MCO₃: CO₃ contributes 12.01 + 3 × 16.00 = 60.01, so the metal is 100 − 60 = 40.
  3. Ar = 40: calcium. The compound is CaCO₃.

Hydrated salts

Water of crystallisation counts in the molar mass: copper(II) sulfate pentahydrate, CuSO₄·5H₂O, has M = 159.6 + 5 × 18.02 = 249.7 g/mol. Heating removes the water, and the mass lost lets you find how many waters there were.

Worked example

2.50 g of hydrated magnesium sulfate, MgSO₄·xH₂O, gives 1.22 g of anhydrous MgSO₄ (M = 120.4 g/mol) on heating. Find x.

  1. Mass of water = 2.50 − 1.22 = 1.28 g, so n(H₂O) = 1.28 ÷ 18.02 = 0.0710 mol.
  2. n(MgSO₄) = 1.22 ÷ 120.4 = 0.0101 mol.
  3. Ratio = 0.0710 ÷ 0.0101 = 7.0, so x = 7: MgSO₄·7H₂O.
Key idea

M (g/mol) equals Mr in value; use n = m/M in whichever arrangement you need.

Common mistake
Leaving out the water of crystallisation when calculating the molar mass of a hydrated salt.

Summary

  • M = m/n, with units g/mol.
  • Include water of crystallisation in hydrates.

Key terms

Molar mass (M)
The mass of one mole of a substance, in g/mol
Water of crystallisation
Water molecules that are part of the crystal structure of a hydrated salt

Questions this page answers

Use them as flashcards: cover the answer, say it out loud, then check.

What does “molar mass (M)” mean?

The mass of one mole of a substance, in g/mol

What does “water of crystallisation” mean?

Water molecules that are part of the crystal structure of a hydrated salt

What is the difference between Mr and molar mass?

They have the same number, but Mr has no units and molar mass is in g/mol.

What is the molar mass of CuSO₄·5H₂O, given M(CuSO₄) = 159.6 g/mol and M(H₂O) = 18.02 g/mol?

249.7 g/mol.

Check your understanding

  1. What is the molar mass of sodium hydroxide, NaOH (Na 22.99, O 16.00, H 1.008)?
    • 23.00 g/mol
    • 39.00 g/mol
    • 40.00 g
    • 40.00 g/mol
    Show the answer

    40.00 g/mol. Add the molar masses of the atoms in the formula: 22.99 + 16.00 + 1.008 = 40.00 g/mol, with units of g/mol.

  2. What mass of glucose, C₆H₁₂O₆ (M = 180.2 g/mol), is needed for 0.050 mol?
    • 0.000 28 g
    • 180 g
    • 3.60 g
    • 9.01 g
    Show the answer

    9.01 g. Rearrange n = m/M to m = n × M: 0.050 mol × 180.2 g/mol = 9.01 g.

  3. A hydrated salt loses 36% of its mass on heating to constant mass. What does "constant mass" ensure?
    • That all the water of crystallisation has been driven off, so the calculated ratio is correct
    • That the balance is calibrated
    • That the salt has decomposed
    • That the salt hasn't melted
    Show the answer

    That all the water of crystallisation has been driven off, so the calculated ratio is correct. Heating until the mass stops changing shows the dehydration is complete; stopping early underestimates the water.
