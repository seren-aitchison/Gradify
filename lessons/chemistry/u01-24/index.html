Scientific notation

Chemistry spans numbers from 10²³ particles in a mole to 10⁻¹⁰ m across an atom. Standard form writes these compactly and makes calculations reliable. This lesson covers standard form, calculator use and prefixes.

  • Write numbers in standard form

Before you start

  • Significant figures

Standard form

A number in standard form is A × 10ⁿ, with 1 ≤ A < 10. The Avogadro constant is 6.022 × 10²³; the radius of a hydrogen atom is about 5.3 × 10⁻¹¹ m. The number of significant figures is shown by A: 6.00 × 10² has three.

Calculating

  • Multiply A values and add the powers; divide A values and subtract the powers.
  • On a calculator, enter standard form with the ×10ˣ or EXP key, not by typing "× 10 ^", which can cause errors in order of operations.
  • Check the answer's size by estimation: (6 × 10²³) × (2 × 10⁻³) ≈ 1 × 10²¹.

Standard form and prefixes

Prefixes are a shorthand for powers of ten: 3.4 × 10⁻⁹ m = 3.4 nm; 5.0 × 10⁻³ dm³ = 5.0 cm³ (because 1 cm³ = 10⁻³ dm³).

Worked example

How many molecules are in 2.5 × 10⁻³ mol of carbon dioxide?

  1. N = 2.5 × 10⁻³ × 6.022 × 10²³.
  2. = (2.5 × 6.022) × 10⁻³⁺²³ = 15.06 × 10²⁰.
  3. = 1.5 × 10²¹ molecules (2 significant figures).

Write A × 10ⁿ with 1 ≤ A < 10, enter powers with the EXP key, and check sizes by estimation.

Common mistakeTyping 6.022 × 10 ^ 23 on a calculator inside a longer calculation. Using EXP avoids order-of-operations mistakes.

Summary

  • Standard form handles huge and tiny chemical quantities.
  • A shows the significant figures; n shows the size.

Key terms

Standard form
Writing a number as A × 10ⁿ, where 1 ≤ A < 10 and n is an integer
Order of magnitude
The power of ten nearest to a value, used for estimates

Check your understanding

  1. Which is 45,000 in standard form?
    • 0.45 × 10⁵
    • 4.5 × 10³
    • 4.5 × 10⁴
    • 45 × 10³
    Show the answer

    4.5 × 10⁴. In standard form A must be at least 1 and less than 10, so 45,000 = 4.5 × 10⁴.

  2. What is the mass of 1.0 × 10²⁰ molecules of water (M = 18.0 g/mol)?
    • 1.8 × 10²¹ g
    • 3.0 × 10⁻³ g
    • 3.0 × 10³ g
    • 6.0 × 10⁻⁴ g
    Show the answer

    3.0 × 10⁻³ g. n = 1.0 × 10²⁰ ÷ 6.02 × 10²³ = 1.66 × 10⁻⁴ mol; × 18.0 = 2.99 × 10⁻³ g.

  3. An atom has a radius of about 1 × 10⁻¹⁰ m and its nucleus about 1 × 10⁻¹⁵ m. How many times larger is the atom?
    • 10⁻⁵
    • 10²⁵
    • 10⁵
    • 5
    Show the answer

    10⁵. Dividing, 10⁻¹⁰ ÷ 10⁻¹⁵ = 10⁵: the atom is about 100,000 times wider than its nucleus.
