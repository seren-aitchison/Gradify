Relative molecular mass

The relative molecular (or formula) mass of a substance is the sum of the relative atomic masses of all the atoms in its formula. It is the starting point for almost every quantitative calculation in chemistry.

Before you start

  • Relative atomic mass

Mr

Relative molecular mass (Mr) is the sum of the Ar values of all the atoms in a molecule. For ionic compounds, which don't form molecules, the same sum is called the relative formula mass. Mr has no units.

Worked example

Calculate Mr for calcium carbonate, CaCO₃ (Ca 40.08, C 12.01, O 16.00).

  1. Mr = 40.08 + 12.01 + 3 × 16.00 = 100.09.

Percentage composition

% of element = (Ar × number of atoms) / Mr × 100

Empirical formula from composition

  • Divide the mass (or percentage) of each element by its Ar to get mole ratios.
  • Divide by the smallest value.
  • Multiply to get whole numbers if needed (a ratio of 1 : 1.5 becomes 2 : 3).
Worked example

A compound is 40.0% C, 6.7% H and 53.3% O. Find its empirical formula.

  1. C: 40.0 ÷ 12.01 = 3.33; H: 6.7 ÷ 1.008 = 6.65; O: 53.3 ÷ 16.00 = 3.33.
  2. Divide by 3.33: C 1, H 2.0, O 1.
  3. Empirical formula: CH₂O.
Key idea

Mr is the sum of Ar values; dividing masses by Ar gives the mole ratios in an empirical formula.

Common mistake
Using atomic numbers instead of relative atomic masses when calculating Mr.

Summary

  • Add the Ar of every atom to get Mr.
  • Percentage composition and empirical formulae both come from Ar and Mr.

Key terms

Relative molecular mass (Mr)
The sum of the relative atomic masses of the atoms in a molecule
Relative formula mass
The sum of the relative atomic masses of the atoms in the formula of an ionic compound

Questions this page answers

Use them as flashcards: cover the answer, say it out loud, then check.

What does “relative molecular mass (Mr)” mean?

The sum of the relative atomic masses of the atoms in a molecule

What does “relative formula mass” mean?

The sum of the relative atomic masses of the atoms in the formula of an ionic compound

Calculate Mr of water (H 1.008, O 16.00).

2 × 1.008 + 16.00 = 18.02.

What are the steps to find an empirical formula from percentages?

Divide each percentage by Ar, divide by the smallest result, and multiply to whole numbers if needed.

Check your understanding

  1. What is the Mr of carbon dioxide (C 12.01, O 16.00)?
    • 22.01
    • 28.01
    • 40.01
    • 44.01
    Show the answer

    44.01. Add the Ar of every atom: one carbon and two oxygens, 12.01 + 2 × 16.00 = 44.01.

  2. Which compound contains the highest percentage of nitrogen by mass?
    • (NH₂)₂CO (Mr 60.06)
    • (NH₄)₂SO₄ (Mr 132.1)
    • NH₃ (Mr 17.03)
    • NH₄NO₃ (Mr 80.04)
    Show the answer

    NH₃ (Mr 17.03). Ammonia is 14.01 ÷ 17.03 = 82%; ammonium nitrate 35%, urea 47%, ammonium sulfate 21%.

  3. A hydrocarbon has empirical formula CH₂ and Mr 70.1. What is its molecular formula?
    • C₁₀H₂₀
    • C₂H₄
    • C₅H₁₀
    • C₅H₁₂
    Show the answer

    C₅H₁₀. CH₂ has Mr 14.03; 70.1 ÷ 14.03 = 5, so the molecular formula is (CH₂)₅ = C₅H₁₀.
