Chemical equations

A chemical equation summarises a reaction: reactants on the left, products on the right, with formulae, state symbols and the numbers of each particle. This lesson covers word equations, symbol equations and ionic equations.

Before you start

  • Chemical formulae

Word and symbol equations

Word equation: magnesium + oxygen → magnesium oxide. Symbol equation: 2Mg(s) + O₂(g) → 2MgO(s). The arrow means "react to form". State symbols: (s) solid, (l) liquid, (g) gas, (aq) aqueous, dissolved in water.

Coefficients

The large numbers in front of formulae are coefficients. They count particles (or moles) and must make the numbers of each atom equal on both sides, because atoms are neither created nor destroyed. Changing a subscript would change the substance; only coefficients can be changed.

Ionic equations

In reactions between ionic solutions, some ions don't take part: they are spectator ions. An ionic equation shows only the particles that change.

Worked example

Write the ionic equation for the reaction of silver nitrate solution with sodium chloride solution.

  1. Full equation: AgNO₃(aq) + NaCl(aq) → AgCl(s) + NaNO₃(aq).
  2. Ions present: Ag⁺, NO₃⁻, Na⁺, Cl⁻; Na⁺ and NO₃⁻ stay dissolved and are spectators.
  3. Ionic equation: Ag⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq) → AgCl(s).
Key idea

Equations conserve atoms; coefficients count particles; ionic equations show only the species that change.

Common mistake
Balancing an equation by changing a formula (writing H₂O₂ to "fix" oxygen). That changes the substance; only coefficients may change.

Summary

  • Reactants → products, with state symbols.
  • Coefficients balance atoms; ionic equations omit spectator ions.

Key terms

Coefficient
A number in front of a formula in an equation, giving the number of particles or moles
State symbol
A label showing the physical state of a substance: (s), (l), (g) or (aq)
Spectator ion
An ion present in a reaction mixture that doesn't change during the reaction

Questions this page answers

Use them as flashcards: cover the answer, say it out loud, then check.

What does “coefficient” mean?

A number in front of a formula in an equation, giving the number of particles or moles

What does “state symbol” mean?

A label showing the physical state of a substance: (s), (l), (g) or (aq)

What does “spectator ion” mean?

An ion present in a reaction mixture that doesn't change during the reaction

What does (aq) mean in an equation?

The substance is dissolved in water (aqueous solution).

What is the ionic equation for neutralisation of a strong acid by a strong alkali?

H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l).

Check your understanding

  1. In 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O, what does the 2 in front of H₂O mean?
    • Each water molecule has two oxygen atoms
    • There are two products
    • Two molecules of water are formed
    • Water is formed twice as fast
    Show the answer

    Two molecules of water are formed. A coefficient gives the number of particles (or moles) of that substance.

  2. What are the spectator ions when barium chloride solution reacts with sodium sulfate solution to form barium sulfate precipitate?
    • Ba²⁺ and Cl⁻
    • Ba²⁺ and SO₄²⁻
    • Na⁺ and Cl⁻
    • Na⁺ and SO₄²⁻
    Show the answer

    Na⁺ and Cl⁻. Ba²⁺ and SO₄²⁻ form the precipitate; Na⁺ and Cl⁻ stay in solution unchanged.

  3. Which ionic equation describes zinc metal reacting with copper(II) sulfate solution?
    • Cu + Zn²⁺ → Cu²⁺ + Zn
    • Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu (with all ions)
    • Zn(s) + Cu²⁺(aq) → Zn²⁺(aq) + Cu(s)
    • Zn²⁺ + SO₄²⁻ → ZnSO₄
    Show the answer

    Zn(s) + Cu²⁺(aq) → Zn²⁺(aq) + Cu(s). Sulfate is a spectator; zinc atoms lose electrons to copper ions, a redox displacement.
